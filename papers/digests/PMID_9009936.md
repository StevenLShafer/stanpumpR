# Minto 1997 (II): remifentanil model application

## Study

No new subjects and no new data. This is a **simulation paper**: it applies the population
pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic models developed in part I (Minto CF et al.,
Anesthesiology 1997;86:10-23, PMID 9009935, 65 healthy volunteers aged 20-85 yr, EEG spectral
edge as the effect measure) to ask whether remifentanil dosing should be adjusted for age and
lean body mass (LBM) once interindividual variability is taken into account.

Methods (printed pp. 25-27 / PDF pp. 2-4):

- Parameters are the **complex** (age- and LBM-covariate) NONMEM models of part I, "tables 3
  and 5 of part I"; the paper restates none of the covariate equations themselves, only their
  evaluated values (Tables 1 and 2 below).
- Simulations in C and Visual Basic for Applications (Excel) by the authors, using the
  three-compartment analytical solution of Bailey and Shafer (IEEE Trans Biomed Eng
  1991;38:522-5). Software distributed as "PKPD Tools for EXCEL" (footnote, printed p. 24).
- Three simulated populations of n = 500 each, ages 20, 50 and 80 yr, LBM 55 kg, drawn from
  the part I interindividual variances (%CVs of all volumes and clearances, EC50, γ and ke0
  for the bolus simulations; of Cl, EC50 and γ only for the infusion simulations). EEG effect
  scaled E0 = 0%, Emax = 100%.
- Volume of distribution at the time of peak effect, Vd_pe = V1 / (percent decrease), where
  "percent decrease" is the fractional fall in blood concentration from time zero to t_peak
  (equation 1, printed p. 25); equivalently bolus dose divided by blood concentration at
  t_peak. Attributed to Shafer and Gregg (1992) and Henthorn et al. (1992).
- Bolus = EC50 × Vd_pe (equation 2, printed p. 25). Steady-state blood concentration Css = R /
  Cl (equation 3, printed p. 26); infusion rate R = Cl × EC50 (equation 4, printed p. 26).
- Recovery: 20%, 50% and 80% **effect site decrement times** after a pseudo-steady-state
  infusion of variable length, for typical 20- and 80-yr-olds (LBM 55 kg), and the variability
  of the 50% decrement time in the three simulated populations.
- Titratability: stepped constant-rate infusions targeting 20%, 50% and 80% of maximum EEG
  effect in typical 20- and 80-yr-olds.

Nomograms: figure 2 (printed p. 28 / PDF p. 5) gives bolus dose (upper) and infusion rate
(lower) for 50% of maximum EEG effect as functions of age (20-80 yr) and LBM (35-75 kg). No
numeric grid is printed; the tabulated values above are the paper's numeric summary.

Reference simulations: a **200 µg bolus** gives a peak effect of 50% of maximum EEG effect in
a typical 50-yr-old (printed p. 27-28), but a typical peak of 30% in a 20-yr-old and 75% in an
80-yr-old (figure 3). An infusion of **30 µg/min** gives 50% steady-state effect in a typical
50-yr-old, 22% in a typical 20-yr-old and 84% in a typical 80-yr-old (figure 4, printed
p. 29-30 / PDF pp. 6-7). At 5 min after an unadjusted bolus, 55% of the 80-yr-olds versus 24%
of the 20-yr-olds exceeded the 50% target.

Target-controlled infusion rates to hold 50% EEG effect (figure 5, printed p. 30 / PDF p. 7):
in the young the rate fell about 25% over the first 15 min; in the elderly it was nearly
constant once the target effect site concentration was reached.

Decrement times (figure 6, printed p. 31 / PDF p. 8, read from the rendered image; the figure
prints no numbers, so these are approximate read-offs): after long infusions the 80% effect
site decrement time is about 13 min (80 yr) and 12 min (20 yr), the 50% time about 5.5 min
(80 yr) and 4 min (20 yr), and the 20% time about 2 min and 1.3 min; all three are nearly flat
from 0 to 600 min of infusion. Figure 7 shows that about 1% of simulated 80-yr-olds need three
to four times the typical 80-yr-old's time for a 50% decrease (printed p. 30 / PDF p. 7).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points

- **End point throughout: EEG** (spectral edge, from part I), not analgesia or ventilation.
  The paper is explicit that all its simulations are EEG-based.
- **EC50** 16.1 / 11.6 / 7.2 ng·mL⁻¹ at ages 20 / 50 / 80 yr (Tables 1 and 2, printed p. 28 /
  PDF p. 5), blood concentration, EEG end point. These are evaluations of part I's
  age equation; the equation itself is not restated here. (derived: the three values are
  exactly 13.1 − 0.148(age − 40).)
- **t½ ke0** 0.94 / 1.32 / 2.20 min and **t_peak** 1.22 / 1.57 / 2.26 min at the same ages
  (Table 1). No single ke0 number is printed in this paper; the t½ values are its form.
  (derived: they correspond to ke0 = 0.735, 0.525, 0.315 min⁻¹, i.e. part I's
  0.595 − 0.007(age − 40).)
- **No MEAC / minimum effective analgesic concentration** is reported or derived.
- **Potency comparisons quoted from other papers**, not measured here (discussion, printed
  pp. 30-32 / PDF pp. 7-9): EC50 for opioid-induced EEG depression is approximately three to
  five times the concentration needed for adequate ventilation on emergence (attributed to
  Shafer and Varvel, Anesthesiology 1991;74:53-63); Kapila et al. (Anesthesiology
  1995;83:968-75) targeted a blood concentration of 1 ng/mL for depression of minute
  ventilation to 7.5% end-tidal CO₂, far below these EEG EC50 values, and measured times to
  50% recovery from respiratory depression after a 3-h infusion of 5.4 min for remifentanil
  and 54 min for alfentanil; Cambareri et al. and Lemmens et al. found no potency difference
  between alfentanil and trefentanil. No remifentanil-to-fentanyl (or other) equipotency ratio
  is given.
- Clearance variability for context (discussion, printed p. 32 / PDF p. 9): remifentanil Cl
  %CV falls from 23% to 14% when age and LBM enter the model (part I table 3), against 48%
  (alfentanil, Maitre), 59% and 32% (fentanyl and alfentanil, Scott and Stanski) and 23%
  (sufentanil, Gepts).

Dosing conclusions (abstract and conclusions, printed pp. 24, 32): an 80-yr-old needs about
**half** the bolus dose and about **one third** the infusion rate of a 20-yr-old of the same
LBM. The reduced bolus requirement is pharmacodynamic (lower EC50), not kinetic — Vd_pe barely
changes with age because the smaller V1 and the slower ke0 offset each other. The reduced
infusion rate requirement comes from both the lower clearance and the lower EC50. Unadjusted
dosing in the elderly produced a faster apparent onset and a longer duration of EEG effect,
explained by the sigmoid Emax model acting on a relative overdose.

## Covariates

Age and LBM, carried over from part I; the paper tests their practical importance rather than
estimating them. LBM is the main determinant of Vd_pe (about 14 to 20 L as LBM rises from 35 to
75 kg) while age is not; age determines EC50, clearance and ke0. LBM is part I's James
equation. Sex does not appear once LBM is included (part I).

## Caveats

Authors': all conclusions rest on the EEG model, and the influence of age may differ for
ventilatory depression, sedation and analgesia; the simulations show some individuals with
little EEG effect, which does not mean no opioid effect, and significant ventilatory depression
can occur at concentrations well below the EEG EC50; the tails of the simulated distributions
are extrapolated from an assumed normal distribution and were never observed (citing Schnider
and Shafer); some simulated 50% decrement times are longer after short than after intermediate
infusions, an artefact of the effect-site targeting algorithm's initial loading infusion.

Noticed here: it is a simulation study with no validation data of its own; everything depends
on part I's estimates and its %CVs; all tabulated values assume LBM 55 kg, which is a small
lean body mass for a typical adult male; the printed V1 at age 50 (5.1 L) does not follow part
I's own equation (4.9 L expected, derived); and the decrement-time figure carries no numbers,
so values must be read off the plot.
