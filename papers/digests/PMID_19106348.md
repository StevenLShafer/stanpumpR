# Rybak 2009: ASHP/IDSA/SIDP consensus on vancomycin monitoring in adults

## Study
Consensus review (not a primary study) by ASHP, IDSA and SIDP; PubMed literature 1958-2008, evidence graded
with the Canadian Medical Association scheme (Table 1, PDF p2 / printed 83). Adults only; paediatrics out of
scope. Recommendations summarised in Table 2 (PDF pp3-4, printed 84-85). PDF page = printed page - 81.

## Model and parameters
No model is fitted. Background PK quoted from the literature (PDF p2, printed 83; refs 14-18):
alpha-distribution phase 30 min to 1 h; beta-elimination half-life 6-12 h with normal renal function; volume of
distribution 0.4-1 L/kg; protein binding 50-55% "most often stated" (refs 19-20). CSF 0-~4 mg/L with uninflamed
meninges, 6.4-11.1 mg/L with inflammation; lung 5-41% of serum; blood:ELF 6:1.
Exposure arithmetic (PDF p9, printed 90): 1 g q12h, CLcr >=100 mL/min, 80 kg gives a 24-h AUC of about
250 mg.h/L.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. AUC/MIC is named the most useful PD index (concentration-independent killing). Trough is a
surrogate for AUC. With MIC 1 mg/L a trough of at least 15 mg/L is said to be needed to reach AUC/MIC 400.
Trough below 10 mg/L linked to VISA-like strains (in vitro, case data). Ototoxicity associated in older reports
with serum concentrations above 40 mg/L; nephrotoxicity risk associated with troughs over 15 mg/L in several
observational series (Hidayat, Jeffres, Lodise, Nguyen; PDF pp11-12) and AUC >=952 mg.h/L (Lodise). Peak
monitoring not recommended. No MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Dose on actual body weight (obese: start on ABW, adjust by levels). Renal function (CLcr) drives exposure.

## Caveats
Evidence mostly observational, levels II-III. The panel itself notes no data show sustained 15-20 mg/L troughs are
safe. Superseded by the 2020 revised consensus (AUC-guided dosing, troughs no longer recommended).
