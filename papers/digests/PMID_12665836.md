# Nilsson 2003: metronidazole raises busulfan trough levels

## Study
Non-randomised, partly retrospective comparison of busulfan (Bu) trough plasma concentrations in adults with leukaemia (AML, CML, MDS) conditioned with Bu/cyclophosphamide before allogeneic SCT at Huddinge, Stockholm (May-Oct 2000; 14 adults named in the text, but Table 1 lists 5 + 9 patients, Group A and Group B; each group has a "P20" with different age/sex, so IDs repeat). No pre-existing hepatic dysfunction.
- Bu **oral**, 1 mg/kg every 6 h x 4 days (days -9 to -5); dose adjusted toward a trough of about 500 ng/mL (PDF p2). Cy 60 mg/kg on days -4 and -3.
- Group A (n=5): metronidazole 3 x 400 mg p.o. pre-transplant and post-transplant (GVHD prophylaxis) throughout Bu. Group B (n=9): Bu alone 0-54 h, then Bu + metronidazole 60-90 h (metronidazole started 48 h after Bu start). Group C (n=10): retrospective controls, Bu alone, transplanted 1999 (Table 1, PDF p2).
- Mean weight 78.2 kg (SD 7.9) A, 70.3 (10.5) B, 70.9 (9.1) C; median age 35, 49.5, 42 y (Table 1).
- Sampling: heparinised plasma from a central line immediately before each dose (13 trough samples per patient); AUC from 1, 3, 6 h after the first dose (Hassan 1996 method, not reported here). Assay: gas chromatography with electron-capture detection after derivatisation to 1,4-diiodobutane (plasma, total drug). Troughs are **adjusted to a dose of 1 mg/kg** (Results, PDF p3). Mann-Whitney test.

## Model and parameters
No PK model was fitted; only trough concentrations. Values are mean +/- SD of troughs, adjusted to 1 mg/kg (Table 2, PDF p3; read on the page image):

Per-patient adjusted mean troughs (Table 3): group A 771-1231 ng/mL; group B 298-552 before and 640-996 after metronidazole; group C 360-721. (Derived: group A mean of the five patient means = 975 vs the 948 in Table 2, so Table 2 appears to pool the individual samples rather than the patient means.) Actual (unadjusted) troughs are also in Table 3.

Toxicity (text PDF p4 and Table 3): group A: all five had raised liver tests, VOD in 3 (1 mild, 1 moderate, 1 severe), one death from multi-organ failure, mucositis grade II-IV in all five, one haemorrhagic cystitis. Group B: 5/9 raised bilirubin, no VOD, mucositis II-IV in 6/9. Group C: no VOD, one grade II mucositis.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, effect site or concentration-effect relationship. The only end points are clinical toxicity (VOD by Jones criteria, mucositis grade, liver tests). The 500 ng/mL trough was the dosing target, not a derived therapeutic concentration; it is an oral-dose, total-plasma trough. The paper quotes (without values) a published link between Bu exposure and VOD. Mechanism proposed: glutathione depletion and CYP inhibition by metronidazole (speculation, not tested). Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency data.

## Covariates
None modelled. Troughs were normalised to dose per kg; no age, weight or sex analysis.

## Caveats
Very small groups; group C historical (1999) with only partial overlap in time; no randomisation; metronidazole was not the only difference (the oral route and variable absorption bring wide inter-patient variation); troughs only, no clearance or AUC reported for the comparison; metronidazole co-medication in the same patients was part of a GVHD-prophylaxis regimen. The 13 trough samples include the early ones before steady state.
