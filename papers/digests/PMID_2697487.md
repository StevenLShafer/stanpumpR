# Williams 1989: Reference values for resting blood flow to organs of man

## Study
Literature review and distillation (Oak Ridge National Laboratory). Compiles published blood-flow measurements in healthy young/middle-aged adults (mostly 18-50 y, mostly males; animal data used where human data are thin) and converts each to a "study value" as a percentage of cardiac output (%CO). Reference organ masses (Table 2) are for a 35-y-old male of 73 kg and female of 60 kg (ICRP 23 modified). Where studies did not measure CO, flows were converted with reference CO (PDF p5, printed 190): supine male 6700 mL/min (BSA 1.88 m2), supine female 5800 mL/min (BSA 1.62 m2); sitting or standing CO is 20% lower than supine. The provisional measure of central tendency (PMCT) is the median of five estimators (median, weighted median, weighted mean, unweighted mean, trimmed mean); selections were then adjusted so %CO sums to 100%. Resting state only; a later paper on age and activity is promised. Sections 3.1-3.14 give per-organ tables of study values (Tables 3-16). No drug is studied.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio. The paper reports blood flow only.

## Covariates
Sex only (separate male/female columns). Female GI flow is raised by assuming flow per m2 BSA is 1.15 times the male's (PDF p10 text); other female differences follow organ mass (adipose, muscle) or are set to balance CO. Age and activity are explicitly not treated. Position: sitting/standing CO is 20% below supine.

## Caveats
Authors: human data fragmentary for stomach, bronchial circulation, thyroid, pancreas (extrapolated from animals); female values rest on less data than male; skin is highly uncertain and the 5% was set arbitrarily to balance CO; a few arbitrary small adjustments were made to force 100%. Muscle was adjusted upward for xenon underestimation (factor about 1.7). PET-based brain flows were excluded as lower. Fractions marked parenthesised in Table 17 are sub-parts, not additive.
