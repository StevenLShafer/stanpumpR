# Fabry 2014: polyhexanide and antibiotics against S. aureus in vitro

## Study
- 100 clonally distinct clinical S. aureus isolates (50 MSSA, 50 MRSA, one of them VISA), consecutive from infected patients at Essen and Rostock university hospitals; PFGE-typed (PDF p2).
- MIC and MBC by serial broth microdilution per DIN 58940 in cation-supplemented Mueller-Hinton broth (Ca 50 mg/L, Mg 25 mg/L); inoculum 5 x 10^5 cfu/mL; MIC read at 24 h, 35 C.
- **MBC method**: 10 uL from wells near the MIC turbidity threshold subcultured in duplicate onto blood agar **after 6 h**, then 24 h incubation; MBC = lowest concentration with no visible regrowth. For selected strains, cephalosporin, carbapenem and vancomycin MBCs were also done with transfer after 24 h (PDF p2). No neutralising agent (DIN 58940-8; p4).
- Cefazolin dilution range 0.031-64 mg/L. EUCAST (v3.0, 2013) breakpoints S/R used, cefazolin 1/2 mg/L (PDF p2).
- Checkerboard synergy of polyhexanide with each antibiotic (FIC/FBC index <=0.5 synergy, >0.5 to <=4 indifference, >4 antagonism; p3).
- Breakpoints were applied to MBCs as to MICs to count "resistant" by MBC (p3).
- A 2017 corrigendum (PDF p7) changes only the first author's affiliation.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points are in-vitro MIC (24 h visible growth) and MBC (no regrowth on subculture after 6 h exposure). The authors attribute the cephalosporin and imipenem MBC excess over MIC in MSSA (described as "a factor of two to three") to the short 6-h incubation and say it was smaller with 24 h; the 24-h values are not tabulated (p3-4). Polyhexanide MBCs did not differ by more than one dilution at 3, 6 or 24 h. No antagonism between polyhexanide and any antibiotic; synergy with erythromycin, doxycycline, linezolid (and cefuroxime by MIC) in some strains (Tables III-IV, p4). Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None (in vitro). Inoculum effect was tested for polyhexanide only (1.5 x 10^5 to 10^8 cfu/mL); results for antibiotics at other inocula are not reported.

## Caveats
- MBC at 6 h subculture is non-standard for the CLSI M26 method (which uses 24 h); it inflates MBCs of slowly bactericidal beta-lactams, as the authors themselves say.
- MBC "susceptibility" counts apply MIC breakpoints to MBCs, which has no regulatory meaning.
- Single region (two German hospitals), isolates from 2013 or earlier; methicillin status known.
- The paper's focus is polyhexanide; antibiotic data are secondary.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Readability** (antibiotics.md:55): Table I's cefazolin row is legible on the rendered page and in the publisher PDF. The listing as unreadable is now superseded. **Discrepancy (status).**
- **MIC** (R/antibioticMbc.R:91, from EUCAST, MIC50 0.5, MIC90 1 mg/L): this paper gives the same MIC50 0.5 and MIC90 1 in 50 MSSA. **Matches** (independent corroboration).
- **MBC** (R/antibioticMbc.R:91-95: MBC50 0.5, MBC90 4, "MIC x 1 / x 4", note "No modern measured cefazolin MBC for MSSA could be read"; antibiotics.md:30 "the one study that measured its MBCs (Jones ... 1977)"): this paper is a modern measured series, MBC50 1 and MBC90 64 mg/L. The library's MBC50 is one dilution lower and its MBC90 sixteen-fold lower. The direction is consistent with the library's own caution that cefazolin has a tail, but the magnitude is far larger. The paper's 6-h subculture likely inflates the MBC (authors, p3-4), so the paper does not straightforwardly support replacing the values. **Discrepancy** in the stated evidence ("no modern measured MBC", "the one study"); the numeric choice is a decision (open question).
- **Vancomycin, uncited comparison** (R/antibioticMbc.R:362-366, MIC50/90 1/1, MBC50/90 2/8, "MSSA's are lower"): MRSA here MBC50 2 (matches), MBC90 >16 (higher); MSSA MBC50/90 4/16, higher than MRSA, against the note's direction. See open question.
- **Gentamicin**: staphylococci are excluded from gentamicin's band (R/antibioticMbc.R:329-331), so the MSSA values (MIC50/90 0.125/0.5, MBC50/90 0.25/1) have nothing to compare with.
- Both values are free-drug broth values, matching the library's statement that MBCs are free-drug (antibiotics.md:22). Units mg/L throughout, as in the library.
