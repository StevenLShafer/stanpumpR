# Kretschmar 2021: oxybutynin and N-desethyloxybutynin popPK, oral vs intravesical

## Study
Re-analysis of data from Krause 2013 (J Urol 190:1791): 18 healthy adults, crossover. Single oral 5 mg immediate-release racemic oxybutynin; single intravesical 10 mg oxybutynin solution; then 7 intravesical 10 mg doses (08:00, 14:00, 20:00, 12 h night gap), 3-day washout between periods. Dense sampling 24 h after single doses and 0-24 h and 48-72 h of the multiple-dose period. Plasma, enantioselective LC-MS, LLOQ 0.1 ng/mL for both analytes (p.3). 23% of concentrations were below LLOQ (M3 method). Weight, age and sex are not given in this paper. NONMEM 7.4.1 (IMP/SAEM), 1000-sample bootstrap, VPC. Model built on the (R)-enantiomer, then applied to (S) (Table S1, not in the PDF). Bioavailability is not identifiable without IV data, so the model assumes complete oral absorption and NDO as the only elimination route (parent clearance equals NDO formation; p.3-4, Fig 1).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment parent (R-oxybutynin) plus one-compartment metabolite (R-NDO). Oral absorption by transit compartments (n, MTT); intravesical absorption first-order with its own ka and F per application time. Combined error. Concentrations modelled in nmol/L. Text-reported results: oral bioavailability of (R)-oxybutynin median 7% (95% CI 6-8%); intravesical bioavailability 10-22% across applications, no trend; ka,ivs rose from 1.74 to 3.47 1/h over the course (p.4). ## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis, and no effect-site analysis. No MEAC, analgesic concentration or potency ratio; oxybutynin is an antimuscarinic, not an opioid, so the opioid audit does not apply. Discussion (p.6-7) says exposure-response data are scarce: one small saliva-output study of transdermal vs extended-release oral oxybutynin (Appell 2003, ref 53); lower NDO exposure was associated with greater saliva output. The authors state that the (R)-enantiomer is the more active, and that (R)-NDO is slightly more active than (S)-NDO. Protein binding quoted from other work: 83-95% or 99% (refs 40, 50). Simulated (R)-NDO maxima after intravesical dosing exceeded 100 nmol/L (150 nmol/L twice daily); oral median Cmax ran about 95 to over 200 nmol/L (p.5, Fig 5).

## Covariates
None retained. Allometric scaling of clearances and volumes did not improve the model (OFV rose 20.9). Interoccasion variability was included, handled through application-specific ka and F.

## Caveats
n = 18 healthy volunteers, fasting, restricted fluid intake. No IV data, so oral F rests on the assumption that NDO is the only metabolic route (other metabolites exist: N-oxide, hydroxylation). Urinary excretion was not measured. Intravesical ka varies by dose number and has large IIV (CV up to 145%). Racemate behaviour was not simulated. Doses in the simulation exceed the labelled intravesical dose. Number of transit compartments and ka values are empirical. The methods text refers to "Table 1" for the ka trend where Table 2 is meant. The legend of Table 2 and the printed Q row label disagree on whether Q belongs to the parent or the metabolite (Fig 1 shows it as the parent's).
