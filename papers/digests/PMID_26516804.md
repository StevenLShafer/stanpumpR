# Struys 2016: the history of target-controlled infusion

## Study
Narrative review (Groningen, Ghent, Demed, GlenPharma, St Gallen), one of three companion TCI reviews (with Absalom 2016 and Schnider 2016). Handled by S.L. Shafer as editor. Several statements rest on personal communications (Reves 2014, Shafer 2014, Engbers 2014, Coetzee 2014).

## Model and parameters
No new parameters. Explains TCI principles: the Euler "chocolate box" difference equations, the 3-compartment model plus effect site (Fig. 2), sum-of-exponentials and hydraulic views (Fig. 3). States that 1 s Euler steps nearly match the closed-form solution (citing Maitre & Shafer 1990). The Diprifusor effect-site modification uses **ke0 0.6/min** (propofol), approved but labelled only in Germany (p. 64).

ke0 described generally (from PK/PD studies or from time to peak effect after a bolus, refs 17, 31, 32). No MEAC. Not an opioid PK/PD paper.

## Key historical facts (by page)
- p. 56: TCI term proposed by Glen, used by White and Kenny after 1992; consensus 1997 (Glass et al., ref 10).
- p. 58-59: BET scheme (Bolus = target x V1; Elimination = target x CL; Transfer = exponentially declining); limits: plasma only, assumes no drug on board.
- p. 59: closed-form approaches (Shafer & Gregg, Shafer et al., Jacobs 1988) tracked concentration exactly but the rate was approximate; **Jacobs 1990 first described the exactly correct solution; the following year Bailey & Shafer published a simplified exact algorithm; extended by Shafer & Gregg to the effect site, this became the basis of all TCI systems.**
- p. 59-60: Widmark 1919; Kruger-Thiemer 1968; Vaughan & Tucker (lidocaine). First TCI by Schüttler and Schwilden in Bonn, **1 May 1979**; Schwilden 1981 general method; CATIA with etomidate 0.3 ug/mL and alfentanil 0.45 ug/mL; 1985 etomidate PD by CATIA; 1988 propofol and alfentanil.
- p. 60: Leiden TIAC (Ausems and Hug; Janssen support), measured-predicted variability 22-32%; 1988 study showing the same variability with repeated bolus/variable-rate infusions; Bristol (Tackley) BET propofol; Reves and Alvis CACI (Pascal, Apple II Plus, IMED 929; bilinear Z-transform); CACI II at Duke with Jacobs (fentanyl, alfentanil, sufentanil, midazolam, propofol).
- p. 61: Stanski's Leiden sabbatical; Shafer tested CACI by simulation; **STANPUMP** written in C for MS-DOS, pumps IMED 929, IMED C2, BARD Chronofusor, Harvard 22, Graseby 3400; early versions Euler, final version analytical (ref 37, Bailey & Shafer); effect-site control added "after 1993" (ref 11, Shafer & Gregg); test bed for Tpeak approach, real-time Bayesian update, PK/PD output files, external drug files, syringe sizes, batch mode; library of hypnotics, opioids, benzodiazepines, lidocaine, relaxants, plus dogs, rats, horses; freely distributed, used in nearly half of the 559 studies; principles and PK-engine code incorporated into later research systems and commercial open TCI devices (Shafer, personal communication).
- p. 61-62: Leiden platform (Engbers; Atari Portfolio, Psion 3A), Glasgow (White & Kenny, Atari), STELPUMP (Coetzee & Pina, Turbo Pascal, Euler plasma + Jacobs-Williams effect site), RUGLOOP (De Smet & Struys, C++), IVFEED (Erlangen; Bailey-Shafer for plasma), AnestFusor (Chile, ezFUSOR), TOOLBOX (Barvais, Brussels).
- p. 62-65: Diprifusor: propofol launched in Europe 1986; ICI meeting 1990; Hague World Congress 1992; Zeneca supplied a module, not pumps; White & Kenny algorithms; 16-bit exact plus 8-bit Euler check; tagged prefilled syringes; first CE mark 1996 (Vial Medical); Graseby 3500, Alaris IVAC P6000, Fresenius Master TCI, later Terumo TE-372; approvals in >50 countries; no effect-site control.
- p. 65-66: US: FDA talks from 1993; PMA to CDRH 1995; approvable letter 1997 (with dial-range demand); not-approvable 2001; NDA withdrawn 2004, 9 years after submission; Hu, Horstman & Shafer 2005 proof cited as a possible path.
- p. 66: open TCI from 2002 (Carefusion, Fresenius first).
