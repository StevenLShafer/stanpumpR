# Kashuba 1999: Aminoglycoside Cmax/MIC and response in nosocomial pneumonia

## Study
Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected clinical-pharmacy data, Bassett Healthcare (Cooperstown NY). 78 consecutive adults with first-episode gram-negative nosocomial pneumonia (CDC criteria), admitted Feb 1983 to Nov 1993 (95% in 1987-1993), treated >= 72 h with gentamicin (n = 38) or tobramycin (n = 40). Table 1 (medians, IQR): age 69 (58-75) y; 52 male / 26 female; total body weight 65.6 (55.0-79.0) kg; IBW 65.0 kg; P. aeruginosa 58%; concurrent beta-lactam active against the isolate in 72%. MIC interquartile range 1-4 ug/mL (Microscan).

Dosing: physician-chosen, compared with empiric hospital population PK; mostly every 8 h (63% before IPM) or 12 h. Individualised PK monitoring (IPM) within 72 h: predose sample, 30-min infusion, one post-distribution sample >= 60 min after end of infusion, a second >= one half-life later. Assay: fluorescence polarisation immunoassay (TDx). One-compartment IV-infusion fit (Sawchuk-Zaske). **Cmax was extrapolated to 30 min after the end of the 30-min infusion**; Cmin to just before the next dose; AUC0-24 = daily dose / CL. Institutional targets: **Cmax 7 to 10 ug/mL, Cmin < 2 ug/mL** (Methods p. 624). Creatinine clearance by Cockcroft-Gault.

End points: days to temperature resolution (<= 37.9 C for rest of stay) and to leukocyte resolution (5,000-10,000/mm3). Analyses: Cox proportional hazards, CART, logistic regression (SAS 6.08).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- First Cmax/MIC predicted days to temperature resolution; second Cmax/MIC predicted days to leukocyte resolution (Cox, all analyses concordant).
- CART breakpoints for response by day 7: first Cmax/MIC **4.7** for temperature resolution (89% success); for leukocyte resolution 4.5 (86% success; abstract and Discussion, first Cmax/MIC per Fig. 3) and 3.5 for the second Cmax/MIC (Results).
- Logistic regression: **Cmax/MIC >= 10** within the first 48 h gives >= 90% probability of temperature (and leukocyte) resolution by day 7 (abstract; Fig. 1). For leukocytes, first Cmax/MIC >= 10 and second >= 12.5 (Results). AUC0-24/MIC >= 150 (SIT^-1 x 24 h) for >= 90% temperature resolution.
- Fig. 3: Cmax/MIC 4.7 corresponds to 68% probability; 23.6 (highest observed) to 99%.
- No PK/PD model, no effect site. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled. Age, prognosis, ICU, ventilation, nutrition, Pseudomonas, MIC alone, concurrent antibiotics: not significant predictors.

## Caveats
Retrospective; gentamicin and tobramycin pooled; most patients dosed two to three times daily, and the authors caution against extrapolating to once-daily dosing. Nephrotoxicity in 7 (9%) during therapy. Bronchial concentrations not measured. Funded by Abbott Diagnostics. The abstract labels which Cmax (first or second) its 4.5 breakpoint uses less clearly than the Results.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Timing of the peak (extrapolated to 30 min after the end of a 30-min infusion, not the model's end-infusion maximum): **matches** (Methods p. 624).
- "Peak/MIC of 8-10" attributed to Kashuba (drugs_gentamicin.R:49): **discrepancy (attribution)**. Kashuba's Cmax/MIC targets are >4.7 (CART) and >=10 (logistic, 90% response); the ratio 8-10 does not appear. The 8-10 peak/MIC figure is from earlier literature (e.g. Moore 1987), not this paper.
- Band 8-10 mg/L as "historical peak benchmark" (gentamicin.md:15; drugs_gentamicin.R:128-129): **partly matches**. Kashuba's institutional target was Cmax **7 to 10** ug/mL (and Cmin < 2), and the achieved median peaks were only 5.3 and 6.7 ug/mL. Saying 7-10 would match the paper exactly.
- Trough "below 1-2 mg/L" (drugs_gentamicin.R:51): **matches** the Cmin < 2 target here (the < 1 mg/L figure is Smit 2020's).
