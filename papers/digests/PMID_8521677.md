# Salvà 1995: zolpidem PK/PD review

## Study
Narrative review (no new data, no meta-analysis) of zolpidem kinetics and dynamics, drawing heavily on the
1988 Raven Press symposium volume and an unpublished UK product licence application (Priest 1993, ref 25,
"internal file"). Assay in the cited studies: HPLC with fluorimetric detection (p145). Zolpidem is dosed as the
hemitartrate; Table I lists a salt/base ratio (the table's column alignment is garbled in the text layer; 1.24 is
the value that fits salt/base, MW 392.4 salt vs 307.4 base implies 1.28 (derived) — unclear).

- Absolute oral bioavailability "about 70%" (p146), from Thenot and Patat: range 29.6-106.4% after 10 mg and
  41.9-100.7% after 5 mg. This is a review-level statement, not a fitted F.
- Protein binding about 92% (albumin 66%, alpha1-acid glycoprotein 56.6%); unbound fraction 10.8% renal failure,
  14.9%/9.8% before/after haemodialysis, 11.3% cirrhosis (p145).
- Vd 0.54-0.68 L/kg; urinary excretion of unchanged drug <1%; metabolites I (51.5% of dose), II (11.5%),
  X (10.0%), none active (Fig 2).
- Dose proportionality 2.5-40 mg (Table III): Cmax 29.96, 58.57, 120.82, 220.47, 388.78 ug/L at 2.5, 5, 10, 20,
  40 mg; AUCinf 144.27, 281.56, 551.49, 1100.43, 2158.58 ug.h/L.
- Unit problem: CL is printed as 0.24-0.27 mL/min/kg, which is inconsistent with V 0.54 L/kg and t1/2 1.7 h
  (CL = 0.693 x 0.54 / 1.7 = 0.22 L/h/kg, i.e. about 3.7 mL/min/kg; derived). The printed values fit L/h/kg
  (0.26 L/h/kg x 70 kg = 18.2 L/h; derived), so the unit "mL/min/kg" is almost certainly a typo.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, tPeak, EC50 or concentration-effect relation reported. End points are polysomnographic (latency to
persistent sleep, sleep efficiency, wake time) and subjective; dose response plateaus above 10 mg (p149-151):
PSG sleep latency 38 min placebo vs 21 and 15 min at 5 and 15 mg; 49 min placebo vs 23 and 21 min at 10 and 20 mg.
Recommended 10 mg adults, 5 mg elderly (>65 y). Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Not modelled; descriptive only. Sex: Cmax/AUC about 45% higher in young and 63% in elderly women, about 25%
after weight correction (n.s.). Age: children (n=6) clearance 3x young adults; Table IV (10 mg-normalised):
AUCinf 326 (8-14 y), 498 (19-45 y), 1064 ug.h/L (81-95 y). Hepatic impairment: Cmax x2, t1/2 x3, AUC x5.
Renal insufficiency: AUC +60%, t1/2 nearly doubled. Obesity: no dose change needed. Race: Table V.

## Caveats
Review of mostly symposium and unpublished sponsor data; no individual-level analysis. Table alignment in the
PDF text is unreliable. The CL unit is misprinted (see above). The ~70% F has a very wide range.
