# Cloesmeijer 2020: clonidine population PK in ICU patients, for sedation dosing

## Study
Open-label, non-randomised study at Deventer Hospital ICU, NCT02466373. 24 adult mechanically ventilated patients already sedated with morphine plus midazolam or propofol (16 men, 8 women). Median age 67 y (range 25-83), body weight 84 kg (53-113) (text, PDF p. 4). 3 patients on CVVH.
Clonidine was given as a continuous IV infusion of 600, 1200 or 1800 ug/day (25, 50, 75 ug/h), 8 patients per dose; 4 of each 8 also had a loading dose of half the daily dose over 4 h (Fig 1, PDF p. 5). Arterial samples at 2, 4, 8, 12 h after the start, then daily, then 0, 8, 16, 24, 48 h after stopping; about 12 per patient (5-16). Assay: LC-MS/MS on plasma, LLOQ 0.1 ug/L, ULOQ 20 ug/L. 11 samples (2.8%) below LLOQ, discarded in the final model. NONMEM 7.3, FOCE-I; 500-sample bootstrap and VPC; Monte Carlo of 1000 virtual patients (weight normal, 53-113 kg, mean 84).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, IV infusion, combined proportional and additive residual error. IIV only on CL and V1 (omitted on Q and V2 because it exceeded 125% and was unstable). Allometric scaling to 70 kg: exponent 0.75 on CL (fixed), 1 on V1. Table 3 (PDF p. 6, printed p. 1625), image-confirmed:

Derived: CL rises from 17.0 to 20.4 L/h after 4 days (authors, p. 8 text). Equation 6 of the methods prints the time effect as (1+Cov) x slope; the form actually fitted is not unambiguous from the text. Final model OFV -519.63 (Table 2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD was measured and no ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak is reported. The authors adopt a target steady-state plasma concentration of 1.5-4.0 ug/L for ICU sedation, drawn from the literature (intro, PDF p. 2): Hall 2001 volunteers, significant reduction in observer-assessed sedation at 1.96 (+/-0.5) ug/L; Davies 1977 volunteers, 1.5-5.0 ug/L; one ICU study with levels 1.5-6.0 ug/L. The range is stated to be not well defined. Plasma, total concentration (no protein-binding measurement; 20-40% binding quoted from DrugBank). Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Tested: weight, BSA, BMI, height, age, sex, CLcr (Cockcroft-Gault, CKD-EPI, 24-h urine), albumin, bilirubin, time after infusion, CVVH. Retained: time on infusion for CL, and body weight on V1 (allometric, exponent 1). CVVH (n=3) did not change CL or V1 significantly; renal function was not a covariate.

## Caveats
Authors: small n, so covariates may have been missed; large IIV; target range poorly defined. Also: CL rising with time is unexplained (suggested recovery of organ function); only 3 CVVH patients; the cohort is elderly, heavy (median 84 kg) and mostly male; the 600 ug/6 h loading regimen and the 1200 ug/d dose (simulated: >1.5 ug/L at steady state in 95%, Fig 4A; 50% reach target at 14.5 h without a load, 11 h with a 150 ug bolus, 5 h with 100 ug/h for 6 h) are simulation results, not tested in patients.
