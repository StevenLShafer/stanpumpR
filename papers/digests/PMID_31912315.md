# Arfman 2020: TDM of antiepileptic drugs before, during and after pregnancy (review)

## Study
Narrative review (PDF p2, printed 428). PubMed search with MeSH terms, literature January 2007-September 2018
for the seven drugs of Harden 2009 (AAN/AES), any date for four newer drugs; non-English papers and reviews
excluded, monotherapy preferred; 508 records, 373 screened, 44 studies included (Fig. 1). Eleven drugs:
carbamazepine, clobazam, gabapentin, lacosamide, lamotrigine, levetiracetam, oxcarbazepine (licarbazepine),
perampanel, topiramate, valproate, zonisamide. Non-pregnant PK values in Table 1 come from UpToDate (ref 14).
No new data, no meta-analysis, no pooled ratios.

**Pregnancy findings** (abstract; sections 3.1, 3.9; Tables 1-2):
- **Carbamazepine**: clearance of CBZ and CBZ-epoxide not significantly changed (refs 15 Johnson 2014, 16
  Reisinger 2013); seizures increased in 22%, decreased in 33%, stable in 44% of pregnancies (ref 15, PDF p3).
  Text: unbound CBZ increased throughout pregnancy; epoxide slightly lower in the first trimester then normal.
  Table 1: total and unbound CBZ and epoxide did not change substantially. Postpartum data missing. Routine TDM
  in pregnancy judged questionable; measure a total trough reference concentration before conception.
- **Gabapentin**: no data on PK, seizure frequency or dosing during or after pregnancy (section 3.9, PDF p14,
  printed 440); abstract groups it under inadequate or no evidence for change. Table 1 notes renal excretion
  unchanged, metabolism "not influenced" in pregnancy (ref 14). Advice: total trough reference before conception,
  monitor if clinically indicated.
- Comparators: changes are against pre-pregnancy (or, for some cited studies, postpartum) values in the cited
  studies; the review states no ratio of its own.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, no EC50, no ke0. End point is seizure frequency against pre-pregnancy. Therapeutic ranges are not
given. The 65%-of-baseline lamotrigine threshold from other authors is mentioned (section 4.3) and not adopted
for other drugs. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None quantified. Oral contraceptives lower lamotrigine about 50% (refs 22-23); enzyme inducers and valproate may
raise CBZ clearance (Table 1).

## Caveats
- Narrative review; non-pregnant PK from UpToDate, not primary sources.
- Literature window 2007-2018 for carbamazepine, so Tomson 1994, Yerby 1990 and later MONEAD (2022) are not in it.
- Carbamazepine evidence rests on small studies (authors' own statement, section 4.5).
- Text and Table 1 disagree on unbound carbamazepine (see `open_questions`).
