# Douma 2010: PCA meperidine, remifentanil and fentanyl in labour

## Study
Prospective, randomised, double-blind comparison of three IV PCA opioids in healthy (ASA I-II) term labouring women declining epidural analgesia; 180 enrolled, 159 completed (remifentanil 52, meperidine 53, fentanyl 54); 21 excluded for delivering within 1 h (p. 211). Exclusions: BMI 40 or more, opioid allergy, pre-eclampsia, etc. Table 2 means (SD): age 33.1-33.6 y; weight 84 (14) meperidine, 79 (12) fentanyl, 81 (13) remifentanil kg; height 169 cm.

PCA settings (PDF p. 2, printed 210), all fixed absolute doses, no background infusion:
- Remifentanil: 40 ug load, 40 ug bolus, lockout 2 min, limit 1200 ug/h.
- Meperidine: 49.5 mg load, 5 mg bolus, lockout 10 min, limit 200 mg total.
- Fentanyl: 50 ug load, 20 ug bolus, lockout 5 min, limit 240 ug/h.
Pain by VAS 0-10 cm hourly; observer sedation 1-5; SaO2 every 30 min; Apgar, cord gas and NACS at birth. Opioid concentrations in maternal and cord blood were said to be sampled (p. 211) but none are reported in this paper.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model or concentrations. Opioid administered (Table 2, mean (SD)): meperidine 133 (50) mg, fentanyl 0.632 (0.263) mg, remifentanil 1.84 (1.09) mg over a treatment duration of 187 (122), 200 (99), 234 (136) min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No MEAC, minimum effective or analgesic concentration, no ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, no hysteresis estimate, and no stated equianalgesic ratio. The effect end point is the labour pain VAS (0-10 cm); no concentration-effect relation is given. The Introduction quotes remifentanil context-sensitive half-time 3-4 min and half-time 10-20 min (p. 209-210), cited to Westmoreland 1993 and Michelsen 1996, not measured here.
- Table 3 (PDF p. 4, printed 212), mean VAS (SD): baseline meperidine 7.41 (1.5), fentanyl 7.40 (1.6), remifentanil 7.8 (1.6). At 1 h: 6.61 (2.3), 5.96 (2.5), 4.56 (2.4). At 2 h: 6.78, 6.47, 5.70. At 3 h: 7.19, 7.26, 7.16. Change from baseline at 1 h: -0.8, -1.4, -3.2 cm (remifentanil vs meperidine P < 0.001, vs fentanyl P < 0.01); not significant at 2 or 3 h. Pain returned to baseline values by 3 h in all groups, and the authors point out this need not mean loss of effect because labour pain rises over time.
- Observer sedation (Table 4, from the text layer) greatest with remifentanil at 1 h: 1.85 (0.8) vs 1.45 and 1.39. Desaturation below 95%: remifentanil 74%, fentanyl 56%, meperidine 33%; oxygen given 12%, 2%, 8% (p. 212). Crossover to epidural: meperidine 34%, fentanyl 15%, remifentanil 13%. Satisfaction 8.1 remifentanil vs 7.0 meperidine (P < 0.05) and 7.3 fentanyl (NS).
- Remifentanil's advantage is as measured against these other PCA regimens, with doses fixed by pump and not equianalgesic; the paper does not claim potency equivalence between the three.

## Covariates
None modelled. Doses fixed, not weight-adjusted; weight similar across groups.

## Caveats
Per-protocol analysis with large dropout over time (in study at 3 h: 17, 23, 27 of 53, 54, 52 for meperidine, fentanyl, remifentanil; Table 1); falling n makes later pain means unreliable; meperidine limited to 200 mg; sedation scale is observer-rated and the baseline-to-1-h comparison used the same women. Table 3's and Table 4's text layers are scrambled, so Table 3 was read from the rendered page.
