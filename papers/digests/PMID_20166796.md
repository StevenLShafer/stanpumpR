# Stevens 2010: plasma methylphenidate in youths on high-dose OROS methylphenidate

## Study
Retrospective chart review (residential treatment and outpatient care, Beaverton/Pendleton, Oregon,
Dec 2006–Aug 2007). 18 youths with ADHD on OROS methylphenidate (Concerta) above the FDA maximum (>72 mg/day);
1 excluded for poor adherence, n = 17 (p50–51). Table 1 (PDF p3, image read): 13 male, 4 female; age
16.2 (SD 2.1) years, range 11–20; weight 62.8 (SD 28.8) kg, range 35.4–159.1; height 66 (SD 5) in; all on
concomitant psychotropics (mean 2.23 ± 0.94; bupropion 59%, SSRIs 47%, lithium 29%; Table 2).
Dose: 169 ± 31 mg/day (range 126–270), 2.97 ± 0.76 mg/kg/day (range 1.13–4.21) (p51; the abstract prints
169 ± 5 mg and 3.0 ± 0.8 mg/kg).
Sampling: one venous sample, 4–5 h after the morning dose, on a dose stable ≥2 weeks; EDTA plasma, stored at
about −20 °C. Assay: HPLC/MS-MS at Medtox (14 samples) or Quest–Nichols (3); LLOQ 1 ng/mL at both; precision
RSD 2.2–10.4% (p50). Analyte: **total** methylphenidate (d + l), plasma.

Dose vs concentration: r² = 0.09, not significant (p51, Fig. 2). No association with concomitant-drug class.
Derived: 28.0/169 = 0.17 ng/mL per mg/day (mean ratio of means, not a per-patient value).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No therapeutic range, toxic threshold or alert level is defined. The abstract reports no patient had a
  plasma level ≥50 ng/mL (glyph confirmed on PDF p1) or clinical signs of stimulant toxicity.
- SBP, DBP and heart rate versus concentration: r² 0.05, 0.04, 0.00, all not significant (p51).
- The discussion (p52) says published therapeutic and toxic MPH ranges are poorly established and vary between
  laboratories; it quotes transdermal d-MPH Cmax values from other sources (Pierce 2008: 46.5 ± 27.3 ng/mL;
  Daytrana label: mean 39, range 0–114 ng/mL) as comparators, not as measured here.
- Oral bioavailability 20–30% quoted from Patrick and Markowitz 1997 (p52), not measured.

## Covariates
None modelled; no effect of dose (mg/kg) or concomitant-drug class on concentration.

## Caveats
Authors: single time point, 4–5 h not a reliable plateau, two laboratories, small n, retrospective,
no efficacy measures, heavy comorbidity and polypharmacy (p52). Noticed: the abstract's dose SD (5 mg) disagrees
with the results (31 mg); Table 1's "combined 11 (75)" is 65% of 17; Fig. 2 appears to show points below the
stated 15.5 ng/mL minimum (open question). Adolescents mostly; one 159 kg patient.
