# Shafer 2026: Improving propofol safety (editorial)

## Study
Opinion editorial, no new data. Discusses whether MCT/LCT propofol emulsions (Lipuro, Propoven) carry a lower risk of propofol infusion syndrome (PRIS) than the all-LCT soybean emulsion (Diprivan; the only US-approved formulation). Comments on Lin et al., an ex vivo study in human neutrophils (PANoptosis).

Facts reported (PDF p1-2; printed 1272-1273):
- FDA adverse event reporting: 668 PRIS cases since 2004; 46 in 2024, 46 in 2025, 9 so far in 2026 (as of Feb 2026).
- EudraVigilance: 249 PRIS cases in Europe; formulation not distinguished; no denominators.
- Japanese retrospective analysis of 763,578 children (mostly MCT/LCT): zero PRIS cases (ref 5).
- During the COVID-era US shortage, more than 100 reports under Emergency Use Authorization for Lipuro; no PRIS reports.
- Lin et al. used 100 ug/mL propofol on leukocytes. The editorial equates this to an infusion of nearly 2,500 ug/kg/min, at least 10-fold above clinical use (derived by the editorial, method not shown).
- No MCT/LCT control in Lin et al.; the author found no animal studies comparing LCT with MCT/LCT and no adequately controlled clinical trial showing formulation changes serious complications.
- Proposes three studies: replicate Lin with LCT vs MCT/LCT; review European pharmacovigilance (Germany); maximum tolerated infusion rate in two animal models.
