# Gepts 1986: alfentanil pharmacokinetics and placental transfer at term

## Study
- Single centre (Flemish Free University of Brussels), 15 women in three groups of 5 (Table 1, PDF p3 / printed p1157; mean ± SD):
  - **Group A**: healthy women at term, elective caesarean section; age 30.2 ± 2.6 y, weight 64.2 ± 6.1 kg, haematocrit 34.2 ± 4.7%.
    Thiopental 3 mg/kg + succinylcholine, then vecuronium 0.04 mg/kg and **alfentanil 30 µg/kg IV bolus**; N2O/O2 before
    delivery, isoflurane 1 MAC added after. Arterial samples at 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 15, 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 240 and 300 min,
    plus umbilical venous and arterial samples at delivery (expected within 10 min of the bolus).
  - **Group B**: healthy women at term in labour; age 24.4 ± 5.8 y, 70.6 ± 6.1 kg; **epidural** alfentanil 30 µg/kg bolus then
    30 µg/kg/h until delivery (on average about 4 h), bupivacaine 0.25% if analgesia became inadequate. Maternal venous and
    cord samples at birth only; no PK fit.
  - **Group C**: healthy NON-pregnant women, thyroidectomy; age 43.0 ± 9.0 y, 57.6 ± 9.7 kg, haematocrit 42.5 ± 1.8%.
    Diazepam/atropine premedication, etomidate induction, halothane 0.6-1% in N2O/O2; **alfentanil 120 µg/kg IV bolus**
    before incision. Arterial samples at 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120 min, then hourly to 6 h.
- Ages differed significantly between groups (ANOVA); the nonpregnant women were older (PDF p3).
- Assay: total plasma alfentanil by radioimmunoassay (Janssen; sensitivity 1 ng/mL). Protein binding by equilibrium dialysis
  of spiked samples with tritiated alfentanil (37 °C, final pH 7.40). α1-acid glycoprotein by radial immunodiffusion.
  Free concentration = total × (100 − % bound).
- Analysis: individual fits, multicompartment mamillary models, extended least squares with power variance; model choice by
  log-likelihood and Schwarz/Leonard criteria; elimination from the central compartment. Non-compartmental (log trapezoid,
  AUC/AUMC) as a check.

## Model and parameters

population/typical-value model):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **No MEAC** or minimum effective/analgesic concentration reported; analgesia in group B was clinical only (bupivacaine
  added when inadequate), with no concentration-effect analysis.
- **No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis**; no EEG, pupil, ventilation or CSF end point.
- **No equianalgesic or potency ratio.**
- Neonatal outcome: only cord pH (A 7.29 ± 0.01, B 7.30 ± 0.07; Table 1). The authors infer similar pharmacological effect in
  mother and neonate from comparable free concentrations; this is an inference, not a measured effect.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (term) was the only comparison: no significant or meaningful difference in any PK parameter between groups A and C.
No weight, age or binding covariate model. Maternal fb/fu correlated weakly with maternal total concentration (r = 0.634);
α1-AGP correlated with fb/fu (r = 0.896).

## Caveats
- n = 5 per arm: low power to detect a pregnancy effect; SDs are large (t½ CV ~65%).
- The comparator is not matched: nonpregnant women were older (43 vs 30 y), lighter, had a fourfold dose (120 vs 30 µg/kg),
  different anaesthetics (halothane vs N2O/isoflurane) and surgery (thyroidectomy vs caesarean). The authors cite this as
  evidence of linearity across doses.
- Sampling: group A to 300 min, group C to 360 min, with a terminal half-life near 100 min.
- Mixed structural models (2- and 3-compartment) were averaged across subjects; Vc and Vdss are not from a single model.
- RIA assay (total drug); binding measured in vitro on spiked samples.
- Group B is epidural: concentrations at delivery only, no disposition parameters.
