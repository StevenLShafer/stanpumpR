# Puhringer 1997: rocuronium duration in postpartum women

## Study
Prospective two-group comparison, 24 ASA I-II women aged 18-45: 12 postpartum (2-4 days after delivery, having laparoscopic tubal cauterization) and 12 nonpregnant (9 tubal cauterization, 3 minor ENT). Table 1 (mean ± SD, no significant differences): age 33 ± 4 vs 29 ± 7 y; weight 64 ± 8 vs 60 ± 8 kg; height 167 ± 5 vs 166 ± 7 cm.
Anaesthesia: fentanyl 3 µg/kg, propofol up to 2.5 mg/kg, then propofol 4-5 mg/kg/h with 2:1 N2O/O2. One bolus of rocuronium 600 µg/kg (per kg); tracheal intubation at 60 s. Twitch of adductor pollicis (ulnar nerve, 0.1 Hz, supramaximal), skin temperature 33.0 ± 0.5 °C. Neostigmine 30 µg/kg + atropine 15 µg/kg given when twitch reached 25% of control. Student's t-test. Pharmacodynamic study only: no plasma concentrations, no PK.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is twitch height (force transducer), not an effect-site model: no ke0, EC50 or gamma. All patients had 100% block. The authors call the prolongation about 25% (derived: 31.1/24.9 = 1.25) and six minutes; compare vecuronium +45% in postpartum women from Khuenl-Brady (ref 4). Atracurium was not prolonged in that earlier work. Comparator throughout is the 12 nonpregnant women in the same study, not a model. (Not an opioid paper; the opioid audit does not apply.)

## Covariates
Postpartum status (2-4 days) only. Weight did not differ between groups. The authors speculate on reduced relative hepatic blood flow and hormone competition for hepatic uptake; no PK measured to confirm.

## Caveats
n = 12 per group; postpartum limited to days 2-4; different surgeries; single dose of 600 µg/kg (about two times ED95 range, not stated by the authors); PK mechanism untested. Later work (Gin 2002, in the repo row) found the effect disappeared with lean-body-mass dosing, which this paper did not examine (both groups were dosed per total kg).
