# Denson 1984: bupivacaine protein binding in term parturients, with and without lactic acidosis

## Study
In vitro ultrafiltration binding study on pooled serum from 15 healthy term parturients (38.1 +/- 4.4 wk gestation, age 25 +/- 7 y) and 13 healthy non-pregnant women (age 31 +/- 6 y) (PDF p.2, 4). Sera were pooled per group after albumin and AAG were checked to be in normal limits. Bupivacaine spiked at 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 20, 40, 60, 100 mcg/mL, 4 replicates each, at pH 7.40 and pH 7.00 (lactic acidosis model). Amicon Micropartition, YMT membrane; bupivacaine by gas chromatography; AAG by radial immunodiffusion; albumin and lactate by autoanalyzer. Two-class binding model fitted with NONLIN. Predicted free concentration (Cu) was compared with measured Cu in nine parturients at delivery (epidural catheter, concurrent kinetic study). Clearance, MRT and Vss for term parturients were derived non-compartmentally from mean blood concentration-time data of Magno et al. (not new data), and a Vss partition was computed with the Oie-Tozer equation.

## Model and parameters
Binding model, Eq. 1: Cb/Cu = sum of niPi.Ki / (1 + Ki.Cu), two classes. Class 1 = high affinity, low capacity (AAG); class 2 = low affinity, high capacity (albumin). Mean +/- SD, Table I (PDF p.2, printed 703):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic end point was measured, and there is no ke0, tPeak, MEAC or potency ratio. The paper's concentration statements concern toxicity, all in total serum concentration (Ct), with free fraction by ultrafiltration of serum (not plasma, not whole blood):
- CNS toxicity is argued to occur at Ct of about 3 to 5 mcg/mL, said to match the non-pregnant animal threshold and about 3x the peak after epidural bupivacaine in parturients (Discussion, PDF p.7, printed 708). This is the authors' inference, not a measured threshold.
- Cardiotoxic range stated as above 20 mcg/mL (abstract, PDF p.1; Discussion p.7). Free concentration there does not differ between parturients and controls.
- Free concentration was higher in parturients mainly below Ct about 10 mcg/mL (abstract) and was "minimal" between 5 and 60 mcg/mL (Results, PDF p.4).
- Predicted vs measured Cu in nine parturients at delivery: r = 0.94, P < 0.0002; 88% of variance explained, F = 51.63 (Fig. 4, PDF p.6; Results p.4).

## Covariates
Pregnancy (term) and pH (7.4 vs 7.0). Lactic acidosis (pH 7.0) cut K1 in controls (1.73e6 to 4.00e5 /mol, P < 0.05) but did not change binding in parturients. AAG sites per molecule (n1) fell from 0.98 in controls to 0.48 in parturients, with equal molar AAG (Discussion, PDF p.5). Albumin sites roughly doubled while albumin was lower, offsetting.

## Caveats
Pooled sera, so no between-subject variability; small n (15 and 13); in vitro ultrafiltration at 37 C is not stated in the text read. Cl, MRT and Vss are taken from another group's mean data (Magno), linear kinetics assumed. The in vivo check is nine parturients at delivery. The Table II/III inconsistency and the L/min vs L/h contradiction are in open_questions. Albumin 2.48 g/dL in the pooled parturient sera is lower than normal term values usually reported; the authors say values were within normal limits.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- pregnancyRegistry.csv:16 notes say Denson 1984 shows the binding change is confined to low total concentrations (<10 mcg/mL). The abstract (PDF p.1) and Results (PDF p.4) support this: free concentration raised at Ct below 10 mcg/mL, no difference above 20. **matches.**
- The same note says the same total concentration carries "about 1.5 times" the unbound drug in a parturient. That 1.5 is Wulf 1991's 8.2/5.4 and is not from Denson; Denson's Table II shows a larger in vitro ratio at 1 mcg/mL (3.7x, derived) falling to 2.0x at 5 mcg/mL. Not a miscitation, but the two sources differ in size at low concentration (Denson: pooled serum in vitro; Wulf: individual term plasma). **no discrepancy; difference noted.**
- Registry row sets no cl/v/f factors (all NA) and applies none; Denson reports no clearance or volume derived from subject data (Cl, Vss are from Magno's mean data), so nothing in it could change the plotted total-concentration curve. **matches.**
- Registry says "no intravenous pregnant-vs-non-pregnant study exists"; Denson's Cl and Vss (0.595 /min, 332 L vs 75 L) come from a Magno cesarean epidural study, not IV. consistent.
