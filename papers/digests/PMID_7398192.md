# Dean 1980: serum protein binding of drugs in and after pregnancy

## Study
- Cross-sectional/longitudinal in vitro binding study. Controls: 31 nonpregnant women, 16-46 y, healthy, 5 on oral contraceptives. Pregnant: 53 women, 16-41 y, uncomplicated. Two groups, sampled (usually 5-6 samples each) from 8-15 wk to 38-40 wk, and from about 21 wk to 5-7 wk post partum. Gestation bins: 8-15, 16-20, 21-25, 26-29, 30-33, 34-37, 38-40 wk; post partum 1-5 d and 5-7 wk.
- Serum drawn at least 2 h after a low-fat meal; frozen up to 2 wk; thawed once.
- Binding by equilibrium dialysis at 37 C, pH 7.4 phosphate buffer, drug **added in vitro** to the serum (not dosed): salicylic acid 300 ug/mL, sulfisoxazole 200 ug/mL, 14C-phenytoin 15 ug/mL, 3H-dexamethasone 1 ug/mL (4 h), 14C-diazepam 0.4 ug/mL. Free fraction = buffer (free) / serum (total) concentration, serum total protein (Lowry) and albumin (electrophoresis). Unpaired t tests and correlation coefficients.
- Dexamethasone and diazepam were studied in 25 other women than salicylic acid, sulfisoxazole and phenytoin (28); controls for dexamethasone n = 29.

## Model and parameters
No PK model; binding only. Results are shown in figures, not tables:
- All five drugs had higher free fraction (less binding) in pregnancy; largest effect on sulfisoxazole, diazepam, salicylic acid; phenytoin and dexamethasone changes statistically significant but small. Changes mostly after 15 wk, persisting 1-5 d post partum, back to normal by 5-7 wk (dexamethasone possibly an exception).
- Serum albumin fell gradually through pregnancy, lowest at least 1-5 d post partum, back near normal by 5-7 wk (Fig. 1, 4).
- **Dexamethasone (Fig. 5, PDF p. 5):** n = 29 controls, 13, 15, 18, 6, 20, 20, 6 pregnant in successive bins, 11 (1-5 d) and 12 (5-7 wk) post partum. Free fraction x 100, read from the bars (axis ticks 17, 19, 21; approximate, not stated in text): control about 18; pregnant bins about 19.5-20.5, elevated from the 8-15 wk bin and flat thereafter (text: elevated appreciably at 8-15 wk and remained at that level); post partum about 19-19.5. Caption: all means during and after pregnancy differ from control (p < 0.01 to < 0.001). Mean +/- SD values are not printed.
- Free fraction vs albumin, pregnant women (Table I, confirmed on page image): salicylic acid r = -0.417 (120 measurements, p < 0.001); sulfisoxazole -0.459 (119, <0.001); phenytoin -0.336 (119, <0.001); diazepam -0.446 (98, <0.001); **dexamethasone -0.138 (98, NS)**. (The text layer scrambles the sign and row alignment; the rendered table shows this order, with n = 28 women for the first three drugs and 25 for diazepam and dexamethasone.)
- Nonpregnant women, dexamethasone vs albumin: r about 0.099 all women, 0.062 off contraceptives (both >0.1, NS; Table III, text layer, sign lost in the text layer, not confirmed on image). Dexamethasone free fraction vs other drugs: weak or NS (Table II/IV).
- Salicylic acid free fraction 0.169 +/- 0.032 (no OCP) vs 0.203 +/- 0.043 (OCP), p < 0.05; other drugs unaffected by OCP.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. The end point is serum free fraction by equilibrium dialysis (in vitro, serum, total vs free by drug added to serum). No PD, MEAC, ke0 or potency data. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Gestational age (bins) and serum albumin (negative correlation with free fraction for all drugs but dexamethasone); oral contraceptive use (salicylic acid only). No equation given. The authors caution the albumin correlation may not be causal, since an endogenous displacer (e.g. fatty acids) may rise in parallel.

## Caveats
- Dexamethasone binding here was measured at 1 ug/mL (about 2.5 uM) added to serum; free fraction is concentration dependent (authors' own caution), and a 3H-label specific-activity assay does not separate dexamethasone from metabolite. Dexamethasone free fraction about 18-20% here is a different method and concentration from Tsuei's 66.6-68.1% bound (about 32-33% free) cited in the library; the two are not directly comparable.
- Women sampled at different gestations belong to two groups; the dexamethasone n per bin is small (6-20).
- Nonpregnant controls include 5 on oral contraceptives (no effect on dexamethasone mentioned).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- pregnancyRegistry.csv:59, "free fraction did not correlate with albumin": **matches** (Table I, r = -0.138, NS, in pregnancy; nonpregnant r about 0.1, NS).
- Same sentence as support for "binding is NOT changed by pregnancy": **discrepancy**. Dean's own Fig. 5 and caption show the dexamethasone free fraction significantly higher than controls at every gestational interval (about 18% to about 20%, a rise of roughly 10-15% relative; derived from approximate bar readings), with the authors calling the change small but significant. The lack of albumin correlation does not mean unchanged binding. Direction of the consequence: for a low-extraction drug such as dexamethasone, a 10-15% higher free fraction would, at constant unbound clearance, raise total clearance by about that much; it is within the uncertainty of the x2 factor, so it does not change the conclusion that total vs unbound is a minor issue, but the wording attributes to Dean what Dean did not show. Tsuei's unchanged 66.6 vs 68.1% bound is a separate, different-method (in vivo samples) result and remains the support.
- R/drugs_dexamethasone.R has no binding or free-fraction term; nothing there depends on Dean.
- Diazepam: Dean also reports raised diazepam free fraction in pregnancy (large effect, figure only). R/drugs_diazepam.R has no pregnancy handling and pregnancyRegistry.csv:27 lists diazepam as "unchanged" (Ridd 1989; free clearance similar to nonpregnant). Not checked further here; mention only as a place where Dean's larger binding change might be relevant.
