# Barvais 2000: prospective predictive accuracy of sufentanil and midazolam infusions in cardiac surgery

## Study
Prospective, 20 adults aged 40-75 (ASA II-III, within 25% of ideal body weight, normal renal and hepatic function),
valve (4) or CABG (16) surgery, Erasme Hospital, Brussels. Table 1 (PDF p. 2): age 60 +/- 7.5 y, weight
75.1 +/- 17.1 kg, height 170 +/- 11.3 cm, 13 M / 7 F. Anaesthesia by pharmacokinetic model-driven infusion
(Toolbox software, two Becton Dickinson syringe pumps): midazolam predicted plasma 100 ng/mL constant from 5 min
before sufentanil; sufentanil predicted plasma titrated 1-10 ng/mL (start 1 ng/mL, +/-1 ng/mL steps; lower limit
1 ng/mL; during CPB 1-3 ng/mL and reduced by 1 ng/mL at start of CPB). Bromazepam premedication; pancuronium.
10 arterial samples per patient (6 before, 2 during, 2 after CPB; times T1-T10 in Table 3). Plasma sufentanil by
radioimmunoassay, midazolam by HPLC (plasma, p. 403). Predictive performance by Varvel's MDPE, MDAPE, P10-P90 and
divergence (p. 404).

sufentanil and Maitre et al for midazolam"; the paper's rate-constant unit label "(min)" is min^-1 (an
inverse-time constant, label misprinted). Volume is per kg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis is estimated or stated. Opioid audit: no MEAC or minimum effective
concentration is defined. The clinical targets used were sufentanil predicted plasma 1 to 10 ng/mL (total plasma,
by the Gepts set, titrated to haemodynamics, not a measured effect); 1 ng/mL was the minimum, chosen from the
authors' experience as adequate when combined with midazolam 100 ng/mL (p. 403); mean target at sternotomy
3.5 ng/mL (range 2-10, p. 407); 1-3 ng/mL during CPB. These are anaesthetic targets with a benzodiazepine, not a
MEAC. No potency or equianalgesic ratio is given. End point of titration: MAP within 15% of baseline, heart rate,
lacrimation/flushing/sweating. Haemodynamic events (Table 5, p. 406): hypotension 15 moderate (7 patients) and 2
severe (2); hypertension 25 moderate (13) and 8 severe (3).

## Covariates
None. Sufentanil: Gepts found no effect of age, weight or lean mass (p. 407). CPB lowers measured concentrations
(haemodilution, likely circuit sequestration of midazolam; free fraction rises).

## Caveats
n = 20, CPB samples few (4 of 10 per patient), so pooled MDAPE depends on the sample mix (authors, p. 407).
Sufentanil bias goes from -6.8% before CPB to -42.1% during and after: the set overpredicts measured
concentrations (measured below predicted) once CPB starts. No unbound concentrations measured. Table 3 gives
only sampling times, no concentrations; per-patient data in Figs 1-4 (images).

## Verification against stanpumpR
The paper reports parameter sets that the library also models, so I compared them (the paper's sets are
transcribed from Gepts and Maitre, not new fits).
- Sufentanil (`R/drugs_sufentanil.R:46-53`, Gepts 1995 Table 3 two-stage log means: V1 14.3, V2 63.1,
  V3 261.6 L; CL 0.92, 1.55, 0.33 L/min, then anchored to the cohort). Barvais Table 2 converted to a 70 kg
  patient (derived): V1 17.2 L (vs 14.3), CL1 0.86 L/min (vs 0.92), V2 77.5 L (vs 63.1), V3 383 L (vs 261.6),
  k10 0.05 (library 0.0643), k31 0.0009 (library 0.00126 /min). Different set: **discrepancy** in parameter
  values between the paper's "Gepts" and the library's Gepts, unexplained here (see open question).
  Plasma sufentanil was overpredicted by Gepts during and after CPB (MDPE -42.1%); the library has no
  CPB adjustment and the paper does not suggest one.
- Midazolam (`R/drugs_midazolam.R:151-157`, Buhrer/Zomorodi, healthy men: V1 3.3 L, CL1 0.535 L/min before
  size anchoring): Barvais used Maitre 1989, V1 141 mL/kg (about 10 L at 70 kg) and CL about 3.4 mL/kg/min
  (about 0.24 L/min at 70 kg, derived), so cardiac-surgery patients clear midazolam more slowly than the library's
  reference man (comparator: the Buhrer men in the library, not another published study). Barvais's prospective
  data (MDPE +5.3% before CPB with a 100 ng/mL target) support the Maitre set, not the library's, for this
  population; this is a [decision] for the library (see open question).
- tPeak/MEAC: the paper provides none, so the library's sufentanil tPeak (5.6 min, line 58), MEAC 0.056 (line 59)
  and midazolam tPeak 3.0 min and band (lines 191-195) cannot be checked here.
Verdict: partly-verified (the paper's numbers are transcribed correctly from images, but the library does not
use these sets, and one of them, the sufentanil one, cannot be reconciled with the library's Gepts values).
