# Van Wart 2013: population meta-analysis of hydrochlorothiazide PK

## Study
- **Design:** a population "meta-analysis" in NONMEM 7.2 (ADVAN13, FOCE with interaction). It co-modelled plasma
  concentrations and urinary amounts excreted (p3, p5).
- **Individual data (p2):** one Otsuka trial (Shoaf 2007), 6 healthy men. They received 100 mg HCTZ (2 x 50 mg
  HydroDIURIL) alone or with tolvaptan 30 mg in a crossover. Plasma was sampled 0.5-24 h and urine was collected to
  24 h. Assay HPLC; LLOQ 0.01 mg/L in plasma and 1 mg/L in urine.
- **Literature data (p3, Table 1 p4):** 26 published reports, digitised with Engauge. They covered normotensive or
  hypertensive subjects (n = 30) and CHF patients (n = 7), with CLcr 19-132 mL/min, at oral doses **12.5 to 75 mg**.
  78.6% of the digitised profiles were aggregate (mean) curves, each entered as one "meta-individual". An aggregate
  healthy profile with no CLcr was assigned 120 mL/min.
- **Dose range, a disagreement inside the paper:** the abstract (p1) says doses from **10 to 500 mg**. The Methods
  (pp2-3) and Table 1 give 100 mg (Shoaf) and 12.5-75 mg (literature), so 12.5-100 mg. No 10 mg or 500 mg dose
  appears in Table 1.
- **Analysis set (p6):** 43 individuals or meta-individuals, 823 plasma concentrations and 137 urinary amounts. Mean
  (SD) age 40.6 (17.9) y, weight 73.9 (6.9) kg, CLcr 101 (31.5) mL/min. It included meta-individuals with mild (6),
  moderate (1) or severe (3) renal impairment, heart failure (7) and hypertension (3).
- **CLcr:** Cockcroft-Gault (p5).

## Model and parameters
Two compartments with first-order elimination, and a central compartment feeding a urine accumulator,
dAe/dt = CL_R x Ac/Vc (p5). Oral input is a Weibull-type depot. Rendered p3 prints it as
dA_po/dt = -(1 - e^-(ka·TSLD)^γ) · A_po, where TSLD is the time since the last dose. The paper's text says that
γ = 1 reduces it to a traditional first-order process.

The paper reports no bioavailability. Volumes and clearances are as estimated from oral data, so they are apparent;
the paper does not say "/F". Table 2 (p7, rendered) gives the final estimates and %SEM:

**Supplement (sup0001.doc = sup0002.doc in content, read 2026-10-27).** The final-model control stream:
- `$SUBROUTINES ADVAN13 TRANS1 TOL=6`; compartments DEPOT (default dose), CENTRAL (default observation), PERIPH, URINE.
- `$PK`: TVCLR = THETA(1) x (CRCL/120)^THETA(7); CLR = TVCLR x exp(ETA(1)); CLNR = THETA(8) with **no ETA**;
  CL = CLR + CLNR; VC = THETA(2) x exp(ETA(2)); CLD = THETA(3), no ETA; VP = THETA(4) x exp(ETA(3));
  KA = THETA(5) x exp(ETA(4) + ETA(6) x OCC1 + ETA(7) x OCC2) (IOV by occasion); GAM = THETA(6) x exp(ETA(5));
  WB = 1 - exp(-((KA x TSLD)^GAM)). No cap or truncation on CRCL or any parameter. No F1 or other bioavailability
  term. S2 = VC/1000 (dose mg, concentration ng/mL), S4 = 1 (urine amount mg).
- `$DES`: dA1/dt = -WB x A1; dA2/dt = WB x A1 + K32 x A3 - (K20 + K23) x A2; dA3/dt = K23 x A2 - K32 x A3;
  dA4/dt = CLR x A2/VC. TSLD (time since last dose) is a data item, so the absorption clock restarts at each dose
  for whatever remains in the depot.
- `$ERROR`: Y1 = F + EPS(5) + F x EPS(1) x IND + F x EPS(2) x (1 - IND) x W x exp(ETA(8)), W = 1/sqrt(NIND) for
  aggregate (meta-individual) profiles; urine Y2 = F + F x EPS(3) x IND + F x EPS(4) x (1 - IND) x W.
- `$THETA` (initial values, with lower bound 0): 18.3, **97.5**, 17.2, 178, 1.34, 2.84, 1.38, 2.44, in the order
  CLr coefficient, Vc, CLd, Vp, ka, gamma, CLr-CLcr power, CLnr. `$OMEGA`: BLOCK(2) 0.116, 0.0767, 0.117 (CLr, Vc);
  0.428 (Vp); 0.321 (ka); 0.306 (gamma); 0.124 and SAME (ka IOV); 0.211 (aggregate-plasma ETA). `$SIGMA` 0.0325,
  0.137, 0.136, 0.578, 3.01. All equal Table 2 except Vc. No standard errors are in the supplement.
- Estimation: METHOD=CONDITIONAL INTER, MAXEVAL=9999, with bound tests switched off.
- Supplemental Figure 1 (SAS/GRAPH): the CCV residual component for aggregate plasma and urine profiles against the
  number of individuals per arm, with the fitted curve falling with that number (plasma about 0.097 at n = 2 to 0.015
  at n = 80; urine about 0.41 to 0.063; read from the figure).
**Renal clearance by renal function (abstract; p7):** 18.3, 10.5, 5.47 and 2.70 L/h at CLcr 120, 80, 50 and 30
mL/min.

**Arithmetic checks (derived):**
- The micro-constants at CL = 18.3 + 2.44 = 20.74 L/h give half-lives of 1.576 and 14.80 h, which match the
  printed 1.6 and 14.8 h.
- The terminal half-life is 20.1 h at CLcr 80, 29.2 h at 50 and 42.0 h at 30. This matches the printed "29 to 42
  h" for moderate and severe impairment.
- The footnote-a equation reproduces the clearance ladder: 10.46, 5.47 and 2.70 L/h.
- **Minor inconsistency inside the paper:** the Discussion (p10) gives CL_R reductions of 42.7%, 71.1% and 85.3%
  relative to CLcr 120. The paper's own equation and its 10.5/5.47/2.70 ladder give 42.9%, 70.1% and 85.2%, so the
  moderate figure differs by 1 point. The cause cannot be determined from the paper.

**IIV consistency:** the text (p7) lists unexplained IIV for CL_R, Vc, Vp, ka and γ as 34.1%, 34.2%, 65.4%, 56.7%
and 55.3% CV. These match Table 2. The Results also list "CL" among the IIV terms (p6), but Table 2 has no ω² for
CL_NR.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD model, no effect site, and no therapeutic concentration.
- The stated aim is to predict the urinary excretion rate as a driving function for future PK-PD work (p9).
- Figure 6 shows simulated urinary excretion after 100 mg at various renal functions, as a graph only.
- Intro (p2), from the literature, not this fit: absolute F about 60-70%, peak 1.5-4 h, mean CL_R 19.8 L/h at
  12.5-75 mg, and terminal half-life 6.4 to 34 h from normal to anephric.

## Covariates
- **CLcr (Cockcroft-Gault) on CL_R:** CL_R = 18.3 x (CLcr/120)^1.38 L/h. It cut CL_R IIV from 62.5% to 23.5% CV in
  forward selection (p6).
- **CHF on Vc:** significant in forward selection, but removed in backward elimination.
- **Not significant:** age, weight and tolvaptan (p10). There is no body-size term. A plateau was not reached at
  high CLcr (Figure 4, p7).

## Caveats
- Most of the data are digitised mean curves (meta-individuals). The IIV mixes between-study and between-subject
  variability, and the authors warn of ecological bias (p10).
- Sparse CHF data meant the authors could not test a CHF effect on ka or F (p10).
- Beal M3 for BLQ data did not converge (p7). Five Otsuka concentrations were BLQ and were excluded (p5).
- **The printed Weibull equation (p3) does not reduce as the paper says.** At γ = 1 it gives a rate of
  1 - e^(-ka·t), not first order with rate constant ka. Its asymptotic rate constant is 1 per time unit (1/h in
  this paper's hours), whatever ka is. This is my reading of the printed form. (supplement, 2026-10-27: the control
  stream codes exactly this form, WB = 1 - exp(-((KA x TSLD)^GAM)) multiplying A1, so the printed equation is what was
  fitted, and the paper's sentence about gamma = 1 does not describe the coded form either.) The control stream would show what
  was actually coded, and it is not in the archived PDF.
- The abstract's 10-500 mg dose range is not supported by the Methods or Table 1 (see Study).
- The %SEM of the additive residual error is given without a unit for σ².
