# Volmanen 2008: IV PCA remifentanil vs epidural in early labour

## Study
Randomised, double-blind, controlled trial, 52 healthy term parturients with singleton cephalic pregnancies, first stage of labour, no opioid for at least 4 h and no regional block (p. 249-250). 27 allocated to IV PCA remifentanil, 25 to epidural; 45 analysed (24 remifentanil, 21 epidural; Fig 1, PDF p. 4). Median (IQR) in Table 1: age 27 (24-32) and 28 (27-31) y; weight 80 (73-86) and 79 (75-87) kg; height 165 and 168 cm; primiparous/multiparous 17/7 and 16/5; cervical dilatation before study 4 cm in both, after 6 (5-9) and 7 (5-9) cm.

- Remifentanil arm: PCA bolus of 25 ug/mL solution infused over 1 min, lockout 1 min, into a line carrying saline at about 100 mL/h. Start 0.1 ug/kg, escalated every second contraction through 0.1-0.2-0.33-0.5-0.7-0.9 ug/kg until the woman declined more relief or 0.9 ug/kg was reached. 60-min study period (PDF p. 2, printed 250). Epidural saline for blinding.
- Epidural arm: 20 mL levobupivacaine 0.625 mg/mL with fentanyl 2 ug/mL, as two 10 mL boluses 5 min apart; IV saline PCA for blinding.
- End points: contraction pain NRS 0-10, pain relief 0-4, sedation and nausea 0-3, SaO2, FHR, neonatal outcome. No drug concentrations were measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No MEAC, minimum effective or analgesic concentration, no ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, no hysteresis analysis and no equianalgesic ratio are reported. The only effect end point is the pain score during contractions (NRS 0-10); the paper gives doses, not plasma or effect-site concentrations.
- Table 2 (PDF p. 6, printed 254), remifentanil vs epidural, medians over the last score of each 10 min: pain 7.3 (5.6-8.2) vs 5.2 (2.2-6.7), P = 0.004; pain relief 2.5 vs 2.8, P = 0.11; sedation 2.3 (1.3-2.6) vs 0 (0-0.8), P < 0.001; oxygen supplement 13/24 vs 1/21 (text, P < 0.001). Abstract gives pain P = 0.009 and relief P = 0.17; the abstract and Table 2 disagree (the abstract's P values do not match Table 2's 0.004 and 0.11). Not reconciled here.
- Baseline contraction pain 8 (8-9); parturients' "acceptable" pain 4 (3-5); 6/23 (26%) remifentanil vs 11/21 (52%) epidural reached it (P = 0.08).
- Effective dose and consumption were higher than the authors' earlier dose-finding study (0.5 vs 0.4 ug/kg; 0.14 vs 0.07 ug/kg/min), which they attribute to more advanced labour (p. 254).

## Covariates
Dose given per kg actual weight. Pain scores and the effective dose are not modelled against any covariate; the authors note the dose needed rises as labour progresses and compare primiparous with multiparous women (no difference in consumption, text p. 252).

## Caveats
Small n (24 and 21 analysed); 60-min window; ultra-dilute epidural chosen for blinding, which probably understated epidural efficacy; 7 of 24 remifentanil women stopped escalating for sedation or dizziness; the median bolus at which oxygen was needed was not different from the effective dose, so no safe dose was identified. Table 2's text layer is scrambled (columns shifted); the values above were read from the rendered page.
