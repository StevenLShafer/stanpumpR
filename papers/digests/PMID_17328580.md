# Tomson 2007: newer antiepileptic drugs in pregnancy and the puerperium (review)

PDF page n is printed page 208 + n.

## Study
A narrative review. The authors searched PubMed from 1 May to 1 June 2006 (pregnancy terms crossed with kinetics terms and drug names), and also used reference lists and hand-searched conference abstracts. Abstracts were included only where no full paper existed. Human studies only (p2/210). No new data, and no pooled or meta-analytic estimates: every number is quoted from a cited primary study.

Quantitative pregnancy findings quoted from primary studies (secondary data):
- **Lamotrigine** (p4/212): Tran 2002 (12 pregnancies, 2 on monotherapy) found 3rd-trimester apparent CL (L/kg/day) almost twice prepregnancy. Pennell 2004 (14, monotherapy; CL = dose/concentration) reported a "mean increase from baseline of 361%" in the 3rd trimester (333% weight-adjusted). The wording leaves open whether this means +361% or 3.61x. de Haan 2004 (12): C/D ratio fell to 40% of baseline. Petrenaite 2005 (11): C/D fell 66% in the 3rd trimester. Ohman 2000: dose-normalised concentrations rose 170% from delivery to 2-3 weeks postpartum. Tomson 2006 (p5/213): dose/concentration ratio +295% by mid-gestation on monotherapy against +60% with valproate. Free apparent CL was still 200% higher in the 3rd trimester (Pennell abstract).
- **Levetiracetam** (p5/213; Pennell 2005 abstract, n = 5): apparent CL mean +/- SD 2.15 +/- 0.72 (T1), 2.24 +/- 0.45 (T2), 1.99 +/- 0.75 (T3) against 1.29 +/- 0.57 at baseline, printed in "mg/kg/ug/mL" (presumably (mg/kg/day)/(ug/mL)). Ratios (derived): 1.67, 1.74, 1.54. The review estimates the fall in concentration at about 50%.
- **Oxcarbazepine** (p6/214; Mazzucchelli 2006, n = 5): dose-normalised S-(+)-MHD rose 1.7-, 2.8-, 2.9- and 25-fold from the 3rd trimester to after delivery in 4 of 5 women. There was no prepregnancy baseline.
- **Gabapentin**, Table I (p3/211; Ohman 2005, 3 women on polytherapy): at delivery 30, 40, 42 umol/L against 10, 22, 27 umol/L at 2-3 weeks postpartum (doses 1200, 1800, 1800 mg/day). The authors attribute the difference to sampling time (delivery samples were not troughs).
- **Topiramate**, Table II (p6/214; Ohman 2002, 3 women): at delivery 7.6, 7.9, 5.9 umol/L against 6.3, 20, 4.2 postpartum (doses 150, 200, 200 mg/day; with carbamazepine, valproate, carbamazepine). Same sampling caveat. The table title says "3 weeks", the column "2-3 weeks". Enzyme inducers lower topiramate concentrations by about 50%.
- **Zonisamide** (p6-7; Kawada 2002, one woman on carbamazepine): total 17.5 ug/mL at delivery, 18.9 on day 2, 23.3-25.5 on day 9. Free concentration rose only slightly.
- **Tiagabine, pregabalin, felbamate, vigabatrin** (section 3.7, p7/215): no published pregnancy PK data. For tiagabine (96% bound) the review expects displacement to lower total but not free concentrations, and notes that its oxidative metabolism could in theory be induced in pregnancy.
- Older AEDs, 3rd-trimester falls in concentration (p3/211): phenytoin 55-61% total (18-31% free), carbamazepine 0-42% (0-28% free), phenobarbital 50-55%, primidone 55% (derived phenobarbital 70%), valproic acid 50% (minor change in free). GFR rises up to 100% in pregnancy and falls in the last 3 weeks (p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
The end points are clinical: seizure control and toxicity after delivery. Lamotrigine needed a dose increase in 11 of 12 pregnancies (Tran). Seizures worsened in 9 of 12 (Dutch study) and in 5 of 11 (Danish). EURAP (1956 pregnancies) found more dose increases on lamotrigine and oxcarbazepine monotherapy (p7/215). The review gives no therapeutic ranges. It recommends TDM against each woman's own prepregnancy reference concentration (p8/216), monthly for lamotrigine and probably oxcarbazepine, every 3-4 days for 2 weeks after delivery when the dose was changed, and perhaps once a trimester for the other newer AEDs. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Co-medication: enzyme inducers blunt lamotrigine's fall and halve topiramate concentrations, and valproate blunts the induction of lamotrigine. Free and total concentrations are discussed by binding.

## Caveats
- Secondary source. Several key data are abstracts only (levetiracetam, free lamotrigine). Every comparison is within-woman (pregnancy against prepregnancy or postpartum), not against any population model.
- Search ended June 2006. Since then, studies have been published for topiramate, zonisamide, levetiracetam and lacosamide (see the library's registry), so the "unknown" entries are dated, except for tiagabine and pregabalin.
- Pennell 2004's "361%" could mean +361% or 361% of baseline (p4). The library does not use it.
