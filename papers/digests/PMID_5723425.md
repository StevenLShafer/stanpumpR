# Krüger-Thiemer 1968: Continuous IV infusion and multicompartment accumulation

## Study
Theoretical paper (Boston University; IBM System/360 used for calculations). No subjects, no data apart from a cited experiment (Bünger et al. 1961). Works in drug amounts in compartments, not concentrations.

## Model and parameters
- **Model I** (one compartment, Widmark 1919; Widmark and Tandberg 1924): constant infusion D' with first-order elimination k12; plateau m1(inf) = D'/k12 (eq I.4); time to 97% of plateau 3.506/k12 (eq I.6). A loading dose D* = D'/k12 followed by the constant rate holds m1 constant from time zero (eq I.9; attributed to van Gemert and Duyff 1950 and the author's 1960 papers).
- **Model II** (central + one peripheral compartment, Teorell 1937): with any initial dose and a constant rate, m1 cannot be held constant, because the two hybrid exponents alpha > beta cannot both be cancelled (eq II.4, II.4a). Fig. 1 normalized curves for D1 = 0, 1, 2, 4 and effective volume ratio 1 or 10.
- **Model III** (exact plateau): after the initial dose D*, a time-varying infusion D'(t) = D* (k12 + k13 exp(-k31 t)) (eq III.6) keeps m1 exactly constant; starts at D*(k12 + k13) (III.6a) and decays exponentially to D* k12 (III.6b). Physiological form in clearance, permeability coefficient and partition coefficient (III.6c); normalized form (III.6d), Fig. 2.
- **Model IV** (central + mu outer compartments): D'(t) = D* (k12 + sum_v k1v exp(-kv1 t)) (eq IV.4), a sum of decaying exponentials.
- Defines "multicompartment accumulation" (Sect. 4): approach to plateau from below after an insufficient initial dose plus constant rate; cites thiobarbiturates.
- Footnote: Teorell 1937 eq. 24 has a misprint (k2 should read k4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect site, no ke0, no concentrations, no potency. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Pure theory; the plateau is in the central compartment's amount (plasma), with elimination only from the central compartment. Notation uses compartment 2 for urine and 3+ for peripheral compartments. No clinical test of the scheme is reported (the author calls experiments "conceivable").
