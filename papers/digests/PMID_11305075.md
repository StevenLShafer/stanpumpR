## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Potency / equianalgesia (opioid audit)**: abstract: hydromorphone 5 times as potent as morphine orally and
  8.5 times IV (mg basis). Table 3 (p.86, compiled from 8 refs; "different ratios from different
  investigators"): morphine SC/IV to hydromorphone SC/IV 5-8.5:1; morphine PO to hydromorphone PO 5-7.5:1;
  oxycodone PO 4:1; codeine PO 27:1; meperidine PO 40:1; hydromorphone PO to hydromorphone IV/SC 5:1. Text
  (p.89): PO morphine to PO hydromorphone 5:1 to 7.5:1; IM morphine to IM hydromorphone 6.7:1, 7.9:1, 8.6:1;
  the authors' unit uses 5:1 for both morphine PO to hydromorphone PO and hydromorphone PO to parenteral. IM
  hydromorphone about 5 times diamorphine; IM fentanyl about 15 times hydromorphone (p.90).
- **MEAC / effective concentration**: patients with chronic severe bone or soft-tissue cancer pain appear to
  require serum hydromorphone above about 4 ng/mL for pain control (p.88, citing Reidenberg 1988, Clin
  Pharmacol Ther; assay and total vs free not stated in the review; the ">" is inferred from a lost symbol).
  Authors also note the concentration-analgesia relationship is hard to assess (tolerance, large Vd).
- **Delay / hysteresis**: after IV bolus, onset of analgesia within 5 min; maximum analgesic effect 8-20 min
  after the maximum plasma concentration (lag time), attributed to slow blood-brain barrier penetration
  (p.86, citing Coda 1997 [20] and ref 80). End point: analgesia. No ke0 or t½ke0 given.
- Epidural: CSF at cervical level peaks 1 h after lumbar epidural; epidural dose about 50% of IV.

## Covariates
Qualitative only: renal failure (H3G accumulation), hepatic blood flow, interacting drugs.

## Caveats
- Narrative review; values second-hand, some mis-citations possible (Table 2 alignment unclear).
- Equianalgesic ratios are mostly single-dose chart values; the authors stress they are uncertain guidelines.
- The ">4 ng/mL" is a cancer-pain, chronic-dosing, likely opioid-tolerant population figure.
