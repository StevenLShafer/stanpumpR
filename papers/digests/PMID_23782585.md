# He 2013: vildagliptin in renal impairment

## Study
Multicentre, open-label, parallel-group, multiple-dose study (Novartis; all authors Novartis employees).
96 subjects enrolled: 50 with renal impairment (RI) and 46 healthy controls, each control matched to an
RI patient for age (±5 y), sex and BMI (±10%). RI graded by Cockcroft-Gault creatinine clearance (CrCL;
Jaffe creatinine, IDMS-traceable): mild 50 to ≤80, moderate 30 to <50, severe <30 mL/min; controls >80
mL/min (p. 694). Ages 18-85, BMI ≤42 kg/m2, non-smokers; no dialysis.

Two severe-RI patients withdrew on days 3 and 4 (adverse events judged unrelated) and were excluded from
PK, leaving 16 (p. 696).

Dose: vildagliptin 50 mg orally once daily for 14 days, fasted ≥10 h on PK days, breakfast 30 min after
dose. Plasma sampled predose to 24 h on day 1 and to 120 h on day 14; urine 0-24 h on days 1 and 14.
LC-MS/MS (ESI) for vildagliptin and metabolites LAY151 (major, inactive) and BQS867; plasma LLOQ 2.0
ng/mL each analyte (p. 695). Noncompartmental analysis, WinNonlin 5.2. Group comparisons: linear
mixed-effects model on log AUCτ and Cmax, group fixed, matched pair random; geometric mean ratios with 90% CI.

Background given in the introduction (from other papers, not measured here): median tmax 1.5 h, absolute
oral bioavailability 85%, plasma protein binding 9.3%, hydrolysis to LAY151 accounts for 69% of
elimination, 85% of a dose excreted in urine, 23% as unchanged drug; exposure about 30% higher in older
subjects.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. No PD model, no ke0. The discussion (p. 700) cites an IC50 for DPP-4 under 2 ng/mL (from
ref. 16, not measured here) and gives unpublished mean DPP-4 activity: 9.87 mU/mL/min in healthy subjects,
10.34, 10.67 and 10.53 in mild, moderate and severe RI. The authors recommend 50 mg once daily (half the
100 mg maximum daily dose) in moderate and severe RI, and no adjustment in mild RI.

## Covariates
Renal function (CrCL category) only, as group comparisons. A weak negative correlation of vildagliptin
AUCτ with CrCL (Figure 2A, no equation given); much stronger for LAY151 and BQS867.

## Caveats
- Noncompartmental, arithmetic means in Table 2 but geometric mean ratios from a mixed model in Table 3, so
  Table 2 means do not divide exactly to Table 3 (e.g. 1,323/954 = 1.39 against 1.40; derived).
- Healthy subjects were older (means 60-62 y) than a typical young-adult PK panel; the introduction says
  exposure is about 30% higher in the elderly.
- 20-30% of each RI group had type 2 diabetes; controls had none. Sponsor-run study.
- The authors attribute the vildagliptin rise partly to loss of renal hydrolysis, not filtration alone.
