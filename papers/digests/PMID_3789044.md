# Rayburn 1986: oral acetaminophen at 36 weeks vs 6 weeks post partum

## Study
- Six healthy Caucasian women, each studied twice: at 36 weeks' gestation, and again 6 weeks post partum (paired). Comparisons used a two-tailed paired t test.
- Age: mean 29 y (27-33). Height: mean 62 in (60-65).
- Weight: 162 lb at delivery (147-170) and 137 lb post partum (117-148), i.e. about 73.5 and 62.1 kg (derived). No smokers. No medications other than prenatal vitamins.
- Dose: 1000 mg orally (two 500 mg Tylenol Extra-Strength caplets) after an overnight fast, with 8 oz of water.
- Sampling: blood at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 12 h; urine to 24 h.
- Assay: HPLC (Adriaenssens and Prescott; Galinsky; Howie) for parent drug and the sulfate, glucuronide, cysteine and mercapturate conjugates, in duplicate. Plasma, total drug.
- Analysis: noncompartmental. t½ from the terminal log-linear slope; AUC by trapezoids extrapolated to infinity; CL/F = dose/AUC; CLR = amount excreted/AUC.

- The authors attribute the lower AUC to a larger distribution volume. Volume is not reported (abstract).
- Metabolites were not measurable in serum in either state (PDF p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No analgesic end point, no ke0, no therapeutic concentration. Not an opioid; acetaminophen is not on the MEAC panel.

## Covariates
Pregnancy only (paired). No weight normalisation.

## Caveats
- n = 6, oral only, so F cannot be separated from CL. The lower AUC could be higher clearance or lower bioavailability. The "absorption unchanged" conclusion rests only on Tmax and Cmax.
- The comparator is 6 weeks post partum, not cross-sectional nonpregnant women.
- The paper contradicts itself:
  - The abstract and conclusions say disposition was unaffected, yet Table I gives a significant 42% higher CL/F and 27% lower AUC (derived).
  - The Results text (PDF p3) calls renal clearance 43% greater and significant; Table I and the Comment give CLR NS (0.61 vs 0.35, a ratio of 1.74, derived). The 43% matches the CL/F ratio (1.42), so the text probably meant oral clearance.
  - The Comment says t½ was slightly less in pregnancy; Table I shows it longer (3.7 vs 3.1 h).
- Sampling stopped at 12 h, so the t½ values (3.1-3.7 h) are long for acetaminophen.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/drugs_acetaminophen.R:117 (absorption unchanged).**
  - Matches the paper's own conclusion: Tmax 0.8 h in both states, Cmax NS.
  - The paper cannot separate F from CL. Holding F at 0.859 and attributing the AUC fall to clearance is a modelling choice the paper neither supports nor refutes.
  - **Matches** (as the authors' claim).
- **R/drugs_acetaminophen.R:123 and docs/pregnancy.md:140.** "Unchanged ... rests on Rayburn's six women studied orally": six women, oral. **Matches.**
  - Rayburn's own Table I actually supports a clearance rise of 1.42x (CL/F, p<0.05). That is close to the library's 1.5, though measured against 6-week postpartum women rather than nonpregnant women.
  - The repo itself warns (pregnancyRegistry.csv:40) that early-postpartum comparators overstate the change. At 6 weeks, Rayburn's comparator is later than Kulo's.
- **inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv:40.** Design described as n = 6, oral, 36 wk vs 6 wk postpartum: correct. But "found no significant difference" is **wrong for AUC and CL/F** (both p<0.05, Table I). It is right only for t½, Cmax, Tmax, CLR and urinary fractions. **Discrepancy** in the descriptive text; the applied factors (CL 1.5, V 1.14) do not depend on it.
- **Volume.** Rayburn's means imply V/F about 1.70x in pregnancy (derived, crude), against the library's V factor of 1.14 (taken from Miners). Direction agrees; magnitude is larger and unreliable given n = 6 and the 12-h sampling. Not used by the library.
- **Uncited comparison with the base model (Morse 2022, R/drugs_acetaminophen.R:14-48).**
  - Rayburn's postpartum CL/F is 15.4 L/h at about 62 kg.
  - Morse's CL/F at 70 kg is 24.0/0.859 = 27.9 L/h (derived); scaled to a 62 kg woman, roughly 24 L/h (derived, approximate NFM).
  - Rayburn's postpartum Cmax is 23.7 mg/L at 0.8 h; the library's reference man peaks at 11.0 mg/L at 34 min.
  - So Rayburn's women show about half the clearance and twice the peak of the library model. This 1986 HPLC study disagrees with Morse; the library does not use Rayburn for baseline PK.
- Units: µg/mL = mg/L, as in the library. CL is in L/h, absolute (not per kg). No conversion issues.
