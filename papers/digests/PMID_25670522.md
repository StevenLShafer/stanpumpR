# Elkomy 2015: ondansetron in pregnant and nonpregnant women, cord blood and neonates

## Study
Prospective, open-label, randomised dose assignment (Stanford). 20 healthy nonpregnant women at elective surgery
and 40 pregnant women at elective caesarean, plus their neonates (Table 1, PDF p2 / printed p168):
- Dose 4 mg / 8 mg IV: pregnant 10/30, nonpregnant 10/10 (so the dose groups are unbalanced by pregnancy).
- Age (y): pregnant 33.0 (SD 5.2, 23-43); nonpregnant 37.7 (5.7, 26-45); all 34.6 (5.8, 23-45).
- Weight (kg): pregnant 80.7 (12.0, 60-115); nonpregnant 71.1 (20.5, 51-126); all 77.5 (15.8).
- Height (m): about 1.64 in both groups.
- Gestation 39.1 (0.746) weeks, range 36.4-40.4; delay from dose to delivery 80.8 (55.5) min, range 8-333.
- Neonates: 38 in Table 1 (39 in Results text), birth weight 3.49 (0.49) kg; 18 male, 20 female.
- Sampling: maternal venous plasma before and 7, 15, 40 min and 8 h after the dose; umbilical cord at delivery;
  neonatal dried blood spots (heel prick) at 30 and 90 min and 2, 6 and 24 h after birth (Methods, p4-5 / 171-172).
  371 concentrations: 78 nonpregnant, 191 pregnant, 37 cord, 65 neonatal.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS, ondansetron-D3 internal standard; plasma range 0.338-741 ng/mL, DBS 0.338-82.3 ng/mL.
- NONMEM VII, FOCE with interaction, log-transformed data, additive residual error, exponential IIV.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment linear model for the women (CL, Q, V central, Vss); cord blood = KP x maternal central concentration
(Eq. 1, instantaneous equilibrium); after delivery the neonate is a one-compartment model starting from the cord
concentration at delivery and declining with first-order KN0 (Eq. 2). No neonatal dose, so neonatal CL and V are not
identifiable. Table 2 (PDF p4 / printed p170), confirmed on the rendered page; pooled pregnant + nonpregnant women:

Results text (p2): 8 mg lowered CL by 31% and Vss by 26% (Table 2's 0.75 implies 25%). Cord concentrations correlated
with predicted maternal concentrations at delivery (r2 0.90). For simulation (Methods p6): oral F set to 0.62 (weighted
mean of three earlier studies), absorption rate 1.05 (printed "L/h"; presumably 1/h), neonatal V for external dosing
3.5 L/kg taken from Mondick 2010. Proposed NAS regimen: 4 mg IV to the mother 15 min before cord clamping, or 0.07 mg/kg
oral (0.04 mg/kg IV) to the neonate, matching adult AUC0-24h after 4 mg oral twice daily (Figure 5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured. No ke0, effect-site, EC50 or therapeutic concentration. The exposure target for NAS is an AUC0-24h
equal to adult 4 mg oral twice daily (a dosing assumption, not a measured effect). Not an opioid paper; no MEAC,
ke0/tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Tested (forward addition, p<0.05): pregnancy (categorical, and continuous in gestational age, Eq. 3), dose, maternal
weight and age, birth weight, gestational age, neonatal sex. Only dose was retained (on CL and Vss, as fractional
changes at 8 mg). Pregnancy: P > 0.05, i.e. pregnant women did not differ from the nonpregnant women of this study.

## Caveats
- Pregnancy is confounded with dose (30 of 40 pregnant women had 8 mg; 10 of 20 nonpregnant), and pregnant women
  delivered during sampling; only 5 maternal samples each, the last at 8 h.
- The authors note that the narrow weight and age range may explain why weight and age were not significant, and that
  study design (standard-of-care sampling) limited the test of pregnancy.
- Dose-dependent Vss is unexplained (authors suggest carrier-mediated transport).
- Neonatal DBS (capillary whole blood) and maternal plasma are combined without a blood/plasma ratio.
- Neonatal parameters describe only the first 24 h of life after transplacental loading.
- Table 1 lists 38 neonates; the Results text says 39.
