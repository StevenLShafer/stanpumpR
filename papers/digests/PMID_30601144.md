# Smid 2019: stimulant use in pregnancy (narrative review)

## Study
Narrative clinical review, not a systematic one, with no new data. It covers pharmacology, epidemiology and
maternal, perinatal and childhood outcomes for cocaine, methamphetamine, prescription stimulant misuse
(amphetamine salts such as Adderall, lisdexamfetamine, methylphenidate), MDMA, ephedra and natural or synthetic
cathinones, and then treatment of stimulant use disorder in pregnancy. Table 1 (PDF p. 10, printed 177) gives
outcomes by substance; Table 2 (PDF p. 11) lists research gaps.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic data: no concentrations, clearances, volumes or half-lives for any stimulant, in or out of
pregnancy. The only quantitative pharmacology for prescription amphetamine is about lactation (PDF p. 7, printed
174). Amphetamines pass into breast milk. A 20 mg daily dose is suggested as enough to detect metabolites in
neonatal urine, from one woman taking amphetamine 20 mg/day for narcolepsy (ref. 132, Steiner 1984). No adverse
effects were seen in 103 breastfed infants (ref. 133).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Epidemiology only: in 2015, 1.0% of reproductive-age women misused prescription stimulants in the past
month (PDF p. 1). First-trimester prescription stimulant exposure was not linked to congenital anomalies in cohort
studies. Stimulant use carried a small increase in preeclampsia and preterm birth, and placental abruption rose
with 8-18-week exposure (ref. 129, Cohen 2017) (PDF p. 7). No concentration-effect relation, no target range.

## Covariates
None (no PK).

## Caveats
- Narrative review; the outcome associations it summarises are mostly confounded observational data, as the
  authors say.
- The review does not discuss amphetamine elimination (CYP2D6, urinary pH) or pregnancy-related PK changes.
