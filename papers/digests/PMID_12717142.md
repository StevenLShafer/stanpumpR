# Sveticic 2003: morphine-ketamine PCA by direct-search optimisation

## Study
- Single-centre (Bern), 102 patients consented, 84 analysed, having lumbar spine or hip surgery; 18 excluded (protocol violations, indomethacin, other pain, non-compliance, randomisation error) (PDF p5). Exclusions: age under 16, psychotropic drugs, opioids daily for more than 1 week.
- Anaesthesia: midazolam 7.5 mg oral, fentanyl 0.15-0.2 mg, thiopental 5-7 mg/kg, vecuronium, N2O/isoflurane. PCA started at extubation; study period 48 h starting 1 h after extubation; rescue IV morphine 2.5 mg boluses in the first postoperative hour.
- PCA: 1 mL bolus on demand, maximum 6 boluses/h; bolus raised by 0.2 mL after 6 unmet requests (to a maximum 2 mL), lowered by 0.2 mL for side effects. Variables optimised: morphine mg/mL and ketamine mg/mL in the PCA solution (limits 0-2 each) and lockout 5-12 min (Table 3, PDF p4).
- Outcome: verbal pain score 0-4 at rest and on mobilisation, recorded every 2 h for 6 h, then every 4 h. "Adequate analgesia" = 0 at rest and 2 or less on mobilisation. Sedation, respiratory rate, nausea, pruritus, dreams recorded (Table 2).
- Design: modified Berenbaum direct-search; 8 combinations per complex, 6 patients per combination; the worst is dropped and replaced by a new combination N = G + alpha(G - B), alpha = 1.3 (Appendix, PDF p9-10). Stopped when three consecutive steps gave no fall in mean pain. 12 combinations tested, E and K retested in 6 more patients each (14 groups x 6 = 84).
- Not a PK study: no concentrations measured, no PK model.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: verbal pain score (0 none to 4 very strong), mean of rest and mobilisation observations. No plasma concentrations, MEAC, ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak or hysteresis were measured or reported.
- **Opioid audit (morphine):** MEAC / analgesic concentration: none. ke0 / tPeak: none. Potency or equianalgesic ratio: none; the only ratio is the PCA mixture, morphine:ketamine = 1:1 by mass concentration in the solution (derived from Table 5). Morphine consumption in the best groups 2.1-3.1 mg/h median (Table 5); this is a PCA dose, not a concentration.
- The authors infer that low ketamine concentrations add analgesia to morphine only indirectly, from the optimisation moving toward more ketamine (PDF p6). The study has no morphine-alone arm.
- Side effects (Table 6, rendered PDF p7): respiratory depression leading to discontinuation in none; no combination had the same side effect stop it in more than 2 patients; pain stopped H in 3 patients.

## Covariates
No covariate analysis. Stratified randomisation by sex and operation type (hip or spine).

## Caveats
- Six patients per combination; the authors state the study cannot show superiority over morphine alone or prove safety. The optimum could be local.
- Pain scores were re-scaled for the algorithm (Table 7, Appendix).
- The Table 6 text layer scrambles the columns; the rendered page was used.
