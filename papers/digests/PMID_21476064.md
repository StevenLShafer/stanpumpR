# Yoo 2011: glimepiride population PK and CYP2C9*3 in healthy Korean men

## Study
- Pooled reference-formulation arms of seven bioequivalence studies (same protocol; single-dose, randomised, two-way
  crossover, 7-day washout) (PDF pp. 2-3 / printed 890-891).
- 177 healthy Korean men; age 23.1 ± 2.2 y (19-30); weight 67.6 ± 9.5 kg (44-106.3; median 66.3); height 173.2 ±
  5.8 cm; BSA 1.804 ± 0.136 m²; CrCL 108.3 ± 17.5 mL/min (median 105.3, range 70.08-196.8) (Table 1, PDF p. 2 /
  printed 890, page image).
- CYP2C9*1/*1 163 (92.09%), *1/*3 14 (7.91%); no *3/*3; *2 not genotyped (PCR-RFLP for *3 only).
- Dose: glimepiride 2 mg oral tablet (Amaryl, Handok-Aventis) fasting with 240 mL water.
- Sampling: pre-dose and 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12 h; **serum**. HPLC-UV (350 nm) after DNFB
  derivatisation; range 5-500 ng/mL, LLOQ 5 ng/mL; CVs <15%; recovery 64.54 ± 7.85% (p. 3 / 891).
- NONMEM VI, FOCE with interaction, ADVAN6; PsN 3.2.4 VPC (2,000 simulated sets); Xpose GAM covariate screen.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, first-order elimination from the central compartment, Weibull-type absorption (equations p. 5 / 893).
All parameters are apparent (divided by unknown F). Final model, Table 2 (PDF p. 6 / printed 894, page image):

Final equation (p. 5 / 893): CL/F = (1.84 − 0.694·G[*1/*3]) · (CrCL/105.3)^0.371, L/h.
Base model (no covariates): CL/F 1.78, Vc/F 2.37, Q/F 0.437, Vp/F 4.36, Ka 0.438, γ 1.48; OFV 14,559.77; final OFV
14,517.14. Eta shrinkage all <0.21 (p. 6 / 894).

Derived values stated by the authors (p. 8 / 896, page image): CL/F *1/*3 1.15 L/h, a 1.60-fold difference;
"half-life" 0.693/K20 = 0.91 h (*1/*1) and 1.41 h (*1/*3), which is the central-compartment elimination rate only,
not a terminal half-life. (derived: K20 = 1.84/2.34 = 0.786 h⁻¹, 0.693/0.786 = 0.88 h for a typical *1/*1 subject;
the paper's 0.91 h appears to be a summary of individual values, Fig. 4.)

Observed Cmax 118.56-489.49 ng/mL; mean Cmax 264.36 (*1/*1) and 316.05 ng/mL (*1/*3) (p. 9 / 897, text layer only).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured: no glucose, insulin, EC50 or ke0. Not an opioid paper. The authors only remark that lower CL/F could
raise hypoglycaemia risk (p. 9 / 897).

## Covariates
CL/F: CYP2C9*1/*3 (ΔOFV 37.52, P<0.001) and CrCL (power 0.371 centred on 105.3 mL/min; ΔOFV 5.11, P=0.024). Age,
weight, BSA, ALP/AST/ALT and study did not enter. No weight scaling (range 44-106 kg).

## Caveats
- Healthy young Korean men only; single 2 mg dose; sampling stops at 12 h, so terminal phase is poorly defined (Vp/F
  RSE 61%).
- Vc/F 2.34 L and the "half-life" of about 0.9 h are much smaller/shorter than the 8.8 L volume the authors quote from
  earlier work (p. 1 / 889) and than the 1.9 h half-life in Niemi 2002 they cite (p. 8 / 896); they attribute the gap to
  model structure and population.
- Comparatives: "CL/F higher (1.60-fold)" is *1/*1 against *1/*3 within this study; the 4.31 L/h (Finnish, Niemi 2002)
  and 3.65 L/h (Japanese diabetics, Suzuki 2006) values are other studies' non-compartmental estimates (p. 8 / 896).
- Weibull term written as a cumulative fraction used as a rate (see open question).
- Only CYP2C9*3 genotyped; *2 assumed absent in East Asians.
