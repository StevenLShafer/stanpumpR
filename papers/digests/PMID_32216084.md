# Chen 2020: sodium oxybate population and noncompartmental PK, children and adults

## Study
- Paediatric PK sub-study (n = 29: 11 children 7-11 y, 18 adolescents 12-17 y) of a randomised-withdrawal trial in
  narcolepsy with cataplexy (NCT02221869), on a stable dose of 4-9 g/night (69.8-225.8 mg/kg/night), given as two
  equal doses 4 h apart; night 1 half the night-2 dose (pp. 2-3). Light dinner > 2 h before dose 1; first nightly dose
  treated as fed, second as fasted in the model (p. 3). Samples 0, 0.75, 1.5, 2.5, 4, 4.75, 8 h.
- Table 1 (p. 4): median age 12.0 y, 75.9% male, median weight 43.0 kg (children) and 65.1 kg (adolescents).
- Assay: LC-MS/MS, linear 1.0-160 ug/mL "of SXB", LOQ 1.0 ug/mL (p. 3).
- PopPK: paediatric data pooled with four adult studies (three healthy, one narcolepsy; Table S2), single 4.5 g or
  1-4.5 g twice nightly; 174 participants (29 paediatric, 13 adult narcolepsy, 132 healthy), 3,775 observations (333
  paediatric, 3,442 adult); one paediatric participant later excluded for unusual concentrations (pp. 3-5). NONMEM 7.3;
  forward inclusion dOFV 3.84, backward elimination 10.828; bootstrap and VPC evaluation.
- **Table S2 (sup0006.docx), the five studies** (supplement, 2026-10-27): paediatric narcolepsy N=29, 1-4.5 g twice
  nightly, steady state; healthy adults N=13, crossover, 2.25 and 4.5 g twice nightly 4 h apart, first dose 2 h after a
  meal, sampled to 10 h; healthy adults N=60, relative-bioavailability crossover, 3 g twice in the **daytime**, fasted,
  sampled to 12 h; adult narcolepsy N=13, 4.5 g at bedtime, single dose vs 8 weeks, sampled to 7 h after each test dose;
  healthy adults N=59, PK/bioequivalence/food-effect crossover, 4.5 g fasted and fed, "2 doses 4 hours apart during the
  daytime" while the sampling column describes sampling to 8 h after a single 4.5 g dose.
- **Table S3 (sup0007.docx), N=174, median (range)** (supplement, 2026-10-27): weight 72.1 (31.0-129) kg; BSA 1.87
  (1.08-2.52) m2; lean body mass 53.3 (26.8-78.1) kg; BMI 24.5 (16.7-43.6); continuous age 27.0 (8.00-52.0) y.
  Categories: 7-17 y 29 (16.7%), 7-11 y 11 (6.3%), 12-17 y 18 (10.3%), >= 18 y 145 (83.3%); male 100 (57.5%); White 167
  (96.0%), Black 5, Pacific Islander 1, Other 1, Asian 0; narcolepsy 42 (24.1%), healthy 132 (75.9%); **am dosing 119
  (68.4%), pm dosing 55 (31.6%)**; four bioanalytical methods (26/60/59/29 participants). Prandial footnote: three
  studies (n=101) had both fasted and fed doses, one (n=60) fasted only, one (n=13) fed only.
- **Table S1 (sup0005.docx)**: protocol weight-band dosing for the paediatric trial (adapted from Plazzi): < 30 kg start
  <= 2 g/night, titrate <= 1 g/night/week, max 6 g/night; 30 to < 45 kg <= 3, <= 1, max 7.5; >= 45 kg <= 4.5, <= 1.5,
  max 9 g/night; two equal doses at bedtime and 2.5-4 h later.

## Model and parameters
Two compartments, first-order absorption, Michaelis-Menten elimination from the central compartment (Figure S1). IIV on
Vmax, Km, Ka, VC; inter-occasion variability on Vmax and Km. Final covariates (p. 5): food effect reducing Ka,
allometric weight scalars on VC and Vmax, paediatric and child age-category effects on VC, a diurnal effect on Vmax.
Eta shrinkage 5.86% to 71.67%; epsilon shrinkage 4.42% (p. 6).

percentile)** (supplement, 2026-10-27). Table S4 prints coefficients only: no equations, no reference weight, no
statement of which time-of-day level is the reference, and no residual-error estimate.

Values the main article prints:
- Age effect on VC, relative to adults: children a 12% reduction, adolescents a 22% increase (p. 5).
- Food: about a 70% reduction in Ka for the first (fed) nightly dose (p. 8); simulated Cmax about 30% lower and AUC
  about 15% lower fed vs fasted (p. 6).
- Noncompartmental, paediatric dose 1 (Table 2, p. 5, rendered): median Tmax 0.8 h children, 1.2 h adolescents; Cmax
  109.0 (43-173) and 72.9 (21-136) ug/mL; AUC0-4h 267.9 and 160.0 ug.h/mL. Children's Tmax is printed as "0.8 (1-2)",
  a median below its own range (also "0.8 (1-3)" overall): an inconsistency in the printed table.
- Dose 2 vs dose 1 (full vs half): Cmax ratio 1.97 (90% CI 1.67-2.31, proportional); AUC0-4h ratio 2.53 (2.18-2.94,
  supra-proportional) (p. 4).
- Figure 4 (p. 6, rendered; simulated "4.5 g" fed and fasted): adult fasted Cmax median about 112 ug/mL, mean about
  114; adult fed median about 80; adult fasted AUC0-24h median about 310 h.ug/mL (read from the box plot).
- Figure S4 (sup0004, simulated): log-log slopes on mg/kg dose of 1.19 (Cmax1), 1.24 (Cmax2), 1.68 (AUCinf and
  AUC0-24h); see the supplement card.

Background the paper quotes from the Xyrem label (ref 14, 2018 edition): absolute oral bioavailability 88%, 5%
excreted unchanged, a 2-fold dose giving a 3.7-fold AUC, elimination half-life < 1 h, high-fat meal Cmax -59% and
AUC -37% (pp. 2, 8).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None modelled. No ke0, effect site, or concentration-effect relationship.

## Covariates
Weight (allometric on VC and Vmax, the main intrinsic factor), age category on VC, prandial status on Ka, time of dosing
on Vmax. Tested but not retained: continuous age, BSA, lean body mass, BMI, sex, race, disease status, bioanalytical
method (pp. 3, 7; Table S3 lists the same factors with their distributions). Table S4 gives the exponents and
coefficients (above) but not the reference weight, the functional form of the categorical effects, or the reference
level of the diurnal effect; no control stream is supplied, so these remain unstated in the source as archived.
Figure S1 also draws age category on Vmax, which Table S4 does not estimate (see the sup0001 card).

## Caveats
- The concentration basis is labelled "SXB" in the assay, Table 2, Figures 2-4, and in the captions of Figures S2-S4
  ("concentration of SXB in units of ug/mL"), but Figure 5's axes and Figure S3's legend read "oxybate"; neither the
  paper nor the supplement says whether its ug/mL are salt-mass equivalents or the oxybate ion.
- Figure 4's caption does not state the regimen behind "4.5 g" (single dose or twice nightly, and which dose's Cmax);
  the supplement does not settle it.
- Food and dose order are confounded in the paediatric data (fed = dose 1); the authors note this (p. 8).
- Most participants (119 of 174, the two healthy-adult daytime studies) were dosed in the daytime (Tables S2, S3);
  night-time dosing covers the paediatric, the 13-subject healthy twice-nightly and the adult narcolepsy studies.
- The youngest participant was 8.00 y (Table S3) although the child category is labelled 7-11 y; the oldest 52 y.
- Residual-error model and estimate are not printed anywhere in the paper or supplement.
- Sponsor-authored (Jazz Pharmaceuticals).
