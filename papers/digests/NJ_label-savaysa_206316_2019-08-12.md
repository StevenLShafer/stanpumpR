# SAVAYSA (edoxaban) label, 08/2019

## Study
FDA PLR-format label for edoxaban, an oral direct factor Xa inhibitor (tablets 60, 30, 15 mg). Indications:
stroke and systemic embolism prevention in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF), and DVT/PE treatment after
5 to 10 days of parenteral anticoagulation (section 1). Pivotal trials named: ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48, Hokusai VTE,
Hokusai VTE Cancer (8.5).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Section 12.3 (PDF p19, confirmed on page image):
- Approximately dose-proportional PK, 15 to 150 mg single and 60 to 120 mg repeat doses (healthy subjects).
- Tmax 1 to 2 h; absolute oral bioavailability 62%; food does not change total exposure; crushed tablet (in
  applesauce or via NG tube) gives similar exposure.
- Biphasic disposition; Vdss 107 (SD 19.9) L; plasma protein binding about 55% (in vitro); accumulation ratio
  1.14 with once-daily dosing; steady state within 3 days.
- Minimal metabolism (carboxylesterase 1 hydrolysis, conjugation, CYP3A4 oxidation); active metabolite M-4 less
  than 10% of parent exposure.
- Renal clearance 11 L/h, about 50% of total clearance 22 L/h; terminal half-life after oral dosing 10 to 14 h.
- Renal impairment (PDF p20, image): exposure up 32%, 74%, 72% and 93% for CrCL >50 to <80, 30 to 50, <30
  mL/min and peritoneal dialysis vs CrCL ≥80. A 4-h haemodialysis session removed less than 7% of exposure.
  Weight: exposure 13% higher at median 55 kg vs 84 kg. No additional effect of age, sex or race.
- Mild or moderate hepatic impairment: PK and PD similar to controls (p19).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- PT, INR and aPTT are prolonged, but at therapeutic doses the changes are small, highly variable and not
  useful for monitoring (12.2, PDF p18, image). Peak PD effect at 1 to 2 h, coinciding with Cmax.
- 5.3 (PDF p7, image): the anticoagulant effect cannot be reliably monitored with standard laboratory testing;
  it persists about 24 h after the last dose; no specific reversal agent; with PCC use, PT/INR/aPTT or anti-FXa
  monitoring is not useful and not recommended.
- PCC 50 IU/kg in healthy subjects returned endogenous thrombin potential to baseline 0.5 h after a 15-min
  infusion (vs more than 24 h with placebo), overshooting by about 40% at 22 h (12.2, p18).
- No therapeutic, target or toxic plasma concentration is given anywhere in the label (text-layer search for
  ng/mL found none). No QTc prolongation at 90 and 180 mg. Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio applies.

## Dosing (section 2, text layer)
NVAF: 60 mg once daily; 30 mg if CrCL 15 to 50 mL/min (Cockcroft-Gault); do not use if CrCL >95 mL/min (boxed
warning: more ischemic stroke than warfarin). DVT/PE: 60 mg once daily; 30 mg if CrCL 15 to 50, weight ≤60 kg,
or certain P-gp inhibitors. Stop at least 24 h before surgery; PD onset 1 to 2 h on restarting (2.5). Neuraxial:
remove catheters no earlier than 12 h after the last dose; next dose no earlier than 2 h after removal (5.4,
pp7-8). Not recommended for CrCL <15 mL/min or Child-Pugh B/C.

## Covariates
Creatinine clearance (dose and exposure), body weight (DVT/PE dose; small exposure effect), P-gp inhibitors and
rifampin (Figure 12.1, not read numerically; the figure's values are graphical).

## Caveats
- Label-level summary values; no study n or variability beyond Vdss SD.
- Labelling states monitoring is not useful; it gives no laboratory thresholds (those come from the ICSH and
  Douxfils papers the repo also cites, not digested here).
