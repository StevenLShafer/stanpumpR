# Han 2019: oral hydralazine pharmacokinetics in pregnancy by NAT2 genotype

## Study
- **Design**: prospective, steady-state, non-compartmental (Phoenix WinNonlin); no non-pregnant control arm.
- **Subjects**: 12 pregnant women treated for hypertension (13 consented), 7 non-Hispanic white, 3 Asian, 2 native
  Hawaiian/Pacific Islander; 5 studied in mid-pregnancy (22-26 weeks), 8 in late pregnancy (30-38 weeks), one in
  both (PDF p3, printed 1680; p5, 1682). Height 163 ± 6 cm, **weight 113 ± 38 kg** (mid 122 ± 41, late
  108 ± 38), hematocrit < 28% excluded. All also took atenolol.
- **Drug**: oral hydralazine **hydrochloride** tablets (Teva), 5-25 mg every 6 h, titrated clinically, given q6h
  for 3 days before the study day; fasting 5 h before and 1 h after the study dose.
- **Sampling**: venous blood predose and 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6 h over one interval.
- **Assay**: **whole-blood** hydralazine, derivatized immediately with p-nitrobenzaldehyde at pH 6 (avoids release
  from the pyruvic acid hydrazone), LC-MS/MS, LOQ 0.04 ng/mL; plasma MTP by LC-MS/MS, LOQ 1.5 ng/mL.
- **Genotype**: NAT2 by 4-SNP TaqMan; 6 rapid acetylators (RA: *4/*5 ×2, *4/*6 ×3, *4/*7), 6 slow (SA).

MTP (plasma): AUC 118.1 ± 64.9 vs 56.4 ± 40.7 ng·h/mL; Cmax 32.3 ± 11.7 vs 15.0 ± 8.8 ng/mL; t½ 2.6 ± 1.1 vs
3.2 ± 1.4 h (Table 2). The abstract prints the weight-adjusted CL/F unit as "L/h"; Table 2 has L/h/kg.

The one woman studied twice (RA, 10 mg q6h; PDF p7, printed 1684): CL/F 85.4 (mid) and 81.3 (late) L/h/kg, AUC
0.6 ng·h/mL both times, t½ 3.7 and 3.0 h, MTP/parent ratio 100 and 76.

Regression (PDF p7): genotype affected CL/F/kg (β -50.3, SE 6.6, P < .001), Vz/F/kg and the MTP ratio; gestational
age did not (CL/F β 1.9, SE 6.7, P .8); dose did not (CL/F β -0.8, SE 0.4, P .09).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect model, no ke0 or tPeak. Clinically timed Doppler hemodynamics (timing to dose unspecified):
TPR, MAP and HR did not differ between RA and SA at baseline or with hydralazine (e.g. change in MAP -9 ± 12 vs
-1 ± 9 mm Hg, P .3; PDF p6). Not a measured effect relation; atenolol co-treatment confounds.

## Covariates
NAT2 phenotype (rapid vs slow) only. No gestational-age or dose effect detected (n 12).

## Caveats
- Oral only: CL/F mixes NAT2 first-pass extraction with systemic clearance; F not measured. The introduction gives
  non-pregnant oral F of about 16% (RA) and 35% (SA), citing a textbook (ref 26).
- Whole blood, not plasma; very obese cohort (113 kg); small n; all on atenolol; doses clinically titrated.
- **The "comparable to non-pregnant" judgment's comparator** is two older oral plasma studies in non-pregnant
  hypertensive patients (Shepherd 1980, Ludden 1981; refs 44, 64), adjusted by the 1.65 ratio. The ranges the
  authors print (PDF p9) do not obviously support it: pregnant CL/F 3903-16292 (RA) and 800-2849 L/h (SA) lie below
  the non-pregnant 18750-37500 and 3260-7500 L/h, yet pregnant Cmax (0.3-1.8 and 1.4-9.4 ng/mL) also lies below the
  non-pregnant 4-5 and 7-50 ng/mL. Lower clearance with lower peaks is inconsistent at equal F. The direction of the
  1.65 adjustment is not stated. The authors cite design, assay and formulation (tablet vs solution) differences.
- Terminal t½ 3-4 h is much longer than the 0.3-0.7 h of Shepherd's oral study; the authors attribute this to that
  study's 3-h sampling and less sensitive assay.
