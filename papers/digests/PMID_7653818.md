# Vainionpää 1995: ropivacaine vs bupivacaine 5 mg/mL for axillary plexus block, with 24-h plasma PK

## Study
- Randomised double-blind parallel trial, Oulu; 61 ASA I-II adults enrolled (60 analysed: ropivacaine 29,
  bupivacaine 31); PK subset 10 + 10.
- PK ropivacaine subgroup (Table 1): age 37 +/- 16 (21-69) y, height 168 +/- 8 cm, weight 75 +/- 11
  (57-100) kg, 6 M / 4 F.
- Plain 5 mg/mL solutions; perivascular axillary injection by nerve stimulator: 30 mL (< 70 kg), 35 mL
  (70-80 kg), 40 mL (> 80 kg), plus 1 mL skin infiltration and 4 mL intercostobrachial block: total
  35-45 mL = 175-225 mg (Methods). PK subgroup dose 2.72 +/- 0.25 mg/kg (2.25-3.13) (Table 3).
- Venous (antecubital, unblocked arm) samples to 24 h; GC-MS for ropivacaine; limit 0.02 mg/L. Time zero =
  start of skin infiltration; total injection time 14 +/- 12 (6-39) min in the ropivacaine PK group.
- Noncompartmental: observed Cmax, tmax; terminal t1/2 by log-linear regression.

Bupivacaine (for comparison, Table 3): Cmax 1.52 +/- 0.52 mg/L (abstract says 1.28 +/- 0.47), tmax median
0.96 h, t1/2 median 11.5 h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Clinical: onset of analgesia 5-20 min, anaesthesia 12-45 min (ropivacaine), complete motor block 5-20 min;
  38% of ropivacaine patients needed supplementation; block lasted > 8 h in about one third.
- No toxicity seen at peaks 1-2 mg/L. Discussion cites IV ropivacaine t1/2 of 111 min (Lee 1989) and
  attributes the longer post-block t1/2 to slow absorption (flip-flop).

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- n = 10 for PK; venous sampling; noncompartmental only; injection lasted up to 39 min.
- Internal inconsistency: the abstract's Cmax (1.28 mg/L for both drugs) disagrees with Table 3 (ropivacaine
  1.48, bupivacaine 1.52 mg/L).
