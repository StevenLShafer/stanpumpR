# Shafer 2021: The titration paradox (commentary)

## Study
Two-page opinion commentary, not a study. It summarizes Schnider, Minto and Filipovic (Clin Pharmacol Ther 2021;110:401-408), who analysed intraoperative anesthesia-record data from about 9,000 patients: propofol/sevoflurane dose against an electroencephalographic anesthetic effect, and norepinephrine dose against blood pressure, sampled every 10 s over several hours. In those data, more drug was associated with less effect (p.292).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio. The conceptual claims (p.292-293):
- The reversed dose-response is the expected result of titration: the clinician adjusts the dose to reach a target effect, so exposure becomes a function of response.
- The most resistant patients (or the most hypotensive, for norepinephrine) receive the largest doses.
- A worked example with two hypertensive patients (1x dose, 140/90 to 120/85; up to 4x dose, 190/110 to 130/90) shows pooling can make an antihypertensive appear to raise blood pressure.
- The authors suggest a nonlinear mixed-effects model with per-patient PD parameters would likely recover the expected relationship, but NONMEM is impractical for thousands of patients and high-resolution effect time courses are often unavailable.
- Randomized trials with specified dosing are not affected. Comparative-effectiveness analyses can be, through choice of drug rather than dose.
