# Schüttler 1983: CATIA with etomidate and alfentanil (practical implications)

## Study
Preliminary feasibility study of computer-assisted total intravenous anaesthesia (CATIA): a microprocessor-controlled
infusion system that can generate arbitrary concentrations (or amounts) in the central or peripheral compartment from
the pharmacokinetic data of the companion paper (Schwilden et al., "Theoretical considerations", same supplement).
n = 6 women, ASA 1-2, gynaecological (abdominal; hysterectomy in Fig. 3) surgery; mean age 35 yr (SD 11.6), mean
weight 72 kg (SD 12.5) (p. 54). Premedication flunitrazepam 1 mg + atropine 0.5 mg IM 60 min before induction.
Induction by starting the etomidate infusion; alfentanil infusion started 20 min later (abstract: from skin incision),
stopped 20 min (SD 8.9) before end of surgery. Pancuronium 0.1 mg/kg; ventilation with air/oxygen (FIO2 0.4); no N2O.
Infusion scheme: BET (Bolus to fill the initial volume; infusion at the Elimination rate; exponentially declining
infusion replacing drug Transferred to the periphery), from Schwilden 1981 (ref 6).
Assay not described in this paper.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit (alfentanil):
- Targets: etomidate 0.3 µg/mL, alfentanil 0.45 µg/mL plasma (p. 55; abstract).
- Achieved (pooled, during infusion): alfentanil 0.41 µg/mL (SD 0.13, n = 64 samples); etomidate 0.29 µg/mL (SD 0.11,
  n = 87) (p. 55). Adequacy judged by EEG analysis and clinical observation; no quantitative EEG values given.
- Background concentrations quoted from earlier work (p. 54, not measured here): etomidate hypnotic threshold 0.3 µg/mL
  given alone; alfentanil threshold 0.1-0.2 µg/mL after bolus with an induction agent; alfentanil 0.4-0.5 µg/mL plasma
  needed for analgesia in cardiac and abdominal surgery without N2O. These are therapeutic plasma concentrations under
  anaesthesia, not a postoperative MEAC; assay (total plasma) not stated.
- Recovery: verbal response 12.5 min (SD 5.2) after end of etomidate infusion (8.3 min (4.1) after end of surgery),
  etomidate 0.19 µg/mL (0.02) then; extubation 22 min (19.4) after end of surgery, 42 min (16.6) after end of alfentanil,
  alfentanil 0.2 µg/mL (0.08) at extubation (p. 55). Elimination t1/2 after infusion: etomidate 75 min (31.8),
  alfentanil 116 min (51.0).
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, hysteresis or potency ratio reported.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n = 6, single centre, no control group; parameters are from separate small bolus studies; 2-compartment models;
no effect-site concept; assay not described; figures poorly reproduced. Text and legends differ slightly
(CL 1600 vs 1650 mL/min etomidate; 340 vs 330 mL/min alfentanil).
