# Shen 1980: hydralazine and its acid-labile hydrazones, rapid vs slow acetylators

## Study
- Ten healthy volunteers, 5 men and 5 women, aged 21-40; weights 51.0-82.0 kg (mean 63.2, Table I). Acetylator phenotype by the sulfamethazine test (44 mg/kg oral): 5 rapid, 5 slow (subject 6 assigned slow on the urine result although the blood result was borderline rapid) (printed 55, 58).
- Each received, in random order at least a week apart, **20 mg hydralazine hydrochloride IV** (Apresoline injection, 1 mg/min for 20 min) and **100 mg oral** (injection solution given by mouth); 3 subjects (1, 5, 7) also took 50 mg orally. Fasting, supine during infusion (printed 55).
- IV sampling at 5-min intervals during the infusion and to 8 h from its start; oral to 8 h.
- Assay: GLC of the tetrazolo derivative (Zak et al.), run at **0.5 N HCl for hydralazine** and at 2.5 N HCl for "total apparent" hydralazine; acid-labile hydrazones by difference. The authors call it the most specific assay then known but state its specificity was **not validated** (printed 54). Concentrations **expressed as hydralazine hydrochloride** (base = 0.816 x HCl, printed 56). Samples iced; 6.7% degradation in blood over 2 h at 25 ng/mL.
- Analysis: biexponential stripping (CSTRIP) of post-infusion data; CL = dose/AUC; Vbeta = CL/beta (printed 56-57).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. Arterial pressure was recorded every 2 min during and after the infusion (printed 55), but no blood-pressure results, ke0, tPeak or concentration-effect relation are reported. Hydrazone activity in man is stated to be unproven (printed 65).

## Covariates
Acetylator phenotype: no effect on IV parameters; oral AUC, Cp,max and F about 2-3 times higher in slow than in rapid acetylators (comparator: the rapid acetylators of this study). Oral dose: saturable presystemic clearance.

## Caveats
- The "clearance approached hepatic blood flow" claim is against a literature range of 0.8-1.7 L/h/kg (Greenway and Stark), not a measured flow.
- Table V's "several times higher" volume and clearance are against Reidenberg 1973 and Talseth 1976 (non-specific acid-hydrolysis assays), not against later specific-assay studies.
- Specificity of the 0.5 N HCl assay unvalidated; the Addendum notes 2.5 N HCl may under-hydrolyse some conjugates. Residual hydrazone interference would lower apparent clearance and lengthen the half-life.
- Small n; 8-h sampling; several printed means inconsistent with their rows (open_questions).
