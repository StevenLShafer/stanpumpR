# Schols-Hendriks 1995: intranasal, buccal and IV clonazepam

## Study
Three-way crossover, 2-week intervals, 7 healthy men aged 26-58 y, 69-82 kg. Doses: 1.0 mg intranasal (5 mg/mL
solution with dimethyl-beta-cyclodextrin, 0.1 mL per nostril), 1.0 mg buccal (0.4 mL Rivotril oral solution 2.5
mg/mL rubbed on mucosa), 0.5 mg IV (Rivotril injection, forearm vein, over 2 min). Overnight fast. Venous serum at
0, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 60, 90 min, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 24 h. HPLC-UV (210 nm), linear to 50 ng/mL, limit of
determination 2 ng/mL (CV 16%). Non-compartmental (Cmax/tmax observed; AUC 0-2 h). IV data of one subject excluded
(extravasation); buccal data of another lost (assay interference).

## Model and parameters
No model. Table 1 and text:
- Intranasal 1.0 mg: Cmax1 6.3 +/- 1.0 ng/mL at median 17.5 min (15-20); second (oral) peak 4.6 +/- 1.3 ng/mL at
  1.7 h (0.7-3.0).
- Buccal 1.0 mg: Cmax1 6.0 +/- 3.0 ng/mL at 50 min (30-90); second peak 6.5 +/- 2.5 ng/mL at 3.0 h (2.0-4.0).
- IV 0.5 mg: serum 27 +/- 18 ng/mL at 2 min (individuals 5, 50, 15, 20, 45, 25 ng/mL; n = 6). Concentrations fell
  over 10-15 min, matching the buccal/intranasal peaks by 20-40 min. AUC(0-2 h) 11 +/- 3 ng.h/mL.
- Relative dose-normalised AUC(0-2 h): buccal 0.41, intranasal 0.45.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No effect measured. Quoted (Discussion): therapeutic benefit in status epilepticus at serum 13-90 ng/mL (ref 3,
Pinder 1976); a threshold of 18 ng/mL suggested (ref 4, Rylance 1986); not reached after 1.0 mg buccal or nasal.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n = 7 (6 for IV); one IV sample time at the end of a 2-min injection, so the 2-min value is very variable (SD 67%
of mean); HPLC-UV with 2 ng/mL limit; serum, not plasma; low IV dose (0.5 mg) for safety.
