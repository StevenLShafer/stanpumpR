# Schwartz 1976: GFR in children from body length and plasma creatinine

## Study
Retrospective derivation from all endogenous creatinine clearance (Ccr) studies done in 1973 at the
Albert Einstein pediatric renal function unit: 186 children aged 6 months to 20 years (first study per
child only); infants under 6 months were excluded because their GFR does not bear a constant relation to
surface area (PDF p3, printed 261). Ccr distribution bimodal (peaks 10-30 and 110-140 mL/min/1.73 m²,
Fig 1), i.e. both renal patients and children without renal disease.
Ccr: oral water load (800 mL/1.73 m²), 3-5 timed urine collections of 20-30 min, venous Pcr near the end;
UcrV/Pcr, expressed per 1.73 m² BSA; a period rejected if any value differed from the mean by >10%.
Assay: creatinine by a modified Technicon AutoAnalyzer method (Jaffe-based; sample 0.8 mL, blank before
each sample) (PDF p1, printed 259).
Validation: 146 children studied in 1974 against Ccr, and 77 children against inulin clearance (Cin)
(PDF p3).

## Model and parameters
Least-squares regressions of three creatinine derivatives (y) on Ccr (x), y = a + bx (Table I, PDF p3,
confirmed on the page image):

| y | b (slope) | a (intercept) | r |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/Pcr | 0.0115 | 0.3109 | 0.736 |
| L/Pcr | 1.6480 | 20.45 | 0.893 |
| SA/Pcr | 0.0137 | 0.1611 | 0.846 |

L/Pcr correlated best. The intercept was not significantly different from zero, so the line was drawn
through the origin, giving **GFR (mL/min/1.73 m²) = 0.55 × L (cm) / Pcr (mg/dL)** (abstract; PDF p3 and
p5). No separate regression differed by sex or by age band (6 mo-5 y, 6-12 y, >12 y) at the 95% level;
the Ccr ≤60 subgroup (≈ L/Pcr 100) had the same slope, r 0.935.
Validation: r 0.935 vs Ccr (n 146, Fig 3) and 0.905 vs Cin (n 77, Fig 4); neither slope differed from 1.
SD of a single estimate (residual variance): 20.7% against Ccr, 28.6% against Cin (PDF p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not applicable (a renal-function estimator; no drug).

## Covariates
Body length (cm) and plasma creatinine (mg/dL) only. No sex or age term (tested, no difference). No
infant constant: the paper gives only 0.55, and does not cover under 6 months.

## Caveats
- Creatinine by a Jaffe AutoAnalyzer method; modern enzymatic/IDMS creatinine reads lower, which is why
  the 2009 "bedside" revision uses 0.413.
- Reference standard mostly Ccr, which overestimates GFR; Pcr appears in both sides of the Ccr comparison
  (authors acknowledge; Cin comparison free of it).
- Muscle wasting not tested systematically.
- Mixed population (renal disease and normal).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- R/renalFunction.R:206-222 `egfrSchwartz()`: `k x height / scr`, mL/min/1.73 m², k 0.55 from 1 year.
  The 0.55, the length in cm, creatinine in mg/dL and the per-1.73 m² output all **match** this paper.
  Height for length is the usual reading.
- Same function, k 0.45 under one year: **not supported by this paper** (no infant constant; infants
  under 6 months excluded, and 6-12 months got 0.55). Probably the 1984 infant paper; see open questions.
- R/renalFunction.R:39-45 and covariates.md:76 (pregabalin uses the child's own creatinine with Schwartz
  under 13): consistent with the formula; the 13-year cut-off is Chan's, not this paper's (this paper's
  range runs to 20 years). **Cannot verify** here.
- Assay basis: the app's assumed child creatinine (R/renalFunction.R:84-125) is enzymatic; this 0.55 is
  Jaffe. Not a transcription error but a basis mismatch for the blank-creatinine path; see open questions.
- covariates.md:74 lists "bedside Schwartz" among the estimators; that is the 2009 equation
  (egfrBedsideSchwartz, 0.413), a different paper, correctly attributed at R/renalFunction.R:226.
Verdict: partly-verified (formula matches; infant k and assay basis unresolved).
