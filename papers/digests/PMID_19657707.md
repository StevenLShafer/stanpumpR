# Juric 2009: zolpidem placental passage and obstetric outcome

## Study
Prospective cohort from the Emory Women's Mental Health Program (study of psychotropic PK in pregnancy).
51 pregnant women with psychiatric illness took zolpidem at some point in pregnancy; 6 excluded, leaving
45 exposed women and 45 matched comparators (matched on age, race, SCID diagnosis and non-zolpidem
psychotropic exposure; comparators took no hypnotic) (p443, PDF p3). Zolpidem: 43 immediate release,
2 controlled release; mean dose 8.8 ± 3.9 mg/day; mean exposure 13.8 ± 12.9 weeks; 1st/2nd/3rd trimester
exposure n = 17/25/35; 11 exposed throughout (p443). Groups matched well (Table 1, PDF p4); the exposed group
had higher BDI scores (mean 16.7 ± 9.6 vs 12.5 ± 7.6, p < .008); sleep hours did not differ.
Sampling: maternal and umbilical cord blood at delivery only, chilled EDTA tubes, plasma stored at −80 °C;
HPLC at ARUP Laboratories, limit of detection 4 ng/mL (p442, PDF p2). Cord/maternal ratio = cord plasma ÷
maternal plasma; values below the LOD were set to 4 ng/mL; pairs with both undetectable were dropped.
Outcomes: McNemar's tests, paired t-tests, conditional logistic regression.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration–effect data, no therapeutic range, no ke0. The paper quotes (from PDR 2009, not measured
here) single-dose peaks in healthy adults of 59 ng/mL (range 29–113) after 5 mg and 121 ng/mL (58–272) after
10 mg, and an elimination half-life of 2.5 h (p442–443). Cord zolpidem was detectable only when the last dose
was within 11 h of delivery (p443).
Outcomes (Table 2, PDF p5): preterm delivery 26.7% vs 13.3% (p < .18); low birth weight 15.6% vs 4.4%
(p < .18); birth weight 3.18 ± 0.55 vs 3.26 ± 0.41 kg; no major malformations in either group; no
significant association of zolpidem with any adverse outcome on conditional logistic regression.

## Covariates
None modelled. Subgroup comparisons (3rd trimester vs earlier, ≤5 vs >5 mg/day, ≥10 vs <10 weeks) showed no
significant differences (data not shown, p443).

## Caveats
- Single delivery time point; dose timing self-reported; n = 6 dyads, 4 ratios, two of them LOD-bounded.
- All but 3 exposed women took other psychotropics; homogeneous, psychiatrically ill population.
- The paper's own statement that zolpidem is mainly renally excreted (p442) is about its metabolites; the
  paper gives no metabolic pathway data.
- Rough check (derived: 121 ng/mL peak at ~1.4 h, 2.5 h half-life, both quoted values, not this study's):
  a 10 mg dose 11 h earlier predicts about 8 ng/mL, close to subject D's 6.0; at 20 h (subject E) it predicts
  under 1 ng/mL against 8.4 observed. Self-reported timing or slower late-pregnancy elimination could explain
  this; the paper does not analyse it.
