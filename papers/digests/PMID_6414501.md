# Armstrong 1983: steady-state IV nitroglycerin in congestive cardiac failure

## Study
- **Patients (p2):** 10 with congestive cardiac failure, 5 men and 5 women, aged 57-68 y. Weights were not
  reported. Seven had coronary disease (2 after recent MI, 5 chronic) and 3 had cardiomyopathy. The eight chronic
  patients were NYHA class 3-4 and refractory to digoxin and frusemide.
- **Monitoring:** Swan-Ganz and radial arterial lines.
- **Dosing:** GTN (Nitrostat IV) at 100 mcg/mL in glass, given by a Harvard pump through **Teflon tubing (non-
  adsorbing)**. The stock concentration was verified by GLC. The infusion ran at 21 mcg/min for 120 min in the arm
  opposite the arterial line.
- **Non-responders:** if PCWP did not fall by at least 25%, the rate was stepped up every 10-15 min (patients 2, 5
  and 10).
- **Sampling:** **arterial** plasma at control and at 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60 and 120 min. Assay GLC with a
  dinitrobenzene internal standard; 1 ng/mL = 4.4 nmol/L.

## Model and parameters
**One-compartment fits (rendered pp3-4):**
- Haemodynamics: y = Cp + Ap(1 - e^(-t/B)), with one common time constant B across patients.
- Concentration: y = Css(1 - e^(-t/B)), t in minutes.

**Clearance, two ways (p2, p4):**
- (i) 21 mcg/min divided by each patient's average concentration at 30, 60 and 120 min: **6.2 ± 2.7 (SD) L/min**.
- (ii) 21/Css from the nonlinear fit: **5.75 L/min** (approximate SE 1.4).

**Derived volume and half-life:** V = CL x B = 6.2 x 8.1 = 50.2 L, or 5.75 x 8.1 = 46.6 L with the fitted Css.
The implied half-life is 0.693 x 8.1 = 5.6 min.

The model's plateau at 21 mcg/min is 21/6.2 = 3.39 ng/mL (derived), against the fitted 3.65 and the observed
3.0-4.1 at 15-120 min.

**Inconsistency inside the paper (p4):** it says the concentration reaches 69% of steady state, 2.5 ng/mL, after
one time constant. For y = Css(1 - e^(-t/B)) that fraction is 63%, or 2.31 ng/mL. The paper's 2.5 follows from its
69% (0.69 x 3.65). It compares this with the observed 2.6 at 10 min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Table 1 means, control to 120 min:**
  - SPA fell 52 to 45 mmHg, with a nadir of 41 at 60 min.
  - PCWP fell 25 to 20 and RAP 12 to 8.
  - HR, SBP and cardiac index were unchanged.
  - The falls in SPA, PCWP and RAP were significant, P < 0.01 by 2-way ANOVA.
- **Timing:** the effect appeared within 5 min and approached maximum by 10 min.
- **Concentration-effect correlation (p4):**
  - SPA: none.
  - PCWP: r = -0.45.
  - RAP: r = -0.40 (P < 0.001).
  - No EC50 or slope is reported.
- **Effect delay:** the haemodynamic time constants (8.1-9.7 min) match the concentration's 8.1 min, so no
  effect-site delay can be seen.
- **Prior concentration range:** the earlier work suggested 1.2-11.1 ng/mL in arterial plasma (p1, citing
  Armstrong 1980).
- **Non-responders (p4):** 3 of 10 missed the PCWP end point.
  - One responded only at 220 mcg/min.
  - In one (Figure 2a/b), GTN went from 2.0 ng/mL at 21 mcg/min to over 70 ng/mL at 160 mcg/min.
  - In another (Figure 3), the concentration was 6 ng/mL at the peak effect and later fell to 1 ng/mL, which the
    authors attribute to a delivery problem.
- **Tolerance:** none was seen over 120 min.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Small n, all with heart failure. No body weight was reported.
- The clearance is "apparent": the model is a single exponential rise in arterial plasma.
- The post-infusion half-lives (1.1-2.8 min) are much shorter than the 5.6 min implied by the 8.1-min rise, so one
  compartment cannot describe both onset and offset.
- The arterial-venous extraction is 60% (p5, citing Armstrong 1982), so venous values would be much lower.
- Clearance here is lower than the group's earlier 13.9 ± 8.7 L/min in heart failure (p5), which the authors
  attribute to sampling before steady state in that study.
- The 69% statement is internally inconsistent (see above).
