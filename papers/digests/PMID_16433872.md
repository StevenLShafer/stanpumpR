# Friberg 2006: PK-PD model of QT prolongation after citalopram overdose

## Study
52 patients, 62 deliberate citalopram overdose events (reported dose median 280 mg, 20-1700 mg); age median 30
(13-72); 16 M / 36 F (Table 1). 39 patients were also on therapeutic citalopram (baseline concentration estimated).
Single-dose activated charcoal (50 g) in 17 events. 1-12 PK samples per event (median 2); 166 ECGs 0.45-81.5 h after
overdose, QT and RR measured manually (median of ≥6 leads). Fully Bayesian population PKPD in WinBUGS 1.4; PK model
from an earlier paper.

## Model and parameters
PK (from ref. 11, structure kept): one compartment, first-order absorption; CL/F 22.1 L/h, V/F 1280 L, ka 1.48 /h,
baseline concentration 0.097 mg/L; BSV 27% (CL) to 52% (V); charcoal: CL +72%, fraction absorbed -22%.
PD: QT = QTci·RR^α (individual α); QTci = QTci0 + Slope·Ce, Ce in a hypothetical effect compartment.

Typical patient: Cmax at 2.9 h, maximum QT prolongation at 7.8 h. Emax and exponential models did not converge;
linear chosen. Charcoal cut the cumulative hazard of abnormal QT-RR by ~60% for 600-1800 mg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: heart-rate-corrected QT (individual α) after overdose; effect-compartment equilibration half-life 1.43 h
(teq, on QT, not analgesia). Therapeutic concentrations 0.05-0.1 mg/L predict only 2-4 ms. No MEAC, no potency.
Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Sex and age on QTci0; co-ingested QT drugs (4.2 ms, judged insignificant). Charcoal on PK.

## Caveats
Overdose population; uncertain doses and times (dose estimated within the clinician's veracity limits); sparse
early PK, informative priors; teq poorly identified (wide interval); metabolite contribution not excluded; manual ECG
measurement; no TdP observed.
