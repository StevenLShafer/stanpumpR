# Casele 1999: enoxaparin pharmacokinetics during and after pregnancy

## Study
Prospective paired study. 14 women with an indication for thromboprophylaxis enrolled; 13 completed all
three phases (Results, PDF p3). Women with a hypercoagulable state warranting therapeutic anticoagulation were
excluded. Age 30 ± 6 y, height 64 ± 3 in, weight in early pregnancy 149 ± 42 lb (mean ± SD; ≈ 67.6 ± 19 kg,
derived: lb x 0.4536); individual weights 120-277 lb (Table I, PDF p2), two women 272 and 277 lb.
Enoxaparin 40 mg (4000 U) SC once daily, self-administered. Three 12-h study periods in the same women: 12-15
weeks' gestation, 30-33 weeks' gestation, and 6-8 weeks postpartum. Pre-dose sample (≥ 24 h after the previous
dose; one late-pregnancy study excluded because the prior dose was < 12 h earlier), then samples at 30, 60, 90,
120, 150, 180, 210, 240, 300, 360, 420, 480, 600 and 720 min. Plasma anti-factor Xa activity by amidolytic
(chromogenic) assay, detection limit 0.01 anti-Xa U/mL, CV 6%. Noncompartmental analysis (Lagrange, W.J. Jusko);
repeated-measures ANOVA with Ryan-Einot-Gabriel-Welsch post hoc (SAS 6.1).

literature's 5.2-6.4 h. Peak anti-Xa about 3.5 h after a dose.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD model, no effect site. End point is plasma anti-Xa activity only; no clinical outcomes. The authors
cite (ref 17) a recommended prophylactic anti-Xa target of 0.1-0.2 IU/mL and suggest twice-daily dosing may be
needed to keep anti-Xa above 0.1 IU/mL through 24 h in pregnancy (PDF p4-5). No anti-Xa was measurable at 24 h in
any pregnant study; 4/13 postpartum women had measurable baseline activity; at 12 h activity was unmeasurable in
6/13 (early), 2/13 (late) and 2/13 (postpartum) (PDF p3).

## Covariates
Pregnancy stage only (early, late, postpartum within subject). No weight, renal or other covariate model.

## Caveats
- Small (n = 13), wide weight range (two women > 270 lb); no weight normalisation.
- The comparator is the **same women 6-8 weeks postpartum**, not a nonpregnant population model.
- Variability is labelled SEM, but SEMs as large as the means (12-h concentration 0.09 ± 0.09) look like SDs; the
  label cannot be settled from the paper.
- 12-h sampling for a drug with a ~4-7 h half-life; Vss by NCA after SC dosing (MRT includes absorption).
- Late pregnancy differs from postpartum significantly only in Cmax (the authors say so, PDF p4); the abstract's
  "AUC significantly lower in pregnancy" holds for early pregnancy by the Table II footnotes.
- Late-pregnancy clearance is lower than early (authors: clearance apparently decreases in late pregnancy).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Header :73-75** — "Casele 1999 (13 women, paired against 6-8 weeks postpartum) found the anti-Xa AUC and peak
  lower in pregnancy". Paper: 13 completers, postpartum 6-8 weeks, Cmax lower in both early and late pregnancy
  (significant); AUC lower in both, significant only for early. **Matches** the authors' summary; precise only for
  early pregnancy for AUC.
- **Reference :104-106** — authors, journal, volume, pages 1113-1117, DOI: **matches**.
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:144** — "AUC and Cmax significantly lower in pregnancy than in the same women
  postpartum"; n "13 women early/late", "40 mg SC": **matches** design and dose; "significantly" for AUC is true for
  early pregnancy only. The factors applied (CL x1.5, V x1.4) come from Lebaudy 2008, not from Casele. Casele's
  own early-pregnancy CL ratio (1.46) agrees with 1.5, but its late-pregnancy ratios (CL 1.17, Vss 1.19, both NS)
  are smaller, while the row is labelled "third" trimester: **partly supported** (see open question).
- **Unit checks**: Casele's CL is mL/min absolute (not per kg); 10.0 mL/min = 0.60 L/h (derived). The library's
  model (Berges 2007, elderly) gives 0.70 L/h at 65 kg and CLcr 69: Casele's postpartum women clear ~15% less per
  person (derived). Casele's NCA Vss/F 3.1 L is far smaller than Berges' central + peripheral 6.43 + 8.18 = 14.6 L
  (derived); the authors note their volume is below the literature's 5.2-9.3 L too. The comparison is not used by
  the code. No MEAC/band in the code (`MEAC = 0`, :182) and the paper supplies none for the model; consistent.
- **Comparator check**: Casele's "lower"/"higher" are against the same women postpartum; the code applies Lebaudy's
  pregnant-vs-nonpregnant ratio to an elderly (> 75 y) model, which its header already states (:89-90).
