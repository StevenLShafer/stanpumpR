# Eugene 2016: sex-based metoprolol dosing in the elderly (population PK)

## Study
- A secondary analysis. The data were digitised from Lundborg & Steen 1976 (Acta Med Scand 200:397-402), a study of 12 chronically ill geriatric inpatients in Göteborg. Ten of them took one 50 mg oral metoprolol dose after fasting at least 10 h, and **7 were analysed** (3 women, 4 men) (PDF p2, printed 210).
- Age 61-88 y, mean 75 ± 9 y (men 65 ± 6, women 83 ± 13). Weight 45-75 kg, mean 62 ± 10 kg (men 65 ± 6, women 58 ± 13).
- The assay is not described; it was the original study's. The concentrations are plasma, in ng/mL. The salt was not stated in this paper, though the label it cites is for the tartrate.
- The plots show samples out to about 6 h (Figs 2-3).
- Model: MONOLIX 4.3.3 (SAEM), one compartment with first-order absorption, a lag time and first-order elimination. Sex on CL was the only covariate kept, because weight made the AIC worse. −2LL was 592.59 and AIC 612.59.
- Simulations: A4S/MATLAB, 5000 virtual patients, BID dosing over 24 h. The young-male comparator came from Kaila 2007's model.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- The study measured no effect. Discussion (PDF p6, printed 214) quotes Luzier 1999 [ref 16], which the paper calls healthy young male and female participants:
  - Emax: 18% for SBP reduction (CV ~20%) and 30% for HR (CV ~10%).
  - Hill γ: SBP 6.15 (men), 2.45 (women); HR 1.1 (men), 1.3 (women).
  - EC50: SBP 28 ng/mL (men), 25 ng/mL (women); HR 15 ng/mL (men), 21 ng/mL (women). The concentrations are plasma, with the enantiomer/racemate basis not stated. The paper does not say whether the measurements were at rest or during exercise.
- Lundborg 1976 (as quoted): after 50 mg, SBP fell at most 30 ± 10 mmHg and HR fell 19 ± 3 beats/min on average.
- No ke0, tPeak or hysteresis is reported. This is not an opioid paper, so it has no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
- Sex on CL only: CL_male = CL_female × exp(0.572). Body weight was tested and rejected.

## Caveats
- The data are **7 patients**, digitised from 1976 figures, with sampling to about 6 h. V/F has an RSE of 95% and an IIV of 155%.
- The V/F of 38 L is implausibly small for metoprolol. Every comparator in Table 3 is 159-868 L.
- The text's "57% decrease" in women's clearance does not match Table 1. The derived figure is 1 − 59.1/105 = 44% lower, or men 77% higher.
- The CYP2D6 genotype of the elderly patients is unknown. The Discussion's model-comparison table is called "Table 2" but is Table 3.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **EC50 28 ng/mL in men, 25 ng/mL in women, Emax ~18%: matches.** These are the values printed on PDF p6, and the units are ng/mL in both places. The row correctly flags itself as a secondary extraction (the primary source is Luzier 1999).
- **Population "hypertensive adults": discrepancy.** Eugene attributes the PD values to healthy young male and female volunteers. The cited Luzier reference title also says healthy volunteers.
- **End point "exercise SBP": cannot verify from this paper.** Eugene says only that the EC50 is for a 50% reduction in SBP. See `open_questions`.
- **No other use.** The library's metoprolol PK model (`R/drugs_metoprolol.R:131-136`) is Lammers 2017 and does not cite this paper. In that model, CL 65.8 L/h with F 0.45 gives CL/F 146 L/h (derived) in young men. Eugene's elderly men at 105 L/h are 0.72× that and elderly women at 59.1 L/h are 0.40× (derived). The model has no sex or age covariate beyond size scaling. It takes Vss/F = (187 + 97.6)/0.45 = 632 L (derived), against Eugene's 38 L. The comparison is between studies (elderly comorbid inpatients versus healthy young men), not a within-study effect.
