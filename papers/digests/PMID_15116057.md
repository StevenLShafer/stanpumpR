# Engels 2004: ketoconazole inhibition of docetaxel clearance

## Study
Randomised two-period crossover, 7 adult cancer patients (Table I: median age 40 y, range 36-59; 4 men, 3 women; median BSA 1.8 m2, range 1.6-2.1; WHO performance 0-1; normal marrow, renal and liver function). Course A: docetaxel 100 mg/m2 as a 1-h IV infusion (median dose 190 mg, range 160-210). Course B, 3 weeks apart (either order): docetaxel 10 mg/m2 (median 19 mg, range 16-21) plus oral ketoconazole 200 mg x 3 (1 h before the infusion, then 24 h and 48 h). Dexamethasone premedication. Plasma sampled to 72 h after the infusion (16 time points). Docetaxel by LC-MS/MS (LLOQ 0.0004 ug/mL = 0.5 nmol/L), ketoconazole by HPLC-UV; protein binding by equilibrium dialysis. Noncompartmental analysis in WinNonlin 4.0; Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Concentrations are total plasma docetaxel. Linearity between 10 and 100 mg/m2 was assumed from the literature (5-145 mg/m2), so clearances were compared without dose correction (p. 449, PDF p. 3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. Concentration-effect is not studied; the authors cite Bruno 1998 for odds of grade 4 neutropenia (4.3-fold) and febrile neutropenia (3.0-fold) with a 50% fall in CL; the observed maximum 74% fall in CL corresponds to 6.5-fold and 4.5-fold (p. 452). Single-agent docetaxel cycle: grade 4 neutropenia in 3 of 7, grade 3 leukopenia in 2 of 7. Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Ketoconazole exposure (AUC) vs fractional change in CL (Fig 2, above). Time to ketoconazole peak was unrelated (R2 0.047). The patient with the least inhibition (ratio 0.68) took ranitidine, which lowers ketoconazole absorption. CYP3A5 expression is discussed as a source of variability; not measured.

## Caveats
n = 7; fixed ketoconazole regimen with 3-fold-plus spread in ketoconazole AUC; the ketoconazole course used a tenth of the docetaxel dose, so linearity of docetaxel PK was assumed, not tested; 72-h sampling with a sensitive assay gives a terminal t1/2 (41 h) and Vss (477 L) much longer and larger than a 24-h-sampled population model; docetaxel levels at 10 mg/m2 near the end of the sampling window are close to the LLOQ. Ethnic mix not given.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Independent check of the docetaxel model (not cited there).** `R/drugs_docetaxel.R` gives CL = BSA x (22.1 - 3.55 AAG - 0.095 age + 0.225 albumin) L/h; at Bruno's mean covariates its header gives 36.7 L/h and Vss 148.6 L, terminal half-life 11.4 h (lines 28-37). Engels's untreated CL is 35.0 L/h (mean, 7 patients, BSA about 1.9 m2), 5% below Bruno's mean: **consistent**. Vss (477 L against 148.6 L) and terminal t1/2 (41 h against 11.4 h) are about 3x larger and longer in Engels. Direction: the two studies differ in sampling (72 h with 0.5 nmol/L LLOQ here; roughly 24 h, sparse, in Bruno's phase 2 patients) so a deeper peripheral compartment is plausible; the paper does not say this, so treat as an unresolved difference in tails, not an error. It affects late-time curves, not the first day.
- **Help text** `inst/app-help/drugs/docetaxel.md:23`: "CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole and others) were not studied and raise exposure." Engels measured ketoconazole: CL x 0.51 (95% CI 0.36-0.65; range 0.26-0.68), i.e. exposure per mg about 2x (AUC/dose ratio 2.19). The direction ("raise exposure") **matches**; "not studied" is true only of the Bruno cohort. See open question.
- The model has no CYP3A inhibitor term and `docetaxel()` has no argument for one (R/drugs_docetaxel.R:112); nothing to verify against. No unit conversion issues: Engels CL is absolute L/h, the repo's is BSA-scaled L/h then divided by 60 for L/min (line 136).
