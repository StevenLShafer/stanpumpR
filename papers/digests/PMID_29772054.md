# Kinnunen 2018: oxycodone PK and fetal exposure in the pregnant ewe

## Study
Animal study (sheep), part of the KuBiCo consortium; PDF p2-3 (printed 1201-1202).
- 10 time-mated Åland landrace ewes, 3-11 years, 118-127 gestational days (term 145 d), median weight
  54 kg (range 51-60); singleton or twin pregnancies.
- Part 1 (awake, 7 days before surgery): oxycodone hydrochloride trihydrate 0.1 mg/kg IV over 1 min via the
  left external jugular vein; the paper states this equals 0.0867 mg/kg oxycodone hydrochloride. Samples from
  the contralateral jugular at 2, 10, 30, 60 min and 2, 4, 7, 10, 24 h.
- Part 2 (randomized, under general anaesthesia: IM ketamine 2 mg/kg + midazolam 0.2 mg/kg, propofol 4-5 mg/kg,
  sevoflurane 1.5-2.5%, transdermal fentanyl 2 µg/kg/h plus IM fentanyl rescue): 0.5 mg/kg oxycodone HCl
  trihydrate (= 0.434 mg/kg HCl) as a 1-min bolus IV (n = 5) or epidural (n = 5) before laparotomy for fetal
  catheter placement. Maternal samples to 24 h; two paired maternal-fetal (fetal carotid arterial) samples, the
  first when the fetal catheter was placed and the second at end of surgery (55-161 min after the bolus).
- Assay: UPLC-MS, plasma (EDTA). Calibration ranges (ng/mL): oxycodone 0.05-1000, oxymorphone 0.1-500,
  noroxymorphone 0.2-1000, noroxycodone 0.05-100. All concentrations reported as the hydrochlorides.
  (The Discussion, p5, gives the LLoQ as 0.5 ng/mL, which disagrees with the 0.05 lower calibration limit; not
  material to stanpumpR.)
- Noncompartmental analysis (Phoenix WinNonlin 6.3); median (min-max).

- One ewe (sheep 6) in part 1 was excluded as a probable extravascular injection (Cmax 4.2 ng/mL at 60 min).
- The 0.5 mg/kg IV column has n = 4 although Methods randomized 5 to IV; the paper does not say which was dropped.
- Dose basis of Cl (derived: 0.0867 mg/kg / 16.7 h·ng/mL = 5.2 L/h/kg; 0.434 / 154 = 2.8): the clearances are
  computed from the hydrochloride (not trihydrate) dose, consistent with concentrations reported as HCl.
- The authors attribute the twofold higher Cl at 0.1 mg/kg (vs 0.5 mg/kg in the same ewes, anaesthetized) to an
  unobserved elimination phase after the small dose and possibly to anaesthesia (p4).
- Epidural bioavailability about 80% (authors, p5, from median AUCinf 125 vs 154 h·ng/mL across groups).
- Fetal exposure (p3, Figure 3): fetal oxycodone median 10.8 ng/mL (1.7-18.9), F/M 1.01 (0.77-1.95) after IV;
  11.8 ng/mL (6.2-22.4), F/M 0.64 (0.47-1.04, n = 3) after epidural. Oxymorphone maternal 3.8-12.5 ng/mL
  (median 7.2) vs fetal 9.2-40.1 (15.8) after IV, F/M 2.10 (1.34-3.52); epidural maternal 3.6-20.4 (9.4) vs
  fetal 4.5-54.0 (10.9, n = 3), F/M 1.29 (0.86-2.96). Noroxycodone < 2.3 ng/mL; noroxymorphone < LLoQ.
  Two fetuses in the epidural group died of surgical bleeding before sampling.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- MEAC / minimum effective concentration: none reported (no analgesic or other PD end point measured).
- ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported.
- Potency: the Discussion (p4) states, citing a review (ref 19), that oxymorphone has almost 50-fold higher
  μ-opioid receptor affinity than oxycodone. This is receptor affinity, not an equianalgesic ratio, and is not
  measured here.
- Cites an earlier human study (ref 14) in which epidural oxycodone 0.1 mg/kg gave 320-fold higher CSF oxycodone
  than the same IV dose (p5); not data from this paper.

## Covariates
None (n = 10 sheep, descriptive only).

## Caveats
- Sheep, not humans; late gestation; the IV 0.5 mg/kg and epidural arms were under general anaesthesia with
  concomitant fentanyl, ketamine, midazolam, propofol and sevoflurane.
- Very small n (fetal comparisons n = 3 in the epidural group); NCA with short sampling after the small dose.
- In sheep oxymorphone was the main metabolite; in humans CYP3A4-mediated noroxycodone predominates (authors,
  p4), so the fetal oxymorphone finding may not carry over to humans.
- No comparison with a non-pregnant group, so the paper gives no pregnancy effect on clearance.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv:35`: the row says there is no human pregnancy PK study for oxymorphone, that
  sheep data (Kinnunen 2018/2019) show fetal oxymorphone accumulation after maternal oxycodone, and that they
  give no maternal human factor; cl/v/f/gfr factors NA, design "none (animal only)", n 0. The paper supports
  this: it is a sheep study, reports fetal oxymorphone F/M 2.10 (IV) and 1.29 (epidural), and has no
  non-pregnant comparator from which a pregnancy factor could be taken. **matches.**
- The row's statements about oxymorphone clearance pathways (UGT, CYP3A) are not from this paper; the paper only
  names CYP2D6 as the human route from oxycodone to oxymorphone. Not a discrepancy, just not sourced here.
- The "2019" in "Kinnunen 2018/2019" is a separate paper (PMID 30652261, Clin Pharmacokinet 2019), cited in the
  oxycodone row (`data-raw/pregnancyRegistry.R:307`).
- The context lists only oxymorphone, but the paper's PK is for oxycodone. The library's human oxycodone model
  (`R/drugs_oxycodone.R`) is not numerically compared: the ewe clearance (5.2 or 2.8 L/h/kg) reflects sheep
  metabolism and has no human or non-pregnant comparator, and nothing in the repo applies these sheep values.
