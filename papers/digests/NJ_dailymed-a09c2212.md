# Triamterene capsules (Prasco authorized generic of Dyrenium), DailyMed label

## Study
- **Document:** US label for triamterene capsules, a potassium-sparing diuretic.
- **Indication:** edema only. The label lists CHF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, steroid-induced, idiopathic and
  secondary-hyperaldosteronism edema. Hypertension is not an indication on this label.
- **Strengths:** 50 and 100 mg triamterene (free base; MW 253.27). The capsules are imprinted DYRENIUM.

- **Metabolite:** the major metabolite is the sulfate conjugate of hydroxytriamterene. Its plasma and urine levels
  greatly exceed those of triamterene (p2). The label does not say whether it is active.
- **Not reported:** half-life, clearance, volume and absolute F.
- **Urinary recovery:** the label does not reconcile the 21% recovery figure with "less than 50% reaching the
  urine". Each may refer to a different analyte, but the label does not say.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **End points:** natriuresis and diuresis. Most patients respond on day 1; the maximum effect may take several
  days.
- No blood pressure data.
- **Safety:** boxed warning for hyperkalemia (serum K ≥ 5.5 mEq/L).
- **Dosing (p8):** usually 100 mg twice daily after meals when used alone, at most 300 mg/day.

## Covariates
None quantified. It is contraindicated in anuria, severe or progressive kidney disease and severe hepatic disease.

## Caveats
- **Source typo:** the carcinogenesis section gives the MRHD as "300 mg/kg/day (or 6 mg/kg/day based on a 50 kg
  patient)". The arithmetic shows mg/day was meant (300 mg / 50 kg = 6 mg/kg).
- The peak serum level is given without the dose.
- **Use in specific populations:** triamterene crosses the placenta and appears in cord blood. Nursing patients
  should stop nursing if the drug is essential. Pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established.
