# Villikka 1999: CYP3A4-inducing antiepileptics and vincristine clearance

## Study
Open, parallel-group, non-randomised study, Helsinki University Central Hospital, April 1997 - November 1998 (PDF p2). 15 adults with malignant brain tumours on procarbazine/lomustine/vincristine. Group 1 (n=9): at least 2 weeks of carbamazepine (n=8) or phenytoin (n=1); group 2 control (n=6): no obvious CYP-inducing drug (some took oxcarbazepine, gabapentin, vigabatrin or dexamethasone, which the authors call weak or non-inducers). Group 1 was 8 men and 1 woman; control 3 men and 3 women. Mean age (text layer) 45 +/- 1.6 y group 1 and 46 +/- 2.2 y control; BMI 26 +/- 1.0 and 25 +/- 2.0 kg/m2. Weight and height not given. Planned n=20, stopped early for slow accrual.

Drug/route/dose: vincristine 2 mg IV (fixed absolute dose, the 2 mg maximum of the 1.4 mg/m2 regimen) as a 15-minute infusion on day 29 (PDF p2). Salt form not stated. Samples (10 mL, EDTA) pre-dose and about 0.25, 0.75, 3, 6, 10 and 24 h after the end of the infusion; exact times used. Plasma, stored at -40 C.

Assay: plasma vincristine by LC-MS/MS (PE SCIEX API 3000), vinblastine internal standard; LOQ 0.1 ng/mL; interday CV 9.2% at 0.3, 3.6% at 3.0, 4.5% at 30 ng/mL. Total plasma concentration, not free, not whole blood.

Analysis: non-compartmental. ke by log-linear regression of at least 3 terminal points chosen visually; AUC(0-inf) by trapezoids plus Clast/ke; t1/2 = ln2/ke; CL = Dose/AUC; Vd = CL/ke (so Vd is the terminal-phase volume, Vz). Software MK model (Biosoft). Unpaired two-tailed t test, mean +/- SEM.

Ratios stated in the paper: CL 163% of control (+63%), Vd 101%, t1/2 65% (35% shorter), AUC 57% (43% smaller). The two untreated-by-anything patients had the lowest CL (308 and 442 mL/min, PDF p5, text).

Derived (not in the paper): 925 mL/min = 55.5 L/h and 569 mL/min = 34.1 L/h. Vz = CL/ke gives about 1020 L (group 1, 12.7 h) and 960 L (control, 19.4 h), consistent with 12.9 and 12.8 L/kg only if mean weight is about 75-80 kg; the paper does not give weight.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect, toxicity or efficacy end point was measured; the authors say efficacy impact is unknown. No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio (not an opioid).

## Covariates
Only the presence of carbamazepine/phenytoin (group). Sex imbalance noted; the authors saw no obvious sex difference. No weight, BSA, age or hepatic covariate modelling.

## Caveats
Small n (9 vs 6), early termination, non-randomised, groups differ in sex mix and co-medication (dexamethasone, weak inducers in controls). Elimination t1/2 difference was not significant. Sampling to 24 h only, for a drug with a long terminal phase, so ke and hence Vd are extrapolated from a short log-linear segment. Control group clearance (569 mL/min) is the comparator for "63% higher": it is a concurrent patient group, not the general population and not stanpumpR's model. Authors propose induction of CYP3A4, possibly P-glycoprotein, as the mechanism.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No code cites the paper, so there is nothing to verify line by line. Independent comparison with the library's vincristine model (`R/drugs_vincristineIgarashi.R:26,76-83`, Igarashi 2021): CL 31.1 L/h at BSA 1.60 m2 and age 60, three compartments, V1 13.2 + V2 33.9 + V3 544 L (about 591 L total). Villikka's control CL (34.1 L/h, derived) is 1.10x Igarashi's reference value, and the induced group (55.5 L/h) is 1.78x; both are absolute-dose clearances in patients of unreported size, against Igarashi's BSA-scaled value, so this is only an order-of-magnitude agreement. Villikka's terminal Vz (about 1000 L derived, 12.8-12.9 L/kg) is larger than Igarashi's summed V (about 590 L), as expected for a terminal-phase volume from a 24-h sampling window; unit conversion (mL/min to L/h) is the only one needed. The library has no carbamazepine/phenytoin inducer adjustment for vincristine (`R/drugs_vincristineIgarashi.R` has no inducer term); see the open question. Verdict: **not-applicable** (uncited; comparison only).
