# Rambiritch 2016b: dose- and concentration-response of glibenclamide on glucose

Page convention: printed pp. 141-153 = PDF pp. 1-13 (PDF page = printed page - 140).

## Study
Same 24 South African T2D subjects and dose escalation as the PK paper (0, 2.5, 5, 10, 20 mg/day at 2-week
intervals; placebo phase first). Glucose by glucometer (Accutrend Alpha), glibenclamide by HPLC. Methods (printed
p. 142) say all except two subjects contributed all steps and name subjects 16, 20, 21, 24 as incomplete; the
abstract says all except four. All 24 were included. NONMEM.

## Model and parameters
Six inhibitory Emax models, Effect = E0 x (1 - X x Emax / (X + X50)), with X = daily dose (ED50, mg) or the average
steady-state concentration Cpss = Dose / (CL/F x 24), CL/F = 2.16 L/h fixed from the PK paper (Eq. 3). Responses:
fasting glucose (Models 1-2), mean glucose over 8 h (3-4), full glucose profile with a two-harmonic placebo baseline,
period 8 h (5-6). IIV exponential on E0, additive on Fourier coefficients; log-scale additive residual.

Unit check (my calculation): the footnotes' derived ED50 = EC50 x CL/F x 24 reproduces 4.41, 1.87 and 2.26 mg only if
EC50 is in ug/L (0.0852 mg/L x 2.16 L/h x 24 h = 4.42 mg); with mg/L it would be 4,417 mg. Cpss at 5 mg/day is
96 ug/L (derived), the same order as the EC50s. So the "mg/L" unit printed in Tables 1-3 is a source error for ug/L
(ng/mL).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points: fasting blood glucose, 8-h mean glucose, full 8-h glucose profile (breakfast and lunch peaks near 2 and
6 h). No effect compartment, no ke0: the authors saw no acute concentration-effect link and drove the effect with
dose or steady-state average concentration, citing metabolite formation (Rydberg 1997). Maximal effect 31-37%
reduction from baseline (fasting glucose 15.4 to 10.6 or 9.7 mmol/L). Conclusion: the effective dose is under
5 mg/day; higher doses add little glucose lowering. Only 7 (30%) and 4 (17%) subjects reached acceptable and optimal
control at doses above 5 mg/day.

## Covariates
None tested.

## Caveats
Small, poorly controlled sample; glucometer glucose; parent drug only (no M1/M2); Cpss from the PK model's CL/F (the
Results say each subject's own clearance was used; the footnotes derive ED50 with the typical 2.16 L/h);
very low BSV on potency in the fasting models vs 108-221% in the others; EC50 unit misprint; the abstract and
Methods disagree on how many subjects were incomplete.
