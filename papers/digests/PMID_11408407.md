# Peyton 2001b: V/Q inhomogeneity increases gas uptake in anesthesia (log-normal and measured distributions)

## Study
Computer model with no patients dosed. It is a multi-compartment gas-exchange model with mass balance per gas.

- The model assumes arterial and alveolar partial pressures are equal within a compartment (PDF p.1).
- Theoretical log-normal distributions of blood flow (Q) or ventilation are used, with log SD from 0 (homogeneous lung) to 1.75.
- Compartment count is 10 for the basic runs. It was 50 when collapse of compartments with critically low VAI/Q (absorption atelectasis) was modeled, because of quantization error.
- The inspired mixture is 30% O2 and 70% N2O. VAE is 4.1 L/min, Q is 4.8 L/min, VO2 is 250 ml/min and VCO2 is 200 ml/min (p.2).
- Three pairs of measured distributions, narrower (before induction) and wider (after), were taken from Dueck et al. 1980 (subjects 6, 7 and 8).

## Model and parameters
No PK parameters.

Results from the narrative text (p.2-4):
- With Pv-N2O 468 Torr, N2O uptake is 100 ml/min in the homogeneous lung. At log SD 1.75 uptake is more than twice that value.
- With atelectasis modeled, augmentation peaks at a log SD of about 1.25 and then declines. Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction raises uptake somewhat.
- Pv-N2O sweep from 0 to 600 Torr (Fig. 2, 3): the threshold Pv-N2O is about 300 Torr. Below it, N2O uptake falls with inhomogeneity as usual. At or above it, N2O uptake rises. O2 uptake shows the reciprocal pattern, rising with inhomogeneity below the threshold and falling above it. The threshold corresponds to N2O uptake of about 550 ml/min (p.16).
- Severinghaus, quoted: N2O uptake = 1000/sqrt(t) ml/min with t in minutes. The authors say the threshold is passed within minutes of induction.
- Fig. 8: elimination of N2 at 10 ml/min lowers N2O uptake but does not remove the augmentation.
- Measured distributions (Table 1 and p.13): N2O uptake is predicted to double in subject 7 (log SD of Q 1.42 to 2.37), to rise 22% in subject 8 (1.17 to 1.95, with dead space doubling from 25 to 50%), and to fall 5% in subject 6 (0.86 to 1.67, with true shunt rising from about 2% to 27%).
- Dueck's measured N2O uptake was highest in subject 7: 325 ml/min after 85 min of N2O anesthesia (p.14).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None (not an opioid paper). No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio. The end points are modeled N2O, O2 and CO2 uptake and arterial N2O and O2 partial pressures.

## Covariates
None apart from the log SD of the Q (or ventilation) distribution.

## Caveats
- Steady state only. The authors say there are no human data measuring N2O uptake and V/Q distributions together (p.14).
- The measured distributions come from patients with significant chronic lung disease.
- The two ways of generating the log-normal distribution give quantitatively different results (VAE-based versus VAI-based). Atelectasis, HPV and retained alveolar N2 each change the size of the effect.
