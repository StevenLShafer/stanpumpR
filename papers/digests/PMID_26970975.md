# Zaouter 2017: hybrid sedation system (HSS), closed-loop propofol sedation

## Study
- Prospective randomized trial (NCT01020643), McGill University Health Centre, 1 Feb to 17 Nov 2010 (PDF p2). 150 adults (18-85 y) having elective orthopedic lower-limb surgery under spinal anesthesia (7.5-15 mg isobaric bupivacaine) with propofol sedation; 75 closed-loop (HSS), 75 manual (Control).
- Demographics (Table 1, PDF p4): age 64 +/- 27 vs 68 +/- 12 y (SD of 27 as printed); M/F 35/40 vs 33/42; weight 79 +/- 16 vs 80 +/- 15 kg; ASA 1/2/3 23/27/25 vs 35/27/13.
- Target BIS 65 in both arms. Sedation 119.1 +/- 37.9 min (HSS) vs 127.8 +/- 40.5 min (control), not different.
- No propofol concentration was measured and no pharmacokinetic model was used; the controller is rule-based on infusion rate, not target-controlled (discussion, PDF p8).

## Model and parameters
No PK model or parameters. Controller algorithm (PDF p3-4, section 2.3):
- Induction bolus 250 ug/kg over 2 min, then 50 ug/kg/min for 3 min.
- Sedation profile chosen from three BIS values: all below 60 = light (sensitive); between 60 and 90 = moderate; above 90 = profound (resistant). Per-profile maintenance rates are not given in this paper (the companion TAVI paper gives them).
- Maintenance: new dose = previous dose x Km x Kh, recomputed every 2 min from the mean of the last six BIS values. Km scales with (actual BIS - target); Kh scales with (mean BIS over the last interval - target). Coefficients are not given.
- Safety: RR below 8/min or SpO2 below 92% for more than 2 consecutive min cuts the rate by 50%; persisting over 5 min stops the infusion. BIS dropout over 2 min: rate changes only on RR/SpO2. Artifact: previous valid value held.
- Weight, height, age, sex and ASA are entered at start; the paper does not say how they enter the dose.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: BIS held near 65. No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, EC50, hysteresis, MEAC or potency ratio is reported. (Not an opioid paper; the opioid audit does not apply.)
- Clinical control bands: Excellent within 10% of 65, Good 11-20%, Poor 21-30%, Inadequate above 30%.
- Results: Excellent 49% (HSS) vs 26% (control) of sedation time, P<0.0001; Poor 11 vs 20%, Inadequate 10 vs 18% (abstract, PDF p1). The Good category value is not printed in the text; it appears in Fig. 5 only.
- Table 2 (PDF p7, page image): % of sedation time, HSS vs control: BIS<55 14.0 +/- 9.3 vs 21.8 +/- 20.2 (P 0.003); BIS<60 23.2 +/- 11.6 vs 29.0 +/- 23.2 (P 0.059); BIS>75 16.6 +/- 9.9 vs 31.8 +/- 24.5; BIS>80 7.1 +/- 6.7 vs 14.8 +/- 15.6 (both P<0.0001).
- Table 3 (PDF p7): MDPE 2.4 +/- 5.0 vs 4.9 +/- 13.4 % (P 0.13); MDAPE 10.8 +/- 4.0 vs 18.0 +/- 5.4 %; Wobble 9.4 +/- 3.6 vs 11.7 +/- 5.2 %; Divergence -0.11 +/- 0.13 vs -0.06 +/- 0.11 %/min; GPI 131.9 +/- 151.3 vs 23.4 +/- 23.2.
- Table 4 (PDF p8): median dose/h 87.1 +/- 38.5 vs 68.7 +/- 22.3 (labelled mg); dose changes/h 22.2 +/- 4.6 vs 4.7 +/- 2.9; minutes awake per anesthesia hour 4.7 +/- 10.0 vs 13.7 +/- 13.8.
- Authors cite Glass 1997 for BIS 63 (95% CI 62-65) at 50% loss of consciousness and BIS 51 at 95% (discussion, PDF p8); this is their rationale for target 65, not their own measurement.

## Covariates
None analysed. Demographics are only compared between arms.

## Caveats
- Unblinded; possible Hawthorne effect in the control arm; non-morbidly-obese patients only (authors, PDF p8-9).
- Control arm did not see trends, so manual performance may understate routine practice.
- No drug concentrations, so nothing here validates any PK model.
- Dose unit in Table 4 conflicts with how the companion paper cites it (see open question).
