# Iisalo 1977: Clinical pharmacokinetics of digoxin (review)

## Study
Narrative review, no new data. Covers absorption, protein binding, distribution, renal and extrarenal elimination, metabolites, PK-PD relations, special populations (pregnancy, children, renal, heart failure, obesity, elderly, thyroid, liver), interactions, intoxication and serum-level monitoring. Tables compile other authors' data (Table I PK parameters, Table II urinary recovery after IV, Table III urinary excretion at steady state, Table IV renal clearances).

## Model and parameters
Summary and text (PDF/printed pages coincide):
- Oral bioavailability 70-80% from the best formulation (summary p1; p2). About two-thirds absorbed within 6 h after an oral solution (Andersson 1975b; p2).
- Serum albumin binding 20-30% (summary p1); individual studies 1-10% (Doherty, gel filtration), 20-30% (Evered), 23% and 21% (equilibrium dialysis), 30-40% (ultrafiltration) (p2). Plasma ultrafiltrate concentration 60-70% of total (p5).
- At least two compartments; three preferred by Kramer 1974 and Sumner 1976 (p3). Initial dilution half-life about 2 min (p3).
- Elimination half-life in healthy subjects 26-45 h (summary; p3, Table I).
- IV dose recovered in urine 57-80% over 6-12 days in people without renal failure (p3, Table II). 95-98% of urinary digoxin unchanged (Doherty and Kane 1975; p5).
- Nonrenal elimination 25-28% (p6), from Koup 1975 body clearance 188 +/- 44 mL/min/1.73 m2 vs renal clearance 141 +/- 41 mL/min/1.73 m2 (p5-6) and Sumner 1976. Renal fraction 72-75% (derived: 1 - 0.25..0.28). Biliary excretion up to 30% of a dose (summary; p6, Caldwell and Cline).
- Therapeutic serum range 0.5-2.0 ng/mL (p12).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio. Reported qualitatively (p7-8): left ventricular ejection time index tracks the tissue (peripheral) compartment, not plasma, during distribution, with maximum effect 4-6 h after an IV dose (Reuning 1973; Weissler 1972). Chronotropic (ventricular rate in AF) correlation with serum concentration is weak or disputed. Transient nausea 1-3 h after an oral dose suggests peak serum level relates to extracardiac toxicity. Therapeutic serum range 0.5-2.0 ng/mL; toxic and non-toxic levels overlap (p12).

## Covariates
Qualitative: renal function (renal clearance tracks creatinine clearance, Table IV; body clearance linear in creatinine clearance, Koup 1975/1976); smaller Vd in severe renal impairment; lean body mass better than total weight in obesity (Ewy 1971); thyroid state; elderly. Pregnancy (p8-9): only placental transfer data (fetal/maternal serum 0.7/1.3 ng/mL at 12-16 weeks, Allonen 1976); no maternal PK change reported.

## Caveats
- A 1977 review: all numbers are secondary, many from radiolabel or early RIA assays, and the compiled tables mix models (2- and 3-compartment), populations and units.
- Table I header misprints the units of the half-life columns (h-1 for hours).
- The nonrenal fraction is an inference from two cited studies, not a measurement by the author; the population behind Koup 1975's 188/141 values is not stated here.
