# Buck 1989: atenolol PK/PD in children

## Study
Ten children aged 4-16 y (mean 8.9 +/- 4.5 y; Table I, PDF p2) with known or suspected arrhythmias, undergoing cardiac electrophysiologic (EP) studies at the Medical University of South Carolina. Sex from Table I: 6 M, 4 F (derived: count). Weight is not reported. Exclusions: heart failure, airway disease, diabetes, recurrent hypoglycaemia, renal (CrCl < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2) or hepatic dysfunction.
Dose: atenolol 0.1 mg/kg IV infused over 5 min through a central line. Blood from a peripheral catheter before and at 5, 15, 30 min and 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24 h. The 24 h samples of the first three patients were below the 0.02 mcg/mL limit; later patients had no 24 h sample. Plasma assay by reverse-phase HPLC, limit 0.02 mcg/mL, linear 0.02-0.50 mcg/mL, CV 3.22% within-day and 8.93% between-day. Fitting with ADAPT, residual weight = inverse assay variance; two-compartment chosen by Akaike criterion. Responders (3 of 10) were given 1.0 mg/kg PO once daily from 24 h later (oral PK not reported).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak; no concentration-effect model; no effect-site hysteresis analysis. Effects were measured only before and after the dose (paired t test), Table III (PDF p3), mean +/- SD in ms, before then after:
- Sinus cycle length 743 +/- 110 to 831 +/- 148 (n 10, p < 0.01)
- AH conduction 87 +/- 32 to 92 +/- 37 (NS); HV conduction 40 +/- 17 to 43 +/- 15 (n 10, p < 0.05)
- Max CSNRT 380 +/- 151 to 558 +/- 503 (NS); SACT 158 +/- 46 to 184 +/- 61 (n 8, NS); AERP 189 +/- 30 to 185 +/- 39 (n 8, NS); WP 213 +/- 34 to 195 +/- 34 (n 6, NS); AV 2:1 240 +/- 45 to 213 +/- 35 (n 8, NS).
Heart rate and blood pressure stayed clinically stable. The authors judge 0.1 mg/kg a suboptimal IV dose for EP evaluation. No therapeutic or toxic plasma concentration range is given. Opioid audit: not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0 or equianalgesic ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. The authors observe that the children over 10 y (patients 1, 5, 9: 16, 16, 13 y) had t1/2 beta above 5 h and those under 10 y below 5 h (Table II vs Table I); this is an observation, not a fitted covariate. Their terminal half-life is stated as slightly shorter than in 12 adults (6.06 +/- 2.02 h, Mason 1979, quoted); other parameters are similar to adult values (comparator: adults in that cited study, not stanpumpR).

## Caveats
n = 10, wide age range (4-16 y), no weights, children with arrhythmias; the IV dose was low; sampling to 12 h (24 h mostly below the assay limit) so the terminal phase is defined by few sub-0.05 mcg/mL points; no oral or renal-excretion data; no estimate of between-subject variability beyond the SD of individual fits. Abstract and Table II disagree on the SD of CL (see open question).
