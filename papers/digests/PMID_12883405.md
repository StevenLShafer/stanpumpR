# Minto 2003: the tPeak method for linking PK and PD

## Study
Theoretical/simulation paper (no new subjects). Proposal: instead of carrying a published ke0 to a different
PK model ("naive" method), compute tPeak (time of maximum Ce after an IV bolus into a drug-free system) from the
PK-PD study (or use an observed tPeak) and solve for the ke0 that reproduces that tPeak with the new PK. Three
tests: thiopental (Stanski & Maitre 1990 vs Shanks 1993), remifentanil (100 simulated subjects from Minto 1997;
crude one-compartment PK-PD trial sampled at 0, 5-60 min), propofol (Marsh PK vs Schnider 1998/1999 PK-PD).
Excel Solver. Appendix proves existence/uniqueness and gives brackets.

## Model and parameters
- Cp(t) = sum Ai e^(-lambda_i t) (eq 1); Ce(t) = sum ke0 Ai/(ke0 - lambda_i) (e^(-lambda_i t) - e^(-ke0 t)) (eq 2);
  tPeak solves dCe/dt = 0 (eq 3/5). Direct-effect, instantaneous-mixing assumptions.
- Theorem 1: tPeak exists and is unique for given ke0. Theorem 3: unique ke0 for given tPeak; tPeak decreases
  as ke0 increases.
- Theorem 2 bracket: (ln ke0 - ln lambda_1)/(ke0 - lambda_1) <= tPeak <= (ln ke0 - ln lambda_n)/(ke0 - lambda_n).
- Theorem 5 bound: ke0 <= max(1/tPeak, lambda_n + 1/(lambda_n tPeak^2)).
- Unit disposition functions used (ug/mL per 1 mg bolus, t in min, p326-327):
  thiopental Stanski-Maitre (35 y, 70 kg) 0.164e^-0.688t + 0.0135e^-0.0191t + 0.00335e^-0.000904t;
  Shanks 0.28e^-1.704t + 0.0127e^-0.141t + 0.0158e^-0.0181t + 0.00375e^-0.00165t;
  propofol Schnider (35 y, 70 kg, 178 cm man) 0.227e^-1.03t + 0.00623e^-0.0391t + 0.0005e^-0.00233t;
  Marsh (70 kg) 0.0566e^-0.299t + 0.0056e^-0.0301t + 0.00036e^-0.00239t.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Thiopental: 95% spectral edge (Stanski-Maitre) vs EEG burst suppression and loss of motor power (Shanks).
Remifentanil peak effect about 1-2 min after bolus. Propofol: naive Schnider ke0 with Marsh PK gives Ce too low
and tPeak almost twice Schnider's; tPeak method much closer. Discussion (PDF p7, printed p330, second caveat):
the method assumes direct-effect models; for indirect-response end points (e.g. ventilatory depression) the
embedded direct-effect model has a *calculated* tPeak, which can be carried to a new PK model to solve ke0 (its
direct effect is almost never observed). Separately, investigators should still record the *observed* time of
peak effect (e.g. peak ventilatory depression) as a model-independent measure that any derived PK-PD model should
predict. The paper does not use the observed peak of an indirect response as the target for solving ke0.
Elsewhere (p330, first column) a directly observed tPeak after a submaximal bolus is called the model-independent
gold standard for direct effects. STANPUMP and RUGLOOP had used tPeak for ~10 y.
No MEAC or potency ratios (methods paper).

## Covariates
None (method). Each covariate-specific PK set needs its own ke0 solution.

## Caveats
Assumes unimodal Ce (recirculatory models may produce multiple plasma peaks), direct-effect link, IV bolus into a
drug-free system. Simulations, not new data (prospective support cited: Struys 2000).
