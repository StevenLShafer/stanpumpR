# Popovic 2007: ceftriaxone, cefazolin and gentamicin at caesarean vs non-pregnant surgery

## Study
Open, controlled, prospective, comparative study, Novi Sad, Serbia, April 2003 to April 2005 (p.2). Women aged
19-40 under general anaesthesia: 54 at caesarean section (completed pregnancy, gestational age confirmed by
early ultrasound; pre-eclampsia, diabetes, hypertension excluded) and 12 non-pregnant women at gynaecological
surgery; each randomly given one prophylactic IV dose over 2-3 min at induction, 10 min before incision (p.3):
- ceftriaxone 2 g: 18 caesarean, 4 non-pregnant
- cefazolin 2 g: 18 caesarean, 4 non-pregnant
- gentamicin 4 mg/kg (twice the usual 2 mg/kg prophylactic dose): 18 caesarean, 4 non-pregnant
Two maternal plasma samples: immediately after the operation (about 1 h after the dose, varying between
individuals) and 6 h after the dose; amniotic fluid at section; cord plasma at delivery. Assays: HPLC-UV
(274 nm) for both cephalosporins, 0.005-120 mg/L, CV < 9.02%; fluorescence polarisation immunoassay (TDx/FLx)
for gentamicin, sensitivity 0.27 mg/L, CV < 10% (p.3). One-compartment analysis in KINETICA 4.0; Student t-test.
Weights (Table 1, image p.5): caesarean 85 +/- 14, 74 +/- 10, 81 +/- 10 kg; non-pregnant 65 +/- 7, 70 +/- 8,
66 +/- 16 kg (ceftriaxone, cefazolin, gentamicin groups).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis. The authors state that at 6 h only cefazolin still exceeded the MIC for
sensitive bacteria in both groups, and gentamicin was below it in pregnant women (MIC values not given).
Infection complications: ceftriaxone 4.5%, cefazolin 4.5%, gentamicin 9.1% (p.5; the Discussion says "about
6%" per group). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (caesarean vs non-pregnant) only. Blood loss estimated directly and from the haemoglobin fall (Flordal
equation, Nadler blood volume); calculated losses did not differ between groups (Table 1).

## Caveats
- Two plasma samples per woman; the one-compartment ke rests on those two points, the first taken at a variable
  time near 1 h after the dose.
- Control groups of 4 per drug; the authors call the results preliminary (p.5). Different patients, not paired;
  groups differ in weight by 15-20 kg.
- Ceftriaxone values are implausible against the literature the paper itself cites (Vd 5.8-15.5 L, CL
  1.18-1.29 L/h, p.1-2): tabulated Vd 190 and 234 L and CL 24 and 48 L/h, with SDs as large as the means, and a
  measured 22.75 mg/L about 1 h after 2 g. The cause is not stated; the measured analyte (total vs unbound) is
  not specified.
- Cefazolin t1/2 6.3-8.3 h is about four times the 1.4-1.8 h the paper cites; gentamicin Vd 0.11 L/kg (caesarean,
  derived 9.05/81) is below the 0.25 L/kg it cites.
- Concentrations are compared despite larger absolute gentamicin doses in the heavier pregnant women.
