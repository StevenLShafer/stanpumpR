# van Steveninck 1994 (part I): fast vs slow IV infusion to pseudo steady state

## Study
- Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled 3-way crossover; 9 healthy non-smokers (4 men, 5 women),
  18-24 y; mean weight 66 ± 11 kg (Table I).
- Prestudy: 0.4 mg/kg IV over up to 30 min (one stopped at 24 min; total 26 ± 5 mg) to fit each subject's
  two-compartment PK (SIPHAR, weight 1/y²) — the same infusions reported in part II.
- Main study, 1 week apart: (1) fast — linear rise to target in 30 min, then computer-controlled
  exponentially declining infusion holding a pseudo steady state for 120 min; (2) slow — target reached in
  120 min, then 120 min plateau; (3) vehicle (fast schedule). IV vehicle 60% PEG 400 with 5% glucose run at
  three times the rate.
- Venous plasma (opposite arm) to 480 min, HPLC. Free fraction by ultrafiltration at onset of plateau (20 °C),
  and at 30-120 min after fast infusion (37 °C). Free fatty acids measured.
- End points: saccadic peak velocity and latency; EEG beta (12.5-30 Hz, Fz-Cz) and alpha (7.5-12.5 Hz, Pz-Oz)
  amplitudes; BP, HR. Statistics on 8 evaluable subjects.

## Model and parameters
No population PK model reported (individual two-compartment fits used only to drive the pumps).
- Target concentrations: 597 ± 123 ng/mL (individual 325-750), chosen to give about 60% of the maximal
  saccadic-velocity fall seen in the prestudy infusion (maximum observed fall 38 ± 6%).
- Doses (Table I, mean ± SD): linear phase 14.5 ± 3.5 mg fast, 19.1 ± 4.6 mg slow; exponential phase
  11.5 ± 4.5 mg each.
- Rate of rise: 21 ± 4 ng/mL/min fast, 5 ± 1 ng/mL/min slow.
- Plateau concentrations (Table II): 639 ± 132 ng/mL fast, 629 ± 133 ng/mL slow (NS); deviation from target
  15 ± 9% and 12 ± 11%.
- Free fraction at plateau onset: 3.6 ± 2.1% fast (30 min) vs 1.9 ± 0.5% slow (120 min), difference -44%
  (95% CI -19 to -61%); after fast infusion it fell from 3.8 ± 1.2% at 30 min to 2.8 ± 1.2% at 120 min.
  Free fatty acids 54% lower at onset of the slow plateau.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- At target: mean fall in saccadic peak velocity 20% and rise in EEG beta 78% from baseline.
- Plateau effects vs placebo: peak velocity -24 ± 10% fast, -25 ± 11% slow; EEG beta +63 ± 47% fast,
  +46 ± 41% slow; beta larger after fast infusion for the first 30 min of plateau (4.5 vs 3.6 µV/Hz).
- Effects declined slowly during the plateau (all parameters after fast; velocity and latency after slow);
  authors consider acute tolerance or the change in protein binding.
- **No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or EC50 estimated.** The authors argue that arteriovenous equilibration and
  distribution to the effect site are unlikely to explain the changes *during the pseudo steady-state
  intervals*; this is a statement about the slow decline, not a test for an equilibration delay.
- Part I does use concentration-effect information, for target selection only (Methods and Results, PDF p.4,
  printed p.538): each subject's prestudy plasma concentration vs saccadic peak velocity plot set a target at
  about 60% of that subject's maximal observed velocity fall (maximal fall 38 ± 6%); mean target 597 ± 123
  ng/mL (mean ± SD). The plots are not shown (Part I's figures are time courses), and no ke0, EC50 or other
  fitted concentration-effect parameter comes from them. The formal concentration-effect analysis and its
  reproducibility are in part II (ref. 11).

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Healthy young volunteers; venous sampling; research IV formulation; 8 evaluable subjects; the protein-binding
explanation is a hypothesis (in-vitro binding vs effect-site concentration uncertain, per the authors).
