# Rudd 2015: possible rivaroxaban failure postpartum (case report)

## Study
Single case report, no pharmacokinetic measurement. A 35-year-old white woman, gravida 2 para 2, 23 days after
a normal vaginal delivery (PDF p3, printed e166), with hypothyroidism and obesity; she had stopped breastfeeding
about 1 week earlier. Left popliteal/posterior tibial DVT was diagnosed 5 days before presentation and
rivaroxaban 15 mg PO every 12 h, taken with food, was started (PDF p2, e165). She presented with multiple
segmental PE on CT angiogram, without right heart strain. Last dose 8 AM on the day of presentation; pill count
showed 100% adherence (PDF p3). Rivaroxaban was stopped; heparin infusion, then enoxaparin 1 mg/kg (90 mg) SC
q12h bridged to warfarin (weight about 90 kg, derived: 90 mg / 1 mg/kg; weight not stated directly).

Table 2 (PDF p3, ED labs, confirmed on page image): WBC 12.8 x10^3/mm3, Hb 11.0 g/dl, hematocrit printed
11.0% (normal 34-46%; almost certainly a misprint, as Hb 11.0 g/dl implies roughly 33%), platelets 874 x10^3/mm3,
serum creatinine 1.0 mg/dl, BUN 11 mg/dl, AST 36 U/l, ALT 37 U/l, TSH 0.29 uIU/ml, free T4 1.7 ng/dl,
albumin 3.5 g/dl. Thrombophilia work-up (factor X chromogenic activity, AT III, protein C and S, prothrombin
gene, factor V Leiden, lupus anticoagulant, cardiolipin antibodies, JAK2) was normal/negative.

No rivaroxaban plasma concentration, anti-Xa level, PT, or other drug-exposure measurement was made.

## Model and parameters
No model. Background rivaroxaban properties the authors quote from the Xarelto label and their own review
(refs 12, 13), not measured here (PDF p3-4, printed e166-e167; Table 3 confirmed on page image):
- peak concentration about 2-4 h after ingestion; half-life 5-9 h in healthy subjects aged 20-45 with normal
  renal function
- protein binding to albumin 92-95%
- Table 3 "Renal clearance": printed as "33% active drug, 66% inactive drug"; hepatic clearance by CYP3A4,
  CYP3A5, CYP2J2
- Table 3 "Postulated alterations" in pregnancy: protein binding unknown; renal clearance increased; CYP3A4
  increased; CYP3A5 and CYP2J2 unknown. These are postulates, not observations.
- Standard VTE dosing: 15 mg PO twice daily for 21 days, then 20 mg once daily with food.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is clinical (new PE on CTA after 5 days of treatment). No concentration-effect data, no therapeutic
range. Event-rate context (PDF p4): EINSTEIN-DVT included six puerperal patients (0.3%) with no subgroup data;
the authors convert the trial's first-21-day recurrence (21 patients, 1.2%) to 0.003 per 5-day period, and
treated-VTE PE risk (6.3 per 100 patient-years, ref 14) to 0.009 per 5-day period.

## Covariates
None analysed. The authors' background (PDF p2): GFR up to 50% higher in pregnancy, plasma volume up to 50%
higher, CYP3A4/2D6/2C activity increased; hypercoagulability persists 6-8 weeks after delivery.

## Caveats
- n = 1; causality is explicitly uncertain: the authors themselves weigh treatment failure against the natural
  course of the original DVT, and call PE extension unexpected but not impossible.
- The postpartum-PK explanation ("decreased drug exposure") is a hypothesis; no exposure was measured.
- The patient was 23 days postpartum, not antepartum, and obese (~90 kg derived); obesity is itself a
  confounder for DOAC exposure that the authors do not analyse.
- Hematocrit 11.0% in Table 2 is a probable misprint (see Study).
