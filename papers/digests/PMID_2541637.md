# Schwinn 1989: time course of the haemodynamic response to bolus phenylephrine

## Study
- Design: randomised IV bolus doses of (-)-phenylephrine HCl (Winthrop), 50, 100, 150 or 200 ug; 50 boluses in
  18 patients under anaesthesia for elective CABG (PDF p.2, printed 572). The 150 and 200 ug arms were added
  mid-study, so groups are unequal (Methods: n = 24, 17, 3, 6 boluses; Table 2 footnote: N = 14, 12, 3, 5).
- Population (Table 1, PDF p.3, mean +/- SD): age 63.2 +/- 5.6 y; EF 50.8 +/- 8.3% (range 32-63%);
  hypertension 14/18, diabetes 5/18, previous MI 11/18, CHF 0/18; baseline MAP 74.4 +/- 7.8 mmHg, CVP 9.6 +/- 3.3,
  HR 55.6 +/- 18.8, CO 3.9 +/- 1.6 L/min, SVR 1511 +/- 640 dyn.s.cm-5; beta-blockers 13/18, calcium-channel
  blockers 12/18, nitrates 14/18. Anaesthesia: enflurane/fentanyl 3, fentanyl 4, isoflurane 9, sufentanil 2.
  Weight and sex not reported.
- Injection: centrally, through the sideport of a PA-catheter introducer sheath (PDF p.2). Repeat trials 10 min
  apart after return to baseline, CO re-calibrated by thermodilution each trial.
- Measurement: radial arterial MAP (monitor updated every 2 s); CO by esophageal Doppler (Lawrence L3000) calibrated
  to thermodilution; SVR = 80 (MAP - RAP)/CO. MAP, HR, CO and SVR recorded every 5 s for 2 min.
- Analysis: repeated-measures ANOVA, Mann-Whitney U, Bonferroni-corrected t tests. No drug concentrations measured;
  no PK or PD model fitted.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Times to peak (all doses pooled; mean +/- SEM; no significant difference between dose groups), PDF p.3-5:
- Peak MAP: **41.6 +/- 1.4 s** after injection (PDF p.3, printed 573).
- Trough CO: **42.5 +/- 1.7 s** (PDF p.4, printed 574).
- Peak SVR: **41.2 +/- 1.4 s** (PDF p.5, printed 575).
- Nadir HR: **49.0 +/- 3.4 s**, significantly later than MAP and SVR (PDF p.5, Fig. 2).
- The authors summarise: peak MAP, peak SVR and trough CO were simultaneous at about 42 s (abstract; Discussion, PDF p.6).
- Time resolution is the 5-s recording interval.

Peak changes from baseline (Table 2, PDF p.4, mean +/- SEM; the abstract quotes these maxima):

| Dose | max dMAP (mmHg) | max dSVR (dyn.s.cm-5) | max dCO (L/min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 ug | +11.6 +/- 2.1 (40 s) | +766 +/- 190 (50 s) | -0.58 +/- 0.11 (40 s) |
| 100 ug | +15.6 +/- 2.4 (45 s; also 15.6 +/- 2.6 at 50 s) | +930 +/- 310 (55 s) | -0.71 +/- 0.18 (55 s) |
| 150 ug | +14.7 +/- 2.4 (40 s) | +950 +/- 344 (40 s) | -0.73 +/- 0.20 (40 s) |
| 200 ug | +18.0 +/- 1.5 (50 s) | +1732 +/- 824 (55 s) | -0.77 +/- 0.18 (55 s) |

(The abstract's CO maxima list -.68 for 100 ug, which is Table 2's 35-40 s value; the table's largest 100 ug value
is -.71 at 55 s.) Effects were still significant at 115 s for 100 and 200 ug MAP. No EC50, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
No equations. Reported associations (PDF p.5): hypertension -> smaller MAP/SVR/CO changes at the lowest dose; age
negatively correlated with peak SVR at 50 ug; EF positively correlated with peak MAP and SVR at 100 ug; low
baseline CO -> larger fall in CO; high baseline SVR -> larger SVR rise and CO fall. High-dose fentanyl (>50 ug/kg)
and low-dose fentanyl with enflurane were associated with larger dMAP and dSVR.

## Caveats
- Anaesthetised, mostly beta-blocked cardiac-surgical patients; the 42 s is a pooled observation, not a fitted ke0.
- Central (PA introducer) injection; a peripheral injection would add venous transit time.
- 5-s sampling; small 150 and 200 ug groups (3 and 5 or 6 trials).
- Inconsistent trial counts between Methods and Table 2 (see open questions).
- Esophageal Doppler CO may be biased by pressor-induced aortic dilation (authors discuss, PDF p.6).
