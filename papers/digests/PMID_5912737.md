# Severinghaus 1966: Blood gas calculator

## Study
A methods/derivation paper, not a clinical study. It describes a slide rule (Fig. 1, Table 1) that solves blood-gas problems: the whole-blood O2 dissociation curve of adult man with temperature, pH and base excess; anaerobic temperature correction of PO2, PCO2 and pH; true O2 fraction from mixed expired gas; ATPS to BTPS/STPD volume correction; the Henderson-Hasselbalch relation (plasma 10-40 C, CSF 20-40 C); and base excess/standard bicarbonate at 37 C. The new standard O2 dissociation curve (man, 37 C, pH 7.40) is built from pooled data of many authors (Bartels 1961, Roughton, Astrup, Darling, Lambertsen, Naeraa, Nahas, Lundgren and the author's own samples; Fig. 2). Software: none (graphical slide rule).

## Model and parameters
Standard dissociation curve is tabulated (Table 2, PDF p4, printed p1111), not given as an equation (the later closed-form equation is in the 1979 paper, not here). Selected points, PO2 in mm Hg at pH 7.40, 37 C: HbO2 1% = 1.9; 10% = 10.3; 20% = 15.4; 30% = 19.2; 40% = 22.8; 50% = 26.6; 60% = 31.2; 70% = 36.9; 80% = 44.5; 90% = 57.8; 95% = 74.2; 97% = 91.6; 98% = 111; 99% = 159; 99.5% = 225; 99.95% = 700. Other equations: true O2 fraction VO2/VE = 0.265 - 0.265 FECO2 - 1.265 FEO2 (eq 7, PDF p6); Henderson-Hasselbalch with pK' and S taken from tables as functions of T and pH; Hb-O2 curve for full-term newborn at 37 C, pH 7.4 approximated by the adult curve at pH 7.6 (add 0.2 pH unit).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not a drug paper; no drug pharmacodynamics, ke0, MEAC or potency ratio. Not an opioid paper. The physiological end point is the O2-Hb affinity (PO2 at constant saturation) as a function of temperature, pH, PCO2 and base excess.

## Covariates
Temperature (0-45 C), pH (6.6-8.0), base excess (-25 to +25 mEq/L), PCO2, haemoglobin (base-excess scales); newborn offset of +0.2 pH unit. Normal variation of the curve is called considerable at half saturation and minimal at high saturation (PDF p4); carboxyhaemoglobin lowers PO2 at any saturation, so smokers' blood shifts the curve.

## Caveats
Equation 5 fails above about 90% saturation because dissolved-O2 exchange matters; use Fig. 4 (dlogPO2/dT as a function of PO2 at 37 C, curve 0.0125S + 4.63 over S + 66.1, with S the reciprocal slope of the dissociation curve). Second-order pH and base terms are ignored on the slide rule, giving about 12% error in PCO2 correction at pH 7.0. Temperature coefficient not studied adequately below 15 C. The author notes the Bohr shift magnitude with fixed acid (Astrup -0.50) is an unresolved discrepancy against Naeraa's data. Several data sources are personal communications.
