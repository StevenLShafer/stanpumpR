# Kamme 1993: MIC and MBC of loracarbef, cefadroxil, cefaclor and penicillin V for group A streptococci

## Study
In-vitro susceptibility study (Lund, Sweden). 50 group A streptococcal throat isolates. Drugs: loracarbef,
cefaclor, cefadroxil, penicillin V. **Cefalexin was not tested.** Two methods, each on log-phase and
stationary-phase cultures in Todd-Hewitt broth (PDF p4, printed p39):
- **Plate (agar) dilution**: ~10^5 CFU spotted onto horse-blood agar with 2-fold drug dilutions; MIC read at 20 h
  (no visible colonies). Drug then inactivated with beta-lactamase injected under the agar (Bacto Penase for
  penicillin V; B. cereus type I and II beta-lactamases for the other three); MBC read at 24 and 48 h = lowest
  concentration allowing no more than 50 colonies, i.e. 99.95% kill.
- **Broth dilution**: ~10^5 CFU/mL in 2 mL; MIC at 20 h; 10 uL subcultured onto beta-lactamase plates; MBC = no
  growth or no more than 2-3 colonies, i.e. 99.9% kill.
MBCs at 24 and 48 h were identical (p39-40).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetics. In-vitro results (all mg/L, concentrations in broth/agar, protein-free apart from blood agar):
- Plate MICs of loracarbef, cefaclor and cefadroxil 0.06-0.25 mg/L; penicillin V 0.004-0.008 mg/L; the three
  being 16-32 times penicillin's (PDF p4, printed p39; Fig. 1, PDF p3). Same in log and stationary phase.
- Plate, log phase: MBC no more than one dilution above the MIC for every agent (MBC/MIC <= 2; abstract, p39).
- Table I (PDF p3, printed p38), % of 50 strains with MBC/MIC >= 32:

| Drug | Plate log | Plate stationary | Broth log | Broth stationary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Penicillin V | 0 | 18 | 0 | 30 |
| Cefaclor | 0 | 44 | 24 (a) | 76 (a) |
| Loracarbef | 0 | 34 | 2 | 62 |
| Cefadroxil | 0 | 38 | 0 | 72 |

(a) cefaclor: MBC/MIC >= 16 in another 2% (log) and 18% (stationary), MICs 1 mg/L, growth on the highest
concentration tested (8 mg/L).
- Text inconsistencies in the paper itself: Results give 20% for penicillin V plate-stationary (Table I: 18);
  Discussion gives 30% for cefaclor broth-log (Table I: 24). Table values are recorded above.
- Broth MICs: same in both phases for penicillin V, loracarbef and cefadroxil; cefaclor mostly one step higher
  in stationary phase (p40). Broth MICs slightly higher than plate MICs (p41).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points are in-vitro inhibition (MIC) and 99.95% (plate) or 99.9% (broth) kill (MBC). Tolerance
(MBC/MIC >= 32) is mainly a stationary-phase phenomenon and tended to occur in the same strains across drugs
(p39, p41). No PK/PD target, no ke0, no clinical end point. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Growth phase (log vs stationary) and method (plate vs broth); strain (the authors propose tolerance is partly an
intrinsic strain property).

## Caveats
- Cefalexin not tested: any use for cefalexin is by class analogy (cefadroxil is the closest, a
  para-hydroxy cefalexin; cefaclor also a first-generation-like oral cephalosporin).
- 50 isolates from one Swedish centre, early 1990s; doubling-dilution resolution; MIC50/MIC90 and MBC50/MBC90 not
  tabulated (only readable from Fig. 1 curves).
- Plate MBC defined at 99.95% kill, not the conventional 99.9%.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Row content** (`R/antibioticMbc.R:174-182`): MIC90 = 2, MBC90 = 4 mg/L, Basis "MIC x 2 (class analogues)". The
  MIC90 of 2 is from Zhou 2013/Thornhill 1969, not this paper; this paper's cephalosporin MICs (0.06-0.25 mg/L,
  cefadroxil and cefaclor) are lower, which is expected for a different drug and is not used by the row.
- **"MBC/MIC <= 2 for log-phase ... cefadroxil and cefaclor"**: matches for the plate dilution method (abstract;
  Fig. 1A; p39). For broth dilution, cefaclor does not support it (24% of log-phase strains >= 32, Table I); cefadroxil
  does (0%). The repo string omits the method; recorded as an open question. **partly-verified**.
- **Note "stationary-phase cultures were often tolerant"**: matches (34-44% plate, 62-76% broth for the three
  cephalosporin-class drugs, Table I).
- Units: mg/L throughout in both paper and repo; MBCs are in-vitro (free) concentrations, consistent with the
  file's header rule. No transcription error found.
- The paper reports no PK, so there is nothing to compare with the cefalexin PK model.
