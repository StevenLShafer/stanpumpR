# Sica 2005: clinical pharmacokinetics of losartan (review)

## Study
Narrative review (no new data) of losartan and its active metabolite E 3174: physicochemistry, assay, absorption,
distribution, binding, metabolism, excretion, special populations, interactions, pharmacodynamics, dosing and
adverse effects. 134 references. No funding; authors declare no relevant conflicts (p. 810).

## Model and parameters
No model. Values quoted from the cited primary studies (printed page = PDF page + 796):

- Bioavailability (oral 50 mg tablet) 32.6% geometric mean (range 12.1-66.6%); 38.9% with a 100 mg
  14C-labelled solution; 35.5% (90% CI 29.3-43.0) in NYHA II-IV heart failure after multiple doses (p. 800).
  Food lowers AUC of losartan and E 3174 by about 10% (p. 800; Table I).
- tmax losartan 1.0 ± 0.5 h (SD) with 50 mg (p. 800); tmax losartan 1 h, E 3174 3-5 h (p. 802).
- Vd losartan 34.4 ± 17.9 L, E 3174 10.3 ± 1.1 L; 33.6 ± 23.1 L in heart failure (p. 800).
- Protein binding 98.6-98.8% (losartan), 99.7% (E 3174), mainly albumin, over 0.5-5.0 μg/mL (p. 801).
- About 14% of an oral dose converted to E 3174; E 3174 is 10- to 40-fold more potent than losartan (p. 801).
  Metabolism by CYP2C9 (dominant at clinical concentrations) and CYP3A4.
- Total plasma clearance about 600 mL/min (losartan) and 50 mL/min (E 3174); renal clearance about 75 and
  25 mL/min (p. 802). Heart failure IV: CL 566 ± 180 mL/min, Vd 34 ± 23 L, t½β 1.5 ± 0.9 h, CLR 48 mL/min
  (p. 803).
- Terminal half-life 1.5-2.5 h (losartan), 6-9 h (E 3174) (p. 802). Linear and dose-proportional.
- Table II (p. 803, Caucasian vs Japanese volunteers, from text layer): losartan Cmax 0.29 vs 0.19 μg/L, tmax 1.0 vs
  1.3 h, AUC 0.48 vs 0.35 μg·h/L, t½β 2.1 vs 1.7 h, CLR 4.3 vs 5.6 L/h; E 3174 t½β 6.4 vs 3.75 h. The printed
  unit μg/L for Cmax (0.29) is implausible against the 200-250 ng/mL peak given on p. 801 (probably mg/L); not used by
  stanpumpR, not checked on the page image.
- Table I (p. 800) bioavailability of other ARBs: candesartan cilexetil 15%, eprosartan 6-29%, irbesartan 60-80%,
  losartan 33%, olmesartan 26%, telmisartan 42-58%, valsartan 25% (food lowers AUC about 50%).
- Hepatic cirrhosis: CL about 50% lower, oral bioavailability about 2-fold higher, plasma concentrations 4- to 5-fold
  higher (p. 802).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. No PK-PD model: the authors state there is little published information to build one (p. 806).
Time to peak blood-pressure effect about 6 h (p. 802). Threshold dose for angiotensin-II blockade 10-20 mg; no
added blockade above 80 mg orally (pp. 806-807). Trough blockade of angiotensin-II pressor response 35 ± 16%
(100 mg once daily), 54 ± 14% (200 mg once daily), 77 ± 8% (100 mg twice daily) (p. 807). Dosing: start 50 mg/day
(25 mg if hypovolaemic or hepatic impairment), maximum 100 mg/day (p. 807-808). No therapeutic concentration range.

## Pregnancy (the part stanpumpR uses)
Section 1.10 (PDF p. 6, printed 802, read on the rendered page): losartan, like all ACE inhibitors and ARBs,
should be avoided in pregnancy; no effect on fetal development early in pregnancy; in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters
often associated with serious fetal toxicity: hypotension, neonatal skull hypoplasia, anuria, reversible or
irreversible renal failure and death; oligohydramnios with limb contractures, craniofacial deformation and lung
hypoplasia. Losartan and E 3174 appear in rat milk; human milk not studied. The review gives no pregnancy PK data and
uses "avoided" / "not at all during pregnancy", not the word "contraindicated".

## Covariates
Review-level only: no clinically significant age, sex or race effect (women had 2-fold higher losartan
concentrations; elderly 30% higher E 3174); hepatic impairment as above; no adjustment in renal failure; not
dialysed (pp. 802-804, 808).

## Caveats
Narrative review: numbers are second-hand. The class pregnancy statement is about losartan and ARBs generally,
citing developmental-toxicology studies (refs 57-60).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Class contraindication and fetal toxicities** (all seven rows): the rows list oligohydramnios, renal failure,
  skull hypoplasia and death, strongest in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters. Paper p. 802 lists exactly these and says
  early pregnancy is unaffected. **Matches.** ("Contraindicated" is a fair paraphrase of "avoided ... not at all
  during pregnancy".)
- **azilsartan (csv:163)** notes say the statement is about losartan and the ARB class: correct. **Matches.**
- **lisinopril, benazepril, enalapril, fosinopril (csv:164, 167-169)**: ACE inhibitors; the review explicitly
  includes all ACE inhibitors in the avoid-in-pregnancy statement (p. 802). **Matches.**
- **olmesartan, valsartan (csv:165-166)**: own class (ARB). **Matches.**
- Claims about each product's boxed warning, fosinoprilat placental transfer in animals, and the renal-term/GFR
  reasoning in the notes are not in this paper: **cannot verify from this source** (open question).
- "No pregnancy pharmacokinetic studies": the review reports none for losartan; it does not survey the other drugs.
- Independent PK check: the library models olmesartan and valsartan as apparent oral (CL/F) models with no
  bioavailability term (`R/drugs_olmesartan.R:36-50`, `R/drugs_valsartan.R:31-32, 61-63`), so the review's Table I
  bioavailabilities (olmesartan 26%, valsartan 25%) have nothing to compare against. Table I's valsartan food effect
  (AUC about 50% lower) is relevant to any fed dosing in that apparent model but is not a discrepancy.
- Context-file line numbers (161-165) are off by two and omit the fosinopril and enalapril rows; noted for the
  index, not a repo defect.
