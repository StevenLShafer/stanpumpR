# Caudle 2017: CPIC consensus terms for pharmacogenetic results

## Study
- Modified Delphi consensus among pharmacogenetics experts (clinicians, researchers, laboratory staff, implementers, informaticians): 58 completed survey 1, 54 survey 2, 47 survey 3, 46 survey 4, 36 survey 5. Consensus threshold > 70%.
- Terms gathered from seven clinical testing laboratories and the literature for CPIC level A genes (CYP2D6, CYP2C19, CYP3A5, CYP2C9, TPMT, DPYD, HLA-B, UGT1A1, SLCO1B1, VKORC1).
- Final agreement: 100% for allele functional terms, 91.7% for enzyme phenotype terms, 91.7% for transporter phenotype terms. No consensus for VKORC1.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model. Table 2, final terms:

- Allele functional status (all genes): increased function, normal function, decreased function, no function, unknown function, uncertain function.
- Phenotype, drug-metabolising enzymes (CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A5, CYP2C9, TPMT, DPYD, UGT1A1): **ultrarapid metabolizer** (increased activity compared with rapid; two increased-function alleles or more than two normal-function alleles), **rapid metabolizer** (between normal and ultrarapid; normal + increased-function alleles), **normal metabolizer** (fully functional), **intermediate metabolizer** (between normal and poor), **poor metabolizer** (little to no activity).
- Transporters (SLCO1B1): increased, normal, decreased, poor function. High-risk genotypes (HLA-B): positive / negative.
- Every term is to be prefixed by the gene name. "Extensive metabolizer" is retired in favour of "normal metabolizer".

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not applicable: no PK/PD, MEAC, ke0 or potency data. (Table 3 gives example decision-support texts, e.g. CYP2C19 ultrarapid metabolizer and citalopram.)

## Caveats
- Terminology only. It does not say which categories are clinically distinct for a given gene; whether "rapid" applies to CYP2D6 is not addressed here (the Table 2 rapid example is CYP2C19*1/*17).
- Activity-score definitions (e.g. CYP2D6) are not in this paper.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **CYP2D6 values poor, intermediate, normal, ultrarapid** (`R/constants.R:34-38`): all four are CPIC Table 2 enzyme-phenotype terms, and "normal" (not "extensive") is the reference, as CPIC recommends. **Matches**. The omission of "rapid" for CYP2D6 is a stanpumpR choice this paper neither requires nor forbids.
- **CYP2C19 values poor, intermediate, normal, rapid, ultrarapid** (`R/constants.R:48-52`; `docs/adding-a-drug.md:182`): exactly the five CPIC enzyme-phenotype terms. **Matches**.
- **Attribution**: the repo cites no CPIC work for these terms; if a citation is wanted, Caudle 2017 is the defining source. Not a discrepancy.
