# Chaudhri 1992: closed-loop arterial pressure control in hypotensive anaesthesia

## Study
20 patients aged 39-66 yr undergoing local resection of intraocular melanoma, randomized 10 vs 10 to manual infusion by an experienced anaesthetist or computer closed loop (adaptive PID controller, Imed 929 pump, Apple IIe) of a 5:1 mixture of sodium nitroprusside (200 ug/mL) and trimetaphan (1 mg/mL) in 5% glucose. Target systolic pressure 40 +/- 5 mm Hg during choroid dissection. Background: atenolol, pethidine, promethazine, thiopentone 5 mg/kg, suxamethonium 1.5 mg/kg, nitrous oxide with enflurane 1%, tubocurarine.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is arterial pressure control (percent of time more than 10% outside target). Control during profound hypotension was satisfactory and not significantly different between groups. The computer group spent more time more than 10% below target over the whole infusion (P < 0.05), and its infusion and operation durations were longer (P < 0.05). Four computer-group patients had minor choroidal bleeding vs none manual (text, PDF p. 3). No opioid content; no MEAC, ke0 or potency data.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
n = 10 per group; manual operator very experienced; bleeding assessment subjective; no long-term outcome.
