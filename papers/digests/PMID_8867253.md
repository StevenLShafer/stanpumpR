# Woodhouse 1996: morphine vs pethidine vs fentanyl by PCA

## Study
Randomised, double-blind comparison of three IV PCA opioids after surgery (Royal North Shore Hospital, Sydney). 60 recruited (aged 20-70 y, within 15% of ideal body weight, ASA 1-2, normal renal and liver function), 5 withdrawn, 55 analysed (27 men, 28 women). Groups: morphine n=19, pethidine n=18, fentanyl n=18 (Table I; the printed Table I row labels are scrambled in the text layer, so which age row belongs to which drug is not confirmed). Mixed surgery (Table II), no difference in case mix.
Drug syringes (60 mL saline): morphine sulphate 60 mg, pethidine HCl 600 mg, fentanyl citrate 0.6 mg, i.e. 1 mg/mL morphine, 10 mg/mL pethidine, 10 mcg/mL fentanyl (p116 text lists "10 mg fentanyl" per mL, which contradicts the 0.6 mg/60 mL syringe; the syringe content gives 10 mcg/mL; a misprint in the paper).
Graseby PCA pump, bolus 1 or 1.5 mL (2 mL if the pain service judged analgesia inadequate), lockout 5 min, no background infusion, no dose cap. Equi-analgesic design: 1 mg morphine = 10 mg pethidine = 0.01 mg fentanyl, attributed to Lehmann 1988 (p116, Methods).
Measures over 48 h: 4-hourly VAS pain, PCA demands and deliveries, a side-effect questionnaire and satisfaction questionnaire on postoperative day 2, anaesthetist's guess of drug.
No blood concentrations were measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- MEAC / analgesic concentration: **none reported** (no concentrations measured).
- ke0 / t1/2 ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: **none reported**.
- Potency ratio: morphine : pethidine : fentanyl = 1 : 10 : 0.01 by mass (1 mg = 10 mg = 0.01 mg), an assumption from Lehmann 1988, p116. Study outcome tests it only indirectly: by 48 h fentanyl patients had used more drug in morphine equivalents (143 vs 82 and 80 mg, Table IV; fentanyl > both at 48 h, > pethidine at 12 and 24 h), and made more demands than pethidine patients, which the authors read as either shorter fentanyl action or a fentanyl dose set too low (p119-120). Demand ratio (successful/total demands) did not differ between drugs. Pain VAS did not differ between groups at 12, 24 or 48 h (p119). So on this study the 1:10:0.01 ratio probably over-states fentanyl's potency relative to morphine (derived: fentanyl needed about 1.7x the morphine-equivalent amount, i.e. an apparent ratio near 1:0.006), but the comparison is confounded by clinician-chosen bolus size and by the different durations of action; the authors do not state a revised ratio.
- Side effects (Table III, count/n): pruritus morphine 8/15, pethidine 2/18 per the scrambled rows; the text states more pruritus with morphine (chi-square 6.83, P=0.03) and lower vomiting and pruritus intensity with pethidine. Row alignment of Table III in the text layer is uncertain.

## Covariates
None modelled. Age (Table I) not different between groups; sex and surgery type noted.

## Caveats
Retrospective self-reported side effects, uncontrolled antiemetics, one anaesthetist's judgement (correct for 35%), heterogeneous surgery, bolus size at clinician discretion, 48 h only. Group sizes of 17 gave 80% power only for a 1 SD difference. Assumed equipotency, not verified by concentrations.
