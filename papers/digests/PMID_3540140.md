# Moore 1987: aminoglycoside peak/MIC ratio and clinical response

## Study
- Pooled secondary analysis of four randomised double-blind trials at Johns Hopkins (refs 7-10) of gentamicin,
  tobramycin or amikacin for suspected sepsis; 236 adults with culture-documented Gram-negative infection, levels
  measured and response evaluable (PDF p1-2, printed 93-94).
- Population: mean age 62 (19-100), 126 (53%) women; gentamicin 103 (44%), tobramycin 85 (36%), amikacin 48 (20%).
  Sites: urinary tract 150, lower respiratory 38, cutaneous 32, intra-abdominal 7; bacteraemia 87 (37%).
  E. coli 149, Klebsiella 63, Proteus 44, Pseudomonas 30; polymicrobial 82 (PDF p2-3).
- Dosing: initial IV 2 mg/kg (gentamicin, tobramycin) or 8 mg/kg (amikacin), q8h, renally adjusted (modified Chan
  nomogram, Siersbaek-Nielsen CrCl); maintenance titrated to keep levels 5-10 µg/mL (gent/tobra) or 20-40 µg/mL
  (amikacin). Each combined with a beta-lactam (methicillin, nafcillin, penicillin or cephalothin) (PDF p2).
- Levels: plasma, radioenzymatic assay (CV 7% at 6 µg/mL), drawn before and 1 h after a 30-min infusion on
  alternate days. Peak = level 1 h after infusion; trough = just before a dose; maximal peak = highest measured;
  GM = antilog(log peak/2 + log trough/2). **Amikacin levels were divided by 4** for the concentration analyses.
  MIC by agar dilution; with several organisms, the highest MIC was used (PDF p2, printed 94).
- Response: ≥2 of fall in peak temperature 1.7 °C or normal, WBC fall ≥15% or normal, bacterial eradication,
  resolution of local signs; death attributed to infection = non-response. 188/236 (80%) responded.
- Statistics: Wilcoxon, t, chi-square, forward stepwise logistic regression (SAS), Kaplan-Meier for time to maximal peak.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: composite clinical response (above), not bacteriological kill or survival.
- Exposure index: peak (1 h after a 30-min infusion) over the agar-dilution MIC; maximal and mean peak/MIC
  strongest; trough and GM associations disappeared after adjustment for renal function/peak ratios.
- Graded "dose-response"; no single threshold is declared. Discussion (PDF p6, printed 98): with MIC 4 µg/mL a
  maximal peak of 16 µg/mL gives a ratio of 4 and ≥70% response; to keep peak <10 µg/mL and reach ≥80% response,
  MIC ≤2 µg/mL would be needed. Conventional thresholds cited as MIC ≤4 (gent/tobra) and ≤16 (amikacin).
- Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Underlying prognosis (McCabe-Jackson), Pseudomonas, cutaneous site, urinary site, temperature, systolic BP,
creatinine clearance (Table 2). Aminoglycoside type was not significant.

## Caveats
- Observational analysis inside trials; peaks were titrated by protocol, so peak/MIC variation comes mostly from MIC.
- Three drugs pooled, amikacin ÷ 4; the paper does not say explicitly whether the amikacin MIC was also scaled
  for the ratios.
- "Peak" is 1 h after the end of a 30-min infusion, later than a 30-min post-infusion peak, so a ratio here is
  on a lower-concentration basis than a 30-min definition.
- The discussion's "peak <10 and MIC ≤2 for ≥80%" implies a ratio ≤5, whereas Fig. 2 places 80% response near a
  ratio of 6-8 (derived; internal inconsistency or a different reading of the figure).
- Table 2's NOTE says mean ± SE, but values (age 61 ± 18) are clearly SDs; Table 1's dispersion is unlabelled.
- Q8h dosing era; not extended-interval dosing.
