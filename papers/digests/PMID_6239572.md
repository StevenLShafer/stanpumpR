# James 1984: Anesthetic considerations at moderate altitude

## Study
Narrative review with bench experiments, University of Zimbabwe (Harare, about 4800 ft). Bench work in a hypobaric pressure chamber at sea level (101.5 kPa / 760 mm Hg), 5000 ft (83.2 kPa / 624) and 10,000 ft (69.0 kPa / 518). No patients; no drug PK.
- Gas and vapour analysers (Tables 1-4, p.1098-1100): a paramagnetic O2 analyser, a Datex CD 300 infrared CO2 analyser and an Engstrom EMMA halothane analyser all respond to partial pressure, not percent.
- Vaporizers (printed p.1100): two Fluotec Mk II and one Drager Vapor halothane vaporizer, flows 3, 5, 8 L/min, EMMA analyser (reads partial pressure). Output changed by no more than 0.1 kPa from the sea-level value at 5000 and 10,000 ft, with and without a Manley ventilator in the circuit; halothane partial pressure was roughly constant (so its concentration rose).
- Flowmeters (printed p.1101-1102, Figs 2-3): three N2O and three O2 floating-bobbin/ball flowmeters, flow by spirometer over 60 s, 0.5-8 L/min, at 4800 ft (84.8 kPa) and 10,000 ft. Actual flow exceeds indicated flow; the error rises with flow and plateaus above about 4 L/min. At 4800 ft the density-based prediction is 8.5%, measured about 7.2% at higher flows. At 10,000 ft predicted 20.3%, measured about 18.5% (O2) and about 21% (N2O). Safar and Tenicela's "1% per 1000 ft" was not supported. Low-range flowmeters were not tested. Ventilator back-pressure made no significant difference.
- Venturi masks 28/35/40% (Tables 5-7, p.1103-1104): with a sea-level-calibrated flowmeter there is a small rise in O2 percent and fall in total flow at altitude; the O2 partial pressure delivered still falls.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No measured PD. Table 8 (PDF p.8, printed p.1104; image read) tabulates MAC (% of 1 atm) at sea level / 5000 ft / 10,000 ft with a MAPP (minimal alveolar partial pressure, proposed as a replacement term for MAC): nitrous oxide 105.0 / 126.5 / 152.2 %, MAPP 106.1 kPa (798.0 mm Hg); ethyl ether 1.92 / 2.31 / 2.78 %, 1.94 kPa (14.6); halothane 0.75 / 0.90 / 1.09 %, 0.76 kPa (5.7); enflurane 1.68 / 2.02 / 2.43 %, 1.70 kPa (12.8); isoflurane 1.2 / 1.45 / 1.73 %, 1.22 kPa (9.1). These are derived, not measured (the source of the sea-level MAC is not stated; no assay, population or end point). No ke0, tPeak, hysteresis or potency ratio between drugs. Text states that analgesia from 50% N2O falls by nearly 50% at 5000 ft and to insignificant levels at 10,000 ft, citing James 1982 (see PMID_7091603).
Opioid audit: not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Barometric pressure (altitude) only.

## Caveats
Vaporizer test used halothane vaporizers only (no isoflurane, sevoflurane or desflurane vaporizer); pressurised Tec 6-type vaporizers not studied. Flowmeters tested only from 0.5 L/min, no low-range meters. Chamber at constant temperature. The MAC conversion rests on the premise that potency follows partial pressure, which this paper asserts and does not test.
