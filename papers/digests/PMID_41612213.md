# Wang 2026: ciprofol population PK, Chinese children 1 to 9 y

## Study
Prospective single-centre PK study (Wenzhou, Jan to Aug 2023; ChiCTR2200058405). 27 children recruited (toddlers 1-2 y n=9, preschool 3-5 y n=9, school-age 6-9 y n=9); 2 excluded, so 25 analysed (14 male, 11 female; 317 samples; 8 planned samples, mostly 180 min, were missing). ASA I-II, BMI between the 25th and 75th percentile for age and sex, elective urologic surgery. Mean age 4.2 y (1-9), mean weight 18.5 kg (9.8-37), mean BMI 16.7 kg/m2 (Table 1).
Dose: ciprofol 0.6 mg/kg IV over 30 s, with midazolam 0.1-0.2 mg/kg beforehand and fentanyl 2 ug/kg at induction, then cisatracurium; sevoflurane 1 MAC maintenance. Arterial blood (0.5 mL) at 0 (blank), 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, 180 min (13 samples). Plasma (EDTA), UPLC-APCI-MS/MS, range 5-20000 ng/mL, LLOQ 5 ng/mL; no sample was below LLOQ. NONMEM 7.4; covariates tested: sex, age, weight, BMI, HGB, TBIL, ALT, AST, Scr, BUN, TP, ALB; allometric weight scaling and age maturation were tested for CL and not retained (PDF p.4-5).

## Model and parameters
Three-compartment model (better than two-compartment, dOFV 65.8). All parameters are per kg body weight (the dose was per kg; weight was not a significant covariate on top of that). IIV on CL, V1, Q3 only; V2, V3, Q2 had no IIV (estimates near 0). IIV correlation CL-V1 = -0.821. Residual error combined proportional plus additive. Table 3 (PDF p.8), estimate (RSE%), bootstrap median (2.5-97.5th):
- CL 31.2 mL/min/kg (5.0); 31.2 (28.2-34.2)
- V1 506 mL/kg (8.1); 492 (377-576)
- Q2 28.2 mL/min/kg (19.9); 29.1 (20.7-68.6)
- V2 231 mL/kg (14.7); 237 (158-302)
- Q3 19.8 mL/min/kg (10.8); 19.8 (16.0-24.7)

- IIV (CV%) CL 26.3, V1 35.1, Q3 38.3; shrinkage 0.3, 5.8, 7.8%
- residual: proportional 13.5%, additive 4.83 (the table labels both "%"; units of the additive term not stated, presumably ug/L)
Covariate model (Eq. 7, PDF p.5): V1 = 0.506 x (BUN/5)^-0.821 x exp(eta), with 0.506 in L/kg and BUN in mmol/L (the reference BUN 5 mmol/L). I checked: BUN 3 gives 0.770 L/kg and BUN 7 gives 0.384 L/kg, matching the paper's quoted values. The BUN term lowered the OFV by 9.55 and V1 IIV fell from 41.6% to 35.1%.
Derived (not in paper): total Vss = 506+231+1360 = 2097 mL/kg; for the mean 18.5 kg child CL is about 577 mL/min and V1 about 9.4 L.
Non-compartmental (Table 2, PDF p.8, mean +/- SD, all n=25): Cmax 975.5 +/- 357.6 ug/L (at 2 min); AUClast 16,090 +/- 3677 and AUCinf 17,795 +/- 4083 ug/L.min; t1/2 58.8 +/- 16.8 min; Vd 3010 +/- 1060 mL/kg; CL 35.5 +/- 8.2 mL/min/kg. No significant differences among age groups (P 0.16-0.70).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Only plasma concentration was measured; BIS was used to time intubation (BIS < 60) but no concentration-effect, ke0, EC50 or MEAC analysis is reported. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Only BUN on V1 (Eq. 7). Age, weight, sex, BMI and the other labs gave no further improvement after BUN. Simulation (Table 4, text layer only): for 0.6 mg/kg, AUC 19,231 ug/L.min at BUN 3, 5 and 7 mmol/L; C2min 640.7, 880.3, 1056.1 ug/L; C180min 19.6, 17.9, 17.1 ug/L. The authors judge the BUN effect clinically negligible.

## Caveats
n=25, healthy non-obese Chinese children, single induction bolus only (no infusion data, no validation set, extrapolation to prolonged infusion not supported). Premedicated with midazolam, co-administered fentanyl and sevoflurane. Sex imbalance (1 of 9 male in preschool, 7 of 7 in school-age). Concentrations are plasma, total (not free; ciprofol is 93.5-96.6% protein bound per the authors' ref). The "children have higher CL and V1 per kg than adults" claim is against the adult infusion model of Liu 2024 (ref 12); the same paper also says the child values are within the range of paediatric propofol.

If a ciprofol model is built from this paper, note: parameters are per kg (convert to absolute by weight), time unit is minutes, and V1 depends on BUN in mmol/L (the paper does not say whether its BUN in mmol/L is urea or urea nitrogen; a unit to confirm before use).
