# Allam 2013: patient-maintained propofol sedation with reaction-time monitor, volunteers

## Study
- Single-arm safety study, 20 healthy volunteers, ASA 1-2, 12 M / 8 F, age 36 (SD 5.3) y, weight 74.4 (SD 15.3) kg (PDF p3, printed 156). Fasted 6 h, room air, anaesthetist present.
- Device: Graseby 3500 patient-maintained sedation system, modified so the handset vibrates every 1 min and the time to press is logged. Volunteers were urged to over-sedate themselves by double-pressing the button.
- Target-controlled effect-site propofol: starts at 1 mcg/mL; each double-press (within 1 s) adds 0.2 mcg/mL, maximum 3 mcg/mL. PK lockout: no increment until plasma concentration falls to within 10% of the effect-site target. PD lockout from reaction time relative to the volunteer's own baseline (mean of 2 of >=3 baseline trials): <=150% increment allowed; >150 to <200% hold target 2 min; >200 to <300% lower target by 0.2 mcg/mL; >300% lower by 0.4 mcg/mL; <30% of baseline treated as "too quick", rechecked after 20 s.
- Stop points: OAAS <=2, SpO2 <90%, airway intervention, apnoea, three consecutive reductions of target, or 30 min.
- No blood assay: all concentrations are model-calculated.

## Model and parameters

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0 estimated; t1/2 ke0 2.6 min was a device setting (propofol; end point behind it not stated in this paper).
- Maximum calculated Ce reached: 1.7 (SD 0.4) mcg/mL, range 1.2-2.4, all 20 (PDF p3). By stopping reason (Table 1, rendered page): 3 consecutive decrements group (n=10) 1.66 (0.4); 30-min completed group (n=10) 1.8 (0.4).
- Table 1 other values: lowest OAAS median 4.5 [3-5] vs 3 [3-5]; lowest SpO2 97 (1.9) vs 97 (1.6)%; full word recall 5 (50%) vs 1 (10%); sedation duration in the decrement group 21 min (IQR 5.1; range 12-29).
- Overall: lowest OAAS 3 in 10 volunteers, 4 in 5, 5 in 5; no loss of verbal contact; mean lowest SpO2 97 (1.7)% on room air; no oxygen or airway intervention.
- No MEAC, EC50, hysteresis or potency ratio reported. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None analysed. Reaction time is expressed as a percentage of the individual's own baseline.

## Caveats
- Artificial design (volunteers told to over-sedate), n=20, no control arm, no comparison with the earlier non-reaction-time system in the same subjects; authors call it a pilot safety study and say a much larger database is needed.
- Concentrations are model predictions only; the Marsh model is a weight-proportional set built for children and adults and was not validated here.
- Half of the volunteers ended by the reaction-time rule, so the sedation they reached was limited by the algorithm, not by the 3 mcg/mL ceiling.
