# Blume 2015: levomethadone 5 mg tablet vs oral solution, relative bioavailability

## Study
Single-centre, open-label, randomised, 2-period crossover (PDF pp 3-5, printed 337-339). 36 healthy **male**
volunteers randomised, 33 completed (1 withdrawn for vomiting, 2 withdrew consent); age 29 +/- 6.0 years (19-39),
weight 76.2 +/- 8.64 kg (56.5-92.5), height 179 +/- 6.2 cm, BMI 23.9 +/- 2.50 (Table 1, PDF p6). Single oral doses of
**5 mg levomethadone hydrochloride** as one tablet (test) or 1 mL of 5 mg/mL solution (reference), fasted >= 10 h,
240 mL water, washout >= 2 weeks. **Naltrexone** block: 25 mg the evening before and 50 mg >= 2 h before dosing and
on day 3. Plasma sampled pre-dose and 0.17-72 h (23 samples). LC-MS/MS, **non-stereoselective** (levomethadone given
alone), LLOQ 0.100 ng/mL, range 0.100-20.000 ng/mL. NCA in WinNonlin 5.3; ANOVA on log AUC and Cmax; truncated
AUC0-72 primary (all subjects quantifiable at 72 h).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: **no MEAC** or analgesic concentration (naltrexone-blocked volunteers, no PD end point).
**No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis.** Potency: only a literature statement (introduction, PDF p2, ref 7) that
levomethadone's analgesic efficacy is 50-fold that of dextromethadone and its mu-receptor affinity 10-fold that of the
racemate; no equianalgesic ratio measured. Adverse events: 55 in 21 subjects, headache 13, nausea 5, none severe.

## Covariates
None studied. Men only (to avoid OC interactions); the authors argue the comparison extrapolates to women and the elderly.

## Caveats
- Naltrexone co-administration in both periods; affects both arms equally, but absolute exposure may not be
  representative.
- Levomethadone (R) alone, not racemate; non-stereoselective assay; whether concentrations are expressed as base
  or HCl is not stated.
- 72 h truncation; AUC extrapolation ~25%, so t1/2 (~37-39 h) is likely underestimated.
- 5 mg strength only; sponsor-funded (Sanofi), CRO-run.
