# Koch-Weser 1971: procainamide dosing, plasma concentration and effect

## Study
Observational, 218 hospitalised or ambulatory patients treated for arrhythmias at the Massachusetts General Hospital (dosage chosen by the treating physician; effect judged with that physician, generally unaware of the plasma level), plus seven volunteers for PK. Concentration-effect analysis in the 142 patients (102 men, 40 women, mean age 56.6 y) in whom a concentration was effective without other antiarrhythmics; 136 had ventricular and 10 atrial arrhythmias; acute MI in 76. Dose-concentration relation in 186 patients at equilibrium on oral dosing. Assay: spectrophotometric (diazotisation) and spectrofluorimetric, specific for procainamide, sensitive to 0.05 mg/L, agree within 5%, CV 1.8-3.2%. Concentrations are as procainamide base, in plasma; plasma is about 25% higher than whole blood (plasma:erythrocyte ratio 1.58).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Therapeutic range, plasma procainamide base, 4-8 mg/L (Figure 1, p2-3; abstract and Comment): usually effective antiarrhythmic range, defined from arrhythmia control in the 142 patients at the time of each determination; concentrations below 4 mg/L suppressed arrhythmias in a minority only; higher concentrations helped under 10% of those uncontrolled below 8 mg/L. Occasional patients needed 8-16 mg/L (abstract) or 8-12 mg/L (Comment): inconsistent, see open question.
- Toxicity (minor: GI, weakness, mild hypotension, 10-30% PR/QT/QRS prolongation; major: serious hypotension, conduction disturbance, active ventricular arrhythmia, arrest): very rare below 12 mg/L (abstract), minor effects rising above 8 mg/L, toxic effects the rule above 16 mg/L; four patients dying with intoxication implicated had 17.6-25.2 mg/L within 1 h of death (p3 text, confirmed on image).
- No MEAC in an analgesic sense, no ke0, hysteresis, effect-site model or potency ratio; not an opioid.
- Dosing statements: 50 mg/kg/day hydrochloride in 3-hourly doses of 250, 375 or 500 mg; dose interval should not exceed half-life to limit fluctuation above 50%; priming dose twice the maintenance dose, parenterally if urgent; IV infusion 100 mg/min for 10 min gives 16-20 mg/L at end in a 70 kg normal, infuse at no more than 50 mg/min.

## Covariates
Renal function: equilibrium concentration per mg/kg/day rises with BUN (Figure 4, not read on image); severe renal failure can give about three times normal concentration. Low cardiac output lowers Vd and elimination. Weight: dose in mg/kg.

## Caveats
Uncontrolled observational data, concentrations drawn at varying times after oral, IM or IV dosing; effect classified clinically; 1971 assay; plasma not whole blood; Vd in volunteers differed by 28% and elimination rate by 77% in a small group.
