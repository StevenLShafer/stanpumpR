# Upton 2005: recirculatory PK-PD model of propofol in man

## Study
No new data were collected. A "meta-modelling" study: published mean data were digitised and fitted by hybrid modelling for a 30-year-old, 69-kg, 174-cm "standard man" (organ volumes and flows from the P3M program, NHANES III) (PDF p 2). Data sources (all plasma/blood concentrations, plasma used to avoid storage sequestration in erythrocytes, blood:plasma ratio taken as about 1):
- Brain: Ludbrook 2002 (arterial, jugular bulb, calculated brain concentration, cerebral blood flow by transcranial Doppler, BIS during a two-stage infusion; midazolam premedicated; ASPECT 2000).
- Lungs: He 2000 (9 patients, 5 mg propofol + 15 mg indocyanine green over 1 s, radial artery samples at up to 1-s intervals for 1 min; mean weight 62.3 kg, scaled to 70 kg).
- Systemic: Ludbrook's 5-min then 20-min infusion with washout, and a 10 mg/min x 300 min infusion, both simulated with the Schnider 1998 population model for a standard man (not raw data).
- Validation: Peacock 1995 (jugular bulb, 6 mg/kg/h); Kazama 2001/2003 induction times by age.
Software: Scientist 2.01 and MATLAB; fit judged by the Model Selection Criterion, equal weighting.

## Model and parameters
Recirculatory, physiologically based. Venous mixing compartment (injection to pulmonary artery), lung as three tanks in series (each one third of the lung volume) with 13 s delay, brain as two compartments with a permeability term, and lumped liver, fast and slow (membrane-limited, with a deep compartment) tissue. Blood flows are parameters. Declining arterial concentration after a bolus has four apparent half-lives: 1.1, 5.4, 23.4 and 167 min (PDF p 5, text). Final-model MSC 5.04 for the systemic fit; brain 4.71; lung 5.24.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **BIS**: a linear function of the global brain concentration, BIS = BISint + BISslp x Cbrn, constrained to 0-100. Table 1: **BISslp = -4.77 BIS per mg/L** (SD 0.16), **BISint = 78.5** (SD 1.06), where 100 is to be used for normal use and 78.5 reflects the baseline midazolam sedation of the Ludbrook subjects. (Initial estimates before the final fit, from the Figure 2 caption in the text layer only: slope -4.46, intercept 77.7.) Concentrations are mg/L (propofol, brain, total plasma-equivalent).
- **Cerebral blood flow**: Emax model on brain concentration, Emax 85.7 % reduction (SD 2.96), **EC50 7.86 mg/L** (SD 0.76) (Table 1), minimal hysteresis.
- **ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak**: none reported. No effect compartment: BIS is tied directly to the brain compartment concentration, which carries the equilibration delay. Delays of up to 30 s between brain concentration and BIS were tried and not supported; the final model ignores it (printed p 351). No MEAC or analgesic concentration; no potency ratio.
- Simulated 2 mg/kg over 20 s with a BIS threshold of 80: loss of consciousness at 35, 34, 39 s and recovery at 2.9, 8.6, 18.7 min for cardiac output 8.5, 5.5, 2.5 L/min (printed p 348). The Kazama induction times needed a BIS threshold of 77 rather than 80 (printed p 348).
Opioid audit: not an opioid.

## Covariates
None estimated. Body size scaling is not yet in the model (authors plan to use P3M); cardiac output and cerebral blood flow can be varied (sensitivity analysis; age effect via Kazama's cardiac outputs and a 20% fall of cerebral blood flow from 20 to 80 y).

## Caveats
Authors: built from mean literature data, not for fitting individuals; no peripheral venous data used, so peripheral venous concentrations may need changes; the lumped body is unsuitable for body-composition changes; short regimens, so long infusions and the washout tail fit poorly (systematic misfit in Figure 8). Lung extraction (0-30% explored) and renal extraction of the fast compartment (30%, equivalent to 40% renal extraction) are poorly determined and controversial. Several SDs (Ehep 37 %, Vfast 15.3 L, Vslow 15.1 L) are large. Systemic data were themselves Schnider 1998 model simulations. BISint comes from midazolam-premedicated patients.
