# Kloprogge 2014: oral quinine PK in pregnant women with malaria

## Study
Population PK of oral quinine in pregnant Ugandan women with uncomplicated *P. falciparum* malaria, nested in a larger efficacy trial (NCT00495508; Piola 2010). 23 women in the 2nd/3rd trimester enrolled, 1 excluded for a dosing/concentration mismatch, so **22 analysed** (Table 1, PDF p.3 / printed 3035): median age 21.0 y (range 18.0–37.0), body weight 56.5 kg (44.0–71.0), estimated gestational age 26.0 weeks (13.0–37.0), 12/22 in 2nd trimester and 10/22 in 3rd, admission body temperature 37.2 °C (36.0–38.9). Dosing: oral quinine sulphate (Remedica; 300 mg salt/tablet), **10 mg salt/kg three times daily (0, 8, 16 h) for 7 days** (replacement dose if vomited <60 min). Venous plasma samples (2 mL) at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 144, 160, 161, 162, 163, 164, 168, 170, 172, 176 and 184 h after the first dose (PDF p.2). Assay: LC with fluorimetric detection; LLOQ 1 mg/L, calibration 1–10 mg/L, recovery 76–80% (PDF p.2). Doses converted from quinine sulphate (MW 782.96 g/mol) to **quinine base equivalent (MW 324.42 g/mol)**; concentrations modelled as natural logs. Software: NONMEM 7.2, FOCE-I, ADVAN5 TRANS1; PsN, Xpose, R.

## Model and parameters
First-order absorption + **two-compartment** disposition with additive residual error on log-transformed data. Relative bioavailability F fixed at 100% with IIV and inter-occasion variability (IOV). Parasitaemia (last observation carried forward) as a time-varying covariate on F; admission body temperature on CL/F; body weight allometric (exponent 2/3 on clearances, 1 on volumes). All values from **Table 2 (PDF p.5 / printed 3036-3037), image-confirmed** (population estimate, % RSE in parentheses).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD or concentration–effect model, no ke0, no MEAC. Efficacy was clinical (parasite clearance / no recrudescence to day 42 or delivery). The authors note the therapeutic window for **unbound** quinine is narrow and that only **total** plasma quinine was measured; free-concentration measurement is flagged as necessary to judge therapeutic relevance (abstract; Discussion, PDF pp.6-7). Simulated first-dose AUC0-8 rose with parasite biomass (29.2, 36.5, 43.7, 51.1, 57.6 mg·h/L for 10^7–10^11 infected erythrocytes) and with fever (≈2.05 mg·h/L per °C, 36→39 °C) (PDF pp.5-7, image-confirmed).

## Covariates
- Relative bioavailability F: time-varying parasitaemia, +38.9% per log10 (a typical 10^11 vs 10^7 biomass raises exposure ~50.7%).
- Elimination clearance CL/F: admission body temperature, exponential, −0.243 → 51.8% lower CL/F at 39 vs 36 °C (so ~15% higher 0–8 h exposure at 39 vs 36 °C).
- Allometric body weight: exponent 2/3 on CL/F and Q/F, 1 on volumes (reference not a fixed 70 kg stated; scaling centred on study weights).
- EGA/trimester: not retained (significant in forward addition on F, dropped at P<0.001 backward; full-covariate approach showed a non-significant trend of +1.73%/week on CL/F, i.e. ~41.5% higher CL/F at week 37 vs week 13).

## Caveats
Small n (22), oral data so all disposition parameters are apparent (/F); F fixed at 100%. High RSE on Q/F (44.6%) and Vp/F (29.1%) and high IOV shrinkage (up to 84.5%); authors warn against using the model for dose optimisation. Only total quinine measured — exposure differences vs non-pregnant patients may not translate to free-drug/efficacy differences. Comparative claim: quinine exposure was **lower, and CL/F higher, than previously reported in non-pregnant malaria patients** — the stated comparator is Supanaranond 1991 (ref 5) oral quinine (acute malaria CL/F 0.0906 L/h/kg; convalescent 0.1602 L/h/kg) vs this study's 0.188 L/h/kg; different study, assay and population, not a within-study pregnant/non-pregnant contrast.
