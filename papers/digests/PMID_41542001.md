# Brazeal 2025: clevidipine for severe preeclampsia before caesarean delivery (case report)

## Study
- A single case report from Kaweah Health, Visalia, CA. The patient was a 29-year-old G1P0 at 29+2 weeks with
  severe preeclampsia: BP 224/130 despite labetalol 300 mg TID, BMI 48.8 (125 kg, 160 cm).
- Labs: platelets 228 × 10⁹/L, creatinine 0.75 mg/dL, AST 62, ALT 32 U/L, urine protein:creatinine 0.35.
- She received hydralazine 10 mg IV and magnesium sulfate, then had an emergent caesarean under spinal
  anaesthesia.
- **Clevidipine timeline** (Table 1, rendered p2):
  - 11:42: started at 0.5 mg/h.
  - 12:01: reduced to 0.4 mg/h.
  - 12:16: reduced to 0.1 mg/h.
  - 12:27: spinal anaesthetic.
  - 12:31: stopped.
  - 12:57: delivery.
- BP went from 182/105 (11:41) to 166/99 (11:46), 150/88 (11:56), 146/81 (12:01) and 137/79 (12:16). After the
  spinal it was 116/69 (12:30) and 101/57 (12:48), and 129/93 on arrival in PACU (13:36).
- The text gives a total run of 49 min and 0.29 mg in total. (Derived from the timeline: 19 min at 0.5 + 15 at
  0.4 + 15 at 0.1 mg/h = 0.28 mg over 49 min, consistent.)
- Outcome: neonate 1,075 g, Apgar 6 and 7, NICU, discharged on day of life 62. The mother had no rebound
  hypertension in PACU, but was hypertensive again on postoperative days 1-5 and discharged on labetalol plus
  nifedipine XL.

## Caveats
- n = 1, with confounders: hydralazine 11 min before clevidipine, magnesium, and the spinal anaesthetic.
- The authors call it the first description of clevidipine in a pregnant patient. They cite one earlier
  postpartum case.
- **Text-layer artefact:** the extracted Table 1 pairs events with the wrong times (for example, the start at
  11:30). The rendered table and the narrative ("after 19 minutes", "49 minutes") agree with each other.
