# Schwartz 1990: aging, verapamil elimination and dynamics

## Study
- 29 healthy volunteers, 23-81 years, screened by history, examination, labs, CXR, ECG; treadmill test for those 40 and over and an echocardiogram (p. 365). No medications.
- Groups (PDF p. 3, printed 366): young 20-39 y, n = 11 (9 M, 2 F), age 30.5 +/- 5, weight 75.7 +/- 22.3 kg; middle-aged 40-59 y, n = 8 (4 M, 4 F), 49.3 +/- 6.3 y, 78.4 +/- 15.6 kg; elderly >60 y (five were 75 or older), n = 10 (5 M, 5 F), 70.5 +/- 7.4 y, 73 +/- 7 kg.
- Single dose: racemic verapamil 0.15-0.22 mg/kg IV over 15 min by constant-rate pump (one subject 0.30 mg/kg). The paper does not say whether doses were the hydrochloride or the base (drug supplied by Knoll and Searle). Mean doses in Table 1: 13.5 +/- 2.8, 14.2 +/- 2.7, 12.6 +/- 2.6 mg.
- Sampling: 3, 5, 8, 10, 12, 15 min of infusion, then 5 min to 600 min after it (p. 365).
- Multistage steady state (hospital day 3): 30-min loads (0.2, 0.2, 0.4 mg/min mean) and 75-min maintenance (0.04, 0.07, 0.15 mg/min mean), aiming for 30, 60 and 120 ng/mL, about 6 h in all.
- Assay: plasma verapamil and norverapamil by HPLC with fluorescence; CV 3-4% at 20-200 ng/mL; lower limit 2 ng/mL. Unbound drug not measured (p. 366).
- PK analysis: 3-compartment model (Prophet) and noncompartmental analysis.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak is estimated.** Hysteresis is described for the PR interval: the largest spontaneous PR prolongation came 5-10 min after the end of the 15-min infusion (PDF p. 4, printed 367).
- Heart rate (Table 2, p. 4): rose during the infusion (young 58 -> 72 bpm at 15 min; middle-aged 59 -> 71 at 10 min; elderly 57 -> 68 at 15 min) and was back to baseline 10-15 min after it in the middle-aged and elderly, 30 min in the young. Peak heart rate is thus close to the end of a 15-min infusion, not a bolus tPeak.
- PR interval vs concentration slope, data 15-180 min after infusion: 0.561 young, 0.584 middle-aged, 0.058 elderly (p < .0002); units not printed (presumably ms per ng/mL).
- MAP fell more, with a steeper MAP-concentration slope, in older subjects (p < .02); slopes not printed.
- Junctional rhythm/isorhythmic AV dissociation in elderly only: one at about 80 ng/mL in the single-dose study, three during the third steady state (>100 ng/mL).
- Paced AV conduction (transesophageal pacing at 1.3 x basal rate and at 90 bpm) prolonged equally at all ages; Wenckebach cycle length rose as much or more in the elderly (Fig. 6).
- No therapeutic or toxic concentration range is defined; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Age only (groups, and the linear regression of lambda-z on age above). Weight reported but not modelled.

## Caveats
- Small groups; healthy and, as the author notes, very active elderly (most joggers): a selection bias that may understate aging effects.
- Group means only; the compartmental fit cannot be reconstructed.
- Racemic drug; enantiomers not measured; protein binding assumed unchanged with age (prior study).
- Dose basis (salt) not stated.
