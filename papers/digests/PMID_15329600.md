# Angst 2004: dexmedetomidine vs alfentanil, analgesia and mental effects

## Study
Placebo-controlled, double-blind, three-way crossover (Latin square, sessions at least 5 days apart) in 12 healthy volunteers (8 M, 4 F; age 24±2 yr; weight 74±12 kg men, 63±8 kg women), Stanford (PDF p1). Each received IV dexmedetomidine, alfentanil and saline by computer-controlled infusion (STANPUMP driving a Harvard 22 pump) in four geometrically increasing (x2) PLASMA-targeted steps, each held 45-60 min: dexmedetomidine targets 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 ng/mL; alfentanil 20, 40, 80, 160 ng/mL. Glycopyrrolate 0.2 mg IV given before infusion. Testing began 15 min into each step (assumed equilibrated; effect-site assumed close to plasma), then two post-infusion cycles. Venous plasma sampled at 15 and 30 min and end of each step.
Assays: dexmedetomidine by negative-ion GC-MS (LLOQ 10 pg/mL, CV 4.0-5.4% over 50-1500 pg/mL); alfentanil by LC-MS/MS (LLOQ 0.92 ng/mL, CV 3.2-6.0% over 3-345 ng/mL); venous heparinised PLASMA.
End points: heat pain threshold/tolerance (thermode, 1 °C/s from 35 °C, cutoff 53 °C), electrical pain threshold/tolerance (5 Hz, 3 s, max 20 mA), trail-making test, reaction time, VAS sedation (0-100). Analysis used the 15-min concentration of each step; power-model nested PD forms, equations not given.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit (alfentanil): NO MEAC or minimum effective/analgesic concentration is defined; no ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis is estimated (equilibration assumed by 15-min wait; washout data deliberately excluded because plasma-effect site equilibrium could not be assumed, PDF p5). No equianalgesic or potency ratio between alfentanil and dexmedetomidine is stated. Concentrations are venous PLASMA (total; free not measured).
Alfentanil analgesia (Table 3, PDF p6, median; step 1-4 mean-ish plasma about 13.4, 33.8, 67.8, 126.1 ng/mL per abstract): electrical pain tolerance 6.8, 8.2, 9.8, 12.4 mA vs preinfusion 5.7 mA (placebo 7.3, 7.8, 7.6, 7.5); electrical threshold 1.7, 1.7, 1.6, 1.9 mA vs 1.1 preinfusion (placebo 1.8, 2.1, 2.1, 2.1); heat tolerance 49.0, 48.9, 49.9, 51.5 °C vs 49.1 preinfusion (placebo about 48.1-48.3); heat threshold 45.5, 44.8, 45.6, 46.1 vs 43.8. The paper states that alfentanil raised heat tolerance and electrical threshold/tolerance in a concentration-dependent, linear way, and that heat threshold changes were not concentration-dependent. Analgesia was seen at low concentrations that gave little sedation.
Dexmedetomidine: no change in heat or electrical threshold or tolerance versus placebo at any concentration (up to about 1.2 ng/mL), despite VAS sedation reaching a median 95/100 (Table 4 text layer: preinfusion 28, step 3 95, step 4 80, mean). Trail-making time rose by a factor of 2.0 (dexmedetomidine) and 1.4 (alfentanil) at the top concentration; reaction time by 1.8 and 1.4. Dexmedetomidine lowered BP and heart rate with concentration; alfentanil lowered respiratory rate and SpO2 (PDF p6).
Dexmedetomidine sedation: sedative effect is concentration-dependent (no EC50 estimated). No ke0 or tPeak.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
Only 12 volunteers, experimental heat and electrical pain only (the authors note it may not predict clinical pain), steady-state testing with effect-site assumed equal to plasma, venous sampling, alfentanil plasma runs 20% under target, and 2 subjects too sedated to complete the top-dose dexmedetomidine tests. Funded by Abbott (dexmedetomidine manufacturer). The PK sets are those used to drive the pump, not estimated from these data; the dexmedetomidine set is two-compartment and unpublished (Abbott).

The sedation range 0.4-0.8 ng/mL is bracketed by this paper's steps (VAS sedation median 78-95 at 0.24-1.24 ng/mL), but the paper does not define that range, so no verdict on it.
