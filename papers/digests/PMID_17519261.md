# Mourisse 2007: BIS and blink reflex (R1) PKPD with sevoflurane or propofol

## Study
- Adults >18 y, ASA I-II, elective plastic surgery, no premedication, spontaneous breathing. 54 enrolled; 18 sevoflurane (Group S) and 29 propofol (Group P) analysed (Table 1: age 40.2 and 42.5 y; weight 76.6 and 82.0 kg; height 1.76 and 1.74 m). Four patients took part twice.
- Sevoflurane: facemask, vaporizer raised 1 vol% every 3 min; end-expired concentration (nasal catheter, Datex) used as Cx. Propofol: 5, 10, 15, 20 mg/kg/h, 3 min each. Dosing was stopped once the blink reflex vanished, then restarted when it returned, to loss of consciousness (OAAS 2) a second time.
- Effects: BIS (A-2000, BISXP, v4.0, 15 s smoothing, stored every 5 s) and area under the curve of the EMG R1 component of the electrically evoked blink reflex (supraorbital nerve every 15 s), normalised to control.
- Propofol concentrations were not measured: arterial plasma concentration was simulated from the infusion record with the Marsh 1991 set (ref 11, a paediatric-derived set; Marsh et al., Br J Anaesth 1991;67:41-8).
- Analysis: two-stage (individual Excel Solver fits, then averaged) for the tabulated values; NONMEM V also run on thinned data (supplement, not in the PDF).

## Model and parameters
Sigmoid Emax E = E0 - (E0-Emax)*CE^g/(CE^g+EC50^g); dCE/dt = (Cx - CE)*ke0 (PDF p.4). E0 and Emax taken from each patient's data (BIS: control mean and plateau mean; blink: 100 and 0 % of control).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: BIS and normalised R1 area; the clinical end points were OAAS 2 for loss of consciousness (LOC) and OAAS 3 for return (ROC).
- ke0 is per minute and the geometric mean (the authors warn that deriving t1/2 ke0 from a mean ke0, or the reverse, can err >2-fold). (Derived: ln2/ke0 gives t1/2 ke0 about 1.5 min for propofol BIS and 2.5 min for propofol blink; no tPeak is reported.)
- Blink ke0 is about half the BIS ke0 in both groups, so the blink reflex lags BIS; the authors read this as different effect sites. The sevoflurane/propofol ke0 values are not comparable with each other (end-expired versus predicted arterial concentration, sampling delay).
- The authors say their BIS ke0 values exceed those of other studies and give reasons: unventilated lungs with hypercapnia, geometric versus arithmetic means, a shorter BIS processing delay, and the choice of PK set. The comparator is therefore other published BIS ke0 values, not any stanpumpR value.
- Potency: ratio EC50 blink/EC50 BIS 0.27 (0.16) for sevoflurane against 0.70 (0.29) for propofol (P<0.001). At 1 EC50 BIS, area-R1 was 6.9 (3.8) % of control for sevoflurane against 27.2 (21.5) % for propofol (p.742). At loss of consciousness the BIS was 70 (14) (S) and 61 (12) (P).
- Fig 4 axis lengths 3 x EC50 BIS = 3.87 vol% and 8.07 mg/mL, consistent with Table 2.
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, analgesic concentration or opioid potency ratio. The authors cite propofol 10 mg/mL (Cp50 for skin-incision immobility, Kazama 1997) and sevoflurane 1.8% atm (1 MAC) as concentrations at which the blink reflex is abolished (p.745).

## Covariates
None modelled (age and weight are summarised only; no equation).

## Caveats
- Propofol Cp is a Marsh-model prediction (a paediatric-derived set), not measured; ke0 and EC50 depend on that set, so they do not carry to a different PK model.
- Hypercapnic, spontaneously breathing patients; sevoflurane ke0 is against end-expired, not arterial or brain, concentration.
- Periods of excitation were excluded, which the authors note was more frequent in Group S; three propofol patients could not be fitted and were excluded.
- Within-patient variability of R1 is large; blink ke0 CIs overlap those of BIS only little (0.28 vs 0.46).
