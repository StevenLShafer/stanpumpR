# Hinderling 1985 (Part I): atropine pharmacokinetics in three healthy men

## Study
- Three healthy male volunteers, A, B and C, aged 21-24 (PDF p2, printed 704). Table I header (PDF p4) gives weight
  and height: A 66 kg, 173 cm, BSA 1.79 m2; B 72 kg, 174 cm, 1.86 m2; C 79 kg, 180 cm, 1.99 m2. Mean creatinine
  clearance 140 (+/- 10) mL/min. B did not smoke; A and C were light smokers.
- Each man had two doses in random order, at least 3 weeks apart. The nominal doses were 1.35 and 2.15 mg of atropine,
  stated as the **base**, given IV as a constant 3-min infusion (PDF p2). The Results (PDF p3) repeat that every dosage
  refers to the base. Table I gives the measured doses: A 1383 and 2290 ug, B 1353 and 2136, C 1326 and 2026. The means
  are 1350 and 2150 ug.
- Preparation (PDF p2): the contents of 2.5 or 5 ampules of 0.5 mg atropine sulfate were made up to 22.5 mL. 21 mL
  was injected, and the remaining 1.5 mL was assayed to give the actual dose.
- Subjects lay supine from 2 h before the dose to the end of the study (22-36 h), and fluid intake was set. Plasma was
  sampled up to 10 h. Urine was collected every 0.5 h for the first 3 h, then up to 22 h, and up to 36 h in some
  experiments.
- Assay: GC-MS with selected-ion monitoring. Atropine in plasma and urine, and tropine in urine, are measured as the
  heptafluorobutyryl derivative of tropine, with a deuterated internal standard. The lower limit is 0.5 ng/mL, and
  precision is 5% (plasma) and 10% (urine) for atropine and 15% for tropine. The assay is not stereoselective: racemic
  atropine is measured.
- Analysis: TOPFIT nonlinear least squares, fitting each subject's kinetic and dynamic data (heart rate, saliva flow;
  see Part II, PMID_4032241) simultaneously to Scheme IA. Untransformed data, unit weights. Noncompartmental
  quantities (AUC by trapezoid plus C(last)/beta, CL = D/AUC) were computed separately.

## Model and parameters
Scheme IA (PDF p2) is a linear two-compartment IV-infusion model, central P and peripheral T, with all exit from P.
Exit is renal (k_PU) plus metabolism to tropine M1 (k_PM1). M1 is excreted renally, and a second, unknown metabolite
M2 is formed from M1. Scheme IB, with parallel metabolism to M1 and M2, is kinetically equivalent. The effect
compartments are kinetically identical to T.

All the parameters are referenced to the total plasma concentration. Table I (PDF p4, printed 706, rendered) gives
the mean +/- SD of six experiments. The kinetics come from the integrated fit, CL is D/AUC (eq. 6), V_P is D/(A+B)
(eq. 3) and Vd_ss is V_P(1 + k_PT/k_TP) (eq. 17).

Text-only values:
- Renal plasma flow 712 (+/- 38) mL/min and whole-blood-referenced CL_R 603 (+/- 108) mL/min (PDF p5).
- CL_R depends on urine flow, with CL_R = 368 + 187 ln(urine flow, mL/min), r = 0.605 (Fig. 4, PDF p6). It does not
  depend on urine pH over 5.5-7.5.
- Whole-blood hepatic clearance is 476 (+/- 136) mL/min. The hepatic extraction is 0.63 against plasma flow and 0.32
  against blood flow (PDF p5). Hepatic plasma flow was taken as 825 mL/min (PDF p3).
- The red-cell-to-plasma partition coefficient is 1.21 at 37 C, taken from ref. 2 (PDF p2).

Where the abstract, text and table disagree:
- The terminal half-life is 140 min in the abstract (PDF p1) and 136 (+/- 25) min in the text (PDF p5). Table I
  gives 132 (+/- 31), and the Discussion (PDF p6) rounds it to 2.2 h. Part II also quotes 132 (+/- 31) min. This is
  unresolved. For comparison, ln 2/0.00527 = 131.5 min (derived).
- The abstract's renal clearance of 660 mL/min is Table I's 656 rounded. Its Vss of 210 L matches the table.

Derived checks:
- 19.1 x (1 + 0.560/0.0557) = 211 L, against Vd_ss 210 (derived).
- From the table means, k_PO x V_P = 1.41 L/min. The mean of the six individual products is 1.21 L/min. D/AUC gives
  1175 mL/min (all derived).
- The paper notes that dose-normalised plasma concentrations were higher after the larger dose from 3 to 90 min
  (Fig. 1, PDF p5). It concludes that distribution may be dose-dependent, so the distribution parameters are averages
  over concentration and time.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Part I gives the structure only. Heart rate increase and saliva flow decrease are sigmoid (Hill) functions of the
amount in the peripheral compartment (eqs. 1-2, PDF p2). Both effects peaked later than plasma. Linking the effect to
P failed to fit, and linking it to T fitted. The PD estimates are in Part II (PMID_4032241). There is no ke0, no
t1/2ke0, no tPeak and no MEAC.

## Covariates
None was modelled. CL_R rises with urine flow (see above). Distribution volumes were significantly smaller at the
higher dose.

## Caveats
- n = 3 young men, six experiments.
- The distribution appears dose-dependent, and V_P varies threefold between experiments (10.0-30.8 L).
- In two of the six experiments, CL_R referenced to plasma exceeded renal plasma flow.
- The kinetics are fitted jointly with the PD data, so the PD influences the PK estimates (Part II Discussion).
- Racemic atropine was assayed. The paper says the (-)-(S) form is reportedly **primarily** responsible for peripheral
  effects (PDF p1).
- The dose-preparation arithmetic does not reproduce the stated doses (derived): 2.5 or 5 ampules x 0.5 mg sulfate x
  21/22.5 gives 1.17 or 2.33 mg sulfate, which is 0.97 or 1.94 mg base. The stated means are 1.35 and 2.15 mg base,
  a dose ratio of 1.59 rather than 2. The paper's doses are the assayed ones, and it states the base basis twice. The
  cause of the gap is not shown and is left unresolved.
