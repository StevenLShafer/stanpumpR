# Greco 1995: the search for synergy, a response surface review

## Study
A methods review, not an experiment. PDF pages 1-55 = printed pages 331-385 (PDF page = printed - 330). It sets out
consensus terminology for two-agent combined action (Table 1, p333 / PDF 3), argues over the null reference model
(Section IV), then applies 13 rival methods for continuous data (Section V) and 3 for quantal success/failure data
(Section VI) to two common **simulated** data sets, and ends with experimental design (VIII), a proposed paradigm
(IX) and two appendices on the Chou-Talalay median-effect method.

Common continuous data set (Table 3, p350 / PDF 20, rendered): 38 points, D1 0-50 and D2 0-5 (checkerboard plus 3
control points), generated from Eq. 5 with Econ 100, IC50,1 10, IC50,2 1, m1 -1, m2 -2, **α 0.5** (a small Loewe
synergy), with 10% relative (proportional) normal error. The quantal data set (Table 4) was simulated with true
α = 1 (p373).

## Model and parameters
Terminology (Table 1): with both agents active, a combination equal to the reference is **Loewe additivity** (Eq. 6,
1 = D1/IDx,1 + D2/IDx,2) or **Bliss independence** (Eq. 11 or 14, the fractional-product rule); greater than predicted
is Loewe or Bliss synergism; less is antagonism. With one agent inactive: inertism / synergism / antagonism; with
neither active: inertism or coalism.

Single-agent model (Eq. 1-2, p335): the Hill (sigmoid Emax / median-effect) curve E = Econ (D/IC50)^m / (1 +
(D/IC50)^m), with m negative for an inhibitory (falling) curve; Eq. 7 inverts it: IDx = IC50 (E/(Econ - E))^(1/m).

Flagship interaction model, **Eq. 5** (p337 / PDF 7, rendered; Greco et al. 1990, the "universal response surface
approach"): 1 = D1/[IC50,1 (E/(Econ-E))^(1/m1)] + D2/[IC50,2 (E/(Econ-E))^(1/m2)] + α D1 D2 /
[IC50,1 IC50,2 (E/(Econ-E))^(1/(2m1) + 1/(2m2))]. α > 0 Loewe synergy, α < 0 antagonism, α = 0 additivity; slopes may
differ; E is implicit and is solved with a bisection root-finder. Eq. 8: the interaction index I = 1 - (the α term).
Eq. 10: for the 50% isobol, α = 4(S² - S), S being Hewlett's isobol-bowing ratio. Eq. 9 is the general form any
interaction term can take. Variants: Eq. 28 (Finney 1952 / Hewlett 1969; the α term raised to the ½ power), Eq. 29
(Weinstein et al. 1990 "robust potentiation": PC1, PC2, bp1, bp2; synergy only), Eq. 30 (Machado and Robinson
1994, one parameter η).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No drug, no clinical end point: effects are generic "% of control" (survival, virus titre). No ke0, EC50 of a real
drug or potency ratio. Results of the method comparison (Table 5, p374 / PDF 44, rendered; continuous data, true α
0.5):
- Loewe-based methods (isobologram by hand, Berenbaum's interaction index, Berenbaum 1985 surface, Sühnel 1990 3-D
  spline, Greco 1990 and Weinstein 1990 surfaces) concluded Loewe synergy (Berenbaum's I < 1 at 21 of 25 combination
  points, Table 3). Bliss-based methods (Webb's fractional product, Drewinko, Prichard-Shipman) mostly concluded Bliss
  antagonism, which the authors say is the correct Bliss reading of these data; the Bliss independence surface
  approach found Bliss independence, missing the small Bliss antagonism (p373). Steel and Peckham: additivity
  envelope.
- Only the Chou-Talalay median-effect method gave the opposite of the expected answer (Loewe antagonism and
  synergy depending on fa), attributed to artefacts of the CI vs fa plot (p373; Appendix B).
- Quantal data (Table 6): Gessner 1974 claimed Loewe additivity with a hint of synergy; Greco and Lawrence 1988 found
  borderline Loewe synergy (α 0.903 ± 0.46); the multivariate linear logistic model concluded Loewe antagonism,
  the wrong answer.

## Caveats
- The authors advocate Loewe additivity as the universal reference (Section IV, p348), and say the preference is
  partly personal; they place Bliss independence second. Eq. 5 is offered as a model, not the model: for negative
  α its surface has a saddle and rises back to Econ at high joint concentrations (Fig. 26, p366), and Eqs. 28 and 30
  give isobols outside the unit square for some parameter values.
- All comparisons use one simulated data set per data type, so the method rankings show behaviour on that surface,
  not general performance.
- D-optimal design for Eq. 5 at the ideal parameters needs only six points (p375-376), with concentrations capped at
  1000 because proportional error drives points to infinity.
- Source slip: p375 (PDF 45) says the continuous common data set is "listed in table 2"; Table 2 (p336) is the symbol
  list and the data set is Table 3 (p350). The rendered pages show this; it is the paper's own mis-reference.
- The text layer is OCR-like (e.g. "#{246}" for special characters, scrambled equations); every number above was
  read from a rendered page.
