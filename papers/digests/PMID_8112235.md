# Tomson 1994b: disposition of carbamazepine and phenytoin in pregnancy

## Study
Companion to PMID 8112234, same Stockholm cohort: 86 of the 93 pregnancies treated with carbamazepine or phenytoin
from the start; 70 on monotherapy (41 carbamazepine, 29 phenytoin); co-medication clonazepam 8, ethosuximide 7,
phenobarbital 4, primidone 3, valproate 2 (PDF p1). Dose changed in 16 cases (8 each). Table 1 (PDF p2): unchanged
dosage 42 carbamazepine and 28 phenytoin cases; monotherapy and unchanged dosage 35 and 22. Monthly visits with weight;
trough samples before the morning dose; baseline at least 10 weeks postpartum. HPLC for total and free (Amicon
ultrafiltration) carbamazepine, carbamazepine-10,11-epoxide (CBZ-E) and phenytoin. Definitions (PDF p2): apparent
clearance = daily dose / total concentration; plasma clearance = daily dose / (total concentration x weight x 24 h);
unbound clearance = daily dose / (free concentration x weight x 24 h); bioavailability assumed 100%. Free fractions
from all 86 cases; everything else from the 70 with unchanged therapy and dose.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None (seizure control is in the companion paper).

## Covariates
Body weight enters the per-kg clearances; no other covariates.

## Caveats
Trough-based clearances (trough taken as a surrogate of AUC/tau); no samples at delivery; postpartum baseline. Free
fractions come from all 86 cases (including the 16 with dose changes), concentrations and clearances from the 70 with
unchanged dose, so the free fraction (7.2% to 10.1%, x1.40) and the ratio of mean free to mean total concentration
(6.1% to 13.2%, x2.15, derived) disagree; the paper does not reconcile them.
