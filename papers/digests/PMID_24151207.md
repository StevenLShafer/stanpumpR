# Van Wart 2014: pooled population PK of furosemide (plasma and urine)

## Study
- Meta-analysis in NONMEM 7.1.2 (ADVAN13, FOCE-I). Individual data from an Otsuka tolvaptan-furosemide interaction study (Shoaf et al.): 6 healthy men, 80 mg oral Lasix (2 x 40 mg tablets) fasting, alone or with tolvaptan 30 mg; plasma at 0.5-24 h, urine hourly to 8 h then 8-12 and 12-24 h (PDF p2).
- Supplemented with individual and aggregate ("meta-individual") plasma and urine profiles digitised (Engauge) from 28 publications (Table 1, PDF p4): IV or oral IR furosemide 10-500 mg (also 0.5 and 1 mg/kg IV) in healthy subjects, CHF, cirrhosis, elderly and renal failure. 67.4% of digitised profiles were aggregate.
- Total 45 individuals/meta-individuals, 624 plasma concentrations, 779 urine amounts (PDF p6). 36 male, 9 mixed-sex groups. Mean (SD) age 37.6 (15.8) y, weight 73.3 (7.83) kg, CLCR 102 (30.5) mL/min (Cockcroft-Gault). Renal impairment mild n=3, moderate n=2, severe n=2; CHF n=9; cirrhosis n=4.
- Assay (Otsuka data): reverse-phase HPLC, LLOQ 0.02 mg/L plasma, 1 mg/L urine. 16 BLQ plasma values excluded; M3 failed to converge.
- External qualification set: individual CLR vs CLCR from refs 20, 28, 31, 34 (Figure 4). PC-VPC (1000 sims), bootstrap (200 replicates, PsN 3.1).

## Model and parameters
Three compartments, first-order elimination split into renal (to a urine compartment) and non-renal clearance. IV input zero order (k0); oral input from a depot with a Weibull-type rate (PDF p5, rendered):

dApo/dt = -(1 - exp(-(ka*TSLD)^gamma)) * Apo, initial condition Dpo (times F); TSLD = time since last dose.
dAe/dt = CLR * (Ac/Vc), urine compartment reset to zero at the end of each collection interval.

Note the printed rate coefficient (1 - exp(...)) is dimensionless; it rises from 0 toward an asymptote of 1 (per hour, implicitly), with ka setting only the time scale. This is the same printed form as the companion hydrochlorothiazide paper (PMID 24123104).

Renal clearance covariate (Table 2 footnote a, rendered p7): CLR = 3.97 * (CLCR/102), CLCR in mL/min. A power exponent of 0.977 was estimated first and simplified to linear (PDF p7). CHF on Vc was significant in forward selection but dropped in backward elimination.

Derived (not in the paper; derived: eigenvalues of the three-compartment micro-constants from Table 2, CL = CLR + CLNR): total CL 6.69 L/h at CLCR 120 (renal fraction 70%); half-lives 0.34, 1.27 and 13.4 h at CLCR 120; 0.43, 1.78 and 15.9 h at CLCR 30. The long terminal phase comes from Vp1 18.1 L with slow CLd1 1.10 L/h. The paper itself quotes no half-life for its model; its introduction cites literature elimination half-lives of 0.5-2 h and clearance 0.09-0.18 L/h/kg (PDF p2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None fitted. The paper is PK only; it argues that urinary excretion rate (not plasma concentration) is the driver of diuresis/natriuresis and offers the model as a PD driver for later work (PDF p2, p9). No EC50, ke0 or effect-site term. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
CLCR (Cockcroft-Gault, mL/min) on CLR, linear, normalised to 102 mL/min. Age, weight, CHF, cirrhosis not retained (CHF on Vc dropped at backward elimination). No body-size scaling: all volumes and clearances are absolute (L, L/h).

## Caveats
- Most data digitised aggregate means; ecological bias acknowledged (PDF p10). Renal-impairment data all from the literature and aggregate.
- Formulations differed across studies; healthy volunteers predominate; mostly male.
- Internal inconsistency: the text says IIV was estimated only for CLR, CLNR, Vc, CLd1, ka and gamma (p6, p8) and could not be estimated for CLd2 and Vp2 (p10), yet Table 2 (rendered p7) prints "omega2 for Vp2" 0.232 (48.2% CV). Which parameter that row belongs to is not settled by the paper (open question). It does not affect typical values.
- The Vc row in Table 2 carries footnote b, which is the residual-variability footnote; apparently a typesetting slip (no covariate on Vc in the final model).
- Weibull rate term as printed is dimensionless (see above); ka's IIV bootstrap %CV is 84.6.
