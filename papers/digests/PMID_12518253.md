# von Mach 2002: insulin lispro vs aspart, PK/PD in healthy men

## Study
- Randomised, double-blind, two-period crossover (washout at least 7 days), 20 healthy non-smoking Caucasian men,
  22-31 y (mean 24.9 +/- 2.0), BMI mean 22.9 +/- 2.2 kg/m2, fasting glucose mean 4.3 mmol/L (p2/417). Body weight
  not reported; the discussion says the dose averaged about 4 units (p4/419).
- Single SC dose 0.05 U/kg of insulin lispro (Humalog U-100) or aspart (NovoRapid U-100) into the abdominal wall,
  8-mm needle, after a 12-h fast; fasting throughout the 5-h sampling.
- Samples: pre-dose, every 5 min to 20 min, every 10 min to 90 min, every 15 min to 150 min, every 30 min to 300 min.
- Assays: serum insulin by BioChem RIA (antibodies to human insulin, no analogue specificity), validated by
  spiking human, lispro and aspart into fasted pooled serum at 5-80 mU/L (Fig 3): differences under 10% in the
  5-40 mU/L range (p3/418). The subjects' insulin values are total immunoreactive insulin; endogenous insulin was
  not subtracted (C-peptide shown separately). C-peptide by RIA; plasma glucose by hexokinase.
- Statistics: mean +/- SEM; repeated-measures ANOVA, then point-by-point two-tailed t-tests. No PK model, no AUC,
  no clearance or half-life.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points: plasma glucose and C-peptide suppression (fasting healthy men). No EC50, ke0 or effect model.
- Glucose nadir: aspart 3.2 +/- 0.1 mmol/L at 50 min vs lispro 3.5 +/- 0.1 at that time (p = 0.026); lispro minimum
  3.4 +/- 0.1 at 60 min. At 90 min aspart 3.5 vs lispro 3.7 (p = 0.047). tmin glucose 59.3 +/- 3.4 (aspart) vs
  63.5 +/- 5.3 min (lispro), ns (p2/417, Fig 1). Baseline 4.3-4.4 mmol/L.
- C-peptide: baseline 456 (lispro) / 447 (aspart) pmol/L; at 40 min 225 (aspart) vs 309 (lispro) pmol/L
  (p = 0.031); minimum at 105 min, 117 (lispro) and 105 (aspart) pmol/L (Fig 2).
- Comparator for every "higher/lower/faster": aspart vs lispro in the same 20 subjects at the same weight-based
  dose. The authors' inference of faster aspart absorption assumes equal clearance of the two analogues (citing
  Kang 1991); clearance was not measured. They note the result contradicts Hedman 2001 (type 1 diabetes, 10 U,
  with a meal), where lispro rose and fell slightly faster.

## Covariates
None studied.

## Caveats
- Small (n = 20), young healthy men only, one low dose (0.05 U/kg); authors say differences may be dose dependent.
- Non-specific RIA; residual analogue-specific bias not excluded (authors). Fig 3's caption colour key (human white,
  lispro grey, aspart black) is all the figure gives; the text gives no numeric recoveries.
- Insulin values include endogenous insulin (baseline 7-8 mU/L, and C-peptide falls during the study), so the
  exogenous increment is smaller than the reported concentrations.
- The mean-curve peak time (40 min) and the mean individual tmax (33 min) are different statistics.
