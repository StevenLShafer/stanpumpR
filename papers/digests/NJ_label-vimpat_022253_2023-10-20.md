# Vimpat label 2023: lacosamide prescribing information

## Study
An FDA-approved label for tablets (50/100/150/200 mg), a 200 mg/20 mL single-dose IV vial and a 10 mg/mL oral solution. Indications: partial-onset seizures from age 1 month; adjunctive therapy for primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures from age 4 years.

Dosing (Table 1, 2.1):
- Adults: monotherapy starts at 100 mg bid, adjunctive at 50 mg bid, rising weekly by 50 mg bid. Maintenance is 150-200 mg bid as monotherapy and 100-200 mg bid as adjunctive therapy. The maximum is 200 mg bid; more than 400 mg/day was not more effective.
- Children are dosed by weight, e.g. 2-4 mg/kg bid at 30 to < 50 kg. Under 6 kg the IV dose is 0.66 mg/kg tid initially and 2.5-5 mg/kg tid for maintenance.
- Optional loading (Table 2): 200 mg once, then 100 mg bid 12 h later. A single 200 mg load approximates the steady state of 100 mg bid (12.3).
- IV use is for when oral administration is temporarily not feasible, at the same regimens as oral. The table footnote says oral and IV dosages are the same unless specified (2.1). Infusions run 30-60 min, or as fast as 15 min in adults (2.7). Clinical experience with IV dosing is limited to 5 days.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Section 12.3 gives the following.

- Absolute oral bioavailability about 100%, negligible first pass, no food effect.
- Tmax 1-4 h after oral dosing; Cmax at the end of an IV infusion.
- 30- and 60-min IV infusions are bioequivalent to the tablet. A 15-min infusion met bioequivalence for AUC but not Cmax (Cmax 20% higher). The oral solution is bioequivalent to the tablet.
- Elimination t½ about 13 h, unaltered by dose, multiple dosing or IV administration. Steady state in 3 days on bid dosing. PK are dose proportional over 100-800 mg and time invariant.
- Vd about 0.6 L/kg (close to total body water); protein binding under 15%.
- Elimination ([14C], 100 mg oral and IV): about 95% of radioactivity in urine, under 0.5% in faeces. In urine, about 40% of the dose is unchanged drug, about 30% O-desmethyl metabolite (inactive; plasma exposure about 10% of parent; formed by CYP3A4, 2C9 and 2C19), and about 20% a polar fraction. The metabolite has Tmax 0.5-12 h and t½ 15-23 h.
- Pediatric population PK (1655 patients aged 1 month to < 17 y): typical t½ 7.2 h at 10 kg, 10.6 h at 28.9 kg and 14.8 h at 70 kg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
A PK-PD (efficacy) analysis pooled from 3 partial-onset trials found exposure correlated with reduction in seizure frequency, without additional benefit above 400 mg/day (12.2). No model, coefficients or target concentration are given. PR interval rose with dose: placebo-subtracted maximum +7.3 ms at 400 mg/day and +11.9 ms at 800 mg/day in healthy subjects, with no QTc prolongation. The label gives no therapeutic range. Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or tPeak.

## Covariates
- Renal function. AUC rose about 25% in mild (CLCR 50-80) and moderate (30-50) impairment and about 60% in severe (≤ 30 mL/min), with Cmax unaffected. In severe impairment or ESRD, reduce the maximum dose by 25%; CLCR is Cockcroft-Gault in adults and Schwartz in children. A 4-h hemodialysis cuts AUC by about 50%; supplement up to 50%.
- Hepatic function. Child-Pugh B raises AUC by about 50-60%; reduce the maximum dose by 25% in mild or moderate impairment. Not recommended in severe impairment.
- Age. Elderly (> 65 y) have about 20% higher dose- and weight-normalised AUC and Cmax.
- Sex, race and CYP2C19 genotype: no clinically relevant effect.
- Enzyme inducers (carbamazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin): 15-20% lower concentrations in population PK.

## Caveats
Labels summarise without variability for most values. The half-life of 13 h is from healthy-volunteer studies; the pediatric population PK gives 14.8 h at 70 kg. Overdose above 800 mg causes conduction disorders and seizures, and doses of several grams have been fatal (section 10); no toxic concentration is given.
