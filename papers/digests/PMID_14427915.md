# Nunn 1960: dead space and arterial to end-tidal PCO2 difference under anaesthesia

## Study
12 fit adult patients (Table 1, PDF p. 2: ages 16-47, mean 35 y; weight mean 63 kg; BSA mean 1.69 m2; 8 F / 4 M by the printed rows; routine surgery) under thiopental induction (200-400 mg) and nitrous oxide-oxygen (70:30) maintenance, with pethidine or more thiopental, plus a muscle relaxant in every case and a cuffed tracheal tube ending about 5 cm above the carina. Each was studied during artificial ventilation (bellows, 16.0-16.4 breaths/min, constant stroke volume, 25-35 min after induction) and again, towards the end of surgery, when breathing spontaneously. Measured together: anatomical dead space (Fowler single-breath, CO2 indicator, infrared analyser, mean of six determinations, SD 8.5 mL spontaneous and 8.8 mL artificial per measurement), physiological dead space (Bohr equation using arterial PCO2 by the Astrup method and mixed expired CO2 from a 5 min collection, with apparatus dead space subtracted), alveolar dead space (physiological minus mean anatomical), end-tidal PCO2, and CO2 output. Volumes are BTPS. The measured dead space is that below the tracheal tube tip (upper airway bypassed).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None: no drug concentrations or effects; not an opioid paper (opioid audit: none). The respiratory end points are dead space and the arterial minus end-tidal PCO2 difference. Authors' conclusions: physiological dead space was a roughly constant fraction of tidal volume (about 0.3) across VT 163-652 mL, independent of spontaneous or artificial ventilation; the a-ET PCO2 difference did not depend on VT or arterial PCO2 (Fig. 5); the alveolar dead space was about proportional to VT (Fig. 4). Authors' suggested rule: alveolar ventilation approximately 0.7 x minute volume in an intubated anaesthetised patient (Discussion, PDF p. 5). Correlation of (a-ET PCO2)/PaCO2 with VD(alv)/VT: r = 0.46 (Fig. 6).

## Covariates
Tidal volume only (fraction of VT; anatomical dead space falls in proportion to VT below 350 mL). Weight: anatomical dead space below the tube was 48% of body weight in lb for VT above 350 mL (Discussion). No age or sex analysis.

## Caveats
n = 12, healthy adults, nitrous oxide anaesthesia with a muscle relaxant, no volatile agent; the study did not reach a steady CO2 state (authors say arterial PCO2 settles by about 20 min but VCO2 and R may take 60 min), and the patients were over-ventilated (mean PaCO2 32.9) under artificial ventilation and under-ventilated (51.7) when breathing spontaneously. Spontaneous and artificial data come from the same patients at different times. Apparatus dead space was subtracted, so the 0.3 refers to the patient's own dead space below the tube, not including the breathing circuit. The paper reports CO2 dead space only, not volatile-agent or oxygen end-tidal differences. Possible error in VD(phys) up to +/-12% (methods).
