# Topamax label 2026: topiramate prescribing information

## Study
Regulatory label, not a study. Products: TOPAMAX tablets 25, 50, 100, 200 mg; sprinkle capsules 15 and 25 mg
(Section 3). 12.3 states the sprinkle capsule is bioequivalent to the immediate-release tablet. No intravenous
product. Underlying studies are summarised without design detail (n given only for some: elderly n = 16;
paediatric popPK 1217 subjects including 258 aged 2 to <16 y; obese-children popPK 129).

Specific populations (12.3): clearance reduced 42% (CrCl 30-69 mL/min/1.73 m2) and 54% (CrCl <30) vs
normal (>70); hepatic impairment (moderate to severe) plasma clearance down a mean 26%; elderly (65-85 y,
n = 16, CrCl about 20% lower): plasma and renal clearance down 21% and 19%, half-life 13% longer, Cmax 23% and
AUC 25% higher; no effect of gender or race on clearance. Paediatric: higher clearance per kg than adults,
higher still in younger children; clearance independent of dose. Obese children: Css,avg up to 20% and trough
up to 19% lower, no dose adjustment.

Table 11 (12.3, PDF p. 39; the page prints no page number; read on the page image 2026-10-14), effect of the
co-administered AED on topiramate concentration: phenytoin 48% decrease; carbamazepine 40% decrease; valproic
acid 14% decrease; lamotrigine 13% decrease; phenobarbital, primidone and CBZ epoxide not evaluated. Effect of
topiramate on the AED: phenytoin no change (<10%) or 25% increase in some patients (generally on twice-daily
phenytoin); valproic acid 11% decrease; carbamazepine, CBZ epoxide, phenobarbital, primidone no change;
lamotrigine no change at topiramate up to 400 mg/day.

Non-AED interactions (12.3, PDF pp. 39-40, page images):
- Hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg q24h with topiramate 96 mg q12h, healthy volunteers, steady state: topiramate Cmax
  +27% and AUC +29% when HCTZ was added; clinical significance unknown; HCTZ kinetics unchanged; serum potassium
  fell more with the combination (p. 39).
- Oral contraceptive (norethindrone 1 mg + ethinyl estradiol 35 mcg): topiramate 50-200 mg/day alone, no
  significant change in either component; in patients also taking valproic acid, ethinyl estradiol exposure
  fell 18%, 21% and 30% at topiramate 200, 400 and 800 mg/day; norethindrone not significantly affected in
  either study; clinical significance not known (p. 39).
- Digoxin AUC -12% (single dose, p. 39). Metformin Cmax +18% and AUC0-12h +25% with topiramate 100 mg q12h;
  topiramate oral clearance appears reduced with metformin, unquantified (p. 40). Pioglitazone AUC -15% (not
  significant), its hydroxy metabolite Cmax/AUC -13%/-16%, keto metabolite -60% (p. 40). Glyburide Cmax -22%,
  AUC24 -25% with topiramate 150 mg/day, topiramate unaffected (p. 40). Lithium unaffected at topiramate
  200 mg/day, Cmax +27% and AUC +26% at up to 600 mg/day (p. 40). Haloperidol single dose unaffected (n = 13);
  amitriptyline AUC and Cmax +12% at topiramate 200 mg/day (n = 18) (p. 40).

Dosing (Section 2): adult monotherapy target 400 mg/day in two divided doses, titrated weekly from 25 mg
twice daily (Table 1); adult adjunctive 200-400 mg/day in two doses; migraine 100 mg/day in two doses;
children 2-9 y by weight (Table 2); renal impairment (CrCl <70 mL/min/1.73 m2) half the usual dose;
supplemental dose may be needed with haemodialysis.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No therapeutic or toxic concentration range is given in the label, and no numerical concentration-effect function
or EC50. Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

Qualitative exposure-response (14.1 Monotherapy Epilepsy, Pediatric Patients 2 to 9 Years of Age, PDF p. 43): the
initial-monotherapy indication at 2-9 y rests on pharmacometric bridging from the controlled epilepsy trials. The label
states the exposure-response relationship was similar in children down to 2 y and adults on adjunctive therapy, and in
patients 6 to <16 y and adults on initial monotherapy; 2-9 y dosing came from simulations using the plasma exposure
ranges observed in children and adults on initial monotherapy. No exposure-response parameters are given.

## Covariates
Renal function, hepatic impairment, age (via renal function), enzyme-inducing AEDs, lamotrigine, HCTZ; categorical
or percentage statements only, no equations.

## Caveats
- The 80% is tablet vs oral solution (relative), not absolute oral bioavailability; the label gives no IV data.
- Label half-life 21 h is shorter than the ~30 h of modern IV-tracer work; summary values are means without
  population detail.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **tmax 1-2 h (topiramate.R:31-32)**: label gives approximately 1 to 2 h after 100 mg (elderly vs young
  comparison) and approximately 2 h after 400 mg. **Matches** (the 1-2 h figure is the 100 mg statement).
  ka 2.0/h giving a 1.2 h peak was a calibration, not a label value; on the library's disposition it in fact peaked at 1.81 h, and since 2026-11-01 ka is Ahmed 2015's 2.38 /h (peak 1.5 h).
- **"the label's figure of 80 per cent is contradicted by both intravenous studies" (topiramate.md:7;
  topiramate.R:29-30 "The handoff's F of 80%")**: **discrepancy in characterisation**. The label's 80% is the
  relative bioavailability of the tablet against an oral solution, not an absolute oral F; it is not
  contradicted by Clark 2013 / Ahmed 2015 (absolute F ~1 of the formulations studied) in the way stated. The code's
  bioavailability_PO = 1 is unaffected; only the wording of the help/header is wrong about what the label claims.
- Context checks (not cited locations): label CL/F 20-30 mL/min (1.2-1.8 L/h) brackets the model's 1.31 L/h
  (R/drugs_topiramate.R:77) — consistent. Label t½ 21 h vs model ~30 h (header line 39 cites 20-30 h) — the
  model is at the long end; not a transcription issue. Sprinkle capsule bioequivalent to tablet; the app offers
  only "mg PO" forms, consistent.
