# Persson 1992: postoperative codeine kinetics, IV and oral

## Study
- 12 cholecystectomy patients: 9 women (age 53 ± 14 y, weight 76 ± 12 kg), 3 men (49 ± 5 y, 83 ± 8 kg). All extensive metabolisers of dextromethorphan (CYP2D6 phenotype by dextromethorphan/dextrorphan ratio).
- Day 1 after surgery: 20 mg codeine IV over 1 min (stated as 42 µmol). Day 4: 50 mg oral codeine (105 µmol; Kodein, Kabi Pharmacia) with 100 mL water, fasted.
- Sampling: IV 2.5 min to 8 h (occasionally 12, 24 h); oral 5 min to 8 h (occasionally 12, 24 h).
- HPLC-UV for codeine, norcodeine, morphine, codeine-6-glucuronide. Morphine LOD 3.3 nmol/L (1 ng/mL); some samples re-run at 0.7 nmol/L (0.25 ng/mL).
- Non-compartmental analysis: AUC and AUMC by linear trapezoid 0-8 h plus extrapolation; CL = Dose/AUC; Vss = Dose·AUMC/AUC²; terminal t½ by regression of 3-8 h. Mean (SD).
- Postoperative state: α1-acid glycoprotein raised on day 1 (1.44 ± 0.45 g/L); codeine protein binding cited as 7%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No pharmacodynamic end point measured. No ke0, tPeak or hysteresis.
- No MEAC measured. The Discussion cites a morphine analgesic concentration above 53 nmol/L (16 ng/mL) in paediatric patients (Dahlström 1982), from the literature, to argue that formed morphine is too low to matter.
- No potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Values reported per kg body weight. Dextromethorphan phenotype used only to confirm all were extensive metabolisers.

## Caveats
- Postoperative patients (day 1 IV, day 4 oral), not volunteers; mostly women; n = 12. One patient (5) had CL 22.5 mL/min/kg; the authors could not explain him.
- Vss stated two ways (2.2 and 2.6 L/kg).
- The dose-to-µmol conversion (20 mg = 42 µmol, 50 mg = 105 µmol) implies about 476 g/mol, i.e. a salt, but the salt is not named (derived).
- Morphine undetectable, so no morphine formation estimate.
