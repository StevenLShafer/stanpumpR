# Hagemann 2024: ethosuximide lowers lamotrigine serum concentrations

## Study
Retrospective therapeutic-drug-monitoring analysis, Krankenhaus Mara / Bethel Epilepsy Center, Bielefeld.
Inpatients in whom lamotrigine (LTG) and ethosuximide (ESM) serum concentrations were measured at the same
time between 2017 and 2021, plus the authors' own earlier published cohort (May 1999, their ref 8) to
enlarge the no-ESM arm. **523 samples from 209 patients** (52.6% female); more than one sample for 95
patients (45.5%).

Inclusion/exclusion (printed e74, PDF p 2): morning trough samples before intake only; excluded if LTG was
unmeasured or < 0.5 ug/mL, not at steady state, intake time unknown, non-compliance suspected, or if an
**enzyme-inducing ASM (phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine) was co-prescribed** - too
few such patients for covariate analysis. So the inducer interactions are designed out of this dataset.

Four groups (printed e74-e75): LTG alone (n = 127 samples, 24.3%), LTG + ESM without valproate (n = 209,
40.0%), LTG + VPA without ESM (n = 109, 20.8%), LTG + ESM + VPA (n = 78, 14.9%).

Assay: validated QMS turbidimetric immunoassay (Thermo Scientific) for the recent samples; validated
reversed-phase HPLC for the 1990s cohort. The authors flag the assay change across eras as a limitation but
argue continuous validation makes a systematic error implausible (printed e77).

Analysis: a multiplicative (log-linear) model fitted by **generalized estimating equations** (GEE,
unstructured working correlation, log link), with backward elimination of non-significant terms; IBM SPSS
v27. The model is

`LTG conc = a x (LTG dose/body weight)^b x d^Age x e1^ESM x e2^VPA x f^(ESM x Age)`

Age was mean-centred (mean 20.4 y across samples).

Interpretation given by the authors (Table 2 footnote a and Results/Discussion, printed e76-e77): under
Model 2, for **adults** the ESM effect is e1 alone = 0.86, i.e. LTG serum concentrations are **14% lower**;
for **children/adolescents** it is e1 x f2 = 0.86 x 0.73 = **0.63**, i.e. **37% lower**. With valproate,
LTG concentrations are **94% higher** (e2 = 1.94). Sex was not significant (c = 1.055, p = .300 when added
to Model 1) and no other interaction was significant. The ESM effect did not differ between samples with
and without additional VPA.

Derived clearance-equivalent factors (mine, assuming concentration is inversely proportional to apparent
clearance at a fixed dose): adults **1 / 0.86 = 1.163**; children **1 / 0.63 = 1.587**. The paper itself
never states a clearance factor - it reports concentration ratios only.

Descriptive values, all samples (Table 1, printed e75): LTG daily dose median 300 mg (range 6.25-1200),
dose/weight 5.77 mg/kg (0.22-23.53), LTG concentration 6.79 ug/mL (0.36-23.65 - the text layer interleaves
Table 1's columns; these are read as median then range), level-to-dose ratio 1.14. Patients: median age
19.9 y (1.2-80.8), weight 58.0 kg (10.0-143.0).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Serum concentration only; no seizure outcome, no effect site, no ke0/tPeak, no concentration-response.
The authors' clinical recommendation (monitor levels when ESM is started or withdrawn) is dosing advice, not
a PD model.

## Covariates
LTG dose per body weight (power, b = 0.58), age (as a continuous centred term in Model 1 and as a < 18 y
indicator in Model 2 - **not significant as a main effect** in either), ESM comedication, VPA comedication,
and the ESM x age interaction (significant in both forms). Sex tested and dropped. No renal, hepatic,
albumin or genotype covariate. Enzyme-inducing comedication is an exclusion, not a covariate.

## Caveats
- **Retrospective, with heterogeneous other comedication**; the authors' own limitation list (printed e77).
- **Two eras, two assays** (HPLC in the 1990s, immunoassay 2017-2021), and the no-ESM arm draws on the older
  cohort while the ESM arm is recent - so the ESM contrast is partly confounded with era and method. The
  authors argue against a systematic error but do not quantify it.
- **The mechanism is hypothesised, not demonstrated.** The paper says UGT1A4 induction "has been discussed"
  for methsuximide (a close chemical relative that lowers LTG 53-70%, their refs 8 and 15) and that this
  "might also be true" for ESM (printed e77). No enzyme, metabolite or genotype measurement supports it here.
- **The model is nonlinear in dose** (b = 0.58). A concentration ratio at fixed dose therefore does not map
  exactly onto a clearance ratio; the 1/0.86 = 1.16 conversion is an approximation that is exact only if the
  deviation from proportionality is the same in both arms.
- Age is modelled as moderating the ESM effect although it has no main effect - a two-way interaction
  without a main effect, defensible here but worth noting.
- No monotherapy/alone PK: nothing in this paper estimates lamotrigine clearance, volume or half-life.
