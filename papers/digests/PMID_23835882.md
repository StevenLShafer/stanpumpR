# Quinney 2012: semi-mechanistic CYP3A model of midazolam and nifedipine in pregnancy

## Study
- **Design:** a simulation study, not a new clinical study. The authors' published healthy-volunteer
  semi-mechanistic model of oral midazolam (refs 7-9) is adapted to pregnancy by adding placental and fetal
  compartments (Figure 1, p2). Its parameters are tuned by hand to mean data.
- **Calibration data:** Hebert 2008 (ref 10), 2 mg oral midazolam at 28-32 weeks of gestation and again at least
  6 weeks postpartum (Methods p7). The Figure 2 caption says the data are from 26-30 weeks, and the Discussion
  says 25-34 weeks for both drugs. The paper does not reconcile these. It does not give the number of women.
- **Validation data:** steady-state oral immediate-release nifedipine in 20 women at 24-36 weeks treated for
  preterm labour (Haas 2012, ref 17), dosed 10-20 mg every 4-8 h. Doses of 10 mg were normalised to 20 mg (p7).
- **Model:** two-compartment distribution with gut wall, portal vein and liver organs. Clearances in the gut wall,
  liver and fetal liver come from the well-stirred model, with intrinsic clearance Vmax/(Km + fu·C) in the liver
  (equations 1-3, p6). QH = 3.75 × weight^0.75 L/h, unchanged in pregnancy (eq. 5, p7). Fetal CYP3A7 Vmax is
  scaled from in vitro data (eq. 4) to 88 mg/h.
- **Software:** R 2.13.1 with odesolve, 500 simulated individuals against the calibration data, 1000 for
  nifedipine. NCA in the R PK package (p7).

**Fit to the pregnant data** (Table 2, p3; observed AUC 9.5 ng·h/mL and Cmax 6.4 ng/mL, from Hebert 2008):
- The initial model (Vmax ×2 in both gut wall and liver, giving 5,098 and 5,510 mg/h; fu 0.048; ka 1.17) gave
  AUC 5.7 (-40%) and Cmax 2.1 (-67%).
- The final model (ka 2.5, Vmax,H 4,592, Vmax,GW 2,549) gave AUC 9.5 (-0.9%) and Cmax 4.8 (-23%). Residual sum of
  squares fell from 31.2 to 4.3.

**Postpartum** (p2): the observed AUC0-∞ was 17.4 ng·h/mL and the healthy-volunteer model predicted 16.9 (3%
error). Ratio of oral total clearance, pregnant against postpartum: 17.4/9.5 = 1.83 (derived).

**Sensitivity** (Table 2): QH and ka changed Cmax but hardly AUC. Removing fetal metabolism, or raising it 20-fold,
changed maternal AUC by at most 0.2 ng·h/mL (5.7 to 5.5). In Table 2, fu ×0.5 gives AUC 2.8 and fu ×2 gives 11.5,
the same as Vmax,H ×2 and ×0.5. The text (p5) states this equivalence. The direction is the reverse of what a
lower unbound fraction does to total concentrations in a standard well-stirred liver. The paper gives no
explanation, and its code (supplement) was not available to check.

**Nifedipine validation** (p3): in healthy volunteers, predicted AUC0-8 was 156 ng·h/mL (149-163) against 180
observed (150-210). In pregnancy at steady state on 20 mg every 6 h, AUC0-6 was underpredicted by 11% (210
against 237 ng·h/mL) and Cmax by 3% (178 against 184 ng/mL). The Figure 4b caption says "10 mg ... every 6 h",
but the text says 20 mg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect end point, ke0 or sedation measure.

## Covariates
Pregnancy is applied as fixed multipliers:
- fu ×1.2, from albumin 38.8 ± 2.9 g/L in the third trimester against 47.7 ± 2.5 g/L non-pregnant (p5, ref 23).
- Hepatic Vmax ×1.6 (Table 1: ×1.667). Gut-wall Vmax and QH unchanged.
- Central volume raised with plasma volume, and the peripheral volume takes the rest of the 12.5 kg weight gain.

No gestational-age function.

## Caveats
- Manual fitting to mean curves with no estimation of variability. The conclusion that hepatic CYP3A rises 1.6-fold
  is conditional on the assumed 20% rise in fu and on an unchanged QH.
- The authors say that the unchanged gut-wall clearance needs confirming, for example by semi-simultaneous IV and
  oral dosing (p6).
- All data are oral. The IV clearance of midazolam in pregnancy is not modelled or predicted.
- Gestational ages for the calibration data are stated three ways (26-30, 28-32, 25-34 weeks).
