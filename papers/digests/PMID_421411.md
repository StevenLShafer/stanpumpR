# Dam 1979: phenytoin, phenobarbital and carbamazepine clearance through pregnancy

## Study
Open prospective study, University Clinic of Neurology, Hvidovre (Denmark). **14 pregnant Caucasian women
with epilepsy** (Table I; ages about 21-32). Treatment (p 3): 8 on phenytoin (3 with phenobarbital, 2 with
primidone), 3 on carbamazepine (2 with phenobarbital), 2 on phenobarbital alone, 1 on primidone alone; no
folate, 3 took iron. Pre-dose morning levels 7 to 22 times during pregnancy and after delivery, to 20 weeks
postpartum. **Doses were adjusted to keep levels in the therapeutic range.** Assays: phenytoin by GC,
phenobarbital by UV spectrophotometry, carbamazepine and its 10,11-epoxide by TLC; carbamazepine binding by
equilibrium dialysis. Apparent clearance = daily dose (mg) / trough level (mg/L), in mL/min. For the two women
on primidone, phenobarbital clearance was computed from one fifth of the primidone dose. Friedman and Wilcoxon
tests.

## Model and parameters
No model; per-woman apparent clearances (CL/F) by gestational and postpartum week.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Seizure frequency (Table I): increased in 2, decreased in 2, unchanged in 10; no correlation with clearance,
which the authors attribute to the frequent dose adjustments (pp 5, 9).

## Covariates
Gestational week only.

## Caveats
Small, uncontrolled, doses changed throughout, so dose/level is CL/F at a held level, not at a fixed dose;
for phenytoin, a saturable drug, this ratio depends on the level held. Trough timing only. Phenobarbital
clearance for two women is inferred from primidone dose. 1970s assays. The authors favour increased hepatic
metabolism as the cause; binding of phenytoin and phenobarbital was not measured.
