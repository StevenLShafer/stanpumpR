# Lockwood 2016: pregabalin in breast milk, plasma and urine of lactating women

## Study
Pfizer-sponsored, open-label, non-randomised, multiple-dose PK study (NCT01727791, protocol A0081181), Pfizer
Brussels Clinical Research Unit, December 2012 to August 2013 (PDF p. 2, printed NP2). All authors were Pfizer
employees.

- **Population** (Table 1, PDF p. 4 / NP4): 10 healthy, white, premenopausal, actively breastfeeding women at least
  12 weeks postpartum (median 35.6 wk, range 15-81); age 31.7 ± 4.5 y (24-37); weight 70.8 ± 11.5 kg (52.9-87.1);
  BMI 25.1 ± 4.3; creatinine clearance 135.0 ± 27.8 mL/min (85.6-168.1; equation not stated). Daily milk volume over
  the 24 h after the day-3 dose, median 238.9 mL (110-575). Not pregnant; exclusion included estimated CLcr ≤ 60 mL/min.
- **Dosing**: pregabalin 150 mg orally every 12 h, 4 doses (evening day 1 to morning day 3), no dietary restriction.
- **Sampling**: plasma pre-dose days 1 and 3, then 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 18, 24 h after the day-3 dose;
  urine 0-2, 2-4, 4-8, 8-12 h; breast milk (electric pump, both breasts pooled) 0-2, 2-4, 4-8, 8-12, 12-24 h, extended
  to 48 h in the last 5 women. Infants were not breastfed during the study.
- **Assay**: HPLC-MS/MS (WuXi AppTec); LLOQ 0.0250 µg/mL plasma, 0.0600 µg/mL urine and milk; accuracy -6.4% to 1.3%,
  imprecision ≤ 5.2% plasma, ≤ 4.4% milk, ≤ 8.1% urine (PDF p. 3).
- **Analysis**: non-compartmental (eNCA 2.2.4); AUCτ log-linear trapezoidal over 0-12 h; CL/F = dose/AUCτ; milk times
  are interval midpoints. Pregabalin is not protein bound, so the authors treat the values as unbound.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No ke0, effect delay, EC50 or therapeutic concentration is reported. Adverse events only: 8 of 10
women, 17 events, 12 mild and 5 moderate, none serious; dizziness 6 (60%), headache 2 (20%) (Table 4, PDF p. 7).
Not an opioid paper; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None tested (descriptive NCA, n = 10).

## Caveats
- Lactating women 15-81 weeks postpartum, not pregnant women; 7 of 10 were more than 6 months postpartum, and many
  probably produced less milk than exclusively breastfeeding mothers (authors, PDF p. 7).
- "Lactation appears to have had little influence" is a comparison of CL/F 76.9 mL/min with 75.8 mL/min in healthy
  adults from Bockbrader 2010 (ref. 4, a different study and population), not a within-subject or matched control
  (PDF p. 7).
- Renal clearance (68.2 mL/min) is below creatinine clearance (135 mL/min, Table 1); the introduction (PDF p. 2)
  attributes renal CL below GFR to tubular reabsorption.
- Milk AUCτ uses an interpolated 12-h concentration from collection-interval midpoints.
- n = 10, all white; Pfizer-sponsored, all authors Pfizer employees.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:39**: the row says Lockwood studied lactating women at least 12 weeks postpartum, n = 10,
  150 mg q12h, about 89% of the dose recovered in urine, renal clearance 68.2 mL/min, and little influence of
  lactation on PK. Paper: n = 10, ≥ 12 weeks postpartum, 150 mg q12h, Aeτ 88.6%, CLr 68.2 mL/min (text; 68.16 in
  Table 2), and the "little influence" conclusion. **Matches.** The row uses the paper only descriptively and applies
  no factor, so the comparator behind "little influence" (Bockbrader 2010's healthy adults, see Caveats) does not
  feed a number. The row's own sentence that pregabalin is cleared "almost entirely unchanged by glomerular
  filtration" is not attributed to Lockwood; the paper's urine recovery supports "unchanged and renal", but its
  introduction notes renal clearance below GFR (tubular reabsorption), so "by glomerular filtration" is a
  simplification. The row's `evidence_read` is "abstract"; the full text agrees with what the row draws from it.
- **Uncited comparison with the library's model** (`R/drugs_pregabalin.R:215-217`, Chan 2021, not citing this
  paper). For a woman of the study's mean weight, 70.8 kg, evaluated at total body weight and with normalised CLcr
  above the 96.4 breakpoint (mean CLcr 135 mL/min, Table 1): CL/F = 4.96 × (70.8/70)^0.52 × 0.92 = 4.59 L/h = 76.5
  mL/min (derived), against 76.9 mL/min observed (0.995×); predicted Cavss for 150 mg q12h = 150/(4.59 × 12) = 2.72
  µg/mL (derived) against 2.71 observed. V/F = 39.8 × (70.8/70)^0.70 × 0.83 = 33.3 L (derived), giving a half-life of
  5.0 h (derived) against 5.62 ± 0.66 h observed, about 11% shorter. The model's clearance agrees with these
  postpartum women; the pharmacokinetic-weight setting (`adjustToFFM = TRUE`) was not evaluated here. The library
  plots total plasma concentration, which for unbound pregabalin equals free, as the paper also assumes.
