# Momper 2016: fluconazole population PK in infants <750 g birth weight

## Study
- Substudy of a multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled prophylaxis trial. n = 141 infants, birth weight <750 g, <120 h old at randomisation (PDF p1-2).
- Dose: 6 mg/kg twice weekly (Tue/Fri) for up to 42 days, IV over about 60 min or oral (enteral feeders).
- 619 plasma samples; 15 excluded (7 below LLOQ, 3 collected >120 h after last dose, 3 outliers, 2 rises without a dose), leaving 604. 368 (61%) were scavenged samples. Up to 3 timed samples per infant, plus up to 10 scavenged. About 20% of samples followed oral dosing.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS on plasma, LLOQ 10 ng/mL, CV up to 8.5%. Total plasma concentration (binding about 12% per the introduction).
- NONMEM 7.2, FOCE-I, ADVAN2 TRANS2; 1-compartment, first-order absorption, combined proportional and additive residual error. Clearance scaled by WT^0.75, volume by WT^1.0 before covariate testing.
- Table 1 (median, range; at first PK evaluation): PNA 23 d (3-47); GA 24.7 wk (22.6-28.7); PMA 28.3 wk (23.7-35.1); weight 710 g (345-2,680); SCr 0.7 mg/dL (0.1-3.6); albumin 2.5 g/dL (1.0-4.7). Male 40%; intubated 81%; Caesarean 67%; Black 53%, White 40%.

## Model and parameters
Final model (PDF p4, printed 5542, Table 3, point estimates; SEE = standard error):
CL (L/h per kg^0.75) = 0.0127 x (SCR/0.8)^-0.41 x (PMA/28)^2.05, with SCR in mg/dL and PMA in weeks. V = 1.00 L/kg x WT. ka = 0.96 /h. F = 100% (Table 3 prints 1.00; the text gives 99.5% in the abstract and 100% in Results).

Table 2 (PDF p4) univariable screen, change in OFV from base (9,624): PNA -132, GA -25, PMA -174, SCR -219, Caesarean delivery -7 on CL; PMA -4 and SCR -24 on V. In the final model, SCR plus PMA (written as a function of GA and PNA) beat SCR + GA + PNA by an OFV of 37.3 (text, p4).
Fixed-effect bootstrap medians were within 1% of the estimates. VPC: 3.1% of observations above the 95th percentile and 4.1% below the 5th. A timed-samples-only refit gave CL 0.0119 vs 0.0127 L/h/kg^0.75 (6% difference), other parameters within 10%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model and no ke0. Target used for simulation: trough >2,000 ng/mL (total plasma), chosen from typical Candida MICs in infants (250-4,000 ng/mL, discussion) and adult prophylaxis experience. Monte Carlo (14,100 virtual infants, 6 mg/kg twice weekly): 80% of troughs exceeded 2,000 ng/mL at week 1 and 59% at week 4 (PDF p4). Of observed concentrations, 95.7% of the first and 89.9% of all exceeded 2,000 ng/mL. Observed concentrations: median 4,144 ng/mL (range 491-14,050) scavenged, 6,154 (320-13,167) timed. Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Serum creatinine (exponent -0.41, referenced to 0.8 mg/dL) and PMA (exponent 2.05, referenced to 28 wk) on CL. Weight enters by allometry (0.75 on CL, 1.0 on V). GA, PNA and Caesarean delivery were significant on univariable screening but dropped in favour of SCR plus PMA. No effect of sex, race, ethnicity, intubation or mode of delivery in the final model. Discussion compares the typical CL with Wade et al. (0.015 L/h/kg^0.75 for GA 26 wk, PNA 2 d) as similar.

## Caveats
Only 2 infants had invasive candidiasis, so no exposure-outcome analysis. About 20% of samples followed oral dosing and absorption sampling was sparse, so ka has no BSV. The 2,000 ng/mL target rests on assumed MICs. Infants were extremely premature (median weight 710 g), so extrapolation to older infants is not supported by this data set. The SCR effect is confounded with PNA and GA (authors).
