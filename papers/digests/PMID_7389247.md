# McClain 1980: Intravenous fentanyl kinetics

## Study
- 7 healthy men, 22-29 yr, 65-85.5 kg (Table II mean 75.4 kg in the 6.4 ug/kg group), awake volunteers breathing 100% O2.
- Tritiated fentanyl IV over 90 s: 6.4 ug/kg fentanyl base (= 10 ug/kg citrate; n = 5) or 3.2 ug/kg (n = 2).
- Arterial sampling to 8 h; urine and stool 72 h. Assay: toluene extraction of 3H-fentanyl (radiochemical, with paper chromatography to confirm specificity); total 3H also measured. Plasma, total (bound + free) drug.
- Triexponential fit by nonlinear least squares per subject; 3-compartment micro constants derived.

- 98.6% of the dose leaves plasma within 60 min; combined peripheral volume about 4 L/kg.
- Excretion: <8% unchanged; ~85% of radioactivity recovered in urine and feces in 72 h.
- Fig. 1 overall curve for the 6.4 ug/kg mean is given as a triexponential (coefficients garbled in the text layer).
- Shafer 1990 (PMID 2248388, Table 2) re-expressed this study unscaled as V 26.6/45.8/196 L, CL 0.883/4.59/3.48 L/min, terminal t1/2 242 min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak reported; no EC50.
- Ventilation: all subjects showed marked ventilatory depression in the first ~15 min (4 apneic); slight persistent depression for 2-3 h while plasma fentanyl stayed above 0.7 ng/mL (Fig. 5, compared with PETCO2 data of Harper et al. in 10 other subjects given 6 ug/kg). Mild sedation at 60 min when plasma levels fell below 1.5 ng/mL.
- No MEAC or analgesic concentration reported; no potency ratio.
- Secondary plasma peaks 2-4 h and 5-7 h after injection in all subjects.

## Covariates
None; volumes reported per kg, clearance absolute. Two dose levels: kinetics dose-proportional.

## Caveats
n = 7, healthy young men, awake. Sampling only to 8 h, so the terminal half-life is likely underestimated (Shafer 1990 makes this point). Radiochemical assay. Group-mean micro constants could not be read cleanly from the text layer; treat k values as tentative.
