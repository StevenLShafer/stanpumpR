# Searle/Pfizer 2021: CALAN (verapamil HCl tablets) US label (NDA 018817)

## Study
Not a study: the old-format (non-PLR) US prescribing information for oral immediate-release verapamil HCl
tablets, 80 mg (scored) and 120 mg. Revised February 2021 (PDF p14, rendered); FDA digital signature dated
11/19/2021 (PDF p15, rendered). The archive date in the file name (2022-06-01) is the download/listing date, not
the label revision. Every page carries the banner that the label may not be the latest FDA-approved version.
Adverse-event figures come from clinical trials in 4,954 patients (Warnings and Adverse Reactions, text layer).

Dosing (Dosage and Administration, PDF p13, text layer): individualize by titration; do not exceed 480 mg/day.
Angina 80 to 120 mg three times daily (40 mg t.i.d. if increased response expected, e.g. hepatic impairment,
elderly). Atrial fibrillation in digitalized patients 240 to 320 mg/day; PSVT prophylaxis 240 to 480 mg/day,
divided t.i.d. or q.i.d. Hypertension: usual initial 80 mg t.i.d. (240 mg/day); no evidence of added effect
beyond 360 mg. Hepatic insufficiency: about 30% of the usual dose (Precautions).

Dose-concentration relation is nonlinear; half-life rises during titration/chronic dosing (PDF p3). Metabolized by
CYP3A4, 1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C18 (Precautions, text layer). Not removed by hemodialysis.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Blood pressure: the label states no relationship has been established between plasma verapamil concentration and
  blood-pressure reduction (PDF p3, rendered).
- PR interval: related to plasma concentration in early titration; the relationship may disappear with chronic
  dosing (PDF p3; Warnings, AV block).
- No therapeutic or toxic concentration range is stated. "Therapeutic verapamil plasma concentrations" is used
  (hepatic dysfunction paragraph, PDF p3) without a number.
- Local anaesthetic action 1.6 times procaine on an equimolar basis (PDF p2, rendered).
- No ke0 or effect-site data. Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio applies.

## Covariates
Hepatic insufficiency (half-life 14-16 h, clearance ~30% of normal, dose ~30%); age (half-life may be prolonged
in the elderly); renal impairment (caution, no numbers). Interactions: CYP3A4 inhibitors raise and inducers
(rifampin) lower verapamil levels; verapamil raises digoxin 50% to 75% in the first week. No equations.

## Caveats
- Oral product only; the IV verapamil label is a separate document. Exposure figures refer to oral dosing.
- Old-format label; PK numbers are unreferenced ranges from older studies, and racemic verapamil is reported
  without enantiomer detail (except the sirolimus interaction, S-(-) verapamil).
- The banner says the label may be superseded.
