# Barakat 2008: propofol EC50/EC95 with 70% xenon versus 70% nitrous oxide

## Study
Randomised, double-blind trial, 75 premedicated (temazepam 20 mg, 60 min before) ASA I-II women having elective breast or body-surface surgery. 30 in the nitrous oxide group (N), 45 in the xenon group (X) (PDF p.4, printed 826; the xenon group was extended by 15 patients after the first 30 because the up-and-down sequence had not plateaued). Age 50 +/- 11 (X) and 47 +/- 12 (N) years, weight 61 +/- 8 and 62 +/- 8 kg, height 163 and 164 cm, BMI 23 (Table 1, mean +/- SD).
Propofol by target-controlled infusion at an effect-site target, run from Stanpump with the Marsh pharmacokinetic model (ref. 14, a paediatric-derived set) on a Graseby 3400 pump. Start 4.5 ug/mL in the first patient of each group; Dixon up-and-down, step 0.1 ug/mL (up after a mover, down after a non-mover), with a 0.5 ug/mL step from 2.0 to 1.5 ug/mL late in the xenon series. 15 min of 70% xenon or 70% N2O by tight face mask for equilibration, then skin incision, movement scored over 60 s by a blinded observer (Eger's definition). Blood propofol (HPLC, plasma supernatant; assay CV within-day 5.1-8.5%, between-day 8.5-10%) taken at laryngeal mask insertion and just before incision, in the first 60 patients only. EEG SEF90 and auditory evoked potential index (AEPex) recorded. Probit analysis in SPSS 12.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model fitted. The Marsh model drove the pump; its parameters are not listed. Measured against the predicted (Marsh) blood concentration at incision (Fig. 2, fitted: measured = 0.59 + 0.93 x predicted, ug/mL): TCI bias and precision (Table 3, PE = (measured - predicted) x 100 / predicted): xenon 7.31% / 20.75%, nitrous oxide 13.80% / 28.92%, overall 10.56% / 24.84%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points

Text: EC50 with xenon 1.5 and EC95 2.3 ug/mL (abstract). Reductions vs N2O stated as 32% (EC50) and 72% (EC95). Equipotency ratio xenon:N2O given as between 1.5:1 (from EC50) and 2.5:1 (from EC95), based on published MACs (Discussion, PDF p.5). The authors note a negative lower CI is an artefact of probit analysis, and that with xenon MAC 0.71 the propofol EC50 should be near zero. Earlier studies cited: EC50 with 67% N2O 4.5 ug/mL in premedicated patients (Davidson 1993), 4.9 ug/mL unpremedicated (Stuart 2000), both predicted concentrations.
No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak is reported or estimated. Not an opioid paper (alfentanil was given only after the observation period); no MEAC.
EEG: SEF90 and AEPex did not track propofol concentration; Pk (Table 4, PDF p.4): SEF90 LOC 0.685 xenon, 0.634 N2O; MR 0.693, 0.618; AEPex LOC 0.71, 0.68; MR 0.72, 0.66 (SE 0.08-0.12).

## Covariates
None analysed (single sex, ASA I-II, groups comparable on age, height, weight, BMI).

## Caveats
Female, premedicated only; skin incision only (the authors say the ratio may differ at stronger stimuli). EC95 is an extrapolation from probit analysis of up-and-down data. Predicted and measured based EC values differ (xenon 0.8 vs 1.5; N2O 3.5 vs 2.9). The authors' stated N2O EC values (2.2, 8.2) are not those of Table 2 (open question). Plasma samples at incision may not be at steady state at the effect site.

For the library's propofol model (R/drugs_propofol.R: Eleveld 2018, tPeak 1.6 min, line 276) the paper offers no independent kinetic check: it used Marsh kinetics and never fitted an effect-site rate. Its N2O-group EC50 (2.2-3.5 ug/mL depending on measure and table) sits near the library's default propofol target band of 2.5-4 mcg/mL (inst/extdata/drugDefaults_global.csv line 2; therapeuticRanges.csv line 57), but this is a different end point (movement at incision with 70% N2O, premedicated) and a Marsh-predicted Ce, not an Eleveld Ce; no claim in the repo rests on it. Verdict: not applicable.
