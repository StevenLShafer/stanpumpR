# Babalonis 2016: oral oxymorphone vs oral oxycodone, miosis, respiration, experimental pain and abuse liability

## Study
- Within-subject, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover, ~2.5-week inpatient (University of Kentucky; NIDA funded;
  IND 69,214).
- n = 9 completers (of 12 enrolled): healthy non-dependent prescription-opioid abusers, 30.5 +/- 1.6 y; 7 men,
  2 women; 7 smokers. Weight not reported.
- Seven 6.5-h sessions, >= 48 h apart: oral immediate-release oxymorphone HCl 10, 20, 40 mg; oxycodone HCl 10, 20,
  40 mg; placebo (over-encapsulated tablets). First 3 subjects got oxymorphone in ascending order for safety;
  sensitivity analysis found no group effect.
- Measures: pupil diameter (NeurOptics PLR-200), respiration rate, EtCO2, SpO2, HR, BP; cold pressor (1 °C, 5-min
  cut-off) and pressure algometer (thenar, 40 kPa/s, 1500 kPa cut-off) at 0, 60, 120, 180 min; subjective VAS,
  opioid adjective scales, street value, DSST.
- **No plasma concentrations were measured.** Mixed models (SAS 9.3), Finney parallel-line bioassay for relative
  potency (oxycodone reference).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Miosis**: dose-related for both; oxycodone about twice as potent as oxymorphone per oral mg; trough at
  1.5-2.0 h after the oral dose for both; 20-40 mg effects persisted to 6 h.
- **Respiratory**: oxycodone 20 and 40 mg raised peak EtCO2 and lowered respiratory rate; oxymorphone only at 40 mg
  and less than oxycodone 40 mg.
- **Experimental pain**: oxycodone 40 mg raised cold-pressor threshold and tolerance and pressure threshold and
  tolerance; oxymorphone did not change cold threshold or pressure measures, and raised cold tolerance only at 40 mg.
- **Abuse-liability measures** (dose-response, Results pp. 8-9, Table 1 p. 23, Figures 4-5 pp. 20-21): drug liking
  peaked significantly above placebo after oxycodone 20 and 40 mg and only after oxymorphone 40 mg, whose peak
  (42.8 mm) the authors call comparable to oxycodone 40 mg (46.9 mm; no between-drug difference marked). Time to peak
  liking 1.1-1.6 h for both drugs; Drug Effect, High and Good Effect peaked at 0.97-1.7 h with the same dose pattern;
  no dose effect on Bad Effect or Desire for Opiates. Participant-rated agonist sub-scale: same pattern (OC 20, OC 40,
  OM 40). Observer-rated agonist sub-scale: raised by both 40 mg doses, oxycodone significantly more than
  oxymorphone. Street value: about $0.86-1.40 per mg oxycodone vs $0.60-0.70 per mg oxymorphone; peak values above
  placebo after OC 20, OC 40 and OM 40 (Table 1 bold; Figure 5 filled symbols agree). The Figure 5 legend's sentence
  naming only high-dose oxycodone for street value refers to the time-course post hoc test, not the peak analysis, so
  text, table and figure do not conflict. All timings follow an oral dose; no concentrations.
- **Relative potency** (oral, per mg, oxymorphone vs oxycodone): pressure-pain threshold 0.48; drug liking/effect/
  good/high 0.84-0.85; most assays invalid (preparation criterion) because oxymorphone was much weaker.
- Published equianalgesic claims cited for contrast: oral oxymorphone 2x oral oxycodone and 3x oral morphine (label,
  Gabrail 2004, Hale 2005); parenteral oxymorphone ~10x morphine (Eddy 1959); oral oxymorphone 16% (pain intensity
  change) and 7% (peak) as potent as IM (Beaver 1977b). Oral bioavailability 10-11% cited (Endo, Adams & Ahdieh 2005).
- **No MEAC, no concentration-effect relation, no ke0/t½ke0/tPeak** (all timing is after oral dosing, absorption
  included).

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- n = 9 non-dependent opioid abusers; experimental pain; equal mg doses only; no plasma levels, so oral
  bioavailability differences (oxycodone 60-87% vs oxymorphone ~10%) are confounded with intrinsic potency.
- Tables 1-2 are images in this manuscript (no text layer); they were transcribed from page images above. No plasma
  concentrations were measured, so none of these dose-effect values can be placed on a concentration scale.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- R/drugs_oxymorphone.R:78-80 "Babalonis 2016 measured experimental effects after oral immediate-release
  oxymorphone but reports no potency": **discrepancy (wording)**. The paper does report relative potency estimates
  versus oral oxycodone (0.48 for pressure-pain threshold, 0.84-0.85 for subjective VAS items) and a roughly 0.5
  miosis ratio, all per oral mg. What it lacks is a concentration-based potency or MEAC. The code's conclusion that
  equal-milligram oral comparisons cannot supply a concentration potency is correct, but "reports no potency" is not.
- R/drugs_oxymorphone.R:102-105 "measured experimental effects after ORAL ... oxymorphone, where absorption dominates
  the onset": **matches** (pupil nadir 1.5-2 h for both drugs after oral dosing; no IV arm; no plasma levels).
- Audit note (derived, not in the paper): if the 0.5 per-mg miosis ratio were corrected only for bioavailability
  (oxycodone F 0.67 in stanpumpR vs oxymorphone F 0.10), systemic oxymorphone would be about 3.4x as potent as
  oxycodone. stanpumpR's provisional MEACs (oxymorphone 0.8 ng/mL, R/drugs_oxymorphone.R:137; oxycodone 12 ng/mL)
  imply about 15x in concentration terms. Clearance differences make this only a rough indication, but the miosis
  data do not support a potency as high as 15x and could inform the planned opioid MEAC audit.
