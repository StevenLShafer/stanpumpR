# Delavenne 2017: heparin anti-Xa PK and ACT Emax model on cardiopulmonary bypass

## Study
- **Design:** prospective, observational, routine-care study of 50 consecutive adults having cardiac surgery
  with CPB, University Hospital of Saint-Etienne, February to April 2016 (p2).
- **Population (Table 1, p3, rendered):** medians (range): age 67.5 (36-81) years, 38 men and 12 women, weight
  77.5 (40-133) kg, BMI 27.2, GFR 74.2 (33.9-107) mL/min/1.73 m², ASA I/II/III/IV 1/18/25/6.
- **Bypass:** normothermic CPB, flow 2.0-2.4 L/min/m², 1500 mL prime (500 mL HES plus crystalloid), cell
  salvage, haematocrit kept at 25-30% (p2).
- **Heparin (p2):** 300 IU/kg bolus, then 5000 IU boluses as needed to maintain an ACT >400 s. Protamine was
  dosed to bring the ACT back within 10% of baseline. Table 1: CPB time 128 min; first dose 25,000 IU, total
  35,000 (20,000-55,000) IU, 4 (1-8) boluses.
- **Samples (p2):** ACT by Hemochron Junior2 at baseline and regularly. A venous citrated sample at each ACT for
  anti-Xa (BIOPHEN Heparin chromogenic assay on a BCS analyser), calibrated 0-1.55 IU/mL, prediluted 1:5 or
  1:10 above that, LLOQ 0.05 IU/mL. In all, 361 anti-Xa activities and 341 ACTs (p3).
- **Analysis:** joint PK/PD in MONOLIX 4.3.2 (SAEM), BLQ left-censored. Covariates entered on log scale as
  log(BW/70) power terms. Three further patients were used for Bayesian (MAP) prediction (p3).

## Model and parameters
**Structure (p3, equations rendered):** a two-compartment model of anti-factor Xa activity with linear
elimination; IV bolus doses enter the central compartment. One- vs two-compartment and linear vs Michaelis-Menten
elimination were tested (p2). There was no relevant IIV on Q or VP. Weight was significant on VC (ΔOFV −39) and CL
(ΔOFV −13.16). The VC coefficient was estimated at 1.02 and fixed to 1. Weight reduced the IIV of CL by 11% and of
VC by 15%. Age, GFR, sex and ASA status had no effect (p3).

**Half-life (derived):** the paper's "elimination half-life" of 153 min (p5) equals ln 2 × VC/CL = 0.693 × 3.1 /
0.841 = 2.55 h. It is not the terminal half-life. From Table 2 the rates are α = 3.72 h⁻¹ and β = 0.153 h⁻¹, a
terminal t½ of 4.5 h. The paper's comparison values for Jia 2015 (3.0 L and 107 min) are the same quantity,
0.693 × 3.04 / 1.18 = 107 min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **End point:** the activated clotting time (Hemochron Junior2), as a direct Emax function of central anti-Xa
  activity: ACT = ACT0 + Emax × CC/(C50 + CC). There is no effect compartment and no ke0 (p4). A linear PK/PD link
  was tested and was worse. No covariate affected the PD parameters.
- **Observed (p5):** all patients kept an ACT >400 s, and 20% had values above 600 s. Some falsely high ACTs did
  not match the anti-Xa activity.
- **Simulations for a 70 kg patient (Fig 3, p6):** 350 IU/kg plus 5000 IU hourly, against 300 IU/kg plus
  55 IU/kg/h. The infusion controlled the ACT within the first 0.5 h and reduced its variability. The authors add
  that repeated boluses do not reliably keep the ACT above 400 s (p6).
- **Protamine (Table 1; pp5-6):** the model's protamine estimate assumes a 1:1 heparin:protamine ratio, giving a
  median 23,475 IU (12,443-40,492). The ratio of the given first protamine dose to this estimate was 0.86 (range
  0.59-1.44). 30% of patients needed a second dose.

## Covariates
Body weight only, on VC (exponent 1) and CL (0.767), centred at 70 kg.

## Caveats
- One centre, 50 patients, normothermic CPB, a short course. IIV remained large, and the authors say weight-based
  dosing alone is not enough, so monitoring is still needed (p6).
- The ACT is device-specific (Hemochron Junior2, upper limit 1000 s).
- **Abstract versus Methods:** the abstract (p1, rendered) says the boluses were given "to maintain ACT <400 s".
  The Methods (p2) and Discussion (p5) say the aim was to keep the ACT >400 s, and that all patients did. The
  abstract's "<" contradicts the paper's own body. It is a source typo.
- **Source typo (p3):** the per-patient means of 7.2 (3-12) for anti-Xa and 6.8 (3-13) for ACT are printed with
  units (IU/mL and s), but they are evidently numbers of samples per patient. 361/50 = 7.2 and
  341/50 = 6.8 (derived).
- All 50 patients were in the estimation (p3); the three patients used for prediction were additional.
