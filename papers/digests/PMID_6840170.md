# Högstedt 1983: increased oral clearance of metoprolol in pregnancy

## Study
- Five women aged 26-28 years with hypertension detected in pregnancy (patient 1 possibly borderline essential
  hypertension), no proteinuria (p1). Paired design: a single oral dose of **metoprolol tartrate 100 mg** in the last
  trimester (completed weeks 29, 35, 33, 33, 34), and again 12-23 weeks after delivery (15, 23, 21, 12, 22), all still
  nursing (Table 1, PDF p2, rendered). After the first study dose they took metoprolol + hydralazine until delivery
  (maximum metoprolol 50-100 mg twice daily); whether any drug was still taken at the postpartum study is not stated.
- Weight pregnant/postpartum (Table 1): 68/63, 63.5/55, 73/62.5, 65/55, 100/91 kg (derived means 73.9 / 65.3 kg).
  Patient 5 smoked up to 10 cigarettes/day.
- Fasted overnight; breakfast at 2 h. Plasma at 0, 15, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 240, 360, 480 min; GLC (Ervik 1975)
  (p3). Concentrations in nmol/L.
- Non-compartmental: terminal β by log-linear regression, t½ = 0.693/β, AUC trapezoidal + C_last/β,
  Clo = Dose/AUC, reported per kg body weight (p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect, ke0 or concentration-effect relation measured; the authors speculate that effect per dose would be
smaller in pregnancy, and note no dose-response study in pregnant hypertensives existed.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (paired, same women) is the only one. The 4.4-fold ratio is of **per-kg** values; weight fell from
73.9 to 65.3 kg (derived means), so the absolute CL/F ratio is larger, about 5.0 (derived: 4.4 × 73.9/65.3, or from
mean AUCs 1298/262 = 4.95).

## Caveats
- n = 5, single oral dose, no intravenous arm: the authors say a change in extent of absorption could not be
  excluded by this design and argue for increased hepatic metabolism on indirect grounds (similar tmax and half-life,
  low protein binding ~12%). The paper does not itself separate F from systemic clearance.
- All women were hypertensive and then treated with metoprolol + hydralazine; postpartum women were nursing. No
  CYP2D6 phenotype (not then known).
- Comparator is the same women 12-23 weeks postpartum, not a non-pregnant reference population.
- Absolute Clo appears to use 100 mg as if it were metoprolol base (MW ~267): derived for patient 1 pregnant,
  100 mg / 267.4 g/mol = 374 µmol; 374 µmol / 226 nmol·h/L = 1655 L/h = 27.6 L/min, /68 kg = 406 mL/kg/min vs 407
  printed (postpartum: 108 derived vs 108). 100 mg tartrate is about 78 mg (292 µmol) base, so if the assay reports
  base, the absolute Clo values are about 1.28x too high. The ratio is unaffected. Not used by stanpumpR.
