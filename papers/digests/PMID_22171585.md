# Falcão 2012: eslicarbazepine population PK, interactions and exposure-response

## Study
Pooled sparse data from three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III trials of adjunctive
eslicarbazepine acetate (ESL) 400, 800 or 1200 mg once daily in adults with refractory partial-onset seizures on
1-3 other AEDs (Elger 2009, Ben-Menachem 2010, Gil-Nagel 2009; plus the Elger 1-year open-label extension)
(PDF p3). PK population n = 641 (Table I, PDF p7): age 37.5 +/- 11.7 y, weight 70.7 +/- 15.1 kg (median 69),
height 168 +/- 10 cm, 52% male, 84% Caucasian, CLCR (Cockcroft-Gault, capped at 160) 117.7 +/- 25.9 mL/min;
carbamazepine co-medication 54.8%, barbiturates or phenytoin 13.1%. Samples mostly pre-dose troughs at
randomization and at the end of the 12-week maintenance. Eslicarbazepine assayed by chiral LC-MS/MS (PDF p3).
NONMEM V (FO and FOCE tried), SAS 8.2. PD population n = 1048 (3952 seizure-frequency measures). Sponsored by
BIAL; three authors employees, two paid consultants (PDF p12).

## Model and parameters
A one-compartment model with first-order absorption was tried first; the ADVAN1 base model without an absorption
parameter "failed" (PDF p4). The final analysis fitted log trough concentrations (Cmin,ss) with a user
one-compartment steady-state trough equation, ke FIXED at 0.0693 /h (terminal half-life about 10 h from phase I
studies), estimating only CL/F (PDF p4). Cav,ss = AUC24,ss / 24 with AUC24,ss = daily dose / (CL/F) (PDF p5).
No V/F and no ka were estimated.

Final model (Table II, PDF p8, confirmed on the rendered page):

CL/F (L/h) = (theta1 + D_CAR * theta2 + AED_PB * theta3) * (weight/70)^0.75

| Parameter | Estimate | SEE | 95% CI | Variability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| theta1, intercept CL/F, L/h | 2.36 | 0.0802 | 2.20, 2.52 | |
| theta2, carbamazepine effect, L/h per mg/day | 0.00149 | 0.000149 | 0.00120, 0.00178 | |
| theta3, barbiturate or phenytoin (0/1), L/h | 1.41 | 0.286 | 0.85, 1.97 | |
| IIV on CL/F (exponential), variance | 0.193 | 0.0245 | 0.145, 0.241 | 44% CV |
| residual on log concentration, sigma^2 | 0.00362 | 0.000276 | 0.00308, 0.00416 | 6% |

Derived checks: sqrt(0.193) = 0.44 and sqrt(0.00362) = 0.060, consistent with the stated CVs. Over the
carbamazepine dose range 200-2400 mg/day, typical CL/F 2.66-5.94 L/h (+152% at 2400 mg) (PDF p6; derived
2.36 + 0.00149*2400 = 5.94). Barbiturates/phenytoin add 1.41 L/h, +60% for a typical subject (PDF p6). At the
average carbamazepine dose, 937 mg, CL/F rises 1.40 L/h (PDF p10). The exponent 0.75 was fixed a priori
(PDF p9). Lamotrigine, valproate, topiramate, gabapentin, clobazam, levetiracetam, AST, ALT, ALP, bilirubin,
CLCR, age, ethnicity and sex had no effect.

Individual estimates by ESL dose (Table III, PDF p10, rendered):

The 800 mg CL/F CI (3.00, 3.20) has its upper bound equal to the estimate, probably a rounding artefact.
AUC24,ss = acetate dose / CL/F holds (derived: 400/3.40 = 118, 800/3.20 = 250, 1200/3.00 = 400), so the dose in
the model is mg of eslicarbazepine acetate.

ESL effect on other AEDs (PDF p6, Tables IV-V): carbamazepine CL/F up to +14% (ESL 1200 mg, CBZ 200 mg; additive
model; carbamazepine CL/F without ESL 2.50 L/h at 200 mg to 10.26 at 2400 mg); lamotrigine +0.282 L/h (+12%);
topiramate up to +16% (1.60 -> 1.85 L/h without CBZ); none on clobazam (8.19 L/h), gabapentin, levetiracetam
(renal 5.77 + non-renal 0.831 L/h), phenobarbital, phenytoin or valproate.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: seizure frequency per 4 weeks from patient diaries (log scale), responder (>=50% reduction) and
seizure freedom, against model-predicted Cav,ss (PDF p5). Seizure frequency: Emax model, baseline ln 0.888
(2.4 seizures/28 days), maximum decrease 0.668, half-maximal at Cav,ss 1080 ng/mL, the one imprecise parameter;
near-maximal effect already at the 400 mg exposures (PDF p8). Responder (logistic, Emax): maximum +0.947
logit, half at 5870 ng/mL, baseline slope -0.0159; predicted responder probability 0.33, 0.38, 0.40 at 400,
800, 1200 mg after 12 weeks (PDF p8). Seizure freedom: max 0.446 logit, half at 1370 ng/mL, intercept -0.86
(PDF p8). Fig. 3 median Cav,ss 4598, 10708, 17006 ng/mL at 400, 800, 1200 mg (PDF p12). Concomitant AEDs did
not alter the exposure-response. No effect site, ke0 or hysteresis (once-daily trough-based). Not an opioid:
no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
CL/F = (2.36 + 0.00149 x carbamazepine mg/day + 1.41 x [barbiturate or phenytoin]) x (WT/70)^0.75, total body
weight. The discussion twice says CL/F was affected by "body height" (PDF p9, p12), but height is not in the
model; this looks like an error for bodyweight.

## Caveats
- Only CL/F estimated; ke fixed at a 10 h half-life, so the model says nothing reliable about V/F, ka or peaks.
- Sparse troughs; Cav,ss is a model prediction, not measured.
- No CLCR effect in a cohort with near-normal renal function (mean 117.7 mL/min, values >160 capped), so this
  does not exclude a renal effect in impairment (the authors' own ref. 3 studied renal impairment).
- Barbiturates and phenytoin pooled (limited data); fewer than 100 patients for phenobarbital and phenytoin
  CL/F (PDF p9).
- Carbamazepine effect is on DOSE, not concentration.
- Sponsor-run analysis.

- **CL/F value** (`R/drugs_eslicarbazepine.R:130`, `:141`; `antiseizureParameters.csv:55` "Falcao 2012 / poster";
  help `eslicarbazepine.md:3`; test line 5): code uses 2.43 L/h. Falcao's intercept is 2.36 L/h (Table II).
  The header (lines 18-21) correctly says 2.43 is the poster's base value, but the help page, the
  investigators table, the reference string and the CSV row present the parameters as Falcao's. **discrepancy**
  (attribution; 3% numerically).
- **Structure** (`R/drugs_eslicarbazepine.R:15-16`: "fitted ... one compartment, first-order absorption"):
  Falcao's absorption model failed; the final model is a trough equation with fixed ke, estimating CL/F only
  (PDF p4). V/F 61.3 L and ka 2.34/h (csv:56-57, lines 129-131) are not in Falcao, yet csv:56-58 carry Falcao's
  DOI. **discrepancy** (description and provenance).
- **Dose basis** (`R/drugs_eslicarbazepine.R:27-34, 132`; csv:58): the code says the apparent parameters were
  fitted to eslicarbazepine-equivalent dosing. Falcao's Table III shows AUC = acetate dose / CL/F, i.e. Falcao's
  CL/F is per mg of acetate. For a 70 kg patient without inducers at 800 mg, Falcao predicts Cav,ss 14.1 mg/L
  (derived 800/2.36/24); the library gives 11.8 mg/L (derived 800 x 0.858/2.43/24), 0.83x. Whether this is an
  error depends on the poster's dose basis, which this paper cannot settle. **discrepancy** against Falcao;
  open question.
- **Weight** (line 124, 130): Falcao scaled by total body weight^0.75; the library uses the pharmacokinetic
  (fat-free-mass-based) weight when the switch is on. A modelling choice, stated in the header (lines 44-45);
  V/F scaled linearly is not from Falcao.
- **No CLCR term** (lines 40-42, help :11, used_for): matches (PDF p2, p10), with the caveat above about the
  near-normal renal function of the cohort.
- **Inducer factors** (`antiseizureInteractions.csv:25-27`): 1 + 0.00149 x 800 / 2.36 = 1.505 and
  1 + 1.41 / 2.36 = 1.597, arithmetic **matches** Table II, and the comparator (the same model's no-inducer
  intercept) is right. Two things the paper does not support: (a) 800 mg/day of carbamazepine as the dose that
  yields 8 mcg/mL is the repo's assumption (the paper's mean CBZ dose was 937 mg, median 1025 mg; no
  concentration link given); (b) folds are applied to 2.43, not 2.36, and multiplicatively across perpetrators,
  where Falcao's terms add (see open question). The rows' `evidence_read` ("abstract ... full text not in PMC")
  and `to_obtain` "yes", and `docs/antiseizure-interactions-review.md:268-271` ("STILL OUTSTANDING"), are now
