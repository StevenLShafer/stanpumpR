# Kokki 2012: MEC and MEAC of IV oxycodone after laparoscopic cholecystectomy

## Study
- Prospective open pilot study (Kuopio, Finland, 2005-2006; EudraCT 2005-004596-38). No sample-size calculation.
- 26 consented, 23 had laparoscopic cholecystectomy (3 converted to open, excluded). ASA 1-2, non-obese
  (BMI 18-33), no opioids within 4 weeks, no renal/hepatic/pulmonary disease.
- Table 2 (n = 23): age mean 45 y (median 39, range 28-60); weight mean 74 kg (range 50-97); 17 female, 6 male.
- Anaesthesia: diazepam 10 mg PO premedication, propofol induction, sevoflurane, cisatracurium; remifentanil
  0.5-2.0 µg/kg/min (mean total 560 µg), stopped 10-15 min before end of surgery.
- Oxycodone hydrochloride trihydrate 0.1 mg/kg IV (= 0.078 mg/kg oxycodone base, p. 470) 10-15 min before end of
  surgery. In recovery, pain (NRS 0-10) at rest and with standardized 20 N compression of each of 4 port wounds,
  every 15 min.
- Protocol (Fig. 1): pain >= 3/10 at rest or >= 5/10 on compression -> sample 1 (first MEC); then 2 mg IV
  oxycodone every 10 min until pain < 3/10 at rest and < 5/10 on compression; sample 2 drawn 10 min after the last
  dose (first MEAC). Repeat on pain recurrence (second MEC, sample 3) and relief (second MEAC, sample 4). Rescue
  ketoprofen 100 mg IV if 16-20 mg oxycodone failed.
- Sampling: venous plasma from a cubital-vein catheter in the contralateral arm. Assay GC-MS (SIM), linear
  1-200 ng/mL; oxycodone and oxymorphone LOD 0.5, LOQ 1.0 ng/mL; noroxycodone LOD 1.0, LOQ 3.0 ng/mL (Table 1
  gives precision, CV 2.2-12.8% for oxycodone). Total (not free) plasma oxycodone.
- Statistics: SPSS 17; descriptive; Pearson correlations; intra- and interindividual CVs.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEC** (minimum effective concentration): plasma oxycodone at onset of significant pain (NRS >= 3 at rest or
  >= 5 on wound compression). First MEC mean 21 ng/mL (95% CI 13-29); second 34 ng/mL (11-57). Authors' summary
  20-35 ng/mL.
- **MEAC**: plasma oxycodone 10 min after the dose that achieved pain relief (NRS < 3 rest and < 5 compression).
  First mean 55 ng/mL (19-91); second 47 ng/mL (14-80). Authors' summary 45-50 ng/mL.
- Population: adults (28-60 y) after laparoscopic cholecystectomy with remifentanil-based anaesthesia; assay total
  plasma (venous) oxycodone by GC-MS; not whole blood, not free.
- Variability: MEC intraindividual CV 38%, interindividual 20%; MEAC intraindividual 18%, interindividual 32% (p. 472).
- Cumulative titration dose in 17 responders: 4-18 mg (median 10 mg, 2-9 doses); second phase 2-6 mg.
- Two patients with only mild pain had 17 and 19 ng/mL at 4 h after the 0.1 mg/kg dose.
- Low breathing frequency (6-9/min) in 2 patients; oxycodone concentration correlated with low breathing frequency
  (p = 0.028).
- Metabolites: noroxycodone 3.4-15 ng/mL in 10 patients; oxymorphone 1.0-1.2 ng/mL in 3 patients.
- Earlier values cited for comparison (refs 14, 15): MEC 6-12 ng/mL, MEAC 15-25 ng/mL (Pesonen, cardiac surgery);
  children MEC 3-32 ng/mL (Olkkola).
- **No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis analysis.** Samples were 10 min after a bolus, i.e. not at steady state;
  plasma and effect site need not be in equilibrium. **No equianalgesic or potency ratio.**

## Covariates
None modelled. Sex did not correlate with dose or MEC/MEAC; no correlation of surgical duration or remifentanil
dose with oxycodone need (p. 472-474).

## Caveats
- Pilot, small n (23; 13 for the second cycle), no control group; time-varying non-steady-state concentrations
  (10 min post-bolus) used as MEAC.
- MEAC mean includes 4 patients who never achieved relief (sampled at 71-79 ng/mL after 16-20 mg), which inflates
  the first MEAC.
- Concomitant diazepam premedication, residual remifentanil-induced hyperalgesia possible; antiemetics in 4.
- Dose given as the hydrochloride trihydrate; concentrations are of oxycodone (base, GC-MS).
- Visceral and somatic pain mix after laparoscopy; values may not generalize (authors note differences from
  cardiac and paediatric studies).
