# Miller 2018: cefazolin inoculum effect and mortality in MSSA bacteraemia

## Study
- Prospective observational cohort, 3 hospitals in Buenos Aires, Argentina, January 2011 to July 2014; a subset of a Latin American S. aureus bacteraemia cohort (Seas 2017). Argentina has no isoxazolyl penicillins, so cephalosporins are first-line for MSSA (usual cefazolin 2 g every 8 h, cephalothin 2 g every 6 h, renally adjusted; individual doses not collected) (PDF p. 2).
- 77 adults with MSSA bacteraemia, 89 isolates (one 14-day episode each; polymicrobial, relapse, transfers and death or discharge within 48 h excluded). Mean age 66.9 y in both groups.
- Microbiology at a reference laboratory (Bogota): cefazolin broth microdilution at standard (~5 x 10^5 CFU/mL) and high (~5 x 10^7 CFU/mL) inoculum, top concentration 64 ug/mL, triplicate, three readers. Cefazolin inoculum effect (CzIE) defined as MIC >= 16 ug/mL at high inoculum. Whole-genome sequencing, MLST and BlaZ typing of all isolates.
- End points: all-cause mortality at 7 and 30 days. Univariate tests, Mantel-Haenszel stratification, log-link generalized linear model with stepwise removal (Stata 8.2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- The univariate comparison of 30-day mortality is significant by Fisher's test (P = .034, Table 1), but the crude risk ratio's CI includes 1 (0.91-7.45, P = .074, Table 2); only the adjusted RR (2.65, 1.10-6.42) excludes 1. The abstract reports the univariate P = .034 and the adjusted RR, not the crude RR.
- No difference in 7-day mortality, severity scores (APACHE II, Pitt, McCabe), endocarditis (6 vs 2) or ICU admission. CzIE patients had higher baseline creatinine (median 1.4 vs 0.9 mg/dL, P = .005) and more catheter-related or unknown-source bacteraemia; creatinine > 1.0 mg/dL was not associated with mortality (adjusted RR 1.59, 0.76-3.31).
- Definitive therapy was a cephalosporin in 31 of 42 CzIE and 22 of 35 non-CzIE patients; vancomycin, combinations and others in the rest (Table 1). No exposure (dose, concentration, time above MIC) was related to outcome.
- Genomics: CzIE not confined to one lineage; ST188 with type C BlaZ (8/9 CzIE), ST30 and ST8 (type A) mostly CzIE; ST5 (type B) none.

## Covariates
Not applicable (no PK). Confounders handled: age, Charlson, previous hospitalization, Pitt score, previous S. aureus infection, secondary source, creatinine (Table 2).

## Caveats
Small (20 deaths), single region with a high CzIE prevalence, non-randomized, treatment not uniform and not all cephalosporin, doses unrecorded, 6 patients missing mortality; a sensitivity analysis was unstable (authors, p. 7). Attributable mortality was not used. Association, not causation; the authors call for confirmation.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- antibioticMbc.R:137 and :141, an association of the inoculum effect with higher 30-day mortality in MSSA bacteraemia: **matches** (39.5% vs 15.2%, P = .034; adjusted RR 2.65, 1.10-6.42). The repo's word "associated" is right; the crude RR alone was not significant, which the repo does not need to say.
- antibioticMbc.R:135, CzIE definition MIC >= 16 mg/L at high inoculum: **matches** Miller's definition (MIC >= 16 ug/mL). Miller's high inoculum is ~5 x 10^7 CFU/mL in Methods (10^7 in the abstract); the repo's "10^7 CFU/mL" follows the abstract-level convention.
- antibioticMbc.R:135, "19 to 37% of MSSA": attributed in the repo to Nannini 2009 and the two Rincon papers, not to Miller. Miller's Argentinian cohort had 54.5%, above that range, which Miller itself contrasts with the 19-36% of earlier surveys. Not a transcription error; the range understates the prevalence in this cohort.
- Miller's MIC90 at standard inoculum (1 ug/mL) agrees with the EUCAST MSSA MIC90 of 1 mg/L the repo uses (antibiotics.md:49).
