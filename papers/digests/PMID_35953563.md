# Elarabi 2022: buprenorphine elimination rate constant and illicit opioid use

## Study
Secondary analysis of the STAR-T randomised trial (ISRCTN41645723), National Rehabilitation Centre, Abu Dhabi, UAE.
141 adults with opioid use disorder on sublingual buprenorphine/naloxone film (BUP/NX-F); 139 (98.6%) male; age at
presentation 29.0 (SD 8.16) y in Table 1 (the Results text says 29.9). Weight not reported. Randomised to incentivised
adherence and abstinence monitoring (n = 70) or treatment as usual (n = 71) for 16 weeks.
Dose: total 24-h dose keeping COWS ≤ 5 set as the stabilisation dose; mean 14.8 mg/day (SD 4.0), range 8-24 mg
(Table 1; p3). Whether the dose was given once daily is implied ("24-h dose", trough before "the" dose) but not stated.
Sampling (p3, printed 3215, confirmed on the page image): at steady state (assumed after 2 weeks on an unchanged dose),
a sample 40 min after the dose ("peak"), one 30 min before the dose ("trough"), and the trough repeated 48 h later;
a fourth random sample in the pilot phase validated the model. Buprenorphine quantified by LC-MS/MS in blood/plasma
(the paper says both; matrix not specified further). Free vs total not stated (total presumably).
Elimination rate constant estimated from peak and trough with a pharmacokinetic model described in Elarabi 2020; the
model itself is not given here. Statistics: Pearson/Spearman correlations, linear regression adjusted for arm, SPSS 24.

No peak concentration, AUC, clearance or volume reported.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: percentage of urine drug screens negative for opioids over 16 weeks; secondary, retention.
- Elimination rate constant correlated negatively with % negative opioid screens: Pearson r = -0.570 (95% CI -0.720
  to -0.361); adjusted for arm, standardised beta -0.567 (95% CI -221.57 to -97.44), R² 0.322 (p5, printed 3217).
  No correlation with retention (Spearman rho -0.092).
- Trough concentration and daily dose were not significantly associated with either outcome (Table 2, p5).
- MEAC / analgesic concentration: none measured (not an analgesia study). The discussion (p7, printed 3219, page
  image) quotes from other work a minimum of 1 ng/mL corresponding to 50% mu-opioid receptor occupancy to control
  withdrawal and craving (Greenwald 2003), and 1-3 ng/mL as optimal (Hiemke 2017, AGNP); these are citations, not
  this study's findings. Assay basis (plasma, total) of those cited values not given here.
- ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported.
- Potency / equianalgesic ratio: none. The introduction says norbuprenorphine has 20% of buprenorphine's activity
  (cited, p2) and oral bioavailability is about 14% (Mendelson 1997, cited).

## Covariates
None modelled for PK. Age correlated with % negative screens (r 0.17) but not after adjusting for arm.

## Caveats
Exploratory secondary analysis; almost all men; ke derived from two to three samples per person by an unpublished
(here) model; odd unit and an implausible trough IQR upper bound in Table 1; age differs between Table 1 and text;
Table 2 shows a 95% CI (-0.720 to -0.361) beside the trough row that belongs to the elimination-rate row. Opioid
users on maintenance therapy, not surgical patients.
