# de Rouw 2021: pemetrexed PK in renal impairment

## Study
Population PK of pemetrexed in patients with a wide range of renal function. Rich anonymised phase I data (the renal-impairment study of Mita 2006) obtained from the manufacturer through the Clinical Study Data Request platform. N = 47 patients, 548 paired time/concentration observations over 0-72 h after a single dose (PDF p3, Sec 3.1). NONMEM 7.4. Table 1 (PDF p3): 36 men (77%); age median 62 y (25-79); weight 79.3 kg (48.1-124.3); BSA 1.95 m2 (1.44-2.47); Cockcroft-Gault CrCL 69.4 mL/min (16.8-202.4); CKD-EPI eGFR 73.4 mL/min (14.4-145.6), 42 patients >= 45 and 5 < 45; dose 500 mg/m2 (150-600). Renal-function candidates for CL: Cockcroft-Gault CrCL, MDRD and CKD-EPI (the last two as absolute values, not BSA-normalised). The assay is not described in the main text.

A simulation compared this model with the manufacturer's earlier model (Latz 2006, ref 7): 1000 virtual patients from NHANES age/sex/height/weight, serum creatinine median 110 (men) / 95 (women) umol/L with 25% variability, dose 500 mg/m2 BSA-based, split at CrCL 45 mL/min (PDF p2-4).

## Model and parameters
Three-compartment model, linear. Renal function enters as a linear covariate on the renal part of clearance; CL = CL_NR + CL_R(eGFR). CKD-EPI explained the IIV in clearance best: IIV fell from 38.7% to 21.0% (about 45% explained; PDF p3). Typical values, PDF p3 (printed 651), with 95% CI:

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No concentration-effect model. The Discussion (PDF p4) hypothesises that haematological toxicity may be threshold-driven (time above a concentration) rather than AUC-driven, but gives no threshold value. No therapeutic or toxic concentration range is reported. Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
CKD-EPI eGFR (absolute mL/min, not per 1.73 m2) on renal clearance, linear. Cockcroft-Gault CrCL and MDRD were tested and fitted worse. No covariates on volumes are reported in the main text. Dose in this cohort is per m2; weight 48-124 kg.

## Caveats
Small cohort (47), only 5 with eGFR < 45, few < 30 (the authors' limitation). Phase I data from a study that met toxicity in renal impairment. The main text does not say whether vitamin supplementation was given in that study. The third compartment rests on samples to 72 h. CL_NR has a wide CI (0.24-1.13 L/h). Simulated AUC ratios depend on the comparator (Latz 2006), which was built without patients below CrCL 44 mL/min.

## Verification against stanpumpR
Comparison with the library model, which is this paper's "manufacturer's model" (Latz 2006, ref 7): R/drugs_pemetrexed.R:90-98 has CL = 43.0 + 47.2 x CrCL/92.6 mL/min, V1 6.13 x BSA^1.32 L, Q 14.5 mL/min, V2 3.38 L.
- **Renal share**: the library model at the median CrCL gives 47.2/(43.0+47.2) = 52% renal (derived), consistent with the "about 50%" this paper attributes to Latz. This paper gives up to 84%. Direction: pemetrexed is more renal-dependent here than in the library model, so the library overstates clearance in renal impairment. The library's non-renal intercept of 43 mL/min (R/drugs_pemetrexed.R:138; 2.6 L/h) is about 4x this paper's CL_NR of 0.66 L/h (11 mL/min).
- **Volume**: Vss 15.94 L here (derived) against 16.1 L for the library at the Latz median patient (header line 43). Agrees.
- **Exposure**: AUC ratio 1.7 at CrCL < 45 and 1.2 at >= 45 (Table 2) of this model against Latz. The library's header already warns that its intercept is unsupported below CrCL about 40 mL/min (lines 45-51).
- The exact renal slope cannot be compared (open question).
