# Meid 2024: QSP potassium model with spironolactone, applied to hospital records

## Study
Methods and pilot paper. Takes the published potassium-homeostasis QSP model of Maddah and Hallow (renal K handling, aldosterone feedback, spironolactone/canrenone PK as a two-compartment canrenone model with an Emax effect on the mineralocorticoid receptor) and tests whether it can predict next-day plasma potassium in inpatients from routine records, using Bayesian a posteriori updating (R: rxode2, nlmixr2, posologyr, deSolve, FME, pksensi). Local and global sensitivity analysis (eFAST), identifiability analysis (collinearity index, threshold 15), IIV from 20 randomly chosen historical spironolactone starters (FOCEi), then MAP prediction run twice daily (midnight, 11 am) in 9 pilot patients at Heidelberg University Hospital (Jan-Mar 2023). Pilot patients (Table A2): mean age 68 +/- 12 y, mean weight 87 +/- 14 kg, 22% female, baseline K 4.12 +/- 0.34 mmol/L, eGFR 72 +/- 28, median spironolactone 25 mg [25, 100], median stay 10.2 d; 4 also had oral potassium; no renal impairment, hemolysis or diarrhea by selection.

## Model and parameters
No PK or PD parameter table is given; the model is the Maddah and Hallow one. Five parameters were retained as influential and estimable (Results p3-5): extracellular fluid volume, hyperaldosteronism effect, mineralocorticoid receptor abundance, potassium intake, sodium intake. Plasma potassium normal value was fixed to the population-typical value; GFR and nephron number must be scaled together (single-nephron GFR is the mechanistic quantity).
IIV (CV%) from the 20 historical patients (p4-5): sodium intake 5.2%; extracellular fluid volume 18%; hyperaldosteronism 36%; MR abundance and potassium intake 131% each, capped at 80% in the application. Global-SA ranges (Methods 4.3.2, p9): K intake 0.073-0.084 mEq/min; Na intake 0.01-0.17 mEq/min; ECF volume 10,000-25,000 mL; MR factor 0.8-1.2; hyperaldosteronism effect -0.5 to 0.5. Steady state was reached at t = 3000 min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is plasma potassium (mmol/L) over the next 24 h. Spironolactone effect is canrenone concentration through an Emax model (from the reference model, no values given here). No ke0, tPeak, hysteresis or MEAC. Not an opioid. Performance: authors report "satisfactory" by AFE/AAFE (satisfactory 0.8-1.25; acceptable 0.5-0.8 or 1.25-2) with a tendency to overestimate; where an unexpectedly high K followed a dose, the individual response was overestimated (p6-7).

## Covariates
Patient GFR (CKD-EPI) and plasma sodium used as covariates (last observation carried forward). Co-medication (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics, K supplements) recorded for subgroup assessment only; hyperaldosteronism effect may proxy ACE-inhibitor suppression.

## Caveats
Nine patients, selected as homogeneous; no severe renal insufficiency; dosing-time errors and intra-individual K fluctuation perturb the estimates (simulations, Figures A6 and A7); sodium homeostasis only rudimentary; model lacks angiotensin II and ACTH effects; IIV for some parameters artificially capped.
