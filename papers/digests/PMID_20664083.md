# Short 2010: response surfaces and MAC (editorial)

## Study
Two-and-a-half-page editorial introducing two papers in the same issue (Johnson 2010, remifentanil-sevoflurane; Syroid 2010, isoflurane-fentanyl). It reviews MAC (Merkel and Eger 1963) and response-surface drug-interaction models. No new data, no assay.

## Model and parameters
No model of its own; figures quoted from other work (all secondary):

  - {name: remifentanil_Ce_high, value: 5, unit: ng/mL, source: "PDF p.1", note: "narrows sevoflurane IQR at MAC-equivalent end point from 0.8-1.8% to 0.2-0.6%"}

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, tPeak or hysteresis reported. No MEAC as such: the editorial's concentrations are effect-site remifentanil at a movement-to-pain (MAC-like) end point, not an analgesic MEAC.
- Figure 1 (PDF p.2): propofol-remifentanil response surfaces for hypnosis (lack of response to shouting and shaking) and for tolerance of laryngoscopy, remifentanil axis 0-16 ng/mL, propofol 0-8 ug/mL, thick line the 50% isobole; text says remifentanil alone does not reach either end point at common doses.
- Johnson 2008 (its ref 14): remifentanil or propofol concentrations varied by about a factor of 2 across end points.
- Age: a 60-year-old needs about half the remifentanil dose of a 20-year-old (PDF p.2). No potency ratio between opioids given beyond fentanyl being converted to remifentanil equivalents in Syroid (ratio not stated).

## Covariates
Remifentanil requirement said to depend on age, weight, height, sex and infusion duration (via Minto 1997); obese patients need less per total body weight.

## Caveats
Opinion piece; numbers are second-hand from the cited studies. Models were derived largely in volunteers without sustained noxious stimulus.
