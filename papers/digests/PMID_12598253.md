# Luginbuhl 2003: remifentanil plus small-dose ketamine, experimental pain in volunteers

## Study
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, four-session crossover in healthy volunteers (PDF p2-3). 15 enrolled (8 men, 7 women); 14 completed (mean age 24 yr, sd 2.5; weight 70.6 kg, sd 9.2; height 176 cm, sd 9.2). Two sessions were lost (one computer failure, one hallucinations), so n per arm is 13-14.
Sessions at least 4 days apart: saline; remifentanil alone; remifentanil + ketamine at target plasma 50 ng/mL; remifentanil + ketamine 100 ng/mL.
Both drugs by target-controlled infusion using the Stanpump program: remifentanil with the Minto 1997 parameter set (ref 13), ketamine with Domino 1984 (ref 14). The sequence: baseline, series 1 (15 min after ketamine start, ketamine alone), then remifentanil target 1, 2, 1, then 0 ng/mL, each with 10 min equilibration (S1-S5, Fig. 1).
Ketamine plasma measured by HPLC, detection limit 10 ng/mL, CV 2.1% at 106 ng/mL; seven samples per subject. Remifentanil concentrations not measured (the authors considered the predicted values reliable, citing about 20% inaccuracy of the Minto set).
End points: pain detection threshold (mA) to single and repeated intramuscular electrical stimulation, repeated transcutaneous (sural) electrical stimulation; pressure pain tolerance (kPa) on the toes; 10-cm sedation VAS; reaction time; SpO2, end-tidal CO2, heart rate, blood pressure.

## Model and parameters
No new PK model. The PK sets are cited, not reported. Predicted (not measured) remifentanil targets 1 and 2 ng/mL; ketamine target 50 and 100 ng/mL, constant. The 350 ng/mL used in a previous study caused central side effects.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t1/2 ke0 or tPeak reported; the paper says only that remifentanil equilibrates rapidly (ref 16, Minto 1997 II) and used a 10-min equilibration per step.
- No MEAC or minimum effective concentration, and no EC50: only two remifentanil targets (1 and 2 ng/mL, predicted plasma) were tested. Remifentanil alone raised every pain threshold and the sedation score in a dose-dependent way (Fig. 2, percent of baseline, graph only). About 0.2 ng/mL (simulated, series 5) had no detectable analgesic effect (Discussion, PDF p6).
- Ketamine at 100 ng/mL (target plasma, Domino set) alone raised the repeated intramuscular threshold and was sedating at both 50 and 100 ng/mL; the transcutaneous test did not detect it. With remifentanil, ketamine 100 ng/mL raised intramuscular single and repeated thresholds at both remifentanil targets; no added sedation; no effect on pressure tolerance, transcutaneous threshold or reaction time.
- Remifentanil-induced hyperalgesia (pressure pain tolerance falling after stopping, P<0.01) was seen in all sessions but placebo and was not prevented by ketamine; electrical tests showed none. Tolerance (1 ng/mL, S4 vs S2) reached significance only in the ketamine 100 ng/mL session.
- Respiration: end-tidal CO2 at remifentanil 2 ng/mL median 43.5 mmHg (IQR 42-47) alone, 42 (38-43) with ketamine 50, 37.5 (35-47) with ketamine 100, 38 (35-41) with placebo (P<0.05). Lowest SpO2 90% (on nasal oxygen). Maximum CO2 51 mmHg. Heart rate and blood pressure unchanged.
- Dreams in 5 of 14 at ketamine 100 ng/mL; pruritus 64% remifentanil alone.
- No equianalgesic or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
Small n; healthy young volunteers; remifentanil not assayed; ketamine concentrations in series 1-2 were below the Domino prediction target (steady state not reached, p730-731); fixed order of remifentanil steps (tolerance and hyperalgesia confounded, as the authors note); pressure tolerance and electrical detection thresholds are different endpoints.
