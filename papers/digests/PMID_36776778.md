# Castanheira 2023: beta-lactamases in US E. coli and K. pneumoniae, 2016-2020

## Study
Surveillance study (INFORM programme, JMI Laboratories; funded by AbbVie). 19 453 consecutive clinical isolates
(13 968 E. coli, 5485 K. pneumoniae) from 56 US hospitals that submitted every year 2016-2020; infection sources
pneumonia 2643, urinary 9999, bloodstream 4152, skin 1407, intra-abdominal 1252 (p.2). CLSI reference broth
microdilution. Isolates with MIC >= 2 mg/L for at least 2 of ceftazidime, ceftriaxone, aztreonam, cefepime, or
imipenem/meropenem MIC >= 2 mg/L, went to whole-genome sequencing (MiSeq, SPAdes; >94% identity, 40% coverage).
Novel CTX-M and OXA-1 variants were cloned into E. coli DH5alpha. Trends by linear regression of yearly
percentages (slope coefficient). No patient or PK data.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Prevalence results (the numbers stanpumpR cites):
- Screened without carbapenem resistance: 3026/19 453 (15.6%): E. coli 2277/13 968 (16.3%), K. pneumoniae
  749 (text p.3) or 751/5485 (13.7%) (Table 2, image p.6). The two K. pneumoniae counts disagree by 2 in the
  source; 2277 + 749 = 3026, and Table 2's 77.3% CTX-M rate is 579/749, so 751 looks like the outlier, but the
  paper does not say.
- ESBL (genotype) overall: E. coli 14.2% (1977/13 968), K. pneumoniae 12.5% (686/5485) (text p.3); these are
  86.8% and 91.6% of the screened isolates; 2663/3026 = 88.0% of screened isolates (abstract and Figure 1 legend
  "ESBL-genotype isolates (2663)" agree).
- Table 1 (E. coli, image p.5): CTX-M 1944 (85.4% of 2277 tested); CTX-M-1 group 1329 (58.4%); CTX-M-9 group
  619 (27.2%); OXA-1-like 720; SHV ESBL 16; TEM ESBL 11; transferable AmpC 226 (9.9%). Carbapenem-resistant
  E. coli 20/13 968 (0.1%). Title count 2297 = 2277 + 20.
- Table 2 (K. pneumoniae, image p.6): CTX-M 579 (77.3%); CTX-M-1 group 554; SHV ESBL 135 (18.0%); OXA-1-like
  368; AmpC 31 (4.1%). Carbapenem-resistant 146/5485 (2.7%), KPC-3 66, KPC-2 58. Title count 897 = 751 + 146.
- blaCTX-M-15 in 1771/3026 (55.5%) of screened isolates: 1193 E. coli (52.4%), 533 K. pneumoniae (71.2%) (p.3).
  Table 1 footnote a prints "One hundred ninety-three CTX-M-15", but its group sum (1329) needs 1193, so the
  footnote has dropped "one thousand" (derived: 1193+104+14+8+6+1+1+1+1 = 1329).
- Trends (Figure 1, image p.4): ESBL-genotype isolates 13.05% (2016), 13.94, 12.90, 13.23, 15.40% (2020) of all
  isolates; by organism E. coli 13.69 to 15.56%, K. pneumoniae 11.50 to 14.98% (significant for K. pneumoniae,
  slope 0.8, P = .05). Significant rises for bloodstream isolates (slope 0.9, P = .045), Middle Atlantic and
  South Atlantic. Carbapenem-resistant isolates fell from 44 (2016) to 28 (2020).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Susceptibility (CLSI breakpoints), not concentration-effect. Against 2663 carbapenem-susceptible ESBL producers:
ceftazidime-avibactam >99.9%, meropenem 99.5%, imipenem 99.7%, piperacillin-tazobactam 50.4%, levofloxacin 20.0%
(p.8). Figure 2A (image p.9): ceftriaxone susceptibility among E. coli ESBL producers is at or near 0% (bar not
readable above 0; transferable AmpC producers about 2%). Table 3 (cloned enzymes in DH5alpha): ceftriaxone MIC
0.12 mg/L for the host, >256 mg/L with CTX-M-15. No MBC, no PK/PD index. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
None (species, infection type, census division, year).

## Caveats
- ESBLs were sought only in isolates with elevated MICs, so the prevalences are those of ESBL genes among all
  isolates as detected by that screen (a lower bound, not a phenotype survey).
- Hospital-submitted isolates from 56 sites that submitted every year; not population-based.
- Source inconsistencies: K. pneumoniae screened 749 (text) vs 751 (Table 2); Table 1 footnote a 193 vs 1193
  CTX-M-15 (arithmetic shows 1193); Figure 1 legend 2574 CTX-M producers vs 2523 in the text (1944 + 579 in
  Tables 1-2), unresolved, perhaps because Figure 1 also counts carbapenem-resistant isolates; Figure 2A legend
  216 AmpC producers vs Table 1's 226, unresolved; ceftolozane-tazobactam "2017 to 2022" (p.8) for a 2016-2020
  study, a source typo (the AmpC sentence on the same page says 2017 to 2020).
- Industry-funded (AbbVie, maker of ceftazidime-avibactam).
