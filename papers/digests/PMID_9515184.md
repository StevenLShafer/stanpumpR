# Davies 1998: review of ibuprofen clinical pharmacokinetics

## Study
Narrative review (54 pages, ~350 references) of assays, absorption, topical delivery, chiral inversion, distribution, protein binding, metabolism, elimination, therapeutic range, disease, age and interactions. Doses refer to the racemate unless stated.

## Model and parameters
No model of its own. Key facts with locations:
- Absorption rapid and complete orally (Summary). Bioavailability of total ibuprofen 0.95 ± 0.08 and R 0.92 ± 0.11 in one oral/IV study (p 118).
- Half-life typically ~2 h (p 137); Table III (healthy volunteers, oral IR) t1/2 mostly 1.5-2.5 h, Vd/F ~0.1-0.2 L/kg, CL/F ~0.04-0.07 L/h/kg; IV 200/400 mg: t1/2 1.58/1.57 h, Vd 0.17/0.16 L/kg, CL 0.048/0.05 L/h/kg (ref 87).
- Vd/F 6.37-23.51 L (0.1-0.2 L/kg); total-drug Vd/F rises with dose, free-drug Vd/F does not (p 125).
- Protein binding > 98% (albumin, site II); unbound fraction in plasma 0.0128 at 2 mg/L rising to 0.0155 at 50 mg/L (p 125). Summary: above 600 mg the unbound fraction increases, raising clearance and reducing total AUC.
- AUC vs dose (250-1200 mg) nonlinear for total, more linear for free; attributed to saturable binding, not absorption (p 117). Table III example: 400/800/1200 mg CL/F 0.0468/0.0524/0.0654 L/h/kg (ref 129).
- Chiral inversion: unidirectional R(-) to S(+), systemic and possibly presystemic; reported extents 52-69%, average 53-65% (p 130). S(+) carries most activity (about 160x R for prostaglandin synthesis inhibition in vitro, p 102).
- Elimination: glucuronide conjugates of oxidative metabolites in urine; little unchanged drug.
- Therapeutic window 10-50 mg/L, toxicity > 100 mg/L (p 137).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Section 3.2 summarises: effect-compartment modelling of evoked potentials with S-ibuprofen (EC50 8.71 ± 3.67 mg/L; pain rating 24.4 ± 1.2 mg/L), 30-60 min lag; antipyretic lag 1-3 h in febrile children (slope -0.242 °C per mg/L effect-site); antipyretic IC50 10.1 mg/L (indirect response). No single ke0 value tabulated. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Reviewed qualitatively: age (elderly, children), renal and hepatic disease, cystic fibrosis, rheumatoid arthritis, drug interactions.

## Caveats
Review of heterogeneous studies; many nonstereospecific assays; no pooled estimates.
