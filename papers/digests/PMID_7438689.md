# Ludden 1980: IV hydralazine kinetics in hypertensive patients (specific HPLC assay)

## Study
- 8 hypertensive patients (4 rapid, 4 slow acetylators); characteristics in the companion paper (ref 16), not here.
  Described in the discussion as older, hypertensive patients (PDF p4, printed 739).
- Hydralazine HCl (Apresoline) 0.3 mg/kg = **1.53 µmol/kg** (so the mg are the hydrochloride: 0.3 mg / 196.6 g/mol =
  1.53 µmol; derived), diluted to 5 mL, **constant infusion over 100 s** into a fast saline line (PDF p2, printed 737).
  Per-subject dose 1.42-1.78 µmol/kg (Table I).
- EDTA blood at 0, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 75, 90, 105, 120, 150, 180, 240, 360, 480 min after the end of
  infusion, chilled; plasma assayed by selective HPLC for hydralazine and HPH, and by nonselective GLC (Jack) for
  "apparent" hydralazine.
- NONLIN fits of 2- and 3-compartment models, zero-order input, elimination from central, weights 1/C²; model chosen
  by Boxenbaum's criteria. Whole-blood/plasma ratio measured in vitro (spiked 50 ng/mL, 5 subjects): **1.65 ± 0.10
  (SEM)**.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured: no blood pressure, heart rate, ke0, tPeak or concentration-effect data. Not an opioid. The authors
note in vitro plasma t½ 8.4 min (unpublished) vs 54 min in vivo, and suggest elimination is limited by return
from peripheral sites or back-conversion of labile metabolites (PDF p6).

## Covariates
Acetylator phenotype: no effect on CL, Varea, Vss or t½; V1 differs (rapid 0.379 vs slow 0.645 L/kg, p < 0.05),
which the authors attribute to 3- vs 2-compartment fits. Hepatic extraction and CLH higher in rapid acetylators
(Table IV, p < 0.01).

## Caveats
n = 8; demographics only in the companion paper; single dose; 1/C² weighting; mixed 2/3-compartment fits so the
phenotype V1 difference is partly a model artefact; blood/plasma ratio from in vitro spiking; E and CLH assume
complete oral absorption. The paper compares its results with Reece 1980 (abstract, ref 9: 0.375 mg/kg over 5 min,
mean t½ 34.8 min, plasma CL 148 mL/min/kg, young healthy subjects): the comparator for "higher clearance" there is
Reece's young volunteers, with a different infusion length and weighting.
