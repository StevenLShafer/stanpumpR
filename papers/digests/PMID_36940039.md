# Groen 2023: antimicrobial PK in pregnancy, part III (non-penicillin, non-cephalosporin)

## Study
A systematic review following PRISMA. PubMed was searched on 1 September 2021, with an update on 28 August 2022 for
some drugs. It covered 62 antimicrobials, excluding penicillins and cephalosporins. Two investigators screened and
extracted the studies. Studies were included if they gave at least one PK parameter in pregnant women. Where a
study also had non-pregnant or postpartum women, the authors computed the percentage change. 29 studies on 18 drugs
were included, 11 of them with both Vd and CL (abstract, p.1; methods pp.2-3). There is no pooled analysis and no
new model.

## Model and parameters
No model. Here is what the review extracts for the three stanpumpR drugs.

**Gentamicin (section 3.2.1, p.4; Table 2, p.5, image-confirmed).** The only study is Popovic 2007, a prospective
cohort: 18 third-trimester women at delivery (P3/D) against 4 non-pregnant women (NP). All were having caesarean
section or gynaecological surgery and got 4 mg/kg IV over 2-3 min, 10 min before incision. Values are mean (SD):

| | P3/D (n=18) | NP (n=4) |
|---|---|---|
| weight, kg | 81 (10) | 66 (16) |
| Cmean 6 h after dose, mg/L | 3.44 (2.22) | 3.42 (1.09) |
| Cmax, mg/L | 25.82 (4.99) | 15.72 (5.82) |
| Vd, L | 9.05 (2.49) | 12.05 (6.32) |
| t1/2, h | 1.78 (0.58) | 2.33 (0.72) |
| CL, as printed "L/h" | 216 (60.6) | 204.6 (52.8) |
| ke, /h (text p.4) | 0.4127 (0.0736) | 0.3198 (0.0943), p < 0.05 |

The text (p.4) reports these changes:
- Cmax 39% higher in pregnancy. Derived: (25.82-15.72)/25.82 = 39%, so the change is expressed relative to the
  pregnant value; relative to the non-pregnant value it is 64% higher.
- Vd 24% lower. Derived: -24.9%.
- CL minimally increased, by 5%.
- Only the change in ke was tested for significance.
- Conclusion: a PK change is plausible but "more robust studies are needed". No target attainment or dose advice.

The printed CL of 216 and 204.6 L/h does not fit the same table. Derived: Vd x ke = 9.05 x 0.4127 = 3.73 L/h
(P3/D) and 12.05 x 0.3198 = 3.85 L/h (NP). The printed values divided by 60 give 3.60 and 3.41. The cause cannot be
settled from the review alone; Popovic's primary paper is the source to check.

**Vancomycin (section 3.5.1, p.10; Table 5, pp.11-13).** The review found four studies:
- Bourget 1991: a case report at 26+3 weeks, 15 mg/kg every 12 h for 13 days. Cmax 28.30-41.55 mg/L, AUC0-inf
  66.29 mg.h/L, Vd 1.48 L/kg, t1/2 4.55 h.
- Laiprasert 2007: 13 women at caesarean, a single 1 g dose. Maternal serum 2.6-19.8 mg/L.
- Onwuchuruba 2014: 55 women in three dosing phases. Therapeutic maternal levels in 32%, 50% and 83% of the
  phases, the last at 20 mg/kg every 8 h.
- Towers 2018: 30 women on 20 mg/kg every 8 h. 77% reached therapeutic levels, or 92% of those dosed correctly.
  Maximum level 44.4 mg/L.

Only Bourget reported PK parameters, and no study compared pregnant with non-pregnant women. The review concludes
that the change in vancomycin PK in pregnancy cannot be determined. It also says 20 mg/kg every 8 h (maximum single
dose 2 g) gave therapeutic levels in more than 80% of mothers.

The text says the four studies included 86 pregnant women. Derived: the Table 5 counts sum to 1 + 13 + 55 + 30 = 99.

**Clindamycin (section 3.12.2, p.23; Table 9, pp.24-25, image-confirmed for Muller).** Three studies, 44 subjects
in all:
- Philipson 1973: 14 women at abortion (10-22 weeks), 450 mg orally. Cmax 5.16 mg/L after a single dose and 2.55
  mg/L after multiple doses.
- Wear 2016: 23 women in labour, 900 mg IV every 8 h. Maternal serum 4.46 mg/L [0.7-8.4]; all mothers above
  0.5 mg/L.
- Muller 2010: 7 women. Mean age 36.1 y, weight 86.1 kg, gestation 38.3 weeks. Vd central 12.4 L (95% CI
  9.83-14.9), first peripheral 52.2 L, second peripheral 6250 L (3447-9052), CL 10.0 L/h (2.65-17.35).
  fAUC/MIC at an MIC of 0.5 mg/L: 55.8 at 65% binding (lowest 95% CI 24.6), 39.7 at 75% (17.3) and 23.9 at 85%
  (10.2). Dose advice: 900 mg IV every 8 h is adequate for an MIC of 0.5 mg/L at 65% binding, but not at higher
  binding.

The text (p.23) adds three points:
- Muller's CL is lower than literature values of 19.8-26.4 L/h.
- Muller made no comparison with non-pregnant women, so it cannot be judged whether clindamycin PK changes in
  pregnancy.
- Muller's volume is reported as "Vd/F" of 6.32 x 10^3 L at steady state, although the dosing was intravenous.

The text also prints the GBS dose as "900 mg/kg every 8 h"; Table 9 has 900 mg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
PK/PD targets are reported only as the source studies give them:
- **Clindamycin:** fAUC0-24/MIC against an MIC of 0.5 mg/L, from Muller.
- **Vancomycin:** "therapeutic maternal levels", as each study defined them.
- **Gentamicin:** no target attainment.

These are not opioids, and the review has no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
The review has none of its own. Pregnancy stage is the only stratifier.

## Caveats
- Very sparse data: one gentamicin study with 4 controls, and no vancomycin or clindamycin study with a
  non-pregnant arm.
- The review contradicts itself on vancomycin. Results (p.10) say no comparison with non-pregnant women was made.
  The discussion (p.33) says the four studies found similar PK in late pregnancy and in non-pregnant women; Bourget,
  moreover, is a second-trimester case.
- The printed gentamicin CL does not fit Vd x ke.
- The search did not pick up Goyal 2022, the vancomycin pregnancy popPK paper.
