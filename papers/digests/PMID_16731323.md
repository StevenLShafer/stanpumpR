# Ng 2006: fentanyl vs tramadol PCA, cognition and analgesia

## Study
Prospective randomized double-blind trial, Hong Kong. 30 analyzed adult Cantonese-speaking Chinese patients, ASA I-III, elective colorectal (lower abdominal) surgery; 31 randomized, 1 excluded early. Group F (n=17) fentanyl PCA, group T (n=13) tramadol PCA. Table 1 (PDF p3): age 60.7 +/- 15.1 vs 62.9 +/- 13.0 y; weight 54.2 +/- 7.1 vs 60.7 +/- 9.2 kg (P=0.041); M/F 10:7 vs 4:9; ASA I:II:III 6:9:1 vs 3:9:1; surgery 150 min median both.
PCA bolus: fentanyl 10 ug (group F), tramadol 20 mg (group T), each in 1 mL over 1 min, lockout 5 min, max 6 boluses/h, no background (PDF p2). Intraoperative opioid was the study drug as boluses; induction fentanyl 1.5-2 ug/kg in all patients (the text states this for both groups), thiopentone, isoflurane. Rescue pethidine 0.4 mg/kg IM, never needed. Outcomes: MMSE and Benton Visual Retention Test (BVRT) pre-op, day 1, day 2; NRS 0-10 at rest and on cough. No drug concentrations measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- MEAC / analgesic concentration: none (no concentrations measured).
- ke0 / t1/2 ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none.
- Potency ratios (PDF p2, stated from the authors' citation of Lee 1993, ref 3; not measured here): tramadol is 10 times weaker than morphine; morphine is 100 times less potent than fentanyl; hence fentanyl:tramadol about 1000:1 (by dose, mass basis). Route and end point not specified (assumed IV analgesia; the source of the numbers is the review, not this trial).
- Observed dose ratio: 1252:1 whole study (see open question on the 24-h figure); the authors call the doses nearly equipotent.
- Pain (Table 4, PDF p4): NRS at rest 0-24 h 4.2 +/- 2.0 (F) vs 3.6 +/- 2.0 (T), P=0.417; on cough 0-24 h 7.6 +/- 1.1 vs 6.0 +/- 2.0, P=0.018; at rest 25-48 h 1.8 +/- 1.0 vs 2.7 +/- 1.4, P=0.057; on cough 25-48 h 5.5 +/- 1.3 vs 6.1 +/- 2.2, P=0.335.
- Cognition (Tables 2-3): no difference in MMSE or BVRT among completers; BVRT completion day 1 was 5/17 (29.4%) in F vs 11/13 (84.6%) in T, P=0.010; day 2 8/17 vs 10/13, P=0.098.
- Side effects (Table 4, rendered page): vomiting 2 (11.8%) vs 3 (23.0%), P=.418; nausea 6 (35.3%) vs 6 (46.2%), P=.547; dizziness 3 (17.7%) vs 6 (46.2%), P=.097. (The text layer scrambles these rows; the rendered page is as given.)

## Covariates
None modelled. Body weight differed between groups (P=0.041); doses were fixed per demand, not per kg.

## Caveats
Small n; powered only for about 30-33% differences in test scores. Many patients could not complete BVRT, so completer-only scores are biased. Equianalgesia assumed rather than measured; the tramadol bolus was chosen at 20 mg after prior experience that 10 mg was inadequate, so group T was not dosed at the nominal 1000:1 ratio (20 mg vs 10 ug = 2000:1 per bolus). Fentanyl was also given at induction in both groups.
