# Goswami 2014: metformin population PK with transcription-factor variants

## Study
- Pooled pharmacogenetic study, 546 subjects in all (abstract). PK: 235 subjects, 2,382 plasma samples
  (102 healthy volunteers, UCSF studies 6112/6113/865/767, plus 133 type 2 diabetes patients from Kaiser Southeast with
  sparse samples) (p. 2, printed 371; Table 1, p. 4). PD: HbA1c in 440 patients (Marshfield, Kaiser, Vanderbilt).
- Table 1 (p. 4, printed 373; medians, range): healthy 6112/6113 n=57, age 25 (18-45), weight 73 (49-136) kg, 58%
  African American; 865/767 n=45, age 31 (18-44), 70 (44-112) kg, 84% European American. Kaiser patients n=133, age
  58 (33-79), 98 (58-182) kg, 76% African American, dose 1,000 (250-2,500) mg/day.
- Dosing: 6112/6113/865 got 1,000 mg then 850 mg 12 h later (table footnote; Methods says the 850 mg was on the second
  day); 767 a single 850 mg dose. Formulation not stated. Assay not described in this paper.
- NONMEM 7, FOCE-I; PsN stepwise covariate modelling; bootstrap 1,000 samples (p. 9, printed 378). Secretory clearance =
  renal clearance minus creatinine clearance, non-compartmental, in 57 healthy subjects with urine.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment model with first-order absorption delayed by one transit compartment; all parameters apparent (/F)
(Figure 2, p. 5; text p. 3). Reference individual (Eqs 1-3, p. 4): African American, CrCL 112 mL/min, 75 kg,
homozygous reference for rs784888, rs555754, rs316019. Table 2 (p. 7, printed 376), median (%RSE), bootstrap 90% CI:

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: HbA1c (treatment HbA1c, minimum value 3-18 months after starting). No concentration-effect model.
  rs784892 (SP1 region): -0.32% HbA1c and -76.9 mL/min secretory clearance per G allele (p. 5, printed 374);
  GG vs AA 1.1% lower HbA1c and 98 mL/min lower secretory clearance (pp. 7-8).
- Figure 3 (p. 6) draws 1 mg/L as the lower target of a therapeutic range (no source given for it); the Introduction
  cites mean concentrations of 0.4-1.3 mg/L at 1,000 mg twice daily (p. 1).
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Equations (p. 4, printed 373), confirmed on the page image:
- CL/F = 78.4 x (1 + θCRCL (CRCL - 112)) x (1 + θEthnicity,CL) x (1 + θrs784888 x SP1), SP1 = imputed G-allele dose 0-2.
- Vc/F = 76.8 x (1 + θrs555754 (OCT3rs555754 - 0)) x (1 + θWT (WT - 75)) x (1 + θrs316019).
- Q/F = 18.1 x (1 + θrs12943590) x (1 + θEthnicity,Q).
- The θ values are not printed. Figure 3 (European American reference) gives the effects as ratios: CL/F race 1.26
  (African American vs European American), rs784888 0.76 (GG; text: 12% per G allele, up to 24%), race + CrCL
  0.94-1.37, all covariates 0.71-1.37; Q/F race 1.79, rs12943590 1.29; Vc/F weight 0.68-1.9, rs555754 0.68,
  rs316019 1.39, all 0.46-2.6.
- Text (p. 6, printed 375): against African Americans, European Americans about 26% and Asian Americans 22% lower CL/F;
  European Americans 46% lower Q/F. The comparator is African Americans in this pooled cohort.

## Caveats
- The model explains only 5% of CL/F variability (IIV 50%); covariate effects need replication (authors).
- Internal inconsistency: Figure 3a's race ratio 1.26 makes European Americans 21% lower than African Americans
  (derived: 1/1.26 = 0.79), while the text says about 26% lower; the dose advice (26% more for African Americans)
  matches the figure's ratio, so "26% lower" looks like a loose restatement.
- Vp/F is poorly estimated (RSE 43%, CI to 3,293 L).
- Table 2 lists residual error for "Study 787"; Methods and Table 1 call it 767. Figure 2b's y-axis reads mg/dL, the
  rest of the paper mg/L.
- Healthy volunteers sampled only after one or two doses; no steady state (authors).
- The "metformin secretory clearance" analysis used urine data from 57 healthy subjects only.

- **Independent comparison with the library model** (`R/drugs_metformin.R`, Chae 2012, one compartment): Chae CL/F
  52.6 L/h at CLcr 106.5 mL/min (healthy Korean adults); Goswami 78.4 L/h for an African American at 112 mL/min, which
  is about 62 L/h for a European American (derived: 78.4/1.26, Figure 3a) and about 61 L/h for an Asian American
  (derived: 78.4 x 0.78). The library's Chae CL/F is thus roughly 15% below Goswami's non-African-American typical value,
  in a different population and with an unstated CrCL method in both. Volumes are not comparable: Chae's single V/F
  113 L against Goswami's Vc/F 76.8 + Vp/F 413 L, because sampling to ~40 h (Figure 2b) finds the slow phase (terminal
  half-life ~19.6 h, derived) that Chae's one compartment (apparent half-life 1.5 h, drugs_metformin.R:50) does not.
  ka 0.312/h (plus 0.207 h transit) against Chae's 0.41/h with no lag. The header's warning against splicing the two
  is borne out: the parameterisations are not interchangeable. No discrepancy in the library model is implied, since it
  does not cite this paper.
