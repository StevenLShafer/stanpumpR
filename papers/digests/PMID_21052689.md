# Shiga 2011: single-dose IV amiodarone in healthy Japanese men

## Study
Phase I, single-blind, dose-escalating study. 32 healthy Japanese men, 20-32 y, 50.9-69.5 kg. Three steps 2 weeks
apart: 1.25 mg/kg (n = 6 active + 2 glucose controls), 2.5 mg/kg (10 + 2; one withdrawn after a vasovagal event, so
n = 9 analysed) and 5.0 mg/kg (10 + 2). Each dose diluted to 150 mL in 5% glucose and infused over **15 min**.
Serum sampled at 0, 15, 18, 23, 30, 45 min, 1, 2, 4, 8, 14, 24, 34, 48 h, then days 5-77. HPLC-UV for amiodarone and
desethylamiodarone (DEA), LLOQ 5 ng/mL. Noncompartmental analysis (WinNonlin 3.1); a four-exponential fit to the
5 mg/kg data was used for regimen simulations. ECG (Holter, 12-lead, signal-averaged), BP, safety. Funded by
Taisho-Sanofi.

Serum DEA never exceeded 50 ng/mL. Parent fell below 1000 ng/mL by 60 min; below 2000 ng/mL within 30-45 min of the
end of infusion. The four-exponential coefficients themselves are not tabulated. Simulation (Fig 6): US label regimen
(150 mg/10 min, 1 mg/min 6 h, 0.5 mg/min) and 5/6 of it both keep mean serum amiodarone at 1000-2000 ng/mL over 7 days.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0 or concentration-effect model. PR prolonged at 15 and 18 min after 5 mg/kg (0.16 ± 0.01 and 0.17 ± 0.01 vs
0.15 ± 0.01 s, p = 0.03); no other significant ECG, BP or HR change. Authors cite 1000-2000 ng/mL as the therapeutic
window (not measured here). Not an opioid; no MEAC.

## Covariates
None (dose per kg; single population).

## Caveats
Healthy young Japanese men, single doses, small n; NCA only; Table 1 text layer scrambled (DEA tmax assignment
uncertain). Industry funded. Terminal half-life shorter than US reports.
