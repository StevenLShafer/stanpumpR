# Sridharan 2019: primaquine population PK, hepatic and renal dysfunction

## Study
Pooled analysis of three single-dose, open, non-randomised studies at one Mumbai centre (April-December 2013): 53 adults, 458 concentrations. Groups (Table 1, PDF p3): healthy n=13, mild hepatic dysfunction n=12, moderate hepatic dysfunction n=6 (Child-Pugh), renal dysfunction n=22 (NKF-KDOQI by serum creatinine). Median age (range) 25.5 (19-34), 45 (22-61), 50.5 (26-61), 43 (20-60) y; median weight 66, 65.5, 56, 55 kg. Sex M:F 5:1, 5:1, all male, 2:1.
Drug: primaquine phosphate 15 mg tablet, single oral dose after breakfast. Samples (heparinised blood, plasma conc.) pre-dose, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24 h. Assay: reversed-phase HPLC (method cited, not described). NONMEM 7.3, FOCEI; VPC (n=1000) and bootstrap (n=2000) via PsN.

## Model and parameters
One-compartment, first-order absorption and elimination. Ka constrained greater than ke to avoid flip-flop. V "normalized to 70 kg" per the methods (PDF p2), but the final model contains no weight term and no allometric exponent is given. Log-normal BSV on CL, V, Ka. Combined additive + proportional residual error.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured. Discussion (PDF p7) quotes in-vitro EC50 181 ng/mL and EC90 543 ng/mL for P. falciparum transmission blocking (a cited secondary source) and ED90 about 0.06 mg base/kg, all above the average Cmax seen here. Not an opioid; no ke0, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Tested: weight, age, sex, hepatic dysfunction (mild/moderate), renal dysfunction. Retained: moderate hepatic dysfunction on V only (forward dOFV 3.84, backward 6.63). Explained 25% of BSV on V. No covariate on CL or Ka; renal dysfunction had no effect. No weight effect retained.

## Caveats
n=6 in the moderate-HD group (all male), small and unbalanced; additive error imprecise (RSE 102.5%); abstract and Table 2 disagree (see open_questions). Severe hepatic dysfunction not studied. Parameters are apparent (CL/F, V/F), so a threefold rise in V/F could equally be a fall in F; the authors themselves suggest reduced absorption (gut-wall oedema). NCA AUC and Cmax are lower in moderate HD (Table 1), and NCA CL/F is about fourfold higher there although the model finds no CL effect. Plasma assay; protein binding not measured.
