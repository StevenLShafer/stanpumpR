# Goudie 2015: two-stage Bayesian population PK of insulin aspart by pump in type 1 diabetic pregnancy

PDF page = printed page minus 3143 (PDF p1 = printed 3144). The drug studied is **insulin aspart**, which stanpumpR does
not model; the library's drugs are listed only because the repo cites it for the insulin pregnancy section.

## Study
- Mainly a statistics-methods paper (two-stage MCMC with reversible-jump covariate selection, OpenBUGS).
- Data (PDF p2-3): two 24-h CSII studies in pregnant women with type 1 diabetes, March 2009 to April 2011, Addenbrooke's,
  Cambridge. Study 1 (ref 8, Murphy 2011 Diabetes Care 34:406): 10 women at 12-16 and 28-32 weeks, sedentary. Study 2
  (ref 9, Murphy 2011 Diabetes Care 34:2527): 12 women twice in mid gestation (12-33 weeks) with meals, snacks and
  exercise. 22 women x 2 visits x 2 meals = 88 profiles; 6 could not be fitted (PDF p11); 1302 plasma insulin
  concentrations.
- Table I (PDF p2), mean (SD): age 32 (4.5) y; BMI 27 (3.3); HbA1c 7.1 (1.0)%; diabetes 18 (8.6) y; gestation 22 (6.5)
  weeks; expected total daily dose 55 (18) U/day; peak bolus rate 9.3 (5.0) U/h. Basal rate median 0.6 (IQR 0.1-1.2) U/h;
  prandial bolus median 8.9 (6.5-12) U (PDF p3). Weight is not tabulated.
- Assay: immunochemiluminometric (Invitron), intra-assay CV 4.7%, inter-assay 7.2-8.1% (PDF p3).

## Model and parameters
Profile-level model (PDF p3, Eq 1-2): two SC compartments in series, each emptying at 1/tmax; plasma insulin =
Q2 / (tmax x wt x MCR), plus a linear "residual insulin" term a(t - tend) + b. Parameters tmax (min), MCR (L/kg/min),
a, b; tmax and MCR log-normal. Best population model: three-level (visit-specific) with covariates. Table II (PDF p12),
posterior mean (SD):

Accumulation rate a (pmol/L/min) and post-prandial b (pmol/L) per study/meal and their effect sizes are also in Table II
(a: 0.056, -0.047, -0.029, -0.034; b: 33, 30, 31, 31). "Typical" means all continuous and binary covariates set to zero
(standardised, centred), so the typical values are at mean covariates.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None: PK only, no glucose model, no ke0.

## Covariates
Notable (inclusion probability > 0.5): tmax with study-2 breakfast (faster, attributed to exercise), gestation (+1.7%
per week) and diabetes duration (-1.1% per year); a with peak bolus rate, total daily dose and multiple boluses. MCR had
no notable association; b none (PDF p12-13). No relation of tmax to prandial dose, total daily dose or BMI (PDF p13).

## Caveats
- Aspart by CSII only; the authors say it is not necessarily applicable to lispro or injections (PDF p13).
- The ~30 min gestational delay in time to peak from early to late pregnancy is cited, not measured here: reference [6],
  Murphy 2012 Diabetologia 55:282-293 (PDF p2, reference list PDF p14). It is not reference [8] (Murphy 2011, the closed-loop
  study that supplied study 1's data).
- The companion letter (Goudie 2014, PMID 24855162) reports 1.6% per week from what appears to be a different analysis;
  this paper gives 1.7%.
- Small n (22 women), within-woman variability large.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/insulinTham.R:100-103** (aspart by pump, 22 women with type 1 diabetes at 12-33 weeks; tmax +1.7% per week;
  clearance per kg -0.33% per week): Table II and PDF p2-3, **matches**. Not applied, and the reason given (aspart is not
  a library insulin) agrees with the authors' own limitation.
- **R/insulinTham.R:146-149** (citation): authors, title, Stat Med 2015;34:3144-3158, DOI, **matches**.
- **inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv:116-123** (MCR per kg -0.33% per week non-notable; tmax +1.7% per week; "a ~30 min
  gestational delay ... had been reported earlier"; source string with PMID 26013427): **matches**.
- **docs/pregnancy.md:204** (Murphy HR et al. closed-loop, Diabetes Care ~2011, "the source of the ~30 min gestational
  delay in aspart time-to-peak cited by Goudie 2015", "no PMID found"): **discrepancy**. Goudie 2015 cites that delay to
  reference [6], Murphy 2012 Diabetologia ("Pathophysiology of postprandial hyperglycaemia in women with type 1 diabetes
  during pregnancy"), not to the closed-loop paper (reference [8]). The closed-loop paper is PMID 21216859 (Diabetes Care
  2011;34:406-411) and reports no insulin concentrations (see that card).
