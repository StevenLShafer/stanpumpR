# Zhang 2020: pregnancy raises CYP- and UGT-mediated buprenorphine metabolite ratios

## Study
Secondary analysis of two prospective, open-label, non-randomised, longitudinal studies from the Magee-Womens
Hospital / University of Pittsburgh group (Caritis, Venkataramanan), PDF pp3-5.
- Study 1: singleton pregnancies stabilised on twice-daily sublingual buprenorphine (2-16 mg) for at least 7 days;
  up to 3 visits (second trimester 14w0d-28w0d, third trimester 28w1d to term, about 4 weeks postpartum); 10
  samples at 0 (trough), 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 8 and 12 h after a dose.
- Study 2: same population, dosing frequency not restricted to twice daily; visits in the first half (<20 weeks)
  and second half (>=20 weeks) of pregnancy and at least 4 weeks postpartum; 10-13 samples to 12 h or the end of
  the dosing interval.
- Exclusions: CYP3A inducers/inhibitors; urine drug screens; no grapefruit juice for 3 days.
- Assay: plasma buprenorphine (BUP), norbuprenorphine (NBUP), buprenorphine glucuronide (BUPG), norbuprenorphine
  glucuronide (NBUPG) by UPLC-MS/MS. Study 1 linear 0.05-50 ng/mL (BUP, BUPG, NBUPG), 0.5-50 ng/mL (NBUP);
  study 2 0.05-100 ng/mL BUP, 0.2-100 NBUP, 0.2-200 BUPG and NBUPG. Bias within 15%.
- Analysis: concentrations converted to molar; steady-state AUC over the dosing interval by trapezoids; ratio
  (NBUP+NBUPG)/BUP as CYP3A N-dealkylation index, BUPG/BUP as UGT index; ln-transformed ratios compared by a
  univariate linear mixed-effect model (time fixed, subject random), Stata 14.
- n: 26 women; 19, 18 and 14 completed the combined first/second trimester, third trimester and postpartum
  studies (PDF p5). Table 1 (PDF p13) splits groups at 29 gestational weeks (not at 28 or 20 as in the Methods):
  study 1 n 7 / 13 / 11, mean weight 71.4 / 74.5 / 66.9 kg, albumin 3.4 / 3.2 / 4.1 g/dL; study 2 n 12 / 5 / 3,
  weight 73.1 / 78.0 / 68.8 kg. Postpartum visits at 6 +/- 3 (study 1) and 5 +/- 1 (study 2) weeks.
- Doses actually received, and dose-normalised BUP exposure, are not reported in this paper.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: **no MEAC** or minimum effective/analgesic concentration; **no ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis**;
**no equianalgesic or potency ratio**. Study 2 is described as a PK/PD study, but no PD end point or result is
reported here. The Discussion quotes other studies' peak concentrations (Concheiro 2011, Kuhlman 1998, Harris
2004; PDF p7) without new data.

## Covariates
Pregnancy stage only (categorical). No weight, albumin or genotype covariate analysis; subjects were not genotyped
(PDF p7).

## Caveats
- Metabolite/parent AUC ratios are not clearances: authors note they also move with metabolite formation versus
  elimination and the fraction metabolised by each path (PDF p7). Some women had lower ratios in pregnancy; in 2
  both pathways fell.
- Third-trimester BUPG/BUP mean 2.07 with SD 3.62 is dominated by a few high values (SD larger than the mean).
- The paper does not measure absorption, bioavailability, tmax or salivary pH; with sublingual dosing a
  metabolite ratio includes gut/hepatic first pass of swallowed drug, so it cannot separate absorption from systemic
  clearance.
- Authors say the increased metabolism could *partially* explain the lower exposure (PDF p6, p7).
- Group boundaries differ between Methods (28 / 20 weeks) and Table 1 (29 weeks); "third trimester" in Table 2 is
  therefore a pooled late-pregnancy group across two studies.
- Small n, unbalanced across studies; assay ranges differ between studies.
- Accepted manuscript: tables could change at proof; numbers here are from the PMC manuscript only.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **First author** (`R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:251` "Liu 2020", `:296` "Liu Y et al."; `inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv:36`
  source "Liu Y, et al. (Venkataramanan group)"): the paper's first author is **Zhang H** (Hongfei Zhang); no Liu is
  an author. Journal, volume, pages and DOI are correct. **Discrepancy** (citation only).
- **Mechanism** (`R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:251-253`: change attributed to faster CYP3A N-dealkylation and UGT
  glucuronidation): matches the paper's direction (Table 2, both ratios higher in pregnancy than postpartum). The
  paper calls the CYP index "N-demethylation"; the repo's "N-dealkylation" is the correct chemistry (and the term in
  the paper's own Introduction). **Matches.**
- **"not to absorption"** (`R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:252`; registry :36 notes "attributes the lower exposure to
  increased CYP3A/UGT metabolism (clearance), not reduced absorption"): this paper does not test absorption and
  does not say absorption is unchanged; it says the metabolic increase partially explains the lower exposure. The
  unchanged tmax and pH the registry pairs it with come from Bastian 2017, not from here. The mapping of the
  factor to CL1 may still be right, but this source supports "metabolism increases", not "absorption does not
  change". **Discrepancy** (overstatement of the source).
- **Magnitude**: the 1.83 factor (`R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:321`, registry :36 `cl_factor`) is from Zhu 2025's
  dose-normalised AUCs, not this paper; this paper gives no clearance ratio. Nothing here pins a number the model
  uses. Its third-trimester ratio rises (CYP index 1.84/1.33 = 1.38x, UGT index 2.07/0.30 = 6.9x; derived) are
  metabolite-ratio changes, not clearance ratios, and should not be read as the clearance factor.
- **Related citation** in registry :36: "A validated PBPK (Zhu 2018)" — this paper's ref 17 shows the 2018
  buprenorphine PBPK paper is **Zhang H**, Kalluri HV, Bastian JR et al., Br J Clin Pharmacol 2018;84:2075-2087,
  as the same row's source list and `R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:298` correctly give. "Zhu 2018" in the finding
  text is a slip. **Discrepancy** (minor, text only).
- Library model: Bjornsson 2023 disposition; this paper has no PK parameters to compare with it.
- Nothing this accepted manuscript reports is pinned by an implemented model, so no number needs re-walking against
  the published PDF.
