# Burm 1987: epidural lidocaine and bupivacaine with simultaneous IV deuterated tracer

## Study
13 ASA 1 adults (21-49 y, 61-95 kg) for minor surgery < 1 h; 12 completed (one lidocaine patient withdrawn).
Gender/age-matched allocation: lidocaine n = 6 (5 M; mean 32 y, 80 kg, 179 cm) or bupivacaine n = 6 (5 M; mean 31 y,
79 kg, 178 cm; Table I). Lorazepam 2 mg SL premed. Epidural at L3: 3 mL test dose then 20 mL at 1 mL/s of 2%
lidocaine HCl (398 mg total, as base) or 0.5% bupivacaine HCl (102 mg total, as base); **all epidural solutions
contained adrenaline 5 mg/L**. 20-25 min later, deuterium-labelled tracer IV via foot vein, 50 mL over 10 min:
lidocaine-D3 22.7 mg or bupivacaine-D3 14.0 mg. Central venous sampling to 24 h. GC plus mass fragmentography;
LOD 3 µg/L (lidocaine), 2 µg/L (bupivacaine). Doses expressed as free base. Biexponential fits (weighted 1/y²) and
NCA for disposition; point-area deconvolution, then one or two parallel first-order absorption processes fitted.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Sensory (pinprick) and motor block recorded (Table I): bupivacaine total analgesia recovery 495 ± 154 min, lidocaine
172 ± 37 min. Toxic thresholds quoted from Tucker 1984: lidocaine 5-10 mg/L, bupivacaine 2-4 mg/L. No PK/PD model,
no ke0. Not opioids; no MEAC.

## Covariates
None (n = 6 per drug).

## Caveats
n = 6 per drug; young healthy adults; adrenaline in all epidural solutions; tracer given during epidural block
(disposition close to unblocked volunteers, Table IV); labelled drug undetectable beyond 8-10 h, so the terminal
phase is partly extrapolated; total concentrations.
