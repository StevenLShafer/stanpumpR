# Clements 1982: ketamine bioavailability, kinetics and analgesia (ischaemic exercise test)

## Study
Two crossover studies in healthy fasting volunteers (Edinburgh/Glasgow), randomised order, subject and pain
assessor blind (PDF p1). Study 1: ketamine HCl IV 0.125 and 0.25 mg base/kg and saline, n 5 (age 33.8 +/- 1.4 SE y,
74.8 +/- 2.1 kg). Study 2: ketamine 0.5 mg/kg IM (triceps) or oral solution in orange juice, with double-dummy
saline, n 6 (31.8 +/- 2.0 y, 70.7 +/- 4.4 kg). Venous blood from the dominant arm at 0, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45 min,
1, 2, 4, 7 h. GC with electron capture (ketamine, norketamine). Two-compartment fit by simplex nonlinear regression.
Pain: modified ischaemic exercise test (Harrison and Bigelow): severe pain after 75-105 s of exercise in controls,
intolerable after a further 7 s (1-13).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: ischaemic exercise pain threshold (pain score none/mild at 75-105 s, or a prolonged time to intolerable
pain). IV: elevation lasted <10 min after 0.25 mg/kg and <5 min after 0.125 (ref 14). IM: marked elevation 15-60 min,
time to intolerable pain prolonged by 24 and 21 s at 15 and 30 min; corresponding plasma 85-330 ng/mL; concentrations
with no or mild pain 186 +/- 23 ng/mL vs moderate or severe 101 +/- 8 ng/mL (Figure 1, PDF p1); correlation of
concentration with prolongation r = 0.712 (Figure 2). Discussion: elevation occurred consistently above 160 ng/mL
and not below 80 ng/mL (pain score or +20 s criterion). Oral: little effect (ketamine 17-65 ng/mL, norketamine
80-390). Earlier reports cited: elevation above 100-150 ng/mL (refs 11, 14). No ke0 or hysteresis analysis. Not an
opioid.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- n 5-6; single doses, not steady state; Figure 1(b) includes a moderate/severe-pain sample near 165 ng/mL (read
  from the figure), so "consistently above 160" rests on the combined criterion.
- Abstract rounds oral F to 17% (Table I 16.6%).
- Eq 1 (hepatic blood flow method) underestimated oral F in two of four subjects.
