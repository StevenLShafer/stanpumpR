# Fokina 2016: bupropion and metabolites in pregnancy

## Study
- Prospective, opportunistic study at UTMB. **28 pregnant women** were analysed (29 enrolled, 1 excluded); they were taking bupropion for depression.
  - Age 29.2 ± 6.9 y. Gestational age 27.5 ± 8.5 wk. Weight 86.8 ± 24.6 kg (range 50.4-168.8).
  - Ethnicity: 57% white/non-Hispanic and 36% white/Hispanic (PDF p3, printed 1834).
- Study windows: early pregnancy 10-14 wk, mid 22-26 wk, late 34-38 wk, and postpartum (lactating or not).
- Formulations: 16 SR 150 mg BID, 5 SR 150 mg QD, 3 IR 100 mg TID, 2 XL 300 mg QD. A paired comparison required the same dose and formulation in both periods.
- Steady state was assumed after at least 4 days in a dosing diary. Sampling was at 0-24 h, depending on the dosing interval, with urine collected over the same interval.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS in plasma for BUP, OHBUP, TB and EB. Urine was assayed with and without deconjugation.
- Analysis: noncompartmental (Kinetica 5.0), CL/Fss = dose/AUCss, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Subjects were genotyped for CYP2B6 and CYP2C19.

## Model and parameters
- No compartmental model. Table 1 (PDF p3, printed 1834) gives mean ± SD, paired:

  - The metabolic ratio (M.R.) is the ratio of AUCs corrected for molecular weight, i.e. molar. An asterisk marks P < .05 for mid versus late.
  - Late pregnancy versus postpartum showed no significant differences. For BUP AUC, P = 0.099.
- Table 2 (PDF p4, printed 1835) gives creatinine clearance in mL/min: 185 ± 45 (mid) against 166 ± 33 (late), n = 4, and 175 ± 38 (late) against 128 ± 23 (postpartum), n = 11. Both comparisons were significant.
- Renal clearance in late pregnancy against postpartum (mL/min):
  - BUP: 17.2 ± 19.0 against 38.9 ± 77.7.
  - OHBUP: 2.98 ± 3.58 against 3.08 ± 3.44.
  - The paired differences were not significant.
- Urinary recovery of unchanged BUP was below 1% of the dose.
- In pregnancy, 89 ± 9% of urinary OHBUP was glucuronidated. OHBUP-glucuronide was 13.8 ± 15.7% of the dose in late pregnancy against 6.25 ± 5.47% postpartum (P < .05).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- None measured. OHBUP is described as half as potent as the parent (Introduction, citing Golden 1988).
- No therapeutic range, ke0 or effect data. This is not an opioid paper, so there are no MEAC or potency values.

## Covariates
- CYP2B6*6 carriers in mid-pregnancy tended towards a lower OHBUP/BUP ratio (9.46 ± 4.4 vs 32.8 ± 34.0, P = .086).
- CYP2C19 poor/intermediate metabolisers had higher TB/BUP and EB/BUP ratios, and a higher TB AUC in late pregnancy (5773 ± 2517 vs 2333 ± 1313 ng·h/mL).
- Neither genotype changed BUP CL/F.

## Caveats
- Small paired groups (8 and 12), mixed formulations and doses, and steady state assumed from a diary.
- Postpartum mixed lactating and non-lactating women.
- Conjugated metabolites were not measured in plasma.
- The supplemental tables were not read.
- The text prints the TB/BUP genotype comparison as identical numbers for mid and late pregnancy (11.6 ± 3.16 vs 6.58 ± 3.33 both times; PDF p4), which is probably a typesetting duplication.
- The EB comparison is cited as Table 2 but is in Table 1.
- The authors' mechanism (CYP2B6 induction offset by OHBUP glucuronidation and renal elimination) is an interpretation, not a measurement.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Finding attributed to Fokina: matches.** The row says Fokina found no difference in bupropion oral clearance or in the OHBUP/BUP metabolic ratio across pregnancy, so OHBUP exposure is essentially unchanged. Table 1 shows no significant change in CL/Fss (L/h or L/h/kg) or OHBUP/BUP M.R. across mid, late and postpartum, and OHBUP AUC is 0.96× postpartum (derived). The offset mechanism is correctly worded as the authors' explanation.
- **Comparator: matches.** Fokina's comparator is postpartum in the same women, as the row states. The same-women basis also holds against the library's own model, since no factor is applied.
- **Point estimates.** The point estimates are not zero: late-pregnancy CL/F is 1.25× postpartum and BUP AUC 0.84× (derived, NS, SD around 45%). "Unchanged" with no factor is the authors' conclusion and is defensible.
- **n = 28: imprecise.** 28 is total enrolment, not the paired comparison (n = 12); see `open_questions`.
- **Fay 2021 half of the row: not verified here.**
- **Independent check of the library model.** `R/drugs_bupropion.R:149-154` (Ghimire 2026, single 150 mg SR dose, CKD-weighted sample) has CL/F 148.4 L/h for a 70 kg reference.
  - Fokina's postpartum women had 208 ± 93 L/h, 1.40× the model, and late-pregnancy women 259 L/h, 1.75× (derived). Their body weight was heavier, roughly 87 kg during pregnancy.
  - The direction is consistent with the model header's own remark that Ghimire's sample (CKD, single dose, 38.5-h terminal half-life) may run slow, and with Zhang 2019's 221 L/h quoted there.
  - The model's formed OHBUP/BUP AUC ratio is 16.5 on a mass basis. Fokina's molar M.R. is 14.1-22.5 (means of individual ratios). The ratio of mean mass AUCs is 9857/775 = 12.7 postpartum and 9499/654 = 14.5 in late pregnancy (derived). The two are of the same order.
