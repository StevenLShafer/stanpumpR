# Hannam 2011: acetaminophen-ibuprofen response surface from adult dental-pain data

## Study
- Secondary analysis of published mean time-effect curves (Mehlisch 2010, adults and adolescents after impacted-molar removal): acetaminophen/ibuprofen 250/100, 500/200, 1000/400 mg, acetaminophen 500 or 1000 mg alone, ibuprofen 200 or 400 mg alone, placebo. 85 mean PRID (0-10) observations digitised (GraphExtract) at 0.25-8 h.
- No concentrations measured: one-compartment first-order absorption PK fixed at literature values (K-PD-like approach); effect compartment per drug; Minto-type response surface (normalised units, interaction beta); placebo E0 as an exponential asymptote. NONMEM VI FOCE-I, ADVAN6; 1000 bootstraps.
- Simulations for a 5-year-old, 20 kg child with allometric PK (Berkeley Madonna).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: mean pain relief and pain intensity difference (PRID, 0-10) after third-molar extraction in adults. Ibuprofen effect-site EC50 5.07 mg/L, t1/2keo 1.16 h; acetaminophen EC50 11.9 mg/L, t1/2keo 0.15 h; shared Emax ~4 and Hill 2; additive interaction. Simulation (20 kg child): adding acetaminophen helps when ibuprofen < 5 mg/kg; little early gain above 5 mg/kg; more sustained effect 4-8 h after combination dosing. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Allometric size scaling of PK for the child simulations only.

## Caveats
- Fitted to digitised mean curves, no individual data, no measured concentrations; t1/2keo partly absorbs PK misspecification (authors say so).
- Sparse dose ratios; beta poorly identified. Authors caution against dosing decisions from it.
- Emax 4.03 in Table 1 vs 4.06 in abstract.
