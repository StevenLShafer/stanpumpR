# Miura 2004: once-daily zonisamide monotherapy in children

## Study
Prospective clinical study (described as randomized, no comparator arm reported), single centre (Sagamihara,
Japan). 72 treatment-naive children, age 3 months to 14 y 11 mo (mean 8 y 3 mo), cryptogenic localization-related
epilepsy (simple partial 4, complex partial 23, secondarily generalized 45). No neuropsychiatric handicap. Weight
and sex not reported.

Dosing: zonisamide once daily in the morning, started 2 mg/kg/day, doubled weekly to an initial maintenance dose of
8 mg/kg/day (mean 7.97 +/- 0.55, range 5.82-10.19 mg/kg/day; Table 1). Formulation not stated.

Sampling: 4 weeks after reaching maintenance dose; trough = before the morning dose, "peak" = 4 h after the morning
dose; then every 6 months. Plasma, HPLC (validation not reported). Follow-up 6-43 months (mean 27.2).

Phase II: 12 patients (5-16 y, mean 12 y 1 mo) with uncontrolled seizures at high ZNS levels had carbamazepine
added (ZNS qd 11.1 +/- 2.5 mg/kg/day; CBZ bid 15.1 +/- 3.0 mg/kg/day; Table 2); 9 later switched to CBZ alone.

Level-to-dose ratio (ug/mL per mg/kg/day) rose with age (Fig. 1, values not tabulated); peak/trough ratio did not
vary with age (Fig. 2). Derived: mean trough/dose about 3.4 (ug/mL)/(mg/kg/day) (27.0/7.97).

CBZ interaction (Table 5, n = 12): ZNS trough 35.4 +/- 10.0 -> 22.2 +/- 9.8 ug/mL, peak 43.0 +/- 11.3 -> 28.1 +/- 12.5
(P < 0.05), peak/trough 1.23 +/- 0.12 -> 1.29 +/- 0.22 with CBZ. ZNS did not change CBZ or CBZ-epoxide levels
(Table 6: CBZ trough 6.05 with ZNS vs 6.52 alone; CBZ-E trough 1.32 vs 1.15 ug/mL).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: seizure control. 57/72 (79.2%) controlled on monotherapy. Seizure recurrence was common with trough
about 15 ug/mL; drowsiness or short attention span forced dose reduction in 5 patients with peak > 40 ug/mL. No
clear level-response relation within age groups (Fig. 4). Accepted range cited: 15-40 ug/mL. No ke0, MEAC or
potency ratio (not an opioid).

## Covariates
Age (qualitatively: higher level/dose ratio with age; author advises dosing on age as well as weight). CBZ
co-medication lowers ZNS levels by about 37% (derived from Table 5 troughs).

## Caveats
No weights, sexes, or individual data in text; "peak" is a fixed 4 h sample, not a true Cmax; assay validation not
given; ratio-to-dose relation shown only graphically; children are a pooled 3 mo-15 y group, so a 20 kg child's
expected trough is not separately reported.
