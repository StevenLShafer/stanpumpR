# Hull 1982: codeine PK, oral vs IM bioavailability, and smoking

## Study
- 24 healthy males aged 21-35, 60-90 kg (12 nonsmokers, 12 smokers); 12 nonsmokers and 10 smokers analysed. Age 24.0 +/- 0.6 vs 24.8 +/- 0.8 y; weight 72.6 +/- 2.4 vs 77.0 +/- 1.9 kg (mean +/- SE; p. 851).
- Randomised crossover within group, 1 week apart: codeine sulfate 60 mg tablet PO vs codeine phosphate 60 mg IM (Table 1). Fasted.
- Venous samples 0-12 h (13 times). Codeine and unconjugated morphine by RIA, LLOQ < 1 ng/mL; cross-reactivities in Table 2 (morphine RIA: M3G cross-reacts weakly, IC50 130-820 vs 2.3 ng/mL for morphine).
- Per-subject 1- or 2-compartment fits (NLIN, 1/C weighting, AIC); oral fits needed a 10-15 min lag time. AUC by trapezoid 0-12 h, extrapolated with C12/beta for bioavailability.

Table 4, morphine:codeine AUC(0-12 h) ratio x 100 (mean +/- SE): oral 17.2 +/- 2.1 (nonsmokers), 16.3 +/- 4.1 (smokers); IM 6.1 +/- 0.8, 7.4 +/- 2.0. Oral > IM p < 0.01. Four smokers and two nonsmokers had very low ratios after both routes (Fig. 5).
Fig. 4: mean unconjugated morphine peaks about 10-11 ng/mL near 1-1.5 h after oral codeine, about 5-6 ng/mL plateau 1.5-4 h after IM (read from figure).
Preliminary total (conjugated + free) morphine AUC about 10% of total codeine (p. 853).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured. No MEAC, no ke0/tPeak. Cites Beaver et al. for oral:IM codeine potency of 51% (peak pain relief) and 64% (area under pain-relief curve) (p. 853, secondary). The authors argue the oral > IM morphine ratio, together with Beaver's potency ratios, does not support codeine being a prodrug for morphine.

## Covariates
Smoking only: small increase in IM clearance (17%), greater variability in F and in morphine:codeine ratio; no other effect.

## Caveats
- RIA, 1982; unconjugated morphine RIA cross-reactivity with glucuronides is small but the morphine:codeine ratios (6-17%) are several-fold higher than later chromatographic studies (about 2-3%).
- Different salts PO (sulfate) and IM (phosphate); AUCs and Cmax for oral are base-adjusted.
- Young healthy men; n = 22; CYP2D6 not genotyped (the low-ratio subjects are plausibly poor metabolisers).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **F = 0.5, "50-55% relative to IM"** (codeine.R:49-50; codeine.md:13): paper 50.2% (nonsmokers), 54.8% (smokers). **matches**.
- **Oral > IM morphine:codeine ratio** (codeine.R:79-80): Table 4, 17.2 vs 6.1 (x100), p < 0.01. **matches** qualitatively.
- **"Its size is not separately identifiable from oral data"** (codeine.R:81-82): true of oral data alone, but this crossover gives IM and oral ratios in the same subjects. Taken at face value they imply about 65% of oral morphine exposure is presystemic ((17.2 - 6.1)/17.2, derived), whereas the model's first-pass fraction 0.0015 vs systemic formation of about 0.5 x 0.029 = 0.0145 of the dose (derived) makes presystemic morphine about 9% of the oral total. Hull's oral morphine still peaks at 1-1.5 h (Fig. 4), so a large first-pass share did not produce a 20-min peak here, which bears on the argument at codeine.R:84-92. RIA-era ratios are also 3-6 fold above the 0.020-0.027 the file compares against, so the quantitative weight is uncertain. **partly-verified**: the cited facts match; the paper is not used for, and quantitatively sits uneasily with, the size of the first-pass branch.
