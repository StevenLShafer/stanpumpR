# Beaver 1977: oral vs IM oxymorphone, and IM oxymorphone vs morphine, in cancer pain

## Study
Two double-blind, randomised (Latin square) crossover relative-potency bioassays (four-point design: two doses of standard, two of test) at the James Ewing Hospital (Memorial Sloan-Kettering), in inpatients with pain from a variety of cancers (PDF p1-4, printed 186-189). Many patients had prior opioid exposure and several were assumed substantially tolerant (p2). Test doses were given only for moderate or severe pain and at least 3 h after the last analgesic; a single nurse-observer (A.R.) rated pain intensity (0-3) and, in the oral/IM study only, pain relief (0-4) hourly for 6 h (p2, p4). Total effect = sum of the hourly scores over 6 h; peak effect = greatest score within 6 h. Remedicated patients scored zero for remaining hours (p4). **No drug concentrations were measured.**

- **IM morphine vs IM oxymorphone (earlier study):** four sequential series, each morphine sulfate 8 and 16 mg against oxymorphone hydrochloride 1, 2 mg (Series I, III), 0.5, 1 mg (Series II) or 2, 4 mg (Series IV), plus saline placebo (Table I, PDF p2, printed 187). 34 started, 26 completed at least one round (12 men, 14 women, 32-75 y, mean 54); patients appearing in more than one series were counted separately: 42 "patients", N = 78 crossover comparisons (p4, Table I). Weight not reported. Mean doses across series: oxymorphone 0.89 and 1.78 mg (Fig. 2, PDF p7).
- **IM vs oral oxymorphone:** Series I, IM 0.5 and 1.5 mg vs oral 5 and 15 mg (18 patients, 26 comparisons); Series II, IM 1 and 3 mg vs oral 5 and 15 mg (11 patients, 16 comparisons). 33 started, 28 completed a round (13 men, 15 women, 19-69 y, mean 48). Oral drug was Numorphan tablets in gelatin capsules; double-dummy (p4, printed 189). Combined: 29 patients, N = 42; mean IM doses 0.65 and 1.95 mg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
**Relative potency, IM oxymorphone : IM morphine (mg oxymorphone HCl vs mg morphine sulfate), Table III (PDF p5, printed 190), 42 patients, N = 78, end point change in pain intensity (PID):**

| Measure | Raw score | 95% CL | Ridit | 95% CL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total effect (6 h sum) | 8.66 | 6.02, 12.23 | 10.13 | 7.02, 15.82 |
| Peak effect | 13.41 | 7.82, 70.49 | 15.72 | 8.65, (not given) |

Lambda 0.60, 0.66, 1.01, 1.14; no significant deviation from parallelism; all four doses significantly better than placebo (p5). Text rounds to 8.7 (CI 6 to 12.2) and 13 (CI 7.8 to 70). The authors note this agrees with Eddy and Lee's 9.85 (1959) and with DeKornfeld's finding that 1 mg oxymorphone was indistinguishable from 10 mg morphine (Discussion, PDF p11, printed 196). Derived, on a base basis (assuming morphine sulfate pentahydrate, base fraction 0.752, and oxymorphone HCl, 0.892): about 7.3 (total) and 11.3 (peak) — see open question.

**Relative potency, oral : IM oxymorphone, Table VI (PDF p10, printed 195), 29 patients, N = 42:**

| Measure | Pain relief raw (95% CL) | Pain relief ridit | PID raw (95% CL) | PID ridit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total effect | 0.16 (0.09, 0.31) | 0.14 (0.076, 0.25) | 0.18 (0.10, 0.40) | 0.16 (0.08, 0.38) |
| Peak effect | 0.068 (0.006, 0.16) | 0.061 (0.003, 0.14) | 0.12 (limits not given) | 0.12 (not given) |

The authors' headline (pain relief raw score, the most sensitive index) is 1/6 for total and 1/14 for peak effect (p7, printed 192). Peak PID did not give a significant common slope, so that bioassay is not valid (p6). Oral oxymorphone peaked later and lasted longer than IM (Fig. 4, p7). These match morphine's oral/parenteral ratios from the same group (1/6 total, 1/13 peak) and metopon's (1/5, 1/11) (Discussion, p7).

- **MEAC / minimum effective concentration:** none; no concentrations were measured.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis:** none. Hourly ratings only; mean IM PID curves peak at the first (1 h) observation for both drugs (Fig. 2, p7), which cannot resolve a tPeak of minutes.
- **Side effects:** qualitatively similar and dose related; no substantial difference between equianalgesic doses of IM oxymorphone and morphine, or IM and oral oxymorphone (Tables IV, VII).

## Covariates
None analysed. Tolerance (prior opioid use) was present but not quantified.

## Caveats
- Dose ratios, per mg of salt (sulfate vs hydrochloride), in a mixed, partly tolerant cancer-pain population; not concentration ratios.
- Peak-effect IM potency has a very wide upper limit (70.49); peak oral/IM PID assay invalid (no significant slope).
- The morphine-oxymorphone data are from an earlier study, reported here; about 20% of the time a second study drug was given the same day (p2).
- The same patient may appear in up to three series, analysed as different patients (p4).
- Hourly observation over 6 h; no information on onset in minutes.
