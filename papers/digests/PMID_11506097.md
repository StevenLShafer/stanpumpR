# Barr 2001: lorazepam vs midazolam infusions for surgical ICU sedation, PK/PD model

## Study
Double-blind randomised study, VA Palo Alto surgical ICU. 24 adults (18-85 y) ventilated after major
vascular, urologic or general surgery: 14 lorazepam, 10 midazolam (4 lorazepam subjects replaced: 3
alcohol withdrawal, 1 fentanyl pump failure). Table 2 (rows reassigned): lorazepam age 74 (SD 6) vs
midazolam 63 (10) y (P = 0.0071); weight 85 (15) vs 94 (47) kg; 13 M / 1 F vs 10 M; APACHE II 10.1 vs
7.4. TCI by STANPUMP (first 13 with literature models, the rest with revised models), started at 50
ng/mL and titrated to Ramsay 3-4 for 12-72 h; analgesia by fentanyl TCI (1.5 ng/mL) or epidural
morphine/bupivacaine. Arterial samples during titration and up to 24 h after stopping; HPLC, LOQ 16
ng/mL. Lorazepam infusion 36.9 (30.9) h at 0.91 (0.39) mg/h, total 31.7 mg. PK: NONMEM mixed effects
(23 subjects); PD: naive pooled logistic model (18 subjects).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: modified Ramsay sedation score. Model H: P(Sedation >= ss) = C^gamma / (C^gamma + C50,ss^gamma),
with C the **plasma** concentration (no effect compartment, no ke0), a "virtual drug" for residual
surgery/anaesthesia effect (initial 159 ng/mL midazolam-equivalent, half-life 12.4 h), fentanyl as a
binary factor (C50 x 0.82), and age (C50 -18% per 10 y). Table 5, model H, for a **71-year-old**:
midazolam C50,ss for SS >= 2-6 = 68, 101, 208, 304, 375 ng/mL; lorazepam potency factor 2, so lorazepam
**34, 51, 104, 152, 188 ng/mL** (abstract, Results). gamma midazolam 4.5, lorazepam 3.3 (row
reassignment; consistent with the text that midazolam's curve is steeper). 49% correct, 76% within one
score. "ss" in C50,ss denotes the sedation score; the authors describe the predictions as sedation at
steady-state plasma concentration. Observed median lorazepam concentrations at SS 2-5: 32.2, 47.6,
62.2, 61.9 ng/mL (Table 6; with fentanyl in many). Amnesia C50 about 6 ng/mL lorazepam vs 25 midazolam
(amnestic potency 4). Observed emergence to SS 2: 8.71 (5.97) h lorazepam vs 3.02 (2.58) h; extubation
21.2 vs 5.4 h; lorazepam at extubation 17.39 ng/mL. Predicted emergence after 72-h infusions (71 y, 70
kg man, Table 9 / abstract): light (SS 3) 11.9 h, deep (SS 5) 31.1 h lorazepam; 3.6 and 14.9 h
midazolam.

## Covariates
PK: none for lorazepam; age and weight for midazolam. PD: age, fentanyl (binary), virtual drug.

## Caveats
Small (9 lorazepam subjects in PD), mostly older men; naive pooled PD; fentanyl effect binary from
predicted fentanyl concentrations; age effect not extrapolable to the young; C50s quoted for a
71-year-old without fentanyl; titration design confounds onset times. Steven L. Shafer is a co-author.
