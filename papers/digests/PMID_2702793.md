# Greenblatt 1989 (CPT): IV diazepam, midazolam and EEG beta activity

## Study
- 11 healthy volunteers, 6 men and 5 women, aged 28-42 y; no oral contraceptives. Weight not reported in the text layer.
- Double-blind three-way crossover, ≥ 1 week apart: diazepam 0.15 mg/kg, midazolam 0.1 mg/kg, placebo, each infused IV over 1 min.
- Venous samples at end of infusion, 5, 10, 15, 30, 45 min, 1-6 h, 8, 10, 12, 15, 24 h. Assay GC-ECD (diazepam, desmethyldiazepam, midazolam).
- PK: sums of two or three exponentials (end-of-infusion point excluded), Vc, Varea, t½, CL. For diazepam, 24 h sampling was too short; t½ and CL are "only tentative" (authors).
- EEG: F3-C3, 64-s segments at sampling times, FFT; end point = increase over baseline in % of total EEG amplitude in 13-30 Hz. Subjects kept awake (counting).
- PD model: E = Emax·C^A/(B + C^A) on plasma concentration; no equilibration delay assumed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: % EEG amplitude in 13-30 Hz (beta + sigma), awake subjects, venous plasma, total drug.
- Diazepam EEG effect maximal at the end of the 1-min infusion; midazolam maximal 5-10 min after infusion. Diazepam effect significant to 5 h, midazolam to 2-2.5 h; both gone by 6-8 h.
- No ke0/t½ke0 estimated (instantaneous equilibration assumed). Potency ratio about 7-fold (EC50 269 vs 35.2).

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
- Diazepam t½ and CL tentative (24 h sampling).
- Diazepam individual fits possible in only a few subjects (few concentrations < 70 ng/mL).
- Venous sampling with no hysteresis model; the authors suggest acute tolerance may contribute to offset.
