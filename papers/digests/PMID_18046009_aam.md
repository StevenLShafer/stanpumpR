# Pennell 2008: lamotrigine clearance across pregnancy, TDM and seizures

## Study
A prospective observational cohort at Emory, enrolled December 2002 to March 2006 (PDF p 3).
- **Population:** 53 pregnancies in 53 women (Table 1, PDF p 13): 39 with epilepsy, 14 with psychiatric
  diagnoses. Mean age 31.0 years (range 17 to 42). 38 were white, 9 black, 4 Asian and 2 white-Hispanic. Mean
  gestational age at delivery was 38.9 weeks. Weight is not given.
- **Exclusions** included any co-medication known to alter lamotrigine (LTG) metabolism.
- **Visits** were every 1 to 3 months through pregnancy and the first postpartum year. Time since the last dose
  was recorded at each blood draw.
- **Assay:** serum total and free LTG by HPLC-UV (Chromsystems). Free drug was separated by Centrifree YM-30
  ultrafiltration. Limit of detection 0.25 µg/mL, linear to 20 µg/mL. Intra-assay CV 2.0 to 3.2% and inter-assay
  CV 7.5 to 12.5% (PDF p 3).
- **Clearance:** apparent oral CL = daily dose (mg/kg) / serum concentration (mg/L), computed for total and free
  drug (PDF p 3). This gives a weight-normalised CL/F per day, (mg/kg)/(mg/L) = L/kg/day (derived).
- **Baseline:** preconception samples (6 women), or samples taken more than 6 weeks postpartum in women not
  breastfeeding (19 more). Trimesters: T1 under 14 weeks, T2 14 to 28 weeks, T3 29 to 42 weeks.
- **Analysis:** a linear mixed model on log CL with linear and quadratic terms for hours post-dose, and with race
  and age (PDF p 4). 305 samples.

## Model and parameters
No population PK model; the analysis is a mixed-effects comparison of observed CL/F.
- **Text (PDF p 6, and the abstract, p 1):** mean total LTG CL rose **94%** and free CL **89%** above
  nonpregnant baseline in the third trimester. All trimesters differed from baseline (p<0.001), and T2 and T3
  differed from T1 (p<0.03).
- **Figure 2 (PDF p 11), read from the bars, approximate:**

  | | Baseline | T1 | T2 | T3 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Total CL, L/kg/day | about 1.03 | about 1.25 | about 1.70 | about 2.0 |
  | Free CL, L/kg/day | about 2.7 | about 3.85 | about 4.8 | about 5.15 |

  These figures are consistent with the +94% and +89% in the text (derived: 2.0/1.03 ≈ 1.94, 5.15/2.7 ≈ 1.91).
- **Derived free fraction:** the ratio of total to free CL is about 0.38, i.e. about 62% bound. The paper itself
  says LTG is about 55% bound (introduction, PDF p 2).
- **Figure 3 (PDF p 12):** free CL by month peaks at about 5.8 in month 10 and falls to about 2.9, 2.4 and 1.6
  in postpartum months 1 to 3. The text says CL returned to baseline within the first postpartum month.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Ratio to target concentration (RTC):** total LTG divided by the individual's preconception target
  concentration. In the second trimester, a lower RTC went with more seizures (p<0.001), in 36 women with
  epilepsy (83 RTC values). On a ROC curve, **RTC 0.65** gave 83.3% true positives and 4.2% false positives;
  RTC 0.78 gave 100% and 25.0% (PDF p 6). No association was found in T1 or T3.
- **Seizure course:** 39% of women had more seizures, 33% fewer and 28% no change (PDF p 5). The peak was 23.5%
  of women with increased seizures in gestational month 7 (Table 2, PDF p 14).
- **Postpartum toxicity:** 4 of 6 women who did not follow the empiric taper had toxicity, against 3 of 21 who
  did (p=0.040).
- No population therapeutic range is given; targets were individual.

## Covariates
- **Race:** free CL was higher in white than in black women (p=0.031); total CL showed no significant difference.
- **Hours post-dose:** associated with higher apparent CL; this was adjusted for in the model.
- **Age:** no effect.
- **Gestational trimester:** as above.

## Caveats
- An observational study with sparse, non-steady-state sampling times.
- The clearance is a dose/concentration ratio, normalised per kg, with the weight basis unstated.
- Baseline came mostly from postpartum samples (19 of 25).
- The free:total relationship implied by Figure 2 (about 38% free) differs from the 55% bound quoted in the
  introduction.
- This digest is from the accepted manuscript; figure values may differ slightly in the published version.

## Verification against stanpumpR
The code's pins depend on Karanam 2025, not on this paper; Pennell's numbers are used only as cross-checks.
- **R/drugs_lamotrigine.R:53-54 (+94% total, +89% free, third trimester).** The paper (PDF p 6 and abstract) gives
  the same figures, against the nonpregnant baseline. **Matches.**
- **R/drugs_lamotrigine.R:64 ("Pennell 2008 1.94").** 1 + 0.94 = 1.94. **Matches** as arithmetic. The comparator
  differs, though: Pennell's ratio is between weight-normalised CL/F values (per kg, against the same women's
  preconception or late-postpartum samples). Karanam's 2.32 and the MONEAD 2.28 are ratios of absolute CL/F (see
  `open_questions`).
- **R/drugs_lamotrigine.R:67 (free and total change alike; about 55% bound).** The 89% and 94% figures support it.
  The 55% is the paper's own introductory statement (PDF p 2), although Figure 2 implies about 62% bound
  (derived). **Matches.**
- **R/drugs_lamotrigine.R:95 (citation).** Authors, journal, year, volume, pages and DOI agree with PubMed.
  **Matches.**
- **inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv:104.** The row's n of 221 and its popPK design are Karanam's, and Pennell is
  cited only for +94% and +89%. **Matches.**
- **Pinned number:** LAMOTRIGINE_PREGNANCY_CL_FACTOR = 2.3 (R/drugs_lamotrigine.R:105) is not taken from this
  paper. Pennell's 1.94 is a lower cross-check of it, and it would be lower still if the weight-basis question
  resolves toward pregnant weight. When the published PDF arrives, re-check the 94% and 89% and Figure 2 against
  it.
- **Baseline model:** Milosheska's CL/F of 2.32 L/h at 70 kg is about 0.80 L/kg/day (derived: 2.32 x 24 / 70).
  Pennell's baseline total CL of about 1.03 L/kg/day is about 30% higher. That direction fits Pennell's young
  women free of interacting drugs, against Milosheska's mixed co-medication population, which includes valproate.
  The figure is approximate, read from the bar chart.
