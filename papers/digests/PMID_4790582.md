# Zabransky 1973: MIC and MBC of seven antibiotics against Bacteroides fragilis

## Study
In-vitro susceptibility study. 50 clinical isolates of *B. fragilis* (Milwaukee, Mount Sinai Medical Center),
tested against penicillin, tetracycline, chloramphenicol, erythromycin, lincomycin, clindamycin and rifampin.
Broth dilution in 1-mL volumes of thioglycolate broth with dextrose, no indicator (THIO); final concentrations
0.008 to 128 ug/mL (twofold, log-convenient series). Inoculum: 48-h culture in THIO + 10% horse serum diluted 1:50;
the text layer reads "7 X 100 organisms per ml" (exponent lost; page 2 not rendered). Incubation 35 C, 48 h,
anaerobic (Gas-Pak or anaerobic incubator, 10% CO2/10% H2/80% N2).
- MIC: first tube without turbidity.
- MBC: 0.01-mL calibrated loop from each tube at or above the MIC onto enriched blood agar; lowest concentration
  allowing **no growth** (complete kill, not 99.9%).
- THIO vs trypticase soy broth (TSB): MIC one dilution lower in TSB for clindamycin; MBC one dilution lower in TSB
  for clindamycin (p. 2, printed p. 153).

## Model and parameters

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points are in-vitro MIC (turbidity) and MBC (no growth on subculture). Authors: clindamycin was the only drug
bactericidal at serum-attainable levels; 88% killed by 8 ug/mL, attainable "with higher doses" (p. 155). Other
drugs (p. 153-155): penicillin MBC 4-8x MIC; chloramphenicol MBC >= 32x MIC; tetracycline MBC 64x MIC; rifampin
MBC about 128x MIC. Clindamycin significantly more effective than lincomycin. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or
potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- 1973 isolates; resistance today is far higher (the repo cites 25-60%).
- Thioglycolate broth (a reducing agent) gave MICs/MBCs one dilution higher than TSB for clindamycin.
- Total drug in broth; no protein; "attainable in serum" refers to a 1971 textbook (Seneca).
- MBC by complete kill on a 0.01-mL loop, stricter than a 99.9% criterion; derived MBC50/90 depend on that.
- Inoculum exponent unreadable in the text layer.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Row at `R/antibioticMbc.R:272-286`: MBC90 = 1.6 mg/L, Basis "measured, 80% of isolates (MBC80)", from Levison 1977.
  The note (line 276) says the MBC is 4 to 128 times the MIC, and the source (lines 282-283) credits Zabransky for it.
  - The 4-128-fold range in Zabransky is the span over **all seven antibiotics** (abstract). For clindamycin the paper
    says four- to eightfold (p. 155); the Table 1 distributions give MBC90/MIC90 = 16 and MBC50/MIC50 = 8 (derived).
    The 128x end is rifampin (p. 154). **Discrepancy** (attribution), see open question.
  - Zabransky's own data do not support 1.6 mg/L as a near-MBC90: only 34% of 50 strains were killed at 1 and 44% at
    2 ug/mL; MBC90 16 (derived). The row does not cite Zabransky for the 1.6 value, so this is a conflicting
    independent source, not a transcription error. Because the core row's MBC90 sets clindamycin's upper band edge
    (rule at `R/antibioticMbc.R:40-43`), the choice matters; left as a `[decision]`.
  - The other statements in the note (slow killing, no concentration dependence, 25-60% resistance, label dosing) are
    attributed to other sources, not checked here.
- Units: ug/mL in the paper = mg/L in the code; consistent. The code's MBCs are free drug; the paper's are in protein-free
  broth, so equivalent to free drug.
