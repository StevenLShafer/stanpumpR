# Smith 1984: alprazolam oral vs IV kinetics, bioavailability and sedation

## Study
- 6 healthy nonsmoking men, 20-32 y; open-label, not placebo-controlled, balanced crossover, 1-week washout.
- 1.0 mg alprazolam as an oral tablet (180 mL water) or slow IV injection over 1 min (1 mg/mL in propylene
  glycol-water), after a 12-h fast; 4-h fast after dosing.
- Plasma at 10, 20, 40 min and 1-36 h; GLC with electron capture (Greenblatt 1981).
- PD: digit-symbol substitution (DSS), perceptual speed (PS) at 0, 1.25, 2.5, 5, 12.5 h; Stanford Sleepiness Scale;
  Nurse-Rated Sedation Score (NRSS, 1-8) at each blood sample.
- Curve stripping (CSTRIP): biexponential IV, triexponential oral; AUC trapezoidal + extrapolation; CL = dose/AUC;
  Vd = CL/beta; F = AUC oral / AUC IV.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- NRSS onset 12 min IV vs 40 min oral (P < 0.05); peak scores similar; time of peak sedation in Table 2 reads
  0.86 h IV and 1.13 h oral, but the abstract says 0.48 h for IV and the discussion a mean peak effect at 50 min
  (internal inconsistency).
- DSS and PS impaired at 1.25 and 2.5 h after both routes, back to baseline by 5 h.
- Counterclockwise hysteresis of NRSS vs plasma concentration after both routes (Fig. 3); authors attribute it to
  distribution delay and note apparent acute tolerance. No ke0 estimated.
- No EC50, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None examined (n = 6 young men).

## Caveats
- n = 6, open label, unblinded PD; F has a wide range (0.58-1.60), and the reported 0.92 is not simply the ratio
  of mean AUCs.
- Single 1 mg dose; GLC assay.
