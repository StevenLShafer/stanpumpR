# Wattanakul 2020: piperaquine PK and QTc effect in African malaria patients

## Study
Prospective, observational, open-label post-licensing pharmacovigilance study of dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine (Eurartesim), NCT02199951, 10 sites in Ghana, Tanzania, Burkina Faso, Mozambique. Nested ECG/PK cohort: 1000 patients analysed (of 1305 randomised; 30 lost, 11 baseline QTcF >450 ms, 262 incomplete). Table 1 (image, PDF p. 3): 482 male / 518 female; age groups <1 y 6, 1-<5 y 238, 5-<12 y 450, 12-<18 y 127, >=18 y 179 (Total column); about 70% aged <12 y. Median (IQR) age 7.5 (5-12) y, weight 21 (15-38) kg.
Dosing: weight-banded once daily for 3 days (Table 5, PDF p. 10): e.g. old 5-12 kg 160 mg piperaquine, 36-74 kg 960 mg; new 5-<8 kg 160 mg up to >80 kg 1600 mg. Fasted; 3 h fasting either side. PK samples at about 0, 48, 52, 120, 144, 168 h after first dose (1-5 per patient; 2989 concentrations, 1.44% below LLOQ omitted). Assay: plasma, SPE plus LC-MS/MS, LLOQ 1.50 ng/ml. ECG: predose day 1 (triplicate), predose day 3, postdose day 3 (triplicate), day 7. NONMEM 7.3, FOCE-I; frequentist prior ($PRIOR) from the 728-person pooled piperaquine meta-analysis (ref 10) because sampling stopped at 7 days.

## Model and parameters
Three-compartment disposition, two transit absorption compartments, oral. Allometric scaling on weight (CL, Q exponent 0.75; V exponent 1), maturation of CL with age, dose-occasion effect on bioavailability. Parameters are apparent (/F) for a 54 kg typical individual. The prior's reported terminal half-life is 18-28 days (methods) or 20-30 days (intro); the study data alone could not define it.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Concentration-QTc: plasma piperaquine (total, assay in plasma) against QTc corrected with a study-specific exponent. Rate correction: log QT on log RR from 2907 pretreatment pairs gave alpha 0.476 (95% CI 0.468-0.484); day 3 0.442, day 7 0.435; QTcSSB had zero RR slope (0.00180, P=0.332), QTcF slope 0.0877, QTcB -0.0137. Absolute-QTc model: Emax with gamma (Table 4). Linear slopes of delta-QTc per 100 ng/ml by correction: SSB 5.90 ms, F 7.97, B 5.30, DAYS 4.11 (p. 7); an earlier absolute-QTcSSB linear fit gave 4.87 ms. Peak QTc on day 3 after the last dose, back near baseline by day 7. No ke0, tPeak or hysteresis analysis is reported (QTc was modelled directly on the predicted plasma concentration, sequential PK-PD). Simulations: probability QTcSSB >500 ms 1.1-1.3%; delta-QTcmax >60 ms 1.98-2.25% (abstract 1.98-2.46%). Observed: 7.0% of patients had delta-QTcSSB >60 ms and 2.7% max QTcSSB >500 ms. No cardiovascular adverse events. Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
PK: weight (allometric), age maturation of CL, dose occasion; no further covariate significant. PD: potassium on baseline QTc (-1.06 ms per mmol/L) judged negligible and dropped; age on EC50 retained (lower EC50 in young children, so more QTc effect at the same concentration). Fever at enrollment associated with larger delta-QTc, confounded with malaria recovery.

## Caveats
Authors: sampling only 7 days, hence the prior; malaria and fever resolution confound the day-3 QTc rise; patients with long QT, QT-prolonging drugs or family history excluded; fasted dosing only; the mass-drug-administration simulation is extrapolated from patients. Also: the PK table is for a 54 kg typical patient while most subjects are children (median 21 kg), so scaling is by the allometric model only.
