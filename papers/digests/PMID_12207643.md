# Sheiner 2002: is intention-to-treat analysis enough?

## Study
Conceptual review (Rubin causal model); no data, no drug, no patients. Presented at the Royal Society of Edinburgh meeting, 13-14 June 2001 (p.203).

## Content
- Intention-to-treat estimates "use effectiveness" (effect of prescribing); the author argues "method effectiveness" (effect of actually taking the drug) matters more for dosing and extrapolation (p.206).
- Compliance types a, t, c, n (Table 1) and responder types (Table 2); estimands ACE, ACACE, TACE, CACE (Eq. 2.1-2.3).
- Instrumental-variable estimator IV = ACE-hat / p-hat(at); valid when ACEc = ACEn = 0, which holds under Zelen's conditions (pp.207-208).
- Per-protocol estimator is valid for ACACE only under non-confounding compliance (p.209).
- Dropout: LOCF generally leaves non-ignorable missingness; unbiased imputation needs scientific (PK/PD) models whose adequacy cannot be tested on observed data (p.210).
- Design advice (p.211): Zelen conditions, monitor compliance throughout, longitudinal endpoints, baseline and serial prognostic covariates, prespecified models.

## Caveats
Expository; the author assumes large trials and no small-sample variability (p.206).
