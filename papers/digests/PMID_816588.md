# Buchanan 1976: once-daily versus divided ethosuximide in children with petit mal

## Study
- 9 institutionalized patients with petit mal epilepsy (Parke-Davis / Coldwater State Home), age 8.7-19.8 y (median 16.5), weight 18.6-57.3 kg (median 35.0); all but one also took other antiepileptic drugs at constant doses; stable ethosuximide dose for at least 3 months.
- Design: previously established total daily dose given divided (bid, tid or qid) 4 weeks (Phase I), as a single 8:00 AM dose 4 weeks (Phase II), divided again 4 weeks (Phase III). Individual doses 11.1-40.3 mg/kg/day; mean about 21 mg/kg/day.
- Sampling: 2 h after the first morning dose every Monday; predose every other week; a 24-h profile (predose, 2, 4, 6, 8, 24 h) on day 9 of each phase.
- Assay: GC (Glazko and Dill), plasma, total.

Table II mean plasma levels (mcg/mL): predose 53.6 (Phase I), 38.4 (Phase II), 40.5 (Phase III); 2 h post 64.5, 68.9, 47.8; pooled 60.6, 57.9, 46.8. Mean levels across phases 47-61 mcg/mL at a mean 21 mg/kg/day.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: clinical observation of petit mal seizures; none occurred in any phase. Three patients had grand mal seizures, frequency unchanged.
- Therapeutic range cited from earlier reports: 40-70 mcg/mL (refs 9, 11, 13); usual dose 20 mg/kg/day.
- No effect site or concentration-effect model.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
- n = 9; older and heavier than a typical absence-epilepsy child (median 16.5 y, 35 kg); most on concomitant antiepileptic drugs, which may induce metabolism; institutionalized.
- How the 29-h half-life was estimated is not described beyond a sentence.
- Unexplained lower levels in Phase III.
