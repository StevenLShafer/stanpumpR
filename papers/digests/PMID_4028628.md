# Chow 1985: cocaine two-compartment kinetics and biophase chronotropic effect with attenuation

## Study
- 5 otherwise healthy adults with a history of cocaine use (at least weekly for the 2 months before), on a clinical research unit; no cocaine for 24 h before. Table I (PDF p2, printed 319): 4 M / 1 F, age 21-36 y, weight 51.6-76.2 kg (derived mean 66.9 kg), all within 10% of ideal body weight (96-109%).
- Dose: 1 mL of 32 mg/mL **cocaine HCl** IV over 2 min (an aliquot assayed for the exact dose). Plasma (NaF/oxalate tubes) at 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, 180 min; all urine collected; ECG heart rate at each sample for the first 60-90 min.
- Assay: GC with a nitrogen-phosphorus (thermionic) detector, a modification of Jatlow & Bailey; linear 5-500 ng/mL; CV <10% at 10-500 ng/mL and 10-20% at 5-10 ng/mL.
- Analysis: SAAM 23. Individual fits of a two-compartment open model with central elimination, with a renal-excretion "summer" compartment for renal clearance. PD: a linear-effect biophase model (Holford & Sheiner) with no net mass transfer, plus a first-order attenuation "function generator" (Fig. 1, PDF p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio. End point: **heart rate from the ECG** (chronotropic effect).
- Model (PDF p3-4): E = E0 + G·S·Cb. Cb is a hypothetical biophase concentration with entry constant ki and exit constant ko, and no net mass transfer. S is the initial slope. G starts at 1 and decays first-order at kg (attenuation, attributed to either acute tolerance or counter-regulation).
- Table III (PDF p5; rendered), mean ± SD (n = 5): ki 1.794 ± 0.954 /min; ko 0.155 ± 0.039 /min (derived t½ 4.5 min); E0 78.2 ± 7.2 bpm; S 0.288 ± 0.133 bpm per ng/mL; kg 0.0294 ± 0.0233 /min (t½ 23.6 min, PDF p5 text); **t_lag 7.3 ± 4.0 min**, the simulated delay from an instantaneous bolus to maximal chronotropic response (range 3.6-13.3).
- Observed: heart rate peaked 5-15 min after the start of the infusion and was back to baseline within ~60 min; mean fit deviation 3.3%.
- The biophase uses two constants (ki, ko), not a single ke0, and the paper does not give the biophase-to-plasma ratio at equilibrium. So S (per ng/mL of *biophase*) cannot be read as a slope per ng/mL of plasma, and ko is not interchangeable with a ke0 without the model equations.

## Covariates
None. Per-kg Vss is reported alongside absolute values; no covariate analysis.

## Caveats
- n = 5, cocaine-experienced users, one 32 mg (HCl) dose, sampling to 180 min.
- Individual parameters are very variable (CLI CV ~106%).
- The salt-versus-base basis of the dose used in the calculations is not stated.
- Comparisons in the paper: Vss/Varea are larger than Barnett 1981's (121 L, 1.59 L/kg) and smaller than the 161 L derived from Javaid 1983. CL 2.10 L/min lies between Javaid (2.45) and Barnett (1.57). t½ 48 min is within Javaid 1978's 16-87 min and close to Barnett (58) and Javaid 1983 (43). Kogan's 2.8 h is attributed to assay limits. All comparators are other IV studies.

- **One-compartment reduction**: k = 2.10/131.8 = 0.015933/min, t½ 43.5 min (`:51-52`, test `:35-37`). Arithmetic **matches**. The test's framing, a half-life "between Javaid's 41.4 and Chow's 48", is correct as arithmetic.
- **"The micro-constants (V1, Q, V2) are not in the accessible report, so the two-compartment model cannot be rebuilt"** (`R/drugs_cocaine.R:41-42`; `inst/help/drugs/cocaine.md:7`; `inst/extdata/illicitRouteRegistry.csv:8`, "published only Vss, clearance and the terminal half-life"): **discrepancy**. Table II gives Vc (25.9 L), Vp (105.9 L) and CLI (31.79 L/min) for each subject and as means. The reduction, and with it the stated reason for refusing the IV route, rests on a premise the paper contradicts (open question 1). Rebuilt from mean values, the derived model has a distribution t½ under 1 min and a terminal t½ ~45 min. That is unlike Jeffcoat's 11 min distribution half-life, which the repo cites (`:55-60`), so the IV question is not settled by the rebuild alone.
- **Mean weight ~67 kg (131.8/1.96)** (`:97-99`, help `:33`): Table I gives a derived mean of 66.9 kg. **matches**
- **Biophase: peak effect 7.3 min after an IV bolus, slope 0.3 bpm per ng/mL, first-order attenuation** (`:21-27`, help `:27`, spec `:244`): match Table III (t_lag 7.3 min, S 0.288) and the text (0.3 bpm per ng/mL, kg attenuation). The repo's reason for not implementing it (the single ke0 cannot carry attenuation) is consistent with the paper. Note that "peak chronotropic effect 7.3 min" is the paper's simulated t_lag, not an observed time. **matches**
- **Registry `parameter_dose_basis` "cocaine hydrochloride as dosed"** (`illicitRouteRegistry.csv:7-8`): the paper doses the HCl salt but never states the calculation basis, so it cannot be verified (open question 2). **cannot verify**
- **"five adults (Chow)"** (registry row 8): matches Table I.
- Verdict: **discrepancy** (the "no micro-constants" claim), with the dose basis unverifiable.
