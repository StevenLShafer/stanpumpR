# Dutta 2001: dexmedetomidine-propofol pharmacodynamic interaction

## Study
Single-centre, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled two-period crossover, 14-day washout, 10 healthy non-smoking males 18-45 years enrolled, 9 completed (mean age 24 y, range 18-28; weight 73.7 kg, 61.0-87.3; height 175 cm, 168-183; PDF p.5, printed 175). Dexmedetomidine (or saline) by computer-controlled infusion (Harvard 22 pump driven by Stanpump) to a plasma concentration held at 0.66 +/- 0.080 ng/mL; 45 min later propofol (Dipravan 1%) by a second pump in up to 11 logarithmically ascending steps, 1.00 to 13.8 ug/mL, 10 min per step. Pharmacodynamics assessed at 8 min (hold a syringe, eyelash reflex, motor response to a 10-s 100-Hz electrical stimulus) or 5 min (Ramsay score) into each step; both infusions stopped once all three end points were lost in sequence. Dexmedetomidine assay GC/MS, LLOQ 10 pg/mL, CV up to 15%; propofol assay HPLC with fluorescence, LLOQ 0.061 ug/mL, CV up to 5%, on plasma. Logistic regression with repeated measures (SAS GENMOD); the Appendix model is P = logistic(a1 + a2 Pr + a3 D + a4 Pr D) with D coded 0 or 1, a4 the interaction.

Propofol dose 994 +/- 404 mg (placebo) vs 492 +/- 259 mg (dexmedetomidine); duration 1.34 vs 0.93 h; propofol concentration at end of infusion 5.97 +/- 2.00 vs 3.97 +/- 2.34 ug/mL (p. 175-176). Recovery time 16 +/- 10 vs 15 +/- 7.7 min, not different; propofol at emergence 2.32 +/- 0.694 vs 0.803 +/- 0.226 ug/mL; dexmedetomidine at emergence 0.470 ng/mL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Plasma (not effect-site) concentrations, near steady state for each 10-min step, so no ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis is estimated or reported. Not an opioid; no MEAC.

(EC50app: the propofol concentration for a 50% probability of the end point at dexmedetomidine 0.66 ng/mL, a single level; Appendix eq. A10.) Sedation end points fell 65-80% (hold syringe and Ramsay >3, >4, >5); motor response about 40%, but the dexmedetomidine term a3 was not significant for motor response, so the reduction rests on the point estimate plus the lower propofol concentrations at the stopping criterion. Interaction (a4) was significant (antagonism, in the paper's "alertness" orientation) for eyelash reflex and Ramsay >2 only, where subjects were already sedated by dexmedetomidine alone; none for the other end points. The dexmedetomidine EC50 itself was not computed (one level only).
Context quoted by the authors: propofol about 1-2 ug/mL for sleep, 2-4 for maintenance of anaesthesia, 7-10 for surgical anaesthesia (literature).

## Covariates
None analysed (nine healthy men; dexmedetomidine as present/absent).

## Caveats
n = 9, males only, a single dexmedetomidine concentration (0.66 ng/mL, mid therapeutic range per the authors), wide confidence intervals (the hold-syringe EC50app interval includes negative values), electrical-stimulation movement rather than surgical stimulus. Propofol concentrations are plasma assay values during a staircase, not effect-site.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No locations to verify. Independent comparisons with the library:
- Dexmedetomidine adult model (R/drugs_dexmedetomidine.R:107-113, Morse 2020): at 70 kg standard, CL1 0.897 L/min = 53.8 L/h and V1+V2+V3 = 25.2 + 34.4 + 65.4 = 125.0 L (derived). Dutta (noncompartmental, 73.7 kg men) found CL 41.3 L/h (0.69 L/min) and Vss 126 L. Vss agrees; Dutta's clearance is lower, about 0.77 of the Morse value (derived), the direction being Dutta lower. Dutta is a different population and analysis (noncompartmental, a 1.7-h infusion), so this is a sanity check only; Morse's clearance also includes the 70-kg standard scaled by fat-free mass^0.75.
- Therapeutic band: the library's dexmedetomidine range 0.4-0.8 ng/mL, typical 0.6 (drugDefaults_global.csv line 12) brackets Dutta's 0.66 ng/mL, which the authors describe as the middle of the anticipated range. Dutta found that concentration alone left a 0.42 probability of lost eyelash reflex, i.e. marked sedation (Table 2, footnote c).
- Propofol: Dutta's propofol Ce-free plasma EC50s for loss of syringe grip, eyelash reflex and Ramsay (1.1-2.0 ug/mL) lie below the library's default target band 2.5-4 mcg/mL (drugDefaults_global.csv line 2), consistent with that band being a maintenance range, not a loss-of-response one. Dutta's values are plasma, the library's tPeak (1.6 min) model is effect-site, so they are only loosely comparable.
Verdict: not applicable (no repo location cites this paper).
