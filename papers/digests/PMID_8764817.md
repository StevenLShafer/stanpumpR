# Eriksson 1996: lamotrigine interactions in children with intractable epilepsy

## Study
Prospective add-on study, Karolinska Hospital (Stockholm) and Children's Hospital (Helsinki), February 1992
to September 1993, in an **unselected consecutive series of 31 children and young adults** aged over 2 years
with intractable generalised epilepsy (not seizure-free despite adequate trials of at least three
conventional antiepileptics). 27 were aged 2.5-15 y, 2 were 16.9 and 17.6 y, and 2 were young adults of 21
and 22 y; 15 boys and 16 girls, 10.9-89 kg. Median 140 seizures a month (range 4-680). Ten were on three
antiepileptics, 17 on two and 4 on one when lamotrigine was added; the concomitant combinations are listed
in Table 1 (printed p 770).

Two parts:
- **Single-dose study**: one oral lamotrigine dose as close as possible to 1 mg/kg (mean 0.8, range
  0.4-1.2 mg/kg), with blood before the dose and at 0.5, 1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 24, 48, 60 and 72 h. Existing
  drugs were in steady state and their levels were held constant by TDM-guided dose adjustment throughout.
- **Continuous-treatment study**: lamotrigine 1 mg/kg/day in two doses (0.5 mg/kg/day for children on
  valproate without carbamazepine/phenobarbital), increased every 2 weeks until response or side effects,
  plasma kept <= 10 ug/mL; the final dose was reached after a mean of 5 months (range 2-12).
- CSF was sampled by lumbar puncture in 23 children before lamotrigine and at steady state.

Assay: a reversed-phase HPLC with UV detection at 270 nm developed in-house, LOQ 0.1 ug/mL; other drugs by
TDx or HPLC. Pharmacokinetics by standard non-compartmental methods (terminal slope by regression on at
least three points, AUC trapezoidal with extrapolation); only **Cl/F** and **Vd/F** could be computed, as
no intravenous formulation existed. **Eight of 31 patients were excluded from some calculations** (missing
data, fewer than three points, or no identifiable linear slope); two children on carbamazepine had no
measurable lamotrigine by 9 and 12 h.

Three comedication groups: **VPA** (valproate without carbamazepine or phenobarbital, n = 8),
**CBZ/PB** (carbamazepine and/or phenobarbital without valproate, n = 13), and **other combinations**
(valproate *and* carbamazepine/phenobarbital, **or other antiepileptic drugs**, n = 10). There is **no
monotherapy group**.

## Model and parameters
Results, printed p 771 (confirmed on the rendered page):

| Group | n | Median LTG t1/2 (range) |
|---|---|---|
| VPA | 8 | **43.3 h** (34.7-77.0) |
| CBZ/PB | 13 | **14.1 h** (5.6-43.3) |
| Other combinations | 10 | **28.9 h** (15.1-57.8) |

VPA vs CBZ/PB p = 0.0004. Cl/F was significantly greater in CBZ/PB than in VPA (p = 0.004) and than in the
other-combination group (p = 0.007); **Vd/F was similar in all three groups** (Figure 2, printed p 771 -
the figure gives Cl/F on an axis in mL/min/kg but no group medians are printed in the text). Age was not
linearly related to t1/2 (p = 0.21), Vd (p = 0.66) or Cl/F (p = 0.49).

Whole-group single-dose values: median Cmax 0.7 ug/mL (0.3-1.4) and median Tmax 2.5 h (0.5-6), similar in
the three groups. In the subgroup aged 4.8-11 years (n = 20) the half-lives were 51.1 h (6 on VPA), 12 h
(8 on inducers) and 27.3 h (6 on other combinations) - the same pattern.

Continuous treatment: dose and trough were linearly related in all groups; the CBZ/PB group split into fast
(t1/2 < 12 h, n = 5) and slow (> 12 h, n = 6) eliminators. **No clinically important change** in plasma
carbamazepine, valproate, ethosuximide or phenobarbital over 2-12 months of follow-up, and no comedication
dose adjustment was needed; **clonazepam fell** a mean 38% in 4 of 8 patients (unchanged, < 20%, in the
other 4), and in 3 of 5 patients clonazepam became undetectable in CSF after lamotrigine.

CSF (Table 2, printed p 773): lamotrigine CSF/plasma ratio about **43%** (median plasma 2.5, CSF 1.1 ug/mL,
n = 22-23).

Derived ratios (mine): t1/2 VPA/CBZ-PB = 43.3/14.1 = **3.07**; with Vd/F reported as similar across groups,
this is also the approximate clearance ratio between those two regimens.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis. The only pharmacodynamic context is the clinical dosing ceiling
(plasma not allowed above 10 ug/mL, the manufacturer's recommendation at the time) and seizure frequency as
an inclusion criterion. CSF concentrations are reported as a distribution measure, not an effect site, and
no ke0 or equilibration time is estimated.

## Covariates
Concomitant antiepileptic regimen only: the lamotrigine half-lives are reported by group (valproate alone,
inducers alone, both, other combinations). No weight, age or sex covariate is modelled; age and weight enter
only as the study's range (27 of 31 aged 2.5-15 y; 10.9-89 kg).

## Caveats
- **No monotherapy arm**, so every number is a contrast between comedication regimens. The familiar
  "inducers halve and valproate doubles" framing cannot be derived from this study alone.
- Small groups (8, 13, 10) with **8 of 31 patients excluded** from some parameters, including two
  carbamazepine patients whose lamotrigine was undetectable by 9-12 h - an exclusion that removes the most
  extreme inducer cases and biases the CBZ/PB half-life **upward**.
- Wide ranges (the CBZ/PB half-life spans 5.6-43.3 h and overlaps the VPA range), so the medians separate
  the groups much more cleanly than the individuals do.
- Apparent parameters only; bioavailability is not separable, which the authors state explicitly as a
  limit on interpreting the Cl/F difference.
- The "other combinations" group is heterogeneous by construction (valproate **plus** inducers, **or** other
  antiepileptics), so its intermediate 28.9 h is not a clean "both" group.
- Single oral dose in children already at steady state on several drugs; no autoinduction was detectable.
