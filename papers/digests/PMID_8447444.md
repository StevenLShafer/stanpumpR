# Davison 1993: metabolic clearance of AVP and dDAVP across late pregnancy and postpartum

## Study
Serial paired study, Newcastle and Chicago: six normotensive healthy women, not breastfeeding or on oral
contraceptives, each studied in late pregnancy (LP, third trimester), 24-48 h after delivery (PD), 5-6 wk (PP1) and
10-12 wk postpartum (PP2), with two constant-infusion tests at each time in random order, one AVP and one dDAVP
(PDF p1). Water load 20 mL/kg; plasma osmolality held at 270 (pregnant) / 275 (postpartum) mosmol/kg to suppress
endogenous AVP. Three-tier primed constant infusions of 75 min each; samples at 45, 60, 75 min. AVP infusion 84,
144, 246 pg/kg/min in LP and PD, 24, 42, 72 pg/kg/min at PP1 and PP2 (Table 1). RIA (AVP standard: highly purified
AVP, potency 400 U/mg, Ferring; PDF p2). MCR = infusion rate x infusate concentration / steady-state plasma level.

MCR was unchanged across the roughly threefold range of steady-state plasma AVP within each period (2-9 pg/mL,
physiological) (PDF p3). Vasopressinase undetectable by PP2; PD MCR 30% below LP (P < 0.01). Placental in-situ
inactivation estimated at up to about a third of MCR (PDF p4). Earlier work (ref 6): MCR rises from gestational
week 7-8 to fourfold by shortly after midgestation, then plateaus. Volume of distribution not measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Urine osmolality: similar submaximal concentration needed plasma dDAVP about 3-4 times plasma AVP (dDAVP weaker per
pg/mL). dDAVP raised VWF and factor VIIIc about fourfold in pregnancy, twofold nonpregnant (NS). No pressor data.

## Covariates
Pregnancy stage.

## Caveats
n = 6; physiological concentrations only (no data at pressor doses); 10-12 wk postpartum used as the nonpregnant
state; purity of the dDAVP lots not confirmed by the authors.
