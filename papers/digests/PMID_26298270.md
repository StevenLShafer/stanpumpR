# Dash 2016: simple models of blood HbO2 and HbCO2 dissociation curves

## Study
Modelling paper (no new subjects). Revises Dash and Bassingthwaighte 2010 (Ann Biomed Eng 38:1683): an invertible Hill-type SHbO2 and SHbCO2 model, now with a PO2-dependent Hill coefficient so it is accurate over the whole saturation range, a refined P50 "equation of state", a simpler K4' expression, and numerical inversion schemes. Fitted to published human-blood curves: Severinghaus and colleagues (Roughton 1972/1973; Severinghaus 1979), Winslow 1977 (standard conditions); Joels and Pugh 1958, Naeraa 1963, Hlastala 1977, Reeves 1980 (pH, PCO2, temperature); Bauer and Schroder 1972 and Matthew 1977 (CO2 binding). Compared with the models of Adair 1925, Kelman 1966, Severinghaus 1979, Siggaard-Andersen 1984 and Buerk and Bridges 1986 (Fig. 7). Software not named.

## Model and parameters
Core equations (PDF pp. 3-5):
- SHbO2 = KHbO2[O2] / (1 + KHbO2[O2]); SHbCO2 = KHbCO2[CO2] / (1 + KHbCO2[CO2]) (Eq. 1a,b). Concentrations are molar in the RBC water space; [O2] = alphaO2 * PO2 (alphaO2 1.46e-6 M/mmHg at 37 C), [CO2] = alphaCO2 * PCO2 (3.27e-5 M/mmHg).
- Hill form: SHbO2 = (PO2/P50)^nH / (1 + (PO2/P50)^nH) (Eq. 6).
- nH = alpha - beta * 10^(-PO2/gamma) (Eq. 11), the new variable-cooperativity term.
- P50 = P50,S * (P50,pH/P50,S) * (P50,CO2/P50,S) * (P50,DPG/P50,S) * (P50,T/P50,S) (Eq. 10), with polynomials (Eq. 9a-d): pH term -25.535(pH-pHS) + 10.646(pH-pHS)^2 - 1.764(pH-pHS)^3; PCO2 term 0.1273(PCO2-40) + 1.083e-4(PCO2-40)^2; DPG term 795.63(dDPG) - 19660.89(dDPG)^2 (DPG in M); temperature term 1.435(T-37) + 0.04163(T-37)^2 + 6.86e-4(T-37)^3. Standard values: PO2 100 mmHg, PCO2 40 mmHg, pHpl 7.4, pHrbc 7.24 (pHrbc = 0.795 pHpl + 1.357), [DPG] 4.65 mM, T 37 C (Table 1, PDF p. 4).
- Total O2: [O2]tot = Wbl*alphaO2*PO2 + 4*Hct*[Hb]rbc*SHbO2 (Table 1); Hct 0.45, [Hb]bl 2.33 mM, [Hb]rbc 5.18 mM, Wbl 0.81.
- Inversion: fixed-point (Scheme 1) and quasi-Newton (Scheme 2), Appendix (Eqs. A-1, A-2), 3-8 iterations to 1e-3.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not a drug paper; no ke0, MEAC, EC50 or potency ratio. The physiological end point is haemoglobin O2 saturation (SHbO2) and CO2 saturation against PO2/PCO2. Reported fit quality (PDF p. 6, printed p. 102): residual error below 0.05% saturation for variable nH below 40% saturation versus about 3-4% for constant nH (Fig. 1c). Fig. 7: this model, Severinghaus 1979 and Siggaard-Andersen 1984 had the smallest residuals; most models exceed 99.5% accuracy over the whole range (printed p. 111).

## Covariates
PCO2, pH, 2,3-DPG and temperature enter only through P50 (Eq. 9-10); pH dominates the shift (printed p. 107). No patient covariates.

## Caveats
Equilibrium (steady-state) model; fits to literature curves, not new measurements. The 2010 model was only accurate for SHbO2 0.30-0.98 with nH 2.7 (printed p. 98). Two data sets give different nH parameters and P50 (26.8 vs 29.1 mmHg). Table 1 values marked c are estimated from Severinghaus's data. Fig. 2 compares P50 only after rescaling factors 0.833, 0.588, 1.02 on the pH, PCO2 and T terms.

Content = 1.34*Hb*S + 0.003*PO2 (R/arterialOxygen.R:120-145). Independent check (derived: I evaluated the repo's equation and this paper's Eq. 6 + 11 with Table 1 / Fig. 1e values alpha 2.82, beta 1.20, gamma 29.25, P50 26.8): saturation at PO2 5/10/20/26.86/40/60/100/150 mmHg is 0.036/0.097/0.320/0.500/0.750/0.906/0.978/0.993 for Severinghaus versus 0.033/0.096/0.320/0.502/0.752/0.906/0.976/0.992 for Dash 2016. They agree within 0.003 above 10 mmHg, so the repo's curve is consistent with this paper's fit to the same Severinghaus data at standard conditions (the test at tests/testthat/test-arterial-oxygen.R:6-7 pins P50 26.86 and 0.977 at 100 mmHg, matching). The largest relative difference is below 10 mmHg (0.036 vs 0.033 at 5 mmHg); Fig. 7b,d (printed p. 110) shows Severinghaus's equation among the lowest-residual models against its own data. Dash's pH, PCO2, DPG and temperature P50 shifts (Eq. 9) are not in stanpumpR; the 1.34 mL/g and 0.003 mL/dL/mmHg constants are not in this paper (it works in molar units).
