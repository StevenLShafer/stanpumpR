# Lemmens 1990: age and sex effects on alfentanil pharmacokinetics

## Study
- Open-label PK study, 36 adults having elective lower abdominal surgery under N2O/alfentanil anaesthesia: 21 women (age 25-79) and 15 men (age 24-78); normal cardiac, hepatic, renal and pulmonary function; malnourished, grossly obese, drug/alcohol abusers and premedicated patients excluded (PDF p2-3). No difference in age or weight distribution between sexes.
- Dosing: Wagner-scheme infusion, initial 15.5 mcg/kg/min for 15 min, then 1.21 mcg/kg/min until the peritoneum was closed. Infusion lasted 45-140 min; plasma concentration at end of the initial infusion 606-1740 mcg/L (units as printed "ng/mL"-equivalent mcg/L).
- Sampling: arterial blood at 1, 2, 5, 7, 10, 15, 17, 20, 25, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90 min and then every 30 min during the infusion; after stopping 0-240 min arterial, then venous at 240, 360, 480, 600 min. Venous concentrations at 240 min were 14 +/- 11% (mean +/- SD) higher than arterial.
- Assay: capillary gas chromatography with nitrogen detector, total plasma concentration (CV not above 5%); protein binding by equilibrium dialysis, 4 h at 37 C; fu = Cd/(Cp - 2Cd).
- Analysis: two- and three-compartment models fitted by extended least squares; 18 patients were best described by a triexponential equation; the rest (women nos 11, 13, 16, 17, 19 and men nos 6, 8, 12 are listed in the text, whose OCR also hints at a patient 14) by a biexponential one, so the exact list is not settled from the text layer. Spearman rank correlation with age; Mann-Whitney-Wilcoxon for sex comparison. 
- Co-medication: oral temazepam 10-20 mg, atropine, pancuronium, N2O; sufentanil 25 mcg to 3 patients; antibiotics to 15 patients (not different).

## Model and parameters
No population model. Per-patient CL (L/h), fu (%), terminal t1/2 (min), Vc (L), Vss (L) in Table I (women, PDF p3) and Table II (men, PDF p5). Summary values the paper itself gives:

- CL, women under 50 y: median 24.84 L/h (range 16.8-31.62); women over 50 y: median 14.52 L/h (range 4.38-18).
- CL, men under 50 y: median 19.44 L/h (range 11.16-22.56); men over 50 y: median 16.2 L/h (range 10.26-22.74).
- Under 50 y, women's CL exceeds men's (p = 0.03); over 50 y no difference.
- Spearman r with age, women (Table I): CL -0.79 (p < 0.001 in text), fu 0.20, t1/2 0.74, Vc -0.49, Vss -0.21. r2 for CL vs age 0.62. Men (Table II): CL -0.17, fu 0.53 (significant), t1/2 0.14, Vc -0.49 (printed without asterisk in Table II), Vss 0.38. All men and women combined: CL vs age r = -0.57, p = 0.003.
- Ranges read from the tables: women CL 4.38-31.62 L/h, Vc 1.8-9.3 L, Vss 21-87 L, t1/2 81-324 min, fu 3.3-23.2%; men CL 10.26-22.74 L/h, Vc 1.2-9.6 L, Vss 21-58 L, t1/2 86-417 min, fu 5.0-20.5%.
- CL, Vss and t1/2 were independent of infusion time and body weight.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: none reported. No MEAC or analgesic concentration, no ke0 / t1/2 ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis, no equianalgesic ratio. The authors cite their own earlier work (ref Lemmens 1988) that alfentanil dose requirement fell with age in women with no pharmacodynamic difference found; that is not data in this paper. Sufentanil 25 mcg rescue criteria were clinical (BP, HR, autonomic and somatic signs). Free fraction (fu) was measured as protein binding in plasma (total vs free: all concentrations reported are total plasma).

## Covariates
Age and sex only examined (Spearman, no equation). Weight and infusion time showed no correlation. CL falls with age in women but not men; fu rises with age in men (r = 0.53) but not women. Smokers: 3 women (Table I patients 4, 5, 9) and 4 men; no stated effect.

## Caveats
- Small n, rank correlation only; median CL by sex and decade split at 50 y were post hoc.
- Last samples are venous (from 360 min), venous 14% above arterial at 240 min; the authors judge the effect on CL small.
- Surgical patients on N2O, multiple co-medications; the authors have no explanation for the male fu trend.
- Authors argue liver blood flow alone cannot explain the drop in women (would need about 70% reduction).

- The library's set is Scott and Stanski 1987 as printed in Shafer and Varvel 1991: V1 2.1853 L, V2 6.698864, V3 14.52582 L, CL1 0.1988623, CL2 1.433557, CL3 0.2469389 L/min (lines 29-34), scaled by fat-free mass (volumes) and FFM ratio^0.75 (clearances), with no age or sex term.
- Model CL1 at the 70 kg reference = 0.1988623 x 60 = 11.93 L/h (derived). That is below all four Lemmens medians (14.52-24.84 L/h). The comparator differs: Lemmens' patients are surgical men and women measured on total plasma by GC, whereas Scott 1987 studied men only. The model value is a little above the lowest individual in men over 50 (10.26 L/h) and below the median of every group. This is not an error in the library (different cohort, and the library sets are Scott's), but Lemmens' women under 50 clear about twice as fast as the model (24.84 vs 11.93 L/h, derived).
- Model Vss = 2.1853 + 6.698864 + 14.52582 = 23.41 L (derived) against Lemmens Vss 21-87 L; model V1 2.19 L within Lemmens Vc range 1.2-9.6 L. Terminal half-life quoted in the file's comment (about 111 min) is within the paper's range (81-417 min).
- Units: paper uses L/h and min; the library uses L/min. No conversion fault in a comparison, none claimed.
- Default MEAC 39 ng/mL and tPeak 1.4 min (lines 40-41) are not addressed by this paper.
