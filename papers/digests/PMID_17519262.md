# Mourisse 2007: BIS and tetanic-stimulus withdrawal reflex (TIWR), sevoflurane vs propofol

## Study
- Healthy ASA I-II adults, elective plastic surgery, no premedication, no analgesics. Companion paper (part 1) covers BIS and blink reflex.
- Group S (sevoflurane) analysed n=16 of 20; Group P (propofol) n=17 of 34. Age 40 (15) and 42 (15) y, weight 76 (8) and 76 (14) kg, height 176 (11) and 170 (8) cm; 9 and 12 women. Four patients took part twice.
- Sevoflurane by mask, vaporizer raised 1 vol% every 3 min; end-expired concentration from a gas analyser, sampled every 15 s. Propofol infusion 5 then 10, 15, 20 mg/kg/h, 3 min each.
- TIWR: 1 s, 100 Hz, 50 mA tetanic stimulus to the sural nerve every 60 s, EMG of biceps femoris; effect = area under the rectified EMG for 1 s (mV ms), capped at 100 mV ms. Started after the second loss of consciousness (OAAS 2), anaesthetic raised until TIWR vanished, stopped until it returned, raised again.
- BIS (BISxp, 15 s smoothing). Propofol concentration was CALCULATED arterial plasma, Marsh 1991 PK (one value per second); no measured propofol assay.
- Two-stage analysis: individual sigmoid Emax fits, effect-site concentration from first-order ke0; parameters averaged. Excel Solver, SPSS.

## Model and parameters
E = E0 - (E0 - Emax) * Ce^g / (Ce^g + EC50^g); dCe/dt = (Cx - Ce) * ke0 (Cx = calculated arterial propofol or end-expired sevoflurane). E0 for TIWR fixed at 100 mV ms (the cap). Initial Ce for TIWR taken from the blink-reflex run of the companion paper.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Two end points: BIS (cortex) and TIWR (spinal-cord reflex, biceps femoris EMG area). ke0 for TIWR is smaller than for BIS: sevoflurane 0.25 vs 0.41, propofol 0.25 vs 0.34 min^-1 (Table 1); the difference is statistically significant only for sevoflurane (text, printed p.751). Half-lives (derived: ln2/ke0): propofol BIS 2.0 min, TIWR 2.8 min; sevoflurane BIS 1.7 min, TIWR 2.8 min.
- No tPeak is reported. The authors note their BIS ke0 is larger than values reported by others (comparator: other published BIS ke0 values, not named on the page read) and that absolute values are not critical to their within-study comparison.
- EC50 ratio TIWR/BIS: 0.70 (0.23) for sevoflurane, 0.65 (0.20) for propofol (text, p.751).
- Sevoflurane reduced TIWR to below 5 mV ms (weak movement) in 50% of patients at 1.68 vol% effect-site (abstract and discussion), against the 1.71 vol% MAC cited from Katoh and Ikeda; their extrapolated AD95 for sevoflurane is 3.46 vol% (individual fits), against 2.07 vol% from Katoh and Ikeda.
- Propofol did not abolish TIWR (Emax 4.1 mV ms) and TIWR fit was poor for propofol; paradoxical increase of lower-leg (tibialis, gastrocnemius) reflexes with rising propofol.
- Propofol C50 for skin incision of 10 ug/mL is cited from Kazama 1997, not measured here.
- Opioid audit: not an opioid study; fentanyl appears only incidentally (Fig 6 case). No MEAC, opioid ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Obesity noted as a reason for no recordable reflex in two patients.

## Caveats
- Calculated, not measured, propofol concentrations from Marsh PK (a model derived in children/adults with a different structure); ke0 depends on that PK model.
- TIWR E0 is an assumed cap (100 mV ms); high between-patient variability (CV of TIWR measures about twice BIS's); propofol TIWR poorly modelled; half the propofol group was discarded (17 of 34). Healthy volunteers-like patients, small n.
- Sevoflurane Ce is end-expired, not blood or brain.
