# Philip 1986: Gas Man, a computer simulation for teaching uptake and distribution

## Study
Descriptive educational paper, not a pharmacokinetic study. Describes the Gas Man computer-assisted instruction
package (program plus textbook, Addison Wesley 1984; about 200 copies sold at $150) whose goal is teaching
inhalation anaesthetic uptake and distribution. Frames CAI types (tutorials, drill, games, simulations) and
functions (teaching, training, testing); lists the educational objectives (Table 2) and the 27 exercises
(Table 3, chapters 3-10: one-compartment wash-in, two sequential compartments, the open-circuit alveolar tension
curve with its initial rise, knee and tail, solubility differences, optimal anaesthesia, the concentration
effect, low-flow and closed-circuit liquid injection including Lowe's square-root-of-time regimen). Objectives
are explicitly based on Kety 1950. Software: Applesoft Basic compiled to 6502 code for a 48K Apple II+, runs about
8x real time, game paddles control fresh gas flow and delivered tension; most system parameters stored separately
on the disk so they can be modified. Effectiveness supported only by cited abstracts and reviews.

## Model and parameters
Structure only, from the display descriptions (Fig 1-2): delivered gas (anaesthesia machine) -> breathing circuit
-> alveoli (FRC) -> arterial blood -> vessel-rich group, muscle, fat -> mixed venous return; flows are fresh gas
flow, alveolar ventilation and cardiac output. Tension is expressed in per cent of one atmosphere (equal to
concentration in gas compartments; equal in all compartments at equilibrium). Circuit types: open
(non-rebreathing), semi-closed, closed.

No numerical parameters (volumes, blood flow fractions, partition coefficients, default ventilation or cardiac
output) are tabulated. The only values given are the exercise settings in Fig 3: 5% delivered halothane, alveolar
ventilation raised from 1.25 to 7.5 L/min at 3 min, cardiac output 1.25 to 7.5 L/min at 6 min, both 5 L/min at
9 min; Fig 7-8 closed-circuit halothane liquid boluses of 0.5 and 0.9 mL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No MAC values, no effect-site model; "brain anesthetic tension" mentioned only as the clinical target in
the optimal-anaesthesia exercise. Agents illustrated: halothane and nitrous oxide (sevoflurane and desflurane
post-date the paper). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None described (no weight scaling or age adjustment mentioned).

## Caveats
A teaching-software report; the model's equations and parameter values are not published here. Claims that the
inhalation route is inherently safer than IV because end-tidal tension is measurable are the author's opinion.
