# Singh 1982: procainamide kinetics in children

## Study
University of Miami / Jackson Memorial (PDF p1-2, printed pp607-608).
- 6 boys, 7-12 years, with atrial or ventricular arrhythmias (Table I, p2): doses 5.8, 6.0, 5.8, 5.9, 3.9 mg/kg IV
  (patients 1-5) and 28.0 mg/kg (patient 6, an accidental overdose, not part of the prospective study). Weights not
  given. All had compensated cardiac function and no known hepatic or renal disease; no other antiarrhythmics.
- Patients 1-5: single IV dose 5.5 ± 0.9 mg/kg (mean ± SD) over 5 min; serial serum samples (times not tabulated;
  Fig. 1 runs to ~9 h). Salt (hydrochloride or base) not stated.
- Assay: GLC with nitrogen-phosphorus detector for procainamide and NAPA (modified Valentine method); CV 1 %
  duplicate, 5 % day-to-day (p2).
- Analysis: biexponential fit of each child's curve by MLAB, weighted 1/C²; r² ≥ 0.99. t½α = 0.693/α,
  t½β = 0.693/β, CL = Dose/AUC, Vdss from Dose, A/α² + B/β² and A/α + B/β (the printed denominator shows no square), K21 = (Aβ + Bα)/(A + B), Ke = αβ/K21
  (p2-3).
- Acetylator phenotype not determined.

Fig. 1 (p3), the four children given 5.8-6 mg/kg, is labelled Cp = 8.2 e^(−9.5t) + 2.1 e^(−0.4t) (µg/mL, t in
hours by the axis). Its α of 9.5 /h is a distribution half-life of 4.4 min and β 0.4 /h a half-life of 1.7 h
(derived: 0.693/rate) — the curve's α is faster than the 10.3 min mean of the individual fits, which is not
explained (a fit to pooled data vs a mean of individual fits).

Comparators the authors cite (p4): adult Vdss 1.93 ± 0.32 L/kg (Graffner, ref 9) and 1.2 ± 0.3 L/kg in young
adults (Gibson, ref 10); adult t½β 2.5-4.7 h, shorter in rapid acetylators; adult CL 9.3 ± 0.3 (Graffner) and
8.32 ± 1.09 mL/min/kg (Gibson); a neonate exposed in utero had t½β 13.5 h (ref 15). Each "higher"/"shorter" in the
paper is against these other studies' adults, not a parallel adult group.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, effect-site model, EC50 or therapeutic range is reported; the authors only argue that the short
  half-life makes continuous infusion necessary to keep concentrations therapeutic (p4-5).
- Patient 6 (28 mg/kg): hypotension (lowest systolic 80 mm Hg) and complete heart block (ventricular rate
  70-80 /min) resolving in about 15 min (p2); serum procainamide barely fell during the first hour (~20 µg/mL,
  Fig. 2, p4), then declined first-order with t½ 2.5 h once below 10 µg/mL.
- NAPA appeared in the first samples, peaked at 1-2 h, stayed below procainamide until ~4 h, then exceeded it.
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. The paper contrasts its children with published adults (above) and with a neonate.

## Caveats
- n=5 (patient 5's 3.9 mg/kg curve is in the means but not in Fig. 1), all boys, all 7-12 years, weights not given.
- Acetylator phenotype unknown; the authors say rapid clearance may reflect a chance selection of fast
  acetylators (p4).
- Sampling times and individual parameters are not tabulated; the dose salt is not stated.
- Fig. 1's pooled α (4.4 min) differs from the mean individual t½α (10.3 min).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/drugs_procainamide.R:86-91** — "a half-life of about 10 minutes in children, Singh 1982". Paper: distribution
  half-life 10.3 ± 3.4 min (mean ± SEM, n=5, 7-12 y; Results p3, abstract p1). **Matches**, in minutes as stated; it
  is the α-phase half-life of a biexponential fit, not a ke0. The header's practical conclusion (give the loading
  dose over 25-30 min) rests on the label, not on this paper. The short citation is confirmed as this paper.
- **Independent check against the library's model (Grasela 1984), which does not cite Singh for it:** for a
  10-year-old boy with a blank creatinine the model takes the adult-equivalent creatinine 0.9 mg/dL
  (`R/renalFunction.R:60, 173-178`), so FR = 130/(133 × 0.9) = 1.09 and total clearance = 14.4 × 1.09 + 10.1 + 1.2
  = 26.9 L/h per 70 kg of pharmacokinetic weight, about 6.4 mL/min/kg of base (derived;
  `R/drugs_procainamide.R:183-205`, fat-free-mass switch off), with V 1.94 L/kg and t½ about 3.5 h (derived).
  Singh's children: CL 19.4 mL/min/kg (about 3x higher), Vdss 2.2 L/kg (similar), t½β 1.7 h (about half). So the
  library would overpredict a school-age child's concentrations after the first hour, if Singh's five boys are
  representative; the model's header does not mention paediatric clearance. Recorded as a `[decision]` question,
  with the dose-salt ambiguity (factor 0.866) as a `[source]` question. No code location claims anything about
  children's clearance, so this is not a discrepancy in what the repo says.
