# Meredith 1984: diphenhydramine disposition in chronic liver disease

## Study
Nine men with alcohol-related cirrhosis (age 32-61 y, mean 49.1 +/- 3.0 SE) and eight healthy age-matched men (30-53 y, mean 43.4 +/- 3.1). Single IV dose, 0.8 mg/kg diphenhydramine (Benadryl, Parke-Davis) into an antecubital vein; mean dose 54.8 +/- 2.8 mg (patients) and 57.1 +/- 3.4 mg (controls); the first patient was capped at 50 mg. Heparinised venous blood at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12, 24, 32, 48 h; urine for 48 h. Plasma assayed by gas-liquid chromatography (Bilzer and Gundert-Remy method); protein binding by equilibrium dialysis with 3H-diphenhydramine. SAAM-23 fits of a biexponential. Weights are not given (derived: mean dose 57.1 mg at 0.8 mg/kg implies about 71 kg in controls). Biexponential analysis was possible only in six controls and five patients (the alpha phase was erratic 2-5 h in the others); the beta phase (6-48 h) was fitted in all.

Cirrhotic patients (for contrast): beta 0.049 /h, t1/2 beta 15.2 h, CL 558.7 mL/min (7.8 mL/min/kg), CL_f 25.9 mL/min, k10 0.090 /h, Vdss 8.5 L/kg, V1 4.8 L/kg (Table II; none of CL, Vdss or V1 differs significantly; beta, t1/2 beta, k10 and CL_f do). alpha 0.4037 +/- 0.131 /h. Extraction ratio about 0.5 in controls (hepatic blood flow assumed 1.5 L/min).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, EC50 or MEAC is reported, and no concentration-effect analysis. The only effect data are clinical: every subject was sedated 1-2 min after injection, most slept 1-2 h, easily roused, followed by 15-45 min of drowsiness; no difference in sleep duration between groups, and no apparent greater cerebral sensitivity in cirrhotics (printed p. 476). Not an opioid; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
In cirrhotics, t1/2 beta correlated with serum bilirubin (r = 0.723, P = 0.028); no correlation with albumin or prothrombin time ratio, and free fraction did not correlate with Vd beta (r = 0.259). No weight, age or sex covariate is fitted (all subjects male).

## Caveats
First sample at 15 min, venous, so any faster distribution is unseen and V1 is a back-extrapolation. Alpha phase fitted in only six controls; two controls and four patients excluded as erratic (authors suspect posture). Small n, men only, the two groups not matched for weight (not reported). Cirrhotics had ascites, which the authors suggest may enlarge the central volume. Assay specificity versus the N-demethyl metabolite was not addressed (compare Blyden 1986, which found the two co-elute on standard GC columns).
