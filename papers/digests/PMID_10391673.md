# Gupta 1999: oxybutynin XL vs IR dose-efficacy and dose-dry mouth (mixed-effects)

## Study
- Two parallel-group randomized trials of oral oxybutynin chloride, extended-release osmotic XL (once daily, morning) vs immediate-release (IR, doses split, 3 times daily at 15 mg).
- Study 1 (Table I): double-blind, placebo- and active-controlled, forced escalation 5 mg/day x 2 wk, 10 x 2 wk, 15 x 2 wk after a single-blind placebo week; XL n=34, IR n=32, placebo n=16; women 40-85 y with urge urinary incontinence (age mean about 57-60 y). Weekly urge incontinence episodes (7-day diaries) and dry mouth (4-point scale: 1 none, 2 mild, 3 moderate, 4 severe) at every dose.
- Study 2: double-blind, individually titrated in 5 mg steps (every 4-7 days) to minimum effective, maximum tolerated or maximum allowed dose (XL up to 30 mg/day, IR up to 20 mg/day as 5 mg doses); XL n=53, IR n=52; men and women. Incontinence recorded only at baseline and final dose, so only dry mouth was modelled.
- Analysis: NONMEM IV, FOCE; -2LL (objective function) drop >6.6 = P<.01 for one parameter. No drug concentrations measured or modelled.

## Model and parameters
- Efficacy (study 1): weekly urge episodes ~ Poisson(lambda); log(lambda) = Int + fd + fp (eq 2). Int = alpha_i + eta1; fd = -(gamma_i + eta2) x Dose (dose in mg/day); fp = log[(1-A) exp(-(beta_i+eta3) Time) + A], A = logistic(phi + eta4) (placebo/time effect plateaus; dose effect assumed additive on the log scale and confounded with time).
- Dry mouth: proportional-odds model, logit P(Y<=m) = f_m + eta, f_m = Int + fd + fp, fd = nu_i x Dose; time term kappa not significant in either study.
- Reported in running text (p.678-681): model B, dose-effect slope gamma = 0.178 (SE 0.018) per mg/day for XL vs 0.126 (SE 0.014) for IR (objective function 5358 vs 5363 for common slope 0.144, SE 0.012; a 5-unit drop for 1 parameter, "borderline"); the authors call XL's slope about 40% steeper (derived check: 0.178/0.126 = 1.41).
- Study 2 dry mouth (Table IV, journal p.680): mu11 3.08 (SE 0.52), mu21 4.1 (0.39), mu31 4.57 (0.80), nu XL -0.223 (0.036), nu IR -0.357 (0.058), eta 7.98 (2.17). The nu comparison was significant (steeper for IR).
- Study 1 dry mouth (Table III): a single slope for XL and IR (value garbled in the text layer; see open questions); no time effect.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: weekly urge incontinence count (efficacy) and 4-category dry mouth score (side effect), both versus daily DOSE, not concentration.
- No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, EC50, Emax, MEAC or analgesic concentration; no equianalgesic ratio (not an opioid). Dose-based comparison only: XL slope vs IR slope as above; ED50/TD50 therapeutic index shown graphically (Figure 6), no numeric value in text.
- Authors attribute the lower dry-mouth probability of XL to lower peaks and slower absorption (no concentration data shown).

## Covariates
None modelled beyond treatment, dose and time (placebo effect); individual random effects on intercept, dose slope, time slope and plateau.

## Caveats
- Dose and time (placebo) effects confounded in study 1; additivity assumed. Study 2 dose titration causes informative censoring. Efficacy difference XL vs IR did not reach significance. Poisson independence is approximate (authors).
