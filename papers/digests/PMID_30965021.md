# Bergh 2019: carfentanil and norcarfentanil in rats, with nonlinear accumulation

The only in-vivo pharmacokinetic study of carfentanil in **rats**, and the only one that measures a
dose-dependence of its kinetics. It is NOT the whole of the in-vivo literature, which is what
`R/drugs_carfentanil.R:23` says and what this card said until CodeRabbit challenged it on PR #888
(2026-11-25); see *Verification against stanpumpR*, where the verdict is now a discrepancy, and the
two papers it turns on are recorded in `papers/pending-work.csv`.

**Why this card was written by hand.** `digest_run.py` records this paper as refused by the model's
safeguards even when batched alone, and the run log notes it took a batch-mate down with it on
2026-11-09. The PDF, its extracted text and its context file were all filed normally — only the
card was missing. See *Caveats*.

## Study
Male Sprague-Dawley rats with indwelling jugular catheters and implanted temperature transponders,
given a single **subcutaneous** dose of carfentanil 1, 3 or 10 ug/kg, or saline (PDF p. 4). Blood
was drawn before dosing and at 15, 30, 60, 120, 240 and 480 min; core temperature and catalepsy
were scored at the same times. N = 6, 6 and 5 for the three doses (Table 4); figure legends give
N = 5-6 per group.

Assay: liquid-liquid extraction of plasma, then UHPLC-MS/MS for carfentanil and its metabolite
norcarfentanil, validated to SWGTOX guidelines. Limits of detection and quantification 7.5 and
15 pg/mL for both analytes (abstract, p. 1). Calibrators 10-5000 pg/mL, QC samples 7.5-4000 pg/mL
(p. 2). Stability data are in Table 3 (PDF p. 6).

Pharmacokinetic constants were computed per animal from the time-concentration profiles and
compared between dose groups; the paper does not fit a compartmental model.

**The nonlinearity, which is the paper's finding.** Multiplying the observed 1 ug/kg AUC by 3 and
by 10 predicts 87 and 290 min*ng/mL; the observed values are 148 and 696. So a three-fold dose
gives 5.1 times the exposure and a ten-fold dose 24 times. Two-way ANOVA on observed against
predicted: carfentanil F[1,28] = 8.04, p < 0.01; norcarfentanil F[1,28] = 16.30, p < 0.001, in both
cases with the observed exceeding the predicted at 10 ug/kg (p. 6, Fig. 5). Half-life rises with
dose over the same range, and Ke falls. The authors attribute this to impaired clearance at the
high dose and note a parallel rise in plasma creatinine at 3 and 10 ug/kg (Table 5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Two end points, both measured in the same animals as the concentrations: **core temperature** and a
**catalepsy score** built from three scored behaviours. Carfentanil produced dose-related
hypothermia and catalepsy lasting up to 8 h (Fig. 3, PDF p. 6). No EC50, no effect-site model and
no ke0 is fitted; the relation is reported as correlation only. Carfentanil AUC against mean body
temperature gives Pearson r = -0.751, p < 0.001, and against summed catalepsy score r = 0.784,
p < 0.001 (Fig. 6, PDF p. 7). No potency ratio to any other opioid is measured here.

## Covariates
None. One species, one sex (male), one route, one strain; no body-size, age or renal covariate is
fitted. Plasma creatinine is measured as a marker of renal function (Table 5) but is not used as a
covariate on any parameter.

## Caveats
- **Rats, not people** -- but a human report does exist (Uddayasankar 2018, PMID 29679387), so this
  paper does not carry the whole weight of that claim in the library, as this card first said.
- **Subcutaneous, so nothing absolute.** With no intravenous arm, bioavailability is unknown; the
  paper reports no volume of distribution and no clearance, and neither can be recovered from what
  is printed. See *open_questions*.
- **The metabolite's elimination: what the paper does and does not claim.** It DOES claim impaired
  norcarfentanil clearance at 10 ug/kg, on the same basis as for the parent -- observed AUC above
  the value predicted from the 1 ug/kg dose (F[1,28] = 16.30, p < 0.001, Fig. 5) -- so it is wrong
  to say, as this card first did, that it makes no claim about the metabolite's elimination
  (CodeRabbit, PR #888, 2026-11-25). What it does NOT claim is a dose-dependent norcarfentanil
  HALF-LIFE; that claim is the parent's. The half-life uncertainty below is why.
- **Small groups and wide uncertainty.** N = 5-6 per dose. Norcarfentanil's half-life at 3 ug/kg is
  350 +/- 215 min and at 10 ug/kg 537 +/- 200 min — SEMs of that size will not support a
  dose-response claim about the metabolite's HALF-LIFE, and the paper makes none (its metabolite
  claim is the AUC one above).
- **Male rats only**, so nothing can be said about sex.
- **AUC is truncated at 8 h** (AUC0-8h, not AUC-infinity), and with half-lives of 35-64 min for the
  parent and 176-537 min for the metabolite, the truncation bites far harder on norcarfentanil.
- **Table 4's text layer is scrambled.** Anyone re-reading this paper from the extracted text will
  get the 10 ug/kg Cmax on the 1 ug/kg row and the half-lives out of order. Render PDF p. 7.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **`R/drugs_carfentanil.R:23`, `the only in-vivo carfentanil pharmacokinetics are in rats (Bergh 2019)`, and the same header's `Every human study that measures carfentanil uses [11C]carfentanil
  as a PET RADIOTRACER`.** This paper IS in rats and its own introduction does say the human
  literature is radiotracer work, so the card first recorded both as matching. **Both claims are
  wrong, and this is the card's discrepancy** (CodeRabbit on PR #888, 2026-11-25; its lead was the
  goat and rabbit data, and the human paper beside it is the larger miss). Two papers contradict
  them, neither in the archive:
  - **Uddayasankar 2018** (PMID 29679387, Pharmacotherapy 38:e41-e45, doi 10.1002/phar.2117) is a
    HUMAN pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic case report after recreational exposure: three timed
    blood samples modelled, an elimination half-life of 5.7 h for carfentanil and 11.8 h for
    norcarfentanil, and awakening with spontaneous breathing at an interpolated 0.52 ng/mL. Its
    abstract calls itself the first such report. So human carfentanil kinetics have been measured,
    and a pharmacokinetic model WAS fitted to them. The header's claim is unqualified -- it says
    there is no published human pharmacokinetic model at all -- so that claim does not survive
    either. Only the narrower statement, that no published human POPULATION model exists, does, and
    the header does not make it.
  - **Mutlow 2004** (PMID 15732589, J Zoo Wildl Med 35:489-96, doi 10.1638/03-074) gives
    carfentanil pharmacokinetics in eight domestic goats, serial blood to 120 h by LC-MS, with rapid
    absorption and a biphasic decline over 12-48 h. **Read its abstract past the first sentence**:
    the opening clause names three routes, but every goat received carfentanil 40 ug/kg
    INTRAMUSCULARLY, and the intravenous, intramuscular and subcutaneous crossover was of
    NALTREXONE, the reversal drug -- which is why the abstract's route comparisons are of time to
    standing after intravenous against subcutaneous naltrexone, and why it reports that
    carfentanil's half-life did not differ between goats given naltrexone by different routes.
    So this paper supplies NO intravenous carfentanil arm either, and no absolute volume or
    clearance can be had from it. It still refutes the header's claim that the only in-vivo
    carfentanil kinetics are in rats, which is all it is cited for here (CodeRabbit, PR #888,
    2026-11-25, correcting this card's first reading of that sentence).
  The 5.7 h human half-life also runs against the model's basis: sufentanil's terminal half-life is
  of the order of 2.5-3.5 h, so the one human measurement there is suggests carfentanil is SLOWER
  than the congener whose disposition the model borrows -- the direction
  `inst/app-help/drugs/carfentanil.md` already warns about. Recorded, not acted on: see the
  `[decision]` above and the two rows added to `papers/pending-work.csv`.
- **`the area under the curve rises faster than the dose`.** Table 4: AUC0-8h is 29, 148 and 696
  min*ng/mL at 1, 3 and 10 ug/kg. Proportional scaling from the 1 ug/kg value predicts 87 and 290.
  The paper tests this and finds the difference significant (F[1,28] = 8.04, p < 0.01).
  **Matches.**
- **`the half-life lengthens as the dose rises`.** Table 4: 35.4, 55.1 and 64.4 min across the
  three doses, with the 10 ug/kg value significantly above the 1 ug/kg one. **Matches.**
- **`inst/app-help/drugs/carfentanil.md`**: repeats the non-linearity in the same terms
  (**matches**), but its opening paragraph also repeats BOTH contradicted claims, in the header's
  words -- that every human study uses [11C]carfentanil as a PET radiotracer, and that the only
  in-vivo carfentanil pharmacokinetics are in rats. **Discrepancy:** the page needs the same
  correction as `R/drugs_carfentanil.R:23`. This card first said the page did not repeat the claim,
  from reading only its later *Where to be careful* section (CodeRabbit, PR #888, 2026-11-25).
- **`inst/extdata/illicitRouteRegistry.csv:26-27`**: the registry rows cite this paper as the
  source for carfentanil's routes. The paper studied the **subcutaneous** route only, which is
  consistent with the library offering carfentanil intravenously on sufentanil's absolute
  disposition rather than on anything measured here. **Matches**, with the note that this paper
  supports no route's input model.
- **No parameter of the library's carfentanil model comes from this paper**, so there is no number
  to reconcile. The model's own header says as much, and says the disposition is sufentanil's on
  Shafer's judgement. **Consistent.**
- Nothing in the paper contradicts a value stanpumpR holds.
