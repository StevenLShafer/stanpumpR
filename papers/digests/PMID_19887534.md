# Perl 2009: propofol in breath by MCC-IMS vs serum GC-MS

## Study
13 ASA I-II ENT-surgery patients (9 M, 4 F; mean age 45, range 18-69 yr; BMI 24.6 SD 6.2). Induction propofol 2.1 (SD 0.7) mg/kg, remifentanil 0.5 ug/kg/min, rocuronium 0.5 mg/kg. Maintenance propofol 3.9 (SD 1.8) mg/kg/h plus remifentanil, no fixed protocol. 24 paired samples (1-2 per patient), all taken at steady state after at least 15 min of constant infusion. Exhaled propofol was measured by multicapillary column ion mobility spectrometry (MCC-IMS, 63Ni source) on a CO2-triggered end-tidal sample (8 mL loop). Venous serum propofol (arm not used for the infusion) was measured by GC-MS. The IMS was calibrated with humidified gas at 1-10 ppb (r2 0.984, LOD 700 ppt, LOQ 1.5 ppb). The GC-MS linear range was 2-10 ug/mL (r2 0.93, intraday precision 12.2%).

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Serum propofol ranged 0.3-4.3 ug/mL; exhaled propofol 5-15.6 ppb. Breath vs serum r2 = 0.73 (Fig 3, PDF p4). Both signals were normalised to their own maximum (= 100%) before Bland-Altman analysis, so no absolute ppb-per-ug/mL conversion is given. Bias -10.5%, SD 12.3%, limits of agreement -35.1% to +14.1% (Fig 4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio. Dynamics are mentioned only from other work (Takita 2007): about 41 s from bolus to first detection of breath propofol, peak at 333 s (PDF p5).

## Covariates
None analysed. The authors note species and inter-individual slope differences (Grossherr) and lower breath levels with V/Q shunt, as discussion points from other studies.

## Caveats
Steady state only, so it says nothing about the lag between plasma and breath during changing infusions. Venous, not arterial, samples. Normalised data hide absolute calibration. n = 13. Sampling was 20 s of multiple breaths. The authors say none of the published methods is ready for routine use.
