# Rolston 1984: antimicrobial tolerance in group C and G streptococci

## Study
- 61 clinical isolates (35 group G, 26 group C streptococci) from blood, urine, wounds, oropharynx and synovial
  fluid, Hahnemann University Hospital, July 1981 - March 1983; grouped by Phadebact co-agglutination.
- Microtitre broth dilution in Todd-Hewitt broth, inoculum 2.5 x 10^5 (per mL), 18-24 h. MBC: 0.01 mL subcultured
  onto Mueller-Hinton agar with 15% sheep blood for 24 h; the method detects 99.9% kill (PDF p2, printed p390).
- Tolerance defined as an MBC/MIC ratio of 32 or more (the paper writes it as "MIC/MBC ratio", PDF p2; the data in
  Table II show MBC/MIC is meant).
- Drugs: penicillin G, piperacillin, azlocillin, cefotaxime, cefmenoxime, latamoxef, cephalothin, vancomycin;
  combinations with gentamicin or rifampicin by checkerboard. **Cefazolin was not tested.**

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.
- Tolerance (Table II, PDF p3): 6 of 61 isolates tolerant, each to one drug only: group C, 1 to penicillin
  (MIC 0.03 / MBC 32) and 1 to cefotaxime (0.03 / 8.0); group G, 1 each to penicillin (0.06 / 32), cephalothin
  (0.03 / 2.0), latamoxef (0.03 / 8.0) and vancomycin (0.06 / 4.0). Abstract rates: group G 11.4% (4/35),
  group C 7.7% (2/26).
- Adding gentamicin to the beta-lactams, or gentamicin or rifampicin to vancomycin, made the combination
  bactericidal against the tolerant isolates (Table III; e.g. cephalothin + gentamicin MBC 0.03). No antagonism.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Cefazolin itself not tested; cephalothin is a first-generation class analogue.
- Groups C and G pooled in Table I; no MBC percentiles.
- Lowest dilution tested was 0.03 mg/L, so many MIC50s sit at the floor.
- Table II's cephalothin-tolerant isolate (MBC/MIC ≈ 67, derived) and the abstract's "MIC to MBC ratio" wording
  invert the usual ratio; the meaning is clear from the data.
