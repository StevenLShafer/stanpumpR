# Macpherson 2016: rosuvastatin population PK in children and adolescents with HeFH

## Study
- Two pediatric studies pooled: CHARON (phase 3, open label, n=196 in the PK set, age 6 to <18 y, 5 mg start, titrated to 10 mg max if 6 to <10 y or 20 mg max if 10 to <18 y) and Study 4522IL/0086 (n=18, age 10 to <18 y, single doses 10, 40, 80 mg, six per dose, plus 80 mg daily x 7 d in the 80 mg group). Patients had heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH). Total 214 patients, 2,029 concentrations (1,735 + 294). (PDF p.2-4)
- Table 1 (PDF p.4): CHARON median weight 42.0 kg (20.0-111), median age 11 y (6-17), 44% male; 4522IL/0086 median weight 63.2 kg (32-116), age 14 y (10-17), 78% male. Race 89% Caucasian overall.
- Sampling: CHARON pilot (12 patients, single 10 mg) intensive 24 h; otherwise sparse pre-dose samples at month 1 then every 3 months for 2 y (at least 90% of data; mostly trough at steady state). 4522IL/0086 intensive to 96 h after single dose, 24 h at day 7.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS, plasma. CHARON range 0.02-20 ng/mL (liquid extraction); 4522IL/0086 0.1-30 ng/mL (solid-phase extraction). Total rosuvastatin (no free-fraction measurement stated).
- NONMEM 7.2, FOCE, log-transformed data; covariate selection forward alpha 0.01, backward 0.001; VPC with 500 bootstraps.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, first-order absorption and elimination, apparent (oral) parameters. One-compartment fit was 320 OFV points worse. Dose linearity held; a 16% rise in CL/F over the 2-y study was found (not in the final model). Allometric scaling with fixed exponents 0.75 (CL) and 1 (V) raised the OFV by 44 and was abandoned; the CL/F exponent was estimated instead. Parameters from Table 2 (PDF p.5, printed p.23), read on the page image. Time in hours, volumes in L (not per kg), clearances in L/h.

Final equation (PDF p.6, printed 24): males CL/F = 129 x (WT/42)^0.352 x 1.41; females CL/F = 129 x (WT/42)^0.352. Examples the paper gives: male 20 kg 140 L/h, male 99 kg 246 L/h, female 20 kg 99 L/h, female 111 kg 182 L/h (derived check: the first agrees with the equation).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamics. LDL-C response is not modelled. No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, MEAC, EC50 or potency ratio. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
- Weight (power, exponent 0.352, reference 42 kg) and sex (male 1.41x) on CL/F. Weight alone dropped OFV by 25.3; sex by a further 27.2. IIV in CL/F fell 44.7% to 40.0%.
- Age not significant once weight is in (OFV -3.1, slope 0.043, RSE 108%); the youngest (<10 y) had about 15% lower CL/F in a descriptive view (about 18% against 14-18 y in the Discussion).
- Race not tested (89% Caucasian). Dose and time on CL/F: dose-independent, 16% rise over 2 y.
- Informal comparison with adults (from Tzeng 2008, via Fig. 3a): median CL/F 302 L/h in healthy adult volunteers, 78.8 L/h in adult dyslipidemic patients; the children lie between. Predicted pediatric dose-normalized AUCss is similar or lower than adult patients (3.8-fold lower at 6 to <10 y on 10 mg, 2.3-fold lower at 10 to <18 y on 20 mg). (PDF p.6)

## Caveats
- Sparse trough data dominate, so Vc/F, Q/F and Vp/F are poorly informed (shrinkage 55-60%, IIV 65-105%); only CL/F was judged robust.
- Allometric scaling with fixed exponents showed prediction bias; the authors suspect the narrow weight range, sparse data or non-allometric kinetics.
- Intro statement: adult CL/F in dyslipidemic patients is lower than in healthy volunteers by an unexplained mechanism (Tzeng 2008). The pediatric patients are HeFH, so the disease population is not matched to either adult group.
- Sex effect is not seen in adults (Martin 2002, cited by the authors).
- Table 2 footnote spells "Ka" as "association constant"; it is the absorption rate constant (/h).
