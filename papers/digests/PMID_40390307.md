# Rascher 2025: empagliflozin PK and HbA1c PK/PD in children 10-17 y with T2DM

## Study
Re-estimation, in a Bayesian framework (NONMEM 7.5, NUTS, 4 chains), of adult/adolescent popPK and PK/PD models for empagliflozin using the phase 3 DINAMO trial (NCT03429543; p2391-2392). Patients aged 10-17 y with type 2 diabetes; 26-week double-blind, empagliflozin 10 mg once daily with optional increase to 25 mg at Week 14 (placebo arm re-randomised at Week 26). PK data: sparse steady-state trough and 1.5 h post-dose samples at Week 26 and 52. PK set: 74 patients, 278 observations, 223 quantifiable, 55 BLQ (19.8%); 46 on 10 mg, 16 on 25 mg, 12 on 10 then 25 mg (Table 1, p2393). PK/PD set: 103 patients (52 empagliflozin, 51 placebo), 394 HbA1c observations (Table 2, p2394). 49.4% on background insulin, 90.3% on metformin. Pooled-empagliflozin-group mean weight 98.66 +/- 24.35 kg and mean eGFR 130.09 +/- 26.78 mL/min/1.73 m2 (discussion, p2397-2398). Adult comparator: EMPA-REG OUTCOME 10 mg group, weight 85.9 kg, eGFR 74.3. Concentrations are in nmol/L (residual error SD 353 nmol/L; AUC50 in nmol*h/L); the assay is not described in this PDF.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment model, sequential zero-order then first-order absorption, linear elimination. Fixed allometric scaling by body weight: exponent 0.75 on CL/F and Q/F, 1 on V2/F and V3/F (p2392). IIV on CL/F only (exponential). Combined proportional plus two additive error terms (non-outliers and outliers). Weakly informative priors on CL and V, informative priors from the adult fit on the other parameters (p2397). Age effect on CL/F removed relative to the previous model; IIV on V3/F removed (p2397).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: HbA1c (%) over 26 weeks (not a concentration end point; no ke0, no MEAC; not an opioid). Indirect-response (turnover) model: dHbA1c/dt = kin*(1-INH) - kout*HbA1c; kin rises with time at a zero-order rate PROG (disease progression, estimable in patients on insulin); INH = IMAX*AUCss/(AUC50 + AUCss), AUCss = dose / (CL/F) per individual (p2392). Table 4 (PDF p6, printed p2395; image-confirmed), median (95% CDI): kout 0.0489 /day (0.0346-0.0717); BASE 7.35% (6.99-7.72); PROG 5.64e-07 (2.94e-07-9.20e-07), printed unit "%/h/h"; IMAX 10.1% (7.39-12.9), printed unit %, though the formula exp(theta4)/(1+exp(theta4)) is a fraction; AUC50 703 nmol*h/L, fixed to the adult-model value; insulin effect on BASE 1.15 (1.07-1.24); eGFR effect on IMAX 1.03 (0.917-1.15); baseline-HbA1c effect on IMAX 2.04 (1.86-2.21); IIV BASE 16.1% CV, IIV PROG 7.35% CV; residual 6.32% (additive on log scale). Simulated placebo-adjusted HbA1c change at Week 26, 10 mg: -0.699% paediatric vs -0.528% adult (abstract); the paper attributes the larger paediatric effect mainly to higher eGFR. Simulated AUCss is similar in paediatric patients and adults (Figure 3).

## Covariates
CL/F: body weight (fixed allometry), eGFR (0.407), race Black (0.897) and Asian (0.934), sex female (1.19, CDI includes 1); age removed (p2397). The paper says paediatric females had higher CL/F than males, with a CDI including the null (p2394-2395); the comparator is paediatric males in the same model. Black/Asian/eGFR effects are informed by priors from the adult fit.

## Caveats
Sparse sampling (trough and 1.5 h), so no distribution-phase information; narrow eGFR range; adult priors needed (p2400). Vc is wide (CDI 1.30-6.63). Cmin,ss variability slightly over-predicted, attributed to diary-based dosing imputations. Reference weight for the typical values not given in the main text. 19.8% BLQ samples.
