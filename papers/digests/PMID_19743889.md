# Ververs 2009: paroxetine concentrations through pregnancy by CYP2D6 genotype

## Study
- Observational cohort (OAZE study, ISRCTN25383361), 12 Dutch centres, recruitment July 2003 to July 2007 (p. 678).
  There were 74 pregnant women taking paroxetine for depression or anxiety from at least early pregnancy. Age
  32.5 ± 4.9 y, range 23-45 (Table I and Results, p. 679-680); the abstract says 25 to 45. Weight 76.3 ± 18.3 kg at
  16-20 wk, 80.4 ± 17.5 at 27-31 wk and 85.4 ± 19.5 at 36-40 wk. Caucasian 95.9%; 23% smoked.
- Daily dose 20.4 ± 9.4 mg (range 5-60) at 16-20 wk, 20.3 ± 9.5 at 27-31 wk and 19.9 ± 8.8 (3-40) at 36-40 wk
  (Table I). Dosing was decided by the women's own clinicians.
- Venous samples at routine visits at 16-20, 27-31 and 36-40 weeks; 190 concentrations in total (p. 680). The time
  since the dose was **not standardised**: it depended on evening or morning dosing, so the samples are not troughs.
  Plasma stored at -20 °C. There was no postpartum or pre-pregnancy sample.
- Assay: total plasma paroxetine by HPLC-UV (LLOQ 5 µg/L on 500 µL) or LC-MS/MS (LLOQ 1 µg/L on 100 µL), CV below
  15%. Values below LLOQ were set to 0.5 µg/L.
- Genotypes (Table I): 43 EM, 1 UM, 25 IM, 5 PM, assigned from CYP2D6 *3, *4, *5, *6, *9, *10 and *41 and gene
  duplication. The paper's heterozygous decreased-activity rule put wt/*9, wt/*10 and wt/*41 (13 women) among the EMs.
- Analysis: linear mixed-effects model (R 2.6.2, nlme) of concentration on gestational week, daily dose and genotype
  (EM vs IM/PM), with genotype interactions. Weight, smoking, co-medication and age were not significant.

- Net IM/PM slope = -0.33 + 0.82 = **+0.49 µg/L per week** (derived from Table II). The paper gives this slope three
  different ways. The abstract states an "increase of 0.82 µg/L" per week, which is the interaction term alone. The
  Results (p. 680) write "0.87 - 0.3 = 0.57 µg/L per week", which matches neither Table II nor the abstract. Table II
  itself gives +0.49 (derived). The direction is the same in all three: rising in IM/PM.
- Worked examples (p. 680-681), at 30 mg/day and 38 weeks: EM 23.7 µg/L; IM 70.4 µg/L, about 3-fold.
- Fit: 79.5% of predictions were within ±10 µg/L of observed and 96.8% within ±20 µg/L. The dose-concentration
  relationship was not significantly nonlinear over 10-40 mg.
- Fig. 3 (dose-normalised, complete samplers: 32 EM, 13 IM, 2 PM): first vs last period P < 0.01 (EM) and 0.05 (PM);
  first vs second period P = 0.04 (IM). Box plots only, with no numerical ratios.
- (Derived) EM at 20 mg/day: 1.69 - 0.33 x 16 + 1.15 x 20 = 19.4 µg/L at week 16 and 12.2 at week 38, a fall of
  about 37% across the second and third trimesters. This is within-pregnancy only: there is **no non-pregnant
  baseline**.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD model, ke0, therapeutic range or concentration-effect relation. EPDS (depression score) rose 0.08 points/week
(95% CI 0.001-0.15, P = 0.05; intercept 4.8). IM/PM scored 2.8 points higher than EM (95% CI 0.5-5.0; P = 0.02).
EPDS above 12 in 15%, 21% and 29% across the three periods. The authors write in the abstract that symptoms rose in
EM/UM and not in IM/PM.

## Covariates
Gestational week, daily dose and CYP2D6 group (EM+UM vs IM+PM), with interactions (Eq. 1). Bodyweight, smoking,
co-medication (2 CYP2D6 substrates: haloperidol, mirtazapine) and maternal age were not retained.

## Caveats
- Sampling time after the dose varied between women, so these are random-time, not trough, concentrations. There is
  no postpartum or non-pregnant comparator, so the "decrease" is relative to week 16 of pregnancy.
- IM and PM are pooled (only 5 PMs). EM includes heterozygotes for decreased-activity alleles.
- Three different IM/PM slopes appear in the paper (0.82 abstract; 0.57 Results; +0.49 derived from Table II).
- No protein binding or haemodilution data (authors' limitation).
- The authors' hypothesis, competition by an endogenous substrate in IM/PM, is not tested.
- Industry support in part (Pfizer, Eli Lilly).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- R/drugs_paroxetine.R:145-151 and paroxetine.md:21: these say concentrations fall in EM/UM and rise in IM/PM,
  with 74 pregnancies genotyped. **matches** (abstract; Table II; Fig. 1). The factor itself,
  PAROXETINE_PREGNANCY_CL_FACTOR = 2.0 (R/drugs_paroxetine.R:186), comes from Westin 2017, not from this paper, so
  Ververs is used only qualitatively. That is appropriate, because Ververs has no non-pregnant baseline to give a
  ratio.
- R/drugs_paroxetine.R:172-174: the citation (journal, volume, pages, DOI) **matches**.
- pregnancyRegistry.csv:71: "fell 0.3 ug/L per gestational week" **matches** the abstract (Table II: -0.33).
  "ROSE 0.82 ug/L per week" **matches** the abstract's wording, but per Table II 0.82 is the interaction term, and the
  net IM/PM slope is +0.49 µg/L/week (derived; Results p. 680 says 0.57). The difference is minor and does not affect
  the model, which uses no Ververs number. The registry's "~30% by week 34" is not stated in the paper. It is
  reproducible as a derived value: 20 mg/day EM, week 16 to 34, 19.4 to 13.5 µg/L, -31%.
- Units: the paper's µg/L equals the library's ng/mL, and the assay is total plasma paroxetine, matching "total serum
  paroxetine" in the code.
- Independent comparison with the library's Kim 2015 model (derived, approximate). At age 32 the model's
  CL25 = 13.1 x (32/71)^-0.702 = 22.9 L/h, before size scaling. On 20 mg once daily, the steady-state mean is
  20 x 0.8^0.363 / (24 x 22.9) x 1000 = about 34 ng/mL non-pregnant, and about 17 ng/mL with the pregnancy factor 2.0.
  Ververs' EM model predicts 19.4 µg/L at week 16 and 12.2 at week 38 on 20 mg/day, at random sampling times. The
  library's pregnant value lies within this range, slightly above the late-pregnancy EM prediction. For IM/PM,
  Ververs' predictions are much higher (derived, 20 mg at week 38: 1.69 - 32.1 + (1.15 + 1.59) x 20 + (-0.33 + 0.82)
  x 38 = 43.0 µg/L). The header already warns that the library is wrong in direction for these women.
