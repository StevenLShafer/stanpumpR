# Lichtenbelt 2010: Propofol reduces the distribution and clearance of midazolam

## Study
- 8 healthy men, age 25.5 +/- 5.8 y, weight 85.0 +/- 8.2 kg, height 188 +/- 5 cm (mean +/- SD, p1600). Within 30% of ideal weight. Randomised crossover, two sessions at least 2 weeks apart.
- Session A (control): midazolam 0.035-0.05 mg/kg IV over 1 min, then 0.035-0.05 mg/kg/h for 59 min. Session B: same midazolam plus a Diprifusor TCI of propofol at a constant target of 0.6 or 1.0 mcg/mL, begun 15 min before midazolam and run 6 h after it ended (7 h total). Dose/target order randomised.
- Arterial samples: midazolam 1-60 min during infusion and to 360 min after it; propofol hourly in session B. Midazolam in heparinised plasma, HPLC-UV 216 nm (CV 2.2% intra, 2.0% inter; 9.7-1120 ng/mL), so TOTAL plasma midazolam. Propofol in WHOLE BLOOD, HPLC-fluorescence 276 nm (CV 4.3%/3.7%; 0.06-6.8 mcg/mL). Measured mean blood propofol 0.62 and 1.2 mcg/mL for the 0.6 and 1.0 targets.
- 368 midazolam concentrations analysed (470 samples collected, p1600). NONMEM VI, FOCE with eta-epsilon interaction, three-compartment model (ADVAN11), proportional error, log-normal IIV. Covariates by AIC over all 64 propofol-on-parameter combinations; propofol entered as each volunteer's mean blood concentration over 420 min (time-independent). Heart rate (and other haemodynamics, averaged over the interval before each sample) tested next. Likelihood-profile CIs and 1000-sample bootstrap model selection.
- Measured midazolam plasma concentration was 5.0 +/- 14.7% higher (P 0.115) at 0.62 mcg/mL propofol and 26.9 +/- 9.4% higher (P < 0.001) at 1.2 mcg/mL (p1601).

## Model and parameters
Three compartments: V1, V2 (shallow), V3 (deep); Cl1 elimination, Cl2 rapid distribution, Cl3 slow distribution. Units L and L/min. Covariate form: P = P_typ * exp(alpha_PROP*(C_prop - median) + alpha_HR*(HR - median)), median propofol 461,697 ng/mL as printed (see open question) and median HR 63.421/min; propofol enters in ng/mL (Table 2 footnote, PDF p5, read on the image).

Fit: -2LL 1622.991 (no covariates), 1604.449 (propofol), 1473.541 (propofol + HR; fitted to 15 sessions because haemodynamic data were lost in one). AIC 1648.991, 1636.449, 1511.541. Bootstrap selected propofol on V1/Cl1/Cl2 in 64.1% of data sets (p1601).

Derived check (mine): the Table 3 values follow from Table 2's final column with propofol in ng/mL and the median as 461.697 (e.g. V1 4.28*exp(-4.9e-4*(1200-461.697)) = 2.98; Cl1 0.31 at 1.2 mcg/mL).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic model or effect measure was fitted; BIS and respiration were monitored but not analysed for midazolam effect. No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, EC50, MEAC or analgesic concentration is reported. Midazolam is not an opioid. No potency ratio. Simulations (text p1602, Fig. 9) give context-sensitive half-time and 80% decrement time with and without propofol 1.5 mcg/mL (figure values not read).

## Covariates
- Propofol blood concentration (mcg/mL, entered as ng/mL): lowers V1, Cl1 and Cl2 (alpha above); Cl2 effect not significant by likelihood profile, retained because AIC selected it.
- Heart rate: lowers V3, raises Cl1 and Cl2 with rising HR; Cl1 effect not significant. The authors say HR mainly reduces within-individual variability (p1604).
- Haemodynamics in Table 1 (p1600, text layer only): with propofol MAP, CO and SV were lower, HR and SVR higher.
- Effect of propofol 0 to 1.2 mcg/mL (abstract, p1597): V1 5.37 to 2.98 L, Cl1 0.39 to 0.31 L/min, Cl2 2.77 to 2.11 L/min. (Text p1601 and abstract give Cl2 control 2.77; Table 3 gives 2.73 at HR 63, so 2.77 is not in Table 3.)
- Simulation conclusion: reduce midazolam bolus 25% and infusion a further 15% with propofol (p1602). No weight, age or sex covariate (all men, 85 kg).

## Caveats
- Eight healthy men, one dose level of midazolam; two propofol targets only (0.6, 1.0), each in 4 men; propofol is a session-mean time-independent covariate, so it is not dynamic.
- Heart rate effect is partly an indicator of haemodynamic state; two of the six covariate effects do not reach significance.
- The authors attribute the effect to reduced hepatic flow and CYP3A4 inhibition (a mechanism claim not tested directly).
- Total (not unbound) midazolam in plasma, arterial sampling.
- Text/abstract Cl2 control 2.77 differs from Table 3's 2.73 (small, different HR/median conditions); not resolved.

Direction: against the library, Lichtenbelt's elimination clearance is lower (about 0.67-0.72x), V1 and V2 larger, V3 and Vss smaller, slow distribution clearance less than half. These are one 8-man, 85 kg cohort; the authors themselves say their set is closest to Buhrer's (p1603), but the numbers differ by up to 2x. Concentration measure is the same as the library's (total plasma midazolam). The library applies no propofol effect; at 1.2 mcg/mL the paper predicts about 27% higher midazolam and the 25% bolus reduction; it is a PK interaction the library does not represent. Nothing here changes the library's tPeak (3.0 min), which comes from Buhrer's PD data, not this paper.
