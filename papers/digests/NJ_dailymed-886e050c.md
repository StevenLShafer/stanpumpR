# Tenex (guanfacine immediate-release) label, NDA019032, 2013

## Study
- **Document:** US label for immediate-release guanfacine HCl tablets.
- **Indication:** hypertension, alone or with a thiazide-type diuretic.
- **Strengths:** expressed as guanfacine base equivalents (How Supplied). The SPL basis of strength is guanfacine.
- **Molecular weight:** 282.56 for the HCl salt.
- This is not the extended-release ADHD product (Intuniv).

- AUC is linear with dose.
- The guanfacine-to-creatinine clearance ratio is above 1, which suggests tubular secretion.
- Clearance is reduced in renal insufficiency, but plasma levels rise only slightly. The low end of the dose range
  is advised.
- Guanfacine is poorly dialyzable (2.4%).
- Enzyme inducers (phenobarbital, phenytoin) shortened the half-life in two renally impaired patients.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Dose-response by dose, not by concentration.

**Monotherapy, seated SBP/DBP change in mmHg (p1, rendered):**

| Group | n (range) | Placebo | 0.5 mg | 1 mg | 2 mg | 3 mg | 5 mg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| White patients | 11-30 | −1/−5 | −6/−8 | −8/−9 | −12/−11 | −15/−12 | −18/−16 |
| Black patients | 8-28 | −3/−5 | 0/−2 | −3/−5 | −7/−7 | −8/−9 | −19/−15 |

**With chlorthalidone 25 mg (p2):**
- Seated: placebo −5/−7, 0.5 mg −5/−6, 1 mg −14/−13, 2 mg −12/−13, 3 mg −16/−13.
- Groups of n = 58-64.
- Effect persists to 24 h.
- Heart rate falls slightly, about 5 beats/min.
- Rebound after withdrawal comes at 2-4 days, later than with clonidine.

**Dosing (p9):** start 1 mg at bedtime; 2 mg after 3-4 weeks if needed. Adverse reactions rise significantly above
3 mg/day.

## Covariates
- Age affects the half-life (younger patients shorter).
- Renal impairment reduces clearance; no numeric adjustment is given.

## Caveats
- The PK summaries give no study sizes.
- The label is old-format, revised 2013, and the product is the original Tenex NDA.
- **Use in specific populations:**
  - pregnancy category B;
  - not recommended in children under 12;
  - the label carries reports of mania, aggression and hallucinations in children treated for ADHD.
