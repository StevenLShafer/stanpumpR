# Heikkinen 2002: citalopram in pregnancy and lactation

## Study
Prospective observational study, Turku, Finland. 11 women taking citalopram 20-40 mg once daily (median 20 mg;
indication depression n = 6, panic disorder n = 5; 10 on it at conception, 1 from 20 weeks) and 10 unmedicated
controls matched at delivery (Table I, PDF p. 2; p. 185). Median age 34 (25-42) yr; weight not reported.
Trough (pre-dose) maternal plasma at 20-24, 28-32 and 36-37 gestational weeks, at delivery (with umbilical artery
and vein), and postpartum days 2 and 4, 2 weeks and 2 months; infant plasma and breast milk at matching times
(pp. 185-186). Citalopram (CIT), desmethylcitalopram (DMCIT) and didesmethylcitalopram (DDMCIT) by reversed-phase
HPLC with fluorescence detection; racemic total (no enantiomers); interassay CV 10%; LLOQ 3 nmol/L plasma, 6
nmol/L milk (p. 186). Concentrations were also dose-normalised to the 20 mg defined daily dose (CIT/DDD).
Postpartum n = 9 (breast-feeding mothers). No PK model; descriptive statistics, paired t tests for metabolic ratios.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model or clearance estimate. Key concentrations (Table II, PDF p. 4, mean ± SD, nmol/L, trough,
confirmed on the page image):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No effect-site or concentration-effect model. Clinical response by the treating psychiatrist: only one woman needed
a dose increase in pregnancy (trough 56 nmol/L before it) and one after delivery (p. 186, 188). Maternal pregnancy
troughs 46-214 nmol/L, which the authors set against 120-600 nmol/L reported in non-pregnant women on similar doses
(a literature range, Milne 1991) (p. 186). Not an opioid; no MEAC/ke0/potency.

## Covariates
Pregnancy vs 2 months postpartum, the same women: CIT/DDD lower (Table II); metabolic ratios higher, which the
authors read as CYP2D6 induction in pregnancy, with little change in CYP2C19/CYP3A4 (p. 189). The text says the
DMCIT/CIT ratio was 23% higher in pregnancy (p. 187) and later calls it a 23% decrease (p. 189).

## Caveats
n = 11 (9 postpartum); concentrations only, no dose-time AUC; trough-only sampling; dose-normalisation assumes
linearity; no genotyping; the within-subject comparator (2 months postpartum, lactating) is not a pre-pregnancy
baseline, and postpartum levels themselves rose from delivery to 2 weeks. The paper never states a percent fall
in citalopram concentration with pregnancy. One maternal suicide (postpartum psychosis, 2 weeks). Lundbeck
performed the drug assays (acknowledgements).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **42% fall** (`R/drugs_citalopram.R:133-134`, `pregnancyRegistry.csv:69`): not a number in the paper. It is
  reproducible from Table II (derived: CIT/DDD 77 at 36-37 weeks / 132 at 2 months postpartum = 0.58, -42%; the
  trimester mean 76.7 gives -42% too; raw CIT 100/169 = -41%). The comparator is the same women at 2 months
  postpartum (lactating), not Westin's non-pregnant baseline, and the repo text does not say so. Not applied in
  any calculation (the factor `CITALOPRAM_PREGNANCY_CL_FACTOR` = 1.32 at line 189 is from Westin's 24%). **cannot
  verify** the attribution as stated; plausible as a derived figure (see open question).
- **n = 11 pregnancies**: matches (p. 185, Table II heading). **matches**.
- **Reference entry** (`R/drugs_citalopram.R:165-166`, registry source): authors, journal, year, volume, pages,
  DOI and PMID match. **matches**.
- **Registry statement that the decline is mostly CYP2D6 / R-enantiomer**: the paper supports CYP2D6 induction from
  metabolite ratios but measured racemic CIT only, so the enantiomer attribution is Westin's, not Heikkinen's.
- Units: the paper reports nmol/L; the library plots ng/mL total racemic citalopram (derived: × 324.4 g/mol, 77
  nmol/L ≈ 25 ng/mL; the pregnancy range 46-214 nmol/L ≈ 15-69 ng/mL, against the library's 50-110 ng/mL AGNP band).
  No conversion is made in code from this paper.
- No clearance is reported, so the library's Akil 2016 model cannot be compared directly.
