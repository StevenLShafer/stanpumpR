# Freeman 2008: sertraline pharmacokinetics across pregnancy and postpartum

## Study
Prospective longitudinal study, University of Arizona GCRC (FDA Office of Women's Health and NIMH funding).
Pregnant women 18-45 y already taking sertraline (SER) for DSM-IV major depression (at least 2 weeks on a stable
dose; at least 14 days on a constant dose before each visit). Up to 3 visits: second trimester (22-26 weeks),
third trimester (30-34 weeks), postpartum (12-52 weeks, after weaning) as each woman's own non-pregnant control.
11 enrolled; 8 completed the second-trimester visit, 6 also the third, 3 all three (p.649). The 8: all white
non-Hispanic, age 23-36 y (mean 30.5, SD 4.0). Weights 62.0-137.5 kg (Table 1, p.648); daily doses 25-200 mg,
changed by the prescribers during the study (Table 1). Observed oral dose with 200 mL water after at least a 2 h fast;
serum sampled at 0 (pre-dose), 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 24 h. Assay: HPLC-MS (SIM 306 m/z SER, 275 m/z
norsertraline [NOR]), no internal standard, linear 5-160 ng/mL SER and 10-320 ng/mL NOR; intraday CV <= 7.3%,
interday <= 12.7% (p.648-649). **Total serum** concentrations. Non-compartmental analysis (WinNonlin 5.1): AUC(0-24 h)
at steady state, Cmax, Tmax; SER AUC, Cmax and NOR AUC dose-normalised (divided by the dose at that visit). Paired
t tests and non-parametric tests; body weight added as a covariate.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. No concentration-effect analysis: depression scales (SIGH-SAD, EPDS, Zung, CGI) were collected
but not related to concentrations. No ke0, no therapeutic range, no MEAC. Not applicable.

## Covariates
Body weight tested (no effect on the conclusions); no equations. Genotype not measured (the authors suggest CYP2D6,
2C9, 2B6, 2C19 and 3A4 polymorphism explains the heterogeneity).

## Caveats
- Very small: 6 paired pregnancy profiles, 3 postpartum; target enrolment was 20.
- Doses changed between visits (Ms D 50 -> 100 -> 200 mg); dose normalisation assumes linearity, whereas the library's
  own model makes sertraline bioavailability dose-dependent (F = 0.639 D/(15.5 + D): 0.49 at 50 mg, 0.59 at 200 mg).
- Comparator: the paper's "decrease" in the third trimester is against the same women's second trimester; postpartum
  (12-52 weeks, after weaning) is the non-pregnant reference, and only 3 women had it.
- The 4.4% postpartum figure and Table 3's Ms H NOR cell are internally inconsistent (open_questions).
- No internal standard in the assay.
