# Eyal 2010: metformin pharmacokinetics across pregnancy and postpartum

## Study
Longitudinal, steady-state, noncompartmental study (OPRU network). 37 pregnant women enrolled, 35 analysed (PDF p3 / printed 835), taking metformin for preexisting diabetes, gestational diabetes or PCOS; dosing not altered for the study (500-3000 mg/day; 2 on extended release; interval 8-24 h). Study days: early (10-14 wk), mid (22-26 wk), late (34-38 wk) pregnancy and >=3 months postpartum (Table 1 header says >=12 weeks). Age 32.5 +/- 5.9 y, height 167.0 +/- 7.4 cm at first study day. Table 1 (p4): weight 100.8, 108.1, 117.8 and 97.7 kg (early, mid, late, postpartum); creatinine clearance 189, 234, 215 and 164 mL/min; median dose 1000, 1250, 2000 and 1250 mg/day; median interval 12 h. OCT2 (SLC22A2 808G>T) genotyped: 25 G/G, 9 G/T, 1 T/T.

Sampling: predose, then 0.5-24 h truncated to one dosing interval; urine collected over the same interval (0-8, 0-12 or 0-24 h), giving measured CrCL = (urine volume x urine creatinine)/(serum creatinine x interval). Assay LC-MS, LOQ 50 ng/mL plasma. CL/F = dose/AUC; Vbeta/F = (CL/F)/k; net secretion = CLrenal - fu x CrCL with fu = 1. Actual body weight used for per-kg values. Cord plasma (12 women) and breast milk (6 women).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured (no glucose or efficacy end point). Cord plasma: undetectable (<LOQ) in 3 of 12; venous cord 400 +/- 387 ng/mL in the other 9, arterial 417 +/- 399; arterial/venous 103 +/- 13% (p5). Breast milk/plasma AUC ratio 0.40 +/- 0.11; infant dose 0.13-0.28 mg/day, relative infant dose <0.5% (p5).

## Covariates
Across all 62 study days renal CL correlated with secretion CL (r = 0.97) and CrCL (r = 0.80); secretion CL with CrCL r = 0.64 (p4-5, Fig 3). In the 500 mg bid subset CL/F correlated with secretion CL (r = 0.80) and CrCL (r = 0.65) (Fig 5). OCT2 genotype had no evident effect.

## Caveats
- Small paired groups (late n = 10, postpartum n = 15), clinical dosing (500-3000 mg/day, mixed formulations, two on extended release), mixed indications. Because F falls with dose, the all-dose CL/F comparison (Table 2) mixes doses; the authors therefore analysed a same-dose subset (Table 3), which shows larger CL/F rises (mid 1.76x, P<0.05; late 1.38x, untested, n = 4).
- The paper's own abstract and Discussion stress renal clearance; CL/F rose but not significantly in Table 2.
- Postpartum women were heavy (97.7 kg), which the authors note could bias extraction estimates.
- Sampling was limited to one dosing interval, so the long terminal phase is not captured.
