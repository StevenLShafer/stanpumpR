# Blardi 1993: adenosine plasma kinetics after infusion

## Study
Healthy volunteers, plasma adenosine by HPLC (LOD printed as 'ng/mL' with no number; CV under 4%). Blood into ice-cooled syringes with dipyridamole (50 uM), EHNA (10 uM), heparin; deproteinised plasma stored at -20 C. Infusion into a cubital vein, sampling from the opposite antecubital vein. PK done on concentration minus basal (time-0) concentration.
- Group A: n = 6 (4 m, 2 f), age 52-78 y, weight 55-80 kg, 2.5 mg over 1 min (37.5 (5) ug/kg).
- Group B: n = 6 (4 m, 2 f), 52-69 y, 48-76 kg, 5 mg over 1 min (79 (16) ug/kg).
- Group C: n = 5 (4 m, 1 f), 52-65 y, 55-76 kg, 10 mg over 1 min (148 (23) ug/kg).
- Group D: n = 5 (2 m, 3 f), 51-68 y, 61-79 kg, 200 ug/kg over 10 min then 400 ug/kg over 10 min (0.6 mg/kg in 20 min).
Samples: 0,1,3,5,7,10,15 min (A-C); 0,5,10,15,20,21,25,30 min (D). Source PDF p1.

## Model and parameters
Groups A-C: one-compartment infusion model C(t) = A[(e^b - 1)/b] e^(-bt) fitted by nonlinear regression (b = rate constant, time in min); CL = (dose/infusion time) x b/A; t1/2 = 0.693/b; V = CL/b. Group D: trapezoidal AUC, CL = total dose/AUC, half-life from post-infusion semilog regression, V = CL x t1/2/ln2. Statistics: ANOVA, Tukey. Other values (text, PDF p2): Group D mean concentrations 210 (60) ng/mL at 10 min and 500 (180) ng/mL at 20 min; all returned to baseline within 5-15 min after infusion end. Peak about 0.5 ug/mL after 5 mg/1 min and after Group D.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured (no haemodynamic or ECG end point). No ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, MEAC or potency ratio. Authors link clearance (faster than cardiac output in A and D) to a possible red-cell uptake mechanism that saturates at higher input rates.

## Covariates
None analysed beyond dose/input rate.

## Caveats
Dose-dependent CL (fell about 50% and half-life doubled from 2.5 to 5/10 mg); Group D clearance may be overestimated by the trapezoidal rule (authors); very short half-lives may be artefacts of a steep distribution phase; small groups; ages 48-79 y (older than typical healthy volunteers); different methods (model fit vs NCA) across groups; plasma, not whole blood; units misprinted in Results (see open questions).
