# Watson 2010: liraglutide population PK, sequential zero- then first-order absorption

PDF page = printed page - 885 (PDF p. 1 is printed 886).

## Study
Novo Nordisk pooled analysis (NONMEM V, FOCE-I) of five published studies (Table I, PDF p. 3): A, Elbrond 2002, 54
healthy volunteers (HV), single SC 1.25-20 ug/kg (8 per level) plus 5 ug/kg IV (n = 8); B, Agerso 2002, 20 HV, SC
1.25-12.5 ug/kg, plus 2 unpublished T2D; C, Juhl 2002, 11 T2D, 10 ug/kg SC; D, Nauck 2003, 11 T2D, 7.5 ug/kg SC; E,
Vilsboll 2007, 168 T2D on 0.65, 1.25 or 1.9 mg SC once daily (sparse, 438 of 840 samples usable, weeks 3-9). Studies
A-D: 74 HV and 24 T2D (derived from Table I). Study F (Calara 2005): 28 T2D, exenatide 10 ug SC, for comparison.
ELISA with antibodies against native GLP-1 (samples incubated at 37 C to remove endogenous GLP-1). Concentrations in
nmol/L. Exclusions: 5 study A subjects with low dosing volume; 3 study D T2D outliers (about twice the dose-normalized
peak).

## Model and parameters
One compartment, linear elimination, "sequential dual absorption" (Eqs 1-2, PDF p. 4): for t <= T0 the absorption
compartment empties at a constant rate D x F x kA / (1 + kA x T0); after T0 it empties first order, dA1/dt = -kA x A1.
The depot holds D x F / (1 + kA x T0) at T0, so dA1/dt is continuous there and a share kA T0 / (1 + kA T0) of the
dose is absorbed during the zero-order phase. Better than zero- or first-order absorption alone; a lag time was
tested. Residual error proportional plus additive.

Study A text (PDF p. 4): peak 8-12 h after SC dosing; mean elimination half-life 12 h judged from exposure 24-48 h
after SC dosing, 9.1 h after IV dosing; dose-linear. Derived: 0.693 x 0.17/0.012 = 9.8 h, consistent with the IV 9.1 h
within rounding of the printed values. BSV by subgroup: CL/F 36% HV, 41% T2D; V/F 49%, 44% (PDF p. 5). Study E with
kA, T0, V/F fixed: CL/F 0.013 L/h/kg, the same as studies A-D (PDF p. 7). Exenatide (study F): one compartment,
first-order absorption, ka 0.734/h, V 26.2 L, kel 0.337/h (PDF p. 7).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None modelled. Discussion compares fasting glucose lowering from other trials (exenatide 1-1.5 mM, liraglutide 3-4
mM) and peak-to-trough fluctuation in a simulated steady state (Figure 6, normalized to peak). No exposure-response.
Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Disease status (T2D vs HV) on T0 and kA only; CL/F and V/F per kilogram. No other covariates tested or reported.

## Caveats
- Study A kA 0.0146/h (absorption half-life 47 h, derived) is inconsistent with the paper's own Figure 1 study A fits,
  which fall to near baseline by 48 h, and with its 12 h SC half-life; the pooled HV value is 0.104/h. Possibly a
  misprint (0.146/h would fit), but the paper does not show which; unresolved. Not used by stanpumpR.
- Small phase 1 datasets for T2D (24 subjects); study E too sparse to estimate the model independently.
- Excluding 5 low-exposure subjects changed F from 26% to 51%; excluding 3 high-exposure T2D subjects changed V/F
  (0.11 / 0.20 L/kg if included).
- All authors Novo Nordisk employees.
