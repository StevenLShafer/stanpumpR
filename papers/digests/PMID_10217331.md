# Scheinin 1999: atropine and glycopyrrolate effect compartment on a heart-rate-variability index

## Study
Double-blind, placebo-controlled, 4-way crossover (two 4x4 Latin squares), 1-week washout, Turku. 8 healthy
non-smoking male volunteers, 21-26 y, 59-88 kg, 170-187 cm (PDF p3). Single IV bolus doses: atropine sulfate 10
ug/kg, glycopyrronium bromide 5 ug/kg, scopolamine hydrobromide 5 ug/kg, saline. Paced breathing 0.25 Hz. ECG and
venous plasma at 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 30 min and 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 h (PDF p4). Drug concentrations by radioreceptor
assay (RRA; atropine interassay CV 12%, LLOQ 0.07 ng/mL). PK: two-compartment fits per subject (PCNONLIN 4.2). PD:
effect compartment plus sigmoid inhibitory Emax fitted per subject with PK fixed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point for the link model: Hayano index of HF power (sqrt(RRI HF power)/RRI x 100), not heart rate; subject 8
excluded (biphasic), n = 7. Heart rate rose 22% (atropine) and 18% (glycopyrrolate), maximum at the 10-min sample
(PDF p6). HF power fell 99% (atropine), Hayano index 87% maximum at 10 min; effect duration about 2 h. Hysteresis was
observed only during falling concentrations; venous sampling would bias ke0 (authors, PDF p21). Derived here: with
this paper's own mean atropine PK (alpha, beta, k21), ke0 11.0 /h gives an effect-site peak about 5.5 min after a
bolus; with Hinderling's PK (as stanpumpR uses) 3.15 min.

## Covariates
None (doses per kg).

## Caveats
Too few early samples to define the alpha phase (authors). RRA measures muscarinic-binding activity; the paper does
not say which enantiomer it reflects. Small n, young men, supramaximal doses, so EC50 and gamma (very steep) are
poorly constrained.
