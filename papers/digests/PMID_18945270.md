# Halbsguth 2008: oral heroin vs oral morphine, morphine bioavailability in naive and dependent subjects

PDF page n is printed page 780 + n (PDF p1 = printed 781).

## Study
- Open study in Zürich. 8 opioid-naive healthy volunteers (3 F, 5 M; mean age 30 y, range 21-42; weight 76 kg,
  54-106) and 8 opioid-dependent patients from the Swiss heroin-assisted treatment programme (5 F, 3 M; 35 y,
  26-46; 73 kg, 60-88). The patients were on ≥300 mg/day parenteral or ≥900 mg/day oral diacetylmorphine (DAM).
  Four had hepatitis B/C serology (p3, printed 783).
- Schedule (Table 1, p3):
  - Day 1: morphine-d3 IV, 24.3 µmol (7 mg base), over 5 min.
  - Days 2-3, in randomized order: oral morphine-d3 48.5 µmol (14.0 mg base) or oral DAM-d3 48.5 µmol
    (18.1 mg base; Table 1 gives 19.7 mg).
  - Day 4 (dependent patients only): their usual daily dose of DAM orally (500-800 mg DAM HCl, mean 598 mg,
    stated as 1601 µmol) together with morphine-d3 48.5 µmol.
- Everyone had metoclopramide 10 mg IV each morning. The dependent patients kept their maintenance DAM: after
  the IV session from 30 min, and by the IV/IM route 2 h after the oral doses.
- Sampling to 8 h: arterial in most dependent patients (venosclerosis), venous otherwise. Assay LC-MS (method
  of ref 9). LOQ 1 nmol/L for DAM and MAM, 2.5 nmol/L for morphine and its glucuronides (p4).
- Non-compartmental analysis. F = (AUC_oral/Dose_oral)/(AUC_iv/Dose_iv) on molar doses. The day-4
  concentrations were normalized to dose. S-Plus (p4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC: none reported.** The text mentions "therapeutic concentrations" of morphine after 18.1 mg oral DAM
  (p5) but gives no value or definition.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none.** No effect end point was measured; sampling was plasma only (venous
  or arterial).
- **Potency or equianalgesic ratio: none.** The comparison is equimolar (48.5 µmol of each) and is purely
  pharmacokinetic. Oral DAM gave about 2× the morphine Cmax of oral morphine, earlier (Table 3).
- Assay: total plasma morphine and glucuronides by LC-MS. Free fraction not measured.

## Covariates
- Opioid exposure (dependent vs naive): F was about 2× (DAM) and 1.5× (morphine) higher in the dependent
  subjects. Comparator: the naive volunteers of this study, same protocol. IV CL did not differ
  (1.69 vs 1.59 L/min).
- Dose: F from oral DAM rose from 45.6% (18.1 mg) to 64.2% (mean 598 mg) within the same dependent patients.
- Co-administration of high-dose DAM raised oral morphine-d3 F from 37.2% to 49.0% (same patients).
- Hepatitis serology, baseline opioid levels, substitution dose and tobacco/cannabis use showed no effect
  (Figure 3, p8).

## Caveats
- n = 8 per group. NCA only. Sampling stopped at 8 h, and the IV t½ comes from that window.
- The dependent patients carried pre-study morphine (293 ± 186 nmol/L) and received maintenance DAM on every
  study day. The d3 label separates the analytes, but the maintenance opioid could still affect absorption.
- Arterial sampling in most dependent patients against venous sampling in the naive subjects. The authors judge
  this irrelevant for oral absorption (p3).
- Dose arithmetic: 598 mg DAM HCl is called 1601 µmol (p6). That is about 1473 µmol for the anhydrous HCl salt
  (MW 405.9) and about 1619 µmol for the base (369.4) (derived). The basis of the high dose is unclear. This
  does not affect the low-dose values, which are internally consistent (18.1 mg DAM-d3 base ≈ 48.6 µmol;
  14.0 mg morphine-d3 base ≈ 48.5 µmol; derived).
- The Discussion quotes the high-dose F as 64.5% (p10), against 64.2% in Table 4 and the Results.
- The oral formulation (tablet or solution) of the low d3 doses is not stated; they were taken with 200 mL water.
