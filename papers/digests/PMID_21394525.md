# Salman 2011: tramadol and O-desmethyltramadol popPK in postpartum plasma and breast milk

## Study
- Re-analysis of a previously published sparse dataset (Ilett 2008, ref 4): 75 lactating women after caesarean
  delivery, days 2-4 postpartum, taking oral tramadol HCl 50 or 100 mg (167 or 333 µmol tramadol) 4-6 times
  daily (p 2, printed 900).
- Median (IQR) age 29 (25-32) y; weight 75 (67-91) kg; infant gestational age 38.7 (38.2-39.2) wk; milk
  creamatocrit 4.4% (3.1-6.1), range 1-13.2% (p 4, printed 902).
- Sampling: one plasma sample (73 in total) and 2-3 milk samples (214 in total) per woman, after the 4th or 5th
  consecutive dose, times randomised in 1-h blocks over the dose interval.
- Assay: HPLC of racemic tramadol and racemic ODT (total, plasma; not enantiomer-specific). LOQ tramadol 57 nmol/L,
  ODT 40 nmol/L; LOD 30 and 20 nmol/L. Concentrations in nmol/L throughout.
- NONMEM 6.2, ADVAN5 TRANS1, FOCE with interaction; log-transformed data, additive error; PsN bootstrap (1000),
  VPC/NPC.
- No pregnant or non-pregnant comparator group; no CYP2D6 genotyping (phenotype from a mixture model).

## Model and parameters
Five compartments (gut, tramadol plasma V2, ODT plasma V3, tramadol milk V4, ODT milk V5), first-order absorption
and elimination; one plasma compartment per analyte (the authors note earlier models were two-compartment).
Fixed from literature: kA 1.16 /h, first-pass to ODT 7.92%, total conversion to ODT in EMs 30.9%, PM fraction
0.04 (antimode at a plasma tramadol:ODT ratio of 15), V4 = V5 = 0.125 L, k24 = k35 = 1 /h.
Allometry: volumes × (WT/70)^1, clearances × (WT/70)^0.75. Plasma BSV not estimable (one sample per woman);
plasma residual error absorbs it.

Milk:plasma AUC ratio: tramadol 1.9, ODT 3.2 (p 6). Relative infant dose (EM/PM): tramadol 2.16/2.60%, ODT

154 µg/kg/day (p 9).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic measurement. **No MEAC or analgesic concentration**, **no ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis**,
and **no equianalgesic or potency ratio** reported. The paper states only qualitatively that (+)-ODT is a µ-opioid
agonist with higher µ affinity than tramadol (p 2, citing refs 10-11). It cites 4.3 mg/kg/day as the
recommended infant tramadol maintenance dose at 40 weeks post-conceptual age (p 9), a dose, not a concentration.

## Covariates
Creamatocrit on k53: k53 rises 6.56% per 1% creamatocrit (linear form, P<0.01, ΔOFV 6.867); infant gestational age
not significant. CYP2D6 phenotype via mixture model (PM fraction fixed 0.04). Weight via fixed allometry.

## Caveats
- One plasma sample per woman; plasma BSV not estimable; several structural parameters fixed from literature
  (kA, first-pass, EM conversion fraction, PM fraction, milk volumes, k24/k35), so CL_ODT/F and Metabolism_PM depend
  on those fixed values.
- All clearances and volumes are apparent oral (/F); F not estimated.
- Postpartum days 2-4, not pregnancy; no non-pregnant comparator. Authors note postpartum clearance is returning
  to non-pregnant values (p 7).
- Internal inconsistencies in the paper: Discussion gives tramadol plasma volume 332 L (p 7) but Table 1 gives
  V2/F 324 L; Abstract gives EM tramadol relative infant dose 2.16±0.57% but Results give 2.16±0.47% (p 6);
  Table 1 row "r (k42,k42)" presumably means the k42-k53 correlation.
- Comparisons in the Discussion: the tramadol t½ (7.1 h) is set against healthy-volunteer means 4.3-8.8 h and the
  7.2 h of women in labour (Claahsen 2005); the ODT t½ (11.0 h) is called longer than the 5.5 h of that labour
  study (pooled NCA). CL_T 31.1-35.9 L/h is set against published oral values 19.6-42.6 L/h.
