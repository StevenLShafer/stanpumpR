# Kim 2002: plasma and oral-fluid codeine after 60 and 120 mg/70 kg oral

(No DOI in the PubMed record, the context file or the text layer.)

## Study
- 19 healthy volunteers (12 M, 7 F; 14 African American, 3 Caucasian, 2 Hispanic), age 34.8 ± 1.1 y (SE; 23-43), weight 78.0 ± 3.4 kg (56.6-106.5). All had a history of cocaine and opioid use (positive urine at entry), not physically dependent; resident on a closed NIDA research unit.
- Oral codeine sulfate in lactose capsules: three low doses (60 mg/70 kg) in week 4 and three high doses (120 mg/70 kg) in week 8, ≥ 48 h apart. PK from the first dose of each.
- Plasma at 0.08-48 h; citric-acid-stimulated oral fluid at the same times.
- SPE/GC-MS for free codeine, norcodeine, morphine, normorphine; LOD and LOQ 2.5 µg/L.
- Non-compartmental (WinNonlin 3.2); AUC 0-24 h by linear trapezoid. Data are mean ± SE.
- PD: pupil size, BP, pulse, temperature, respiration, SpO2; ARCI, SDQ, VAS.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Miosis: significant dose-related decrease in pupil size at 4 h after 60 mg/70 kg and from 1 to 4 h after 120 mg/70 kg (Fig. 6A). No ke0, tPeak or hysteresis estimated.
- Subjective: ARCI PCAG (sedation) and LSD scales, and SDQ "feel drug", "relaxed", "high", increased after 120 mg/70 kg only. Other vital signs unchanged.
- No MEAC or analgesic concentration (no pain end point). No potency ratio.

## Covariates
None; dose given per 70 kg.

## Caveats
- Drug-experienced volunteers, mostly African American; mean ± SE, not SD.
- Doses are mg codeine sulfate per 70 kg, not mg base. 60 mg codeine sulfate is about 48-52 mg base depending on hydration (derived; Guay 1988 states 60 mg sulfate = 51.6 mg base).
- Plasma n smaller than enrolled (phlebotomy problems).
