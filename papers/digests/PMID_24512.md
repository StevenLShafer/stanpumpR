# Carruthers 1978: diphenhydramine plasma level vs sedation and antihistamine effect

## Study
Single-blind, three-way crossover, 6 healthy volunteers (4 women, 2 men; 22-33 y, mean 26; 57-90 kg, mean 70), at least 1 week apart, random order: (1) IV saline + 2 placebo capsules; (2) diphenhydramine HCl 50 mg IV over 1 min (Benadryl injection) + placebo capsules; (3) IV saline + 2 x 25 mg Benadryl capsules orally (50 mg, 100 mL water). Venous (antecubital catheter) blood hourly 0-10 h; plasma by GLC with alkali flame ionization (internal standard methapyrilene; sensitivity about 1 ng/mL); two contaminated sample sets re-assayed by GC-MS. Plasma, total drug (no protein-binding measurement). Psychomotor battery at 0-8, 24 and 30 h: 100-mm visual sleep scale (alert to drowsy), light reaction time, tapping, card sorting. Histamine wheals (0.025, 0.05, 0.1 ug intradermal), diameter at 10 min, dose ratio DR10 (dose giving 10-mm wheal with drug / without). Statistics: ANOVA, Duncan, least-squares regression.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Sedation end point: self-rated sleep scale (mm, 0-100). IV: maximum at 1 h in all but one subject (53 +- 8 mm); ratings at 1, 2, 3 h exceed placebo; decline 5.1 mm/h (r = -0.96). Oral: peak 2-3 h, significant at 2 and 3 h only. Placebo baseline 5-10 mm. Individual maxima 42-76 mm (Table I). Mean log concentration vs mean sleep rating r = 0.95 (IV), 0.80 (oral).
- Reaction time: prolonged at 1, 2, 4 h after IV (not 3 h), only at 4 h after oral; r = 0.88 with mean concentration (IV). Tapping and card sorting: no significant effect.
- Antihistamine end point: wheal diameter (0.1 ug histamine). Maximum mean reduction 15.1% at 1 h (IV), 15.2% at 3 h (oral); different from placebo 1-7 h (text), at least 8 h (abstract). DR10-1 = kC: line through intercept 0.04, slope 0.014 per ng/mL, r = 0.978 (Fig. 4, PDF 5); the "average apparent affinity constant" of the 6 subjects.
- Concentration ranges (p. 381, abstract): significant sedation (sleep or marked drowsiness) above about 100 ng/mL; significant sleepiness on the scale above 50 ng/mL; 4 of 6 fell asleep at levels comparable to the more than 70 ng/mL reported by Albert for two subjects. Window with significant antihistamine effect and no significant sedation: about 25 to 50 ng/mL. These are read from group mean plots, not a fitted EC50; total plasma, venous, IV and oral data.
- ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none fitted. The IV peak sleepiness coincided with the first sample (1 h), so onset within the first hour is not resolved. Time courses show sleepiness declining faster than wheal suppression, i.e. the antihistamine effect outlasted sedation (against placebo, same subjects).
- MEAC: none (not an analgesic). Equianalgesic ratios: none.

## Covariates
None analysed. Large between-subject variation (Table I); the authors give no age/sex/weight relationship.

## Caveats
n = 6, single dose, single-blind; samples hourly so the first hour's distribution phase is unobserved; terminal half-life from 1-8 h so likely too short (acknowledged as shorter than others' 6-7 h); assay not able to rule out interference noted with Vacutainer tubes (avoided after the first two sets); venous not arterial samples. CL and Vd are in mL/min and L, absolute (not per kg); the dose in the clearance equation is the base.
