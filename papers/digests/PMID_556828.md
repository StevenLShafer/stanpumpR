# Lander 1977: plasma phenytoin and phenobarbital fall in pregnancy

## Study
Case series (PDF p1, printed p128): the first 11 consecutive ("unselected") pregnant women with epilepsy from whom
data could be collected, Royal Brisbane Hospital. Ages 21-39 (Table, PDF p2); 8 first pregnancies, 3 second; 2 not
yet delivered (cases 10, 11). Ten took phenytoin; five took phenobarbital or a congener (phenobarbital: cases 1, 3, 6;
primidone: case 4; methylphenobarbital: case 10). All took folic acid and iron; co-medication included sulthiame,
clonazepam, carbamazepine, diazepam. Doses were adjusted clinically in several women (not a fixed-dose design).
Sampling "at intervals" during pregnancy and the puerperium; timing not tabulated.
Assays: phenytoin by modified Macgee gas chromatography; phenobarbital first by Wallace's spectrophotometric
method, later by gas chromatography (with methylphenobarbital, butylated derivatives). TOTAL ("whole plasma")
concentrations only; no free levels, no weights for most women, no pharmacokinetic model.

## Model and parameters
No model and no PK parameters. Individual observations, all total plasma, ug/mL (confirmed on page images):
- **Case 1** (PDF p1-2; Fig. 1): weight 73 -> 84 kg; phenytoin raised in stages 370 -> 600 mg/day by term to hold
  10-20 ug/mL; after delivery 11.2 -> 20.3 ug/mL in < 3 weeks at the same dose; about 6 months postpartum 25.3 on
  400 mg/day, which 2 years before pregnancy had given 29.2. Phenobarbital (constant dose, 120 mg/day on Fig. 1) fell
  progressively and was back at prepregnancy values by 6 months postpartum (values only on the figure).
- **Case 2** (PDF p2; Fig. 2): phenytoin 12.5 -> below 5 ug/mL by late pregnancy despite 300 -> 430 mg/day (weight
  72 -> 83 kg); two seizures near term; 1 week postpartum on 500 mg/day, 25 ug/mL (fivefold); 3rd postpartum month
  14.7 on 400 mg/day, a dose that gave 4.7 in the 7th month of pregnancy.
- **Case 3** (PDF p2-3; Fig. 3): phenytoin 9.3 on 400 mg/day 1 year before; 6.6 at 4 months on 400; then 500 mg/day,
  about 9.0 until term; 14.6 at 2 months postpartum on 500. Phenobarbital 180 mg/day constant: 27.2 one year before,
  23.5 at 4 months, 17.4 at 8 months (weight 64 -> 67 kg), 27.9 at 2 months postpartum.
- **Pooled** (PDF p3, printed p130): phenytoin lower relative to dose in late pregnancy than early/before in 9 of 10
  (p = 0.022); the tenth (case 8) needed rising doses to hold a constant level. Postpartum rise at constant dose in
  7 of 9 delivered (p = 0.180); three entered the toxic range and needed dose reduction. Phenobarbital fell in late
  pregnancy in all 5 on a constant dose (p = .062); rose again after birth in the 4 who delivered. Test not named.
- Discussion: the necessary phenytoin increment may be 50% or more (PDF p4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis. Seizures recorded per woman (Table: 4 of 11 had seizures in pregnancy). Not an
opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio. The authors cite earlier work (Hooper 1974) that phenytoin
binding is not significantly altered in pregnancy, and say binding of phenobarbital in pregnancy was unknown.

## Covariates
Pregnancy stage only, descriptively. Mechanisms offered (not tested): larger distribution volume, fetal/placental
metabolism, maternal enzyme induction with postpartum de-induction, folic acid.

## Caveats
- Small case series, uncontrolled dosing (several women's doses changed), co-medication including inducers
  (carbamazepine, primidone) and folic acid, which itself lowers phenytoin; compliance not measured.
- Total concentrations only; the phenobarbital assay changed during the study.
- Gestational timing and the number of samples per woman are given only for cases 1-3.
- Phenytoin is saturable, so "level relative to dose" is not a clearance ratio.
- Derived from single women, assuming steady state at an unchanged dose: case 2, 400 mg/day gave 4.7 in the 7th
  month and 14.7 at 3 months postpartum, 32% (derived: 4.7/14.7); case 3 phenobarbital 17.4 at 8 months against
  27.2 before and 27.9 after pregnancy, an apparent clearance ratio of 1.56-1.60 (derived: 27.2/17.4, 27.9/17.4).
