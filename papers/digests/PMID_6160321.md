# Riva 1980: propranolol plasma levels and electrophysiologic effects in man

## Study
Ten patients (8 men, 2 women, 19-68 y) who had undergone diagnostic cardiac catheterisation for suspected
supraventricular tachyarrhythmia; patients with ischaemic or hypertensive heart disease, asthma, heart failure,
valvular disease, AV block or sustained bradycardia were excluded (Methods, PDF p2, printed 708). Clinical details
in Table 1. **No weights are reported**; the mean 6.5 mg dose at 0.1 mg/kg implies a mean of 65 kg (derived).

**Intravenous phase (n = 10).** 0.1 mg/kg propranolol by slow injection over 2-3 min. ERPA, ERPAVN, heart rate and
plasma propranolol measured at **5, 15, 30, 45, 60, 90 and 240 min** (Study Protocol, PDF p3, printed 709; the
120-min point appears on Figure 2's axis as well). The Summary (PDF p1) says blood was drawn "at intervals for
12 hr", which the Protocol and Figure 2 do not bear out for the intravenous phase - Figure 2's IV panel ends at
240 min, and the 12 h belongs to the oral phase.

**Oral phase (n = 8).** 40 mg every 12 h for 2-4 days (2 patients for two days, 5 for three, 1 for four).
Measurements before and 1, 2, 3, 4, 8 and 12 h after the morning dose. Figure 2 shows n = 8 at the first and third
doses and n = 6 at the fifth.

Assay: gas chromatography (Di Salle et al.); CV 14% at 250 ng/mL and 17% at 5 ng/mL (n = 28 spiked samples).
Refractory periods by the extrastimulus technique, 10 ms decrements; a change of at least 20 ms was taken as
significant. Paired t-test (and Wilcoxon for AUC and troughs). **No compartmental model was fitted**: half-life on
the elimination phase, AUC by trapezoid with extrapolation to infinity, Vd = (D x F)/(AUC x beta),
Clp = (D x F)/AUC (Definition of Symbols, PDF p3).

coefficients and no compartmental parameters are given**, so the curve cannot be reconstructed from the paper.

Oral accumulation (Results, PDF p6): trough 5.6 +/- 1.1 ng/mL on day 1, 13.8 +/- 3.3 on day 2, 15.3 +/- 4.2 on
day 3; AUC(0-12 h) 335 +/- 52 after the first dose, 379 +/- 71 after the third, 418 +/- 70 ng/mL x hr after the
fifth. Only first versus fifth dose was significant. The half-life did not rise, so the authors attribute the
accumulation to rising bioavailability (citing Wood 1978), not to falling clearance.

### The printed means are means of ratios (derived)
Recomputing each parameter from the printed mean AUC gives a smaller number every time:

| | from the mean AUC (derived) | printed | printed / derived |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clp, IV | 710 mL/min | 968 | 1.36 |
| Vd, IV | 188 L | 223 | 1.18 |
| Clp, oral | 700 mL/min | 838 | 1.20 |
| Vd, oral | 205 L | 254 | 1.24 |

All four printed values exceed the value the printed mean AUC implies, which is what averaging per-patient ratios
does when exposure varies (the paper reports sixfold variability in peak concentration). So Table 2's Clp and Vd
are **means of individual estimates**, not parameters of a mean curve - which matters when comparing them with a
model fitted to a mean curve (see *Verification* below).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
The end points are electrophysiologic, measured at the same times as the concentrations; **no PK-PD model, no
hysteresis analysis, no ke0 and no concentration-effect relation were fitted**, and no time to peak effect is
reported.

| | basal | peak | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| ERPA, IV | 245 +/- 10 ms | 282 +/- 13 ms | +15.1% |
| ERPAVN, IV | 310 +/- 26 ms | 382 +/- 30 ms | +23.2% (+/- 2.6) |
| HR, IV | 85 +/- 5 /min | 65 +/- 2 /min | -23.5% |
| ERPA, oral | 265 +/- 10 ms | 290 +/- 14 ms | +9.4% |
| ERPAVN, oral | 320 +/- 30 ms | 382 +/- 35 ms | +19.4% (+/- 2.6) |
| HR, oral | 76 +/- 2 /min | 66 +/- 2 /min | -13.1% |

(Results, PDF p3 and p6; Fig. 1 gives the same values graphically.)

**Timing, which is what stanpumpR takes from this paper.** Two different statements, both confirmed on the page:
- Results (PDF p3): significant changes in the three parameters appeared **5-45 min** after the injection.
- Discussion (PDF p7): effects were seen much sooner after intravenous (**5-8 min**) than after oral
  administration (**86-146 min**), but lasted about twice as long after oral.

Neither is a time to peak effect. **The first measurement after the bolus is at 5 min and the next at 15**
(Protocol, PDF p3), so an effect peaking at or before 5 min is indistinguishable from one peaking earlier, and
nothing is measured between 5 and 15 min. The paper bounds the onset; it does not locate the peak.

Effects returned to control in all but one case once concentrations fell below 20 ng/mL. Plasma levels at the peak
of the effects were 22.3-59.9 ng/mL, which the authors contrast with Seides 1974's 13.6 ng/mL at a single ~60 min
observation. Case 3 did not respond despite "therapeutic" levels; the authors suspect accessory AV fibres.

## Covariates
None. No covariate model was fitted and no weights, so nothing here supports or contradicts a size model.

## Caveats
- **Patients, not volunteers**, after cardiac catheterisation, with arrhythmias; 19-68 y, and no weights given.
- **Follow-up is 240 min for a 184-min terminal half-life.** The alpha phase lasts about 60 min, so the terminal
  slope rests on roughly the 90, 120 and 240 min points - about 1.3 half-lives. By the rule in AGENTS.md
  (*Comparing sources: only clearance is real*) short follow-up biases the terminal half-life short and the volume
  by area with it; both are weak evidence here, and the direction is known.
- **No coefficients, so not implementable**: the paper gives a terminal half-life and an approximate alpha
  duration, never a biexponential.
- Table 2's Clp and Vd are means of individual ratios (see above), not mean-curve parameters.
- The racemate is measured by gas chromatography; only l-propranolol is a beta blocker.
- The Summary's "12 hr" of intravenous sampling is not supported by the Protocol or Figure 2.
