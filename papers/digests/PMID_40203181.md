# Vogt 2025: sedative-dose propofol, dexmedetomidine, fentanyl on memory and pain (7 T fMRI)

## Study
Single-blind, randomized (1:1:1), placebo-controlled crossover, University of Pittsburgh (NCT04062123). Healthy volunteers 18-40 y; 207 screened, 116 enrolled, 92 with data. Each subject had one drug session and one no-drug session (saline 5 mL bolus), in random order, at least 2 days apart, memory tested in person the next day (PDF pages 3-4).
- Demographics (Table 1, PDF page 7, printed 319, mean +/- SD): age about 24-26 y; mass 69.3-70.5 kg; height 168.5-170.7 cm; BMI 23.8-24.3 kg/m2 across groups (by available data per scan session).
- Drug targets (effect-site, held steady): propofol 1.0 mcg/mL; dexmedetomidine 0.15 ng/mL; fentanyl 0.9 ng/mL. Bolus + infusion rates pre-simulated with stanpumpR (stated, with https://stanpumpr.io) accounting for age, sex, height, weight; given in the scanner with a 100 mL/h crystalloid carrier. PK models cited: propofol Eleveld 2018 (ref 24), dexmedetomidine Dyck 1993 (ref 25), fentanyl Shafer 1990 (ref 26). Drug concentrations were not measured; no blood sampling.
- Tasks: 80 spoken words with mental-imagery encoding, 30 paired with a 2-s electric shock to the left index finger (current titrated beforehand to pain 7/10), block-design pain task (five 10-s shocks), brief cognitive battery, 7 T BOLD fMRI. Next-day Remember/Know/New test; d' signal-detection. Sedation by OAA/S (responsiveness and speech only). Mixed models; Bonferroni (Pa) for behavioural data.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, EC50 or MEAC is estimated, and no concentration was measured.
Fentanyl audit: single effect-site target 0.9 ng/mL (planned, not measured; model of Shafer 1990 per ref 26; plasma vs whole blood not stated). It is a study target, not a defined analgesic or minimum effective concentration, and no equianalgesic or potency ratio is given. The paper cites earlier work (not this study) for "smaller reductions" of memory with higher fentanyl doses.
Behavioural results (means, 95% CI; PDF pages 7-8):
- Next-day recollection d' (primary): no drug 1.16 (0.97-1.34); propofol 0.51 (0.182-0.842), P = 0.006, Pa = 0.018; dexmedetomidine 1.04 (0.73-1.35); fentanyl 0.98 (0.68-1.28). Familiarity (Know) d' near zero in all groups.
- Pain intensity (0-10): no drug 6.4 (6.18-6.63); propofol 5.86 (5.46-6.27), Pa = 0.047; fentanyl 5.9 (5.54-6.25), Pa = 0.037; dexmedetomidine 6.24 (5.89-6.59), P = 0.390.
- Pain unpleasantness: no drug 5.64 (5.24-6.04); propofol 4.75, Pa = 0.071; fentanyl 4.9, Pa = 0.113 (neither significant after adjustment); dexmedetomidine printed as 6.24 (see open questions).
- Sedation (OAA/S): no drug 4.98; propofol 4.75; dexmedetomidine 4.87; fentanyl 4.81; minimal sedation predominated; two subjects (both propofol) reached OAA/S 2.
- In-scanner three-back d': no drug 2.61; dexmedetomidine 1.65 (lower); propofol 2.11 and fentanyl 2.48 (no difference). Long-term visual memory d': no drug 1.95; propofol 1.14 and dexmedetomidine 1.43 lower; fentanyl 2.08 no difference.
- fMRI (secondary, data-driven): propofol decreased hippocampal/amygdala encoding activation and pain-related insula/anterior cingulate/amygdala activation; dexmedetomidine decreased hippocampal activation, no pain-area differences; fentanyl decreased hippocampus/amygdala encoding activation, decreased contralateral S1 and insula in pain, increased anterior cingulate, hippocampus, amygdala. Details in Figures 4-6 (not read).

## Covariates
Sex differences explored (exploratory, half the sample each); no drug-concentration covariates.

## Caveats
Single-blind; light (mostly minimal) sedation; one dose of each drug, so no dose-response; concentrations unmeasured, so achieved levels are the model's prediction; familiarity at floor (cannot separate recollection from familiarity); loss to follow-up before both sessions; scanner upgrades during the study; EEG/ECG could not be recorded at 7 T; source memory for shock pairing below chance. Authors state future work should examine dose dependence.

## Used by stanpumpR

## Verification against stanpumpR
Not applicable: the paper contains no parameters for the repo to cite. Context for later work:
- The models the paper names are not the library's current ones for two drugs. `R/drugs_dexmedetomidine.R` now uses Morse 2020 above one year (Dyck 1993 was replaced 2026-11-17, `:104`); `R/drugs_fentanyl.R:2` uses Bae 2020 (three-compartment, allometric) with tPeak 3.6 min (`:117`) and MEAC 0.6 ng/mL (`:114`), against the paper's Shafer 1990 set. So the effect-site targets 1.0 / 0.15 / 0.9 would not reproduce the same rates in the current library. I did not determine the propofol default (`R/drugs_propofol.R:276` gives tPeak 1.6 min citing Schnider 1999).
- The fentanyl 0.9 ng/mL target (effect site) is close to Bae's MEAC of 0.99 ng/mL (venous plasma, after surgery; per `R/drugs_fentanyl.R:60-61`) but the Vogt paper does not call it a MEAC and the populations and end points differ; no inference intended.
- Pain intensity fell by about 0.5 points at 0.9 ng/mL (6.4 to 5.9; Pa = 0.037) in this experimental model, with unpleasantness change not significant after adjustment.
