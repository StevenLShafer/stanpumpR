# Halstenson 1986: labetalol disposition and dynamics on dialysis

## Study
- 16 patients with end-stage renal disease (creatinine clearance < 10 mL/min), mild-to-moderate hypertension
  (supine diastolic > 85 mm Hg). Exclusions: hepatic, bronchospastic, cardiac failure, heart block etc.
- **Hemodialysis group** (n = 8; 3 M / 5 F; age 40 ± 14 y; weight 82.2 ± 19.1 kg; height 168.7 ± 9.6 cm;
  Table I, PDF p. 3): two 30-min IV infusions, the same dose for each patient (range 0.7-1.0 mg/kg actual body
  weight; mean 0.81 ± 0.16 mg/kg), first in an interdialytic period, then (after ≥ 7-day washout) 3 h before
  hemodialysis (Cuprophan CF-1511, 1.1 m²; blood flow 234 ± 14 mL/min, dialysate 406 ± 18.4 mL/min; 4.13 ± 0.83 h).
- **CAPD group** (n = 8; 2 M / 6 F; age 45 ± 12 y; weight 66.3 ± 9.6 kg; Table I): one 30-min IV infusion,
  0.7-1.0 mg/kg (mean 0.78 ± 0.14 mg/kg), sampled to 72 h.
- Dose salt (hydrochloride vs base) not stated. Supplier Glaxo.
- Plasma sampling to 48 h (hemodialysis arms) or 72 h (CAPD); HPLC (Hazelton), "precise and accurate to 2 ng/ml";
  interassay CV < 10.6% at 5.0 ng/mL and 7.8% at 75 ng/mL (PDF p. 3).
- Analysis: bi- or triexponential fits by PCNONLIN (Gauss-Newton), weights 1/C², model chosen by visual
  inspection, residual SS and Akaike; coefficients corrected to an IV bolus; CL = dose/AUC(0-∞); AUC by linear
  trapezoids. Individual (two-stage) estimates, means ± SD reported.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: supine blood pressure (cuff), not modelled with an effect site. No ke0, EC50 or Emax.
- Significant fall in supine diastolic BP 10 min after start of infusion. Maximum reductions: 22/24 mm Hg at
  2.9 ± 2.0 h (interdialytic) and 1.6 ± 0.8 h (dialytic) after end of infusion; a further fall during
  hemodialysis to 42/33 mm Hg at 5.8 ± 2.9 h; CAPD 31/19 mm Hg at 2.5 ± 1.9 h (PDF p. 5).
- Fall in BP correlated with log plasma concentration: interdialytic diastolic r = 0.641, mean r = 0.678;
  none on the dialytic day; CAPD (means, 7 patients without other antihypertensives) diastolic r = 0.839,
  y = 7.78(x) + 4.2 (Fig. 3, PDF p. 6; x is log concentration, base and units of the fit not stated beyond the
  axis "ng/mL"), mean r = 0.782. Authors describe the effect decay as paralleling log concentration; BP stayed
  below baseline about 32 h.

## Covariates
None modelled. Dialysis modality (HD vs CAPD) and dialysis timing compared by ANOVA: no significant difference.

## Caveats
- Small groups (8 + 8), no healthy control arm; comparisons with normal renal function are to literature
  (older studies: CL 870-2330 mL/min, t½ 2.1-5.7 h; newer sensitive-assay studies: CL 32.8 mL/min/kg,
  t½ 6.8-8 h; Wood 1982 ESRD: 1178.3 mL/min, 5.5 h with sampling ≤ 10 h).
- The authors themselves say the long t½ could reflect dialysis-altered disposition, flawed earlier studies,
  or both; per-kg CL here is < 50% of Leitz's healthy value.
- Doses 0.7-1.0 mg/kg; salt basis not stated. Industry (Glaxo) support.

  The paper reports triexponential decline in all 16 dialysis patients, terminal t½ about 12-13 h (Table II).
  **Matches** as an observation. Comparator: the paper's "longer" is against literature values (healthy 2.1-8 h,
