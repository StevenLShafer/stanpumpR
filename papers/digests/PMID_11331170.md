# Pan 2001: cisatracurium block in immediate-postpartum vs non-pregnant women

## Study
- Prospective controlled (non-randomised, two cohorts) study, ASA I-II women. 44 enrolled (22 per group), 2
  excluded for change of surgical plan, 21 + 21 analysed (PDF p 3, printed p 114).
- PP: < 48 h after delivery of a live fetus ≥ 35 wk, for postpartum tubal ligation. NP: > 40 wk since any
  pregnancy, elective gynaecological surgery.
- Demographics, Table 2 (PDF p 3, rendered): age NP 40 ± 5 vs PP 30 ± 5 y (p = 0.0001); weight 71 ± 18 vs
  82 ± 17 kg (p = 0.035); height 163 ± 5 vs 163 ± 8 cm (NS).
- Induction: thiopental 5 mg/kg, fentanyl 2-3 µg/kg, midazolam 0.015-0.025 mg/kg, cisatracurium 0.2 mg/kg
  (actual body weight, p 117; described as 4 × the "EC95", p 116). Maintenance 70% N2O, ~1% ET isoflurane;
  temperature 35.5-37.5 °C, ETCO2 30-36 mmHg.
- Monitoring: EMG of adductor pollicis, ulnar TOF every 10 s (Datex Relaxograph). Intubation at 90 s.
- No drug concentrations measured; no PK.

## Covariates
None modelled. Groups differ in age (10 y) and weight (11 kg) as well as postpartum state; dosing on actual
weight means PP women received a larger absolute dose (derived: 0.2 × 82 = 16.4 mg vs 0.2 × 71 = 14.2 mg on
the group means, ~15% more).

## Caveats
- Comparator is the study's own non-pregnant women, not a PK model; the authors' explanations (higher cardiac
  output, larger Vc/Vss, higher temperature and pH raising Hofmann clearance) are speculation, not measured.
- Non-randomised; age and weight confounded with group; the authors acknowledge weight may contribute.
- Only time to 25% T1 recovery reported for recovery (surgery too short for later recovery indices).
- EMG monitoring; onset times longer than some force-transducer/accelerometry studies (authors' discussion).
