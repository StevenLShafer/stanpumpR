# Woodworth 1994: effect-compartment time-action profiles of SC regular and NPH insulin

## Study
Two single-dose studies in healthy men (22-45 y, within 15% of ideal weight; mean 39 +/- 8.4 y, 177 +/- 9.8 cm, 71.7 +/- 9.5 kg, BMI 23.0; PDF p2). Regular human insulin (Humulin R) 10 U SC in 10 subjects (up to 12 h of clamp); NPH (Humulin N) 25 U SC in 6 subjects (up to 24 h). 1 U = 38.1 ug (6.56 pmol) for regular, 38.4 ug (6.61 pmol) for NPH. Overnight fast, Biostator euglycaemic clamp held 0.3 mM below fasting glucose; glucose infusion rate (GIR) is the pharmacodynamic measure. Serum insulin by commercial RIA (Coat-A-Count; guinea-pig anti-porcine antiserum, I-125 porcine insulin), lower limit 8.61 pM, interday CV 29.3% at 86 pM, 13.3% at 430 and 4,305 pM. Not blinded. Fitted with ADAPT II.

## Model and parameters
One-compartment PK with first-order absorption (ka) and elimination (K), linked to an effect compartment (k1e in, keo out; Fig 1, PDF p3), effect by a Hill equation E = Emax*CE^g/(EC50^g + CE^g) with GIR as E. Rate constants in 1/h, volumes in L. The individual PK/PK-PD parameters (ka, K, V, k1e, keo) are not tabulated. Table 1 (PDF p3, printed p66; means +/- SD, read on the rendered page):

Tmax of GIR (TRmax) is later than tmax of serum insulin for both (regular 3.0 vs 1.7 h; NPH 5.9 vs 5.7 h).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: GIR in a euglycaemic clamp (not an opioid; no MEAC). Counter-clockwise hysteresis between serum insulin and GIR (Fig 4), larger for regular than NPH; the effect compartment closed the loop (Fig 5). No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak value is printed. Onset and duration are defined as the times the effect-compartment-predicted GIR crosses 10% of Emax (0.556 mmol/min, derived) upward and downward. Predicted (Table 2, text layer, printed p68, 95% CI): regular onset 0.5-1.0 h, peak 1.75-3.0 h, duration 5-7.5 h (means 0.75, 2, 6 h); NPH onset 2.75-none h, peak 5-8.5 h, duration none-22 h (means: onset 3.5 h in the text but 3 h in the abstract, peak 6-7 h, duration 13 h). The NPH lower 95% CI stays below 10% Emax, so a 25 U dose may give no clinical effect in some individuals. Single doses in healthy men; no potency or equianalgesic ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled.

## Caveats
Healthy men, not diabetic patients; single doses (10 U and 25 U) only; NPH n=6 with wide variability; Emax assigned rather than estimated; RIA cross-reacts with porcine/animal insulin antiserum design, interday CV up to 29% at low concentration; Cmax in pM, doses in U.

## Used by stanpumpR
Not cited anywhere in the checkout (context lists no locations). The library does model human regular and NPH insulin (R/drugs_insulinRegular.R, R/drugs_insulinNPH.R, both via R/insulinTham.R from Tham 2017), concentration only, no effect compartment.

## Verification against stanpumpR
Not applicable as a citation check. As an independent comparison with the Tham-based model (R/insulinTham.R lines 219-221, ka 0.67/h at the 10 U reference dose and no lag for regular; ka 0.129/h with 0.378 h lag for NPH; CL/F 127 L/h and V/F 178 L at 80 kg at 10 U): a rough derived tmax for regular, using ke = 127/178 = 0.71/h, is about 1.5 h, close to the 1.7 h here; for NPH, ka 0.129 with the lag gives about 3.3 h against 5.7 +/- 3.5 h here (derived, at the 10 U CL/F; the Tham CL/F falls with dose, so a 25 U comparison would differ). The library's NPH therefore peaks earlier than this study's mean, within its SD. Units: the library uses mcU/mL (mU/L); the paper's pM convert at 6.56 pmol/U (regular), so 308 pM is about 47 mcU/mL (derived). Nothing to correct.
