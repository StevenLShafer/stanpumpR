# Crombag 2019: older age and paclitaxel population PK

## Study
- Pooled population PK analysis (PDF pp. 2, 4; Table I, p. 5). Retrospective trial data from the NKI and Erasmus MC (595 patients, 77 of them aged 70 or more; twelve NKI patients given 24-h infusions were excluded). These were combined with a prospective cohort of patients aged 70 or more at the NKI and Radboud UMC, collected Sept 2012 to May 2017 with flexible sampling of 1–10 samples.
- Total cohort (Table I, p. 5): **684 patients** and 5895 samples, 1–20 per patient per cycle (median 4). 166 patients were aged 70 or more (24%), 89 of them prospective. Age median 61 [18–84] years; 383 women (56%). BSA median 1.8 m² [IQR 1.7–2.0]; total bilirubin median 7 [5–9] µmol/L; albumin 41 [37–43] g/L; ECOG PS 1 [0–3]. Hospitals: NKI 359, EMC 321, Radboud 4. Indications: mainly gynaecological, upper GI, lung and breast.
- Dose: 38–290 mg/m². Weekly regimen median 52 mg/m² [38–103], infusion 1 h [1–4]. 3-weekly regimen median 175 mg/m² [88–290], infusion 3 h [1–5]. Infusions overall ran 1 to 5 h; sampling extended to 55.6 h after the start of infusion (p. 4).
- Formulation: the paper does not name the product or vehicle.
- Assay: HPLC-MS/MS at the NKI, validated 0.5–500 ng/mL, inter-assay accuracy and precision both <10% (p. 2). Different assays were used across cohorts, so each cohort has its own proportional error (p. 3). **Total** plasma paclitaxel; the authors note free concentration might predict effect better (p. 6).
- Software: NONMEM 7.3.0, FOCE-I; PsN 4.4.8; Piraña 2.9.2; R 3.0.1 (p. 4).

## Model and parameters
The structure is the earlier Joerger model (ref. 13, Joerger 2012 *Clin Pharmacokinet*) with three compartments (Eq. 1–3, pp. 2–3). Elimination from the central compartment is saturable (VMEL, KMEL). Transport to the first peripheral compartment is saturable too (VMTR, KMTR), with first-order return at K21; that compartment is an amount with no volume. Distribution to the second peripheral compartment (V3) is linear, with rate constants K13 and K31 (Table II gives it as Q and V3). Covariate forms (p. 3): Eq. 5, VMEL = Θ1·Θ2^GENDER, where Θ1 is the value in **females** and GENDER = 1 for males. Eq. 6, VMEL = Θ1·(COV/COV_median)^Θ2 for age, BSA and bilirubin, centred on the study medians. Missing bilirubin (25% of patients) got its own estimate; that estimate is not reported. IIV is exponential on VMEL, V3, VMTR, KMTR and Q; IOV on V1 and VMEL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No effect-site model, ke0, EC50 or PD model.
- Exposure end point: time above 0.05 µmol/L (T_C>0.05). In the discussion (p. 6), citing other studies, the authors link it to efficacy, neuropathy and haematological toxicity. They simulated it for n = 1000 per sex at 80 mg/m² over 1 h, other covariates at the median (Fig. 5, p. 9). Ten years of age raised T_C>0.05 by less than 10%; from 70 to 80 years the rise was 6% (p. 4).
- VMEL falls 5% per 10 years from the median age (p. 4; Fig. 4).
- The paper uses no other threshold (e.g. 0.1 µmol/L).
- Not an opioid: no MEAC, potency ratio or analgesic end point.

## Covariates
On VMEL only: sex (male ×1.12), BSA^1.41, bilirubin^−0.17 and age^−0.31, each power term centred on its median (61 y, 1.8 m², 7 µmol/L). Age as a dichotomous covariate (<70 vs ≥70) was also significant (dOFV 19). Performance status did not change the age relation. The BSA formula is not stated.

## Caveats
- **Internal inconsistency (p. 4, confirmed on the page image):** the dichotomous model reportedly gave VMEL "17% higher in younger patients" but lists medians of 30.8 µmol/h for younger and 36.0 µmol/h for older patients. These run the other way, and 36.0/30.8 = 1.17 (derived). The figures are probably swapped. That model is not the one implemented.
- In one retrospective cohort, BQL observations had been discarded without a record; an additive error term was added for that cohort only.
- Older patients were sampled less (median 3 vs 5 samples per cycle; 3.1 vs 4.1 h).
- Combination chemotherapy (platinum, doxorubicin) was not excluded.
- Total, not unbound, concentrations; formulation not stated; no IV-bolus or 24-h data.
- The authors conclude the age effect is not clinically relevant.
