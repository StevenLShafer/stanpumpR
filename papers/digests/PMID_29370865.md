# Back 2018: mechanism-based PK model of fenofibrate with food

## Study
- Healthy Korean volunteers, n = 24 (13 men, 11 women), mean age 23 y, mean weight 68.75 kg, mean height 173.29 cm (p. 4).
- Randomised three-way crossover (p. 2). Single dose of a 250 mg fenofibrate sustained-release capsule with 240 mL water, taken 10 min after no meal, a standard breakfast or a high-fat breakfast.
- Meals (Table 1, p. 3): fasting 0 kcal; standard 686.3 kcal (carbohydrate 56.3%, protein 23.9%, fat 19.9%); high-fat 1280 kcal (carbohydrate 45.5%, protein 19%, fat 35.5%).
- Sampling: pre-dose and 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 24, 48, 72 h. The analyte is fenofibric acid, the active metabolite; fenofibrate itself is not measured. The assay is not described in this paper (the original study is the authors' ref 3).
- NONMEM 7.3, FOCE-I, Perl-speaks-NONMEM VPC (1000 simulations) and bootstrap (1000 replicates on 90% resamples). The study was run April to November 2002 (p. 4).
- A simulation of 1000 subjects per group (fasted, 400 kcal normal meal, 1280 kcal high-fat meal) took 250 mg once daily for 7 days (Table 2, p. 4).

## Model and parameters
- Three drug compartments (stomach, duodenum/upper intestine, central) plus two calorie compartments (stomach, duodenum).
- Gastric emptying rate: kg_i = TVkg x (1 + Efood x (MPAST(1) - MPAST(2))) x exp(eta). The food effect lasts from MTIME(1) = 0 to MTIME(2).
- Bile effect on the combined metabolism and absorption rate: km&a x (1 + Ebile x X5), where X5 is the duodenal calorie amount.
- Food changes the apparent central volume: Vc/F x (1 + EVc1) for a standard meal, x (1 + EVc2) for a high-fat meal. The authors read this as bioavailability rising 1.65-fold (standard) or 1.86-fold (high-fat) (Discussion, p. 8).
- The metabolism of fenofibrate to fenofibric acid and its absorption are one rate, km&a, because the esterase conversion is rapid and complete.
- Residual error is proportional (0.608).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. This is a PK-only paper: no ke0, tPeak, EC50, MEAC or therapeutic concentration, and no effect measured. Fenofibrate is a lipid-lowering drug and the paper does not link exposure to lipids. It mentions that more exposure might mean more myopathy, without data. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
- Food: fed or fasted, and meal type, entering as calories (Ebile) and as a categorical meal effect on Vc/F. No weight, age or sex covariate is reported.
- Gastric volume is fixed at 49 mL fasted and 1 L fed (from a physiology textbook, ref 19).

## Caveats
- Healthy volunteers only, with one SR capsule formulation.
- Food is described by calories only, not by volume or composition (authors' own limitation).
- Vc/F falling after food is interpreted as bioavailability rising: V/F and F cannot be separated without IV data.
- Large IIV on Vc/F (93%) and kel (86.3%); residual error is 60.8%.
- Internal inconsistencies noted in `open_questions` (IOV placement, 908 kcal against 1280 kcal).
- Fenofibric acid only; the parent drug is not modelled.
