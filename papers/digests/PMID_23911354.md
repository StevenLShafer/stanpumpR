# Reisinger 2013: antiepileptic drug clearance and seizures in pregnancy

## Study
Retrospective chart review, Emory Epilepsy Center, February 1999 to February 2012. Of 135 women on
antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) in pregnancy, 115 pregnancies in 95 women qualified (at least one AED blood
level (ABL) in each trimester, no AED added or removed); 113 singletons, 2 twin sets. Mean age 30.3 y
(15-43); 77% Caucasian, 17% African-American, 6% Asian (Table 1, PDF p. 2). Monotherapy in 100
pregnancies, two AEDs in 15 and three in one (p. 14). AED use (Table 1, rendered page; total / mono /
poly): lamotrigine 80/69/11, levetiracetam 27/15/12, carbamazepine 7/6/1, topiramate 7/3/4, oxcarbazepine
4/2/2, phenytoin 2/1/1, valproate 2/1/1, zonisamide 2/1/1, ethosuximide 1/1/0. The Table 2 footnote gives
3 oxcarbazepine monotherapy pregnancies and Fig. 2 gives 2, so the paper is internally inconsistent there.

Levels: routine clinical laboratory assays, TOTAL plasma concentration (free not consistently
available); oxcarbazepine measured as its 10-monohydroxy metabolite. Sampling time after dose not
standardized; roughly monthly. Trimesters: first <14 wk, second 14-28 wk, third >28 wk to delivery.
Baseline: preconception level, or, if none, a postpartum level >6 weeks after delivery (76 pregnancies had
a preconception baseline, 24 more a postpartum one, 15 had none and were excluded from clearance analyses).

Apparent oral clearance for each level: daily dose (mg/kg) / serum concentration (µg/mL), weight from the
most recent clinic visit (p. 14). Units therefore L/kg/day (derived: (mg/kg/day)/(mg/L)). Maximum
pregnancy clearance compared with baseline by a general linear model (SPSS 20).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. No ke0, MEAC or potency. Clinical end point: seizure frequency vs the 12 months before
conception. 44/115 pregnancies (38.3%) had more seizures in some trimester; LTG mono 38%, LEV 47%, CBZ 0%,
other mono 20%, polytherapy 50% (Fig. 2). Worsening more likely with seizures in the preconception year
(p<0.001) and localization-related epilepsy (p=0.005). A fall in total level >35% below preconception was
associated with more seizures in the second trimester (p<0.001 with prior seizures, p=0.030 without); the
continuous trimester/baseline level ratio was not associated.

## Covariates
Weight is in the clearance (per kg). Trimester. Race and mono/polytherapy tested, no effect.

## Caveats
- "Clearance" is dose over a single total level at an unstandardized time after dose: not a true CL/F
  (no AUC), and adherence was not monitored. Total, not free, levels (matters for CBZ, PHT, VPA).
- The tabulated value is the mean of each woman's maximum within a trimester, so it overstates the typical
  trimester clearance, especially where levels are frequent.
- Small n beyond LTG and LEV; single patients for PHT, VPA, ZNS, ESM.
- Apparent misprints in Table 2 (rendered page): OXC N printed 20, though Table 1 gives 2 and the footnote 3
  oxcarbazepine monotherapy pregnancies; "Polytherapy with LTG or LEV" third-trimester % printed 676,
  while 2.20/1.32 = 1.67 (derived) suggests 67. Neither number is used by stanpumpR.
- Part of the LTG data (22 pregnancies) overlaps Pennell 2008.
