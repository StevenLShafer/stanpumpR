# Ansari 2024: IV calcium chloride to reduce blood loss at intrapartum cesarean

## Study
- Single-centre (Stanford), block-randomized (blocks of 10, 1:1), double-blind, placebo-controlled superiority trial;
  ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05027048, registered 30 August 2021; FDA PIND 158398 (PDF p 2, printed p 105).
- Enrolment April 2022 to March 2023: 828 consented, 120 randomized, 60 calcium and 60 placebo; all received the
  full infusion (abstract, p 1; Results, PDF p 4).
- Population: laboring patients at 34 weeks' gestation or more, aged 18-55 years, given oxytocin in labor, who then
  needed cesarean delivery under neuraxial anesthesia. Excluded: BP above 160/110 needing IV antihypertensives within
  24 h, digoxin within 14 days, serum creatinine above 1 mg/dL, calcium channel blockade within 24 h, known cardiac
  disease (PDF p 2).
- Table 1 (PDF p 5, rendered): age 33.8 ± 5.2 (calcium) and 32.8 ± 5.2 y (placebo); BMI 31.6 ± 4.6 and
  32.2 ± 6.1 kg/m2; gestational age median 39 (39-40) wk in both. **Body weight is not reported.**
- Intervention: 1 g calcium chloride diluted to 60 mL with normal saline, or 60 mL saline, infused over 10 minutes by
  syringe pump starting 1 minute after umbilical cord clamping (PDF pp 2-3). All patients received a 2-unit IV
  oxytocin bolus and an oxytocin infusion of 7.5 units/h at delivery.
- Primary outcome: quantitative blood loss (Triton QBL system), analysed by inverse Gaussian GLM with log link.
  Secondary: PPH (QBL > 1,000 mL), second-line uterotonics, transfusion, change in hematocrit, uterine tone score
  (0-10) at 2, 7 and 12 min, haemodynamics every 5 min for 30 min, side effects.
- **No calcium concentrations (ionized or total) were measured or reported.** No pharmacokinetics.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic model. Clinical outcomes (PDF p 5, rendered; Table 2 PDF p 6, text layer):
- Median (IQR) QBL 840 mL (650-1,434) calcium vs 1,051 mL (796-1,395) placebo; mean reduction 211 mL
  (95% CI -33 to 410), P = .086.
- Adjusted for imbalanced baseline characteristics: 250 mL lower (95% CI 37-425), P = .024.
- Prespecified subgroup excluding documented nonatonic bleeding (n = 39 calcium, 40 placebo; total 79): reduction
  356 mL (95% CI 159-515), P = .001. Nonatonic bleeders (21 calcium, 20 placebo): difference 2 mL (-449 to 453).
- Subgroup PPH incidence 20.5% vs 42.5%; hematocrit change 6.2 vs 9.5 (Table 2; text layer, cells scrambled).
- Any possible side effect 23 (38.3%) vs 25 (41.7%) (Table 3, PDF p 7, text layer). The authors say flushing and
  clinically significant heart-rate changes were more frequent with placebo; the per-row values of Table 3 are not
  transcribed here because its text layer misaligns rows.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points were blood loss, uterine tone score and haemodynamics. Uterine tone did not differ (median 7.0 at 5 min
after the start of infusion and at its end, both groups). No concentration-effect relationship, no ke0, no
therapeutic concentration. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled. Sensitivity analysis adjusted for advanced maternal age, Black and Hispanic race/ethnicity,
hypertension, diabetes and asthma (standardized difference > 0.2).

## Caveats
- Primary outcome not significant; the positive result is a subgroup analysis (prespecified, and the trial was
  powered for it).
- Second-line uterotonics often given before the 10-minute infusion ended (authors).
- Few non-Hispanic Black patients; labouring, oxytocin-exposed patients only.
- No weight, no calcium levels.
