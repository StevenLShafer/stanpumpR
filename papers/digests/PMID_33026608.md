# Jun 2020: unbound phenytoin popPK with four protein-binding models (Vancouver adults)

## Study
- Retrospective, Vancouver General Hospital (British Columbia). 37 adults (> 18 y), >= 5 days on the same regimen,
  37 paired free and total concentrations (one pair each), sampled 3-24 h after a dose (12.9 +/- 7.3 h).
- Age 61.1 +/- 17.9 y; weight 68.5 +/- 15.6 kg (median 70); 27 male, 10 female; 16 critical care; albumin
  2.64 +/- 0.52 g/dL (94.6% < 3.5); dose 378.5 +/- 148.3 mg/day in 1-3 divided doses; IV 16, PO 21 (ER capsule or
  suspension). Total 11.4 +/- 5.3 mg/L, free 1.4 +/- 0.7 mg/L, unbound fraction 12.4 +/- 3.1% (Table 1).
- MonolixSuite 2019R2 (SAEM); bootstrap; pcVPC.

## Model and parameters
One compartment, first-order absorption without lag, Michaelis-Menten elimination **of unbound phenytoin**,
proportional errors on total and free; four binding models compared (Winter-Tozer, linear, non-linear single site,
non-linear multiple site). Linear binding best: C_total = C_free x (1 + Kb) type, Kb rising with albumin.
**F and ka could not be estimated and were fixed from literature** (refs 24, Hennig 2015 critically ill children;
45, Gugler 1976). Table 3, final linear-binding model (estimate, RSE%):

Eq. 5: log(Vmax) = 2.73 + 2.04 x log(weight/70) - 0.0355 x bilirubin. Eq. 6: log(Kb) = 2.04 + 0.799 x
log(albumin/2.7). Derived: Vmax 15.3 mg/h = 367 mg/day = 5.25 mg/kg/day at 70 kg, or 5.36 mg/kg/day if divided by
the mean weight 68.5 kg; with a typical bilirubin of 6 umol/L the bilirubin factor is exp(-0.213) = 0.81.
Winter-Tozer final model: CF 0.276, Km 0.589 mg/L, Vmax 11.7 mg/h, weight exponent 2.11.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None (PK of unbound drug; therapeutic range quoted as 10-20 mg/L total). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Weight and total bilirubin on Vmax; albumin on Kb. Oral formulation (ER capsule vs suspension) tested, not retained.

## Caveats
n = 37 with one sample pair each; F and ka fixed; Km and Vmax initially fixed then estimated with large RSE (Km
78.6%); weight exponent 2.04 is physiologically implausibly steep; mostly hypoalbuminaemic inpatients; IV and oral
mixed.
