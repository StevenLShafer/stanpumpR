# Whitfield 1983: Pregnancy and heparin concentration-anticoagulant effect

## Study
- In vitro: citrated platelet-poor plasma from 7 healthy third-trimester women (18-29 yr, 33-41 wk, mean 36.8 wk; 3 smokers) and 10 nonpregnant women (19-39 yr). Beef-lung heparin (Upjohn) added at 7 concentrations, 0 to 0.7 U/ml; APTT (Automated APTT reagent, Fibrometer, 15-min incubation). No in vivo dosing, no heparin kinetics measured.
- Slope = linear regression of ln(APTT) on heparin concentration (ml/U); r² > 0.989 in all subjects. Also coagulation factors II, V, VII-XII, antithrombin III, fibrinogen, protein fractions, AAG, alpha-1-antitrypsin, alpha-2-macroglobulin, haematocrit, prothrombin time.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Table I (PDF p2), mean ± SD, pregnant (n=7 for first six rows, else 6) vs nonpregnant (n=10):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is in vitro APTT on spiked plasma, not a clinical outcome. Slope ratio pregnant/nonpregnant = 1.68/2.33 = 0.72 (derived). No ke0, no MEAC, no therapeutic concentration range. (Not an opioid.) Authors note clinical dose requirements rise in pregnancy and that increased plasma volume (about 40%, cited) and possibly increased clearance may add to this; neither was measured here.

## Covariates
Baseline APTT correlated positively with slope in nonpregnant women (r = 0.85, P<0.05) but not pregnant (r = 0.54 in the text; sign and value in Table II not confirmed). Slope correlated negatively with factor XI in both groups (Table II, not confirmed on image).

## Caveats
Small n (7 vs 10), in vitro addition of heparin to plasma, one heparin lot, a single APTT reagent, uncontrolled diet, smokers only among the pregnant, different ages not matched exactly. Mechanism not established by the data.

- Registry line 147: "reduced heparin anticoagulant slope (1.68 vs 2.33 ml/U) in third-trimester women": **matches** Table I (PDF p2). Attribution of the mechanism (heparin-binding proteins, factor VIII, fibrinogen) to this paper is partly the authors' association only: factor VIII and fibrinogen are higher, but they state mechanistic interpretation is not possible; "heparin-binding proteins" is not a finding of this paper. - Row says the change "is a pharmacodynamic/binding phenomenon" not an IV clearance factor: consistent with this paper measuring only concentration-effect in vitro; the paper itself does not exclude a clearance contribution.
- Line 148 (heparinCPB): same use; matches.
- Library models: R/drugs_heparin.R (Jia 2015, anti-IIa IU/mL, CL 1.18 L/h) and heparinCPB; neither uses this paper and no factor is applied, so no unit conversion applies. The paper's slope is ml/U (concentration in U/ml); the library's curve is IU/mL anti-IIa activity, which is not the same assay as spiked-plasma APTT.
