# Dan 1997: serum levels and serum bactericidal titres after oral clindamycin 300 and 600 mg

## Study
- Randomized, single oral dose; 20 otherwise healthy adults admitted for minor elective surgery, 10 per dose
  (11 men, 9 women; mean age 39.3 y, range 21-52; mean weight 67.5 kg, range 50-90; normal liver and kidney
  function; no antibiotics in the preceding month) (PDF p1-2).
- Clindamycin capsules (Upjohn) 300 mg (one capsule) or 600 mg (two), taken 2 h after a standard breakfast.
- Serum sampled 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 h (the Methods list on p2 omits 1 h; Table 2 and the abstract
  include it).
- Assay: agar-diffusion **bioassay** (Micrococcus luteus ATCC 9341), lower limit 0.25 mg/L; CV < 5%. A bioassay
  measures antibacterial activity, so it may include active metabolites; total (not free) serum drug.
- Serum inhibitory and bactericidal titres (NCCLS microdilution; diluent 50% pooled human serum, 50% cation-supplemented
  Mueller-Hinton broth; doubling dilutions 1:2 to 1:512) against 5 oxacillin-susceptible S. aureus and 5 S. pyogenes
  isolates, at the peak sample and at 6, 8 and 12 h. Bactericidal = >= 99.9% kill.

## Model and parameters
No PK model was fitted. Observations:

Table 1 (PDF p2): MIC of the test isolates, mean (range): S. aureus 0.112 (0.062-0.125) mg/L; S. pyogenes 0.043
(0.031-0.062) mg/L.

"ml/l", evidently a misprint for mg/L (the abstract and text use mg/l).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: ex vivo serum inhibitory and bactericidal titres. Against S. aureus most serum samples showed
  bacteriostatic but **not bactericidal** activity; inhibitory activity lasted to about 6 h with both doses.
- Against S. pyogenes, bacteriostatic and bactericidal activity to 6 h, borderline bacteriostatic at 8 h.
- Doubling the dose roughly doubled the peak titres but not those at later times.
- The authors attribute the poor S. aureus killing to their strains' higher MICs than in studies that found killing
  (Lemmen 1993 strains mostly MIC <= 0.06 mg/L). No MBCs were reported. No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio (not an
  analgesic).

## Covariates
None examined.

## Caveats
- n = 10 per dose, fed state, bioassay (active metabolites may contribute), only 5 isolates per species.
- Mean curves only; no individual data or variance given. Unit misprint in Table 2; 1 h sample missing from the
  Methods list; abstract/table mismatch for one titre (15.2 vs 15.1).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `R/antibioticMbc.R:293-303`: the row puts S. aureus as "excluded" and quotes that most serum samples after 300 and
  600 mg orally "did not show bactericidal activity against S. aureus". The paper says exactly this (abstract;
  Results, PDF p3). The cited quotation and the citation (Chemotherapy 1997;43:227-231) are correct. **Matches.**
  The row's other claim, that the MBC is many times the MIC, is attributed to the FDA labels, not to Dan; Dan
  measured no MBC.
- The context's line numbers (282-283) point at the Bacteroides row's sources at this commit; the Dan citation is at
  lines 299-301. Line drift only.
- Independent check against the library's clindamycin model (`R/drugs_clindamycin.R`, Bouazza 2012: CL 15.2 L/h x
  (WT/70)^0.497, V 66.2 L, ka 0.967 /h, F 0.876), which does not cite Dan. Derived predictions at 67.5 kg:
  300 mg peaks 2.55 mg/L at 2.0 h (Dan: 3.40 at 1.5 h, 1.3x the model); 600 mg peaks 5.1 mg/L (Dan: 4.8, 0.94x).
  Dan's mean curves fall faster than the model (derived t1/2 1.8-2.4 h vs the model's 3.1 h), so the model is above
  Dan's means from 3-4 h on (e.g. 6 h: model 1.32 vs 0.80 for 300 mg, 2.64 vs 0.80 for 600 mg). Dan's 600 mg mean is
  not dose-proportional (peak 1.41x the 300 mg peak; equal at 6 h), so a linear model cannot match both doses. The
  differences run in both directions and come from a 10-subject fed bioassay study; they do not by themselves argue
  for changing the model, and no repo location uses Dan's concentrations.
