# Dan 1997: ex vivo serum activity after oral clindamycin 300/600 mg

## Study
Randomised clinical PK/PD study in 20 otherwise-healthy adults admitted for minor elective
surgery (11 men, 9 women; mean age 39.3 y, range 21-52; mean weight 67.5 kg, range 50-90 kg;
normal liver/kidney function; no antibiotics in prior month). Single oral dose of clindamycin
(Upjohn), **300 mg (n=10)** or **600 mg (n=10)**, taken 2 h after a standard breakfast
(Methods, p.2). Serum sampled at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 h. Serum concentrations by
bioassay (6-mm disc, *Micrococcus luteus* ATCC 9341; lower limit 0.25 mg/L). Ex vivo serum
inhibitory and bactericidal titres against **5 isolates each of oxacillin-susceptible
*Staphylococcus aureus* and *Streptococcus pyogenes*** by NCCLS microdilution (serial 1:2–1:512,
triplicate; diluent 50% pooled human serum + 50% CSMH broth) (Methods, pp.2-3).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental PK model is fitted; the paper reports mean serum concentration–time data and
ex vivo titres only.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point = **ex vivo serum inhibitory / bactericidal titres** (greatest serum dilution that
inhibits / kills the test pathogen), measured at peak and at 6/8/12 h (Table 3, p.3). This is a
microbiology end point, not a clinical or EEG/analgesia end point. No ke0, tPeak(effect),
hysteresis, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio (not an opioid; clindamycin is an antibiotic).

Mean reciprocal serum inhibitory/bactericidal titres, 300/600 mg (Table 3, p.3):
- *S. aureus*: peak 13.7 / 23.8 (inhibitory titres only — footnote a: "Values for *S. aureus*
  represent serum inhibitory titers", i.e. **no bactericidal titre reported**); 6 h 2.6/3.9;
  8 h 0.4/1.2; 12 h 0/0.
- *S. pyogenes*: peak 15.1/7.5 and 34.7/16 (inhibitory/bactericidal); 6 h 4.6/2.7 and 6.8/3.7;
  8 h 1.8/0.7 and 2.1/1.2; 12 h 0/0.

Key qualitative finding (abstract p.1; Results p.3): bactericidal activity (≥99.9% kill) against
*S. aureus* was **not** shown in most serum samples — effective bacteriostatic (inhibitory)
activity only up to 6 h; against *S. pyogenes* both bacteriostatic and bactericidal activity were
present up to 6 h. Doubling the dose roughly doubled peak titres but not later-timepoint titres.

## Caveats
- Small n (10 per dose), single-dose, healthy surgical patients.
- Bioassay (microbiologic) concentrations, not chromatographic; lower limit 0.25 mg/L.
- "Most serum samples did not show bactericidal activity against *S. aureus*" is a qualitative
  summary; the authors attribute the contrast with other studies (Lemmen 1993, Baxter 1990) to the
  higher MICs of their *S. aureus* strains (mean 0.112 vs <0.06 mg/L elsewhere).
- Table 2 column header reads "ml/l"; from the abstract ("mean peak serum level was 3.4 mg/1 …
  4.8 mg/1") this is a misprint for mg/L.
