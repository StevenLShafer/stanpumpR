# Tomson 2007: levetiracetam in pregnancy, delivery, neonates and lactation

## Study
Prospective Swedish/Austrian case series: 14 women with epilepsy, 21-37 years, 15 pregnancies on levetiracetam
(monotherapy in 6, with other antiseizure drugs in 9: lamotrigine, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine/tiagabine/clobazam;
Table 1, PDF p2). Weight not reported. Not every pregnancy contributed every part:
- 7 pregnancies (6 women) with trough maternal plasma in each trimester (median gestational weeks 10, 20, 32) and a
  baseline at least 1 month after pregnancy (median 2 months postpartum); dose unchanged throughout in all 7.
- 5 more pregnancies with third-trimester and baseline only (dose changed after delivery in 2).
- 13 delivery maternal/cord pairs (not trough); neonatal capillary samples up to 48 h in 13; 11 mother-infant
  pairs during breast-feeding, 4-23 days postpartum (maternal sample 10-15 h after the last dose).
Assay: LC-MS on plasma (total; levetiracetam is unbound), detection limit 0.3 µmol/L, range 1-590 µmol/L, CV
13.2% at 5.9 µmol/L and 2.2% at 118 µmol/L. No PK model; concentrations and dose/concentration ratios only.

Fig. 1 (read off the image, approximate): mean concentrations about 37 µmol/L in the first and second trimesters,
27.5 in the third, 69.1 at baseline; the T1/T2 fall was a non-significant trend (Discussion, p5).
(derived: baseline apparent clearance 124.7 L/day = 5.2 L/h; dividing mg/day by µmol/L directly would give
values about six times smaller, so the paper evidently converted to mg/L, MW 170.2.)

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an analgesic; no ke0, no concentration-effect relation. Seizure control was collected retrospectively in the 7
complete pregnancies: 5 seizure-free throughout, 2 with seizures (both also on lamotrigine). No therapeutic range
stated.

## Covariates
Pregnancy stage only. Apparent clearance was not corrected for body weight (the authors note this may explain a
larger change than Pennell 2005's abstract, which corrected per kg).

## Caveats
Small n (7 for the concentration comparison, 12 for apparent clearance); trough single samples; half the
pregnancies on co-medication; baseline mostly postpartum (median 2 months), not pre-pregnancy. Mean of individual
dose/concentration ratios inflates the clearance ratio relative to the concentration ratio. Cord/maternal ratio
given as 1.09 in Results and 1.15 in abstract/Discussion (internal inconsistency; not used by stanpumpR).
Comparator for every "lower/higher" claim: the same women's own nonpregnant baseline.
