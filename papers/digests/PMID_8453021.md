# Curry 1993: IV, sublingual and aerosol nitroglycerin at cardiac catheterisation

## Study
Randomised parallel groups, 19 consecutive men with angina and documented ischaemia at diagnostic catheterisation
(Gainesville VA / University of Florida; PDF p2-3); anti-anginals stopped >= 24 h, SL nitroglycerin >= 2 h.
IV 0.1 mg (n 6; age 65 (SEM 6), weight 174 lb (~79 kg, derived), EF 49%), SL tablet 0.4 mg (n 7) or Nitrolingual
aerosol 0.4 mg (n 6) (Table 1, PDF p5). **The IV injection site and duration are not stated.** Femoral venous blood at
0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 15 min, tubes with p-hydroxymercuribenzoate; capillary GC-ECD (Noonan's method), CV < 10%
(PDF p3-4). Curve fitting by RSTRIP; IV two-compartment, SL/aerosol one-compartment with absorption.

7.80 min. **Source inconsistency (unresolved)**: the abstract says GTN was lowest after SL, and the text says aerosol

Cmax (0.38, 0.14) is far below either figure's peak of the means.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
LVEDP fell after all three routes; after IV it was significantly lower by 1 min and stayed lower (except at 5 min)
for 15 min (11.2 control to 7.2 at 1 min, Table 4); SBP minimally affected by IV (136 to 123 at 1 min); HR unchanged
(PDF p8-9). No concentration-effect model, no ke0.

## Covariates
None. The authors attribute their higher concentrations and lower clearance than earlier studies to patients
(raised LVEDP) rather than healthy volunteers (PDF p10).

## Caveats
Six IV patients, femoral venous sampling only to 15 min (beta half-life near the window), injection site unstated
(a 1-min sample may be close to mixing), means of individual fits that are mutually inconsistent, SL/aerosol figures,
table and text that disagree.
