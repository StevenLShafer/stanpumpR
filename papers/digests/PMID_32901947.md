# Cloesmeijer 2021: enantiomer-specific ketorolac PK, infants to adults

## Study
Pooled population PK analysis of 5 studies (Lynn 2011, Lynn 2007, Hamunen 1999, Ing Lorenzini 2011,
Välitalo 2017). 80 subjects: 33 infants (median age 0.83 y, IQR 0.56-1.03), 2 children (8 and 14 y),
45 adults (median 30 y, IQR 26-36), adults including women 4-5 months postpartum. Pooled body weight
56 kg [5.36-99] (Table 1; the bracket is labelled IQR but is evidently the range). Single IV racemic
ketorolac tromethamine: 0.5 or 1.0 mg/kg over 10 min (infants), 0.5 mg/kg, 20 mg or 30 mg over 30 s
(adults/children). 1020 plasma S and R concentrations (HPLC-UV in 4 studies, 2-D LC-MS in 1; LLOQ
0.005-0.025 ug/mL). Doses converted to free acid by molecular weight (376.409 g/mol tromethamine =
255.273 g/mol base). 16 S samples < LLOQ (11 < LOD; < LOD removed). NONMEM 7.3, FOCE-I, ADVAN13;
bootstrap n = 1000, pcVPC, NPDE.

## Model and parameters
S-ketorolac three-compartment, R-ketorolac two-compartment, fitted separately then combined for an omega
block. Allometry fixed a priori: clearances (W/70)^0.75, volumes W/70. Table 2, typical values per 70 kg
(RSE %):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD data collected. Uses an adult racemic analgesic EC50 of 0.37 mg/L (plasma, S + R, single dose)
cited from Mandema & Stanski 1996 (ref 1). Simulations (typical subjects, no IIV): at 0.37 mg/L racemate
mean S-ketorolac 0.057 mg/L in adults (48.6-99.6 kg, 30 mg tromethamine over 30 s) and 0.046 mg/L in
infants (5.3-10.6 kg, 0.5 mg/kg over 10 min), 19.3% lower; an infant racemic target of 0.41 mg/L gives
0.057 mg/L S. Adults fall to 0.37 mg/L at 4.5-6 h, infants at about 4 h (Fig 4). No ke0, no Emax/gamma.

## Covariates
Body weight only (fixed allometry). Age (linear, power) and a sigmoid maturation of CL not significant
(dOFV -10.8 for 4 parameters, Age50 RSE > 500%). Renal function, sex not reported as tested.

## Caveats
Only 2 children; 5 of 10 identified datasets unavailable. Single-dose data only; S:R ratio changes with
time and repeat dosing. Typical-subject simulations without IIV. Small bias at 4 and 6 h for S. Mixed
assays. IIV labelling inconsistency (Q3 vs V3) noted above.
