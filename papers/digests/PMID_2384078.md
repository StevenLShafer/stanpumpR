# Yerby 1990: total and free carbamazepine, phenytoin and phenobarbital through pregnancy

## Study
Prospective cohort (University of Washington, Seattle). **42 women with epilepsy**, 20-37 y (mean 28), 98%
Caucasian, enrolled before conception or in the first trimester, followed monthly to 8 weeks postpartum.
**Baseline = the 8th postpartum week** (no difference from pre-conception values in the third who had them).
Typically 2 samples in the first trimester, 4 in the second, 3 in the third, 1 at delivery, 2 postpartum. Free
and total concentrations (assay not described in this paper), weight; doses and seizures by diary. Means by
period compared with baseline by pooled t tests. Table I (p 3): carbamazepine 22 women (18 monotherapy),
phenytoin 14 (10), phenobarbital 11 (7); 83% on monotherapy. Table II: doses unchanged in 49%, raised in 38%
overall; phenytoin 43% none / **57% raised**; phenobarbital 55% / 27%; carbamazepine 50% / 32%. **The analysis
does not adjust for dose or weight** (Discussion p 4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis. Maternal vs cord at delivery (Table VIII): CBZ 4.69 / 1.22 vs 3.57 / 1.21
(total lower in the fetus, p <= 0.05); PHT 6.54 / 0.84 vs 6.53 / 1.05; PB 10.86 / 6.44 vs 8.49 / 6.29.

## Covariates
Period of pregnancy. The largest fall was in the first trimester for PHT and PB and the third for CBZ
(Table V). The authors argue the first-trimester fall excludes plasma-volume dilution as the cause.

## Caveats
No adjustment for the dose changes (38% raised) or weight; 8-week postpartum baseline; whole-number clearances
for phenobarbital; clearance units as printed are not a clearance; free assay not described; table N's
disagree. The authors recommend free-level monitoring.
