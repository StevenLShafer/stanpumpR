# Eriksen 1989: intranasal buprenorphine spray, bioavailability

## Study
Open crossover, 9 healthy volunteers (4 M, 5 F; age 18-44 y; weight 53-92 kg; height 162-195 cm; Table 1, PDF p.1). Intranasal (IN) dose first, IV a week later. Nominal 0.3 mg: three 0.05 mL strokes of a Pfeiffer pump, buprenorphine HCl 2 mg/mL in 5% dextrose, pH 5, one nostril. Actual IN dose by weighing the bottle: 0.23-0.33 mg (Table 2; mean about 0.28 mg, derived). IV dose matched to each subject's IN dose. Only 6 of 9 had the IV phase (2 excluded for earlier nausea/sedation, 1 for sampling difficulty). Plasma sampled 0-1440 min (16 times); radioimmunoassay (Bartlett 1980, Hand 1986), total plasma buprenorphine, duplicates. Non-compartmental: tmax and Cmax read from each subject, AUC by trapezoids to 720 min (12 h); 24-h samples near the assay limit so not used. Relative bioavailability = AUC0-720 IN / AUC0-720 IV per subject.

Mean IN curve peaks near 1.65 ng/mL at 15 min (below the 1.77 mean of individual maxima, as expected). Authors compare tmax: IM 5-10 min, sublingual about 200 min (Bullingham); IM availability 40-90%, sublingual 31-57.7%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect measure, no ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, MEAC or analgesic concentration, and no equianalgesic ratio. Assay: RIA, total plasma (not free, not whole blood).

## Covariates
None analysed. Dose varied by subject (0.23-0.33 mg) by weighing.

## Caveats
Spray dose accuracy was poor (authors blame dextrose crystallising); swallowed/aspirated fraction not excluded; large between-subject spread (F 33-86.5%, Cmax 0.64-4.78); IV curve only 6 subjects; no compartmental fit; only 12 h AUC. Cmax/tmax are means of individual maxima, not the peak of the mean curve.
