# Zhang 2003: simultaneous vs sequential population PK/PD analysis

## Study
Simulation study with NONMEM (FOCE with interaction). Data: one-compartment IV-bolus PK (single dose 200) and a direct Emax PD model, lognormal inter-individual variability, combined proportional and additive error. 200 replicates, each with its own design drawn by Latin hypercube sampling: 10-50 subjects, mean 2-7 PK and 2-7 PD samples per subject, times 0-10 (Table I, printed p.393). Four methods compared: SIM (joint fit), PPP (PD fit given population PK estimates), PPP&D (same, but PK data re-used), IPP (given individual Bayes PK estimates).

## Model and parameters
Simulated values (Table I, text layer, printed p.393): Cl 3.46, V 10, Emax 10 (fixed); C50 range 1-5; variance ranges 0.01-0.09 for the Cl, V, Emax and C50 random effects; 0.04-0.25 for noise variances (the row-to-parameter mapping is scrambled in the text layer). No real drug.

Results (text): sequential methods save 40-75% of CPU time (Fig 2); all runs converged (Table II); SIM has the smallest estimation error, PPP&D nearly as good, then IPP, PPP worst (Fig 3). Type I error of the nominal 5% likelihood ratio test, N=600 (Table III, printed p.400), percent, for a C50 difference in mean / in variance: SIM 3.8 / 2.2, PPP 3.2 / 2.5, PPP&D 3.5 / 2.3, IPP 4.2 / 3.5.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No real end point; no ke0, MEAC or potency ratio. Emax model with Emax and C50 only (no hysteresis). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Only a dichotomous dummy covariate on C50 for the likelihood ratio test.

## Caveats
Best case only: simulated and fitted models are identical, with no misspecification (a companion paper covers that). Time savings are an upper bound (footnote 7, printed p.401).
