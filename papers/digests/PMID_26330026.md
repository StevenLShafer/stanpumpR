# Kajbaf 2016: therapeutic concentrations of metformin, a systematic review

## Study
Systematic literature review (MEDLINE, Scopus, ScienceDirect, Wiley-Blackwell, January 1957 to November 2014,
plus Google and personal libraries; nearly 1000 publications screened). Keywords "metformin" with "therapeutic
concentration/level/range" and related wordings. Every value called "therapeutic" was extracted with the
cited source, the source's study type, and whether the citation matched the source (Fig. 2, PDF p15). Molar
values were converted with MW 129 g/mol: 10^-5 mol/L = 1.29 mg/L, 1 mg/L = 7.75 umol/L (PDF p13, printed
451; exact MW 129.1636).

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Results (PDF p13, printed 451; Table 2, PDF p16, confirmed on page images):
- 120 publications, 65 different "therapeutic" plasma concentrations or ranges.
- Single values ranged 0.129 to 90 mg/L; range boundaries 0 to 1800 mg/L; narrowest range 0.000225-0.003
  mg/L, broadest 150-1800 mg/L. 77% of values/ranges lay between 0.1 and 4 mg/L.
- 4 original research studies (3.3%) determined a therapeutic concentration (refs 8, 47, 80, 119), all
  measuring plasma (and in two, erythrocyte) metformin in diabetic patients on long-term treatment.
- 54 (45%) cited a supporting reference; 62 (51.7%) cited none; of the 54, citation concordant with the
  source in 23 (42.6%), discordant in 31 (57.4%).
- No study was designed to define a therapeutic concentration, and none related concentration to glycaemic
  control (PDF p15-16).

Reference values the review collects (PDF p15-16, page images; all plasma unless stated):
- Lalau/Lacroix 2003 [80], trough in well-tolerated long-term treatment, no renal failure: plasma 0.5 +/- 0.4
  mg/L, erythrocytes 0.8 +/- 0.4 mg/L (Table 3).
- Lalau 1995 [47]: 0.6 +/- 0.5 mg/L before drug intake (Table 1).
- US FDA label [71]: maximum plasma levels did not exceed 5 mg/L in controlled trials, even at maximum doses.
- TIAFT 2004 list [124]: serum "therapeutic" 1-4 mg/L, "toxic" >45 mg/L (Table 3; the text layer garbles the
  ">45"; confirmed on PDF p16).
- Cmax in healthy subjects about 8-16 umol/L (1-2 mg/L) [6]; overdose up to 2 mmol/L, which the paper converts
  to 210 mg/L [149] (PDF p15, printed 453), and elsewhere up to 267 mg/L [149] (PDF p14, printed 452,
  text layer only).
- In vitro: AMPK activated below 50 umol/L in animal hepatocytes; respiratory chain complex 1 IC50 0.45-1.2
  mmol/L (58-155 mg/L) in a human in vitro study (PDF p15).
- Authors' own proposal: on the basis of the few trough studies (Graham 2011 [8]), 2.5 mg/L "probably"
  corresponds to the upper limit of the therapeutic range; the lower limit is not established (PDF p16,
  printed 454).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-response data. The authors state that the only valid way to define the therapeutic window
would be to relate dose efficacy (blood glucose control) to plasma concentration in long-term treated
patients, and that this has never been done (PDF p15-16). They propose four categories (non-therapeutic,
therapeutic and safe, intermediate with risk of hyperlactataemia, excessive) without numeric cut-offs except
the 2.5 mg/L upper therapeutic limit. Table 3 limitations: accumulation need not cause hyperlactataemia, and
metformin can cause hyperlactataemia without accumulation; an 80-fold inter-individual variation in trough
steady-state concentration on 1 g twice daily over 24 months [164].

## Covariates
Not analysed. Noted: renal function (CKD raises trough concentration with severity [177]); solute-carrier
transporter polymorphisms.

## Caveats
- Assay matrix differs across cited values (plasma, serum, erythrocyte); peak and trough values are mixed in
  the literature, which the authors criticise.
- The 2.5 mg/L upper limit is a trough distribution from well-tolerated patients, not an efficacy or toxicity
  threshold.
- Internal arithmetic slip: 2 mmol/L x 129 g/mol = 258 mg/L (derived), not the 210 mg/L printed on PDF p15.
- Authors are from the Lalau group, whose own studies supply several of the four original-research values.
