# Jørgensen 1988: diazepam and oxazepam in first-trimester pregnancy

## Study
- 66 healthy women, 16-40 years (median 24), 9-12 weeks' gestation (median 10), requesting legal termination; Trondheim; ethics-approved (PDF p1-2, printed 493-494). Weight not reported.
- Single oral dose after overnight fast: diazepam 10 mg (24 women, given 1 h, 2.5 h or 4 h before surgery, n = 8 each) or oxazepam 25 mg (24 women, same scheme). From 18 patients (9 per drug) extra venous samples at 6, 8, 10, 12, 15 and 24 h. Thiopental induction (median 500 mg); amniotic fluid, placental tissue and maternal venous serum collected at surgery.
- Assay: HPLC-UV (254 nm), prazepam internal standard; detection limits diazepam 10 and oxazepam 25 (unit garbled in print, presumably µg/L / nmol/L; not used here). Serum = total drug.
- Analysis: noncompartmental. kel by unweighted log-linear regression of the terminal phase; t½ = ln2/kel; AUC by trapezoids; V = D·F/(AUC·kel), CL = D·F/AUC with F assumed 1 (so these are apparent oral V/F and CL/F). Medians with interquartile range.

## Model and parameters
Table I (PDF p4, printed 496), 0-24 h, N = 9 per drug, median (IQR), confirmed on the rendered page:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| diazepam 10 mg | 0.054 (0.042-0.062) | 12.5 (9-16) | 1998 (1578-2083) | 1.9 (1.1-2.2) | 1.4 (1.3-1.7) |
| oxazepam 25 mg | 0.070 (0.054-0.083) | 10 (6-13) | 6464 (4817-10278) | 0.7 (0.5-1.6) | 1.1 (0.7-1.3) |

Clearance unit: dose/AUC from the medians gives diazepam 10 mg / 1998 µg·h/L = 5.0 L/h = 83 mL/min and oxazepam 25 / 6464 = 3.9 L/h = 64 mL/min (derived; a median of ratios need not equal a ratio of medians). The printed 1.4 and 1.1 fit mL/min/kg at about 60 kg, not mL/min (see open_questions).

Table II (PDF p5, printed 497), 0-4 h, N = 48, median (IQR):
- Diazepam: maternal serum AUC0-4 389 (280-434) µg·h/L; placental tissue 118 (73-155); penetration 31.5% (25-36); amniotic fluid 0.
- Oxazepam: maternal serum 847 (770-1084); placenta 415.5 (363-541); penetration 49% (43-56); amniotic fluid 0.
- Results text (PDF p3): diazepam serum 178.5 µg/L at 1 h and 17.5 at 24 h, an unexplained median rise to 97 at 10 h; placental 57 and 18 µg/L over the 4 h. Oxazepam serum 229.5 at 1 h, peak 454 at 4 h, 62 at 24 h; placenta peak 120 at 2.5 h, 112.5 at 4 h. N-desmethyldiazepam near the detection limit in serum, absent in placenta and amniotic fluid.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured. The discussion (PDF p4) cites a minimum effective concentration of 0.1-1 µg/mL for diazepam and 1-2 µg/mL for oxazepam from a textbook (Ritschel 1986, ref 8), not measured here; no end point or population stated. No ke0, no potency ratio.

## Covariates
None examined.

## Caveats
- No non-pregnant control: "within the normal range for healthy adults" is judged against textbook ranges (refs 3, 4, 6), not a measured comparator. The authors exclude diazepam's half-life (12.5 h) from that claim and attribute it to 24-h sampling (PDF p3).
- Only 24 h of sampling for a drug with a 20-90 h half-life: kel is overestimated and AUC0-∞ underestimated, so CL/F is overestimated.
- Oral dosing, F assumed 1; n = 9 per drug for PK; weight not reported; clearance unit doubtful.
- Unexplained 10-h rise in diazepam concentration.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Registry row** (`pregnancyRegistry.csv:27`; `data-raw/pregnancyRegistry.R:334`): "Jorgensen 1988: 66 first-trimester women, oral 10-25 mg, parameters within the normal adult range." **Discrepancy (descriptive, no numeric effect):** 66 is the whole study; diazepam was 10 mg in 24 (+ probably 9) women and PK came from 9; 25 mg was oxazepam. The authors' "normal range" excludes the half-life. `n = 85` (line 338) counts the oxazepam women. `trimester = "third (term) and first"` and `comparator = "historical non-pregnant women"` are right for this paper. Nothing is applied (`cl_factor` NA), so the plotted curve does not depend on this paper.
- **Against the library's diazepam model** (`R/drugs_diazepam.R`, Hung 1996, not citing this paper): model CL 27 mL/min (0.34 mL/min/kg at 80.5 kg), Vss 1.23 L/kg, terminal t½ 45 h, oral F 0.94. This paper's medians give CL/F about 83 mL/min (derived, about 3x), apparent V 1.9 L/kg (Varea-type, not Vss) and t½ 12.5 h. The direction (faster clearance, shorter half-life) is mostly the 24-h sampling artefact the authors name, so this paper is not evidence that pregnancy raises diazepam clearance; the registry's "unchanged" with no factor is a fair reading.
- **Oxazepam:** no oxazepam model in the library (no `R/drugs_oxazepam.R`, not in `drugDefaults_global.csv`); nothing to compare.
