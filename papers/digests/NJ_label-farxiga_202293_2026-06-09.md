# FARXIGA label 2026: dapagliflozin

## Study
US prescribing information for dapagliflozin 5 mg and 10 mg film-coated tablets (as dapagliflozin propanediol), an
SGLT2 inhibitor, initial US approval 2014; "Revised: 06/2026", recent major changes to 2.5 and 5.3 (06/2026). The
document is what its name says (Highlights page 1 names FARXIGA; the URL's s035 supplement).

Indications (1, PDF p. 3): CKD at risk of progression; heart failure; hHF risk in type 2 diabetes with CV disease or
risk factors; glycaemic control in adults and children aged 10 and older with type 2 diabetes. Not recommended for
glycaemic control in type 1 diabetes or with eGFR < 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, nor for CKD in polycystic kidney disease or on
immunosuppression for kidney disease.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Dosing (2.2-2.5, PDF pp. 3-4): glycaemic control start 5 mg PO once daily, may increase to
10 mg; all other indications 10 mg once daily. eGFR >= 45 (glycaemic) or >= 25 (other indications): no dose change;
initiation not recommended below 25; may continue below 25. Withhold at least 3 days before surgery with prolonged
fasting. Missed dose: take as soon as possible, do not double.

Cmax and AUC dose proportional over the therapeutic range. Metabolism mainly UGT1A9; CYP minor. Renal impairment
(20 mg daily for 7 days, type 2 diabetes): exposure +45%, +100%, +200% in mild, moderate, severe impairment; 24-h
urinary glucose excretion 42%, 80%, 90% lower. Hepatic impairment: Child-Pugh A/B Cmax up to +12%, AUC up to +36%;
Child-Pugh C up to +40% and +67% (single 10 mg; PDF p. 20). Population PK: age, sex, race and body weight have no
clinically meaningful effect (p. 19). Paediatric 10-17 y PK and glucosuria similar to adults with the same renal
function. Interactions (Table 5, p. 21): rifampin AUC -22%, mefenamic acid AUC +51%; others within 0.80-1.25.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Glucosuria about 70 g/day at week 12 on 5 or 10 mg in type 2 diabetes; near-maximal at 20 mg/day; returns toward
baseline about 3 days after stopping 10 mg (12.2, PDF p. 17). No QTc effect up to 150 mg/day or 500 mg single dose.
No ke0, no therapeutic or toxic concentration range. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Renal function (exposure up, glucosuria down); hepatic impairment (modest exposure increase). No dose change for age,
sex, race or weight.

## Caveats
- The 12.9 h is a mean terminal half-life after one 10 mg dose; the label does not say in whom (healthy or patients).
- Label summary statements, not a model; the population PK analysis behind them is not described here.
