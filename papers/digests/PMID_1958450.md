# Pöyhiä 1991: IV oxycodone kinetics in young surgical adults

## Study
Open single-dose study (short report). n = 9 otherwise healthy appendicectomy patients (4 M / 5 F), age 27 ± 5.3 y
(19-37), weight 71 ± 11 kg (57-90), height 175 cm (Table 1). Studied on the first postoperative morning, 13.1 ± 3.9 h
after anaesthesia (premedication pethidine 1 mg/kg IM; thiopentone or propofol, enflurane/N2O, alfentanil or fentanyl).
Dose: "Oxycodone chloride (Oxanest, 10 mg ml-1, Leiras, Finland) ... in a dose of 0.07 mg-1 kg-1 (= 0.05 mg-1 kg-1
free base)" (PDF p1, printed 516; the superscript -1 on mg is the paper's own typo). **Oxanest is oxycodone
hydrochloride trihydrate** (405.87 g/mol, base fraction 0.7770), so the two numbers are one dose stated twice:
0.07 x 0.7770 = 0.0544, which is "0.05" rounded. Table 1's dose column (3.1-4.7 mg, mean 3.8) is the BASE contained
in round injected volumes of that product - 0.40, 0.525, 0.55 and 0.60 mL of 10 mg/mL, i.e. 4.0, 5.25, 5.5 and
6.0 mg of trihydrate, which x 0.7770 give 3.108, 4.079, 4.274 and 4.662 and round to exactly the 3.1 / 4.1 / 4.3 / 4.7
of the column. That is a mean 0.0679 mg/kg of trihydrate (0.0645-0.0705), i.e. the nominal 0.07. Venous samples 5 min
to 12 h (15 samples). Assays: oxycodone GC,
LLOQ 3 ng/mL; noroxycodone GC, limit 1 ng/mL; oxymorphone HPLC-coulometric, limit 0.5 ng/mL. Individual bi- (6) or
tri-exponential (3: subjects 1, 5, 7) fits by nonlinear least squares (Systat), equal weights, AIC; t½z, Vss, CL by
standard (Wagner) methods; AUC(0-12) by linear trapezoids.

Individual CL ranged 0.38-1.33 L/min and t½z 110-580 min (Table 1). No kinetic parameter correlated with age, weight
or duration of anaesthesia. Oxymorphone was never detected (< 0.5 ng/mL).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured: no MEAC, no ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak, no hysteresis. The introduction says oxycodone is regarded as similar
in potency to morphine (qualitative, cited); no equianalgesic ratio is given. Discussion: longer t½ than morphine
reflects lower clearance, volumes being similar.

## Covariates
None identified (no correlation with age, body weight, anaesthesia duration; n = 9).

## The dose basis, and what is inferred
Settled by **Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-29**: Oxanest is the trihydrate, and Pöyhiä computed CL and Vss on the base.
The paper states neither. What is read and what is inferred, kept apart:

- **Read** (PDF p1): the product, the strength, "0.07 ... (= 0.05 ... free base)", and (p2) "Values of elimination
  half-life, steady-state volume of distribution and clearance were calculated by standard methods (Wagner, 1976)".
- **Read** (Table 1, p2): weights 75 78 90 80 76 61 62 61 57 kg; Dose 4.1 4.3 4.7 4.3 4.1 3.1 3.1 3.1 3.1 mg;
  CL 0.59 1.33 1.22 0.66 0.38 0.82 0.92 0.64 0.45 L/min; Vss 2.12 2.44 2.83 2.40 3.65 2.74 2.84 2.56 1.83 L/kg.
- **Inferred**: that CL was computed on that dose column. The text says only "standard methods", and the column is
  the only dose the paper prints. The round-volume reconstruction above is what identifies the column as base in
  trihydrate: on anhydrous hydrochloride, 0.07 mg/kg of these patients would need 3.99-6.30 mg, not 3.1-4.7; on the
  paper's own implied 0.05/0.07 = 0.714 it would need 4.35-6.6.
- **Cannot be decided from Figure 1**: the mean curve gives an AUC of roughly 5.9 mg.min/L reading to the 6 h point
  with a terminal tail, and about 7.6 using the 12 h point - which carries no SD bar and is likely censored at the
  3 ng/mL assay limit. Predicted mean(dose/CL) is 5.58 on the base column and 7.16 if the doses were trihydrate.
  The figure spans both, so it discriminates nothing.

**Conversion to the app's basis.** stanpumpR doses anhydrous hydrochloride (351.83 g/mol), so a base-computed CL and
Vss are multiplied by 351.83/315.37 = **1.1156**: CL 0.779 -> 0.869 L/min, Vss 2.60 -> 2.90 L/kg.

**Say which average.** Per kilogram the answer depends on how the nine patients are pooled, and the two conventions
differ in the third digit, so the card states both rather than picking one silently:

- **ratio of means**, mean(CL) / mean(weight) = 0.77889 / 71.111 = 10.95 -> **12.22 mL/min/kg**. This is what
  `R/drugs_oxycodone.R` and `test-drugs-oxycodone.R` use, and what the paper's own rounded means (0.78 / 71) give.
- **mean of ratios**, mean(CL_i / w_i) = 10.93 -> **12.20 mL/min/kg**.

The same applies to the dose: mean(d_i / w_i) over the nine tabulated doses is 0.0529 mg/kg of base, while
sum(d) / sum(w) = 33.9 / 640 is 0.0530, and reconstructing from the round volumes before rounding gives 0.0527. None of this
matters at the precision the model is fitted to; it matters that two cards or two tests quoting "Pöyhiä's
clearance per kilogram" can differ by 0.2% for no reason anyone can find later.

## Caveats
Small n; postoperative patients ~13 h after general anaesthesia with other opioids; GC assay LLOQ 3 ng/mL limits the
terminal phase; mixed bi/tri-exponential fits; CL reported in absolute L/min while Vss is per kg. The dose basis above
is an inference from the dose column, not a statement by the authors.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/drugs_oxycodone.R, the dose-basis conversion: discrepancy, correction in flight.** At master 50fdf531 the model
  rescales each patient's CL and Vss by 0.07 x weight / tabulated dose, a mean factor of 1.33, giving CL
  14.5 mL/min/kg and Vss 3.46 L/kg. That factor treats the tabulated dose as the base and the 0.07 mg/kg as

  351.83/315.37 = 1.1156, giving **CL 12.22 mL/min/kg and Vss 2.90 L/kg** on the model's own ratio-of-means pooling
  (12.20 as a mean of per-patient ratios; see above). The 1.33 is superseded. The model change
