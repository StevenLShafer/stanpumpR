# Lennertz 2022: connected consciousness after intubation in young adults

## Study
- International prospective cohort, 10 hospitals (US, Israel, Belgium, Australia, Germany, New Zealand); NCT03503357. Planned 500, stopped early for funding; 344 enrolled, 338 analysed (age mean 30, SD 6.3 y, 18-40 y; 232 female = 69%). PDF p. 1-3.
- Anaesthesia at the discretion of the attending (not standardised); rapid-sequence inductions excluded. Most subjects got propofol for induction (37/37 responders and 300/301 non-responders per the Table 1 text, so about 99.7%); median propofol about 2.1-2.3 mg/kg total body weight (female 2.1, IQR 1.8-2.6; male 2.3, IQR 1.8-2.8; difference -0.2, 95% CI -0.3 to 0.0; p. 3).
- Isolated forearm technique (cuff inflated before neuromuscular blocker): commands and syllable-matched nonsense statements read from 10 s after intubation; a hand squeeze counted as a response. "Continuous anaesthesia" = volatile, propofol infusion or repeated propofol boluses between induction and laryngoscopy. Assessor not blinded.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic model. No drug concentrations, effect-site estimates or end-tidal agent values were measured.

Outcome numbers (p. 1, 3-4): responses 37/338 = 11% (95% CI 8-15); female 31/232 (13%) vs male 6/106 (6%); adjusted OR female vs male 2.7 (1.1-7.6, p = 0.022); continuous anaesthesia OR 0.43 (0.20-0.96; p = 0.041 in the abstract, 0.043 in the results text, a printed inconsistency); age OR 1.1 per SD (0.60). Command-only responders 13, nonsense-only 4, both 7 (McNemar p = 0.049). 18/37 responders (49%) endorsed pain. Possible explicit recall 1.5%, definite 0.3%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not applicable: behavioural endpoint (hand squeeze) only. The time between induction and laryngoscopy and the median doses were similar in responders and non-responders (p. 3), and the authors state that dosing differences, if present, were small and do not explain the sex difference (p. 5). No MEAC, ke0, tPeak, or potency ratio reported. Not an opioid paper beyond listing induction opioids (alfentanil, fentanyl, remifentanil, sufentanil) as covariates in Table 1.

## Covariates
Mixed-effects logistic regression (site random effect) with sex, age, continuous anaesthetic exposure (Table 2). Female sex increased and continuous exposure decreased the odds of responding.

## Caveats
Assessed only after intubation; observational, early termination, unblinded assessors; mostly propofol induction; heterogeneous responses; a negative response is ambiguous. The text gives the continuous-exposure p-value as 0.041 and 0.043 in different places (page 1 versus page 3 text layer).
