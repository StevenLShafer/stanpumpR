# Porter 2001: ropivacaine protein binding and placental transfer in labour

## Study
Prospective, uncontrolled (no non-epidural arm). 15 ASA I multiparous women at term having induction of labour
(ARM plus syntocinon) and requesting epidurals were recruited; data complete in 10 (4 unsuitable for induction, 1
delivered within 1 h of ARM) (PDF p 3, printed 420). Table 1 (mean (SD), n = 10): age 32.7 (2.2) y, weight 77.1
(10.7) kg, gestation 39.2 (0.9) wk, birth weight 3.7 (0.4) kg, total ropivacaine dose 114.4 mg [range 57.4-146],
duration of epidural 253 (105.3) min, duration of labour (ARM to delivery) 389 (134.7) min, Apgar 8.7 (1.5) at 1 min
and 9.9 (0.32) at 5 min.

Dosing (PDF p 2, printed 419): 5 mL 0.2% (10 mg), then 5 mL 0.1% (5 mg) twice at 5-min intervals with position
changes, then infusion 0.1% at 10 mL/h (10 mg/h) until delivery; top-ups of 5 mL 0.2% (10 mg) or 5 mL 0.75% (37.5
mg) on request. Venous samples for AAG pre-induction, 1 h after infusion start and at delivery; maternal total and
unbound ropivacaine at 1 h and at delivery; umbilical venous (UV) AAG and total ropivacaine at delivery. Serum
(plain tubes).

Assays: AAG by radio-immunodiffusion (Hitachi 704, Dako antiserum), LLOQ 4.25 umol/L, range 4.25-43.8 umol/L, CV 5% at
15.5 and 10% at 43.8 umol/L. Total and unbound (ultrafiltration) ropivacaine by HPLC (JASCO); standards 0.1-2.0
mg/mL total and 0.02-0.2 mg/mL unbound as printed (presumably ug/mL; unit not relevant to the repo); CV 6.5% at 0.2
and 2.3% at 2.0. Neonates scored by NACS at 30 min, 2 h and 16 h. Statistics: paired t-tests, simple linear
regression, Spearman.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect model. Analgesia rated excellent in 8, satisfactory in 2. Bromage motor block (Table 2):
grade 1/2/3 in 8/2/0 at 60 min and 0/6/4 at delivery. No hypotension. NACS (Table 4): < 35 in 9/10 at 30 min (median
33.5 [28-40]), 3/10 at 2 h (37 [27-40]), 0/7 at 16 h (39 [38-40]). No association of UV ropivacaine with NACS (r²
0.3, 0.16, -0.035 at 30 min, 2 h, 16 h). Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Binding: maternal AAG was associated with 1/free fraction at 60 min (r² 0.77, Fig. 1) but not at delivery (r² 0.002,
Fig. 2). No correlation between maternal unbound ropivacaine (MV) and UV ropivacaine (r² printed as -0.03, Fig. 3)
or UV/MV (r² 0.26). AAG did not rise between induction and delivery under epidural analgesia. The discussion cites
Haram 1983 (ref 9) for term AAG 12.5 (6) against non-pregnant 18 (5.3), printed there as mmol/L.

## Caveats
- n = 10, no control group, two sampling times; no non-pregnant comparison of binding from this study itself.
- Figure 1 (read on the rendered page): of about 8 points, one at AAG about 13 umol/L with 1/free fraction about 55;
  the rest lie at 1/free fraction below 8. The r² 0.77 appears driven by that single point (my reading of the plot,
  not an author statement).
- Mean unbound fractions 0.14-0.19 are far above the 0.06-0.08 usually quoted; the authors do not comment.
- Negative r² values are printed (Fig. 3, Table 4), an impossibility for a squared coefficient; probably r.
- Units: the AAG values from Haram are printed as mmol/L, elsewhere umol/L; the ropivacaine standard ranges read
  mg/mL. Printing slips, not used by the repo.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:17, "AAG falls in pregnancy"**: Porter's own data show no fall during labour (14 -> 12
  umol/L, not significant); the pregnancy fall rests on Haram 1983 as cited by Porter (12.5 vs 18 at term vs
  non-pregnant). Consistent if read as Porter citing Haram. **matches** (as a cited statement).
- **"Porter 2001 showed the free fraction of ropivacaine tracks 1/AAG"**: overstated. The association (AAG vs
  1/free fraction) was found at 60 min only (r² 0.77, apparently driven by one point) and was absent at delivery
  (r² 0.002); Porter also found no link between unbound drug and placental transfer. **discrepancy** (wording only:
  the row applies no factor, cl_factor NA, so no plotted curve is affected).
- **Comparator check**: Porter has no non-pregnant arm, so it cannot support a pregnant-vs-non-pregnant binding
  ratio; the row's notes ("Unbound fraction likely higher at term (lower AAG)") rest on Haram via Porter, not on
  Porter's measurements. The row correctly applies no factor.
- **Library model** (`R/drugs_ropivacaine.R`, Simon 2006 IV tracer, older adults, total plasma, F = 1 epidural/RA
  depot): Porter gives no clearance or volume, and its epidural regimen (boluses, infusion, top-ups with individual
  timing not reported) does not allow a model-vs-observed comparison. Not compared. The model header already notes
  that AAG rises raise total but not unbound concentration; Porter's high unbound fractions are a reminder that the
  plotted total understates unbound exposure at term.
