# Tomson 1994a: seizure control and free and total carbamazepine and phenytoin in pregnancy

## Study
Prospective, population-based study, Soder Hospital, Stockholm (urban area of 400,000; 0.24% of live births): 93
pregnancies in 70 women on AEDs from the start of pregnancy, seen before gestational week 20 (median inclusion week 11,
range 1-19) (PDF p2). Ages at delivery 21-44 y (median 29). 77 on monotherapy; Table 3 (PDF p3, rendered): carbamazepine
50 cases (41 monotherapy), phenytoin 36 (29 monotherapy), clonazepam 11, ethosuximide 7, phenobarbital 6, primidone 4,
valproate 3. Dose kept constant unless seizure control prompted a change (16 cases changed, 15 for seizure control);
low or falling levels were not a reason to raise the dose (PDF p3). Monthly visits; trough samples before the morning
dose; baseline at least 10 weeks postpartum (no prepregnancy levels). Total and free (ultrafiltration) carbamazepine and
phenytoin by HPLC; units uM.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Seizure frequency against the 9 (and 3) prepregnancy months: unaltered in 61%, improved 24%, worse 15% of 89 cases;
worse more often in localization-related (29%) than generalized idiopathic epilepsy (7%) (PDF p3). Table 6 (PDF p5):
on monotherapy, patients whose seizures increased had higher total and free phenytoin (44.5 vs 20.3 and 5.2 vs 2.4 uM)
and higher free carbamazepine, reflecting higher baseline levels; the fall from baseline did not differ (total 41% vs
39%, free 31% vs 32%, to the lowest value in pregnancy; PDF p5).

## Covariates
None modelled.

## Caveats
Postpartum (not prepregnancy) baseline; trimester means pool different numbers of samples per woman; no samples at
delivery. Internal inconsistencies: the abstract says total carbamazepine was slightly lower in the third trimester,
the Results and Table 5 say second and third; the French summary says first. The stated free phenytoin 82% differs
from the Table 5 means (84%); the source of the 82% is not given.
