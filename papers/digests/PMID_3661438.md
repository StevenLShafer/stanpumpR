# Abernethy 1987: labetalol in young and elderly hypertensive patients

## Study
Twenty patients with uncomplicated essential hypertension (sitting DBP 95-114 mmHg), off drugs at least 2 weeks:
10 young (32 +/- 6 y, 95 +/- 22 kg, 8 M/2 F) and 10 elderly (67 +/- 4 y, 84 +/- 17 kg, 8 M/2 F) (Table I, PDF p3).
Randomized two-way crossover, at least 1 week apart: 50 mg labetalol hydrochloride IV by 10-min constant infusion, and
200 mg orally after an overnight fast (PDF p2). Venous samples at 5, 15, 30, 45, 60 min and 2, 3, 6, 8, 10, 24, 32,
48 h after the IV dose (4 h added and 5 min dropped after oral); HR and BP sitting and standing at each sample.
HPLC with electrochemical detection. Post-infusion data fitted to 2 or 3 exponentials (weighted nonlinear least
squares), corrected for the infusion period; half-life, Varea, Vss, MRT, CL by moment/coefficient methods (PDF p2).
Linear PD model E - E0 = S x C (PDF p2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- IV, sitting: max SBP fall -25 +/- 13 (young) vs -39 +/- 8 mmHg (elderly, P < 0.05); DBP -17 vs -18; HR -17 +/- 11 vs
  -11 +/- 8 beats/min (Table I). BP returned to baseline within 30 min; HR stayed down for 3 h (PDF p3).
- Oral, standing: SBP -16 vs -41 mmHg (P < 0.05) (Table I).
- Linear slopes (oral SBP): 0.326 +/- 0.490 (young; printed "326" in Table I, 0.326 in the abstract) vs
  0.928 +/- 1.05 mmHg per ng/mL (NS). HR slope after IV: 0.406 +/- 0.303 vs 0.176 +/- 0.063 beats/min per ng/mL
  (P < 0.05); elderly also right-shifted (Fig. 5). IV BP-concentration modelling not possible (falls transient and small).
- No ke0 or effect compartment.

## Covariates
Age (group comparison only): lower CL, longer half-life and MRT in the elderly; no regression.

## Caveats
Ten per group, groups differ in weight (95 vs 84 kg); per-kg values; 50 mg dose, salt (hydrochloride) basis stated.
