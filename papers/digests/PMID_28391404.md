# Dallmann 2017: pregnancy PBPK model for renally cleared cephalosporins

## Study
In silico study: population PBPK models (PK-Sim/MoBi 7.0, Open Systems Pharmacology) for cefazolin, cefuroxime
and cefradine, first in non-pregnant women (PK-Sim 18-organ standard structure, fitted to literature
non-pregnant/postpartum data), then switched to a 27-compartment pregnancy structure (9 extra: breasts,
myometrium, endometrium, maternal and fetal placenta, fetus, umbilical arterial/venous blood, amniotic fluid)
parameterised from the authors' gestation-dependent physiology meta-analysis (ref 19). Drug parameters were
NOT refitted for pregnancy; tubular secretion rate kept at the non-pregnant value; fetal elimination ignored.
fu in pregnancy scaled from albumin/AAG concentration assuming a constant association constant (Eq. 11).
Virtual populations n = 2000 each (Table 2).

Comparison data (Table 2, p. 4, rendered; all from the literature, none collected here):
- Cefazolin: Philipson 1987 [29], 500 mg IV (sodium salt), n = 6, longitudinal (pregnancy and postpartum),
  GA median 24.5 weeks (19-33). Simulated non-pregnant weight 62.5 kg (53.4-72.7), pregnant 66.8 (60.1-76.2).
- Cefuroxime: Philipson & Stiernstedt [35], 750 mg IV, n = 7, GA 13 (11-35) and 41 (37-42); Bousfield [36],
  1500 mg IV, n = 10, term only.
- Cefradine: Philipson [29], 500 mg IV (GA 15) and oral (GA 20), n = 12, longitudinal.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK-PD model. Only PD statement: cefuroxime 750 mg IV falls below an E. coli MIC50 of 4 mg/L at 3.4 h
(non-pregnant) vs 2.5 h (GA 13) in simulation (p. 11). Not an opioid paper; no ke0.

## Covariates
Gestational age, through the meta-analysis physiology (GFR, renal blood flow, kidney volume, organ volumes,
hematocrit, albumin/AAG). The authors attribute the higher clearance in pregnancy (against the same women
postpartum, or a non-pregnant simulation) to increased fu, GFR, renal blood flow and kidney volume, not to
increased transporter (OAT1/3, MRP4, MATE1) activity (pp. 13-14). GFR SD doubles 18 to 36 mL/min at term
(p. 11).

## Caveats
- Cefazolin rests on one comparison study of 6 women at mid-pregnancy (GA 19-33), bioassay; no term or
  caesarean data for cefazolin.
- PBPK under-predicts non-pregnant cefazolin CL (ratio 0.84) but not pregnant (0.98), so its predicted pregnancy
  CL ratio (1.83) overstates the observed 1.57 (derived).
- Total concentrations only; no unbound cefazolin is reported, though fu is a model input.
- Funded by and largely authored at Bayer (PK-Sim developer). ESM (fu table, scaling factors) not read.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `pregnancyRegistry.csv:49` claim that Dallmann's PBPK model reproduces the pregnancy data: Table 4 cefazolin
  predicted/observed ratios 0.88 (AUC), 0.98 (CL), 1.06 (Vd) at GA 24.5; within 2-fold throughout. **matches**.
  "Not used for numbers" is also true: the factor (creatinine clearance x 1.5, unbound CL x 1.27) comes from
  Ansari 2016 (`R/drugs_cefazolin.R:75-94`), not from this paper.
- The same row's Philipson figures (CL 7.30 vs 4.66 L/h; volume 11.7 vs 11.6 L; 6 women at 19-33 weeks; AUC 31%
  smaller) agree with this paper's Table 2 and Table 4 transcription of Philipson (derived AUC ratio 0.69).
  Half-life and significance are not given here, so not checked from this paper.
- Comparator: Philipson's 1.57 is total clearance against the same women postpartum. The library applies its
  1.27 to Komatsu 2024's UNBOUND clearance (`R/drugs_cefazolin.R:146`). Dallmann attributes part of the
  total-clearance rise to a higher fu, which would not raise unbound clearance; so a smaller factor on unbound
  CL fits the direction of this paper, as the registry note already says. Dallmann also finds GFR and renal blood
  flow alone insufficient without kidney enlargement (p. 14), but reports no unbound clearance, so the paper
  cannot test the 1.27. **cannot verify** (the factor's size), not a discrepancy.
- Independent model comparison (`R/drugs_cefazolin.R:144-149`, Komatsu: CLu 29.3 L/h at CrCL 70, Vc 36.6 L,
  Vp 42.2 L, unbound): not directly comparable with Philipson's total CL 4.66 L/h and Vd 11.6 L (bioassay,
  total drug, 500 mg). With Table 1's fu 0.11 held constant the total CL would imply ~42 L/h unbound (derived:
  4.66/0.11), but cefazolin binding is saturable (Komatsu's own binding relationship), so this is only an order-
  of-magnitude check: same order as the library's 29.3 L/h.
- Cefuroxime and cefradine: no library model (`R/drugs_cef*.R` holds cefalexin, cefazolin, cefepime,
  ceftriaxone only).
