# Rudkin 1992: patient-controlled propofol vs patient-controlled midazolam

## Study
Randomised comparison, 40 fasted, unpremedicated ASA 1 day-surgery patients (n=20 per group; 5 males propofol, 4 midazolam; age about 21-23 y) having third-molar extraction under local anaesthesia, one surgeon blinded by a screen.
- Every patient first received fentanyl 0.7 ug/kg IV (anaesthetist-given, before the local anaesthetic).
- Patient-controlled sedation (PCS) pump: Ohmeda 9000 syringe pump driven by a PC; bolus 2 ml over 6 s at 1200 ml/h; lockout 1 min measured from the end of the bolus.
- Propofol 10 mg/ml: 20 mg per bolus. Midazolam diluted to 0.25 mg/ml: 0.5 mg per bolus.
- Pump accuracy: delivered volume exceeded calculated by 4.3% (SD 0.14); mean bolus 2.08 ml (SD 0.06, n=69).
- Demands (Table 5): successful 8.0 (4.2) propofol vs 14.0 (6.1) midazolam; unsuccessful 3.2 (4.7) vs 19.1 (21.6). The abstract gives 8.0 (4.4)/2.8 (4.1) and 14.0 (6.3)/17.6 (19.8), which differ slightly from Table 5.
- Total dose (Table 5): propofol 2.72 (1.37) mg/kg; midazolam 0.11 (0.05) mg/kg.
- Table 3 compares with the earlier slow pump (0.7 mg/kg bolus at 100 ml/h): successful 6.4 (3.6), unsuccessful 34.8 (52.3), ratio 0.2, against 2.9 here.
- Tests: delayed free recall of 9 pictured objects and FAST index accuracy, pre-op, at 10 min in recovery and at discharge; day-1 telephone follow-up.
- No blood sampling, no assay, no PK modelling.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio from concentrations. End points are sedation depth (five-point scale, Table 2), demand counts, recall and FAST accuracy.
- Deepest sedation: most patients in both groups reached eyes closed but rousable to command; 2 midazolam patients needed mild physical stimulus (Fig 2, p378).
- Onset: the authors say time from demand to felt effect was 35-40 s for propofol and not reliably measurable for midazolam; they cite the literature (ref 10) for maximal EEG change 5-10 min after sedative midazolam doses (p380). That is a citation, not a result of this study.
- Table 6 recall, mean (SD) objects: propofol 7.2 (1.3) pre, 3.8 (2.5) post 1, 4.1 (2.0) post 2; midazolam 7.1 (1.3), 0.9 (2.3), 2.9 (2.6).
- Table 7 FAST accuracy %: propofol 87.4 (6.7), 85.0 (9.8), 82.8 (10.2); midazolam 88.8 (6.5), 80.3 (10.2), 81.7 (10.2).
- Group differences p<0.001 (repeated-measures ANOVA). Recall was dose-dependent for propofol only; FAST had no dose dependence in either group.
- Satisfaction did not differ (19 vs 18 "good").
- Opioid audit: fentanyl 0.7 ug/kg was a fixed premedication for all; no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Age, sex, surgical difficulty, predrug score, test time, midazolam and propofol dose were entered; nonsignificant ones were dropped (equations 1-4, unconfirmed).

## Caveats
Anaesthetist not blinded; fixed bolus sizes chosen by the authors, not matched for equieffect, so propofol and midazolam are not compared at equipotent doses; 17 midazolam patients recalled none of the 9 pictures at the first test, a floor effect; groups differ in dose-response range.
