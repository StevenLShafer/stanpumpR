# Golden 1988: bupropion metabolites and clinical outcome in depression

## Study
- NIMH clinical research unit; 11 depressed patients (RDC unipolar or bipolar I/II) entered, 1 dropped (psychosis,
  no follow-up sample), so 10 patients and 12 drug trials (patients 7 and 10 studied twice). Ages 26-75; mostly women
  (Table 1).
- Bupropion HCl capsules from 150 mg/d (divided 9 am / 6 pm), titrated to 300-500 mg/d (final 225-500 mg/d, Table 2),
  3-6 weeks. One patient also on lithium, levothyroxine and furosemide.
- Plasma at 9 am, about 15 h after the evening dose (morning dose held); lumbar CSF in 6 patients (4 responders,
  2 nonresponders) at the same time. HPLC-UV (214 nm) for bupropion, hydroxybupropion (HB), threohydrobupropion (TB),
  erythrohydrobupropion (EB); CV <= 5% within-day, < 10% between-day.
- Response: HRS fall >= 10 points or 50% and Bunney-Hamburg fall >= 3 into the normal range. Nonresponse: no such
  change by 4 weeks, or psychosis requiring discontinuation. Wilcoxon rank-sum; Pearson correlations.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: clinical response (HRS + Bunney-Hamburg) at 3-6 weeks; 7 responders, 5 nonresponders (12 trials).
- Bupropion itself: responders 21.7 ± 3.1 vs nonresponders 41.2 ± 17.1 ng/mL (P > .50), no relation.
- Metabolites higher in nonresponders: HB 1771.3 ± 144.8 vs 994.5 ± 68.9 ng/mL (P < .006); EB 128.9 ± 16.2 vs
  82.6 ± 8.2 (P < .04); TB 769.2 ± 121.2 vs 378.0 ± 47.4 (P < .04). HB > 1,250 ng/mL in all nonresponders and
  < 1,200 ng/mL in all responders (complete separation).
- Plasma HB correlated with post-treatment plasma HVA (r = .71, P < .05).
- Interpretation offered: a toxic threshold, or a curvilinear (nortriptyline-like) concentration-response.
- Mean dose did not differ (371 ± 33 vs 395 ± 40 mg/d). No EC50, ke0 or tPeak. Not an opioid; no MEAC.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Very small (10 patients, 12 trials), 4 of 5 nonresponders were psychotic reactions; single 15-h trough samples;
  1980s HPLC-UV with known bupropion instability in plasma; unblinded titration.
- An inverse association, not a validated therapeutic window; not replicated here.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Conclusion (no validated concentration-response, hence no effect site): supported; Golden's finding is a small,
  inverse, unreplicated association and the parent showed no relation. **matches** in substance.
- Wording "responder contrast was tiny": the paper's contrast in hydroxybupropion was large (1771 vs 995 ng/mL,
  P < .006, complete separation at about 1,200-1,250 ng/mL); what was tiny was the sample (10 patients), and the
  direction was inverse (higher metabolite, worse outcome). **discrepancy (characterisation)** at
  `R/drugs_bupropion.R:100`; suggest "Golden 1988's sample was tiny and its association inverse".
- Of note for the AGNP band (850-1500 ng/mL on the hydroxybupropion row): Golden's nonresponders all had trough HB
  above 1,250 ng/mL, inside the upper part of that band. Not a repo error; context only.
