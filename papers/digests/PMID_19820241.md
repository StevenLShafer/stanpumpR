# Johnson 2010: remifentanil-sevoflurane response surface at emergence

## Study
- Part I (re-analysis of the earlier volunteer data of Manyam 2006, ref 2): the sevoflurane-remifentanil interaction was refit with a Greco-type surface (naive pooled fit, Matlab, bootstrap CVs from 10,000 iterations). Two sets of models: remifentanil Ce with measured end-tidal (ET) sevoflurane, and remifentanil Ce with a predicted sevoflurane effect-site (Ce) concentration (Lerou physiological gas model, refs 4-5; details only in the online appendix). The original data were collected at 5000 ft altitude and the models were revised for altitude (p. 387).
- Part II (prospective): 20 adults for elective surgery (12 M, 8 F; ASA I-III; age 45.4 +/- 14.6 yr, weight 78 +/- 18 kg, height 173 +/- 14 cm, BMI 26.3 +/- 5.1), opioid-naive. Midazolam 12.5 mcg/kg IV premedication; induction with remifentanil 0.1-0.5 mcg/kg/min plus propofol 75-300 mcg/kg/min; maintenance sevoflurane + remifentanil at the anaesthesiologist's discretion; fentanyl given before emergence at their standard practice.
- Emergence was assessed every 20 s with OAA/S; return of responsiveness (ROR) = first of 2 consecutive OAA/S > 1. Recovery room: 30 min, VAS pain every 5 min, fentanyl 0.5-2 mcg/kg when VAS exceeded the tolerable VAS.
- Drug records at 5 s resolution drove PK simulations: fentanyl (Shafer 1990), remifentanil (Minto/Vuyk 2003 ref 11), propofol (Schnider 1999), midazolam (Albrecht 1999). Fentanyl Ce was converted to remifentanil-equivalent Ce with a relative potency "remifentanil to fentanyl = 1:1.2" (see open question).

## Model and parameters
Greco response surface (Eq. 1, printed p. 388): effect = Emax * U^n / (1 + U^n), with U = Cer/C50r + Ces/C50s + alpha * (Cer/C50r)(Ces/C50s). Effect runs from 0 (0% probability of no response) to 1. Cer = remifentanil Ce, Ces = sevoflurane. Table 2 (PDF page 3, printed 389; CV in parentheses; confirmed on the page image):

Fit in volunteers: 92% / 93% (OAA/S, effect-site / ET) and 90% / 90% (tibial pressure) of predictions > 0.5 matched observed responses; Spearman rho 0.83 (OAA/S) and 0.61 (tibial), all P < 0.001. CVs across all parameters ranged 8%-149% (p. 390).

Evaluation in the 20 surgical patients (pp. 390-392):
- Mean time from stopping anaesthetic to ROR 5.3 +/- 6.1 min.
- Patients emerged 0.9 +/- 4.3 min after the effect-site model predicted 50% probability of unresponsiveness, and 4.1 +/- 4.9 min after the ET model (P = 0.02).
- At ROR: ET sevoflurane 0.13 +/- 0.13 vol%, predicted Ce 0.44 +/- 0.17 vol%, remifentanil-equivalent Ce 3.5 +/- 1.7 ng/mL. Mean predicted probability of no response 53% +/- 37% (effect-site) vs 9% +/- 19% (ET), P < 0.001.
- Residual propofol Ce at ROR 0.1 +/- 0.1 mcg/mL and midazolam Ce 4.3 +/- 2.2 ng/mL (model-estimated).
- At first fentanyl request in recovery (13 of 20 needed it): ET sevoflurane 0.00 vol%, predicted Ce 0.02 +/- 0.01 vol%, remifentanil-equivalent Ce 1.9 +/- 1.3 ng/mL; mean probability of no response to 30 PSI 61% +/- 30% (effect-site) and 59% +/- 30% (ET), P = 0.893.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: OAA/S = 1 (unresponsive to loud voice and non-painful tactile stimulus), and no response to 30 PSI anterior tibial pressure (pressure algometry). Not analgesia scores; the recovery-room fentanyl request (VAS above tolerable VAS) was the clinical check.
- Remifentanil C50 for tibial pressure 30 PSI: 1.3 ng/mL (effect-site model) and 1.2 ng/mL (ET model) in the sevoflurane-interaction surface, Table 2. This is a model C50 (50% probability of no response to 30 PSI) in volunteers, not a MEAC. The concentrations are model-predicted remifentanil Ce, not measured; no assay.
- Remifentanil C50 for OAA/S = 1: 50.9 ng/mL (poorly determined; CV 28%; the authors give this as a possible reason for disagreement with other remifentanil C50s, p. 390).
- Concentration at which patients asked for fentanyl in recovery (remifentanil-equivalent Ce): 1.9 +/- 1.3 ng/mL, mean +/- SD (p. 391); at ROR 3.5 +/- 1.7 ng/mL. Neither is a MEAC; the first is an observed state at analgesic request, derived from predicted Ce.
- ke0 / t1/2 ke0 / tPeak for remifentanil: not reported; Ce comes from the cited Minto model (ref 11). The sevoflurane Ce lag comes from the Lerou model, which is in the online appendix only. No hysteresis half-time is given in the main text.
- Potency ratio: remifentanil : fentanyl = 1 : 1.2 used to convert fentanyl Ce to remifentanil-equivalent (p. 389; direction not stated, see open_questions).
- Authors note 30 PSI may be a slightly less painful surrogate than recovery-room pain, since mean predicted probability of no response at fentanyl request was 59-61% rather than about 50% (p. 392).

## Covariates
None in the response surface. Altitude (5000 ft) was accounted for in the revision. The sevoflurane Ce model assumes constant cardiac output (normalized to weight) and constant cerebral blood flow of 16% of cardiac output (p. 393).

## Caveats
- n = 20, preliminary, not designed for calibration (authors, p. 391).
- Volunteer OAA/S was scored without pain or a tracheal tube; applied in intubated surgical patients. Premedication midazolam and residual propofol may prolong emergence, judged small.
- Interaction parameters poorly determined (alpha CV 56%-94%, n CV up to 149%).
- Remifentanil Ce at ROR (3.5 ng/mL) and C50 for unresponsiveness (50.9 ng/mL) differ by more than 10-fold; at emergence the 50% isobole in remifentanil-sevoflurane space is the relevant comparison, but the figures were not read.
- Some authors have equity in Applied Medical Visualizations (disclosed).
