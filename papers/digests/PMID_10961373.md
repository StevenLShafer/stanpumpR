# Gillen 2000: tramadol and metabolites at the cloned human mu-opioid receptor

## Study
In vitro. Membranes from CHO-K1 cells stably expressing the human mu-opioid receptor (Grünenthal). Compounds: racemic tramadol; racemic M1-M5; (+)- and (-)-M1; references morphine, levo-methadone, fentanyl, DAMGO.
- Affinity: [3H]naloxone competition under low-salt and high-salt (GTPgammaS assay) conditions; Ki by Cheng-Prusoff. [3H]naloxone KD 0.5 +/- 0.02 nM.
- Potency/efficacy: [35S]GTPgammaS binding (EC50, Emax relative to DAMGO); relative intrinsic efficacy by Ehlert 1985. Triplicate, two experiments.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: receptor binding and G-protein activation in vitro; no in vivo PD, no plasma concentrations, no MEAC, no ke0/tPeak.
- Stated ratios (p. 117-118): racemic tramadol about 700-fold lower affinity than (+)-M1; morphine 5.5-fold higher affinity than (+)-M1; (+/-)-M5 about 30-fold lower than (+)-M1. These are affinity ratios, not equianalgesic potency ratios.
- Conclusion: (+)-M1 is the species responsible for tramadol's mu-opioid effect; (+)-M1 is a partial agonist with intrinsic efficacy between morphine and fentanyl.

## Covariates
Not applicable.

## Caveats
- In vitro, sponsor laboratory; Ki depends strongly on salt/GDP conditions (low- vs high-salt differ about 1000-fold).
- Disagrees with Lai 1996 (racemic M1 Ki 3.19 uM), discussed but unresolved.
- Says nothing about plasma concentrations, free fraction, or enantiomer ratios in vivo.
