# Mapleson 1996: effect of age on MAC, meta-analysis

## Study
- Meta-analysis of 30 peer-reviewed articles reporting MAC (movement to surgical incision, inhalation induction, no premedication
  or atropine only, one exception) with group n and mean age; 12 inhaled agents. MAC for intubation, "grimace or movement", and
  electrical stimulation excluded. Nitrous oxide MAC derived indirectly from volatile MAC with and without N2O in nine articles
  (appendix).
- Altitude corrections to sea level (Iowa 92.5%, Duke 91.5%, Milan 91.5%; Berne 96% as reported).
- Weighted (by n) multiple linear regression of log10 MAC on age in GLIM 4, three age ranges (all, >= 1 yr, >= 18 yr) and three
  structures (line per agent, per institution, per study). Table 1 lists the input data.

## Model and parameters
Optimal model: ages >= 1 yr, separate parallel line per institution per agent, no curvature, no difference in slope between agents.

MAC = a(i,j) x 10^(b x), x = age - 40 yr (eq. 2), b = -0.00269 per yr (eq. 1), 95% CL -0.0030 to -0.0024 (about ±12%).
About 6% per decade; 22% decrease from 40 to 80 yr, 27% increase from 40 to 1 yr. Residual SD 0.0179 log10 units (one line per
institution); 0.0453 around the global lines. **Not valid for age < 1 yr.**

Global fitted MAC at 40 yr (abstract, Table 2), 95% CL about ±7% (±10% desflurane, ±17% enflurane):

individual-patient data from nine groups gave slope -0.00288 (CL ±27%).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: MAC, no movement to surgical incision in 50% of patients, end-tidal concentration at 1 atm. No ke0. Theory appendix:
common slope expected if the site-of-action concentration is the same for all agents and site/gas partition is age-independent.

## Covariates
Age only (and institution as a nuisance factor).

## Caveats
Literature-level meta-analysis (group means); institutional differences up to tens of percent; barometric pressure unreported;
end-tidal vs arterial gradient ignored; under-1-year data fit poorly (only 10 of 14 within the 95% band).
