# Tomson 2005: AED pharmacokinetics in pregnancy and breast-feeding (review)

## Study
Narrative review, single author (Karolinska Institutet), published in the proceedings of the 9th International
Congress of Therapeutic Drug Monitoring & Clinical Toxicology (Louisville, April 2005). PDF pages 1-4 = printed
718-721; 32 numbered references. No new data, no systematic search, no pooling: every number is a restatement of a
cited primary study, and the review gives neither n, dose, gestational week nor variance for most of them. Sections:
old-generation AEDs in pregnancy, new-generation AEDs in pregnancy, placental transfer, breast-feeding (Table 1),
conclusions.

Reference-list oddities seen on the rendered page 4 (printed 721): references 16 and 27 are the same paper (Nau H,
Kuhnz W, Egger HJ, et al. Clin Pharmacokinet 1982;7:508-543). In Table 1 (PDF p3) the valproate row carries
superscript 20, which is the oxcarbazepine newborn case report (Bulau 1988); the text on PDF p2 attributes valproate's
low milk ratio to reference 28 (von Unruh 1984). Which is intended is not stated.

## Model and parameters
No model. The pregnancy and lactation numbers, each with the primary reference the review cites for it:

Old AEDs in pregnancy (PDF p1-2, printed 718-719):
- **Phenobarbital**: total and unbound concentrations fall by up to 50% by the end of pregnancy. Ref 5, Yerby MS,
  Friel PN, McCormick K. Antiepileptic drug disposition during pregnancy. Neurology 1992;42(Suppl 5):12-16. A maximum,
  not a mean. Primidone itself little changed, derived phenobarbital falls more in late pregnancy (ref 6, Battino 1984,
  Clin Pharmacokinet 9:252-260; no number).
- **Carbamazepine**: total down 42% and unbound down 22%, prepregnancy to delivery, in 22 pregnancies (ref 5, Yerby
  1992); in 50 pregnancies the third-trimester total only slightly below baseline and the unbound change not
  significant (ref 7, Tomson T, Lindbom U, Ekqvist B, et al. Epilepsia 1994;35:122-130).
- **Phenytoin**: total falls to about 40% of prepregnancy levels (refs 5 and 7); free concentration to approximately
  80% of baseline (ref 7 only).
- **Valproate**: no significant change in unbound concentrations despite a fairly marked fall in total (ref 5, Yerby
  1992 only). No number for either the total or the unbound change. The abstract hardens this to unbound levels that
  "remain unchanged"; the body's wording is a non-significance statement.
- The review's conclusion: for highly bound drugs (valproate, phenytoin) total levels may mislead in pregnancy and
  unbound levels should ideally be measured (PDF p3, printed 720).

New AEDs in pregnancy (PDF p2, printed 719):
- **Lamotrigine**: mean rise in apparent clearance above 65% vs preconception (ref 10, Tran TA, Leppik IE, Blesi K, et
  al. Neurology 2002;59:251-255); a peak about week 32 with a 330% rise from baseline (ref 11, Pennell PB, Newport DJ,
  Stowe ZN, et al. Neurology 2004;62:292-295); free apparent clearance 200% higher in the third trimester than
  postpartum baseline (ref 13, Pennell 2004 abstract, Epilepsia 45 Suppl 7:233); about 55% protein bound.
  **Abstract vs body**: the abstract gives a 300% rise "in the third trimester"; the body gives 330% at about week 32
  (ref 11). Same source, the abstract's figure is rounded and its timing broader.
- Tiagabine the only highly bound new AED; vigabatrin, gabapentin, pregabalin, levetiracetam renally eliminated; no
  pregnancy PK numbers for them.

Placental transfer (PDF p2): cord/maternal ratio near 1 for phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine (refs 16-18);
"slightly higher" for valproate, indicating fetal accumulation (ref 16, Nau 1982; no number); near 1 for lamotrigine
(refs 9, 19), oxcarbazepine and its MHD (refs 20, 21), topiramate (5 pregnancies, ref 22), zonisamide (2, ref 23),
levetiracetam (4 pairs, ref 24); gabapentin 1.7 to 2.1 (ref 25, Ohman 2004 congress abstract).

Breast-feeding, Table 1 (PDF p3, printed 720; read from the rendered page): ranges as printed, no n per row.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No concentration-effect relation, no therapeutic range, no ke0. The clinical end points mentioned (seizure
increase with falling lamotrigine, ref 12; no adverse effects reported from breast-feeding on new AEDs) are
qualitative.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (by trimester only loosely) and lactation; no equations. Mechanisms named: falling protein binding (total
falls, unbound unchanged) and rising metabolism, UGT induction for lamotrigine (ref 14).

## Caveats
- Secondary source. Each pregnancy figure comes from one or two primary studies; the review adds no independent
  evidence. For valproate in particular, the only reference is Yerby 1992 (ref 5).
- "Up to 50%" (phenobarbital) is a maximum; "about 40%" and "approximately 80%" (phenytoin) are rounded.
- Dose handling (constant dose or adjusted) is not given for any figure, so a fall in level is not necessarily a
  clearance change.
- Table 1 has no n per row; the valproate row's reference superscript does not match the text (see Study); refs 16
  and 27 are duplicates. The text layer misaligns Table 1's infant-concentration column (it pairs 1.9 with
  lamotrigine, 11 with levetiracetam, etc.); the rendered page is the record used here.
- The abstract states some findings more firmly than the body (valproate "unchanged" vs "no significant changes";
  lamotrigine 300% third trimester vs 330% at about week 32).
