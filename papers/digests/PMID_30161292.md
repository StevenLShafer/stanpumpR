# Salicath 2018: epidural analgesia versus IV PCA after intra-abdominal surgery (Cochrane review)

## Study
Systematic review and meta-analysis (random effects) of RCTs comparing epidural analgesia (continuous, CEA, or patient-controlled, PCEA) with intravenous opioid PCA (IVPCA) in adults after intra-abdominal surgery. Searches to September 2017 (CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, three trial registries) (abstract, p.1).
32 studies, 1716 participants: 869 EA (16 CEA, n=418; 16 PCEA, n=451) and 847 IVPCA; 10 studies awaiting classification, 1 ongoing (p.1). Epidural agents mostly bupivacaine, ropivacaine, fentanyl, morphine; IVPCA mostly morphine and fentanyl (p.4). Individual trials' IVPCA settings appear in the Characteristics tables, e.g. morphine 1 mg bolus with 15 min lockout, 20 mg 4-h limit; morphine 1 mg bolus with 5 min lockout and no background; hydromorphone 0.15 mg bolus with 10 min lockout (not systematically tabulated or pooled).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: **no MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration, no ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak, no hysteresis, no equianalgesic or potency ratio** appears in the portions read; no drug concentrations are reported. The end point is 0-100 mm VAS pain (at rest and on coughing or movement) in three phases (0-6 h, 7-24 h, over 24 h). Authors rate the at-rest difference as modest and unlikely to be clinically important; the on-movement difference may matter but rests on single-trial, low-quality evidence (abstract p.2).

## Covariates
Subgroups by epidural technique (CEA versus PCEA); the 7-24 h at-rest subgroup test gave P=0.07, the cough subgroup P=0.002. No patient covariates (weight, age) analysed in the parts read.

## Caveats
Pooled analyses exclude many trials that reported medians (11-13 trials per phase), so the VAS estimates rest on a minority of studies (for example 11 of 28 trials for 7-24 h at rest, p.20). Unblinded trials, high heterogeneity (I2 69-86% in places), and ten studies awaiting classification that may change conclusions (p.2). Mortality estimate extremely sensitive to missing data. EA arms mix local anaesthetics and opioids, so effects are not attributable to an opioid.
