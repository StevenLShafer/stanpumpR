# Ludden 1983: effect of IV dose on hydralazine kinetics

## Study
- Six hypertensive men (Table I, PDF p2 / printed 149): ages 47-57 yr, weights 85-102 kg; acetylators 3 rapid (patients 1, 2, 5) and 3 slow (3, 4, 6). Co-medication: hydrochlorothiazide in 5 of them; patient 6 took furosemide, digoxin and chlorpropamide.
- Hydralazine HCl injection (20 mg/mL) diluted to 5 mL, given by constant infusion over 100 s into a running saline line (20 mL/min). Each patient got four single IV doses at least 48 h apart (abstract: 2-4 days). Doses ranged from 0.255 to 3.05 µmol/kg. The paper pairs 1.53 µmol/kg with 0.3 mg/kg and 2.29 µmol/kg with 0.45 mg/kg, i.e. mg of the hydrochloride (derived: MW about 196.6, so 0.255 → 0.050 and 3.05 → 0.60 mg/kg). The first dose was 0.51-1.91 µmol/kg (0.1-0.375 mg/kg), then raised or lowered according to the fall in blood pressure.
- Sampling: before the dose, then 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 75, 90, 105, 120, 150 and 180 min after the end of the infusion. Fasted 8 h, supine.
- Assay: specific HPLC for unchanged hydralazine. Patients 1-4: plasma, converted to whole blood using blood/plasma ratio 1.65 (from their earlier report). Patients 5-6: whole blood assayed directly.
- Analysis: two- and three-compartment models with zero-order input, fitted with NONLIN, weights 1/C². The model was chosen for each data set by the Boxenbaum criteria. Parameters are reported per patient per dose; no rate constants or exponents are printed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD model, ke0, EC50 or tPeak. The introduction cites earlier work (the authors' ref 5) in which the peak fall in mean arterial pressure tracks the log of peak concentration or AUC. It also says the drug's effects are not tied in time to circulating concentrations. Neither point was measured here.
- Doses were adjusted according to the hypotensive response, but no response data are reported.
- Not an opioid, so no MEAC or potency ratio applies.

## Covariates
- Acetylator phenotype: no discernible effect on any IV parameter.
- Dose: no consistent effect up to 2.29 µmol/kg (0.45 mg/kg). At 2.29 µmol/kg, patient 4's clearance fell and patient 1's rose. Patient 3 lost 30-40% of clearance at 2.29 and 3.05 µmol/kg compared with 1.02 and 1.53 (Discussion, PDF p4).
- Fig. 2: mean blood AUC against dose looks linear except at the highest dose (n = 1).
- No weight or age covariate model.

## Caveats
- Only 6 patients, all older hypertensive men on other drugs (mostly a thiazide).
- Blood concentrations are partly measured and partly converted from plasma with one fixed ratio (1.65).
- Within one subject, the volumes vary widely from dose to dose (e.g. patient 5 Vss 0.90-4.87 L/kg). The authors put this down to the variable shape of the concentration-time profiles.
- Each data set was fitted with a two- or three-compartment model, whichever fitted best. No exponents are printed, so building a two-compartment model from the means (CL, V1, Vss and β = CL/Varea) averages parameters from models of different structure. It is a mean of individual fits, not a population fit.
