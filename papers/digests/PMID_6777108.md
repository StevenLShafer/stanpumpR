# Perucca 1980: valproate displaces phenytoin and inhibits its free clearance

## Study
Randomised two-period crossover, about 3 weeks apart, in **7 healthy men** aged 21-40 y (two had been in a
pilot study). Each period: phenytoin sodium 250 mg (Epanutin) IV over 30 min in 200 mL saline; in one period
valproate sodium (Depakin) 400 mg orally three times daily (1200 mg/day) for 5 days, the first dose about 48 h
before the phenytoin. Serum to 72 h, saliva to 32 h, three 24-h urines. Assays: GLC with nitrogen detector for
phenytoin (sensitivity about 0.2 µmol/L), GLC for pHPPH and valproate; binding by ultrafiltration of
14C-spiked serum at 6 and 12 h, and by the saliva/serum ratio. Non-compartmental: terminal slope by
regression, CL = D/AUC, intrinsic clearance Cl_i = (CL - CL_r)/fu, Vd = D/(AUC x beta), Vd_free = Vd/fu.
**Subject 7 had non-first-order kinetics (plateau to 24-32 h, no exponential decay before 48 h) and is
excluded from every mean in Tables II and III** (not from Table I).

## Model and parameters
Table II (PDF p 6, printed 784), means ± SD of subjects 1-6, A = phenytoin alone, B = with valproate:
- t½ 15.0 ± 3.6 vs 14.8 ± 2.2 h (no change; also unchanged from saliva, Table III).
- AUC 402 ± 55 vs 307 ± 35 µmol/L·h (p < 0.01).
- CL (total) 33.8 ± 6.5 vs 43.9 ± 6.6 mL/h/kg (p < 0.01); derived ratio 1.30.
- Cl_i (free, intrinsic) 348 ± 60 vs 286 ± 39 mL/h/kg (p < 0.01); derived ratio of means 0.822 (-17.8%).
- Vd 0.72 ± 0.16 vs 0.91 ± 0.18 L/kg (p < 0.05); Vd_free 7.4 ± 1.3 vs 6.1 ± 1.1 L/kg (p < 0.01).
- Subject 7: t½ 38.2 / 41.0 h, CL 8.3 / 12.4, Cl_i 87 / 66 mL/h/kg.

Table I (PDF p 5, printed 783), % bound by ultrafiltration, all 7 subjects, mean ± SD:
phenytoin alone 90.1 ± 1.0 (6 h) and 90.6 ± 0.8 (12 h); with valproate 83.9 ± 1.2 (6 h) and 84.8 ± 1.9 (12 h).

84 ± 1.4% bound. These agree: the abstract's 9.6 and 15.6 are the mean of the 6-h and 12-h unbound fractions
(derived: (9.9 + 9.4)/2 = 9.65; (16.1 + 15.2)/2 = 15.65). Ratio 9.6/15.6 = 0.615 (derived); the 6-h values alone
give 9.9/16.1 = 0.615 too.

- The text (p 6) quotes saliva-derived binding as 90.7 ± 2.1% and 83.1 ± 5.1%. Table III gives 90.8 ± 2.3 and

consistent change (Table V).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. Saliva phenytoin, a surrogate for the unbound level, was higher with valproate at every time
(Fig. 3B); the AUC of saliva rose 28 ± 14% (text p 7). Unbound serum phenytoin was higher with valproate in
all seven subjects (Fig. 3A, computed from total x the mean fu).

## Covariates
Within-subject comparison only. The rise in fu correlated with the rise in total Vd (r = 0.93) and in total
CL (r = 0.85) (Figs 4-5).

## Caveats
Seven healthy men; single IV phenytoin dose, so kinetics were first order in six of them; the authors expect
saturation at steady state to amplify the effect of the inhibition. Free concentrations in Fig. 3A assume a
constant fu. Means exclude subject 7. The authors infer two opposing effects, displacement and metabolic
inhibition; the inhibition is their explanation of the gap between the CL rise (about 30%) and the fu rise
(57%).
