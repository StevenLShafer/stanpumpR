# Jun 2018: rocuronium block in second-trimester pregnant vs non-pregnant women

## Study
Prospective comparative study, Seoul (NCT02797860), Jan-Oct 2016. 50 second-trimester women for elective
transabdominal cerclage or McDonald procedure (Group P) and 50 non-pregnant women for open gynaecological surgery
(Group C); 47 per group analysed (6 excluded). ASA I-II, 20-45 y, 50-100 kg. Table 1 (mean ± SD): age 33.6 ± 3.1
vs 33.5 ± 5.6 y; weight 56.4 ± 5.3 vs 55.7 ± 5.9 kg; height 161 ± 5.7 vs 160.2 ± 4.8 cm; BMI 21.8 vs 21.7;
gestational age 14.3 ± 0.6 weeks; rocuronium dose 33.9 ± 3 vs 33.4 ± 3.4 mg; operation 52 ± 9.2 vs 55 ± 8.7 min.
Glycopyrrolate 0.2 mg IM premedication; propofol 2.0 mg/kg; rocuronium 0.6 mg/kg (total body weight) over 5 s;
sevoflurane 1.5-2.5% (BIS 40-60), 50% O2 in air. Acceleromyography of adductor pollicis (TOF-Watch SX), TOF every
15 s; monitoring to 25% T1 recovery. No drug concentrations measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: adductor pollicis T1 by acceleromyography. Onset (time to maximum T1 depression) not different;
clinical duration (to 25% T1 recovery) 5.1 min longer in pregnancy. No ke0, EC50 or concentrations. The authors
attribute the prolongation to a relative fall in liver blood flow (about 20% relative to the 30-40% rise in
plasma volume, citing others), not measured. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled; groups matched for weight and dose, so the authors argue relative overdose (Gin 2002) does not
explain the difference.

## Caveats
Different surgery in the two groups; not randomised or blinded; recovery followed only to 25% T1 (operations
shorter than full recovery); hepatic blood flow not measured. Abstract gives P <0.037 for the duration, Table 2
0.036. Gestational age 14.3 weeks is the very start of the second trimester.
