# Adhayanti 2026: Trimester changes in antipsychotic pharmacokinetics (systematic review)

## Study
- Systematic/scoping review, PRISMA; PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane to 3 Oct 2023; English peer-reviewed human studies (clinical, PBPK) with at least one PK parameter of an antipsychotic in pregnancy.
- 4,071 records, 1,105 duplicates, 2,966 screened, 12 articles extracted (5 case studies, 5 PBPK, 1 naturalistic cohort, 1 retrospective analysis) (PDF p.3).
- Only two first-generation agents found: haloperidol and perphenazine. The rest are second-generation (aripiprazole, clozapine, lurasidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, risperidone, ziprasidone).

## Model and parameters
No model. Haloperidol, the only drug the library cites this paper for, is in Table 2 (PDF p.5, confirmed on the page image), all from Westin (the review prints "Westin et al. 2018"): serum concentration (SC, ng/mL), N = 2 subjects, dose 8 mg/day.

| SC ng/mL | 5 | 4.4 (-12%) | 3.2 (-36%) | 2.4 (-52%) |

The percentages are changes against baseline/post-partum (4.4/5, 3.2/5, 2.4/5 reproduce them). Table 1 (p.5) lists the Westin study as retrospective, Norway, n = 103, mean age 29.8 +- 6.6 y; the haloperidol line gives 8 mg/day. Text (p.3): haloperidol shows a gradual decline, up to 52% in the third trimester. The abstract repeats 52%. Perphenazine (Table 2): IM 7 mg/day, n=8, 2.1 -> 1.8/1.2/0.9 ng/mL (-14.3/-42.9/-57.1%); PO 30 mg/day, n=7, 2.5 -> 2.1/1.3/0.8 (-16/-48/-68%). The review's abstract gives third-trimester falls of 76.2% (quetiapine) and 76.8% (aripiprazole); these were not checked against the rotated Table 3.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None reported. No ke0, tPeak, EC50, effect measure, or therapeutic range is given for haloperidol. The review notes the plasma level-clinical outcome relation is non-linear, limiting therapeutic drug monitoring (p.5). (Not an opioid paper.)

## Covariates
Pregnancy trimester (descriptive only). The review attributes the fall to raised CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 activity, plasma volume expansion, lower albumin and higher glomerular filtration (p.2, p.4). Haloperidol metabolism via CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 is described (p.4). No equations.

## Caveats
- A secondary source: figures are those of the primary papers, not new data. Haloperidol rests on 2 women in one cohort.
- Sampling timing, assay, whether concentrations were dose-adjusted, and the baseline's gestational or post-partum timing are not given in the review for haloperidol.
- The authors say age, weight, hepatic and renal function, comorbidity and polypharmacy were not explored, and that PBPK cannot replicate real-world complexity.
- Table 3 prints some values with decimal commas and has misaligned rows in the text layer; the review's Table 3 itself shows internal oddities (for example olanzapine rows with 'NA'), so no Table 3 number was used.
