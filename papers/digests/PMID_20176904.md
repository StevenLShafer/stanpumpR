# Muller 2010: clindamycin popPK in peripartum women

## Study
- **Design:** prospective study in The Hague, February 2005 to February 2007.
- **Who:** women from 26 weeks' gestation given IV clindamycin under local guidelines.
- **Indications:** four received it to prevent neonatal GBS disease (penicillin allergy plus a GBS risk factor),
  two for endocarditis prophylaxis, and one for both, so three received it for endocarditis prophylaxis. "None of
  the patients was clinically ill" (p.3). The three endocarditis-prophylaxis patients had valve disease: a minor
  mitral stenosis; prosthetic aortic and mitral valves; and aortic valve dysfunction with ITP and splenectomy.
- **n = 7 (Table 1, p.3, image-confirmed):**
  - age 36.1 (SD 4.24) y, range 31.3-41.8;
  - gestation 38.3 (3.01) weeks, range 34-42.3;
  - BMI 32.1 (22.1-39.1); weight 86.1 (14.2) kg, range 59.5-104.8;
  - creatinine 55.9 (20.3) umol/L, range 38-100;
  - 4 vaginal and 3 secondary caesarean deliveries; one twin pregnancy with PPROM.
- **Dosing:** 600 mg over 20 min every 6 h (endocarditis prophylaxis) or 900 mg over 30 min every 8 h (GBS). Three
  women also had postpartum doses.
- **Sampling:** contralateral arm, dense during and after the infusion, then every 30-45 min. 177 samples, 2 below
  the LLOQ excluded. Sampling spans 0.95-29.53 h. Cord blood (arterial and venous) from the six women in labour.
- **Assay:** HPLC-UV, LLOQ 0.1 mg/L, linear to 50 mg/L, CV <5%. Total serum concentration; binding not measured.
- **Model:** NONMEM VI, FOCE with interaction, ADVAN11. 1000-run bootstrap, of which 598 succeeded; VPC; MICLAB
  Monte Carlo with 5000 subjects.

## Model and parameters
A linear three-compartment model fitted best. IIV could be estimated on CL and V3 only; neither was statistically
significant. Error model proportional. No covariate improved the fit.

Table 3 (p.4, image-confirmed). Mean estimates; "% CV" is the parameter's estimation uncertainty from the
$COVARIANCE step, not IIV:

The text gives two derived summaries:
- Vss = 6.32 x 10^3 L. Derived check: V1 + V2 + V3 = 6314.6 L.
- "Gamma-phase" half-life 2.6 h, repeated in the abstract and discussion.

Derived from Table 3, the disposition rate constants are about 15.8, 0.420 and 0.00108 /h, giving half-lives of
about 2.6 min, 1.65 h and 641 h. A 641 h terminal phase from a 6250 L compartment cannot be the 2.6 h "gamma"
half-life. The reported 2.6 h is not reproduced by the printed parameters; the cause is unresolved.

The authors say V3 was imprecise: only two patients had prolonged profiles, and it was the one bootstrap parameter
that deviated (p.4). IIV in CL as a CV is about 58% (derived: sqrt(e^0.292 - 1)).

The discussion compares CL of 10.0 L/h with the 19.8-26.4 L/h published in non-pregnant subjects; there was no
within-study comparison.

Table 2 (p.3), cord-to-maternal ratios (maternal values are model-predicted):
- Venous cord: 0.22-0.89, plus one value of 1.59.
- Arterial cord: 0.48-0.67, plus one value of 1.59.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **PK/PD index:** fAUC0-24/MIC. The static target is at least 27 for free drug (147 for total drug), from Craig
  2002 (p.2).
- **Monte Carlo, 900 mg every 8 h (Table 4, p.5).** fAUC0-24/MIC as average (lower 95% CI):

| Binding | MIC 0.125 | MIC 0.25 | MIC 0.5 | MIC 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 65% | 223 (102) | 116 (50.3) | 55.8 (24.6) | 27.9 (11.8) |
| 75% | 159 (73.1) | 79.4 (35.8) | 39.7 (17.3) | 19.8 (7.94) |
| 85% | 95.4 (44.2) | 47.7 (21.6) | 23.9 (10.2) | 11.9 (4.44) |

- **Binding:** described as 62-94% in non-pregnant people, with alpha1-acid glycoprotein and saturable binding
  (up to 92% at AAG >=201 mg/dL; up to 88% at 4 mg/L). It is unknown in these patients: likely lower in pregnancy,
  possibly higher with labour, stress or infection (p.6).
- **Conclusion:** exposure may be too low to protect all neonates from GBS. The authors also note that clindamycin
  is bacteriostatic, whereas penicillins are bactericidal (p.6).
- No ke0. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
None retained. Weight, height and laboratory values were tested in an exploratory analysis (p < 0.05) and none
improved the fit.

## Caveats
- n = 7, with sparse and variable sampling; V3 is unidentifiable.
- The printed gamma half-life is inconsistent with Table 3.
- Binding was not measured, so the free-drug PTA rests on assumed binding.
- No non-pregnant control.
- The patients were not infected: GBS and endocarditis prophylaxis only.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **n = 7, mean 86 kg, three-compartment model, CL 10.0 L/h (95% CI 2.65-17.35)**: Tables 1 and 3. **matches**.
- **"CV 37.5%"**: this is the estimation CV of CL, not IIV. IIV is a variance of 0.292, about 58% CV. The registry
  text does not say which; it is correct only as uncertainty.
- **"3 of them with endocarditis"**: **discrepancy**. Three women received clindamycin as *endocarditis
  prophylaxis* (valve disease), and the paper says none was clinically ill (p.3). No patient had endocarditis.
- **Gamma half-life 2.6 h and Vss 6.32 x 10^3 L**: the paper states both (p.3). **matches** as a transcription.
  The registry's "implausibly large, poorly identified" fits the authors' own note on V3. The 2.6 h half-life is
  not reproduced by the paper's parameters (see above); the registry does not use it.
- **Cross-study ratio 10.0/15.2 = 0.66**: arithmetic correct (0.658). The 15.2 L/h is the repo's Bouazza value and
  is not in this paper.
- **docs/pregnancy.md:352**: 7 women, CL 10.0, Vss 6320 L, no within-study comparison. **matches**.
- **Registry note**: AAG falls in pregnancy, so the unbound fraction should rise. This is consistent with the
  paper's statement that binding is likely reduced in pregnancy (p.6); the paper also says it may be increased in
  labour.
