# Hedenstierna 2002: airway closure, atelectasis and gas exchange under anaesthesia

## Study
Narrative review, no new data, by the Uppsala group that did the CT and multiple inert gas elimination (MIGET) work. Topics: atelectasis (CT, about 90% of anaesthetised patients), airway closure, the resulting shunt and low VA/Q, three-compartment lung model, prevention (PEEP, recruitment, FIO2 during induction).

Other numbers: atelectasis in almost 90% of anaesthetised subjects, with spontaneous or paralysed breathing, intravenous or inhaled agents; atelectasis and airway closure together explain as much as 74% of the impaired arterial oxygenation; anaesthesia lowers FRC by 0.4-0.5 L (ref 16); mild to moderate hypoxaemia (SaO2 85-90%) in about half of patients at FIO2 0.3-0.4, and SaO2 below 81% for up to 5 min in 20% (PDF p.1, printed 332; text layer only).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No drug concentrations, no ke0, no MEAC, no opioid content. End points are CT atelectasis area, MIGET shunt and low-VA/Q perfusion, PaO2. Induction on 30% O2 produced no atelectasis; on 80% or 60% almost none, with apnoea time to SaO2 90% still above 5 min with 80% O2; with pure O2 and VA/Q of 0 collapse takes about 7 min (PDF p.4, printed 335).

## Covariates
Airway closure and perfusion of low-VA/Q regions increase with age (PDF p.3). Shunt rises with atelectasis area (equation above).

## Caveats
Review; figures are reproduced from the author's earlier studies, population details not given here. Figure 2's 6% is one illustrative case. Two compartments contribute: true shunt (VA/Q 0, atelectasis) and low-VA/Q perfusion (airway closure), kept separate by MIGET.
