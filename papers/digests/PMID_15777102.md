# Adams 2005: single- and multiple-dose PK and dose proportionality of oxymorphone IR tablets

## Study
- Randomised, open (non-blinded randomisation schedule), three-way crossover; Endo-funded (PDF p3, printed p93).
- 24 healthy volunteers enrolled (12 M, 12 F), 23 completed (12 M, 11 F); age mean 36 y (27-44), weight mean
  71.3 kg (54.5-100), height 164.8 cm; 71% large frame (Table II, PDF p5).
- Oxymorphone hydrochloride IR tablets 5, 10, 20 mg (20 mg = two 10 mg tablets): single dose day 1, then the same dose
  q6h days 3-8 (22 doses); 7-day washout between periods. Naltrexone 50 mg each evening days -1 to 7 to block
  opioid effects. Fasted overnight to 4 h post dose on days 1 and 8 (PDF p3).
- Sampling: 0-48 h after the single dose; 0-6 h after the morning dose on day 8 (PDF p4).
- Assay: LC/MS/MS in plasma; oxymorphone and 6-OH-oxymorphone linear 0.1-20 ug/L; oxymorphone-3-glucuronide
  5-250 ug/L (PDF p4). Whether concentrations are expressed as base or salt is not stated.
- Noncompartmental analysis (Table I); dose proportionality by ANOVA on log values normalised to 10 mg (SAS 8.2).

ug/L = ng/mL; CL/F in L/min.

3-glucuronide AUCss 1321.37, Cmax 376.25, Cmin 136.77, Cavg 220.23.

Troughs (Table IV, PDF p7): steady state reached after 3-4 days; day 8 Cmin 0.49 / 1.16 / 2.47 ug/L at 5 / 10 / 20 mg.
Dose proportionality (Table VI, geometric LS means normalised to 10 mg): no significant across-dose difference
(single-dose AUC p = 0.0627, Cmax p = 0.2124; steady-state AUCss p = 0.0600, Cmax p = 0.8338).

oral vs IV terminal t1/2 15.2 +/- 21.0 vs 2.0 +/- 0.6 h, attributed to enterohepatic recirculation; < 2% excreted
unchanged in urine.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No pharmacodynamic end point was measured: naltrexone was given to block opioid effects. No MEAC or minimum
  analgesic concentration, no ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak, no hysteresis.
- Potency: only the background statement (PDF p1-2, citing refs 1-2) that parenteral oxymorphone has greater
  analgesic potency than morphine; no ratio given. Discussion compares tmax with morphine and oxycodone IR tablets
  (1.2 and 1.5 h, cited) and t1/2 with oxycodone (3-5.7 h, cited) (PDF p9).

## Covariates
None examined. Dose (5-20 mg) did not change dose-normalised exposure.

## Caveats
- Healthy volunteers under naltrexone blockade; 23 completers; sponsor-employed co-author.
- 6-OH-oxymorphone below the quantification limit in some 5 and 10 mg samples (authors, PDF p9).
- Oral t1/2 7-9 h here vs the IV 1.3-2 h of the labelling: the long oral terminal phase is not explained by this study.
- CL/F is a mean of individual ratios on the hydrochloride dose (derived check: 10 mg / 9.10 ug.h/L = 18.3 L/min,
  below the 21.42 mean, as expected for a mean of ratios). The salt basis of the concentrations is not stated.
