# Adams 2005: oxymorphone immediate-release tablet single- and multiple-dose PK

## Study
Randomised three-way crossover in 24 healthy adults (23 completed; 12 men, 11 women;
mean age 36 y, range 27-44; mean weight 71.3 kg, range 54.5-100; mostly large body frame),
Table II (printed p.95). Each subject took oxymorphone hydrochloride immediate-release (IR)
tablets at 5, 10 and 20 mg in three separate periods: a single morning dose on day 1, then
the same dose every 6 h from day 3 to day 8 (22 doses total), with a 7-day washout between
periods. Naltrexone 50 mg each evening blocked opioid effects. Single-dose sampling 0-48 h;
steady-state sampling 0-6 h after the day-8 dose. Plasma oxymorphone, 6-OH-oxymorphone and
oxymorphone-3-glucuronide by parallel LC/MS/MS (linear range 0.1-20 µg/L for oxymorphone and
6-OH-oxymorphone; 5-250 µg/L for the glucuronide). Noncompartmental analysis, SAS 8.2.
Funded by Endo Pharmaceuticals.

Note: this is the **immediate-release** (Drugs R D 2005) study; the extended-release crossover
is the separate Adams & Ahdieh Pharmacotherapy 2004;24:468-476 paper (ref 11 here).

## Model and parameters
Noncompartmental, not a structural PK model. Single-dose oxymorphone parent, Table III
(printed p.96, PDF p.6; mean (SD) unless noted; tmax median (range)). Units: µg·h/L = ng·h/mL,
µg/L = ng/mL.

Terminal half-life for the parent ~7.3-9.4 h across doses (abstract; Table III). Steady-state
(Table V, printed p.98): 10 mg q6h AUCss 10.19 µg·h/L, Cmax 3.51 µg/L, Cmin 1.16 µg/L,
tmax 0.50 h. Exposure dose-proportional 5-20 mg (Table VI). Reported absolute oral
bioavailability ~11% (from an earlier Endo study cited in the text, not measured here;
"data on file").

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic end point was measured: naltrexone was co-administered specifically to
block opioid effects, so this is a pure PK study.
- **No MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration** reported (no analgesia, no pain
  model, no concentration-effect).
- **No ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis** reported; no effect-site work. The only timing is
  the plasma **absorption** tmax, median 0.5 h for IR oxymorphone at every dose (Table III),
  compared in the Discussion (printed p.97) with reported mean tmax of 1.2 h for IR morphine
  and 1.5 h for IR oxycodone (refs 12, 13).
- **No equianalgesic / potency ratio value.** The introduction states only qualitatively that
  oxymorphone has greater analgesic potency and more rapid onset than morphine (citing Eddy
  1959 and Sinatra 1988, refs 1-2); no numeric ratio and no concentration are given here.

## Covariates
None modelled (noncompartmental). The paper notes the population was mostly "large frame"
and otherwise unremarkable.

## Caveats
Healthy volunteers under naltrexone blockade; n=23. 6-OH-oxymorphone at 5 and 10 mg fell below
the quantification limit in some single-dose samples, producing ~20% deviation from dose
linearity for that metabolite (vs ~10% at steady state). Oral bioavailability (~11%) is quoted
from unpublished "data on file", not measured here. No central/peripheral volume or exchange
clearance is reported — this study cannot build a disposition model.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Cmax 1.93 ng/mL (10 mg IR)** — `R/drugs_oxymorphone.R:41`, `inst/app-help/drugs/oxymorphone.md:7`,
  `test-drugs-oxymorphone.R:67,71`. Table III 10 mg Cmax = 1.93 µg/L = 1.93 ng/mL (confirmed on the
  rendered printed p.96). **Matches**, including the µg/L→ng/mL identity.
- **AUC∞ 9.10 ng·h/mL (10 mg IR)** — header line 30, `test-drugs-oxymorphone.R:68`. Table III 10 mg
  AUC∞ = 9.10 µg·h/L = 9.10 ng·h/mL (confirmed). **Matches**. The documented ~18% under-prediction
  of oral exposure (header lines 30-33; test asserts simulated AUC 6.5-9.10) is a stated modelling
  choice (IV clearance anchor kept over the oral-implied clearance), not a transcription error.
- **8.92 mg base per 10 mg HCl** — `test-drugs-oxymorphone.R:67,69` (10 * 301.34/337.80 = 8.92).
  Molecular-weight conversion; the paper dosed the hydrochloride. **Matches** (arithmetic).
- **Reference / bibliography / attribution** — `R/drugs_oxymorphone.R:63-64`,
  `inst/app-help/references.md:95`, `inst/app-help/investigators.md:77`. Citation, DOI, authors and
  IR title all correct. The context's title-mismatch warning is **resolved**: references.md:95 now
  carries the IR (Drugs R D 2005) title and the ER (Pharmacotherapy 2004) paper is the separate
  line 97. **Matches**.
- **tmax / time to peak** — `R/drugs_oxymorphone.R:43-44`. **Discrepancy.** The header states the
  calibrated plasma peak falls at 1.4 h and that "no time to peak was available in the sources
  consulted." This paper does report an oral tmax: median 0.5 h at every IR dose (Table III,
  confirmed on p.96; abstract). The model (ka calibrated to Cmax magnitude only, one compartment
  with no distribution phase) therefore peaks ~3x later than Adams & Ahdieh observed. The pinned
  Cmax and AUC are unaffected; this is a factual gap in the header's justification and a shape
  mismatch, raised as an open question above. Note this oral absorption tmax is distinct from the
  provisional effect-site tPeak (20 min) in the same file, which is unrelated to this paper.
