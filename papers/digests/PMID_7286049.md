# Schwilden 1981: General method for calculating the dosage scheme

## Study
Theory paper (Bonn) with a brief clinical note. For any linear compartment model, the input I(t) that produces a prescribed amount/concentration course in any compartment is found from the convolution A_i(t) = integral G_i(t - t') I(t') dt' (eq 1.3, the superposition principle; G = Green's function, the unit bolus response) by Laplace transformation, I(p) = A_i(p)/G_i(p), and back-transformation by the residue theorem (eq 1.5; Appendix B rules). General n-compartment solution via eigenvalues/eigenvectors of the rate matrix (Appendix A, eqs A.1-A.10). Notes the method is model-independent: blood levels after a bolus suffice.

1. Constant blood level c0 from t = 0: I(t) = D c0/(A+B) [delta(t) + kel + k12 exp(-k21 t)] (eq 2.5): bolus, maintenance, and exponentially declining term (the later BET scheme). Fig. 2 example: initial bolus 10 mg.
2. Linearly rising blood level to t1 (eq 3.4; Fig. 3) - a base infusion is needed at t = 0.
3. Linearly rising peripheral-compartment amount (eq 4.4; Fig. 4; bolus 5.9 mg) - shorter rise time needs a larger bolus.
4. Sinusoidal blood level (eq 5.4; Fig. 5) - I(t) >= 0 limits the achievable frequency; infusion maximum precedes the blood-level maximum.

Microprocessor (6502) interfaced to two pumps implementing schemes 1-3; checks the maximum pump rate and reports the minimum drug concentration needed. Clinical use: 7 volunteers (constant level, etomidate and midazolam) and 5 patients (etomidate scheme 1 with fentanyl scheme 2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model; notes plasma level need not parallel effect (Segre 1968, norepinephrine). No ke0. Not an opioid PD paper.
