# McNicol 2015: PCA versus non-PCA opioid analgesia (Cochrane review)

## Study
Systematic review and random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird) meta-analysis, update of the 2006 review (printed p.1, 7-10). Searches: CENTRAL 2014 issue 12, MEDLINE and EMBASE to 28 Jan 2015. Included RCTs of intravenous opioid PCA **without background infusion** against as-needed non-patient-controlled opioid (mostly nurse-given IM, 34 of 49 studies). Excluded partial agonists, mixed agonist-antagonists, co-administered non-opioids in the PCA solution, scheduled control regimens, chronic-pain patients, and arms with fewer than 10 participants (p.8, 12).
49 studies, 1725 PCA and 1687 control participants (p.12). Ages 7 years to elderly; one paediatric study (Berde 1991). Most common PCA opioid morphine (33 studies), commonest bolus 1 mg (16 studies; range 0.5-2.5 mg), lockout 10 min (11 studies) or 5-6 min (18 studies; range 5-20 min); no dose limit in 33 studies (p.12). Control drug same as PCA drug in 40 studies (morphine 29, meperidine 5, fentanyl 2, hydromorphone 1, piritramide 1, ketobemidone 1, papaveretum 1).
All studies unblinded; evidence graded moderate at best (p.13-16). Primary outcome 0-100 mm VAS pain; secondary outcomes opioid use (IV morphine equivalents), satisfaction, length of stay, adverse events.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: **no MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration, no ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak, no hysteresis, no plasma or effect-site concentration of any kind** is reported. The end point is clinical (0-100 mm VAS pain, time-averaged over 0-24, 25-48, 49-72 or 0-48 h; scales of other lengths rescaled to 0-100, p.8).
Potency conversions (all used only to express consumption as IV morphine equivalents, p.9): general conversion by the American Pain Society 2008 equianalgesic table (the table's values are not reproduced in the paper); ketobemidone 1:1 with morphine; papaveretum 0.85 times as strong as morphine (authors call it an approximation); piritramide 15 mg = 10 mg morphine (Micromedex 2014). Authors note the conversion may bias results where different opioids were compared (p.23).
Authors cite morphine elimination half-life 1.7-3.3 h and duration of effect 3-4 h after a single IV/IM dose, and a usual single dose of 5-10 mg (p.22); these are quoted from textbooks (Stoelting 1999, Fee 1996), not measured here.

## Covariates
Subgroups by surgery type (lower abdominal, upper abdominal, cardiothoracic, neurosurgical, mixed/other) for 0-24 h and 25-48 h only; heterogeneity high (I2 often >50%). Paediatric study and non-parenteral-control study removed in sensitivity analyses without changing results (p.10, 18). No pharmacological covariates (weight, age, sex) modelled.

## Caveats
All trials unblinded; many small (24 of 49 had under 50 patients); I2 up to 98% for consumption; morphine-equivalent conversions add error; adverse-event definitions inconsistent and trials underpowered for rare events (p.15, 22-23). One cross-over study (Bollish 1985) analysed as parallel. Control-arm regimen varied (IM, SC, IV bolus), so "non-PCA" is not a single comparator.
