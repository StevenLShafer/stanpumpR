# Esper 2015: blood/gas partition coefficients of isoflurane, sevoflurane, desflurane

## Study
- In vitro headspace gas chromatography (FID), 37 degC, on blood drawn (16-30 mL, EDTA) from 120 fasting adult surgical patients (ASA I-III, age 19-86) immediately before induction, via a new IV or arterial line. Three separate groups: isoflurane n = 41, sevoflurane n = 41, desflurane n = 38 (134 enrolled, 14 excluded technically).
- Table 2 (PDF p4, read on image): age 60.95 +/- 15.28, 59.07 +/- 12.76, 61.76 +/- 9.56 y; female 11/17/9; BMI 27.08/27.41/28.18 kg/m2; Hb 8.54/8.67/8.87 mmol/L; haematocrit 0.41/0.41/0.42.
- Method: 7-10 mL blood in 21 mL vials plus 50 uL aqueous anaesthetic stock, equilibrated 15 min at 37 degC; control vials hold only air plus the same amount; lambda = (Cc.Vc - Ct.(Vc - Vt)) / (Vt.Ct) on gas-phase concentrations (PDF p3). Validity check: distilled water/gas coefficients (Table 5).

## Model and parameters
No PK model; measured partition coefficients (dimensionless, mean +/- SD, Table 3, PDF p4; 99% CI in brackets):

Female vs male (Table 3): isoflurane 1.46 vs 1.44, sevoflurane 0.76 vs 0.73, desflurane 0.57 vs 0.58, all P > 0.2. Venous vs arterial: 1.46 vs 1.40, 0.73 vs 0.77, 0.58 vs 0.57, all P > 0.15. Table 1 (PDF p2, text layer) lists earlier values, e.g. desflurane 0.424 +/- 0.024 (Eger 1987), sevoflurane 0.686 (Strum 1987) and 0.60 (Wallin 1975), isoflurane 1.20-1.55.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None; no MAC, ke0 or effect-site data. Not an opioid; opioid audit not applicable.

## Covariates
Pearson correlations (Table 4, text layer): age, BMI, haemoglobin and haematocrit show no significant relation with any coefficient after the authors' P < 0.01 threshold; isoflurane vs Hb r = 0.32 (P = 0.041) and vs haematocrit r = 0.37 (P = 0.016) are called trends only (99% CI includes 0). No sex or sample-site (venous/arterial) effect.

## Caveats
- The 5.07%, 12.12% and 7.55% "higher" in the abstract are against the authors' own number-weighted mean of previous studies (Table 3: 1.38, 0.66, 0.53), which mix volunteers, cardiac patients and mothers post-delivery, not against any model.
- Samples drawn fasting and before induction; in vitro, EDTA blood, not in vivo (patients had mostly ASA II-III, mean age about 60, a typical anaesthetic population but with no lipid or albumin measure).
- No a priori power analysis; method differs from earlier studies. Distilled water/gas for desflurane has no prior published value to compare.
- Wide CIs on the haemoglobin correlation; the authors note far more subjects would be needed.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `R/gasProperties.R:81`: `lambda_blood = c(0.47, 0.65, 1.3, 0.42, ...)` for N2O, sevoflurane, isoflurane, desflurane, GasMan's gasman.ini values (header comment :80, :12). Esper Table 3: sevoflurane 0.74, isoflurane 1.45, desflurane 0.57. Code lower by 12% (0.65 vs 0.74), 10% (1.3 vs 1.45) and 26% (0.42 vs 0.57) of Esper's value; equivalently Esper is 14%, 12% and 36% higher (derived). The code is also below Esper's own previous-study means (0.66, 1.38, 0.53). Desflurane's 0.42 equals Eger 1987's 0.424 (Table 1). **Discrepancy** in direction: Esper's higher values predict slower wash-in and wash-out for all three agents, desflurane most. Unit: both dimensionless ratios at 37 degC, no conversion issue.
- `R/gasProperties.R:87-89`: tissue:gas values are GasMan's, with desflurane brain 0.54 = 0.42 x about 1.3 (code comment :84-85); this paper does not measure tissue partitions and cannot verify them.
- The code's stated purpose is agreement with GasMan, so a difference from Esper is a model choice, not a transcription error. See `open_questions`.
