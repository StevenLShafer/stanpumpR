# Sepulveda 2018: hysteresis in propofol pharmacodynamics

## Study
Healthy volunteers, n = 19 enrolled (18 with measured concentrations; one lost IV access). Table 1 (18 volunteers, mean (SD), PDF p.3 image): age 31.1 (5.3) y, weight 75.9 (13.7) kg, height 172 (46.6) cm (the SD of 46.6 cm is as printed and is implausible; likely a misprint), baseline MAP 95.6 (11.1) mmHg, HR 67.3 (14.5), RR 14.7 (3.4), basal BIS 96.4 (1.8). ASA 1-2, BMI 20-30, age over 18.
Design: propofol by effect-site TCI with the original Schnider PK model, starting at Ce target 0.5 ug/mL, rising in 0.5 ug/mL steps every 7 min until loss of responsiveness (LOR). The LOR step was held 14 min; two more up-steps followed; then 7-min down-steps of 0.5 ug/mL until return of responsiveness (ROR).
End point: LOR/ROR = loss/return of eye opening or verbal response to the volunteer's name or a verbal command; three unblinded assessors had to agree. BIS recorded.
Sampling: 4 mL venous blood (contralateral arm) at the end of each 7-min step; 235 samples. Five volunteers also had paired arterial samples (57 pairs). Assay: plasma (serum in abstract), HPLC-UV with thymol internal standard, solid-phase extraction; linear 0.1-20 ug/mL, LOQ 0.1 ug/mL; interday CV 8.3, 8.5, 6.6% at 0.25, 5, 15 ug/mL. Total (not free) propofol.

## Model and parameters
No new PK model. The Schnider model was evaluated (Varvel performance errors, Table 3, PDF p.5): population MDPE -0.5%, MDAPE 26% for plasma concentration. By step (ug/mL; MDPE / MDAPE): 0-1 -36%/39%; 1.1-2 0%/18%; 2.1-3 5%/20%; 3.1-4 -9%/21%; 3-2.1 -6%/18%; 2-1.1 5%/29%; 1-0 35%/55%. Arterial minus venous: arterial higher, mean (SD) gradient 22% (93%), stable after the first 15 min (p.4 text; the "(93%)" is as printed).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: responsiveness to voice (LOR/ROR), with BIS as a second measure; Ce targets are Schnider effect-site (ke0 0.456 /min, as quoted in the Fig. 5 legend, p.7; 0.45 /min in the discussion).
- Measured venous Cp at LOR 2.6 vs ROR 1.6 ug/mL (median; p<0.001), i.e. apparent hysteresis. In the 14-min LOR step, Cp at minute 7 vs 14 was 2.59 (0.96) vs 2.58 (0.92) ug/mL (p = 0.96); BIS 61.24 (7.79) vs 58.15 (7.92) (p = 0.058).
- The authors do NOT conclude neural inertia: if the true ke0 is slower than 0.456 /min, 7 min may not equilibrate, so Cp overestimates Ce on the way up and underestimates it on the way down (Fig. 5 simulation).
- ke0 values quoted from other work (p.7, not measured here): Schnider model with BIS data 0.114 /min (Sepulveda 2012) and 0.17 /min (Doufas 2004); Marsh 0.26 /min; Thomson 2009 reaction-time ke0 0.6 /min. No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak is estimated in this paper.
- No MEAC, potency ratio or EC50 reported. Opioid audit: not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
Design cannot guarantee Cp-Ce equilibration (authors); assessors unblinded; venous not arterial sampling (arterial exceeds venous by ~22%, so arterial LOR/ROR values would be higher in both directions); n = 18; Table 3 MDPE for 3.1-4 step and some cells garbled in the text layer, confirmed on the page image; Table 2 ROR range is printed "2.3-0.8" (descending order).
