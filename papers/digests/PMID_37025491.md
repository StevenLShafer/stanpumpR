# Nie 2023: population PK of nalbuphine under general anesthesia

## Study
- Single-centre (Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University), elective surgery 2021. 47 adults: 27 (353 samples) for model building, 20 (100 samples) for external validation (PDF p1, p4).
- Model-building cohort (Table 1, PDF p5): mean ± SD age 53.85 ± 16.63 y (range 21–76), height 163.96 cm, weight 63.32 ± 9.3 kg (48–82), BMI 23.54, 15 female (55.6%), dose 0.24 ± 0.04 mg/kg (0.18–0.31), hourly net fluid infused (HNF) mean 617.96, median 563.56, range 234.26–1202.25 mL/h.
- Excluded: renal/metabolic/cardiopulmonary disease, long-term opioids etc. 55.6% had hepatobiliary disease; 5 were Child-Pugh B.
- Dose: nalbuphine 15 mg IV over 2–3 min at induction, then midazolam 0.05 mg/kg, sufentanil 0.2 µg/kg, etomidate, cisatracurium; sevoflurane/remifentanil maintenance (PDF p2).
- Sampling (build group): pre-dose, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30, 45 min, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 12 h. Plasma, UPLC-MS/MS after protein precipitation, LLOQ 0.1 ng/mL, range 0.1–500 ng/mL (total plasma, not free) (PDF p3).
- NONMEM 7.5.0, FOCE-I, PsN 5.2.6; exponential BSV; combined proportional + additive error.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, IV bolus; AIC fell 519.2 versus one compartment (PDF p4). Final covariate: HNF (power) on Q only (ΔOFV 9.643, p<0.005); ALT on CL was dropped in backward elimination (Table 3, p9). Final Q = 245 · (HNF/617.96)^−0.58 (PDF p4 equation; exponent −0.58 confirmed in Table 4 image).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD model. No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis reported.
- No MEAC or minimum effective/analgesic concentration reported. No plasma target concentration range is given.
- No equianalgesic or potency ratio is computed; the Introduction only states analgesic potency "comparable to morphine" (citing Errick and Heel 1983) and a ceiling on respiratory depression above 0.3–0.5 mg/kg (PDF p2).
- Clinical outcomes only (PDF p6): BIS 40–65 intraoperatively, one mild cough at intubation, no postoperative nausea or vomiting or agitation.

## Covariates
HNF (mL/h; (fluid + blood infused − urine out) / operation time) on Q, power −0.58 around 617.96 mL/h. Tested and not retained: ALT, GGT, Child-Pugh, heart rate, operation duration, cancer, weight, age, etc. (Table 3). Authors' simulations: HNF has limited effect; no dose adjustment advised (PDF p6). Fixed 15 mg was judged no worse than 0.2 mg/kg (bias <6%, lower variability).

## Caveats
- 27 patients to build; ωQ shrinkage 30.7%, Q RSE 14%.
- Co-medications (midazolam, sufentanil, sevoflurane) all CYP3A4 substrates, not modelled; mostly hepatobiliary surgery (authors, p13).
- Sampling stopped at 12 h; clearance 32.9 L/h is far below healthy volunteers (90 L/h, He 2021; 1.783 L/h/kg, Jaillon 1989, as quoted p9). The comparator is those volunteer studies and anesthetised-patient data, not another model.
- Internal inconsistencies: the text says the simulated fixed dose was 12 mg (Methods, p4; Figure 8) while patients received 15 mg; Table 6 (p13) is scrambled in the text layer; one external-validation concentration pair is quoted in µg/mL (Figure 8 legend).
- Intravenous bolus given as a 2–3 min injection; the model has no input duration.
