# Hung 1996: IM vs IV midazolam and diazepam absorption

## Study
- 4 healthy male volunteers, age 33.5 ± 9.0 y, weight 80.5 ± 9.7 kg, height 171.3 ± 4.8 cm (mean ± SD, p. 451).
- Randomised three-way crossover, ≥ 4 weeks apart: (a) simultaneous IM midazolam 5 mg + diazepam 10 mg in separate deltoids over 1 min; (b) IV midazolam 7.5 mg over 5 min; (c) IV diazepam 30 mg over 5 min. 50% O2 by mask.
- Sampling: IV, arterial at 0.5-5 min every 0.5 min, then 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 60, 90, 120 min; venous thereafter to 24 h (midazolam) or 10 days (diazepam). IM: arterial 5-120 min, venous to 10 days.
- Assay: GC (Arendt/Greenblatt method); detection limits 0.1 (midazolam) and 0.5 ng/mL (diazepam), CV ~5%. Plasma, total.
- Analysis: IV data per subject fitted to a three-compartment mammillary model (extended least squares). IM absorption by constrained (positive) numerical deconvolution (Verotta). F by deconvolution and by dose-normalised AUC (trapezoidal + extrapolation).

## Caveats
- n = 4 healthy men; each parameter a mean of four individual fits, large between-subject variability (authors).
- Diazepam IM F of 1.0 is higher than earlier reports (Divoll 1983); authors attribute to small all-male sample.
- Deconvolution assumes time-invariant disposition across occasions.
