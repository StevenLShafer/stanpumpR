# Mertens 2001: propofol alters alfentanil pharmacokinetics

## Study
Randomised two-way crossover, 8 healthy men, 20-30 yr (mean 24 +/- 3), 74 +/- 6 kg, 1.82 +/- 0.07 m, within 30% of ideal weight; 3-week interval (p. 949-951). Both sessions: alfentanil 12.5 ug/kg over 2 min then 25 ug/kg/h for 58 min, breathing 30% O2. Session B added propofol TCI (target 1.5 ug/mL, whole-blood measured means 0.85-1.75 ug/mL per subject) from 10 min before to 6 h after alfentanil. Venous plasma alfentanil samples (342 total) from 2 min to 6 h after stopping, contralateral arm; GC assay, CV <= 4%, detection limit 0.8 ng/mL. NONMEM V (first order), three-step Bayesian/NONMEM approach; three-compartment model with lag time (tlag, constrained 0-2 min); IIV log-normal; propofol presence or mean arterial pressure (MAP) tested as covariates.

Table 2 model: theta = typical x exp(eta + alpha x (MAP - median MAP)); lag time mapped to (0,2) min by a logit transform (Eqs. 2-4, Appendix). The median MAP is not stated in the text read. Stated effects of propofol: Cl1 -15%, Cl2 -68%, Cl3 -51%, tlag -62% (abstract; Table 1 gives 5.2/6.1 = 0.85, 104/326 = 0.32, 1.5/3.0 = 0.50, 0.70/1.88 = 0.37, consistent). Propofol covariate model: -2LL fell 52 vs no covariate; MAP model fell 61 (better) (p. 951-952).
Simulations (p. 953, 956): with propofol, alfentanil concentration was 674% to 82% higher in the first 3 min, 3-12% higher at the end of infusion and up to 88% higher at 420 min; context-sensitive half-time longer by about 3 min on average for infusions of 6-240 min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Pharmacokinetic study. Opioid audit: no MEAC or analgesic concentration, no ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis, no equianalgesic or potency ratio reported. Hemodynamics were measured (MAP and systemic vascular resistance lower with propofol, p < 0.0001 and 0.003; stroke volume p = 0.027, p. 952).

## Covariates
Propofol presence (Cl1, Cl2, Cl3 significant), then MAP (V1, V2, Cl1, Cl3, tlag). Body weight scaling only through per-kg parameters.

## Caveats
Venous sampling (hence the lag time; a model without it overestimated the first samples); eight young men; only one propofol level, so no extrapolation to other concentrations; the authors note a three-compartment model describes the early phase poorly and Cl2 in session A (326 mL/kg/min, CV 120%) is so large the model behaves nearly as two-compartment; extrapolation to fentanyl or sufentanil is cautioned (p. 956).

## Verification against stanpumpR
Not cited, so not-applicable. Independent comparison with R/drugs_alfentanil.R:29-35 (Scott/Shafer set, 70 kg reference; V1 2.1853, V2 6.70, V3 14.53 L; Cl1 0.199, Cl2 1.434, Cl3 0.247 L/min). Mertens control session scaled to 70 kg (derived: per-kg x 70): V1 7.07, V2 20.2, V3 8.68 L; Cl1 0.427, Cl2 22.8, Cl3 0.210 L/min. The library has smaller V1 (about 0.31x), larger V3, and Cl1 about half of Mertens' value; Cl3 is close. The model's central compartment differs because Mertens' lag-time model and venous samples absorb early mixing, so V1 and Cl2 are not comparable; the elimination clearance gap (0.43 vs 0.20 L/min) is a real difference between the studies (young male volunteers vs Scott's surgical patients; not checked here). The library's tPeak 1.4 min and MEAC 39 (R/drugs_alfentanil.R:40-41) cannot be checked from this paper. The propofol interaction is not modelled in the library: the paper implies alfentanil concentrations with propofol are higher, which the library's single set does not represent (cannot verify as a defect; the authors consider it minor clinically after the first minutes).
