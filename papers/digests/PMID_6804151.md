# Bauer 1982: ethosuximide kinetics and valproic acid

## Study
Two parts, University of Washington.

**Prospective three-phase study in 6 healthy adults** (2 men, 4 women), mean age 23.5 +/- 2.5 y, mean weight
66.6 +/- 12.5 kg, all within 15% of ideal body weight, normal examinations and laboratory profiles, no
smokers, no chronic medication including oral contraceptives (printed p 741).

- **Phase 1**: a single oral 750 mg dose (3 x 250 mg capsules) on an empty stomach; serum at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4,
  5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 24, 48 and 72 h; fractional urine 0-12, 12-24, 24-48, 48-72 h.
- **Phase 2**: ethosuximide 250 mg every 12 h for 10 days; one full 12-h dosing interval sampled
  (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 h) with a complete 12-h urine collection.
- **Phase 3**: the same ethosuximide regimen continued for 14 more days with **valproic acid 250 mg every
  12 h added**; sampling after the last ethosuximide dose at 0-72 h, urine 0-12 h.

Phases ran back to back, so phase 3 is compared with phase 2 within the same subjects.
Assay: HPLC with dual-wavelength monitoring (urine extracted with chloroform first); interday CV 5.7% at
30 ug/mL. Valproic acid by gas chromatography (interday CV 6.6% and 6.2% at 20 and 100 ug/mL).
Kinetics by model-independent equations; complete bioavailability assumed for both drugs. Paired t test for
two groups, two-way ANOVA for more.

**Retrospective chart review**: 106 patients on ethosuximide as sole therapy for absence seizures; 10 met a
strict steady-state criterion (stable dose >= 2 weeks, >= 2 levels a week apart agreeing within 10%, and
dose-concentration pairs at >= 3 doses within 6 months). Mean age 17.6 +/- 1.3 y, weight 61.1 +/- 11.2 kg.

The same table's right-hand block gives tpeak 2.0 +/- 0.9, 1.5 +/- 0.8 and 1.2 +/- 0.4 h and Cpeak
15.9 +/- 3.4, 33.7 +/- 9.4 and 32.8 +/- 6.8 ug/mL for phases 1-3, with mean steady-state concentrations
26.8 +/- 8.9 (phase 2, estimated from phase 1 clearance), 30.1 +/- 7.7 and 29.7 +/- 6.5 ug/mL.

**The study's two findings.**
1. Clearance **fell 15% between the single dose and steady state** (13.1 -> 11.1 mL/hr/kg, p < 0.05), with
   renal clearance essentially unchanged (3.0 -> 2.8), so the change is in nonrenal clearance
   (10.1 -> 8.3 mL/hr/kg, p < 0.05). The individual falls ranged from 2% to 26% (printed p 744). The authors
   attribute this to nonlinear (saturable) ethosuximide kinetics, by analogy with phenytoin.
2. **Adding valproic acid changed nothing**: total clearance 11.1 -> 11.2 and nonrenal clearance 8.3 -> 8.3
   mL/hr/kg; Figure 1's inset shows the phase 2 and phase 3 steady-state concentrations superimposed.
   The mean steady-state valproic acid concentration in phase 3 was **42.2 +/- 5.7 ug/mL**.

Chart review: in **7 of 10** patients the steady-state concentration rose more than proportionally with
dose (Figure 3, printed p 744); in 3 it was linear.

The authors reconcile their null with Mattson & Cramer (their ref 11), who saw ethosuximide rise 53% in four
patients who were also taking other antiepileptics but no change in the one patient on ethosuximide and
valproic acid alone, and conclude the interaction "does not appear to be a direct interaction" but may
involve other antiepileptic drugs (printed p 744).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Healthy volunteers, no seizure or other effect measure; the chart review's patients were treated for
absence seizures but only dose-concentration pairs were extracted.

## Covariates
None. Clearances are reported per kg; no age, sex or renal covariate is examined. Ideal body weight is an
inclusion criterion, not a covariate.

## Caveats
- **n = 6**, healthy volunteers, so the null on valproate is a small study with limited power; the authors
  title the paper "**possible** interaction".
- **Low valproate exposure**: 250 mg every 12 h giving a mean 42.2 ug/mL - roughly half the valproate
  concentration at which a clinically meaningful inhibition would be looked for, and about half the
  stanpumpR model's valproate reference (`c_ref` 75 mcg/mL). A concentration-dependent inhibition could
  plausibly be missed at this exposure. The paper does not raise this.
- **Low ethosuximide dose**: 250 mg every 12 h, giving steady-state concentrations near 30 ug/mL, below the
  usual therapeutic band - and in a drug the same paper shows to be **nonlinear**, where an interaction's
  size can depend on the operating concentration.
- The 15% phase-1-to-phase-2 fall is confounded between nonlinearity and time; a design with the same dose
  in both phases would separate them, and this one does not (750 mg single vs 250 mg q12h).
- Phase 2's half-life and volume could not be computed (12-h sampling), so the phase 2/3 comparison rests on
  clearance and concentrations alone.
- Complete bioavailability is **assumed**, not measured, for both drugs.
- The chart-review nonlinearity rests on 10 of 106 charts selected by a strict criterion - informative, but
  a small, selected sample.
