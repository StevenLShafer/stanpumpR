# Rydberg 1997: glibenclamide and metabolites, linked PK/PD

## Study
- 8 healthy Caucasian volunteers (4 men, 4 women, age 21-33 y, Table 1), randomized single-blind placebo-controlled crossover, five single-dose tests 3 months apart, fasting: placebo IV; 3.5 mg IV each of glibenclamide (Gb), 4-trans-hydroxy-Gb (M1), 3-cis-hydroxy-Gb (M2); 3.5 mg Gb tablet orally. Weight not given.
- Serum sampled 0-10 h (21 times) and urine to 10 h. Gb by HPLC (limit 1 ng/mL), M1/M2 by HPLC (limit 5 ng/mL); serum, total (not unbound). Metabolites were not measured in serum after Gb dosing; their profiles were inferred from urinary fractions, constant Gb clearance, and the IV metabolite disposition.
- Effect = percent reduction of blood glucose against placebo at matched times (glucose oxidase); data after 5 h were not used (meal confounding).
- NONMEM population analysis (first the PK fitted separately, then PD fitted to all data with the PK parameters held constant); sigmoid Emax effect-compartment model, with a competitive three-agonist model for combined Gb+M1+M2 action.

## Model and parameters
- Gb (IV and oral together): bi-exponential IV model, two-compartment oral model with first-order absorption and lag. M1 and M2: tri-exponential IV bolus models.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: percent blood-glucose reduction vs placebo (healthy fasting volunteers), serum total concentrations in ng/mL. Counter-clockwise hysteresis for Gb, M1 and M2 (Figure 1).
- ke0 (Table 4, PDF p7, printed 379): Gb 1.59/h, M2 0.479/h, M1 0.178/h; ke0 half-lives given in the abstract and text as 0.44, 1.4 and 3.9 h (ln2/ke0). No tPeak reported.
- CEss50 (steady-state serum concentration for 50% of Emax): Gb 108, M2 37, M1 23 ng/mL; Emax 56, 27, 40%. The text gives the abstract's CEss50 CVs as 25% (M1), 47% (M2), 26% (Gb).
- Model-free values (Table 2, PDF p5, printed 377), from log-linear regression of the descending limb; Gb and oral Gb values are "apparent" because metabolites add to the effect: EC20 M1 19, M2 42, Gb IV 50, Gb oral 87 ng/mL; EC50 Gb IV 243, oral 233 ng/mL.
- No MEAC defined by the authors. They cite an earlier radioimmunoassay study (not metabolite-specific) with a minimum effective glibenclamide plus metabolite level of 30-50 ng/mL. No potency ratio stated beyond the CEss50 comparisons (metabolites lower than Gb; P<0.05).
- Not an opioid paper; the opioid audit does not apply.

## Covariates
None. The final model assumed no baseline and one gamma for all subjects.

## Caveats
- n = 8 healthy volunteers, single 3.5 mg doses, no steady state; metabolite serum profiles after Gb are inferred, not measured.
- ke0 and CEss50 of Gb were fitted with the PK parameters fixed at the individual fits (Table 3 disposition), so ke0 belongs to that disposition.
- Concentrations are total serum; the authors suggest differing protein binding (Gb more bound than M1/M2) may explain the metabolites' greater potency.
- Gb CEss50 and ke0 are partly confounded with the inferred metabolite contribution.
