# Liu 2006: closed-loop BIS-guided propofol TCI vs manual TCI

## Study
Prospective randomized multicenter trial (3 sites). 164 analysed (manual TCI 81, closed loop 83) of 180 enrolled; 16 excluded for protocol violations. Adults 18-100 yr, ASA I-III, elective surgery of at least 30 min, no nitrous oxide. Weight 59 +/- 16 vs 58 +/- 15 kg (Table 1, text layer, scrambled).
Both groups: propofol TCI (Schnider 1999 PK-PD, ref 19) and remifentanil TCI (Minto 1997, ref 18) run by Infusion ToolBox 95 v4.8. Closed loop: proportional-differential controller on BIS (A-2000 XP) with a feed-forward term for BIS > 60; BIS target 50 at induction, 40-60 in maintenance; propofol target limits 1-5 ug/mL; target changes spaced by the time for 95% effect-site equilibration (Schnider model); remifentanil adjusted by hand in both groups. Gain constants not published.

## Model and parameters
No PK model was fitted and no PK parameters are reported; the paper only uses published models.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is BIS (target 50; 40-60 maintenance). Not an opioid PD study: no MEAC, ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or potency ratio reported.
Induction propofol dose 1.8 +/- 0.6 (manual) vs 1.4 +/- 0.5 mg/kg (closed loop) (abstract). Induction propofol target 3.8 +/- 1.2 vs 3.2 +/- 1.0 ug/mL (Table 2, text layer). Remifentanil induction dose 2.5 vs 2.7 ug/kg (Table 2, text layer).
Abstract results: induction 320 +/- 125 s closed loop vs 271 +/- 120 s manual; BIS 40-60 for 89 +/- 9% vs 70 +/- 21% of maintenance; BIS < 40 for 8 +/- 8% vs 26 +/- 22%; time to extubation 7 +/- 4 vs 10 +/- 7 min. Maintenance propofol 4.4 vs 4.8 mg/kg/h and remifentanil 0.22 ug/kg/min in both groups (Table 3, text layer).

## Covariates
Sex, age, weight and height entered for the Schnider model; no covariate analysis.

## Caveats
Prototype controller; authors hold copyright on the gain constants; unblinded; manual group acted as an active control (authors cite a Hawthorne effect); more ASA III patients and blood loss in closed loop; BIS reflects hypnosis, not analgesia.
