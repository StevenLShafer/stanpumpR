# Wasilczuk 2020: resistance to state transitions under volatile anaesthetics (mice)

## Study
Animal study, adult male C57BL/6J mice (n=60; abstract says 14-24 weeks, Methods says 16-24 weeks, a discrepancy in the paper itself). Three cohorts of 20 mice each breathed isoflurane 0.6 vol%, sevoflurane 1.0 vol% or halothane 0.4 vol% in 100% O2 in 200 mL chambers, four 4-h exposures per mouse over about 1 month. Concentrations were chosen in pilot work to be near the EC50 for loss of righting reflex (RR) and to give statistically indistinguishable righting probability (p.310). Fresh gas flow was one chamber volume per minute; concentration was verified with a Riken FI-21 refractometer. RR was tested every 3 min during the final 2 h of each exposure (40 trials per exposure, 160 per mouse); a trial was two chamber rotations and "responsive" meant righting on both. Isoflurane data had been published earlier (ref 8); the isoflurane analysis in Figs 5 and 6 is new.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Behavioural analysis: a 2x2 Markov transition matrix per mouse; Rst = [P(R|R) + P(U|U)] / 2 (0 = never stays in a state, 1 = never switches). Continuous model: diffusion on a two-well energy landscape, dx/dt = -D dE/dx|ec + noise, D = 1, noise sigma fitted per mouse by particle-swarm minimisation of the distance to the observed {P(U|U), P(R|R)}; effective concentration ec fixed to each mouse's overall righting probability. No drug concentration enters in mg or mL terms beyond the vol% above.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: righting reflex (loss of consciousness proxy) in mice. Population "potency" is the fraction unresponsive per trial; the three agents were equipotent (mean response probability not different, F[2,46.30]=0.5915, P=0.5576, Fig 5a). No ke0, EC50 value, MEAC or potency ratio is reported (the three vol% values are the near-EC50 concentrations used; the paper gives no EC50 estimate). Not an opioid paper. Key finding: P(U) was higher after an unresponsive trial than after a responsive one (median P(U) after R vs after U: isoflurane 0.45 vs 0.55, sevoflurane 0.31 vs 0.69, halothane 0.20 vs 0.80; Fig 4), and mice switched state less often than a Bernoulli process predicts (Fig 3). Rst differed among agents (F[2,56.01]=49.59, P<0.0001; all pairs significant by Dunnett, Fig 5b). Fitted noise differed (Kruskal-Wallis H=30.09, P<0.0001; halothane lower than both others, isoflurane vs sevoflurane P=0.84). The discussion argues that response at constant concentration depends on the previous state, not only effect-site concentration, which is contrary to the usual PK/PD assumption, and links it to anaesthetic hysteresis (no hysteresis measured here).

## Covariates
None analysed; male mice only, one strain. Individual response probability and Rst were not significantly correlated (Fig 5c).

## Caveats
Males only; mice, not humans; a behavioural binary end point at steady state; the isoflurane vs sevoflurane noise difference was not significant while the Rst difference was; authors note more trials may be needed. Rst is distinct from noise. Age range stated inconsistently (14-24 vs 16-24 weeks). Supplementary Table S1 not available to this digest.
