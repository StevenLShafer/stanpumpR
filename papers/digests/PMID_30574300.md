# Cooper 2018: intranasal vs intravenous droperidol in seven volunteers

## Study
- Open-label crossover (INKDROP, ACTRN12614000514606), Dandenong Hospital, Nov 2014 - Apr 2015.
- 7 healthy men, median age 23 yr (20-43), weight 73 kg (65-98), height 180 cm (170-195) (Results).
- IN arm first, then 1-week washout, then IV. Target dose 0.02 mg/kg; IN dose given in 12.5 kg weight bands
  (Table 1: 1, 1.5, 1.7, 2 mg) via mucosal atomiser, drug diluted to 0.25 mg/0.1 mL; IV 0.02 mg/kg diluted to 10 mL
  given over 10 min. Mean dose 1.53 mg IV (95% CI 1.3-1.7), 1.63 mg IN (1.4-1.8).
- Samples: pre-dose, 15, 30, 45, 60, 120 min, 4, 6, 8, 10 h (IV times counted from the end of the 10-min injection,
  so the first IV sample is 25 min after the start). EDTA **whole blood** assayed by UHPLC-MS/MS (VIFM), LLOQ 0.1 ng/mL
  (the paper calls them plasma/serum concentrations elsewhere).
- NCA (EquivTest PK); population model in S-ADAPT 1.57 (MC-PEM), IV and IN co-modelled; Monte Carlo in NONMEM 7
  (7000 subjects per regimen).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. Mild sedation in all subjects for 1-2 h; median RASS -1 at 30-60 min after IV, 0 otherwise; no QT
prolongation, no extrapyramidal effects. Authors state no concentration-effect data exist for droperidol (antiemetic,
migraine or sedation). Not an opioid; no ke0.

## Covariates
None tested (n = 7, men only).

## Caveats
Very small n, men only, single low dose. First IV sample 25 min after the start of injection: the authors say IV CL
from NCA is overestimated and model CL (and hence F) underestimated, so the IV branch is poorly defined; F lies
somewhere between 17% and 40%. Whole-blood assay. IN dose diluted; undiluted drug may absorb differently.
