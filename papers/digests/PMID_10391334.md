# McCullough 1999: cerebral Q10 during hypothermic circulatory arrest

Not a drug paper. The context file lists no repo locations, but `docs/gas-engine-expansion.md:191` (Gas Engine Expansion source table) summarises it, so the card checks that row.

## Study
- 37 adults, non-emergent ascending aorta/arch surgery with deep hypothermic circulatory arrest (DHCA), 31 Jan 1996 to 16 Apr 1997 (PDF p.2). Table 1 (text layer, order of rows): age 60 ± 16 y, cross-clamp 144 ± 44 min, CPB 228 ± 58 min, circulatory arrest 32 ± 15 min, minimum oesophageal temperature 10.8 ± 1.4 °C, 73% male, 92% discharged alive.
- Anaesthesia: high-dose opioid, 100% O2, muscle relaxant, isoflurane and midazolam as needed. Membrane oxygenator, non-pulsatile flow 2.0 to 3.95 L/min, cooled to oesophageal 15 °C, then "hard cooling" with 10 °C perfusate. Alpha-stat PaCO2 management.
- CBF estimated by an ultrasonic flow probe on the left common carotid artery; CMRO2 = CBF x (arterial minus jugular-bulb O2 content difference)/100. Nine sampling points from baseline through rewarming.
- Q10: ln CMRO2 regressed on temperature during cooling for each patient, mean slope m taken, Q10 = ratio of CMRO2 at t and t-10. Values expressed as % of the value extrapolated to 37 °C.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No drug. The end point is cerebral O2 consumption, not whole body. CBF is not coupled to CMRO2 during rewarming (CBF 126 ± 51% against CMRO2 45 ± 27% at 10 min). The authors note EEG silence appeared while jugular-bulb values still showed residual metabolism (PDF p.3-4).

## Covariates
None reported beyond temperature. Single patient intercepts, population slope.

## Caveats
- Brain only; the authors do not claim whole-body Q10.
- Slope SD 0.062 is large (individual Q10s vary widely); non-significant hint of steeper Q10 (3.5) below 15 °C, from a small subset; the authors say a larger sample might show it.
- CMRO2 from a single carotid flow probe, which assumes carotid flow represents whole-brain flow. Anaesthetic and hematocrit (23-26%) conditions were not controlled for temperature. Rewarming SDs are large.
- Retrospective review of a clinical database.
