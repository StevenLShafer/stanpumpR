# Coda 1995: epidural vs PK-matched intravenous alfentanil

## Study
Single-blind crossover pilot, 7 healthy volunteers (4 M, 3 F; 23-38 y, mean 29; 46.8-76.0 kg, mean 66.9; within 10% of ideal weight). Pretest: alfentanil HCl 15 ug/kg i.v. over 45 s, plasma sampled to 300 min, bi- or triexponential fit per subject. Day 1: epidural alfentanil HCl 750 ug (690 ug base) in 6 mL over 5 min plus i.v. saline. Day 2 (>= 10 days later): epidural saline plus a computer-driven i.v. alfentanil infusion in stepwise target concentrations (chosen by eye from day 1 curve, 4 h) to reproduce day 1 plasma concentrations. Plasma alfentanil by GC/MS (detection limit 0.05 ng/mL, interday CV 7.56% at 20 ng/mL); plasma (not whole blood), total drug. Pain model: electrical stimulation of finger and toe titrated to 5 (strong pain) on a 0-5 scale; also ETCO2, pupil size (pupillometer), VAS alertness, nausea, pruritus.

## Model and parameters
No population PK model; individual exponential fits only, not tabulated. Matching of concentrations: correlation of mean values 0.93, root mean squared discrepancy 2.81 ng/mL, about 84% agreement (p.333). Epidural peaks: about half the subjects 26-40 ng/mL early, others 15-20 ng/mL. Mean i.v. dose needed to match: 956 +/- 73 ug versus 750 ug epidural (p < 0.01).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC**: peak plasma concentrations were "near" a reported MEAC of approximately 40 ng/mL, attributed to van den Nieuwenhuyzen 1993 (p.334, confirmed on page image). This is a cited literature value, not measured here; plasma, total drug (assay as above); defined for postoperative analgesia in that other paper's patients.
- **ke0/t1/2ke0/tPeak/hysteresis**: none reported. Time course only: analgesia onset within 5 min, maximum at about 15 min, duration (pain report back above 4) about 1 h i.v., 1.5 h epidural. ETCO2 peak at about 25 min (rise 3.9 mmHg epidural, 3.4 i.v.). Pupil decrease max 20.0% (epidural), 24.6% (i.v.). Alertness VAS fell 63.6% / 71.8%.
- **Potency/equianalgesic ratio**: none; analgesia slightly greater epidural at toe at peak (P < 0.05), otherwise route differences not significant.
- Conclusion: most but not all of epidural analgesia explained by systemic redistribution; side effects equal across routes.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
n = 7, low power (Table I post-hoc power); epidural day always first (order effect, investigators unblinded); short plateaus in i.v. targeting gave errors of 3-5 ng/mL near peak; no epidural-saline plus i.v.-saline control day; experimental pain, not clinical.
