# Arzimanoglou 2016: rufinamide safety and population PK in LGS children aged 1 to under 4 years

## Study
- Study 303 (NCT01405053), phase III, multicentre (20 sites, North America and EU), randomised 2:1, active-controlled, open-label, 2 y; this is a 6-month interim report (data cut-off 28 Feb 2014) (p3, printed 395; p5, printed 397).
- 37 randomised (25 rufinamide, 12 any-other-AED; 1 never dosed), so 36 analysed; age 12-47 months, mean 29.2 months; mean weight at baseline 12.9 kg; mostly male (Table 1, p5).
- Rufinamide oral suspension 40 mg/mL, BID: start 10 mg/kg/day, +10 mg/kg every 3 days to 40, then +5 to a target of 45 mg/kg/day, added to 1-3 AEDs (p3).
- PK: sparse plasma samples during maintenance. Population PK in NONMEM v7.2 (FOCE-I) pooling Study 303 (n = 24, 1 to under 4 y) with Studies 022 (n = 65) and 304 (n = 26, Japanese), all LGS aged 4 y or older: 115 subjects, mean age 12.6 y (range 1-35), mean dose 1315 mg/day (range 160-4400); 40.9% female; co-medication valproate 66.1%, lamotrigine 32.2%, phenytoin 13.9%, carbamazepine 13.0% (p4, p6). Assay not described.
- Structural model: constant-input (infusion) model in CL/F only, because absorption could not be characterised (p4, p6). No volume or ka.

## Model and parameters
From p6 (printed 398; rendered):
- Basal CL/F 2.19 L/h (95% CI 1.94-2.44) (Supplementary Table 4, not available).
- Weight power exponent on CL/F 0.831 (95% CI 0.704-0.958). Reference weight not stated.
- Valproate decreases CL/F, "slope" -0.496 (95% CI -0.704 to -0.288); functional form not stated.
- IIV on CL/F 33.3%; additive residual SD 4.31 ug/mL.
- Dose-independent PK. Cav = (dose/interval)/(CL/F).
- Fig. 2 (p7, printed 399): weight-normalised CL/F medians roughly 0.11 L/h/kg (1 to under 4 y, n = 24) and 0.10 L/h/kg (4 y and over, n = 91); AUC medians roughly 200 ug.h/mL in both. Read from the plot, approximate.
- Model-derived Cav and AUC 13.9-33.0% higher with valproate across age groups (Supplementary Table 5, p7).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD model reported here: Cav was calculated for graphical PK/PD exploration (p4), but no results shown. No therapeutic range, no effect-site term. Efficacy not assessed in this interim. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
- Weight (power, exponent 0.831) and valproate (decrease) significant.
- Not significant: other AEDs (carbamazepine, lamotrigine, phenytoin), age, sex, race, hepatic function (alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin), renal function (CrCL by Cockcroft-Gault over 16 y, Traub-Johnson 1-16 y) (p4, p6).
- Comparator: "comparable" clearance and exposure means the 1 to under 4 y group against the 4 y and over LGS patients in the same pooled fit (Fig. 2), not against a separate model.

## Caveats
- Sponsor-run (Eisai) interim analysis; supplement (with the parameter table) not available.
- Sparse sampling; no absorption or volume; an infusion approximation of oral dosing.
- Only 24 children under 4 y in a 115-patient pool dominated by older patients.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **CL/F 2.19 L/h**: matches the paper (p6, rendered) as a value.
- **"(paediatric)"**: partly: the estimate is from a pooled LGS fit, 1-35 y (mean 12.6 y), not from the under-4 children alone; the reference weight behind 2.19 L/h is not given. See open questions.
- **"VPA lowers CL"**: matches (slope -0.496; exposure 13.9-33.0% higher with valproate).
- The row's structure ("BSA-scaled, dose-dependent F") comes from Marchand 2010, not from this paper, which found weight (not BSA) scaling and dose-independent PK. Different sources, not a transcription error, but the two descriptions disagree; noted for whoever implements rufinamide.
- Nothing is plotted (`proposed_unestimated`).
