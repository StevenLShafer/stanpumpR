# Sader 2006: daptomycin and vancomycin MBCs in MRSA, hVISA and VISA

## Study
In vitro susceptibility study (JMI Laboratories; funded by Cubist Pharmaceuticals), PDF p1-2 (printed 2330-2331).
- 207 *S. aureus* isolates. Of these, 102 were wild-type MRSA (MRSA-WT): oxacillin-resistant, homogeneous vancomycin MIC of
  2 ug/mL or less, from more than 50 medical centres worldwide in 2003. 88 were hVISA (vancomycin MIC 4 or less with
  a subpopulation above 4 at high inoculum). 17 were VISA (vancomycin MIC 8, homogeneous).
- CLSI reference broth microdilution (daptomycin with 50 mg/L calcium). Dilution ranges: daptomycin 128 to 0.12,
  oxacillin 4 to 1, vancomycin 32 to 1 ug/mL.
- MBC: the whole 100 uL from the MIC well and the wells above it plated. The MBC is the lowest concentration killing at
  least 99.9% of the starting inoculum.
- Tolerance: MBC/MIC of 32 or more, or MBC/MIC 16 with MBC of 16 ug/mL or more (PDF p2, printed 2331).
- Disk diffusion (30 ug disks) and Etest on all isolates. Time-kill curves at 2x, 4x and 8x MIC (vancomycin to 48 h,
  daptomycin to 24 h) on 18 MRSA-WT (15 vancomycin-tolerant, 3 with MBC = MIC) plus ATCC 29213, 43300 and 33591.
  Bactericidal means a 3 log10 or greater fall within 24 h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
In vitro end points only: MIC, MBC (at least 99.9% kill), tolerance (MBC/MIC), and time-kill (3 log10 or greater
reduction). Time-kill (Table 3, PDF p3): both drugs showed time-dependent killing, with little effect of 2x to 8x MIC.
Vancomycin generally gave about a 1 log10 fall at 8 h and 3 log10 at 24 h. Among the 15 tolerant MRSA-WT, vancomycin was
bacteriostatic in 9 (60.0%). Daptomycin was bactericidal at 4 h in 16 of 21 strains. Not an opioid paper; no ke0, MEAC or
potency ratio.

## Covariates
None (in vitro). Groups: MRSA-WT, hVISA, VISA.

## Caveats
- In vitro, standard inoculum; MBC plating method as described. The vancomycin dilution range stopped at 32 ug/mL, so
  MBCs above 16 are not resolved further (the top column is ">16").
- MRSA-WT are 2003 isolates; hVISA/VISA are a characterised reference collection, not consecutive clinical isolates.
- Table 1 internal inconsistency: hVISA vancomycin MBC 3 (3.4) at 1 and 9 (14.9) at 2 give 12/88 = 13.6%, not 14.9%
  (14.9% implies 13 isolates). Confirmed on the rendered page; it does not affect what the repo uses.
- Industry-funded (Cubist, daptomycin's maker).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/antibioticMbc.R:374 (Source):** tolerance 14.7% MRSA-WT, 69.3% hVISA, 100% VISA. These match Table 2 (rendered,
  PDF p3). **Matches.**
- **R/antibioticMbc.R:373 (Note):** "tolerant in 69% of hVISA and all VISA". **Matches.** The Note also gives
  "MBC 16 to more than 64 mg/L". This paper reports MBCs only up to its ">16" column (top dilution 32 ug/mL), and its
  Table 2 footnote says 16 or more. Nothing here supports "more than 64". **Discrepancy (minor)**: the upper value
  needs another source or should read "16 or more" / "above 16". The clause "which free vancomycin does not reach" is
  the library's own inference and is not from this paper.
- **Independent comparison (not cited for these values):** the "principal" vancomycin row for *S. aureus* MRSA and
  MSSA (R/antibioticMbc.R:362-371) carries MIC90 1 and MBC90 8 from Diekema 2019 and Honda 2011. In Sader's 2003
  worldwide MRSA-WT the MIC90 is 2 and the MBC90 is above 16 (derived from Table 1). The MBC50 of 2 agrees. Sader's
  MRSA-WT values run higher than the row's MIC90 and MBC90. The row's own note says the MBC90 is raised by a tolerant
  minority (4-27% by centre and method), and Sader's 14.7% tolerant falls in that range. The difference is one of
  population and era, not an error in the row.
- No daptomycin model or MBC row exists in the library (no "daptomycin" in R/), so the daptomycin data are unused.

Verdict: the tolerance percentages match. The "more than 64 mg/L" MBC bound in the same row is not supported by this
paper.
