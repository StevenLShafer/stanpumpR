# Thaulow 2023: dose-specific serum ranges for oxycodone, morphine, fentanyl

## Study
Retrospective TDM database (St Olavs, Trondheim, 2000-2020; 1004 samples, serum) plus a prospective cancer-pain group (Oslo, 2015-2016; 50 samples, whole blood converted to serum with blood/plasma ratios 1.4 oxycodone, 1.0 morphine, 0.9 fentanyl, 0.6 M3G/M6G). 1054 samples from 681 adults. Oral morphine and oxycodone, transdermal fentanyl only. Assay: UHPLC-MS/MS (LC-MS earlier); cut-offs in serum 5 nM morphine/oxycodone, 1 nM fentanyl; in whole blood 30 nM morphine, 50 nM oxycodone, 1 nM fentanyl. Concentrations in nM (nmol/L). Doses grouped by 24 h dose (mg/24 h) for oxycodone and morphine and by patch rate (µg/h) for fentanyl; 10th-90th percentiles per dose group, linear mixed models, and a calculated average steady-state concentration Cav = D·F/(di·Cl).

Table 1 (PDF p. 5, printed 781): oxycodone 607 samples, 399 patients, 44% male, median age 53 y (19-106), median dose 75 (5-3000) mg/24 h; morphine 246 samples, 171 patients, 64% male, 49 y, 200 (10-12,000) mg/24 h; fentanyl 248 samples, 167 patients, 31% male, 63 y, 62.5 (12.5-300) µg/h. (The text layer mislabels the sample/patient rows and prints fentanyl as mg/h; the rendered page shows µg/h.)

## Model and parameters

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- **MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration: none.** The paper explicitly has no response data (the authors say they did not know whether patients responded) and does not define a therapeutic or analgesic threshold. The ranges are reference ranges from the 10th-90th percentiles of measured steady-state concentrations (adjusted for dose linearity and calculations) in chronic-pain patients on stable oral CR or patch doses, in serum (total, not free; cancer-group whole blood corrected to serum), not effect concentrations.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported.** No effect-site or PD end point was measured here (pupil data from the cancer group are in another paper, ref 15).
- **Potency / equianalgesic ratios: none stated.** The cancer group were on at least the equivalent of 60 mg oral morphine for at least 4 weeks (p. 2); no conversion factor is given.
- Other quantities: concentration/dose ratios, nM per (mg/24 h): oxycodone CR trough samples median 1.2 (10th-90th 0.29-2.9) vs 1.6 (0.40-4.8) for all CR cases; morphine CR trough 0.46 (0.15-1.4) vs 0.70 (0.15-2.5) (p. 4). Mixed models: oxycodone +0.66 nM per mg/24 h; morphine +0.99 nM per mg/24 h and +345 nM in men; fentanyl +0.06 nM per µg/h and -0.13 nM per year of age (p. 4).
- Authors cite 350 nM as an upper "therapeutic" morphine figure from Schulz 2012 (ref 11).

## Covariates
Dose (strong, linear); sex (higher morphine in men, P = 0.038); age (lower fentanyl with age, P = 0.046; none for oxycodone and morphine). Not designed to test these. Body weight 79.1 kg assumed for per-kg clearances (Norwegian average).

## Caveats
Retrospective TDM samples, unknown adherence and co-medication, renal/hepatic function unknown; sampling time unknown for part of the data (median 8 h after dose for CR); no responder information; metabolites (except M3G/M6G measured but not part of the ranges) not covered; fentanyl cut-off 1 nM may drop low-dose patches; whole-blood to serum conversion in 50 cancer samples. Ranges are wide because interindividual variability is large; authors say they apply to steady state and not to Cmax of immediate-release doses. Values are serum nM, so ng/mL needs the molecular weights.
