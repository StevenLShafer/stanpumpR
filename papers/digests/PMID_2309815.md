# Rogers 1990: oral labetalol pharmacokinetics in pregnancy-induced hypertension

The PDF has 5 pages. PDF page 1 = printed 362, and it opens with the end of the previous article, Lockwood and
Peters. PDF pages 2-4 = printed 363-365. PDF page 5 = printed 366, the last references followed by the start of
another article.

## Study
- Population: 8 women in the third trimester with mild pregnancy-induced hypertension (preeclampsia). Gestational
  age 29.7 (SD 3.8) weeks, weight 78.2 (15.3) kg, age 19 (4.5) years; 7 primigravidas. They were a subset of a
  200-patient randomised study of labetalol (Table I, p. 363-364).
- Dosing: oral labetalol 100 mg every 8 h for at least 48 h before the study, which was presumed to be steady state.
  Serum was sampled at 0 (8 h after the previous dose), 20, 40, 60, 90, 120, 240, 360 and 480 min after a 100 mg dose.
- Assay: HPLC with UV detection (method of Wood et al.); recovery >86%; CV 4.3% at 125 ng/mL; lower limit
  <10 ng/mL; samples run in duplicate.
- Analysis: ke from log-linear regression of the terminal phase, assuming one compartment; t½ = 0.693/ke. AUC(0-8 h)
  by the trapezoidal rule. Apparent oral clearance was computed as dose per kg / AUC(0-8 h), per kg of actual body
  weight (p. 363-364).
- Placental transfer: 16 paired maternal and cord samples at delivery, plus 6 amniotic fluid samples.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No concentration-effect relationship, ke0 or target concentration is reported.
- Blood pressure averaged 142/91 mm Hg at entry and 131/82 mm Hg during therapy. There were no adverse effects
  (p. 364).

## Covariates
- None were modelled. Food delayed tmax and raised AUC but did not change CL/F, t½ or Cmax (p. 364).

## Caveats
- The study is small (n = 8), has no internal comparator, and is oral only, so F is unknown. The paper reports CL/F,
  not CL. The authors nonetheless compare it with IV clearance:
  - Rubin 1983, IV in pregnant women: 24.8 mL/min/kg.
  - Nonpregnant persons: 19-33 mL/min/kg.
  - Because the oral bioavailability of a high-extraction drug is incomplete, CL/F overstates systemic clearance.
    That makes this "similar clearance" claim weaker than it reads.
- The "shorter half-life" is measured against literature values for normotensive volunteers and nonpregnant
  hypertensive patients, given as 6-8 h in the abstract and Methods and as 3-8 h in the Comment.
  - The present half-life comes from sampling to only 8 h after a 100 mg dose, so a slower terminal phase could have
    been missed.
- In 6 of 8 patients the peak fell at the first sample, so Cmax and tmax are at the limit of the sampling design.
- Pregnancy was mid-to-late (24-34 weeks), and all the women had preeclampsia.
