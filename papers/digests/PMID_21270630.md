# Peyton 2011: Nitrous oxide diffusion and the second gas effect on emergence

## Study
- Randomized, two-group study; 21 adults recruited, 1 withdrawn (acute hypoxemia in the nitrous oxide group), 20 analyzed (10 per group). Elective general surgery expected to last 1 h or more with an arterial line. Age 64.7 ± 18.4 (control) vs 67.3 ± 13.0 y (N2O); weight 72.5 ± 16.4 vs 79.1 ± 21.0 kg; sex 5/5 vs 4/6 m/f; operative time 132.3 ± 43.0 vs 124.2 ± 62.6 min (Table 1, mean (SD)).
- Maintenance: sevoflurane titrated to BIS 40-60 in air-oxygen (control) or in 2:1 nitrous oxide-oxygen. Induction propofol 1.5-2.5 mg/kg, fentanyl 1-2 ug/kg and/or morphine 0.05-0.1 mg/kg, neuromuscular blocker; midazolam 1-2 mg premedication.
- At end of surgery: baseline arterial and tidal gas sample, fresh gas changed to 100% oxygen at 9 L/min with unchanged ventilation; samples at 2 and 5 min; arterial sample again at 30 min in recovery. Arterial sevoflurane by double headspace equilibration with an infrared gas analyzer; end-tidal by Datex-Ohmeda Capnomac.
- Primary end point: fraction of baseline arterial sevoflurane partial pressure remaining (Pa/Pa0-S) at 2 and 5 min. Curves fit with f = a t^b + c.

Derived/text: Pa/Pa0-S at 5 min was 39% higher in control (P < 0.04, Bonferroni); the discussion says 35%, an internal inconsistency (derived from Table 2: 0.32/0.23 = 1.39). End-tidal difference 23.7% overall, not significant. Eye-opening and extubation times were compared after removing one outlier per group (SDs 1.3-3.8 min; assignment of SDs to groups is ambiguous, see open_questions).
Baseline sevoflurane was lower with N2O: 22% lower arterial and 30% lower end-tidal (MAC-sparing; p.6). Of the difference in absolute arterial concentration, more than half was attributed to dilution (pharmacokinetic) rather than MAC sparing (p.6/601).
Arterial sevoflurane at 11 min (eye opening) in controls about 0.35% vs 0.4% in Katoh 1992 (p.5-6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, no ke0, no EC50. Clinical end points were time to eye opening and time to extubation, plus BIS at 2, 5, 10 min (no significant difference at 2 or 5 min). The authors note MACawake of nitrous oxide is nearly twice that for sevoflurane relative to their accepted MAC values (discussion, citing others; no value given here). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled. Baseline heart rate was lower in the N2O group (66.3 vs 56.3 beats/min, P 0.01); the authors estimate 10-20% lower cardiac output and argue it cannot explain the effect.

## Caveats
- n = 10 per group, older patients, unequal baseline sevoflurane (1.38% vs 1.06%); smokers included; significance after Bonferroni only at 5 min; 30-min difference not significant.
- End-tidal difference not significant; arterial effect was larger, which the authors attribute to V/Q heterogeneity (low V/Q units dominate arterial gas, high V/Q units dilute end-tidal).
- Table 1 PDF text layer lost row labels; row values here were read from the rendered page.
