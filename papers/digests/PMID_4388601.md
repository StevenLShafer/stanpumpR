# Thornhill 1969: in vitro activity and human pharmacology of cephalexin

## Study
- **In vitro**: clinical isolates: 25 each of S. aureus, E. coli, P. mirabilis, Klebsiella-Aerobacter (22 K. pneumoniae, 3 E. aerogenes); 10 each of indole-positive Proteus, Pseudomonas, D. pneumoniae, group A streptococci. Twofold broth dilution in Trypticase Soy Broth (pH 7.3; 2% sheep blood for pneumococci and streptococci) and nutrient broth (pH 6.6); inoculum 0.5 ml of a 10^-4 dilution of an 18-h culture; MIC = no turbidity at 18 h at 37 C; MBC = no growth on subculture of ~0.005 ml after 24 h (p458).
- **PK**: 7 healthy volunteers (5 men, 2 women, 20-30 years; weight not given), crossover, single oral doses: 250 mg fasting, 500 mg fasting, 500 mg after a standard breakfast, 500 mg after probenecid 0.5 g at 7 h and 1 h before; at least 2 days apart. Serum at 1, 2, 4, 6 h; urine 0-6 h. Also 6 patients on 500 mg every 6 h for urinary infection (Table 2). Assay: disc agar diffusion bioassay, Sarcina lutea ATCC 9341, standards in pooled human serum, standard curve accurate to 12.5 ug/ml (higher samples diluted). Total drug.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Table 1 (PDF p3, printed 459, rendered and confirmed), mean serum ug/ml at 1/2/4/6 h:
- 250 mg fasting: 6.8 / 4.8 / 1.0 / 0.2; urine 830 ug/ml; 253 mg excreted in 6 h.
- 500 mg fasting: 12.3 / 8.2 / 1.7 / 0.4; urine 1,097; 488 mg.
- 500 mg after breakfast: 11.0 / 7.9 / 2.3 / 0.5; urine 1,303; 485 mg.
- 500 mg with probenecid: 19.4 / 13.8 / 6.8 / 3.3; urine 1,101; 467 mg.
Mean of individual peaks (text p460): 7.7 (250 mg), 12.3 (500 mg with or without food), 19.7 (probenecid) (derived check from Table 1: 7.73, 12.30, 12.34, 19.74 -- consistent). Peak usually at 1 h; food delayed peak to 2 h in subjects 2 and 3. Urinary recovery >90% in 6 h. Patients (Table 2): peaks at least 10.9 ug/ml; highest (33.5 at 1 h) in a patient with BUN 22 mg/100 ml.

Derived (not by the authors): from 500 mg fasting means 8.2 -> 1.7 between 2 and 4 h, apparent t½ ~0.9 h (derived: 2 ln2 / ln(8.2/1.7)).

MIC data (Figs. 1-2, read from rendered plots, approximate; text p458-459 exact):
- Group A streptococci and D. pneumoniae: all inhibited by 3.1 ug/ml (text). Fig. 1: group A ~90% at 1.6 (read from figure).
- S. aureus: 88% by 6.3, 100% by 12.5 ug/ml in TSB (text).
- 12.5 ug/ml inhibited 80% of E. coli, 72% Klebsiella-Aerobacter, 56% P. mirabilis; 90-96% of these by 25 (text).
- Pseudomonas (10/10) and 9/10 indole-positive Proteus resistant to 50 ug/ml.
- MBC usually equal to or twice the MIC for all strains (p459).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio. Authors compare mean peak serum (500 mg) with MICs: enough for all group A streptococci, pneumococci and S. aureus, about 85% E. coli, 40-75% Klebsiella-Aerobacter and P. mirabilis (p461); urine enough for >90% of E. coli and P. mirabilis.

## Covariates
Food: no significant change in mean concentrations (p460). Probenecid: higher and more sustained concentrations (P < 0.05 at all times). Renal impairment (two patients) raised concentrations; not quantified.

## Caveats
Seven young volunteers; bioassay; standard curve capped at 12.5 ug/ml; authors note their 1-h values were ~75% of Lilly's own data (p460) -- i.e. lower than the manufacturer's unpublished volunteers, reason unknown. 1969 broth methods, inocula and media differ from CLSI; MBC/MIC statement is qualitative.
