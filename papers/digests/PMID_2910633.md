# Gengo 1989: diphenhydramine drowsiness vs impairment

## Study
Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, balanced crossover, 15 healthy men, 19-41 y (mean 23.8); 6-day washout; single oral capsule of diphenhydramine 50 mg (presumably the hydrochloride; salt not stated) with 250 mL water at 08:00 after an overnight fast; no weight reported. Venous blood (heparinized Vacutainer) at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2.5, 4.5, 6.5, 8.5, 12 and 24.5 h (text prints "1/2, 1, 1 1/2, 2 1/2, 4 1/2, 6 1/2, 8 1/2, 12, 24 1/2"); plasma, total drug, by GC with flame ionization (internal standard orphenadrine); CV 5.5% at 70 ng/mL and 22% at 5 ng/mL, limit of quantitation 4 ng/mL. Tests at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 24 h: Doron driving simulator reaction time, digit symbol substitution (90 s), 100-mm visual analog sleepiness scale. PK by PCNONLIN (first-order absorption, biexponential decline); PD by E = m log C + E0 (eq. 1) and Levy's eq. 2; effects as z scores against placebo and as % of maximum.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Drowsiness (VAS): significantly above placebo up to 6 h (p < 0.05). Impairment: driving reaction time significantly different at 2 h only (placebo mean 0.93 +- 0.3 s; difference about 91-93 ms); digit symbol significantly lower at 2 h only (p < 0.007). Errors: 4/630 placebo, 8/630 drug.
- Concentration thresholds (abstract, PDF 1; text PDF 5): the concentration at which the effect wore off in individual subjects (declining phase) was 30.4 to 41.5 ng/mL for drowsiness and 58.2 to 74.4 ng/mL for mental impairment (digit symbol). The regression lines cross the average placebo z (0.28 drowsiness, 0.32 impairment) at 41.5 ng/mL (drowsiness) and 68 ng/mL (impairment).
- These are offset concentrations on the falling limb after an oral dose, plasma, total, venous; not an onset threshold, not a MEAC. The authors report that effect decline rates are parallel, concluding drowsiness and impairment are probably one effect, and that impairment abates before drowsiness.
- ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none estimated; the data are on the declining limb only (the authors note the y-intercept is not the maximum effect) and sampling started at 2 h.
- MEAC and equianalgesic ratios: none.

## Covariates
None (sequence of treatment had no effect: 91 vs 93 ms driving-RT change).

## Caveats
Oral single dose, healthy young men; thresholds are from the washout side of the curve; PD time points sparse (0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 24 h); well-practised tasks; sponsor Pfizer.
