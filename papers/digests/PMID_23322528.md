# Dubbelman 2013: 14C-bendamustine mass balance and PK in cancer patients

## Study
Phase I, open-label, single-centre mass-balance study (PDF p. 2). n = 6 adults with relapsed or refractory malignancy (3 men, 3 women; median age 66 y, range 48-75; mean weight 72.7 kg, range 59-94; mean height 173.2 cm; mean BSA 1.9 m2, range 1.6-2.2; WHO performance status 0 in 4, 1 in 2; Table 1, PDF p. 4). Exclusions included CYP1A2 inducers/inhibitors and smoking; eligibility required CLCR above 30 mL/min (the symbol was garbled in the text layer; "> 30" is read from context, not confirmed on an image).
Dose: single 60-min IV infusion of 120 mg/m2 14C-bendamustine HCl (80-95 uCi) on day 1; unlabelled 120 mg/m2 on day 2. Table 2 footnote: the printed dose in mg (172-233 mg) is bendamustine FREE BASE. Blood at 0.25 h to 168 h (22 samples), urine in intervals to 168 h, faeces daily.
Assay: LC-MS/MS for bendamustine, M3 (gamma-hydroxy), M4 (N-desmethyl) and HP2 (dihydroxy) in plasma through 24 h only (LLOQ 0.5 ng/mL; HP2 1 ng/mL); total radioactivity (TRA) by liquid scintillation. Noncompartmental analysis, WinNonlin 4.1.a. No population model, no covariates.

Time to Cmax: median tmax 1.10 h for TRA, bendamustine and HP2; M3 1.26 h; M4 1.28 h (PDF p. 5). Plasma:whole-blood TRA ratio about 1.4 at end of infusion, about 1 later (Fig. 3).
Excretion (Table 3, PDF p. 7, text layer only): 0-168 h urine 45.46% of radioactivity, faeces 25.18%, total 70.64%; to end of collection (168-504 h) total 75.98%. 0-24 h urine: unchanged bendamustine 3.31%, M3 0.73%, M4 0.08%, HP2 4.89%, sum 9.01%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No concentration-effect, MEAC, ke0 or effect-site data. Safety only: all six patients had grade 3/4 lymphocyte count decrease; fatigue and vomiting 50% each. Not an opioid paper; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
None analysed (n = 6). Patients are cancer patients with CLCR above 30 mL/min and normal-ish liver function; the authors argue from half-life and excretion, not from data, that renal or hepatic impairment is unlikely to cause accumulation.

## Caveats
- n = 6, noncompartmental; the third (terminal) phase seen by Owen is not detected because the LLOQ is 0.5 ng/mL versus 0.1; authors say it carries under 1% of AUC (PDF p. 8).
- Vss and CL here are from noncompartmental analysis of a 60-min infusion; Vss 20.1 L is much lower than a compartmental Vss that includes a deep peripheral compartment.
- Dose basis is free base (Table 2 footnote). Per-patient dose/BSA = 0.895-0.897 of 120 mg/m2 (derived for patients 1 and 2), against a base/HCl molar fraction of 0.9076 (358.26/394.72, from the repo header); small difference unexplained.
- Plasma metabolite and unchanged-drug concentrations only through 24 h; the long-lived TRA (t1/2 197 h) is unidentified material.
- Text layer of Table 2 is scrambled: the rows are shifted by one analyte block in the plain text.
