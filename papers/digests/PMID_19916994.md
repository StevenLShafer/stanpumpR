# Barras 2009: enoxaparin PK and a proportional-odds model of bleeding and bruising

## Study
- PK and PD data from an RCT (Brisbane; ref 16) of conventional (label) vs individualized enoxaparin dosing in patients
  treated for PE, DVT, ACS or atrial fibrillation. Individualized arm: >=100 kg dosed on LBW (1.5 mg/kg twice daily),
  <100 kg on total weight (1 mg/kg twice daily); CLcr < 50 mL/min dose-reduced at 48 h (PDF p2).
- Exclusions: liver enzymes > 2x normal, pregnancy, age < 18, warfarin or heparin in the past 7 days, INR > 1.2,
  aPTT > 60 s, CLcr < 10 mL/min, haemodialysis (PDF p2).
- PK cohort n = 118: age median 61 (23-91) y; 45 F / 73 M; weight 77 (43-120) kg; LBW (Janmahasatian) 55 (30-86) kg;
  CLcr (C-G, total weight) 85 (15-244) mL/min; CLcr (C-G with LBW) 70 (10-170) mL/min (Table 3, PDF p5). PD cohort
  n = 103 (15 had no bruising assessment beyond baseline).
- 349 anti-Xa concentrations, mean 3 (1-4) per subject, D-optimal windows predose, 15-30, 60-120 and 180-300 min; 93% in
  the first 48 h. Chromogenic anti-Xa (STA-Rotachrom), LLOQ 0.1 IU/mL, interassay CV 5.9%; 6 samples (1.7%) below LOQ
  (PDF p2-3).
- Events assessed daily by a research nurse: S1 no event (n = 36), S2 minor bruise 1-20 cm2 (n = 40), S3 bleed or major
  bruise >= 20 cm2 (n = 27) (Table 2, PDF p4). Mean +/- SD treatment duration 3.5 +/- 2.3 days (PDF p10).
- NONMEM V, FOCE-I; bootstrap 1000 (873 PK runs minimized); VPCs (PDF p3-4, p7).

## Model and parameters
PK: two-compartment, first-order absorption, linear elimination, additive residual error; BSV on CL, Vc, Ka with Vc-CL
covariance. Final covariate model (Eq. 3, PDF p6):
CL (L/h) = 0.3 x CLcr/70 + 0.42 x LBW/55, with CLcr from Cockcroft-Gault using LBW (mL/min) and LBW in kg;
Vc (L) = 3.43 x LBW/55.

Base model (Table 4): CL 0.69 L/h, Vc 3.46 L, Ka 0.25 1/h, Vp 6.14 L, Q 0.3 L/h; OFV -859.9 -> -909.16. Typical total CL
at CLcr 70 and LBW 55 is 0.72 L/h (derived). BSV fell 21.3% (CL), 16.3% (Vc) and 7.4% (Ka), relative (PDF p6; consistent
with Table 4, derived).

PD (Eq. 4, PDF p8): logit P(S <= 1) = 2.83 - 2.75 x Age/61 - 0.536 x cAUC/23; logit P(S <= 2) = logit P(S <= 1) + 2.05,
with cAUC the cumulative anti-Xa AUC from first dose to the event (median 23, range 4-120 h*IU/mL; Table 3 prints the
unit as h IU-1 mL-1). No random effects. At median age and cAUC this gives P(S3) = 0.17 and P(no event) = 0.39
(derived); observed S3 was 27/103 = 0.26.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: occurrence and severity of bleeding or bruising (three ordered categories), not anti-Xa. Age and cAUC were
  the best predictors (dOFV 14.9 and 15.2 alone, 28.3 together); Cmin and CLcr were significant alone, but AUC0-24, Cmax,
  body size, Emax and power models did not improve the fit (PDF p7). Score test for proportional odds p = 0.78.
- Exposure in the PD cohort (Table 3): Cmin 0.45 (0.1-1.33) IU/mL, Cmax 0.91 (0.46-3.38) IU/mL, AUC0-24 13.9 (6.9-25.3).
- Simulations (PDF p8): obese (150 kg) plus CLcr 30 mL/min, probability of an S3 event 0.41 conventional vs 0.25
  individualized at 72 h, 0.58 vs 0.30 at 96 h.
- No therapeutic anti-Xa target, no ke0. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
LBW (Janmahasatian: male 9270 x Wt / (6680 + 216 x BMI), female 9270 x Wt / (8780 + 244 x BMI); PDF p3) on both CL
pathways and Vc; CLcr by C-G with LBW on renal CL. Allometric and other forms gave no further benefit (PDF p5-6). Age on
the PD model.

## Caveats
- Sparse sampling, mostly in the first 48 h; few severe renal impairment patients (7% of subjects, 5% of samples), so
  the nonrenal CL is larger than the 30% previously reported (PDF p9).
- No independent validation; PD cannot separate exposure from treatment duration; therapeutic failure not modelled
  (PDF p10).
- The limitations paragraph counts "a total of 63 events" (PDF p10), but Table 2 gives 40 minor + 27 major = 67 events
  in 103 subjects; the paper does not reconcile the two (unresolved).
- Units printed for Ka (h) and cAUC (h IU-1 mL-1) are misprints for 1/h and h*IU/mL.
