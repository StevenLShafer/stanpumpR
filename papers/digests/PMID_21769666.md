# Mir 2012: doxorubicin and ifosfamide for sarcoma in the third trimester

## Study
Retrospective single-centre series (Institut Gustave Roussy, 1998-2008): 9 women with high-grade sarcoma
diagnosed in the third trimester among 512 women aged 15-45 referred; 4 diagnosed after 34 weeks were
delivered first, 5 (median age 30 y, range 26-34; median gestational age at diagnosis 27 weeks, range 25-28;
Table 2, PDF p. 3) received pre-partum chemotherapy. Regimen "AI50" (Table 1, PDF p. 2): doxorubicin
50 mg/m2 IV bolus day 1, ifosfamide 2.5 g/m2/day as a 48-h continuous infusion days 1-2 (5 g/m2/cycle) with
3 L/24 h saline, mesna 100% of the ifosfamide dose, every 3 weeks; deliberately lower than the non-pregnant
doses (doxorubicin 60, ifosfamide 6-9 g/m2), no G-CSF. 9 cycles in pregnancy (median 2 per patient).
The rest of the paper is a literature review of ifosfamide (n = 11 incl. these 5, Table 4), etoposide
(n = 25, Table 5) and dactinomycin (n = 8, Table 6) in pregnancy.

PK, case 5 only (p. 358): 8 blood samples per cycle, plasma at -20 C, ifosfamide by validated HPLC-UV
(ref. 8, Brain 2007), parameters with Micropharm. Cord blood and amniotic fluid sampled at delivery.
No weight, height or BSA reported for any patient.

## Model and parameters
No model; only the measured concentrations of one patient. Table 3 (PDF p. 3, read on the page image),
ifosfamide 5 g/m2 over 48 h, ug/mL in maternal blood, time from start of infusion:

| time | cycle 1, pregnant (29 weeks) | cycle 2, day 10 post-partum |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | <5 | <5 |
| 24 h | 24.7 | 23.4 |
| 48 h | 20.3 | 19.6 |
| 48 h 15 min | 19.6 | 18.9 |
| 48 h 30 min | 19 | 18.3 |
| 49 h | 16 | 15.2 |
| 51 h | 13.1 | 10.1 |
| 54 h | 6 | 7 |
| 60 h | 2.8 | <5 |
| 72 h | <5 | NA |

(Note the 2.8 at 60 h is printed although it is under the stated <5 limit.)
Derived (not in the paper): post-infusion terminal half-life from 48 h to 54 h, ln(20.3/6)/6 h = 0.203 /h,
t1/2 3.4 h pregnant; ln(19.6/7)/6 h = 0.172 /h, t1/2 4.0 h post-partum. The fall from 24 h to 48 h while
the infusion runs at a constant rate (24.7 to 20.3, 23.4 to 19.6) is what autoinduction predicts.
Authors (p. 361): post-partum clearance and AUC "similar" to pregnancy; no values printed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. Clinical end points only: grade 3 alopecia (5), grade 3-4 neutropenia (3), no nausea/emesis
with metoclopramide 10 mg tid + ondansetron 8 mg bid; all deliveries premature; oligoamnios in case 5 and
in 5 of 11 literature ifosfamide cases (authors suggest foetal renal toxicity). Ifosfamide not detected
(<5 ug/mL) in maternal blood, amniotic fluid or cord blood at delivery (p. 358). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled. Case 5 had albumin 30 g/L and lymphopenia 680/uL post-partum.

## Caveats
- n = 1 for PK; dose in mg, BSA and weight not given, so no clearance can be computed from the paper.
- The "no transplacental transfer" claim rests on samples taken at a caesarean more than 3 weeks after the
  last cycle, when maternal ifosfamide was also undetectable; it does not test transfer during exposure.
- The comparator is day 10 post-partum, not a non-pregnant baseline; a different cycle of the same regimen.
- Assay limit stated as <5 ug/mL, yet 2.8 is reported at 60 h.
- Journal issue is 2012 (69:357-367); online 2011.

  Kerbusch's patients were non-pregnant adults on 72-h infusions of 9-12 g/m2; a direct clearance
  comparison is not possible without the patient's BSA.
