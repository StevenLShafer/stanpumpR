# Dahlstrom 1982: morphine kinetics around abdominal surgery and the PCA minimum effective concentration

## Study
Uppsala. 10 adults for major abdominal surgery (5 inflammatory bowel disease, 4 intra-abdominal malignancy), no
hepatic or renal dysfunction, no analgesics for 48 h (PDF p2). Table I (PDF p3): 6 women, 4 men; age 38 +/- 15 y
(22-58); weight 63 +/- 10 kg (48-80); anaesthesia 260 +/- 120 min and blood loss 1790 +/- 1140 mL in the 8
uncomplicated cases (two needed reoperation for bleeding). Neurolept anaesthesia (thiopentone, droperidol, fentanyl,
relaxant), controlled ventilation. Morphine hydrochloride 10, 15 or 20 mg IV bolus during anaesthesia; then PCA in
the ICU, preset 2 or 3 mg IV doses, lockout 15 min, for 18 +/- 6 h; central venous sampling. Repeat study on
postoperative days 3-7 with 4 or 5 mg IV. Assay: gas-liquid chromatography with electron-capture detection (glass
capillary), working detection limit 1 ng/mL; concentrations are total plasma morphine base (PDF p4). Triexponential
fits per patient in NONLIN (multiple unequal doses).

Duration of anaesthesia correlated inversely with perop CLp (r2 0.48, P < 0.05); blood loss correlated with the
perop/postop change in Vdarea (r2 0.42) (PDF p8).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
**MEC (Table IV, PDF p9, rendered; Methods PDF p4).** Defined per patient as the mean of MECt values, each the plasma
concentration calculated from the patient's fitted triexponential function 2 min before each patient-initiated PCA
dose; a model-predicted demand concentration, not a measured one. Population: 10 adults after major abdominal surgery,
day of surgery. Assay: total plasma morphine base, GLC-ECD. Mean MEC **16 +/- 9 ng/mL** (n 10; individual 6, 7, 7, 12,
14, 17, 18, 20, 26, 33 ng/mL, so range 6-33). Mean measured plasma concentration during PCA 21 +/- 12 ng/mL (9-39);
consumption 2.6 +/- 1.2 mg/h (1.1-4.0). MEC was stable after a 4-h equilibration (trend 0 +/- 5%/h; four patients
changed 5-11%/h). Consumption correlated with mean concentration (r2 0.61) and MEC (r2 0.79), not with clearance,
age or weight (PDF p8). Men vs women (PDF p8): MEC 21 +/- 11 vs 13 +/- 6 ng/mL, concentration 26 +/- 12 vs 18 +/- 11,
consumption 2.0 +/- 1.2 vs 2.4 +/- 1.1 mg/h (not significant); Table IV's four men average 2.9 mg/h (derived), not
2.0, while the women's 2.35 matches: a source inconsistency.
**Equianalgesia (PDF p12-13):** from PCA consumption (morphine 2.6, pethidine 26, ketobemidone 2.3 mg/h, the latter
two from Parts II-III), morphine 10 mg = ketobemidone 8.8 mg = pethidine 100 mg; from Css x CL at each MEC (morphine
1.5 +/- 0.7 mg/h as the hydrochloride), morphine 10 = ketobemidone 9.3 = pethidine 113 mg.
**No ke0, tPeak or hysteresis** estimated. The authors compare their 21 ng/mL with about twice that by radioimmunoassay
(Berkowitz 1975) and attribute much of the RIA difference to cross-reacting glucuronide (PDF p11-12). One patient had
hypercapnia (PaCO2 6.9-7.5 kPa) during hypovolaemia (PDF p6).

## Covariates
None in a model; regressions only (above).

## Caveats
Ten patients, one centre; MEC is a calculated concentration from each patient's own fit, sampled irregularly during
PCA. PCA dose salt not stated explicitly (the bolus and the infusion-rate calculation are hydrochloride; concentrations
are base). Source inconsistencies: Summary CLp 20 vs text 21 ml/min/kg (both round 20.5); men's consumption (above);
t1/2 beta header unit.
