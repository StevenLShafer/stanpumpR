# Achong 1976: duration of the cardiac effects of timolol and propranolol

## Study
- Design: single-blind crossover in 6 healthy men, over two successive 3-week periods. In each period a subject
  was studied on 3 occasions a week apart and received one of placebo (5% dextrose in water), timolol 1 mg or
  propranolol 10 mg, in the order of Table I (PDF p2, printed 149).
- Subjects: 6 healthy men, 21 to 32 (mean 25.2) y, 62 to 80 (mean 71.4) kg; normal history, examination, ECG,
  chest x-ray and hepatic and renal indices; no alcohol or other drugs for the study's duration.
- **Administration: a 10-minute infusion, not a bolus.** "The drugs were administered in volumes of 10 ml at a
  constant rate of 1 ml/min by a servo-control infusion pump (Harvard)" into the sidearm of a rapidly running
  5% dextrose infusion (PDF p2, printed 149, confirmed on the rendered page).
- Dose basis: propranolol was the proprietary **hydrochloride** (Inderal) at 1 mg/mL, so the 10 mg is the salt as
  labelled (PDF p3, printed 150). Timolol was the l-isomer of timolol maleate, 0.25 mg/mL; isoproterenol was the
  hydrochloride (Isuprel) 0.2 mg/mL. All solutions diluted in 5% dextrose immediately before use.
- **Sampling schedule: 5 min, 90 min, 4 hr, 9 hr and 24 hr AFTER THE END of the infusion** (PDF p2, printed 149,
  confirmed on the rendered page). A beta-receptor stimulus was applied at each of those times. Studies started
  between 7 and 8 A.M. with the subjects fasting from midnight; food was allowed after the 4 hr recording.
- Stimulus, first 3 weeks: 4 min of upright exercise on an electrically braked ergometer (Elma-Schonander 369) at
  a constant load set to give at least 150 bpm at 4 min; 1200 kilopond meters/min in 5 subjects, 1100 kpm/min in
  the sixth. Heart rate at the end of the fourth minute was the average of ten R-R intervals.
- Stimulus, final 3 weeks: isoproterenol 0.04 mcg/kg/min by servo-control pump; recordings during the fourth
  minute of that infusion.
- Endpoints: per cent change in maximal exercise tachycardia, MET, as (MET_t - MET_o) / MET_o x 100; per cent
  change in the pre-ejection period of left ventricular systole from systolic time intervals (Weissler's method,
  10 beats, 100 mm/s paper) as an index of contractility; and the isoproterenol-induced rise in heart rate.
- **No drug concentrations were measured.** There is no pharmacokinetic sampling, no assay and no PK model in this
  paper; the authors attribute their intersubject variation to "the presumed variable serum concentrations"
  (PDF p3, printed 150). The only half-lives given are quoted from elsewhere: timolol 5.5 hr (a Merck-Frosst
  brochure) and propranolol 3 to 4 hr (Shand 1970, their ref. 7).
- Statistics: means +/- SEM for 6 subjects, two-way analysis of variance (Sokal and Rohlf), p < 0.05.

## Model and parameters
No structural model and no pharmacokinetic parameters: this is a pharmacodynamic time-course study. The only
quantity stanpumpR takes from it is the time of the largest effect.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Exercise: "The reduction in MET was maximal 5 min after the end of the beta-antagonist infusions" (PDF p3,
  printed 150, confirmed on the rendered page). The reductions by timolol and propranolol over 24 hr did not
  differ significantly, and both differed from placebo in every observation up to 4 hr. At 9 hr the change in
  exercise tachycardia no longer differed from placebo.
- **Fig. 1 plots TIME in HOURS and places the 5-minute observation at the origin** (read on the rendered page,
  PDF p3). Propranolol's mean change in MET is about -16% at that first point, about -14% at 90 min, -10% at
  4 hr, -3 to -4% at 9 hr and near zero at 24 hr; timolol is about -18% at the first point and within a couple of
  points of propranolol thereafter; placebo stays within about +1.5 to -2%. So the figure shows a monotonic
  decay from the first observation, not a rise to a peak: nothing in it locates a maximum earlier than 5 min.
  (Magnitudes read off the figure: approximate, no table of values is printed.)
- Isoproterenol, inotropic: PEP was prolonged up to 90 min, reflecting complete blockade; significantly different
  from placebo in every observation up to 9 hr; no timolol-propranolol difference over 24 hr. Fig. 2: propranolol
  about +24% change in PEP at the first point, timolol about +6%, placebo about -50%.
- Isoproterenol, chronotropic: almost complete blockade up to 90 min; only the 24 hr responses did not differ
  from placebo (Fig. 3, PDF p4).
- Potency: 1 mg timolol and 10 mg propranolol gave a similar degree of blockade, confirming the 10- to 13-fold
  ratio of the authors' 1975 paper (their ref. 1).
- No ke0, no t1/2 ke0, no EC50, no Emax and no concentration-effect relation of any kind is reported.

## Covariates
None. A fixed dose was given to every subject; the authors report only that intersubject variation in effect was
significant and presume it reflects concentration differences.

## Caveats
- The authors' own: the single intravenous doses "should be extrapolated with caution to the effects of chronic
  oral therapy"; the magnitude of demonstrable blockade was much smaller against exercise than against
  isoproterenol, so the stimulus chosen matters; an exercise stimulus of this size is essential because smaller
  increases in heart rate are mainly vagal withdrawal.
- Mine, and the reason this card exists: **5 min is the first sampled time, measured from the end of a 10-minute
  infusion.** It therefore bounds the time of maximal effect from above rather than locating it, and it is not a
  post-bolus time to peak effect. Six subjects, one dose level, no concentrations.
- Internal inconsistency: the Results give the pre-infusion MET as "on average, 167 bpm (range, 157 to 178)"
  (printed 150), while the Discussion says the workload "produced a maximal tachycardia prior to beta-antagonist
  administration in the range 158 to 167 bpm" (printed 151). The Results figure is the one to use.
- The copy in the archive is a Wiley download stamped on each page; the stamp text appears in the extracted text
  layer and should not be mistaken for content.

## Verification against stanpumpR
**tPeak = 5 min (`PROPRANOLOL_TPEAK`, R/drugs_propranolol.R).** The paper does say the reduction in exercise
tachycardia was maximal 5 min after the infusion, so the number is transcribed correctly: **matches** as a
transcription.

Two facts the code could not have had, because until 2026-10-28 only the abstract was available, and both now
read from the full text:

1. The 5 minutes is measured **after the end of a 10-minute infusion**, not after a bolus.
2. It is the **first of five observations** (5 min, 90 min, 4, 9, 24 hr), and Fig. 1 plots it at the origin of an
   hours axis, so the study cannot resolve a maximum earlier than 5 min.

A bolus-equivalent conversion would therefore be defensible, as the library already does for diltiazem (3.1 min,
the bolus equivalent of Salerno 1989's 4.3 min after a 2-minute infusion), and would give a **shorter** tPeak.
**Steven L. Shafer decided on 2026-10-28 to keep 5 minutes and make no conversion.**

**The repo has since recorded this (PR #603, master c3365bc9), and goes one better than the paragraph above:**
it classifies the parameter's evidence as `clinical` rather than `full_text`, on the ground that the paper "is
consistent with 5 minutes, not its source" -- which is the right call, since a study that cannot locate a bolus
time to peak cannot be the source of one. `R/drugs_propranolol.R`'s header, the help page, the registry row
(`no_suitable_model`, read in full) and the parameter audit (`evidence: clinical`, `provenance: fixed`) all say
so. Nothing is outstanding against this paper.
