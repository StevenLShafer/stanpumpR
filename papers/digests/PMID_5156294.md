# Harris 1971: oxygen consumption on bypass with moderate hypothermia

## Study
27 adult patients (Table I, PDF p2; printed p1114) having cardiac operations (mostly homograft aortic valve) on cardiopulmonary bypass, in three consecutive series. Series I (patients 1-7) and III (19-27): flow about 2.0 L/min/m2 or more; Series II (8-18): "warm start" up to 17 min, then flows varied at 20-min steps around 50, 60 and 75 mL/kg/min. 14 patients had a normothermic "warm start" of 5-17 min, the rest were cooled at once to 30 C (lowest temperatures 28.0-32.1 C, Table I). Nine Series III patients also had pre-anaesthesia VO2 by Douglas bag. On bypass, VO2 = arterial pump flow x arteriovenous O2 content difference (Huffner factor 1.36, nasopharyngeal temperature). Not a drug study; no drug doses or concentrations.

## Model and parameters
Regression of log VO2 (corrected to 65 kg by multiplying by 65/weight) on temperature t and log flow Q (p3-4). Weight-scaling candidates (weight, BSA, W^0.6, W^0.75) had correlations 0.770, 0.732, 0.769, 0.769 with basal VO2, not significantly different (p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No drug, ke0, MEAC or potency ratio; not an opioid paper. End point is whole-body VO2 against temperature and pump flow. Flow had no significant effect on VO2 in the pooled regression (b2 not significant in the cold and warm groups), but a 0.220 power relation in the hypothermic Series II data implies VO2 up about 10% from 50 to 75 mL/kg/min (p5, p7). Minimal accepted flow 2.0 L/min/m2 at 30 C is stated to be 52 mL/kg/min for a 65 kg, 1.7 m2 man.

## Covariates
Body size (VO2 scaled to 65 kg), temperature, flow, cooling strategy (warm vs cold start).

## Caveats
Nasopharyngeal temperature is not representative of the whole body (muscle differed by 1.4-2.9 C, p6). VO2 on bypass has no independent check. Authors conclude cold-start patients were probably oxygen-short by up to about 15%, so the Q10 of 2.72 overstates true demand reduction. Adults only, small n.
