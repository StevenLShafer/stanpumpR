# de Winter 2017: canagliflozin HbA1c turnover model, once- vs twice-daily dosing

## Study
Model-based bridging of once-daily (QD) and twice-daily (BID) canagliflozin as add-on to metformin, to support the
canagliflozin-metformin IR fixed-dose combination (Janssen; all authors Janssen employees, past or present). Three
placebo-controlled studies of patients with type 2 diabetes on metformin monotherapy (PDF p3): Study 1 (NCT00642278,
12 weeks; 50, 100, 200, 300 mg QD or 300 mg BID, sitagliptin or placebo; PK troughs at baseline and weeks 3, 6, 12;
HbA1c at baseline and weeks 6, 9, 12; n = 352), Study 2 (NCT01106677, 26 weeks placebo-controlled; 100 or 300 mg QD;
HbA1c at baseline and weeks 6, 12, 18, 26; n = 717), Study 3 (NCT01340664, 18 weeks; 50 or 150 mg BID or placebo; HbA1c
at baseline and weeks 6, 12, 18; n = 278). 5764 HbA1c observations in 1347 patients. HbA1c by boronate affinity
chromatography (University of Missouri). No PK samples in Studies 2 and 3.

Table 1 (PDF p4, pooled dataset, n = 1347): 645 men (47.9%), 702 women; age median 56.0 y (range 21.0-80.0); weight
median 85.0 kg (40.0-188); BMI median 30.9 kg/m2 (18.1-73.0); eGFR median 89.0 mL/min/1.73 m2 (35.0-176); HbA1c median
7.6% (5.5-11.0). Arms: placebo 301, 50 mg QD 60, 50 mg BID 93, 100 mg QD 346, 200 mg QD 55, 150 mg BID 93, 300 mg QD
339, 300 mg BID 60.

Design (PDF p3): model built on an internal dataset (QD data of Studies 1 and 2, all Study 3 baselines and its placebo
arm), validated externally on the Study 3 BID arms; final estimates (Table 2) refitted to the pooled internal plus
external data. NONMEM 7.2.0, FOCE INTERACTION, ADVAN13, with the method of averaging (Dunne 2015, ref 25) for the
ODEs; R 3.0.1. Significance: OFV drop > 10.83 (alpha 0.001). Covariates explored on the EBEs (shrinkage < 25%): age,
weight, BMI, sex, race, MDRD eGFR (PDF p4).

PK: not estimated here. Individual 24-h profiles come from the earlier population PK model of Hoeben et al. (ref 22;
9061 samples from 1616 patients, 5715 rich samples in 245 patients and 3346 troughs in 1371; two compartments;
PDF p3): empirical Bayes estimates where troughs existed (Study 1), baseline covariates otherwise. That PK model was
externally validated against 24-h BID profiles from a separate study (Supplementary Figure S1). No PK parameter is
printed in the paper or its supplements.

## Model and parameters
Structural PD model (Equations 1-3, PDF p5, rendered):
- Eq 1: dH/dt = kin - Ef - kout x H(t), H the HbA1c (%); kout from the turnover half-life, t1/2 = ln2/kout.
- Eq 2: Ef = (Efc + Efp) x (H(0) - 5)/(8 - 5): the treatment effect scaled by the individual baseline H(0) against a
  reference 8.0%, with 5.0% the lower boundary for HbA1c lowering (OFV drop 305: 135 for baseline scaling, 170 for
  the 5% boundary; PDF p6).
- Eq 3: Efc = Emax x C(t)/(C(t) + EC50), C(t) the canagliflozin plasma concentration; no Hill factor (estimating one
  did not improve the fit). Efp, placebo plus diet and exercise, is a step function for t > 0, additive to Efc.
- kin, Efc and Efp were estimated scaled by kout, so Table 2's Emax and Efp read as steady-state changes in %HbA1c
  (PDF p6). The text prints kin = H(0)/kout (PDF p6); at steady state Eq 1 requires kin = kout x H(0) (derived:
  dH/dt = 0 at H = H(0) before treatment), so the printed division is inconsistent with Eq 1.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: HbA1c (%), a turnover (indirect response) model driven directly by the plasma concentration through an
Emax term (Eqs 1-3); no effect compartment, no ke0, no tPeak, no Hill factor. EC50 61.6 ng/mL (log 4.12, Table 2),
Emax -0.738% at steady state for baseline 8.0%, HbA1c turnover half-life 28.2 days. (derived: at the reference
baseline 8.0% and C = EC50 the canagliflozin steady-state effect is -0.369%; at baseline 6.5% every effect is halved
by Eq 2's (6.5 - 5)/3.) Introduction values from other sources (PDF p2): renal threshold for glucose EC50 32 ng/mL
(95% CI 19-45), EC90 289 ng/mL, maximal RTG decrease about 64% (from the US label, ref 9). No MEAC (not an opioid).

## Covariates
Baseline HbA1c only, through Eq 2's scaling of the whole treatment effect (canagliflozin and placebo). eGFR on Emax
(Eq 4) was the strongest other covariate but did not meet alpha 0.001 and was not retained. Study is a categorical
effect on Efp. No covariate on EC50. PK covariates are those of the Hoeben model, not reprinted.

## Caveats
- The text's table cross-references are shifted: Methods (PDF p6) says the kin, Efc and Efp estimates are "in Table
  1", Results (PDF p6) says Table 3 lists the final estimates and that the |PE|% results are in Table 2; the
  parameters are in Table 2 and the prediction errors in Table 3.
- kin printed as H(0)/kout, inconsistent with Eq 1 (see above).
- Reference 22 (the PK model) is printed as Clin Pharmacokinet 2014;55:209-23; volume 55 is the 2016 volume (Hoeben
  2016, PMID 26293616).
- Figure 1's group sizes (internal dataset: placebo 294, 50 mg QD 59, 100 mg QD 344, 200 mg QD 55, 300 mg QD 332, 300
  mg BID 60) differ from Table 1's, whose caption says the demographics are identical for both datasets. Not explained.
- The "What is already known" box says a fixed-dose combination may improve compliance to "antihypertensive agent
  therapy" (PDF p2); elsewhere AHA is defined as antihyperglycaemic agent.
- Population restricted to metformin-monotherapy background; few patients with moderate renal impairment, so the eGFR
  effect was not identifiable (authors, PDF p8). Studies 2 and 3 have no PK samples: their exposures are model
  predictions from covariates only. Sponsor-authored.
