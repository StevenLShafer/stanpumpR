# Hart 2026: AGNP-TDM consensus guidelines, update 2026 (Table 5)

## Study
Consensus guideline, not a study. The AGNP-TDM task force (50 experts) revises the 2004, 2011 and 2017
editions with a new method for setting therapeutic reference ranges (TRR) and dose-related reference
ranges. Abstract (p. 1): TRRs for 160 neuropsychiatric drugs, 30 newly included and 88 revised;
expected-concentration formula for 162 drugs; TDM strongly recommended for 15 drugs, recommended for 39,
useful for 49, potentially useful for 62 (these four add to 165, not 160; an internal inconsistency of the
abstract, not resolved here). Matrix: blood plasma or serum, used interchangeably throughout (pp. 1, 18,
53-54).

Table 5 (PDF pp. 20-52 = printed pp. 185-217; printed = PDF + 165), alphabetical, columns: drug and active
metabolites; time between blood withdrawal and last dose; TRR; indication for which the TRR is valid;
time to steady state; laboratory alert level; level of recommendation to use TDM; conversion factor
(nmol/L = ng/mL x CF); comments; references. Footnote (p. 52): ranges are trough (Cmin) unless otherwise
indicated; drugs in bold are level 1 or 2; a superscript `+` marks a range revised or newly introduced
compared with the 2018 version. Levels (p. 78): 1 strongly recommended, 2 recommended, 3 useful,
4 potentially useful. Laboratory alert level (p. 54): a concentration above the TRR prompting immediate
laboratory notification, typically twice the upper limit.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
The TRR is a population window of steady-state concentrations (mostly troughs) for the stated indication,
built from concentration-effect studies, PET receptor occupancy or pharmacokinetically expected ranges at
approved doses (the comment column says which). Opioid audit (Table 5 only): no MEAC, ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak
is given for any opioid. Opioid entries: buprenorphine 0.5-2.5 ng/mL for substitution (comment cites
Laffont 2022: 2-3 ng/mL, about 70% or more brain mu-receptor occupancy, optimal in opioid use disorder;
24 mg SL peaks 2-6 ng/mL, norbuprenorphine 3-8; p23); methadone 90-620 ng/mL substitution, non-users
markedly lower (100 ng/mL), QTc risk, no alert level (p38); levomethadone 30-400, non-users 300 ng/mL, QTc
above 656 ng/mL (p34); morphine 5-400 pain and 50-520 substitution, 5-1000 for pain management, no alert
level (p40); oxycodone 5-100 pain, alert 200 (p42); diacetylmorphine 70-350 at 1 h in heroin-assisted
treatment, Cmax 60-110 ng/mL 1 h after 50 mg oral in healthy subjects (p28). All opioid ranges are for
serum/plasma total drug, tolerance-dependent; no potency ratios given.

## Covariates
None in Table 5 itself. Comments mention CYP2D6 (atomoxetine, risperidone metabolic ratio elsewhere),
inducers lowering lacosamide and lamotrigine, age (levetiracetam, lithium), formulation (pregabalin IR/XR,
quetiapine ER).

## Caveats
Many ranges are pharmacokinetically expected concentrations at approved doses, not windows of efficacy
(e.g. alprazolam, amphetamines, temazepam, zolpidem, primidone/phenobarbital "extracted from the SPC").
Several opioid and benzodiazepine ranges are level 4 and tolerance-dependent. The `+` marker flags any
revision since 2018, not which column changed. The abstract's level counts sum to 165 against 160 TRRs.
Table 5 does not state the matrix per row (plasma or serum throughout).
