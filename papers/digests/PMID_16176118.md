# Janmahasatian 2005: semi-mechanistic fat-free mass (lean body weight) equation

## Study
373 adults (168 M, 205 F). Population A (index, n = 303: 146 M, 157 F; 88 normal weight, 215 BMI ≥ 25):
anthropometrics, BIA at 50 kHz (Bodystat 1500, SEAC SFB3) and DXA (Lunar DPX-L) FFM; age 18-82 y, weight
40.7-216.5 kg, BMI 17.1-69.9 kg/m² (Abstract; Table I: men 60.6-216.5 kg, women 40.7-196.2 kg). Population B
(external test, n = 70: 22 M, 48 F): DXA FFM, no BIA; BMI 18.7-38.4. Brisbane, Australia. 71% overweight or obese.
Software: NCSS (empirical), MATLAB 6.5 (impedance model, 5000-fold 90/10 cross-validation), NONMEM V (FFM model).

## Model and parameters
Stage 1 (Model 2, Eq. 16-17): predicted impedance Ẑ (ohm) from BMI:
- male Ẑ = 6.68 × 10³ / BMI + 216; female Ẑ = 8.78 × 10³ / BMI + 244.
Stage 2 (Model 4, Eq. 19): FFM = 9.27 × 10³ × Ht(m)² / Ẑ; θ3 = 9.27 × 10³; height exponent θ4 fixed at 2 (estimate
1.7, same OFV); Ẑ exponent θ5 fixed at 1 (estimate 0.922); BSV on θ3 CV 0.27%; no sex interaction.
Combined (Eq. 20-21; derived here algebraically from Eq. 16, 17, 19 because the printed equations are not in the
text layer): FFM (male) = 9270 × WT / (6680 + 216 × BMI); FFM (female) = 9270 × WT / (8780 + 244 × BMI).

Performance: impedance model r² 0.78, RMSE 51.6 ohm (~10%) (Table III); FFM model r² 0.93, ME -0.77, RMSE 3.33 kg
(~6%) (Table IV, Abstract); external population B r² 0.85, ME -0.04, RMSE 4.39 kg (~7%) (Table V; men ME -2.74,
women +1.14). Predictive loss vs saturated empirical model 35% (FFM) and 6% (impedance). Also printed: Cheymol/James
LBW (Eq. 1-2) and empirical models Eq. 15 and 18 (not recommended for clinical use).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not applicable (no drug). No MEAC, ke0 or potency.

## Covariates
Sex, total body weight, height (through BMI).

## Caveats
FFM treated as equivalent to LBW (lipid difference ~3% men, 5% women). DXA as reference. Agrees with the James LBW for
BMI < 35; unlike James it does not fall or turn negative at high BMI (Fig. 6). Adults only; not developed in children.
Population B showed sex-specific bias (men ME -2.74 kg, 95% CI excludes 0; women +1.14); the paper does not define
the sign convention of ME.
