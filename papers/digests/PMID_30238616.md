# Glasser 2018: Drugs don't work in patients who don't take them

## Study
A three-page commentary (not a study) on medication adherence in trials and practice, prompted by a Lefort et al. survey of sex differences in adherence to antihypertensives in French adults over 55 (same issue). No pharmacokinetics, no drug concentrations, no model.

## Model and parameters

Adherence facts quoted as numbers (PDF p1-2, printed p1504-1505):
- Adherence is conventionally cut at 80% (>=80% counted adherent); no standard operational definition exists, and it is higher in closely monitored trials than in clinical practice (p2).
- Less than 50% of post-MI patients filled statin, beta-blocker and ACE-inhibitor prescriptions at one year, even at no copay (ref 6, p1).
- Medicare high-intensity statin users after MI who stayed highly adherent: 54.8% at 182 days and 36.7% at 730 days (p1).
- Heart-failure adherence interventions: mortality RR 0.89 (95% CI 0.81-0.99), readmission OR 0.79 (0.71-0.89) (p1).
- About one third of post-MI patients and about half in primary prevention did not adhere (meta-analysis, p1).
- Adherence falls as daily doses rise; once-daily dosing beats 2-4 times daily (p1). Japanese CVD patients: more than 2 doses daily had OR 4.42 (3.02-6.48) for nonadherence (p2).
