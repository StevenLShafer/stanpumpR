# Fisher 1994: IV IL-1ra (anakinra) in sepsis syndrome, phase II

## Study
- Prospective, open-label, placebo-controlled, phase II; twelve academic ICUs in the United States.
- 99 patients with sepsis syndrome or septic shock: mean age 58.4 +/- 18.3 years, APACHE II 22.6 +/- 7.9, 66% in shock (Results). Table 1 by group (placebo, 17, 67, 133 mg/h): n 25, 25, 24, 25; men/women 14/11, 14/11, 16/8, 14/11; weight 77.4 +/- 25.0, 71.0 +/- 17.0, 67.7 +/- 13.4 (n = 23), 70.6 +/- 17.0 kg.
- Acute renal dysfunction at entry (Table 4): 5, 6, 7 and 2 patients, 15 of the 74 treated.
- Dosing: 100 mg IV loading dose, then a 72-h infusion of 17, 67 or 133 mg/h, or placebo.
- Assay: ELISA (rabbit polyclonal capture, murine monoclonal detection; lower limit < 700 pg/mL); baseline and placebo specimens by a commercial ELISA (limit < 0.23 ng/mL).

## Pharmacokinetics
- Clearance was estimated at 24, 48 and 72 h as the infusion rate divided by the plasma concentration (Methods); no compartmental model.
- Mean plasma concentrations during the infusion (Results, PDF p. 7; Figure 3): 3540 +/- 2900, 14800 +/- 8120 and 25200 +/- 13200 ng/mL at 17, 67 and 133 mg/h, measured in 73 of 74 treated patients.
- Derived (rate / mean concentration): 4.80, 4.53 and 5.28 L/h.
- Endogenous IL-1ra: 8.6 +/- 13.3 ng/mL during placebo; 11.1-28.7 ng/mL at entry (Table 4).
- Clearance correlated positively with estimated creatinine clearance (p < .001, r2 = .51); no equation is printed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
28-day all-cause mortality 44%, 32%, 25% and 16% (placebo to 133 mg/h), linear in the square root of the dose (p = .015). Not used.

## Caveats
- Rate / mean concentration is an apparent steady-state clearance; the 100 mg load is ignored, which matters little by 24 h.
- The SDs are large (CV 52-82%).
- The renal relation is a correlation only; the creatinine-clearance estimate is not named.
