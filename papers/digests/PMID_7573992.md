# van den Nieuwenhuyzen 1995: computer-controlled alfentanil vs PCA morphine after surgery

## Study
- Double-blind randomized, 20 ASA I/II orthopedic patients aged 18-65 yr (one excluded for a surgical complication; 19 analysed: CCiA n = 9, PCAM n = 10; 15 M / 4 F). Table 1 (printed 674, text layer only): age 36 vs 44 yr, weight 68 vs 83 kg (CCiA vs PCAM; weight lower in the CCiA group, mean +- SD).
- CCiA: computer-controlled infusion of alfentanil using the **Maitre 1987** population PK set (Pascal software, Atari Portfolio, Ohmeda 9000 pump, plasma-targeted, rate recalculated every 10 s). PCAM: PCA morphine, 1 mg/mL, 10 min lockout, no background; 2.5 mL (2.5 mg) boluses while loading, then 1 mL.
- Each patient had both systems, one carrying saline. Pump targets were driven by a research assistant when the patient pressed the button.
- Intra-op: etomidate induction, N2O/enflurane, alfentanil CCi starting at target 150 ng/mL, raised in 20-50 ng/mL steps for inadequate anesthesia.
- Post-op titration: on request with sedation score <=3 (Ramsay), target raised by 10 ng/mL (and 2.5 mg morphine/placebo bolus) every 10 min until VAS <= 3.0 (10-cm VAS) with no further request. Maintenance step then 5 ng/mL. Target lowered by 5 ng/mL after 2 h without a request, and again every 15 min until a request. Study ended at 32 h or target <5 ng/mL.
- Arterial plasma alfentanil by capillary GC (detection limit 0.1 ng/mL, CV about 5% above 1 ng/mL): total plasma concentration. Sample taken before each target increase (maximum concentration still associated with pain, MCP) and 10 min after a new target if no further analgesia was needed (MEAC).

## Model and parameters
No PK model fitted. Performance of the Maitre 1987 alfentanil set was assessed (Table 4, PDF p7 = printed 677, image confirmed):
whole-population bias (MDPE) +8.2% (95% CI +1.1 to +13.6, n = 321 samples); inaccuracy (MDAPE) 22.3% (18.9-24.5). The abstract rounds these to 8% and 22%.
Per patient MDPE (%): 1 +2.5, 2 +67.6, 3 +23.5, 4 +28.1, 5 -1.3, 6 +55.7, 7 +8.2, 8 +11.4, 9 -26.6; MDAPE (%): 19.9, 67.7, 23.5, 28.1, 4.9, 55.7, 19.2, 23.6, 26.6.
The earlier study (ref 3, 1993) is cited for a bias of 12%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: pain on a 10-cm VAS (satisfactory analgesia = VAS <= 3.0 with no request) and PCA demands. Not CSF, EEG, pupil or ventilation.
- **MCP and MEAC (measured total plasma alfentanil, post-op, ng/mL), Table 5, PDF p7**; per-patient range over the study period, n = 9 CCiA patients:

  Smallest value anywhere: MCP 10, MEAC 13; largest MCP 243, MEAC 243. Authors say wide inter- and intra-individual variability makes a single population value impossible, and that in their earlier study the MEAC fell significantly by the end of the first post-op day.
  No mean or median MEAC is given in this paper.
- Safety observation: one excluded patient was still in pain (VAS > 4) with predicted and measured concentrations above 200 ng/mL; another had respiratory depression after droperidol, with measured plasma alfentanil 72 ng/mL.
- Efficacy (abstract, PDF p1): onset of satisfactory analgesia median 20 min (range 10-80) CCiA vs 50 min (20-163) PCAM, P < 0.05; total demands median 21 vs 34; time with VAS > 3.0 12% vs 21% of the loading period; in the first 8 h VAS > 3.0 for median 54 min (CCiA) vs 144 min (PCAM).
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak: not reported.** The authors only describe alfentanil as having rapid blood-brain equilibration.
- **Potency ratio: none.** Doses were not compared in equianalgesic terms; the two arms used different opioids (alfentanil about 0.10 mg/kg/24 h vs morphine, post-op consumption row of Table 1, text-layer only, not confirmed on an image).

## Covariates
None analysed for PD. The Maitre set carries its own covariates. Weight did not correlate with onset time of analgesia (text).

## Caveats
- n = 9 per analysed arm; mostly young men; orthopedic surgery. Patients were not in true patient-controlled mode (an assistant pressed the buttons).
- MEAC defined by this titration scheme (10-min after a step with no request) after N2O/enflurane/etomidate anesthesia; high in the first hours after surgery and falling with time.
- Positive bias (+8.2% overall, up to +67.6% in one patient) means measured plasma concentrations exceeded the Maitre prediction on average, so predicted targets slightly under-state the measured ones.
- Table 1 and Table 2 values were not confirmed on the rendered page.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No repo location cites this paper. Independent comparison with `R/drugs_alfentanil.R`:
- `R/drugs_alfentanil.R:41-44` sets MEAC = 39 ng/mL (typical 46.8, lower typical 31.2, upper typical 78). These are plasma concentrations in the library's units (ng/mL), matching this paper's total plasma ng/mL. The paper's per-patient MEAC ranges (13-243) bracket 39 (several patients' MEAC ranges begin at 13-33 and others reach well above 78), so the library's 31-78 band sits inside the observed spread without matching any central value. The paper gives no population central value, so the MEAC of 39 cannot be verified from it.
- `R/drugs_alfentanil.R:40` tPeak = 1.4 min: the paper reports no ke0 or tPeak, so cannot be checked.
- The library's PK (Scott and Stanski via Shafer and Varvel, `R/drugs_alfentanil.R:28-35`) is not Maitre 1987, the set this study tested. The bias reported here (+8.2%) applies to Maitre, not to the library's set. `docs/literature-search-2026-11-16.md:64` records the same Maitre/Scott distinction.
