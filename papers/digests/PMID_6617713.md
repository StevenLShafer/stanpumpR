# Luxford 1983: digoxin in the third trimester vs postpartum, on maintenance therapy

PDF page n = printed page 116 + n.

## Study
22 women attending a cardiac clinic (Auckland) on maintenance digoxin 0.375 mg/day (Lanoxin); 19 rheumatic
valvular disease, 1 atrial fibrillation, 2 cardiomyopathy; 15 completed (4 non-compliant, 3 lost to follow-up).
During pregnancy two developed mild toxaemia and one mild CHF (frusemide); one took debrisoquine. Paired: 24-h
urine and serum (14-16 h post dose) on at least three occasions in the third trimester and again postpartum;
the Methods say 6 to 16 weeks postpartum, the Summary and Discussion 6 to 12 weeks (internal inconsistency).
RIA (125I), sensitivity 0.14 ng/mL. Clearances = urine conc x volume x 1.73 / (serum conc x BSA x time), i.e.
per 1.73 m2. Paired t-tests. Compliance checked by pill counts and levels.

## Caveats
Small (15), cardiac patients on chronic therapy, renal clearance computed from single 24-h collections, BSA
normalised. Serum digoxin was HIGHER in pregnancy despite higher renal elimination; the authors attribute this to
increased absorption (slower small-bowel transit), an untested hypothesis. Their argument that tubular handling
did not change rests on the fall in 24-h urinary digoxin matching the fall in CrCL (both x1.39); digoxin renal
clearance itself rose less (x1.21). Figure captions appear inconsistent with the text: Fig. 3 (serum digoxin vs
CrCL, r = 0.75) is captioned third trimester while text and Table 1 give r = -0.75 postpartum, and Fig. 4
(r = 0.56) is captioned postpartum while text and Table 1 give 0.56 in 3T. Follow-up window stated two ways.
