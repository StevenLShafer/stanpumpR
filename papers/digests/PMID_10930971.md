# Uematsu 2000: IV adenosine (SUNY4001) PK and tolerability in healthy Japanese men

## Study
- 30 healthy Japanese men, 27.9 ± 6.3 y (20-43), 64.7 ± 9.5 kg (PDF p1-2, printed 177-178).
- Adenosine in saline, 30 mL over 6 min (5 mL/min) by pump, dose-escalating: 60, 100 and 140 µg/kg/min (6 subjects each); 120 µg/kg/min (5 subjects) added after 180 µg/kg/min was abandoned for adverse events. Placebo (saline) single-blind, 1-2 per level, pooled (n = 7). Units µg/kg/min confirmed in the figure legends on the rendered PDF p3 (the text layer drops the µ and reads "mg").
- Venous samples at 0, 3, 4.5, 6, 6.5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 16 min from infusion start into ice-cold EDTA + 2'-deoxycoformycin, dipyridamole added immediately; plasma. Urine to 24 h.
- Assays: adenosine by RIA after succinylation (LOD 0.00625 nmol/mL); inosine, hypoxanthine, xanthine, uric acid by HPLC-UV.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic parameters: the authors state plasma adenosine showed no dose-related change, which prevented estimating PK of exogenous adenosine (PDF p4, Discussion). They point to Blardi 1993 for parameters over a similar dose range.
```yaml
parameters: []  # no PK parameters reported; observations only (below)
```
Observations (PDF p3, printed 179; Figure 2 on the rendered page):
- Predose endogenous adenosine below LOD in more than half of each group except placebo (0.005 ± 0.002 nmol/mL, mean ± SEM, n = 7) and 120 µg/kg/min (0.009 ± 0.001, n = 5); detectable values 0.005-0.013 nmol/mL.
- Plasma adenosine did increase during and after infusion (Figure 2: group means roughly 0.06-0.08 nmol/mL near 11 min, read from the graph, including the saline group), but with no dose-related change; the authors attribute the placebo rise to sampling (tourniquet, muscle clamping releasing endogenous adenosine) (PDF p4).
- Hypoxanthine (predose 0.53-0.99 nmol/mL) rose significantly from 3 min after infusion start until 5, 10 and 3 min after its end at 100, 120 and 140 µg/kg/min, with no clear dose dependence. Inosine mostly below LOD. Xanthine, uric acid and urinary metabolites: no significant dose-related change.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Heart rate rose 10-20 beats/min at 120 (P = 0.0010) and 140 µg/kg/min (P = 0.0001) vs placebo (Figure 1, PDF p3); systolic/diastolic pressure tended to fall, not significant.
- Second-degree AV block in 2 subjects each at 120 and 140 µg/kg/min, 2-5 min after infusion start, self-resolving; one at 140 progressed to atrial fibrillation lasting about 3 h.
- Dose-related heat sensation, flushing, dyspnoea, chest discomfort; every subject at 120 and 140 had at least one. 180 µg/kg/min not tolerated.
- No ke0, EC50 or concentration-effect relation. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Small groups (5-7), men only, Japanese; venous plasma, where adenosine is cleared within seconds by red cells and endothelium, so venous levels may not reflect exposure; placebo values rose too, pointing to sampling artefact.
