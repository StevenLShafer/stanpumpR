# Varvel 1992: Measuring the predictive performance of computer-controlled infusion pumps

## Study
A methods paper that defines four per-patient performance measures for a computer-controlled infusion pump (CCIP): MDPE (bias), MDAPE (inaccuracy), divergence and wobble. It illustrates them on an alfentanil CCIP study of 51 surgical patients (printed pp.64-65, PDF p.2-3). Group 1: 29 women, age 34.1 +/- 7.4 y, weight 61.1 +/- 13.1 kg, minor gynaecologic surgery of about 38 min. Group 2: 21 men and 1 woman, age 61.5 +/- 11.3 y, weight range 69-101 kg, surgery of about 243 min (range 83-550). Group 1 had 10-13 arterial samples and group 2 had 15-24. Serum alfentanil by RIA, triplicate, sensitivity 30 ng/mL, CV under 5%. In 40 patients the pump ran the Maitre 1987 set; in 11 it ran the Scott and Stanski 1987 set. Predictions for every patient were then recomputed with both sets by convolution of the infusion record (5-s zero-order steps). Five anesthesiologists judged 102 graphs acceptable or not, and a discriminant analysis tested which measure agreed with them.

## Model and parameters
Definitions (all per patient i, sample j; times in min):
- Performance error PE = (Cm - Cp)/Cp x 100, **normalised to the predicted concentration Cp**, not the measured one (Eq 1). Positive PE means measured is above predicted (the pump under-delivers relative to the model).
- MDAPE = median of |PE| (Eq 2). MDPE = median of PE (Eq 4).
- Divergence = 60 x slope of |PE| against time in min, in %/h (Eq 3).
- Wobble = median absolute deviation of PE from MDPE (Eq 5).
- Population estimates by two-stage mean (Eqs 6-13), pooled-data weighting by N (Eqs 14-21) or variance weighting (Eqs 22-26). The authors found little difference with 15-40 samples per patient.
- MDAPE minimisation is close to iteratively reweighted least squares with constant CV (Eqs 40-48).
- Expected floor on MDAPE from inter-individual variability: CV of V1 and CL of 0.30, 0.40, 0.50 gives MDAPE of about 20, 26, 31% (printed p.92).

Results in the 51 patients (text, printed pp.85-86):
- With PE on the predicted concentration (population MAPE and MPE): Maitre 74.5 +/- 4.8 and 71.7 +/- 5.1; Scott 29.1 +/- 3.3 and 10.1 +/- 4.3.
- With the older measured-denominator definition: Maitre MAPE 39.7 +/- 2.6, MPE 31.9 +/- 2.7; Scott MAPE 33.5 +/- 7.3, MPE -11.7 +/- 7.5. Using medians with the old definition: Maitre MDAPE 35.6 +/- 1.6, MDPE 34.1 +/- 1.9; Scott MDAPE 20.2 +/- 2.1, MDPE -2.6 +/- 3.2.
- Discriminant analysis: MDAPE was the best single predictor of clinician-judged acceptability (canonical loading 0.932; MDPE 0.882; divergence -0.324; wobble 0.148).
- Example patient: divergence 25.7 %/h, MDAPE 14.8% (Fig 3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No MEAC or analgesic concentration, no ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis, no equianalgesic or potency ratio (alfentanil, opioid audit: nothing reported). The paper measures pharmacokinetic prediction only; the end point is serum alfentanil by RIA.

## Covariates
Maitre set: age (CL and K31 fall above 40 y) and weight (V1). Scott set: none. The paper makes no covariate analysis of its own.

## Caveats
- Illustrated on one drug and one sample set; the Scott set was fitted to data from the same group of investigators, and 40 of 51 patients were driven by the Maitre set, so concentrations the pump delivered followed Maitre targets.
- Wobble and divergence overlap (stated by the authors).
- Sampling-time dependence: dense early sampling inflates MDAPE; the authors weight all samples equally and recommend sampling where clinical interest lies (printed pp.91-92).
- The PD and TS standard errors assume independent PEs within a patient.
- The DOI was not available in the context file.
