# Wahba 2024 (EACTS/EACTAIC/EBCP): cardiopulmonary bypass guideline

## Study
Joint European clinical practice guideline (EACTS, EACTAIC, EBCP) on cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) in adult cardiac surgery, 85 pages, with class-of-recommendation / level-of-evidence tables. Not a PK study; no new data. Only the sections relevant to stanpumpR drugs were read (see source_read).

Anticoagulation (section 9.3, PDF pp 38–41; numbers confirmed on rendered pages 38 and 40):
- **Heparin (UFH), 9.3.1 (p38)**: ACT > 480 s is the appropriate threshold on CPB; an ACT above 480 s should be considered with uncoated equipment and cardiotomy suction. Pre-CPB ACT target 480 s, range 400–500 s, reported as Australian practice-survey result [473]. Circuits and ACT assays can justify other targets to avoid overdosing [474–477]. Trials cited: 450 vs 300 IU/kg heparin, no bleeding difference [484]; 600 vs 300 IU: anti-Xa 9.38 vs 5.04 U/mL, no advantage [485] (p39, text layer).
- Recommendation Table 37 (p39, text layer): individualized heparin/protamine management IIa B; regular ACT checks per institutional protocol I C.
- **Protamine, 9.3.2 (p39–40)**: half-life ~5 min; protamine-to-heparin ratio < 1.0 recommended (Table 38, I B).
- **Bivalirudin, 9.3.3 (p40)**: IV bolus 1 mg/kg, then infusion 2.5 mg/kg/h; 50 mg added to the pump prime. Plasma concentration should be 12 to 15 µg/mL, but measurement is unavailable clinically. Preferred monitor: ecarin clotting time [502]; most centres use ACT, target 2.5 × baseline, typically > 300 s [502]. Plasma bivalirudin correlates with ACT only below ~5 µg/mL; aPTT plateaus [503]. Mainly renal clearance: CL falls 20% (eGFR 30–59), 60% (eGFR 10–29), 80% (dialysis); bolus unchanged, infusion reduced [505]. Removed by ultrafiltration (p40; also ZBUF note, text layer).
- **Argatroban (p40)**: bolus 0.1 mg/kg, infusion 5–25 µg/kg/min (up to 40); 4 mg in prime; ACT > 450–500 s reported.
- Recommendation Table 39 (p41, text layer): bivalirudin first-line in acute HIT type 2 (IIa B); argatroban with significant renal dysfunction (IIb C).

Anaesthetics on CPB (section 9.11.2–9.11.4, PDF pp 50–52, text layer only, not used by stanpumpR):
- Total plasma propofol falls 50–78% (hypothermic CPB) and 59–70% (normothermic CPB); unbound propofol returns to pre-CPB within 20 min; plasma propofol 28% higher at 34 °C vs 37 °C. Schnider TCI may underestimate plasma propofol in hypothermic CPB; at normothermia ≥ 5 mg/kg/h keeps on-pump plasma level equivalent to a Marsh Ce of 3 mcg/mL.
- Remifentanil: reduce ~30% per 5 °C drop; recommendation table: reduce by 30% at 32 °C after 20–30 min, by 60% immediately below 28 °C (IIb B); Minto model acceptable on normothermic CPB, may underestimate levels.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points are coagulation tests: ACT (whole-blood point-of-care), ecarin clotting time, anti-Xa, aPTT. No concentration–effect model.
- Opioid content (section 9.11.3, p51, text layer): fentanyl plasma concentrations fall 30–60% of pre-CPB with intermittent bolus, ~30% with infusion; alfentanil falls ~50% at CPB start; fentanyl/sufentanil lung sequestration can raise plasma levels at separation. **No MEAC, no ke0/t½ke0/tPeak, no equianalgesic or potency ratio** reported.

## Covariates
Renal function for bivalirudin (above). Temperature for propofol and remifentanil (above).

## Caveats
Guideline text summarising cited studies; the bivalirudin 12–15 µg/mL figure is given without its own reference in that sentence and is not measurable clinically. Recommendation tables in the text layer were checked only for the rows above. Sections other than those listed were not read.
