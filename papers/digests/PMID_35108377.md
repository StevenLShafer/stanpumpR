# Eleveld 2022: Allometric scaling in pharmacokinetic studies in anesthesiology

## Study
A Clinical Focus Review from the Groningen group, not an original study. It contains no new data, subjects or
assay. It sets out the West–Brown–Enquist theory, its use in compartmental PK models, the evidence for and
against the theoretical exponents, maturation, the criticisms (including Fisher and Shafer 2016), and the
clinical consequences.

## Model and parameters
The paper gives no drug-specific parameters. It proposes generic scaling equations (PDF p 2, printed 610;
Fig. 1, p 3):

- size = WGT / WGTref
- V = Vref · size^1
- CL = CLref · size^0.75 (elimination and intercompartmental clearances)
- k = kref · size^-0.25 (rate constants; time constants scale with exponent 0.25)

About WGTref (p 2, printed 610): if the reference values are estimated from the data, the choice of reference
weight does not matter. The paper says it is often 70 kg, which makes interpretation easier and makes
differences between drugs easier to see (ref 13, Holford 2013). Figures 3 and 4 and the fentanyl example use
WGT/70.

Other quantitative points:
- Bae 2020 fentanyl (95 adults): estimated clearance exponent 0.313 (95% CI 0.037-0.583). The paper shows
  that this is algebraically the same as theoretical 0.75 with an extra covariate term, (WGT/70)^0.75 ·
  (WGT/70)^-0.437 (p 4, printed 612).
- Sinha 2019: discriminating exponents takes more than 100 adults, and with normal or high between-subject
  variability it is almost impossible with fewer than 200 (p 4).
- Fig. 4 (p 6): choosing the wrong linear model gives a clearance 57% of intended in children and 176% in
  adults.
- Fig. 2 (p 3): steady-state remifentanil infusion rates for a 4 ng/mL target, Eleveld vs Kim models, across
  weight.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. The paper reports no ke0, EC50 or MEAC. The only concentration it mentions is the illustrative
remifentanil target of about 4 ng/mL plasma in Fig. 2. That is an example target, not a measured MEAC.

## Covariates
Weight, scaled allometrically as above. Fat-free mass, lean body weight and normal fat mass are named as
alternative size measures. Maturation by postmenstrual age is treated as outside allometric theory
(Anderson and Holford; Fig. 3). Obesity is not explained by the theory.

## Caveats
This is an opinion and review piece by model developers who favour allometry. The theory does not address
maturation, body composition or disease. The authors say the theoretical exponents work for adults and for
children older than about 2 to 5 years.

    makes little difference.
