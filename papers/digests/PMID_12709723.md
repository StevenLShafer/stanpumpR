# Heikkinen 2003: fluoxetine and norfluoxetine in pregnancy and lactation

## Study
Prospective clinical study, Turku, Finland. 11 women taking fluoxetine (depression n = 5, panic disorder n = 6;
median dose 20 mg/day, range 20-40, once daily) and 10 matched controls without psychotropic drugs (controls used
only for obstetric and infant outcomes, not PK). Median age 36 (25-43) y; weight not reported (Table I, PDF p4).
Six took fluoxetine from before conception; five started at 22-35 weeks. Four with panic disorder took occasional
alprazolam or lorazepam; one switched from clonazepam at week 31. One dose increase (20 to 40 mg at 36 weeks).

Sampling: maternal trough plasma (before the daily dose) at 36-37 weeks (n = 8), at delivery (non-trough), and on
postpartum days 2 and 4 (n = 11), 2 weeks (n = 9) and 2 months (n = 8); umbilical artery and vein at delivery;
infant venous plasma at days 2, 4, 2 weeks, 2 months; breast milk (fore-feed, trough) at day 4, 2 weeks, 2 months.
Assay: reversed-phase HPLC-UV (Kelly 1989), total plasma concentrations, LLOQ 10 nmol/L, interassay CV 2.2% and
3.4%. Concentrations reported in nmol/L. No compartmental model; metabolic ratio, child/mother and milk/plasma ratios,
infant half-life by log-linear regression (mostly 2 points). Infant milk dose by Atkinson's method (150 mL/kg/day,
F = 100%).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. No concentration-effect, ke0 or therapeutic range of its own; it compares pregnancy troughs with a
nonpregnant clinical series (ref 24) and infers possible therapeutic failure. Infant outcomes: normal growth and
neurodevelopment to 1 year; 15-min Apgar lower in the fluoxetine group (Table III).

## Covariates
Pregnancy vs 2 months postpartum (same women, lactating). Mechanism proposed: CYP2D6 induction (higher
N-demethylation), with norfluoxetine stable.

## Caveats
Small n (8 sampled in late pregnancy, 8 at 2 months); comparator is 2 months postpartum, not a non-pregnant,
non-lactating state; doses 20-40 mg, so non-DDD-corrected columns mix doses; no weights; no CYP2D6 genotypes;
single milk sample per time; infant half-lives from 2 points in most. Text percentages for the postpartum rise are
expressed oddly (see open question).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Active moiety -32%**: DDD-corrected Table II means give 480/705 = 0.68, i.e. -32% in late pregnancy against 2 months
  postpartum (derived); also the authors' own "32%". **Matches**, with the comparator being 2 months postpartum
  (lactating), not a non-pregnant baseline; the row's `comparator` column ("own non-pregnant baseline") describes Westin.
- **Decline mainly in parent**: fluoxetine 152 vs 388 nmol/L, norfluoxetine 364 vs 310 (Table II); ratio 3.3 vs 1.4.
  **Matches.**
- **"11 pregnancies"**: 11 enrolled, 8 sampled at 36-37 weeks. Minor.
- **"All conclude dose adjustment is generally not needed"**: Heikkinen does not. It concludes that the low
  pregnancy levels might lead to therapeutic failure and that clinicians should watch for undertreated depression
  (Abstract; Discussion PDF p7). **Discrepancy** in the registry prose (no numeric factor affected; category "unchanged"
  rests mostly on Westin and Schoretsanitis).
- **Against the library's model (Han 2025, `R/drugs_fluoxetine.R:49-53`, not citing this paper)**: the model's 20 mg
  female steady-state trough is 83.8 ng/mL fluoxetine + 79.5 ng/mL norfluoxetine, about 271 + 269 = 540 nmol/L
  (derived with free-base MW 309.3 and 295.3, not given in the paper). Heikkinen's DDD-corrected active moiety is 480
  nmol/L in late pregnancy and 705 at 2 months postpartum: the model sits 13% above the pregnancy value and 23% below
  the postpartum value (derived). Split by analyte (not dose-corrected, median dose 20 mg) postpartum is about 120 ng/mL
  fluoxetine and 92 ng/mL norfluoxetine (derived), a higher parent share than the model. Different populations
  (Chinese, median age 17) and the library model is valid only for the trough (its header says so).
