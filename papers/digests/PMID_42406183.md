# Krzyzanski 2026: Gauss-Hermite cohort population estimation, with dexamethasone at birth as the case study

## Study
This is an estimation-methods paper with one author. It treats the Gauss-Hermite quadrature nodes and weights as a
fixed "N-cohort" mixture population, implemented in NONMEM 7.6 as $MIX with zero variances.

**Study I** is a simulation. A one-compartment IV bolus model (100 mg; CL 0.3 L/h, V 3 L, omega² 0.04 and 0.09,
proportional CV 0.1) generated 100 datasets of 20 subjects, dense (1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 h) and sparse (one sample per
subject). They were refitted with 1-cohort (naive pooled), 2x2 to 5x5-cohort, FOCE, IMP and SAEM (Tables 1-2, PDF
p 7). On sparse data, the success rates were 19% for FOCE, 68% for SAEM and 100% for IMP and for every cohort
method. All methods recovered CL and V to within 5%. The cohort methods estimated the IIV parameters more accurately,
but they grossly misestimated the residual variance.

**Study II** is the dexamethasone case. Fourteen women near term, delivered by caesarean section, each received 8 mg
dexamethasone phosphate IM (stated as equal to 6.67 mg DEX) at noon the day before delivery, then a second 8 mg at
10 am on the day of delivery, 22 h later. One maternal peripheral-vein sample and one umbilical-vein sample were taken
at birth, 0.78-4.42 h after the second dose (the Appendix data's TSLD column; the text says 0.8 to 4.4 h). Assay was
HPLC. The data are attributed to Tsuei et al. 1980 (ref 12) and are listed in full in Appendix 1. The model was
fitted with Dose = 8000 µg, the phosphate amount.

## Model and parameters
The model is one compartment with first-order IM absorption and two doses (Eqs 22-24, 30). Fetal concentration =
FMR x maternal concentration (Eq 25). Residual error is additive on the log scale, separately for mother and fetus.
In the 3-cohort model only log(CL/F) carries a between-cohort spread (omega). V/F, ka and FMR are single values. The
fit used FOCE.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. FMR, the fetal:maternal ratio of 0.329, is the only "clinical" quantity the paper highlights. It says this
agrees with Tsuei's 0.45 and the 2023 mPBPK model's 0.31.

## Covariates
None. The author suggests the three CL/F cohorts could be read as slow, medium and fast metabolisers. That is
interpretation, not a covariate.

## Caveats
- The author presents the results as introductory. The DEX analysis is a robustness demonstration with 14 women and one
  sample each.
- ka is not identifiable (RSE above 100%), and V/F in the 3-cohort model has an RSE of 59%.
- Sampling covers only 0.8-4.4 h after the second dose.
- The Discussion quotes the 2023 mPBPK clearance as 26.6 L/h, whereas that paper's Table 1 gives 26.7 L/h. The paper
  also compares the result with Tsuei's CL of 33.5 L/h and gives maternal blood volume at 39 weeks as 5.7 L. It
  attributes the large V/F to protein binding (66.6%, Tsuei), tissue binding, F and the phosphate dose basis.
- The design described here (14 women, two IM doses 22 h apart, birth sampling) is attributed to Tsuei 1980. The
  stanpumpR summaries of Tsuei describe single 8 mg doses in 6 IV and 6 IM women. Tsuei 1980 is not in the archive, so
  it cannot be checked here whether that paper contains both cohorts.
