# Morishima 1992: cocaine in the pregnant rat — hemodynamics, tissue distribution and placental transfer

The sole source behind the cocaine row of `inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv`, and an animal
study, which is why that row is `unknown` and applies nothing. It is cited for one sentence —
that maternal plasma cocaine is lower in pregnancy — and the full text supports that sentence
while explaining it, which the abstract does not.

**Why this card was written by hand.** `digest_run.py` records this paper as refused by the
model's safeguards even when batched alone. The PDF, its extracted text and its context file
were all filed normally; only the card was missing.

## Study

Chronically catheterised Sprague-Dawley rats, 10-12 weeks old: **29 term pregnant and 31
non-pregnant females**, in three groups (PDF p. 2).

| Group | Pregnant | Non-pregnant | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood flow | 17 | 18 | cardiac output and regional flow by microspheres |
| Cholinesterase | 6 | 6 | plasma cholinesterase, non-catheterised |
| Validation | 6 | 7 | whether catheterisation, microspheres and blood withdrawal alter the result |

Catheters in the left ventricle (via the right carotid), the abdominal aorta (left femoral) and
the superior vena cava (femoral vein), placed under xylazine-ketamine 2-3 days before. On the
study day the animal sat awake in a semi-dark box rather than a restraint holder.

**Dosing.** Cocaine hydrochloride in saline, **0.33 mg/kg/min into the inferior vena cava over
15.25 min, total 5 mg/kg** (PDF p. 3). A constant infusion was chosen deliberately over a bolus,
so that the plasma concentration would not be moving while the microspheres were injected and
the reference blood withdrawn; the dose was set in preliminary work to produce locomotor and
stereotypic changes without respiratory or circulatory depression. One arterial sample was taken
at the end of the infusion, immediately before the animal was killed; fetal blood came from
cardiac puncture after hysterectomy.

**Assay.** Gas chromatography (Jatlow and Bailey), lower limit **1 ng/ml** on 1 ml of plasma.
Fetal samples were run at 0.1 ml, so their limit was **10 ng/ml**; measured fetal blood was at
least 30 times that (370 +/- 20 ng/ml). Samples were treated with sodium fluoride and potassium
oxalate to inactivate plasma esterases and frozen at -70 C. Tissue was on dry ice within 2-4
minutes of death, which the authors flag as the step that decides whether a tissue number means
anything.

**The three findings, in the authors' own summary (PDF p. 9):** in pregnancy the cardiovascular
and hemodynamic responses to cocaine are enhanced; plasma cocaine is lower while the
tissue-to-plasma ratio is higher; and placental transfer is small.

**Why plasma cocaine is lower, and the number behind it.** The authors measured it: plasma
cholinesterase was **1.52x higher in the pregnant rats** (2,193.7 against 1,441.4 mU/ml),
reproduced in the catheterised validation animals (2,240.6 against 1,427.3). Cocaine is roughly
90% hydrolysed by plasma and liver cholinesterases, so a higher enzyme activity is a sufficient
explanation for a lower plasma concentration at the same infusion rate. They then argue against
their own explanation being the whole of it: across twelve species they measured activities from
over 6,000 down to 200 mU/ml, and sheep — with 5-6 times less of the enzyme than the rat —
metabolise cocaine faster, so hydrolysis by cholinesterase cannot be the only mechanism.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points

Hemodynamics by radiolabelled microspheres (15 um, about 100,000 in saline with Tween 80,
labelled with 46Sc, 57Co or 103Ru), injected into the left ventricle at baseline and again at
14 min of the infusion, with a reference withdrawal from the abdominal aorta at 0.5 ml/min for
90 s.

Cocaine produced a significant **fall in heart rate with a fall in cardiac output**, and reduced
**cerebral, myocardial and placental blood flow**, in the pregnant rats. Arterial pressure rose
and heart rate fell in both groups, but the changes reached significance **only in pregnancy**,
which is the paper's title finding. No EC50, no effect-site model and no concentration-effect
relation is fitted; the comparison is between two groups at one dose rate.

## Covariates

None fitted. The single comparison is pregnant against non-pregnant at term: one species, one
sex, one route, one dose rate. Body weight is recorded but is not used as a covariate.

## Caveats

- **Rats.** Nothing here transfers to a human model, and the library's cocaine is an opt-in
  research model of intranasal cocaine in people. `docs/pregnancy.md` does not apply an animal
  finding, which is why the registry row is `unknown` with no factor.
- **One sample, at the end of the infusion.** There is no time course, so no clearance, volume or
  half-life can be derived, and the apparent 1.3-fold difference in clearance implied by the two
  plasma values is a single-point inference at one dose rate, not a measurement.
- **The fetal-to-maternal ratios in Table 2 are means of per-animal ratios, not ratios of the
  printed means.** Plasma 0.37/1.66 = 0.223 against the printed 0.278, brain 0.86/9.50 = 0.091
  against 0.120, heart 0.74/5.35 = 0.138 against 0.190. Both are legitimate quantities and they
  are not interchangeable; the difference here runs to 20-40%.
- **The paper contradicts itself on which fetal tissue sits furthest below the mother.** The
  Results say the fetal brain and heart were "11 and 7 times lower"; the Discussion says "7 and
  11 times lower, respectively" for the same two. The Results order is the one the table supports
  (9.50/0.86 = 11.0 for brain, 5.35/0.74 = 7.2 for heart).
- **Table 3 is not this paper's data.** It compares placental transfer of cocaine, lidocaine and
  bupivacaine in **sheep**, from the authors' own earlier work (refs 20, 28, 29): cocaine 0.9 and
  0.09 mcg/ml mother and fetus, ratio **0.1**; lidocaine 2.13 +/- 0.21 and 1.18 +/- 0.15, ratio
  0.55; bupivacaine 1.19 +/- 0.18 and 0.43 +/- 0.14, ratio 0.36. Read it from the rendered page:
  the text layer moves the ratio column up one row and hands cocaine lidocaine's 0.55.
- **The authors warn against the intraperitoneal route** for placental-transfer work in small
  rodents, having seen unexpectedly high cocaine in intestine, myometrium, placenta, amniotic
  fluid and fetuses after it. Worth knowing when weighing any rodent cocaine study dosed that
  way.
- Cocaine's metabolites (benzoylecgonine, ecgonine methyl ester) were not measured.
