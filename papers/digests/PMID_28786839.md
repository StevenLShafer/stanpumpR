# Fahy 2018: Technology of processed-EEG depth-of-anesthesia monitors

## Study
Narrative review (no data, no patients). Covers the design principles of processed-EEG (pEEG) depth-of-anesthesia monitors: EEG acquisition and filtering, epoching and artifact rejection (PDF p2, printed 112, Fig 1), feature extraction (time domain: burst suppression rate, wavelet, autoregressive, symbolic dynamics; frequency domain: Fourier transform, bispectral analysis, entropy; middle-latency auditory evoked potentials) (PDF p2-4, Figs 2-3), classification (weighted sum, plausibility analysis, fuzzy logic, neural network) and index smoothing, which adds display delay. A table of 10 commercial monitors is on PDF p4 (printed 115).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, EC50 or concentration-response values. Statements relevant to drug effect (PDF p5, printed 116): ketamine, nitrous oxide and xenon do not produce the usual EEG pattern of other agents; opioids do not change the pEEG index much although they alter the anesthetic state; index display lags changes in drug effect. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio is given.

## Covariates
Patient factors named as altering the EEG (age, hypothermia, hypoglycemia, acid-base disturbance, brain pathology, ischemia, seizures); no equations.

## Caveats
Review, not systematic. Algorithms for most monitors are proprietary. The authors state no monitor has been proven superior and the ASA advisory does not recommend routine use (PDF p5).
