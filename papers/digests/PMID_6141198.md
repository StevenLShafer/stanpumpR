# Simons 1984: hydroxyzine PK and histamine wheal/flare suppression

## Study
Seven healthy fasting non-obese adults (5 M, 2 F; age 20-42 y, mean 29.3 +/- 9.4 (SD); weight 57.0-84.0 kg, mean 66.6 +/- 9.2), single oral dose 0.7 mg/kg hydroxyzine as Atarax syrup (dose 33.5-49.2 mg, mean 39.0 +/- 5.4) with 250 mL water at 0900 (PDF p.2, Table II p.5). Blood at -0.1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72 h. Serum hydroxyzine by HPLC (UV 229 nm, triprolidine internal standard; linear 0-100 ng/mL, CV 10.6% over 6 months, sensitivity 3 ng/mL). PD: intradermal histamine phosphate (0.01 mL of 0.1 mg/mL) at each sample time; wheal and flare areas (planimeter, 10 min after injection) and an itch score 0-4. Three subjects were retested 6 weeks later without drug (control for time-of-day variation; no significant variation found).

## Model and parameters
No compartment model was fitted: too few absorption-phase samples. Non-compartmental: terminal log-linear fit (K, t1/2), AUC by trapezoid + Cp_last/K, CL = Dose/AUC(0-inf), Vd = CL/K, MRT by moments. CL and Vd are NOT corrected for bioavailability (no IV dose), so they are CL/F and Vd/F. The authors say a two-compartment model with first-order absorption may be needed.

Table II (PDF p.5, printed p.73), means +/- SD, n=7:
- Cpmax 72.5 +/- 11.1 ng/mL; tmax 2.1 +/- 0.4 h (6 subjects 2 h, 1 subject 3 h)
- terminal t1/2 20.0 +/- 4.1 h (individual 13.2-26.9)
- MRT 17.7 +/- 5.2 h
- CL 9.78 +/- 3.25 mL/min/kg (individual 6.17-15.22) (derived: 0.587 L/h/kg)
- Vd 16.0 +/- 3.0 L/kg (individual 12.0-19.1) (apparent, per kg)
- AUC individual 642.0-1581.2 ng*h/mL (no mean printed).Self-consistency (derived): subject 1, 42.6 mg / 0.9598 mg*h/L = 44.4 L/h = 740 mL/min, /72.7 kg = 10.2 mL/min/kg, matching the table.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio vs opioids. End points are histamine-induced wheal area, flare area and itch score.
- Wheal area significantly reduced (p <= 0.01) 2-36 h, when mean serum concentration ran from 70.0 +/- 12.9 to 9.6 +/- 2.9 ng/mL. Flare significantly reduced 2-60 h (70.0 down to 5.6 +/- 1.3 ng/mL). Itch significantly suppressed (p <= 0.025) 1-12 h (70.0 to 22.4 ng/mL) and at 36 h (9.6 ng/mL).
- Maximal suppression: wheal 80% (peak 6-12 h), flare 92% (peak 5-9 h) (PDF p.4-5). Peak effect lagged peak serum concentration (2 h) by 4-10 h (clockwise hysteresis in effect-vs-concentration terms; no ke0 or effect-site model fitted).
- Terminal "half-lives" of the log % reduction vs time lines (Fig 3): wheal 22.0 h, flare 46.5 h, against a serum t1/2 of 20.0 h (PDF p.4).
- Adverse effects (dry mouth, drowsiness, light-headedness) in 5 of 7 subjects, 1-6 h, when mean serum concentration exceeded 39.2 +/- 12.3 ng/mL.
- No EC50 or Emax fitted. The only concentration-effect statements are the ranges above.

## Covariates
None analysed (age, sex and weight listed per subject only; dose given per kg).

## Caveats
Single-dose, n=7, oral only (no IV, so no absolute CL or Vd); only about 2 absorption samples; serum (not plasma, not free) concentrations; the authors note serum levels may not reflect skin-site distribution and that enzyme induction or multiple dosing could change kinetics. The terminal t1/2 of 20 h contrasts with about 3 h in an earlier study that quantitated only 1-8 h (discussion, PDF p.4). The authors cite that earlier study (their ref 13, Fouda 1979, GC-MS) as 1.1-1.4 mg/kg in 4 adults. Fig 4 (itch score) was not read in detail.
