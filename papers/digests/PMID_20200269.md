# Knebel 2011: istradefylline population PK, healthy subjects and Parkinson disease

Istradefylline is not a stanpumpR drug and the paper is not cited in the repository; `drugs` is empty.

## Study
- 16 trials: 8 phase 1 (healthy adult and elderly, Caucasian and Japanese, smokers and nonsmokers; single doses 10-200
  mg, multiple doses 10-160 mg/day for 14 days) and 8 phase 2/3 in PD patients with motor complications on
  levodopa/carbidopa (5-80 mg/day, sparse sampling) (Table I, PDF p3-4).
- 1449 subjects (1219 PD patients, 230 healthy), 10,909 plasma concentrations (PDF p6). Weight mean 77.2 (median 75.8,
  33-191) kg; BMI 26.4 (25.7, 15.6-57.2); age 59.1 (62, 18-87) y; LBM 56.1 (56.1, 26.9-83) kg (Table III, PDF p5).
  995 M / 454 F; Caucasian 1169, Asian 155, Hispanic/Latino 77, African American 27, other 21; smokers 65 (4%), unknown
  69; CYP3A4 inhibitor 48, inducer 38; CYP1A2 inhibitor 39, inducer 47 (Table IV, PDF p5).
- Assay: HPLC-UV (5-2000 ng/mL, LLOQ 5) or LC/MS/MS (1-500 ng/mL, LLOQ 1); BLQ excluded (PDF p2).
- NONMEM VI, FOCE-INT; full-covariate-model approach (no stepwise testing); clinical relevance judged by whether the
  bootstrap 95% CI lies within +/-25% of the typical value; stratified bootstrap 1000; predictive check on Cmed (PDF p4-6).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, first-order absorption (ADVAN4 TRANS4), log-transformed data, additive error on the log scale with a
separate residual for each assay; relative F for fed / fasted / unknown food status (unknown = 1). Equation 4 (PDF p7).
Reference: 70 kg, LBM 55.6 kg, age 62, Caucasian, nonsmoker, no CYP3A4 inhibitor, unknown food status.

The abstract's typical values (CL/F 5.76 L/h, V2/F 198 L, Q 21.6 L/h, V3/F 307 L, Ka 0.464 1/h) match Table VI.
Derived disposition half-lives at the reference: 3.5 h and 67 h (derived from the four disposition parameters); the
introduction quotes a terminal half-life of 70-118 h and tmax 2-5 h from earlier studies (PDF p2).

Consequences quoted in the abstract and text (PDF p1, p8-9): smoking lowers AUCss by 38% (95% CI 26-50%); a CYP3A4
inhibitor raises it by 35% (18-55%). Derived from Table VI: 1/1.63 = 0.61 (-39%) and 1/0.736 = 1.36 (+36%), consistent.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None in this paper (exposure measures for a later PK-PD analysis, PMID 22162533). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Clinically relevant on CL/F: smoking and CYP3A4 inhibitors. Point estimates of WT on V3/F and LBM on Q/F fell in the
clinically significant range but were judged not to matter clinically; WT on V2/F, LBM and age on CL/F, race and food
were within +/-25% (PDF p9-10, p12). CYP1A2 inducers/inhibitors were evaluated but are not in the final model (Table VI).

## Caveats
- Underprediction above 1000 ng/mL at doses not used in phase 2/3; nonlinear and dose-dependent CL models did not help
  (PDF p11).
- Food status was by recall at phase 3 clinic visits; 43% unknown (Table IV).
- Table IV footnote c codes 0 = on the medication, 1 = not on it, the reverse of the usual convention; as printed.
- The discussion gives the fed vs fasted increase in relative bioavailability as 17.1% (16.1-18.1%) (PDF p12), but
  Table VI gives 1.10 / 0.929 = 1.184 (+18.4%, derived; +17.5% from the base-model 1.09 / 0.928). Even allowing for
  rounding of the printed estimates the ratio lies between 1.178 and 1.190; the 17.1% is not reproduced (unresolved).
- Sponsor-funded analysis (authors were consultants to or employees of Kyowa Hakko Kirin).
