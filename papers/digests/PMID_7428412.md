# Creatsas 1980: cephapirin and cefalexin levels at term caesarean section

## Study
- 60 healthy pregnant women, 38-40 weeks' gestation, weight 55-70 kg (range only; no mean), all delivered by
  caesarean section; ruptured membranes and other antibiotics excluded (PDF p2, printed p43).
- 30 received cephapirin 1 g IM; the other 30 received **cefalexin 1 g orally** (single dose). Salt/base of the dose not
  stated.
- Maternal serum sampled repeatedly in the same women; amniotic fluid and cord serum once each, at delivery, so each
  amniotic/cord time point is a different subgroup (n 6-10). 376 determinations in all (PDF p3, printed p44).
- Assay: agar diffusion (microbiological) with *Staphylococcus aureus* ATCC 25923; total drug, serum. No PK model fitted;
  no AUC, half-life, clearance or volume reported. Values are mean ± SD (table titles); the very small SDs (e.g.
  34.00 ± 1.52 with n = 30) suggest these may in fact be SEMs, but the paper says S.D.
- Fasting state, timing of dose relative to surgery and renal function not reported. No non-pregnant comparator.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. MICs quoted from other sources: cefalexin *S. aureus* 2-4, *E. coli* 16, *Proteus mirabilis*
16 µg/mL (Table IV, PDF p5); cephapirin *S. aureus* 1, *S. epidermidis* 1, *Klebsiella* 6.2, *E. coli* 12.5, indole-
negative *Proteus* 12.5 µg/mL (Table III, PDF p5). The authors judge amniotic and cord levels adequate against
*S. aureus* but not *E. coli*/*Proteus* for cefalexin. No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None examined.

## Caveats
Microbiological assay (cannot exclude active metabolites/other activity; no validation data given); single dose; term
caesarean patients only; no non-pregnant control; weights as a range only; cross-sectional fetal sampling; SD vs SEM
doubtful; no PK parameters.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- "Creatsas 1980 measured serum and amniotic levels after 1 g orally in 60 pregnant women with no control group"
  (`R/drugs_cefalexin.R:55-56`; same wording "60 pregnant women" in `inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv:51`):
  **discrepancy (minor)**. 60 women were studied in all, but only **30** received cefalexin; the other 30 received
  IM cephapirin. Cord serum was also measured. The rest (1 g PO, no control group) matches.
- That the paper gives no non-pregnant comparator and so cannot set a factor: **matches**; it reports no PK
  parameters and no control.
- The model the factor modifies (Haynes 2024 apparent oral: CL/F 13.98 L/h, V/F 26.63 L, ka 1.79 /h at 70 kg,
  `R/drugs_cefalexin.R:117-127`) does not cite this paper, but the paper is an independent check (derived, 1 g PO,
  62.5 kg, one compartment): without the pregnancy factor the model predicts 25.0, 18.8, 6.9, 2.4 mg/L at 1, 2, 4, 6 h;
  with ×1.5, 21.4, 13.1, 3.0, 0.6 mg/L; observed 34.0, 16.4, 4.5, 1.3. The observed terminal rate (0.64 /h) lies
  between the model's non-pregnant (0.525 /h) and pregnant (0.79 /h) values, so this uncontrolled series supports a
  clearance rise smaller than ×1.5 if V/F is unchanged, about ×1.2 (derived); the higher observed 1-h peak points
  to faster absorption or a smaller V/F than the pediatric-origin model. Total vs total, but bioassay vs the
  Haynes assay. Recorded as an open `[decision]`, not as a discrepancy in the code: the code never claims this
  paper supports 1.5.
- Unit check: µg/mL here = mg/L = the model's mcg/mL. **matches**.

Overall: **discrepancy** (the "60 women" count at `R/drugs_cefalexin.R:56` and `inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv:51`);
no number from this paper enters a calculation.
