# Rathore 2003: serum digoxin concentration and outcomes in heart failure (DIG post hoc)

## Study
Post hoc analysis of the randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled DIG trial (August 1991 to December 1995),
public-use database. Main analysis restricted to **men** with LVEF ≤45% in sinus rhythm (PDF p2, printed 872;
Figure 1). Of 2642 men randomised to digoxin, 1409 had serum digoxin concentration (SDC) measured 1 month after
randomisation; 1171 had a valid SDC drawn **at least 6 h after the previous dose** (text layer, p2). Placebo: 2611
men alive at 1 month (28 early deaths excluded to mirror the digoxin selection). Total n = 3782.
Digoxin group split by 1-month SDC: 0.5-0.8 ng/mL (n=572), 0.9-1.1 ng/mL (n=322), ≥1.2 ng/mL (n=277). No patient
had a recorded SDC below 0.5 ng/mL. Dosing by a published nomogram (Jelliffe 1974) accounting for age, sex,
weight and renal function; prescribed doses 0.125-0.500 mg/d, mostly 0.250 mg/d (Table 1, p3).
Mean age 63.2 y; mean estimated GFR 65.0 mL/min overall, 57.4 mL/min in the ≥1.2 group (Table 1).
End point: all-cause mortality over mean 37 months (range 24-48); secondary: CV death, death from worsening HF,
hospitalisations (all-cause, worsening HF, suspected digoxin toxicity). Cox models adjusted for ~25 covariates
(Table 3 footnote). SAS 8.02. Secondary analysis in 330 women (p6). Assay method not stated.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Outcome by SDC group (Table 2, PDF p5, printed 875; confirmed on the page image), % (n):

| Group | All-cause mortality | Abs. diff vs placebo (95% CI) | HF hospitalisation | Toxicity hospitalisation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Placebo (2611) | 36.2 (945) | — | 34.8 (910) | 0.8 (22) |
| 0.5-0.8 ng/mL (572) | 29.9 (171) | −6.3 (−10.5 to −2.1), P=.005 | 20.8 (119) | 1.2 (7) |
| 0.9-1.1 ng/mL (322) | 38.8 (125) | 2.6 (−3.0 to 8.3), P=.36 | 31.1 (100) | 0.9 (3) |
| ≥1.2 ng/mL (277) | 48.0 (133) | 11.8 (5.7 to 18.0), P<.001 | 29.6 (82) | 3.2 (9) |

P for trend in all-cause mortality .006. Adjusted HR for all-cause mortality vs placebo (Table 3, p5):
0.5-0.8 ng/mL 0.80 (0.68-0.94); 0.9-1.1 ng/mL 0.89 (0.74-1.08); ≥1.2 ng/mL 1.16 (0.96-1.39). Crude: 0.72, 0.99, 1.34.
Women (text layer p6): crude all-cause mortality 25.2%, 33.3%, 41.8% across the three groups (P=.009 for trend);
adjusted HRs 0.89, 1.18, 1.26, none significant.
Authors' conclusion: 0.5-0.8 ng/mL may be the optimal range in men with HF and LVEF ≤45%; ~1.0 ng/mL may offer
no benefit over placebo; ≥1.2 ng/mL may be harmful. Excluding SDC >2.0 ng/mL did not change results (not shown).
No ke0, EC50 or concentration-effect model; not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Higher SDC groups were older, had lower estimated GFR and BMI, and more NYHA III/IV and non-K-sparing diuretic use
(Table 1). Sex: analysis restricted to men because of a previously reported sex-by-digoxin interaction.

## Caveats
Post hoc, non-randomised subgroups (SDC is an outcome of dose and renal function, so confounded by illness
severity); authors call it hypothesis generating. Single SDC at 1 month. Only ~44% of digoxin-arm men had a valid
SDC. No β-blocker data (pre-β-blocker era). Men only for the main result; women underpowered. Assay not stated.
