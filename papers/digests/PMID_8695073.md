# Kirvelä 1996: IV oxycodone in uraemic renal-transplant patients vs ASA I controls

## Study
- Open study, Helsinki University Central Hospital.
- Uraemic: n = 10 (4 F, 6 M), 36 +/- 9 y, 67 +/- 17 kg, serum creatinine 644 +/- 131 µmol/L; haemodialysis 6 (within
  24 h), CAPD 4; cadaveric renal transplant; no graft showed immediate function. Methylprednisolone, azathioprine,
  ciclosporin; cardiovascular drugs in Table 1 (6 on calcium-channel blockers; one on phenytoin + allopurinol).
- Controls: n = 10 ASA I (5 M, 5 F), 36 +/- 11 y, 66 +/- 11 kg, creatinine 72 +/- 11 µmol/L; thyroid, mandibular,
  breast or abdominal surgery.
- Premedication diazepam 0.2 mg/kg PO. Uraemic patients volume loaded with Ringer's acetate and 4% albumin until CVP
  at least 4 mmHg; controls ~500 mL Ringer's acetate.
- Oxycodone chloride (Oxanest) 0.07 mg/kg (= 0.05 mg/kg free base) IV rapid bolus 30 min before induction
  (thiopental, vecuronium, fentanyl, isoflurane/N2O). Pethidine for postoperative pain.
- Venous samples 0, 2.5, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90 min, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 12, 24 h: from a **central venous
  catheter in uraemic patients** and a **peripheral cubital vein in controls**. Urine 0-24 h.
- Assays: plasma oxycodone GC, limit 3 ng/mL (CV 3.9% at 31.5, 6.3% at 12.8 ng/mL); noroxycodone limit 1 ng/mL;
  oxymorphone HPLC limit 0.5 ng/mL (used for urine; plasma oxymorphone results are not reported).
- Per-subject polyexponential fits (iteratively reweighted least squares, 1/pred^2), AIC; biexponential in all.
  Parameters by standard formulae. Mann-Whitney; medians (range).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. **No MEAC, no ke0/t½ke0/tPeak, no potency ratio.** Opioid study is purely pharmacokinetic.

## Covariates
End-stage renal failure (group comparison only): longer t1/2, larger Vc and Vss, CL lower but not significant;
noroxycodone/oxycodone AUC ratio higher; urinary excretion of free oxycodone, free noroxycodone and both forms of
oxymorphone reduced. Authors attribute the CL change possibly to hepatic flow/enzyme effects of uraemia and
co-medication, not renal excretion.

## The dose basis, and what is inferred
Settled by **Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-29**: Oxanest is oxycodone hydrochloride trihydrate (405.87 g/mol, base
fraction 0.7770), and this paper's CL and Vss are on the base.

- **Read** (PDF p2, printed 14): "Oxycodone chloride (Oxanest) 10 mg/ml was given IV as a rapid bolus dose of
  0.07 mg/kg (0.05 mg/kg free base)" - the same product and the same wording as Pöyhiä 1991, from the same group.
  0.07 x 0.7770 = 0.0544, which is the "0.05" rounded.
- **Read** (PDF p3, printed 15): "Elimination half-life, volume of the central compartment, steady-state volume of
  distribution, and clearance were calculated according to the standard formulae"; control medians CL 1.10 L/min,
  Vss 2.39 L/kg, Vc 0.22 L/kg, t1/2z 2.3 h, at a mean control weight of 66 +/- 11 kg (p2) = 16.67 mL/min/kg.
- **Inferred, and more weakly than in Pöyhiä**: that the parameters are on the base. THIS PAPER HAS NO DOSE COLUMN,
  so there is no arithmetic in it that identifies the basis. The inference is by analogy alone - same group, same
  product, same sentence - and that is the whole of the evidence.
- **The alternative, recorded and not adopted**: had the authors divided by the nominal 0.05 mg/kg while giving
  0.07 mg/kg of trihydrate, the factor to the app's anhydrous-hydrochloride basis would be
  0.07 x 0.86685 / 0.05 = 1.2136, giving CL 20.2 mL/min/kg and Vss 2.90 L/kg instead of the 18.6 and 2.67 below.

**Conversion to the app's basis**: x 351.83/315.37 = **1.1156**, so CL 16.67 -> 18.59 mL/min/kg and Vss 2.39 -> 2.67
L/kg for the controls.

## Caveats
- n = 10 per group, huge variability in uraemics (3 with t1/2 10.5-25.7 h).
- The dose basis is an inference by analogy with Pöyhiä 1991, not a statement here and not supported by any
  arithmetic in this paper (see above).
- The uraemic-against-control clearance difference is NOT significant (median 0.84 vs 1.10 L/min, p = 0.131);
  what reaches significance is Vss (3.99 vs 2.39, p = 0.034), Vc (p = 0.023), t1/2z (p = 0.028) and the AUC ratio
  (p = 0.041). A renal covariate taken from this paper rests on the volumes and the half-life, not on its clearance.
- Different sampling sites (central vs peripheral venous) between groups: biases early concentrations and Vc;
  authors acknowledge it.
- Assay limit 3 ng/mL: controls mostly undetectable after 4-6 h, so terminal phase poorly defined.
- Individual weights not given, so per-kg CL and any Q derived from Vc (L/kg) and CL (L/min) need an assumed weight.
- Uraemics volume loaded and given albumin; graft function evolving during sampling.
