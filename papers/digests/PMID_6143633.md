# Abernethy 1984: obesity and oral alprazolam (and triazolam) kinetics

## Study
- 31 healthy subjects aged 24-67 y, stratified; obese and control subjects pair-matched for age, sex, smoking
  and alcohol use. Ideal body weight from Metropolitan Life tables (men 110 lb + 5 lb/inch over 5 ft; women 100 lb
  + 5 lb/inch).
- Alprazolam: single 1 mg oral dose, 12 obese vs 12 controls (5 M / 7 F each). Triazolam: 0.5 mg oral, 9 vs 9.
- Fasting overnight and 3 h after dose; alprazolam sampled to 72 h. GC-ECD assay. Protein binding by equilibrium
  dialysis at 37 C with 14C-alprazolam.
- Weighted nonlinear least-squares fits (one- or two-compartment with first-order absorption, lag if needed);
  AUC trapezoidal + extrapolation; alprazolam CL = dose/AUC assuming complete availability; Vd = CL/beta.

Triazolam (Table II): obese oral CL 340.2 vs 531.2 mL/min (P < 0.025), t1/2 4.14 vs 2.57 h, Vd about 116-117 L in both;
absorption half-time 43.4 vs 46.1 min; peak 4.6 vs 3.7 ng/mL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No ke0, EC50 or MEAC.

## Covariates
Obesity / total body weight: alprazolam Vd correlated with total body weight (r = 0.82) and with percent ideal
body weight (r = 0.71); Vd per kg TBW unchanged, so excess weight takes up alprazolam like lean weight. Clearance
not increased in obesity (numerically lower).

## Caveats
- Single-dose, oral only, small groups (12 pairs); CL and Vd are apparent (F assumed 1).
- Clearance difference not statistically significant; the study cannot exclude a modest change.
- Authors' implication: steady state takes longer in obesity but the plateau is unchanged if dosing follows ideal
  rather than total weight.

- **3.98 vs 5.28 L/h** (`R/drugs_alprazolam.R:73`): Table I 66.4 vs 88.0 mL/min = 3.98 vs 5.28 L/h (derived).
  **Matches.**
- **Help, line 28**: larger volume (113.5 vs 73.1 L), half-life about twice as long (21.8 vs 10.6 h), clearance
  unchanged (not significantly different). **Matches.**
- **Use as support for pharmacokinetic-weight scaling**: the paper supports not scaling clearance on total weight.
  It also shows that **volume does scale with total weight** (1.06-1.16 L/kg TBW in both groups); the default switch
  evaluates V/F at the pharmacokinetic weight (e.g. 63.9 L for a 120 kg, 170 cm man in the tests, against about 84 L on
  total weight at 0.7 L/kg, derived), so the model underpredicts the volume and half-life in obesity. The header
  lists this as not modelled (line 99), so it is acknowledged. **Matches** (acknowledged limitation).
