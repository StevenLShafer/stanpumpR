# Larsen Burns 2019: TDM of gabapentin in various indications

## Study
An observational study in three parts (PDF pp. 1–4, printed 446–449).
1. **Prescription data**: Norwegian Prescription Database. 42 675 gabapentin users in 2017 (8.1 per 1000 inhabitants), 39% of them over 65; 32 181 users in 2014.
2. **Retrospective TDM data**, National Center for Epilepsy, Oslo, 2014–2017 (Fig. 1, p. 2; Table 1, p. 3): 462 samples from 285 patients; one sample per patient. After exclusions, 263 patients remained: 163 F / 100 M, age median 55 (5–90) y, serum concentration median 30 µmol/L (<10–279). Indication: 60 epilepsy, 60 non-epilepsy, 143 unknown; some indications were inferred from co-medication. Prescribed daily dose (n = 183): median 1500 mg (43–6000).
   C/D ratios used 120 patients, excluding known renal impairment, non-fasting samples and results <10 µmol/L. These patients were 78 F / 42 M, median 53 (18–88) y, concentration median 28 µmol/L (10–103), dose median 1800 mg (200–5400).
3. **Prospective RLS series** (Sandvika Neurocenter, Table 2, pp. 4–5): 10 patients with restless legs syndrome, two consultations each; doses 100–2700 mg/day, mostly afternoon or evening.

Samples were drawn "drug fasting": in the morning, before the next dose, at assumed steady state. A sample was counted as not fasting if the form said so or the last dose was under 8 h earlier (p. 3). Assay: HPLC-UV, a modification of a pregabalin method, with monthly external proficiency testing (p. 3). The LLOQ is 10 µmol/L (Fig. 3 caption, p. 4); some laboratories report <5 µmol/L (Table 2 footnote). Matrix: serum. Statistics: SPSS 25, Mann–Whitney or Welch t test.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model was fitted. Only concentration/dose ratios are reported (Table 1, PDF p. 3, confirmed on the page image). C/D is serum concentration in µmol/L per mg of prescribed **daily** dose, from trough ("drug fasting") samples.

Co-medication had no significant effect on C/D (Table 1). Mean C/D with vs without each drug was: lamotrigine 0.024 vs 0.020 (n 20/100), levetiracetam 0.022 vs 0.021, carbamazepine 0.021 vs 0.021. The half-life of 5–7 h in healthy subjects is quoted from ref 10 (Bockbrader 2010), not measured here.

Of 223 eligible patients, 146 (65%) were inside 20–120 µmol/L, 69 (31%) below it and 8 above (Fig. 1; p. 3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No concentration–effect relation was measured. The authors state no data exist for gabapentin in neuropathic or other pain (p. 5).
- The RLS series reports subjective effect (Likert), IRLS scores and the physician's view of whether TDM helped; it gives no concentration threshold. Most RLS concentrations were low against the epilepsy range, e.g. <5 to 86 µmol/L (Table 2).
- No ke0 or effect-site data.
- Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
In this TDM data set, age ≥65 years raised C/D by about 1.56× against adults under 65 (derived 0.028/0.018). The authors attribute this to renal function but had no renal data to test it (p. 5). Sex, indication and co-medication (lamotrigine, levetiracetam, carbamazepine) had no effect.

## Caveats
Retrospective, naturalistic data: adherence and the time of the last dose are uncertain, and indication was missing for most patients. The adult–elderly comparison is between groups within this data set, not against a PK model. Concentrations below 10 µmol/L were excluded from C/D, which biases the ratios upward. The RLS series is n = 10 with subjective outcomes. No renal function data were available.
