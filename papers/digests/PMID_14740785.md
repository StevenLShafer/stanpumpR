# Davis 2004: intranasal hydromorphone in allergic rhinitis, with and without fluticasone

## Study
Open-label randomised three-way crossover, inpatient. 12 patients with seasonal or perennial allergic rhinitis
(5 M, 7 F; 10 Caucasian, 2 African-American; 21-45 y, mean 29.4). Weight not given. Hydromorphone HCl 2.0 mg:
A IV (diluted to 10 mL, infused over 10 min); B IN, no rhinitis treatment for 6 days before; C IN after 6 days of
fluticasone propionate 200 µg nightly. IN 1.0 mg/100 µL single-dose sprayer, one spray per nostril. 6-day washouts.
Sampling 0-16 h (same schedule as Coda 2003). LC-MS/MS, LLOQ 20 pg/mL. Noncompartmental, WinNonlin 3.2.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured (authors: not designed to measure analgesia).
- MEAC: quoted, not measured: minimum effective plasma concentration about 4 ng/mL in severe chronic pain
  (Reidenberg 1988, ref 39), and a half-maximum concentration of 20 ng/mL for chronic cancer pain (Inturrisi 1988
  abstract, ref 40); both described as highly variable (p. 30-31). Assay/whole blood vs plasma not stated.
- ke0 / tPeak: none.
- Potency: hydromorphone 5-8 times morphine (introduction, refs 15-17), not measured.
- Quotes healthy-volunteer IN F of 52% and 57% (Coda 2003) and 57% (range 36-78%).

## Covariates
Fluticasone pretreatment slowed absorption (tmax) but did not change F or AUC. Sex affected Cmax.

## Caveats
n = 12; allergic-rhinitis patients only; weight not reported; text-layer table partly unreadable; internal
inconsistency in which arm is 46.9% vs 51.9% (Discussion vs Abstract/Table).
