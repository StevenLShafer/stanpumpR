# Zang 2021: oral olanzapine popPK in Chinese psychiatric patients

## Study
Single-centre population PK study (Beijing Anding Hospital). 1546 serum olanzapine
concentrations from 354 adult (≥18 y) inpatients with schizophrenia or bipolar
disorder on oral olanzapine tablets only (no IM, no orally disintegrating tablets);
50 further patients formed a prospective external-validation group (Abstract; Methods
2.1, printed p354-355). Most routine TDM samples were drawn 11 h post-dose; an
additional 216 concentrations were drawn 0–30 h post-dose in 203 patients (Methods
2.1, p355). Doses 1.25–30 mg/day. Demographics (Table 1, printed p358, confirmed on
PDF p5): median age 38 y (18–80), median weight 68 kg (39–115), 239 M / 115 F,
smoking 30%, infection 10%, valproate 41%.

Assay: validated HPLC-MS/MS, range 2.5–160 ng/mL (Methods 2.2, p355). One-compartment
model with first-order absorption, Phoenix NLME v8.1, FOCE-ELS. Exponential IIV;
proportional residual error chosen. Bootstrap (1000 runs, 906 successful) and VPC for
evaluation; forward inclusion P<0.05 / backward deletion P<0.01 (Methods; Results 3.2).

Simplified clearance equation (Eq 14, printed p357): CL/F (L/h) = 12.88 × 1.23^(sex=M)
× 1.23^(smoking) × 0.75^(infection) × 1.23^(valproate) × 0.78^(perphenazine)
× 1.16^(sertraline) × 0.70^(fluvoxamine) × 2.01^(dangguilonghui). These multipliers
reconcile exactly with the Table 3 ln-coefficients. Body weight was tested and **not**
retained (Discussion 4.4, p365). Reported source units are L/h, 1/h, h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None — pure PK/dose-individualisation study. Olanzapine is not an opioid, so no MEAC,
ke0, tPeak or potency ratio applies. The therapeutic target used for dosing simulations
is the AGNP 2017 TDM reference range 20–80 ng/mL trough (Methods 2.7 / Table 6,
printed p360-361), a guideline target, not a PD measurement from this study.
Proposed once-daily doses (Table 6): female nonsmoker 10–15 mg/day up to male smoker
on valproate 17.5–25 mg/day.

## Covariates
On CL/F only: sex (M +23%), smoking (+23%), co-occurrence of infection (−25%),
valproate (+23%), perphenazine (−22%), sertraline (+16%), fluvoxamine (−30%),
dangguilonghui tablets (+101%). All dichotomous (present=1/absent=0). No covariate on
Ka or V/F retained. (Results 3.2; Eqs 13–14.)

## Caveats
Authors' limitations (Discussion 4.4, p365): small sample for some inducers (omeprazole
n=2, carbamazepine n=1, phenobarbital n=1 — excluded for lack of power); dangguilonghui
effect not externally validated; infection/valproate/dangguilonghui modelled only
dichotomously (severity ignored); BW not significant and BMI/body-fat not tested; dosing
regimens need prospective confirmation. Added by this reader: single-centre, Chinese
ancestry (authors argue Asians may clear olanzapine ~0.79–0.93× of Caucasians,
Discussion §5, p365-366); sertraline coefficient %CV is high (35%) with a 95% CI that
touches 0.00; the apparent (/F) parameterisation confounds absorption/bioavailability
with clearance. A correction to Table 3 is referenced in the stanpumpR docs (see
open_questions).

Independent cross-check against the implemented model (Sun 2021, `R/drugs_olanzapine.R`,
which does not cite Zang). Sun's reference is a 70 kg nonsmoking **man**: CL/F 15.5 L/h,
female ×0.862 ⇒ ~13.4 L/h; Vc/F 656 + Vp/F 225 = 881 L; ka 0.861/h. Zang's reference is
a **female** nonsmoker: CL/F 12.88 L/h, single V/F 754 L, Ka 0.30/h. Female-to-female,
the two apparent clearances are close (Zang 12.88 vs Sun ~13.4 L/h, Zang ≈0.96×); Zang's
single-compartment V/F (754 L) sits below Sun's summed 881 L, as expected for a
one-compartment fit; Zang's absorption is ~3× slower (0.30 vs 0.861 /h). Both agree
olanzapine clears ~1.23× faster in smokers and in males. The models are mutually
consistent; stanpumpR's choice of Sun 2021 over the one-compartment Zang model is
reasonable and the exclusion carries no error into the plot.
