# Zang 2021: oral olanzapine population PK in Chinese psychiatric inpatients

## Study
- 354 adult inpatients (≥18 y) at Beijing Anding Hospital with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder, all on oral
  immediate-release olanzapine tablets, 1.25-30 mg/day (PDF p3). Table 1 (PDF p6): age median 38 (18-80) y; weight
  median 68 (39-115) kg; 239 men and 115 women; 105 smokers (30%); 35 (10%) with an infection; valproate in 144 (41%),
  duloxetine 37, Dangguilonghui 27, promethazine 25, paroxetine 21, sertraline 20, fluoxetine 16, bupropion 12,
  perphenazine 11, fluvoxamine 6, chlorpromazine 6; carbamazepine, phenobarbital, omeprazole and clomipramine 1-2 each.
- 1546 **serum** concentrations: routine TDM samples taken 11 h after the last dose, plus 216 sampled prospectively
  0-30 h after the dose in 203 patients (PDF p3; Fig. 1, PDF p6). 34 patients under 18 (149 samples) were excluded
  (PDF p13).
- Assay: HPLC-MS/MS, range 2.5-160 ng/mL; QC at 6.4, 64 and 128 ng/mL; precision within 15%, accuracy 85-115% (PDF p3).
- Phoenix NLME 8.1, FOCE-ELS; exponential IIV, proportional residual error chosen (PDF pp3, 5). Covariates entered
  at ΔOFV ≥ 3.84 and retained at ≥ 6.63. Bootstrap of 1000 runs (906 successful), VPC, and an external validation in
  50 patients (two samples each, ≥7 days apart).

## Model and parameters
One compartment with first-order absorption and elimination, apparent oral parameters. No lag. Table 3 (PDF p7,
printed 359, rendered); typical patient is a nonsmoking woman with no infection and no listed comedication
(PDF p11):

    ka    = 0.30 /h
    V/F   = 754.41 L                        (no covariates; body weight not significant)
    CL/F  = 12.88 L/h x exp(0.21 male) x exp(0.21 smoker) x exp(-0.29 infection) x exp(0.21 valproate)
            x exp(-0.25 perphenazine) x exp(0.15 sertraline) x exp(-0.36 fluvoxamine) x exp(0.70 Dangguilonghui)

Eq. 14 (PDF p5) gives the multipliers 1.23, 1.23, 0.75, 1.23, 0.78, 1.16, 0.70 and 2.01; each is exp of the
Table 3 coefficient (exp 0.21 = 1.234, exp -0.29 = 0.748, exp 0.70 = 2.014; derived).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, no effect site, no ke0. The therapeutic range used for the simulations is 20-80 ng/mL steady-state
serum trough, quoted from the AGNP 2017 guideline (ref [15], PDF pp2, 4, 11). Not an opioid paper: no MEAC or potency
ratio.

## Covariates
On CL/F only, all dichotomous: male sex, smoking, infection (mostly mild-moderate respiratory; severe cases
transferred out and not sampled), valproate, perphenazine, sertraline, fluvoxamine, Dangguilonghui (a Chinese
herbal laxative). Age, weight, ALT, AST, SCr and CrCL tested and not retained. Fluoxetine, paroxetine, duloxetine,
bupropion and the inducers carbamazepine, phenobarbital and omeprazole had no significant effect (PDF p12; the
inducers had n = 1-2).

## Caveats
- Comparative claims and their baselines: "1.23-fold higher" for male sex and smoking are against women and
  nonsmokers in this model. The discussion's Asian vs Caucasian CL/F figures (26.26 vs 22.6 L/h; 18.5 vs 14.6 L/h after
  10 mg) come from other studies (refs [17], [108], PDF p14), not from this dataset.
- Text: the mean CL/F with Dangguilonghui is 25.73 L/h (PDF p12). 12.88 × 2.01 = 25.89 (derived), so the 25.73 is
  presumably a mean over that subgroup rather than the typical value.
- Sparse TDM design: most samples were taken at 11 h, so ka and V/F are less well identified than CL/F. A single
  compartment, against two in Sun 2021.
- No body-size covariate. Inpatients only; adherence not checked; Dangguilonghui, valproate and infection are
  each modelled as present or absent; no external check of the Dangguilonghui effect (PDF p13, limitations).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `R/drugs_olanzapine.R:37-38`: the paper's model is indeed one compartment, fitted in Chinese psychiatric
  inpatients, with apparent oral parameters, and the library uses none of its numbers. **Matches.**
- `docs/antipsychotics.md:87-88`, "corrected Table 3": **cannot verify**. The rendered Table 3 is clean and agrees with
  Eq. 13-14, and no correction is in this PDF (open question).
- Independent comparison with the implemented model (Sun 2021, `R/drugs_olanzapine.R:57-64`, nonsmoker, fasted, 70 kg):
  - CL/F. Sun gives 15.5 L/h for a man and 15.5 × 0.862 = 13.4 L/h for a woman (derived). Zang gives 15.89 L/h for a
    nonsmoking man and 12.88 L/h for a nonsmoking woman (median weight 68 kg, no weight term). Zang is therefore 1.03x
    Sun in men and 0.96x in women. Within this cohort Zang's male/female ratio is 1.23, against Sun's 1/0.862 = 1.16.
    Smoking is ×1.23 in Zang against ×1.30 in Sun, and the library does not apply smoking in either case. Steady-state
    exposure, which depends only on CL/F, agrees within about 5%; the header does not claim otherwise.
  - Distribution and absorption. Zang's V/F of 754 L (one compartment) lies between Sun's Vc/F of 656 L and Vc + Vp of
    881 L. Zang's ka is 0.30 /h with no lag; Sun's is 0.861 /h with a 0.782 h lag. Zang's peak would therefore be
    later and flatter, but its sparse 11-h trough data identify ka poorly.
  - Units. Both papers give L/h and 1/h; the library converts Sun's values to per-minute units (`:92-97`). Nothing
    from Zang needs converting.
- Therapeutic band: Zang uses AGNP 2017's 20-80 ng/mL; the library deliberately uses Wesner 2023's 20-40 ng/mL
  (`R/drugs_olanzapine.R:51-54, 113-115`). That is a different source, chosen on purpose, not a discrepancy.
