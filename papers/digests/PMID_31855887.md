# Burton 2020: propofol TCI for procedural sedation, systematic review

## Study
Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42015026067) of RCTs, 1946 to January 2019, comparing propofol TCI with other propofol regimens (manual infusion, bolus) for adult procedural sedation in any hospital setting (Methods, p.254). 462 records, 32 full texts, 7 trials included (Fig. 1); none was in an emergency department. Elective fasted patients, 40 to 220 per trial (Table 2). Risk of bias by the Cochrane tool (Fig. 2). Meta-analysis was not possible because of heterogeneous designs and adverse-event definitions. Conclusion (p.259): fewer respiratory or hypotensive events with TCI in 3 of 7 studies; no firm conclusion that TCI reduces adverse events.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, EC50 or tPeak given. End points are adverse events: SpO2 below 90% (mild) or 85% (moderate), hypotension, bradypnoea, interventions. Chiang 2013 (Table 1, p.255; text p.258): mild desaturation 30.9% TCI vs 44.5% manual infusion (RR 0.694, 95% CI 0.490-0.983); moderate 15.5% vs 30.9% (RR 0.5). Franzen 2016: mean lowest SpO2 88.3 +/- 5.4% TCI vs 86.9 +/- 7.3% fractionated bolus (non-inferior). Wang 2016: lowest MAP 72.9 vs 67.7 mmHg, lowest SpO2 97.4 vs 95.6% (TCI vs manual). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
The review states that Marsh scales compartment volumes to actual weight whereas Schnider uses lean body mass (p.257), and that Schnider was run with an effect-site target and Marsh with a plasma target in the included trials. These are descriptions of how the trials were run, not properties the review tests.

## Caveats
Poor quality and heterogeneous trials; no ED trial; adverse-event definitions differ (severe desaturation SpO2 <90% in one trial, "mild" in another); none used the SIVA reporting tool; several authors report equipment support from a TCI-pump manufacturer (p.259).
