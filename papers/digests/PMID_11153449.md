# Cox 1999: repeated time-to-event model of ondansetron antiemesis after ipecac

## Study
- Two phase I trials, 86 **healthy men** in total.
  - W91021: 36 men, ondansetron 0.1, 0.25, 1, 4 or 8 mg IV over 5 min; 30 ml syrup of ipecac orally 30 min later.
  - G93048: five groups of 10 men, ondansetron 4 mg IV over 5 min, ipecac at 30 min or 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 or 24 h later; rescue ondansetron 4 mg allowed (12 subjects, 14%).
- Emesis times recorded for 24 h after ipecac; nausea by VAS or 0-10 scale. Ondansetron by HPLC or RIA; individual concentrations predicted from a prior population PK model (not shown).
- NONMEM, Laplacian; log hazard model with ipecac-driven term, ondansetron term and a log-normal frailty; posterior predictive checks.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: emesis events after an ipecac challenge (hazard of vomiting), not PONV or CINV.
- Ondansetron halves the emesis hazard for each 1.4 ng/mL increment (Results), using plasma concentration directly; **no effect site or ke0**. Model 4 (concentration at ipecac time only) fitted about equally well.
- Ipecac hazard peaks ~35 min, half-life about half an hour.
- Nausea score raises the hazard by one third per point (model 2).
- 47 of 86 (55%) had at least one emesis; ondansetron at ipecac time 0.3-47 ng/mL.
- Not an opioid; no MEAC; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Nausea score and previous emeses tested (model 2, 3); no demographic covariates.

## Caveats
Healthy men, ipecac model; PK predictions from a separate analysis; methodological paper.
