# Buvanendran 2010: plasma and CSF pregabalin after a single 300 mg oral dose

## Study
- 9 patients for primary total knee replacement (7 F, 2 M), age 66.6 y (61-72), weight 90.1 kg (73-127); all calculated GFR >= 54 (the paper prints "mL/hr", presumably mL/min).
- Pregabalin 300 mg PO 1 h before surgery; spinal anaesthesia (hyperbaric bupivacaine 11.25 mg + fentanyl 25 mcg) and an intrathecal catheter (~L1) for postoperative fentanyl/bupivacaine infusion and CSF sampling; midazolam sedation.
- Paired venous plasma and CSF at 2, 4, 6, 8 and 24 h (no 1 h or overnight samples). HPLC (Bioassay Laboratory); LLOQ 0.002 mcg/mL CSF, 0.1 mcg/mL plasma.
- Non-compartmental: linear trapezoid AUC0-24, AUC0-inf = AUC0-24 + C24 x t1/2/0.693.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: CSF concentration only; no analgesic or antihyperalgesic effect measured in patients.
- Comparison with rat data: CSF 0.115 mcg/mL at 2 h exceeds the rat brain anticonvulsant EC50 of 0.095 mcg/mL (electroshock); CSF 0.359 mcg/mL at 6 h is near the rat brain 0.334 mcg/mL associated with a 60% reduction in cold allodynia. The authors call this suggestive, not proof.
- No ke0 or effect-site model. Hysteresis: CSF lags plasma markedly; ratio rises with time to 0.26 at 24 h. Authors suggest dosing 4 h before surgery.
- Adverse events: dizziness 3, sedation 1; no relation to CSF AUC.
- Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None studied (n = 9). Renal function screened only.

## Caveats
Small n; elderly surgical patients with concurrent anaesthetics; first sample at 2 h misses the plasma peak (about 1 h); lumbar CSF via a catheter; single dose level, so CSF linearity unknown; GFR unit misprinted.
