## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratios. No opioid audit entries. Facts stated in the text (PDF pp.2-9, printed pp.113-120):
- Incidence depends on detection method. Isolated forearm technique (IFT) median 37% responding to command (Sanders 2012 review); ConsCIOUS-1 12/260 (stated 4.6% in text, 4% in Table 2).
- Structured Brice-questionnaire studies: Sandin 2000 0.15%; B-Aware 0.89% routine care vs 0.16% BIS; B-unaware 0.6% BIS vs 0.3% end-tidal anaesthetic concentration (ETAC); BAG-RECALL 0.24% vs 0.07%; SNAP-1 0.12% (1:800).
- NAP5 spontaneous reports 1:19,600 (0.005%); Pollard 0.0068%.
- Two-thirds of NAP5 cases were at induction/emergence. Risk factors: thiopentone then volatile, rapid sequence, obesity, difficult airway, NMBA use, female sex, young age.
- BAG-RECALL alarm settings: BIS 40 and 60; ETAC 0.7 and 1.3 MAC. BIS did not outperform ETAC alarms (B-unaware, BAG-RECALL, Michigan trial).
- Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic variability is offered as a speculative explanation for some AAGA; no data.
