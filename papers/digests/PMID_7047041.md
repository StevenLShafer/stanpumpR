# Ludden 1982: clinical pharmacokinetics of hydralazine (review)

PDF page n = printed page 184 + n.

## Study
Narrative review by the San Antonio group whose specific assay produced Ludden 1980 (the review's "Ludden et al.
1980b"; Reece 1980 is its "Reece et al. 1980b"). No new data. Main themes: non-selective assays measure
acid-labile metabolites (chiefly the pyruvic acid hydrazone) as "apparent hydralazine"; hydralazine is unstable
in plasma in vitro (half-life about 6 min at 37 C; summary, p1); oral first-pass is acetylator-dependent.

Non-selective assays (Reidenberg 1973, Shen 1980, Talseth 1976c) give lower clearance and longer half-life than
selective ones (Ludden 1980b, Reece 1980b) (2.4, PDF p10); the review says non-selective assays seriously
underestimate Varea (2.3, PDF p7). Shen 1980's modified GC method is more selective than older ones but still
measures labile metabolites to some extent (2.2, PDF p6). Hepatic extraction ratios 0.90 (rapid) and 0.68 (slow)
from the older hypertensive patients' data (p11). IV clearance independent of dose 0.2-0.45 mg/kg in 6
hypertensive men (Ludden 1981b), one rapid acetylator excepted (p10-11). Acetylator phenotype has no detectable
effect on IV kinetics (p11). Evidence of a saturable first pass (2.2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect model. Hypotension lasts 8 h or more after a single dose while plasma hydralazine falls
quickly (5.1, PDF p15); reflex tachycardia, arterial-wall localisation, baroreceptor resetting and a reservoir
hydrazone are discussed as explanations; the pyruvic acid hydrazone has little or no hypotensive activity in
animals (p16). Shepherd 1981: after oral doses the maximal fall in MAP correlates with peak hydralazine
concentration or AUC (p16). Not an opioid; no ke0.

## Covariates
Acetylator phenotype (oral F only); food raises F 2-3-fold (p7); renal failure effect unclear (3.2); heart failure
unstudied (3.3).

## Caveats
A review by the authors of one of the two specific-assay studies it compares. The young-versus-older difference is
left open: different groups, designs and assays (summary, p1; 2.3, p7; 2.4, p10). It describes Reece's subjects as
"10 healthy males" (2.2, p7); Reece's Table I has one woman. Table I ranges, not SDs; no distribution phase for any
study.
