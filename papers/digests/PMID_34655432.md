# Wang 2021: cetagliptin first-in-human single-ascending-dose and food-effect studies

## Study
Two phase 1 studies in healthy Chinese volunteers aged 18-45 y (80 total; BMI 19-26 kg/m2 in Study 1).
- **Study 1** (n = 66): double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled single oral dose escalation. Cetagliptin 12.5, 25, 50, 100, 200, 300, 400 mg (44 subjects; cohort n = 4, 6, 8, 8, 6, 6, 6 on drug, Table 1/Table 2), placebo n = 12, open-label sitagliptin 100 mg n = 10 as positive control (none in the 25 mg cohort). Tablets with 240 mL water, fasted at least 10 h. Mean age 24.9 (SD 5.1) y, weight 61.1 (7.7) kg (46.2-78.6), BMI 21.6 (1.7). Sexes stratified 1:1; the authors say male and female PK were similar and so only males went into Study 2.
- **Study 2** (n = 14 men): open-label two-period crossover, 100 mg fasted vs after FDA high-fat breakfast, 14-day washout. Mean weight 64.8 (4.4) kg (57.5-73.3), age 25.6 (5.5) y.
- Sampling: plasma to 72 h for 12.5 mg (18 timepoints), to 120 h for all others (pre-dose, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120 h). Urine (50, 100, 300 mg cohorts) and faeces (100 mg cohort) to 120 h.
- Assay: validated LC-MS/MS (protein precipitation, deuterated internal standard); plasma range 0.5-5000 ng/mL; urine 0.1-100 ug/mL; faeces 0.1-100 ug/g. Total plasma concentration; protein binding about 54% in human plasma (preclinical data, Introduction, not published). Whole blood not used.
- Analysis: non-compartmental (WinNonlin 8.0); dose proportionality by power model; food effect by crossover mixed model. PK/PD by Emax model in WinNonlin (WNL5 classic).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: plasma DPP-4 activity (fluorometric assay, percent change from pre-dose baseline), Study 1 only. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or potency ratio between opioids.
- Emax model E = Emax*C/(EC50 + C) (p3, Methods; fit text p4, printed 1004, rendered): cetagliptin Emax 92.04% inhibition, EC50 5.98 ng/mL (plasma, total drug); sitagliptin Emax 91.94%, EC50 9.60 ng/mL. Direct-effect model; no effect-site delay, no hysteresis analysis reported. (Preclinical in-vitro IC50 8.1 nM cetagliptin vs 18 nM sitagliptin, Introduction, unpublished data.)
- Table 4 (text layer only): maximum DPP-4 inhibition Rmax 60.6, 74.6, 86.1, 89.1, 90.7, 91.9, 92.0% at 12.5-400 mg vs 89.4% for sitagliptin 100 mg; weighted average inhibition 0-24 h (WAI) 49.8, 62.3, 76.9, 82.4, 87.4, 89.6, 89.8% vs 79.4%; time above 80% inhibition (DUR80) 0, 0, 9.4, 18.2, 33.2, 42.6, 48.3 h vs 13.9 h; DPP-4 Tmax about 1-5 h, consistent with plasma Tmax. Placebo NA.
- Active GLP-1 AUEC0-24 up about 2.1-3.4-fold over placebo for cetagliptin, 2.8-fold for sitagliptin 100 mg. Glucose and insulin AUC0-24 not meaningfully changed (Table 5).
- The authors' comparison of cetagliptin 100 mg with sitagliptin 100 mg is against the open-label sitagliptin group in the same study, using this study's fluorescence endpoint method (sitagliptin 24 h inhibition 65% here vs about 75% in earlier work, which they attribute to the assay).

## Covariates
None modelled. Sex: authors state male and female PK were similar (Discussion), no numbers given. No weight, renal or age analysis. Renal elimination of unchanged drug is 27-46% of the dose (Table 2).

## Caveats
- Healthy Chinese adults, mean weight about 61 kg; small cohorts (4-8 per dose); single doses only; no bioavailability study (so CL/F and V/F are apparent) and no mass balance.
- Cmax rises more than proportionally and AUC slightly more (beta 1.10-1.14); mechanism not established (authors suggest saturable transport, since cetagliptin is a P-gp substrate).
- Vz/F falls with dose (1850 to 787 L, 50-400 mg), which is the same non-linearity seen as falling CL/F.
- Sponsor-funded (CGeneTech); some authors are employees. PK/PD uses sitagliptin as control with a different assay method than earlier work.
- Printed Table 3 criteria line and Table 2 footnotes are consistent; in the text layer the Table 2 rows below Cmax are scrambled.

- **R/drugs_cetagliptin.R** (Lu 2024 two-compartment model, CL/F 30.55 L/h, V/F 204 L, CL2/F 133 L/h, V2/F 160 L, ka 1.56 /h, saturable efflux Vmax 41731 mcg/h, Kt 11.63 mcg/L, linear elimination). This paper does not give those parameters, so it cannot confirm them; it gives an independent check.
  - Clearance: model CL/F 30.55 L/h lies within this paper's per-dose means, 21.4-33.4 L/h (Table 2): **matches** in magnitude.
  - Supraproportional Cmax (beta 1.35, Table 3) is the pattern a saturating efflux produces; the header's statement that exposure rises faster than dose because of efflux, not elimination, is consistent with Cmax here. Not checked by simulation.
  - AUCinf: the linear-clearance model gives dose/CL, which falls short of this paper's AUCinf at high dose (derived: -7% at 100 mg, -16% at 200 mg, -30% at 400 mg; 50 mg matches). See open question 1. Verdict: **cannot fully verify; observed non-linearity at 200-400 mg is not reproduced**.
  - V/F: the model's V1 + V2 = 364 L (derived) is not comparable with Vz/F 787-1850 L, which is a terminal-phase apparent volume; no check made.
  - Tmax 1-3.25 h (Table 2) not simulated.
  - Doses 12.5-400 mg match the header's dose range statement. The header says Lu 2024 gave no demographics and awaits this paper: the weights are 61.1 kg and 64.8 kg (mean), against the model's 70 kg reference (open question 2).
