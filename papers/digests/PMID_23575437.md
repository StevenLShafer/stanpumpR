# Mion and Villevieille 2013: ketamine pharmacology review

## Study
Narrative review (two French anaesthesiologists): physicochemistry, metabolism, kinetics, routes, children, EEG and
imaging, concentration-effect relations, psychodysleptic effects, antidepressant action, NMDA and other mechanisms,
neurotoxicity. No new data; every number is attributed to a reference.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Concentration-effect paragraph (PDF p4, printed 373):
- memory impaired at 70 ng/mL; lateral nystagmus at about 200 ng/mL (ref 45, Bowdle 1998);
- analgesia persists at steady-state plasma concentrations above 100-160 ng/mL, about 0.5 uM (ref 49, Clements
  1982); after a peripheral nerve lesion, effective concentrations against hyperalgesia and allodynia at least above
  100 ng/mL (ref 50); after oral dosing about 40 ng/mL, attributed to norketamine (ref 51);
- narcosis ED50 0.4-0.7 mg/kg; anaesthesia ED50/ED95 0.6/1.3 mg/kg, about 10 uM;
- psychodysleptic effects from as low as 50 ng/mL; 600-1100 ng/mL during emergence; linear relation at 50-200 ng/mL
  in volunteers (ref 45); anxiety and paranoia around 500 ng/mL (ref 52);
- 200 ng/mL reduced pain scores and insular/thalamic fMRI activation (ref 29). Summary: analgesia persists at
  concentrations ten times lower than hypnotic ones.
Norketamine potency 20-30% of ketamine (ref 21). S(+) twice the racemate and four times R(-). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Qualitative: sex (CL 20% higher in women), renal dysfunction (concentrations 20% higher), enzyme inducers and
inhibitors, age (children, neonates).

## Caveats
- "Steady-state" is Mion's word: Clements 1982 measured after single IM doses, and states pain-threshold elevation
  consistently above 160 ng/mL and not below 80, with means 186 vs 101 ng/mL; the "100-160" span is Mion's
  summary, not a number Clements printed (see `PMID_7097501.md`).
- "about 0.5 uM": (derived: 100-160 ng/mL / 238 g/mol = 0.42-0.67 uM).
- Transfer half-life under 1 min is unreferenced.
