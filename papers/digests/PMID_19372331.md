# Vuyk 2009: midazolam lowers propofol clearance (3-compartment, NONMEM)

## Study
Eight healthy men, 20-30 y (mean 21.8 +/- 1.8 y, 73.6 +/- 9.7 kg, 182.1 +/- 6.0 cm, within 30% of ideal weight), randomized crossover, two sessions >= 2 wk apart. Session A: propofol 1 mg/kg over 1 min then 2.5 mg/kg/h (= 41.7 mcg/kg/min) for 59 min. Session B: the same propofol plus a midazolam TCI at a constant target of 125 ng/mL, started 15 min before propofol and held for 6 h after it stopped. The TCI used the three-compartment midazolam set of Zomorodi 1998 (the paper's ref 10, CABG patients). Measured plasma midazolam averaged 224.8 +/- 41.6 ng/mL (Fig 1), i.e. about 1.8x the target; the authors suggest propofol may raise midazolam levels or that volunteers differ from Zomorodi's patients. Midazolam given: 13.1 +/- 2.1 mg in the first hour, total 49.25 +/- 5.32 mg.
Arterial samples (368 propofol values used): propofol in whole BLOOD by HPLC-fluorescence (CV 4.3% intra, 3.7% inter; 0.06-6.8 mcg/mL); midazolam in plasma by HPLC-UV (CV 2.2%/2.0%; 9.7-1120 ng/mL), hourly in session B. Cardiac output by LiDCO. NONMEM VI 1.2, FOCE with eta-epsilon interaction, ADVAN11, proportional error, log-normal IIV; covariate form exp(alpha x (cov - median)); model selection by AIC over all 2^6 midazolam-on-parameter combinations, then the same for MAP (time-varying, mean of the readings before each midazolam sample). Median covariates: midazolam 112.375 ng/mL (session B only), MAP 74.017 mm Hg. Simulations used TIVAtrainer for a 74 kg man.
Propofol blood concentration was on average 25.1 +/- 13.3% higher with midazolam (Fig 2, p1525). Table 1 (p1525, PDF p4), medians session A vs B: MAP 78.3 vs 68.4 mm Hg (-12.5%, P<0.001); HR 61.8 vs 64.7 (+3.5%); SV 107.7 vs 91.1 mL/beat (-9.4%, P<0.001); CO 6.6 vs 5.5 L/min (-4.0%, P 0.15); SVR 909 vs 830 (-4.4%, P 0.12). ## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No ke0, EC50, MEAC or effect measure is reported (Bispectral Index was monitored but not analysed). Simulated context-sensitive times (Fig 8, 50% and 80% decrement, 74 kg man, MAP 78 vs 68 mm Hg): both are shorter with midazolam 225 ng/mL, because less propofol accumulates in the peripheral compartments. No numeric values are tabulated. Dose advice from the simulations: with midazolam, reduce the propofol bolus by 25% and the infusion by 20% for the same plasma concentration (p1527, text).

## Covariates
Midazolam concentration (Cl1, Cl2, Cl3) and MAP (V1, V2, Cl3), as above. No weight, age or sex covariate (all men 20-30 y, 54-90 kg). Higher MAP gives lower V1, higher V2 and higher Cl3.

## Caveats
n = 8 young healthy men, a single propofol regimen (1 mg/kg then 2.5 mg/kg/h), 16 sessions. Blood (not plasma) propofol. Measured midazolam was ~1.8x its target, so the TCI parameters did not fit these volunteers. Authors attribute the effect to haemodynamics (lower MAP and SV) rather than enzyme inhibition (in vitro microsome work shows no inhibition), but a midazolam effect independent of MAP is estimated alongside the MAP effect; the two are partly confounded because midazolam lowered MAP (the covariate MAP was the mean of readings before each midazolam sample, and Cl1 depends only on midazolam). Cardiac output (LiDCO) was not itself a covariate: only MAP entered. SE of Cl3 in column 1 (0.56) is much larger than the estimate's SE in other columns; likely a misprint, not checked further. CV of V2 not estimable.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No cited location, so no verdict. Independent comparisons (derived: Eleveld equations from R/drugs_propofol.R:172-259 run for a 73.6 kg, 182.1 cm, 21.8 y man):
- Eleveld V1 6.38, V2 32.96, V3 220, CL 1.75, Q2 2.27, Q3 0.93 L (L/min) against Vuyk's no-covariate set V1 4.87, V2 26.4, V3 137, Cl1 1.76, Cl2 2.13, Cl3 0.84. Elimination and distribution clearances agree within about 10%; V1 is 24% smaller and V3 38% smaller in Vuyk. The direction is as expected for a whole-blood fit of arterial data to 6 h (blood concentrations run higher than plasma, so fitted volumes come out smaller), but I did not verify the blood:plasma ratio.
- stanpumpR has no midazolam-on-propofol or MAP-on-propofol term (R/drugs_propofol.R:68-290; midazolam drugs_midazolam.R:109-207), so the combination effect (propofol concentration +25% with midazolam ~225 ng/mL) is not modelled. Cl1 at midazolam 225 ng/mL is 1.61/1.94 = 0.83 of its value at 0 (derived from Table 3).
- Midazolam TCI: Vuyk drove midazolam with the Zomorodi 1998 set and measured ~225 ng/mL against a 125 ng/mL target. The library uses Zomorodi's Table 3 "Buhrer" column (R/drugs_midazolam.R:150-157), the paper cites only Zomorodi 1998 (CABG patients) for its TCI set and does not say which column; see the [source] open question. No discrepancy asserted.
