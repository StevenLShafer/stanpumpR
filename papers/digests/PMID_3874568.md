# Alvis 1985: Computer-assisted continuous infusion (CACI) of fentanyl in cardiac anesthesia

## Study
- 30 CABG patients (SBP > 100 mmHg, EF > 0.40, age < 75), University of Alabama in Birmingham. Premedication diazepam, scopolamine, morphine; induction diazepam 0.3 mg/kg + pancuronium in 50% N2O.
- Three groups of 10: C (manual boluses 150-250 ug), S (CACI constant set point 7.5 ng/mL), V (CACI starting at 7.5 ng/mL, set point varied by the anesthesiologist). Study period to bypass or first hour.
- CACI: PASCAL on an Apple II Plus driving an IMED 929 pump; loading dose Vc x Cc x weight given within 90 s by a proportional algorithm, then u(t) = Vc Cc Mp (k10 + k12 e^-k21 t + k13 e^-k31 t); three-compartment model discretised (bilinear transform), 15-s update; pump stopped when the target was lowered. Fentanyl rate constants from McClain and Hug 1980 (ref 4).
- Arterial plasma fentanyl by GC/MS (alfentanil internal standard), LOD 1.0 ng/mL, values < 2 ng/mL not reported; samples at 5-60 min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Hemodynamic end points (HR, BP, rate-pressure product at laryngoscopy, chest incision, sternotomy, mediastinal dissection; Table 3, PDF p6, printed p46) and adjuvant drug use. Statistics (Methods, PDF p3, printed p43): count and frequency data by chi-square contingency tables, P < 0.05. All from PDF pp5-7, printed pp45-47, n = 10 per group:
- **Responders** (SBP rise > 30% pre- to post-event): laryngoscopy C 4, S 2, V 3 (n.s.); chest incision C 3, S 1, V 0 (V vs C P = 0.09); none at sternotomy or dissection. HR in V significantly lower than C at all periods (Table 3).
- **BP episodes** (Table 4; sustained > 30 s; hypotension SBP < 90, hypertension SBP > 170 mmHg). Patients with >= 1 episode: hypotension C 5, S 4, V 2; hypertension C 6, S 5, V 3. Episodes: hypotension C 10, S 5, V 3; hypertension C 10, S 7, V 3. Combined out-of-bounds **episodes** C 20, S 12, V 6: V fewer than both, P < 0.05 (the test is on episode counts; no P is given for the patient counts).
- **Adjuvant drugs** (Table 5: patients needing each drug at least once; ephedrine, Neosynephrine (phenylephrine), nitroprusside, nitroglycerin, lidocaine, enflurane). Patients needing >= 1 adjuvant: C 7, S 8, V 4 (no P given). "Pharmacologic interventions" C 18, S 15, V 4, V fewer than both, P < 0.05; these totals equal the Table 5 column sums (derived: patient-drug pairs, not individual doses). Only per-drug patient-level significance: nitroglycerin V 0 vs C 4, P < 0.05.
- Two S patients had SBP < 80 mmHg in unstimulated periods (no samples drawn), so the authors judge 7.5 ng/mL with diazepam, N2O and pancuronium not a desirable set point without stimulation (PDF p8, printed p48). Mean SBP fell after pre-laryngoscopy fentanyl most in C (27% vs S 4%, V 18%, P < 0.05). Total fentanyl over the study hour: C 19.6 +/- 0.85, V 22.8 +/- 1.41 ug/kg (mean +/- SEM; S 27.1, significantly more); S had received significantly more than V by chest incision (P < 0.05), not at later events (PDF pp4-5, printed pp44-45).
No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Weight (dosing per kg via Vc in L/kg).

## Caveats
Assay insensitive below 2 ng/mL; short sampling (60 min); n = 10 per group; only fentanyl studied. Authors (PDF p8, printed p48): hemodynamics and interventions are not necessarily a measure of anesthetic depth, and the vasoactive adjuvants given (enflurane, nitroglycerin, ephedrine) may confound the clinical comparison.
