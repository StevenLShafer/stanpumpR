# Lindauer 2019: mechanistic depot-redissolution model of insulin glargine U300 vs U100

## Study
Sanofi modelling and simulation study (both authors Sanofi; Sanofi-funded). No new subjects. Data: mean PK
profiles from euglycemic clamps in type 1 diabetes. Study 1: double-blind randomized crossover, 24 people of
European ancestry, 18-65 y, single SC Gla-300 0.4, 0.6 and 0.9 U/kg (2.4, 3.6, 5.4 nmol/kg) and Gla-100 0.4 U/kg,
36-h clamp (ref 20, Shiramoto 2015). Study 2: single SC Gla-100 0.4 U/kg, 26 people with T1DM, 24-h clamp (data on
file) (PDF p2). Fitting to the Gla-100 mean profile of study 1 using MSE at 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24 and 28 h; validated
against the study 2 Gla-100 mean and the study 1 Gla-300 means (PDF p4). Matlab R2013b, OPTI Toolbox 2.05, NOMAD
3.6.2 (PDF p5). Simulations for an 80 kg person with T1DM. Assay not described.

## Model and parameters
Mechanistic, not compartmental (Equations 1-6, PDF p3, rendered): a single amorphous SC precipitate whose
redissolution rate is proportional to its surface area, SA = fvol x cuberoot(36 pi V^2), with depot volume =
GlaDepot / CFormulation, so a U300 depot (1/3 the volume) has about half the surface area of a U100 depot (PDF p2).
Equation 1: dpw/dt = -kFP pw (pw = fraction of solution vs precipitate at the depot surface). Equation 2: depot loss
rate = (kpre(1-pw) + ksol pw) x (36 pi (fvol GlaDepot/CFormulation)^(2/3)). Hexamer, dimer and monomer reaction-
diffusion equations in the SC volume (Eqs 3-5, with a D^3 term for D to H), monomer absorption kMIPlasma, then a
plasma pool with VPlasma = VD x BM and saturable elimination kClearance x Gla / (1 + alpha x Gla) (Eq 6).
The model does not track conversion to metabolite M1 (PDF p3).

Not given in the paper: the initial pw, the geometry and boundary conditions of the SC diffusion domain, and the
"mean parameter set" of the repeated fits (Supplemental Table 1, not archived). Goodness of fit only as MSE in the
supplement. No variability model (authors: inter-subject variance not accounted for, PDF p6).

Results (PDF p5-6): Gla-300 redissolution about half that of Gla-100 (Fig. 1); simulated steady state after 3 days
(Gla-300) vs 2 days (Gla-100); the model overestimates Gla-300 0.6 U/kg and underestimates Gla-100 0.4 U/kg at the
start and end of 36 h (Fig. 2). To equalize 24-h exposure the simulations dosed Gla-300 at 0.432 U/kg (2.593
nmol/kg), 8% above Gla-100 0.4 U/kg (PDF p5) (derived: modelled Gla-300/Gla-100 24-h exposure at equal dose about
1/1.08 = 0.93). Clinical treat-to-target trials needed about 12% more Gla-300 (meta-analysis, ref 16; PDF p6).
Observed T1/2 cited as 12 h (Gla-100) vs 18 h (Gla-300) (PDF p7). Switching products: about 3 days to regain steady
state with a 7-8% dose change.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None modelled. Plasma insulin about 10 microU/mL (60 pmol/L) is cited as needed to control hepatic glucose
production in T1DM, reached with about 0.4 U/kg (PDF p1-2, refs 25-26). 1 U = 6 nmol (0.4 U/kg = 2.4 nmol/kg).

## Covariates
Body mass on plasma volume only (VPlasma = 0.1421 L/kg x BM). Formulation concentration enters through depot volume.

## Caveats
Industry authors. Fitted to mean profiles of one Gla-100 arm; Gla-300 data used only for validation; several
constants borrowed from soluble-insulin models (refs 2, 4, 5, 43). Depot shape assumed single-bodied; no in-vivo
evidence of the depot properties (authors, PDF p7). Likely underestimates SC loss of Gla-300 at higher doses.
