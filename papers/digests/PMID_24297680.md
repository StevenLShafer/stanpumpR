# Fischer 2014: population PK of oral labetalol across pregnancy and postpartum

## Study
- Prospective, open-label, longitudinal popPK (PDF p2, printed 374). 64 women enrolled, 57 analysed (649 plasma
  concentrations), on oral labetalol for chronic (37) or gestational (20) hypertension, from 12 weeks' gestation to
  12 weeks postpartum; 30 contributed both pregnant and postpartum data, 17 all three periods (PDF p4).
- Table 1 (PDF p5, printed 377), median (range): age 30 (18-41) y; pre-pregnancy TBW 90 (49-161) kg, LBW 50
  (32-68) kg (Janmahasatian), BMI 32 (21-62); gestational age at entry 20 (11-39) wk; 11 (1-33) samples per subject;
  doses 50-2,400 mg/day (25-2,400 postpartum); concentrations 10.0-1,381 ng/mL. 34 Black, 15 White, 5 Hispanic,
  3 Asian. 11 with pre-eclampsia, 13 diabetes. AAG fell in pregnancy (69 and 58 vs 120 mg/dL postpartum); albumin
  3.1, 2.9 vs 3.4 g/dL; CLCR 123, 123 vs 105 mL/min.
- Sparse sampling in D-optimal windows (pre-dose, 0.5-1, 1.5-3, 4-6, 6-10 h); MEMS caps for dose times.
- Assay: HPLC (modified Alton 1984), racemic labetalol, linear 10.4-1,020 ng/mL, plasma. 8% of samples below
  LLOQ, handled by Beal M6. Protein binding by equilibrium dialysis in 9 women.
- NONMEM 7.2, FOCE; exponential IIV, proportional residual; bootstrap (1,000) and VPC.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, first-order absorption and elimination; all clearances/volumes apparent (/F). ka fixed at
1 h^-1 (could not be estimated; <10% change in other parameters for ka 0.4-6 h^-1). Table 2 (PDF p7, printed 379),
read from the rendered page:

- CL/F = CL/F_non-pregnant + CL/F_pregnancy, with CL/F_pregnancy = θCL,GA × (1 + GA/40), or 0 if not pregnant
  (GA in weeks). Both terms are per 50 kg LBW, linear in LBW.
- Vc/F (or Vss/F) = non-pregnant value × (1 + θV,pregnancy), linear in TBW per 70 kg; θV = 0 if not pregnant.

Covariances (%): CL/F-Vc/F 42, CL/F-Vss/F 55, Vc/F-Vss/F 16 (bootstrap intervals cross zero). No IIV on Q/F.
Derived (from Table 2): at 50 kg LBW, CL/F 258 L/h at 12 weeks (1.37x) and 296 L/h at 40 weeks (1.57x) of
188; pregnant Vc/F 374 L and Vss/F 1,313 L per 70 kg TBW. The paper rounds these to 1.4x, 1.6x and 1.9x
(abstract, PDF p1; text PDF p6: pregnancy adds about 70 L/h at 12 weeks and 108 L/h at 40 weeks).
**Comparator**: the 1.4-1.6x is pregnant against the model's non-pregnant typical value, which is fitted largely
from the same women postpartum (postpartum CL/F did not change over 12 weeks; PDF p6); the authors set the 188 L/h
beside 165 L/h in non-pregnant women (Johnson 2000, PDF p8).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamics: no blood-pressure model, no ke0, no target concentration range. Labetalol is not an
opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
- CL/F: linear in LBW (per 50 kg) plus the gestational-age term above. TBW (power 0.4) and BSA (power 0.6) were
  significant alone but added nothing after LBW (PDF p5).
- Vc/F, Vss/F: linear in TBW (per 70 kg); pregnancy status x 1.9, a single shared coefficient.
- Not retained: AAG, ethnicity, CLCR, albumin, age, site, concomitant drugs.
- Protein binding unchanged: 62% (SD 16) second trimester, 59% (17) third, 61% (18) postpartum, n = 9 (PDF p4).
- Simulation (Fig. 4): dosing per 50 kg LBW keeps median AUC constant across TBW groups; per 70 kg TBW raises it
  about 37% from <=70 to >130 kg; a flat 300 mg lowers it about 36%.

## Caveats
- Oral only; F not measured, so CL/F rise may be higher intrinsic clearance or lower F (authors favour
  UGT1A1 induction by progesterone and greater first-pass extraction, PDF p9-10).
- Racemate measured; stereoisomers differ in activity (authors' limitation).
- ka fixed; IIV of CL/F nearly sixfold; protein binding in only 9 women; obese population (median BMI 36 in
  pregnancy).
