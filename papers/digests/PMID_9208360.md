# Saseen 1997: late concentration peaks with brand-name vs generic oral verapamil in the elderly

## Study
- Randomized, double-blind, crossover trial (PDF p2-3, printed 527-528). 16 nonsmoking patients over 59 years old
  with essential hypertension were enrolled. 8 completed (4 men, 4 women; median age 69.5 y, range 60-79). Weight
  is not reported.
- Drug: immediate-release oral verapamil, brand-name (Calan, Searle) or generic (Mutual). 80 mg twice daily for
  3 days, then 120 mg twice daily for 14 days; 7-15 day washout between arms. On the last day the 8 AM 120-mg dose
  was given in the clinical research center with standard meals.
- Sampling: serum at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 20, 22 and 24 h after the dose (the 8 PM dose was
  not given). HR and P-R interval (mean of 10 cycles on the surface ECG, plus Holter) and BP (supine and standing).
- Assay: stereospecific HPLC (Shibukawa and Wainer) for R-/S-verapamil and R-/S-norverapamil, serum. Lower limit
  5 ng/mL; 21 of 944 concentrations were below it and set to 2.5 ng/mL. Control reproducibility within ±15%.
- Analysis: noncompartmental only. AUC0-12 by linear trapezoid; apparent oral clearance Clpo = dose/AUC0-12, with
  dose taken as 60 mg for each enantiomer and 120 mg for total. One patient lacked the 0.5, 1 and 2 h samples, so
  the PK and bioequivalence analyses use n = 7. Bioequivalence by two one-sided tests, 90% CI for log-transformed
  ratios, range 0.8-1.25. The authors say compartmental modelling failed because of the late peaks (PDF p7).

## Model and parameters

median (range), n = 7, steady state, oral 120 mg IR) were confirmed on the rendered page:

Table III (PDF p5), generic/brand ratios, mean (90% CI): AUC0-12 R 1.09 (0.78-1.52), S 1.16 (0.87-1.55),
total 1.11 (0.81-1.52); Cmax R 0.91 (0.54-1.55), S 0.90 (0.66-1.19), total 0.90 (0.57-1.43). Generic failed the
FDA criteria.

Late peaks (PDF p4-6, Table IV): 70 peaks after 6 h (33 R, 37 S; 31 brand, 39 generic), in all patients, clustered
at about 8-14 h (evening), 16-20 h (nocturnal) and 22-24 h (morning). Median increase over the previous sample was
10.1 ng/mL (range 0.2-53.1), 48.3% with brand and 8.4 ng/mL (0.4-48.1), 36.3% with generic. 50 of 70 (71%) were
followed within 4 h by a norverapamil peak. The authors suggest enterohepatic recirculation.

Literature values quoted in the discussion (PDF p7-8, not this study's data): Abernethy (ref 13) elderly Clpo
42.3 L/h (R) and 161 L/h (S); Schwartz (ref 14) IV clearance in elderly 45.8 L/h (R) and 91.5 L/h (S); S-verapamil
half-life 10.8 h (Abernethy).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Table I (PDF p3, printed 528; median (range), n = 8, maximum % change from baseline): baseline supine BP
  171/91 mmHg, standing 174/101, HR 67.5 beats/min, P-R 0.153 s. Supine systolic fall 22.3% brand vs 23.85% generic;
  diastolic 23.5% vs 28.6%; HR fall 18.7% vs 12.2%; P-R maximum increase 18.8% vs 12.1%.
- Largest BP difference at 0.5 h (supine 144.5/80.5 brand vs 137/74 mmHg generic; P = 0.052/0.091).
- Figure 4: P-R prolongation rises with S-verapamil serum concentration (graph only, no fitted EC50).
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis analysis; no EC50 or therapeutic concentration. The authors judge
  concentrations after 12 h subtherapeutic by BP response. S-isomer stated as 10-20 times more potent than R for
  negative dromotropic effects, and norverapamil as keeping 20% of verapamil's vasodilator potency (introduction,
  from other sources). Not an opioid paper; no MEAC applies.

## Covariates
None modelled. All elderly; sex split 4/4; no weight.

## Caveats
n = 8 (n = 7 for PK), elderly hypertensives only, oral IR at steady state, noncompartmental, serum. Clpo is CL/F
with enantiomer dose assumed to be half the racemate. Late peaks are within assay CV in many cases (authors' own
caveat, PDF p8). Wide ranges; small-sample tests.
