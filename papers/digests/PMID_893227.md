# Jones 1977: BL-S786 (ceforanide) vs six cephalosporins in vitro, with MIC/MBC comparisons

## Study
- In vitro microbiology, four collaborating US laboratories (two did the testing: CDC and Sacramento Medical
  Center). 407 clinical isolates (Materials and Methods, PDF p2, printed 584).
- Drugs: BL-S786 and cefamandole, cefazolin, cefoxitin, cephaloridine, cephalothin, cephradine. **No cefalexin.**
- MIC: broth microdilution, Mueller-Hinton, frozen trays, final inoculum 1 x 10^5 (CDC) or 5 x 10^5 CFU/mL
  (SMC), read at about 18 h, 35 C. Neisseria by agar dilution.
- MBC: 76 organisms from five genera; 5 uL subcultured from each well, read after 48 h; endpoint the lowest
  concentration giving no more than 0.1% survivors (99.9% kill) (PDF p3, printed 585).
- Inoculum effect: 103 rapidly growing facultative anaerobes at 10^3, 10^5 and 10^7 CFU/mL.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetics. The numbers stanpumpR uses are the MBC/MIC ratios.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points are in vitro: MIC (no visible growth, about 18 h) and MBC (99.9% kill, 48 h subculture). No
concentration-time or clinical end point, no PK/PD index.

## Covariates
Inoculum size (Table 7): MICs mostly stable from 10^3 to 10^5 CFU/mL and rose 2- to more than 64-fold at
10^7 for Gram-negatives; S. aureus MICs rose 2- to 4-fold (cefoxitin unchanged).

## Caveats
- 14 strains per species for MBCs; 1977 isolates; 48 h MBC subculture, tested at 10^5-range inocula.
- S. aureus methicillin status is not given for the Table 5 MBC strains (Table 3 MICs separate MSSA and MRSA).
- Cefalexin was not tested.
- The statement that MBCs were within one dilution "for most species" is a class summary; per drug, cefazolin
  was the outlier.

## Verification against stanpumpR
Checked against the checkout at master 7be358cf.

- **`:101-103` (MSSA, MBC50 0.5 = MIC50, MBC90 1 = MIC90)**: the quoted phrase "the same or within one
  dilution" is the abstract's, and is a fair class summary — **matches as a quotation**. For cefazolin
  against S. aureus specifically, 10 of 14 had ratio 1, 2 had 2 and 2 had 4 or more (Table 5), so MBC = MIC is
  right for the median; at the upper tail 2 of 14 (14%) were at least 4-fold, so the paper does not show that
  MBC90 = MIC90 x 1. Methicillin status of those strains is not stated. **Partly supported.**
- **`:124-125` (Enterobacterales, Basis "MIC x 1")**: E. coli 14/14 at ratio 1 supports it; P. mirabilis
  12/14 within one dilution; K. pneumoniae only 10/14, with 4/14 (29%) 4-fold or more. The paper itself names
  cefazolin as the cephalosporin that most often had elevated MBCs (PDF p8). Citing Jones for "within one
  dilution" for cefazolin against Klebsiella is **a discrepancy** (decided 2026-11-01: see the re-check below). The 1977 MICs (Table 7:
  P. mirabilis median 4 mg/L at 10^5) are not what the row uses; it takes MIC from Wang 2016 and CLSI, so no
  comparison is made.
- **`:153-155` (cefalexin)**: Jones contains **no cefalexin**; the row applies the class statement by analogy.
  The nearest drug in the study, cephradine, had all 14 S. aureus within one dilution, which agrees with the
  row's MBC = MIC, but that is an extrapolation the citation does not make visible (citation corrected 2026-11-01: see the re-check below).
- Units: MIC and MBC in ug/mL = mg/L, as the repo uses; no conversion involved.

Verdict: **discrepancy** — the class quotation is accurate, but applied to cefazolin (the paper's own
high-MBC outlier, 29% of Klebsiella 4-fold or more) and to cefalexin (not tested) it says more than the
paper does.
