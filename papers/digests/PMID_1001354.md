# Talseth 1976: oral bioavailability of hydralazine (IV vs oral crossover)

## Study
- Crossover (randomised order, at least 2 weeks apart), 4 healthy volunteers of known acetylator phenotype (sulphadimidine test). Table 1 (PDF p2, printed p396): J.P. M 36 y 72 kg 22 mg slow; K.L. M 44 y 85 kg 25.5 mg slow; L.U. F 28 y 62 kg 19 mg fast; T.T. M 31 y 75 kg 23 mg fast.
- Dose 0.3 mg/kg hydralazine (HP) orally (pure substance in 100 mL water, fasting) and IV (rapid injection within 15 s; commercial 20 mg/mL preparation with propylene glycol and parabens). Salt/base not stated.
- Serum sampled every 5 min to 30 min, then 45, 60, 90, 120 min, then hourly to 8 h. Urine to 24 h.
- Assay: serum and urine HP as the tetrazolo-5,1,a-phthalazine derivative (ref 12, Jack 1975, GLC). New GLC assay for urinary MTP (acetylated metabolite): recovery 45%, LLOQ 20 ng/mL, linear 20-500 ng/mL (PDF p4).
- Analysis: graphical curve-peeling (residuals), two-compartment model with central elimination; L.U. fitted one-compartment. AUC by trapezoids plus C_last/b. Bioavailability = AUC(or)·b(or) / AUC(iv)·b(iv).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. Heart rate rose within 5 min of IV injection, maximal at 10-20 min, 29-36% above rest, lasting 45-60 min, back near rest by ~2 h (PDF p6). Only fluctuations in blood pressure (no systematic fall reported). Diuresis after IV. Side effects: headache, palpitations, nasal congestion, flushing, one vomiting. No ke0, EC50 or concentration-effect model.

## Covariates
Acetylator phenotype: higher oral F in slow acetylators (n = 2 per group). No covariate model.

## Caveats
n = 4; one female fitted differently (one compartment); graphical fitting; nonspecific-era derivatisation assay (later work showed hydrazone metabolites inflate 'apparent' hydralazine); oral and IV given on different breakfast conditions; dose basis (salt/base) not stated; printed distribution half-life range inconsistent with Table 2.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Location line**: the citation is at line 95 (row `hydralazine__review__ludden1982`), not 93. Line drift only.
- **Claim that Talseth is an IV study reporting Varea, CL, t½**: Talseth reports more than that (per-subject A, B, a, b, k10, k12, k21, Table 2), but the registry sentence describes Ludden's Table I, not this paper, so not a discrepancy. **Matches** as far as this paper can say.
- **"Nonselective assay that understates the volume"**: Talseth's Vd,β 1.39-2.24 L/kg and Vc 0.53-0.90 L/kg are well below the library model's (Reece 1980, `R/drugs_hydralazine.R:52`) V1 2.77 and Vss 5.9 L/kg, so the direction holds. The paper itself does not judge its assay's selectivity: **cannot verify** from this paper (open question).
- **Independent comparison with the library model** (Reece 1980, specific HPLC assay, `R/drugs_hydralazine.R:52`: V1 2.768 L/kg, V2 3.132, CL 8.315 L/h/kg, Q 9.433): Talseth's derived apparent clearance 0.37-0.90 L/h/kg is about 10-20x lower and its terminal half-lives (0.95-3.6 h) much longer than Reece's. This is the direction expected if the derivatisation assay also measured hydrazone metabolites (more slowly cleared 'apparent' hydralazine), which is what the registry row and the model header attribute to the pre-1980 assays. Talseth gives no reason to change the model. Talseth's oral F (0.26-0.55) is not used (the library models IV only).
- Verdict: **partly-verified** (registry claims consistent; the assay-selectivity attribution rests on Ludden 1982, not on this paper).
