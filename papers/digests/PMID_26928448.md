# Höglund 2016: chloroquine and desethylchloroquine population PK, P. vivax, Thai-Myanmar border

## Study
- 75 patients with P. vivax mono-infection (8 Thai, 67 Burmese; 36 M, 39 F; age 17-52 y), Mae Tao clinic, 2010-2011. Median (95% CI) admission parasitaemia 4,898 (1,206-29,480)/µL. 100% cure at 42 days. (PDF p.1-2)
- Dosing: chloroquine 25 mg/kg over 3 days: 10 mg/kg at 0 h and 5 mg/kg at 6-12 h on day 0, then 5 mg/kg on day 1 and on day 2. Dose basis: "base" in the Methods, "phosphate" in the abstract (see open questions). Oral tablets, 250 mg chloroquine phosphate; given with 250 mL water, supervised. Primaquine daily for 14 days from day 1.
- Sampling: pre-dose and 1, 6, 12, 24, 25, 36, 48, 49 h after the first dose; days 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42. Whole blood chloroquine and desethylchloroquine (DCQ) by HPLC-UV; LLOQ 2 ng/mL both; accuracy 0.25-5.7% RE, precision <5% CV. Under 5% below LLOQ, excluded.
- NONMEM 7.12, FOCE, log-transformed data; covariates BW, age, sex, parasite clearance time, fever clearance time (forward p=0.05, backward p=0.001); bootstrap 1000 (only 603 successful); VPC 1000 simulations. Median PCT 30 h (18-36), FCT 24 h (12-42).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment for chloroquine (CQ) with one transit compartment absorption; first-order transformation of CQ into DCQ; DCQ with central and peripheral compartments; first-order elimination of both. The metabolite transformation clearance (CLm) was fixed at 18% of the CQ transformation clearance (from Karunajeewa 2010, ref. 26), so the DCQ parameters rest on that assumption. Relative bioavailability F retained (typical 100%), and every clearance and volume is a ratio to F. No covariates retained (fever clearance time on VP CQ/F passed forward selection but not backward). Table 1 (PDF p.5), read on the page image. Units as printed: L, L/h, h, days; absolute, not per kg. Weight was tested by allometric scaling (exponent 0.75 on CL, 1 on V) but dropped.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PK-PD model, no ke0 or tPeak. The authors found no treatment failures, so no failure concentration could be derived. Not an opioid.
- Concentrations quoted from the literature in the Discussion (PDF p.7, refs 45-48, not measured here): minimum effective concentration of chloroquine 15-30 ng/mL in plasma or serum, or 90 ng/mL in whole blood; parasitaemia with whole-blood CQ + DCQ above 100 ng/mL taken as chloroquine resistance. Bolivian cases (ref. 49): CQ 197-535 and DCQ 75-223 ng/mL in whole blood at day 7 or at recrudescence.

## Covariates
None retained. Tested: body weight (allometric), age, sex, parasite clearance time, fever clearance time.

## Caveats
- DCQ formation is a fixed fraction (CLm = 18%), probably biased low, and bidesethylchloroquine is ignored; the authors flag DCQ clearance, volume and half-life as lower than published values.
- The authors say the apparent CQ clearance is lower than prior studies, probably from different matrices (whole blood here).
- Whole blood, not plasma, with total (bound plus free) drug.
- Adults on a 3-day regimen, sampled to 42 days; the 1-h, 6-h samples inform absorption in only a few time points.
- Table 1 is internally inconsistent (see open_questions); treat the DCQ peripheral parameters as unreliable until checked.
- The bootstrap succeeded in only 603 of 1000 runs; some typical values (MTT, Vc CQ/F) lie outside the printed bootstrap CIs.
