# Svendsen 2017: eslicarbazepine TDM variability, efficacy and tolerability in Norway

## Study
- Retrospective, observational, nationwide study of Norwegian TDM data from four laboratories, 2012–2014.
- Population data came from the Norwegian Prescription Database (NorPD).
- PK set: 168 patients (mean age 41.3 years, range 4–82; 95 women and 73 men). They took 0–3 other AEDs and made up
  71% of Norway's eslicarbazepine acetate (ESL) users (PDF p2–3).
- Clinical subset: 104 adults with drug-resistant focal epilepsy and full medical records, followed for up to 3 years.
  Of these, 69 were women; mean age 41 (14–82); 10 on monotherapy and 94 on polytherapy (Table 1, PDF p3, printed
  127).
- Sampling: the latest assumed steady-state trough, drug-fasting, in the morning before the dose.
- Assay: HPLC-UV or UPLC-MS/MS, **non-stereoselective**. The measured analyte is R- plus S-licarbazepine, reported
  in µmol/L, serum, total.
- Efficacy was rated from the records on a modified Likert scale (none, some, >50% reduction, seizure-free ≥1 year).
- Statistics: SPSS 22.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: seizure control from the clinical records (Likert categories) and adverse effects. No exposure–response
  model.
- No significant difference in serum concentration between responders and non-responders, or in those with CNS
  adverse effects (p = 0.386; PDF p4).
- The authors conclude (Sec. 4.2, PDF p5) that the 13–99 µmol/L spread in responders, in the lower part of the
  12–140 range, supports considering a lower reference range for ESL than for OXC.
- They give no numerical range of their own and no 80% S-enantiomer argument.
- Not an opioid, so there is no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
- Age: no significant effect.
- Co-medication was discussed: enzyme inducers were not thought to be important, and ESL inhibits clobazam
  metabolism.
- Dose against concentration: significant linear correlation.
- No equations.

## Caveats
- Retrospective, polytherapy in 90%, refractory patients.
- Some samples may have been taken during titration.
- Non-stereoselective assay.
- The efficacy groups are small, and concentration did not discriminate response.
- The "responder range" is the observed spread, not a validated therapeutic range.
- Small internal inconsistencies: retention 63% against 64% at 3 years; range 13–132 (n = 168) against 13–103
  (n = 104); hyponatraemia cut-off <136 in Table 2 against <137 in the text.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Band in use** (`R/drugs_eslicarbazepine.R:157`, `drugDefaults_global.csv:119`: 3, 35, 15 mg/L): this does not
  come from Svendsen. It is consistent with the oxcarbazepine MHD range 12–140 µmol/L that Svendsen quotes from
  Patsalos 2008 (3.05–35.6 mg/L, derived). **matches**, as context only.
- **"3–26 mg/L (12–100 µmol/L)" attributed to Svendsen**: **discrepancy**.
  - Svendsen gives neither 3–26 mg/L nor 12–100 µmol/L. It reports the 13–99 µmol/L spread in responders (PDF p5)
    and suggests ESL's range be lower than OXC's.
  - Its reason is that OXC's MHD contains both R- and S-licarbazepine. That is related to, but not stated as, the
    repo's "eslicarbazepine is about 80% of racemic MHD"; the 80% figure appears nowhere in this paper.
  - The numbers probably come from Reimers 2018, which the repo cites jointly; see open_questions.
  - The help text's "Svendsen ..., adopted by Reimers" overstates what this paper says.
- **Unit conversion**: 12–100 µmol/L × 0.2543 = 3.05–25.4 mg/L, which rounds to 3–25, not 3–26 (derived). The
  doubt is flagged rather than called an error, since the source of 26 is not this paper.
- **Assay**: Svendsen's concentrations are total R+S licarbazepine (non-stereoselective). The model plots
  eslicarbazepine (S) only. In ESL-treated patients R is a minor fraction, so the difference is small but in the
  direction of the measured values being slightly higher than S alone.
- **Steady-state trough**: Svendsen's median 53 µmol/L (13.5 mg/L, derived) at a median 800 mg/day is higher than
  the model's stated trough of about 7.4 mg/L at 800 mg (header line 37, from Sunkaraneni 2018). A comparison:
  - The Norwegian patients were mostly on polytherapy (90%), and their dosing frequency is not given.
  - Responders' C/D 0.061 µmol/L/mg × 800 mg = 49 µmol/L ≈ 12.4 mg/L (derived), about 1.7 × the model's
    trough.
  - Inducer co-medication would lower, not raise, the concentrations. Drug-fasting morning sampling in Norway
    might not be exactly 24 h post-dose.
  - Not a transcription issue. Recorded as an independent check, which suggests the model may under-predict typical
    clinical troughs.
- Verdict: **discrepancy**. The attribution of a specific numeric range to this paper is not supported by it.
