# Riva 1996: interactions between antiepileptic drugs (review)

## Study
Narrative review (Bologna) of antiepileptic-antiepileptic pharmacokinetic interactions, drug by drug
(section 2) then pair by pair (section 3, sixteen subsections). No primary data: each statement is a
summary of cited studies, usually phrased as a dosage consequence rather than a parameter.

PDF page N = printed page 469 + N (page headers 472, 474, 476, 478, 480, 482 at PDF 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13).

## Model and parameters
No model, no fitted parameter. The quantitative statements, by section:

**3.1 Phenytoin-phenobarbital** (printed p 476-477): reciprocal, both induction and competitive inhibition;
outcome ranges from no change to significant, unpredictable individually. Phenytoin more likely to be
affected than phenobarbital.

**3.2 Phenytoin-carbamazepine** (printed p 477): phenytoin alters carbamazepine, "**doubling its
clearance**" and reducing plasma concentrations; the epoxide:parent ratio rises from 10-20% to about 40%.
"After the introduction of phenytoin, the carbamazepine dosage must be almost doubled." The converse
(carbamazepine on phenytoin) is more variable - both an increase and a decrease in phenytoin clearance are
reported; in most patients inhibition prevails.

**3.3 Phenytoin-valproate** (printed p 477-478): phenytoin "significantly (**up to 2-fold**) enhances the
elimination rate of valproic acid"; valproate dosages "should be doubled". For the reverse direction,
valproate displaces phenytoin from protein (**unbound fraction up 2-fold**) and inhibits its metabolism, so
total phenytoin may be reduced, unaltered or increased while unbound concentrations can remain higher -
with daily swings in unbound phenytoin tracking the valproate concentration.

**3.4 Phenobarbital-valproate** (printed p 479): phenobarbital "significantly accelerates valproic acid
elimination, **halving its plasma concentrations** in long term therapy"; valproate dosages "should be
approximately doubled". Conversely valproate inhibits phenobarbital p-hydroxylation and N-glucosidation and
reduces its clearance: on starting valproate, "a concomitant phenobarbital dosage reduction of **at least
20 to 30%** should be made".

**3.5 Phenobarbital-carbamazepine** (printed p 479): phenobarbital significantly increases the clearance of
carbamazepine and of the epoxide; in practice "steady-state plasma carbamazepine concentrations **may be
halved**" with epoxide concentrations unchanged. Carbamazepine on phenobarbital: only a slight decrease in
clearance or none; no dose change needed.

**3.6 Carbamazepine-valproate** (printed p 480): carbamazepine induces valproate catabolism, "**doubling its
clearance and halving its plasma concentrations**". Valproate on carbamazepine: in most patients it does not
alter, or only slightly decreases, plasma carbamazepine, but markedly raises the 10,11-epoxide by
inhibiting epoxide hydrolase.

**3.8 Ethosuximide** (printed p 481): inducer antiepileptics increase ethosuximide clearance; all the
studies that examined it confirm "a significantly accelerated clearance in the presence of carbamazepine,
phenobarbital, primidone or phenytoin". "**Daily doses of ethosuximide should be at least 20 to 25% higher**
on these polytherapies with respect to monotherapy to yield the same plasma concentrations." On valproate
as perpetrator the results are **equivocal** - an increase, a decrease or no modification of ethosuximide
clearance have all been reported - and "the magnitude of the effect, if any, appears to be clinically
negligible, since the ethosuximide dosage does not need to be adjusted". Ethosuximide itself does not
appear to alter other antiepileptics significantly (sporadic reports of raised phenytoin and
primidone-derived phenobarbital).

**3.10 Lamotrigine** (printed p 481): relative to monotherapy, lamotrigine clearance is "**doubled** in
patients receiving phenobarbital, phenytoin, primidone or carbamazepine, and **more than halved** in
patients on valproic acid"; with inducers *and* valproate together, clearance is similar to monotherapy.
Plasma half-life about **30 h** on monotherapy, **15 h** with phenobarbital/phenytoin/carbamazepine/
primidone, and about **60 h** with valproate. The lamotrigine dosage should be doubled on inducers and at
least halved on valproate. Lamotrigine has no clear-cut effect on other antiepileptics; the epoxide rise is
unresolved.

Carbamazepine autoinduction is stated in section 2 (printed p 473): carbamazepine "significantly induces
its own metabolism, **more than doubling its clearance** during the initial weeks of treatment".

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Clinical consequences are expressed as dose adjustments and monitoring advice; no
concentration-effect model, no seizure end point.

## Covariates
None fitted. The review repeatedly notes that the magnitude depends on the perpetrator's plasma
concentration (explicitly for lamotrigine, section 3.10: "the exact magnitude ... depends considerably on
plasma concentrations of the interacting drug"), which is the premise of the stanpumpR model's
concentration-dependent `fold(C)`.

## Caveats
- **Secondary source, mostly verbal.** Figures are "doubled", "halved", "at least 20 to 25% higher" - round
  clinical statements, not estimates with uncertainty. No n, no variance, no dose range for any of them.
- A **dose** statement is not a clearance statement. "Doses should be 20-25% higher" maps to CL x1.20-1.25
  only under linear kinetics; ethosuximide kinetics are reported as nonlinear in some patients
  (`PMID_6804151`), so even the direction of the mapping error is unclear.
- The lamotrigine half-lives are cross-study summaries spanning different populations and assays; under the
  repository's own rule (AGENTS.md, "Comparing sources"), a terminal half-life ratio is weak evidence about
  clearance.
- Several of Riva's round factors are **larger than the population-model factors the library applies**
  (see the Verification section) - a systematic feature of 1990s verbal reviews, not necessarily a sign that
  either is wrong.
- 1996 vintage: lacosamide, eslicarbazepine, cenobamate and all modern population models postdate it.
