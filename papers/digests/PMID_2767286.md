# Takeda 1989: oxytocin metabolic clearance in pregnant, puerperal and nonpregnant women

## Study
- Cross-sectional, constant-infusion clearance study, Dept of Obstetrics & Gynecology, Tokyo University (PDF p2, printed 183).
- n = 14 women: 5 nonpregnant (age 34-45 y, admitted for surgery for uterine myoma or ovarian cyst), 5 pregnant
  at 38-41 weeks (age 28-35 y; samples taken while oxytocin was infused for a contraction stress test), 4 puerperal
  (postpartum day 3-5, age 29-36 y). Weights per subject in Table 1 (PDF p4): group means 61.9 ± 2.7, 57.0 ± 1.3
  and 62.1 ± 4.6 kg (mean ± SE). No men.
- Drug: synthetic oxytocin, 6 mU/mL solution, by syringe pump into an antecubital vein, 3 mU/min or 6 mU/min for
  1-2 h (supine at rest). Some subjects received 3 mU/min for 1 h then 6 mU/min for 1 h (Fig. 2).
- Sampling from the opposite antecubital vein (heparin-coated cannula): 15 min before, just before, then 10, 20,
  30, 40, 50, 60, 80, 100, 120 min (or 20, 40 ... 120 min). Plasma kept on ice, centrifuged, stored at -20 °C,
  assayed usually within 1 month.
- Assay: Florisil extraction of 1-2 mL plasma (recovery of added 125I-oxytocin 71.3-84.5%), then RIA with
  125I-oxytocin and a rabbit anti-oxytocin antibody. Sensitivity 0.5 μU/tube; intra-assay CV 5.8-12.3% (mean 7.1%),
  inter-assay CV 5.6-14.8% (mean 8.4%); cross-reactivity with lysine-vasopressin, arginine-vasopressin and
  vasotocin below 0.07%, 0.04% and 0.08% (PDF p2).
- Serum cystine aminopeptidase (CAP, "oxytocinase") by a commercial colour test before infusion.
- In vitro: plasma from one pregnant woman (37 weeks, 25 y) and one nonpregnant woman (20 y) incubated with
  tyrosyl-3H-oxytocin at 37 °C for 1-2 h, separated on Sephadex G-25 (PDF p3, printed 184).
- Statistics: mean ± SE, Student's t-test.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. MCR (mL/kg/min) = infusion rate (mL/min) × infusate concentration (μU/mL) /
[steady-state plasma oxytocin (μU/mL) − basal plasma oxytocin (μU/mL)] / body weight (kg) (formula, PDF p2).
Steady state = mean of ≥ 3 concentrations from 30 min on; basal = mean of the 2 pre-infusion samples.

Plasma oxytocin at 3 mU/min (8 subjects with 10-min sampling, mean ± SE, μU/mL; PDF p3): pre 12.3 ± 0.7,
10 min 11.9 ± 0.8, 20 min 12.9 ± 0.8, 30 min 15.0 ± 0.7, 40 min 14.8 ± 1.0, 50 min 14.0 ± 0.8, 60 min 14.4 ± 0.6;
roughly constant from 30 min (Fig. 1).

Table 1 (PDF p4, printed 185), 3 mU/min, mean ± SE:

| Group | n | CAP (mU/mL) | Oxytocin clearance (mL/min) | Weight (kg) | MCR (mL/kg/min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pregnant | 5 | 143.5 ± 7.8 | 1261.6 ± 119.1 | 61.9 ± 2.7 | 20.9 ± 2.6 |
| Puerperal | 4 | 60.8 ± 3.0 | 949.3 ± 112.0 | 57.0 ± 1.3 | 16.9 ± 2.4 |
| Nonpregnant | 5 | 13.8 ± 2.7 | 1129.9 ± 127.9 | 62.1 ± 4.6 | 19.7 ± 3.3 |

Individual MCRs range 11.9-29.2 (pregnant), 12.3-24.6 (puerperal), 11.7-33.0 (nonpregnant) mL/kg/min (Table 1).
At 6 mU/min (n = 4 per group, PDF p3): pregnant 22.1 ± 3.3, nonpregnant 22.7 ± 3.1 mL/kg/min. No group difference
in MCR, absolute clearance or weight; CAP higher in pregnant women (P < 0.005, Fig. 3).

No half-life, volume or rate constants reported.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD end point measured (no uterine activity data). In vitro, about 85% of 3H-oxytocin was degraded in pregnant
plasma in 1 h, versus under 10% in nonpregnant plasma in 2 h (Figs. 4-5, PDF p5-6). Authors conclude plasma CAP
does not contribute measurably to in vivo clearance. Clinical inference (PDF p7, printed 188): plasma oxytocin needs
about 30 min to reach steady state, so infusion increments should be at least 30 min apart to avoid
hyperstimulation. Not an opioid; no MEAC/ke0/potency data.

## Covariates
Pregnancy state (none significant for MCR). Weight used only to normalise MCR.

## Caveats
- Very small groups (4-5); 6 mU/min data only n = 4 per group.
- Basal oxytocin (about 12 μU/mL) is high relative to the infusion increment (about 2-3 μU/mL), so MCR depends
  strongly on the basal estimate (derived: 3000 μU/min / (15.0 − 12.3) μU/mL ≈ 1100 mL/min); the authors attribute
  their higher basal and steady-state levels to antibody/standard differences (PDF p6).
- Wide individual scatter (11.7-33.0 mL/kg/min). Women only. Japanese-language full text.
- Authors cite Leake 1980 (23.1 ± 2.6 mL/kg/min, pregnant) and Seitchik (17.3 ± 2.4, range 11.2-32.5) as similar.
