# Lehmann 1991: sufentanil PCA and minimum effective concentrations

## Study
- 40 ASA I-III women recovering from major gynecological surgery (age 42 ± 15 y, weight 62 ± 9 kg, height 164 ± 7 cm; PDF p. 1). Balanced anaesthesia with fentanyl, droperidol, thiopental, N2O; surgery 191 ± 76 min, fentanyl 0.6 ± 0.3 mg intraoperatively. PCA began 117 ± 97 min after extubation (median 76 min).
- Loading dose titrated IV in 5-10 µg steps to comfort: 19.1 ± 35.7 µg (mean ± SD, abstract). Then ODAC PCA: demand 6 µg, lockout 1 min, background 1.15 µg/h, maximum 40 µg/h. PCA lasted 17.3 ± 2.1 h with 16 ± 11 demands per patient.
- Consumption including loading dose 131.1 ± 69.4 µg, or 7.5 ± 3.7 µg/h (abstract); 0.12 ± 0.07 µg/kg/h (Table 1 text layer). Without loading dose 6.34 ± 3.58 µg/h.
- Venous blood (5 mL, contralateral forearm) drawn just before each demand, at most 15 samples per patient; serum frozen at -20 °C. Assay: radioimmunoassay (Michiels 1983), detection limit 0.01 ng/mL, in duplicate. Values below the limit were set to 0.005 ng/mL; 119 of 395 analysed samples were below it (p. 4).
- Retrospective pain score (0-5 VRS) 1.85 ± 1.00; VAS 33.1 ± 11.6 (Table 1, text layer). Side effects mostly minor; no clinically relevant respiratory depression.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEC / MEAC.** Defined as the serum concentration measured immediately before a PCA demand, i.e. when the patient became dissatisfied with analgesia (p. 4 discussion). Population: 40 women, early postoperative period after major gynecological surgery with fentanyl-containing balanced anaesthesia. Matrix: **venous serum** (not plasma, not whole blood), total drug by RIA, detection limit 0.01 ng/mL.
  - Pooled values (395) log-normal: median 0.024 ng/mL, range <0.01 to 0.56 (abstract; PDF p. 3).
  - Mean of individual means 0.035 ± 0.029 ng/mL (Table 2).
  - Authors' conclusion: more than 0.03 ng/mL seems necessary to enter the analgesic window (abstract).
  - Many values were censored at 0.005 ng/mL (below LOD), which pulls the median and mean down; the authors say excluding them would overestimate MEC.
  - Over the PCA period MEC did not rise significantly (slope not different from 0; first vs last MEC, P ≥ 0.05): no sign of accumulation or acute tolerance.
  - Comparators quoted: 0.6-1.9 ng/mL (Hudson, general anaesthesia), 0.06-0.54 ng/mL (O'Connor & Sear, balanced anaesthesia; extubation at 0.03-0.37), 0.64 ng/mL at extubation (Ourahma), 0.95 ng/mL at onset of respiratory depression in one overdose case (White). These are the authors' citations, not their data.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis:** none reported. Concentrations were measured, not effect-site modelled.
- **Potency ratio (analgesia):** sufentanil about 3.8 times fentanyl when analgesic effect and duration are considered, and about 2.2 times when consumption is multiplied by the retrospective pain score (p. 4; abstract gives 2.2 to 3.8). Basis: Table 3, relative equipotent dose = (µg/kg/h × pain score) relative to morphine = 1: sufentanil 0.005, fentanyl 0.01, buprenorphine 0.02, alfentanil 0.15, l-methadone 0.50, piritramide 0.96, morphine 1.00, nalbuphine 4.75, pentazocine 4.82, pethidine 8.63, tramadol 10.24. Demand dose (µg) and consumption (µg/kg/h) per drug are in Table 3 (sufentanil 6 µg, 0.12; fentanyl 34 µg, 0.46). The authors call these "historical" comparisons from their own PCA studies, not randomized. The introduction quotes earlier literature at 5 to 11 times fentanyl.

## Covariates
None analysed for MEC.

## Caveats
- Venous (not arterial) serum, immediately before demands, so the concentration is not at equilibrium with the effect site and is lower than arterial; the authors do not discuss this.
- 30% of samples (119 of 395) below LOD, assigned 0.005 ng/mL.
- Postoperative, still under residual intraoperative fentanyl effect (authors, p. 3), which would lower MEC.
- Women only, single centre; wide variability (CV 76-85%).
- Potency ratio comes from historical, non-randomized PCA studies and combines consumption with a retrospective score.
