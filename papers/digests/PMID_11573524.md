# Kuizenga 2001: Biphasic EEG changes at loss of consciousness

## Study
Randomised parallel groups, 25 ASA I-II patients (5 per drug) for elective vertebral disc surgery, no premedication, studied before surgery (PDF p2-3). 3-min baseline EEG, 10-min constant-rate infusion, 2 min after. Dosing (per kg of "dose body weight" = height cm - 100): thiopental 1 mg/kg/min, propofol 0.5, etomidate 0.06, midazolam 0.03 mg/kg/min (derived totals over 10 min: 10, 5, 0.6, 0.3 mg/kg); sevoflurane by facemask in oxygen-enriched air, fresh gas 9 L/min, 2% stepped up by 2% each minute to 8% held 7 more min. EEG Cz-Fpz (Fpz2-M2 not presented), amplitude by aperiodic analysis (Lifescan) in two bands, SEF95 and BIS (Aspect A-1000, BIS 3.12, 15 s smoothing plus internal 60 s) off line. Responsiveness: command by headphones every 30 s; first failure = loss of responsiveness (LR). Biphasic = rose at least 2 SD above baseline then fell 20% below the maximum. Kruskal-Wallis. Fig 1 shows expected plasma concentrations from population kinetics (Stanpump) for the four IV drugs and sevoflurane fresh gas concentration; the parameter sets are not given.

## Model and parameters
No PK or PD model fitted; no ke0, EC50 or concentration values reported. Patient data (Table 1, PDF p3, medians with range): thiopental 2M/3F, age 47 (41-50), 75 kg (62-82), 178 cm, LR 6.6 min (5.9-8.6); propofol 5/0, 32 (18-56), 90 kg, 190 cm, 6.1 (5.0-6.5); etomidate 3/2, 43 (39-51), 85 kg, 180 cm, 5.7 (5.2-6.3); midazolam 2/3, 36 (21-47), 75 kg, 176 cm, 7.4 (5.3-10.8); sevoflurane 4/1, 39 (33-50), 92 kg (75-105), 186 cm, 5.6 (4.8-6.5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points are processed EEG (2-5 Hz and 11-20 Hz amplitude, SEF95, BIS) against time, with loss of responsiveness as the clinical event. No ke0 or hysteresis is estimated; concentrations were not measured, so no effect-site concentration appears. Table 2 (PDF p3, printed 356), n biphasic of 5 per drug, median EEG value at LR:
- BIS at LR: thiopental 77 (63-82), propofol 77 (45-87), etomidate 66 (45-79), midazolam 77 (70-84), sevoflurane 87 (82-95), the last different from the others (P<0.05); no biphasic BIS in any group.
- SEF95 at LR (Hz): 25, 22, 16, 24, 24 in the same drug order; biphasic in 5, 4, 5, 2, 5.
- Amplitude 2-5 Hz: biphasic 4, 5, 5, 3, 4; amplitude 11-20 Hz: 5, 5, 5, 2, 5; LR before/after the peak is mixed, with median interval of peak to LR from -5.8 to +2.6 min, so peak EEG did not reliably coincide with LR. Midazolam lacks a consistent biphasic response. Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None analysed (weight used only for the dose; height-100 dose weight).

## Caveats
- n = 5 per group, parallel groups, no concentration measurements; concentrations simulated only.
- Rapid concentration rise during induction, BIS 3.12 averaging about 75 s bias BIS at LR upward; the authors note this, especially for sevoflurane. No data at return of consciousness.
- LR tested every 30 s, so timing resolution is 30 s.
