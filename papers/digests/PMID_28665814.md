# Warnaby 2018: slow wave activity saturation and neural inertia

## Study
Secondary analysis of 393 EEG datasets from four studies; adults 18-90 y. Study 1: 16 healthy volunteers, ultraslow propofol TCI (Ce steps of 0.2 ug/mL to 4 ug/mL over 48 min, held 10 min, then off; 32-channel EEG), response = button press to auditory words. Study 2: 21 patients, sevoflurane gas induction (no emergence). Study 3: 102 ASA 1-2 patients, propofol 1-3 mg/kg + fentanyl 2 ug/kg induction, desflurane maintenance (BIS 30-40 or 45-60) with fentanyl 2 ug/kg/h. Study 4: 254 routine-care patients, propofol/fentanyl induction, desflurane (66) or sevoflurane (188). Ce computed from "standard population PK models" (refs 16-19 not read in this digest). Slow wave activity (0.5-1.5 Hz) power fitted with a Bayesian sigmoid against Ce; saturation (SWAS) = 95% of plateau.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points: EEG slow-wave power (not BIS); behavioural response (button press, Study 1; verbal command, others). No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or MEAC is estimated; Ce comes from published population models. Hysteresis: in Study 1 mean Ce at loss vs recovery of response was 1.5 vs 1.4 ug/mL, no consistent behavioural hysteresis; Ce_SWAS was lower on emergence (about half the subjects) with a significant induction/emergence difference (p<0.001). Fentanyl co-induction lowered Ce_SWAS roughly by half (median propofol Ce_SWAS 0.82 ug/mL rapid infusion vs 2.7 ug/mL ultraslow, p.16). Volatile Ce_SWAS about 0.7 MAC (range 0.24-0.9, sevoflurane). No equianalgesic ratios. Opioid audit: fentanyl is a co-drug only; no opioid concentration, MEAC or ke0 reported.

## Covariates
Age (negative correlation with P_SWAS and emergence Ce_SWAS), volatile type, surgery duration/severity; sex not significant per the text.

## Caveats
Ce are model-derived (population PK, ultraslow vs rapid infusions not equilibrium for rapid cases); different EEG hardware across studies; unblinded retrospective analysis; delirium findings preliminary.
