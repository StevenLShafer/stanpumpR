# Delavenne 2010: population PK of prophylactic fondaparinux after orthopaedic surgery (POP-A-RIX)

Page convention: printed pp. 252-260 = PDF pp. 1-9 (PDF page = printed page − 251).

## Study
Multicentre prospective cohort (POP-A-RIX, NCT01063543), consecutive patients given fondaparinux 2.5 mg SC once
daily for at least 5 days after major orthopaedic surgery; first dose at least 6 h after surgery, second dose
12 h or more after the first (printed p. 253 / PDF p. 2). 999 enrolled; 190 excluded because their laboratory
calibrated the anti-Xa assay with LMWH, leaving 809 (printed p. 254 / PDF p. 3). 1-4 samples per patient at random
times within the dosing interval; 2,029 anti-Xa activities (mean 2.5 per patient), 101 below the limit of
quantification excluded. Assay: chromogenic anti-Xa, converted to mg/L by regression on three fondaparinux levels;
calibration range 0.07-1.5 mg/L, LOQ 0.07 mg/L, CV <10% (printed p. 253 / PDF p. 2).

Table 1 (printed p. 255 / PDF p. 4, read from the image), whole population n = 809: 73% women; weight 69.4 kg
[35-172] (the table gives a central value with range; it does not say mean or median); age 74.1 [19-100] y
(text: mean 74.1 ± 13 y); 16.2% over 85 y; 10.5% under 50 kg; BMI 26.3 [13-54]; serum creatinine 71.5 [36-205]
µmol/L; CrCl MDRD 89.9 [26.9-247.6] mL/min (10.1% under 60); CrCl Cockcroft-Gault 75 [18.6-208.6] mL/min (23.4%
under 50). Surgery: total hip 226, knee 173, hip fracture 360, other 50. Learning set 566, validation set 243.
Source oddity: the "Other" column prints age 63.5 [69-95], a central value below its own range (on the rendered
page as printed; unresolved).

NONMEM VI, FOCE with interaction; covariate entry ΔOFV > 3.84, backward elimination ΔOFV > 6.6. Internal
validation by GOF and VPC (1,000 replicates); external validation on the 243-patient set (mean prediction error
−0.029, precision 0.021, printed p. 256 / PDF p. 5); also compared with the earlier phase II/III model (Turpie
2009, ref 4), which over-predicted high and under-predicted low activities (Figure 2).

covariance block Cl/F-V2/F, combined additive + proportional residual error. Table 2 (printed p. 255 / PDF p. 4,
rendered) and Eqs 4-5:

Covariates reduced IIV in Cl/F from 51.2% to 43% and in V2/F from 39.5% to 32.8% (text, printed p. 255; Table 2
prints 41.8% for Cl/F). OFV −5003 → −5136. Derived at the reference patient (my calculation from Table 2):
half-lives of the two disposition exponentials 15.2 h and 175.8 h; the slow one is 3.2% of a bolus's initial
amplitude and 27% of its AUC; Vss 23.7 L. Single 2.5 mg SC dose: tmax about 4.1 h, peak 0.26 mg/L, fall to half
the peak 17.4 h after it; day 7 of 2.5 mg daily: peak 0.43, trough 0.21 mg/L (derived: numerical simulation).

Reported exposure (Bayesian individual, steady state, printed p. 257 / PDF p. 6): mean Cmax 0.5 [0.19-1.16] mg/L
(10% above 0.7, 2.5% above 0.9); mean Cmin 0.29 [0.08-0.84] mg/L (8.2% above 0.4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None modelled. Concentration is fondaparinux mg/L from a fondaparinux-calibrated anti-Xa assay. No ke0, no target
range, no bleeding model. Simulations (5,000 patients, 7 days) found AUC on day 7 higher by 34% (CrCl MDRD
<60), 41% (<50 kg) and 9% (>85 y); trough higher by 56%, 63% and 16%; Cmax higher by 24%, 29% and 6% (printed
pp. 256-257, Figure 3). 1.5 mg daily in renal impairment or low weight gave troughs comparable to 2.5 mg in normal
patients (Figure 4). The authors say exposure differences need confirmation against bleeding before dose rules.

## Covariates
Weight, simplified-MDRD creatinine clearance and age on Cl/F; weight on V2/F. MDRD was better than Cockcroft-Gault
here; the authors attribute this to the many elderly. The paper reports CrClMDRD in mL/min; it does not say
whether the MDRD estimate was de-indexed from 1.73 m^2, and does not print the MDRD coefficient (it cites Levey
1999, ref 13). Stated clearance effects (printed p. 255 and Discussion p. 257): 25% lower in moderate (30-60) and
43% lower in severe (<30 mL/min) renal impairment; 15% lower under 50 kg; 7% lower over 85 y. Discussion also says
V is 15% lower under 50 kg. Terminal half-life quoted as 17-21 h from ref 3 (Donat 2002), printed p. 252.

## Caveats
Sparse sampling (2.5 samples per patient), elderly mostly female orthopaedic population at one fixed dose. V3/F is
poorly identified (CI includes zero, IIV 239%), so the slow exponential is weakly supported. Mixed assays across
centres, converted to mg/L. 190 patients dropped for LMWH-calibrated assays. Ka and Q have no IIV.
