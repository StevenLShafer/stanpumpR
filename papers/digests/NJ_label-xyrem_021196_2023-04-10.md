# XYREM (sodium oxybate) label, revised 4/2023

Document identity: Highlights and footers give XYREM (sodium oxybate) oral solution, Initial U.S. Approval 2002,
Revised 4/2023, Reference ID 5153310, consistent with the file name (NDA 021196, 2023-04-10). Each page carries the

## Study
Not a study: the US prescribing information for sodium oxybate, the sodium salt of GHB, 0.5 g/mL oral solution,
indicated for cataplexy or excessive daytime sleepiness in narcolepsy from age 7 (section 1, p. 3).

Single doses above 4.5 g have not been studied (p. 16). PK nonlinear, similar on single and repeat dosing (p. 15).
Metabolism to CO2 via GHB dehydrogenase and succinic semialdehyde; no active metabolites (p. 16). No sex difference
(18 + 18 healthy adults, 4.5 g); paediatric 7-17 y (n = 29) similar to adults, body weight the main intrinsic factor;
renal impairment not studied (pp. 16-17). Divalproex raises GHB AUC about 25% (p. 17).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect data in the sections read. Patients often fall asleep within 5-15 min of a dose (2.3, p. 4).
Overdose (10, pp. 14-15) is described clinically, with no concentrations.

## Covariates
Dosing (2.1-2.5, pp. 3-4): adults 4.5 g/night in two doses (2.25 g at bedtime, 2.25 g 2.5-4 h later), titrate by
1.5 g/night weekly to 6-9 g/night; doses above 9 g/night not studied. Children >= 7 y by weight band (Table 2: 20 to
< 30 kg up to 3 g + 3 g; 30 to < 45 kg up to 3.75 g + 3.75 g; >= 45 kg up to 4.5 g + 4.5 g). Hepatic impairment: start at
half the dose. First dose at least 2 h after eating.

## Caveats
- The 3.7-fold figure compares nightly totals of 4.5 and 9 g given as two doses each, and the label says "blood
  levels" without naming AUC or Cmax.
- The label's mass basis: doses are sodium oxybate; 0.413 g of oxybate per 0.5 g of salt is a ratio of 0.826.
