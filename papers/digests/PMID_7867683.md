# Sun 1994: benazepril-amlodipine pharmacokinetic interaction in healthy men

## Study
Three-way Latin-square randomised crossover, single oral doses, 2-week washout (PDF p2, printed 286). 12 healthy
**male** subjects, 19-45 y (mean 32.3), 59.2-79.0 kg (mean 68.7). Treatments: one 10 mg benazepril
hydrochloride tablet; one amlodipine besylate tablet equivalent to 5 mg amlodipine; or both together. Overnight
fast, 200 mL water, no food until 4 h. Blood at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 h and 1-6 days;
heparinised plasma. Assay: benazepril and benazeprilat by GC/MS (ref 16, Kaiser et al. 1987, the Ciba-Geigy
method); amlodipine by GC-ECD, LLOQ 0.2 ng/mL. Non-compartmental: linear-trapezoid AUC(0-T) with T = 4 h
(benazepril), 24 h (benazeprilat), 6 days (amlodipine); Cmax = highest observed concentration, Tmax its time
(i.e. means of individual peaks). ANOVA, 90% CI, signed-rank test for Tmax. Sponsor and authors: Ciba-Geigy.

## Model and parameters
No model fitted. Mean (SD), n = 12 (Tables 1-3, PDF p4, printed 288; checked on the rendered page):

| Analyte | Treatment | AUC (ng·h/mL) | Cmax (ng/mL) | Tmax (h) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| benazepril, AUC(0-4 h) | alone | 140 (58.1) | 149 (73.4) | 0.6 (0.3) |
| benazepril | + amlodipine | 161 (68.0) | 168 (63.3) | 0.5 (0.2) |
| benazeprilat, AUC(0-24 h) | benazepril alone | 1410 (384) | 260 (93.3) in Table 2; 257 (93.3) in abstract and text | 1.5 (0.6) |
| benazeprilat | + amlodipine | 1470 (358) | 292 (101.2) | 1.7 (0.7) |
| amlodipine, AUC(0-144 h) | alone | 114 (54.1) | 2.3 (0.6) | 9.0 (2.6) |
| amlodipine | + benazepril | 118 (52.6) | 2.5 (0.8) | 8.3 (2.2) |

No half-life, clearance or volume is estimated in this study. The introduction (PDF p1) cites from earlier
work (Balfour & Goa 1991): benazepril Tmax 0.5 h, t1/2 1.1 h; benazeprilat Tmax 1-2 h, mean t1/2 21.4 h;
amlodipine Tmax about 8 h, t1/2 34 h. These are not this study's measurements.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured; the authors say the study was not designed for pharmacodynamics (PDF p4). No ke0, no
effective concentration. Not an opioid paper. Safety: 4 subjects had headache after the combination (3 mild, 1
severe, each lasting a day or less).

## Covariates
None examined.

## Caveats
- n = 12 men; power to detect a 20% difference was 50% (benazepril AUC), 18% (benazepril Cmax), 40%
  (benazeprilat Cmax), so equivalence was shown only for benazeprilat AUC(0-24 h) and amlodipine AUC.
- Benazeprilat Cmax for the alone arm is printed two ways (257 vs 260; see open questions).
- Benazepril AUC is truncated at 4 h and benazeprilat at 24 h; neither is AUC(0-inf).
- Same sponsor and same GC/MS method (Kaiser 1987) as the Kaiser 1989 study the library models, so the two
  are independent in subjects but not in assay.
- The received date (6 Dec 1993) is printed after the accepted date (6 Jul 1993) on PDF p1, as printed.

  Cmax 257 (abstract/text; Table 2 says 260) and AUC(0-24 h) 1410 ng·h/mL, both mass units (ng/mL). Same 10 mg
  salt dose as Kaiser. The comparator values 195 and 1050 are Kaiser 1989's (492 pmol/g and 2649 (pmol/g)·h

  also what the model is fitted to, so the baseline is the same one the code applies it to. Ratios (derived):
  257/195 = 1.318, 1410/1050 = 1.343; with 260, 1.333. Both studies define Cmax as the mean of individual
  observed peaks, so the like-for-like comparison holds. **matches** (with the 257/260 misprint noted).
