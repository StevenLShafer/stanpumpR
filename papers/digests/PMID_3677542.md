# Cohen 1987: lamotrigine PK in normal volunteers

## Study
Three open/controlled studies in healthy volunteers, oral lamotrigine base in gelatin capsules (p. 536):
- Study I: open ascending dose, 5 healthy men, age 36 (27-49) y, 84.9 (64.8-103.3) kg; weekly doubling doses 7.5
  to 240 mg (only 30-240 mg analysed by HPLC; RIA unreliable).
- Study II: open single 120 mg, 10 volunteers (7 M, 3 F), age 33 (19-61), 71.3 (52.1-87.1) kg; fasted.
- Study III: double-blind randomized parallel, 10 lamotrigine + 5 placebo for 7 days, age 28 (19-37), 72.6
  (63.6-90.0) kg (Table I); 120 mg twice on day 1 then 60 mg twice daily days 2-7.
- HPLC-UV, detection limit 0.05 ug/mL; plasma, saliva and urine (glucuronide by beta-glucuronidase).
- One-compartment model with first-order absorption and lag, 1/C weighting (p. 537).

Urine (Table IV, study II): 70.5 % of dose recovered over 144 h; 89.4 % of it as glucuronide. Protein binding about
55 % (plasma/saliva ratio, Discussion). Saliva/plasma about 0.46.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured; no CNS or cardiovascular changes vs placebo. Putative therapeutic range 1-3 ug/mL estimated from
animal models (Introduction). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled. Discussion quotes a patient study (ref 12): mean t1/2 15 h (7.8-33.3) with phenytoin or
carbamazepine, 59 h (30.5-88.8) with valproate. No autoinduction over 7 days. Absolute bioavailability not
measured (at least 70 % from urinary recovery).

## Caveats
Small n (5, 10, 10); healthy, mostly young volunteers; capsule formulation; study I sampling not optimal;
absolute F unknown, so V and CL are apparent.
