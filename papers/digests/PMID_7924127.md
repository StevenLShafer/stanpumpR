# Sheiner 1994: model-based analysis of analgesic trials (bromfenac)

## Study
Methods paper with a re-analysis of single-dose oral bromfenac data from dental-surgery pain studies (impacted third molar), supplied by Wyeth-Ayerst (p6, printed 314). n = 376 patients; doses 0, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 mg; pain relief (PRL, ordered 0-4 scale; the text also says "0-5" for the Y value in the hazard section) recorded hourly from hour 1 to hour 8. Eight patients who remedicated at 1 h were dropped, so the earliest remedication time is fixed at 2 h. Remedication (rescue) censors later PRL values non-randomly. Fit by NONMEM Laplacian ML. The method is described in full in an unpublished companion (Sheiner, Beal, Dunne, "submitted 1993").

## Model and parameters
- PRL: logit of the cumulative probability P(Y<=m) = fp(m) - fd(Ce) - ft(t)*etaY, with fp = a0 - a1*m (no time dependence) and ft = t^zeta (Appendix A1.0-A1.4, PDF p13).
- fd is a monotone natural cubic spline of Ce with fd(0)=0 and 2 internal knots (4 spline parameters). Per the authors the spline parameters are uninformative alone; Fig 3 right shows fd(Ce), flat above Ce about 0.9 (Ce in units of steady-state Cp, mg/L).
- Remedication hazard: logit h(t|Y_t) = sum_{j<=m} gamma1j + sum_{t*<s<=t} gamma2s + etaT, depends only on PRL at t and on time (A2).
- Cp is a biexponential model with first-order absorption and lag, with population-average parameters from a separate healthy-male volunteer study of 50 mg bromfenac (J.J. Zimmerman, personal communication, March 1992); no PK was measured in the trial subjects, so the PK parameters are the same for every subject (PDF p6).
- Ce(t) = keo * integral of Cp(l)*exp(-keo(t-l)) dl (A1.3), i.e. keo is a rate constant (per hour).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. End point: PRL (0-4 scale) and time to remedication after oral bromfenac. keo = 0.55 per hour (derived: t1/2 keo = ln2/0.55 = 1.26 h), estimated on PRL; peak drug effect (proportional to Ce, Fig 3 left) at about 2 h after dosing. No MEAC, EC50 or potency ratio. A dose of about 20 mg is said to give a peak Ce near the flat part of fd (p316). Simulated dosing example (p319): the minimum dose giving at least 50% of patients a better than 75% chance of PRL>=2 is 30 mg at 1 h, 15 mg at 2 h, then 15, 20, 30, 40, 60, 100 mg at hours 3-8.

## Covariates
Age, gender and weight were examined against etaT and etaY and showed no pattern; none included (p316).

## Caveats
PK not measured in trial patients (volunteer values used, 50 mg only); conclusions limited to patients willing to wait at least 2 h; the author notes the model may be inflexible at high doses and that the truth about the 50 vs 100 mg difference may lie between this and the earlier analysis (p317-318).
