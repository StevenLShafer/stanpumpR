# Morgan 1978: meperidine (pethidine) disposition in pregnancy

## Study
Seven women in labour (16-30 yr, 57-85 kg pregnant weight; 33-40 wk; two in premature labour on alcohol infusion, five with induced labour), no clinical hepatic/renal dysfunction. 50 mg meperidine HCl IV over 1-7 min (induced-labour patients also got promazine 50 mg in the same injection). Concurrent: epidural bupivacaine in 3, one on phenytoin 300 mg/day (Patient 4), nitrous oxide as needed (Table I, PDF p2). Plasma and blood sampled to 8 h; GC-FID assay with lidocaine internal standard. Per-patient fits to bi- or triexponential by NONLIN, weighting 1/C^2. Blood/plasma ratio measured in 5 patients and in 6 healthy volunteers. Controls are literature groups (Klotz 1974 with individual data from Wilkinson; Mather 1975), Mann-Whitney U, p = 0.01 threshold.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, effect measurement, MEAC or potency ratio. The authors note high early concentrations (1.5-2.0 ug/mL in patients 1, 3, 6) probably mean greater central effect, without measuring it.

## Covariates
Pregnancy status only, versus literature controls. Authors attribute the smaller volumes to labour stress/hemodynamics (surgical patients are similar), not to pregnancy alone. No equations.

## Caveats
n = 7, mixed gestation (33-40 wk) and co-medication (promazine, epidural bupivacaine, nitrous oxide, phenytoin in one); no concurrent non-pregnant control group; controls come from other studies with different age (43 vs 22 yr), assay and blood/plasma ratio. Clearance deficit stated relative to those controls' blood clearance (1,465 mL/min). The high-clearance patient 4 (1470 mL/min blood) was on phenytoin. Authors say effects cannot be attributed to pregnancy alone.
