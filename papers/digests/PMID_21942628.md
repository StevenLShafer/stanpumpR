# Blaschke 2012: adherence to medications and actual dosing histories

## Study
Narrative review (Annual Review of Pharmacology and Toxicology), no new data. Topics: a taxonomy of adherence
(initiation, execution, discontinuation; persistence = time from initiation to discontinuation, from the EU ABC
project); electronic monitoring (MEMS) of ambulatory dosing histories; PK and PD consequences of erratic dosing;
"forgiveness"; rebound and recurrent first-dose effects; drug development and postmarketing implications;
interventions. PDF page = printed page − 274. Authors disclose consulting for Proteus Biomedical (TFB, LO) and
directorship/shareholding in AARDEX Group (BV, JU), which makes electronic monitors.

Numbers reported (all from the iAdherence / Pharmionic Knowledge Centre database or cited studies):
- Figure 1 (PDF p4, printed p278-279): 16,907 participants, 95 studies (30-1,400 days); about 4% never initiate;
  almost 40% stopped by 1 year; at day 100, 80% persisting and 12% of those not executing, so 70% dosed
  (88% × 80%).
- Database (printed p280): 95 studies, almost 19,000 subjects, dosing once daily 11,643, twice 3,731, thrice 1,308,
  four times 225; 28 therapeutic areas.
- Vrijens et al. lopinavir/ritonavir (printed p285): 35 HIV patients, 216 samples, 3 outside the predicted range
  when concentrations were projected from MEMS dosing histories.
- Harter & Peck variance example (Figure 7, printed p286-287): CVs formulation 20%, adherence 50%, PK 50%, PD 30%;
  combined √(400+2500+2500+900) = 79.4%; cutting PK and PD CVs by nine-tenths gives 54.2%, and formulation to 2%
  gives 51.1%.
- Forgiveness (printed p289): amlodipine effective duration about 72 h on a 24 h regimen gives 48 h forgiveness.
- Rebound hypertension with β-blockers or clonidine within 24-36 h of the last dose (printed p292).
- Vrijens antihypertensives (printed p288): 4,783 patients, only 5% had no dosing interruption in year 1; about 50%
  stopped within the year.
- Marin imatinib (printed p291): 87 patients, mean 98% of prescribed doses taken (range 24-104%); MMR 94% vs 28%
  for >90% vs <90% adherence; CMR 44% vs 0%; no molecular response below 80%.
- Paterson protease inhibitors (printed p292): virological failure 22% with >95% adherence vs 82% with <70%.
- Cochrane review (printed p295): 83 interventions in 70 RCTs; 36 improved adherence, 25 improved an outcome.

## Caveats
Narrative review by authors with commercial interests in electronic adherence monitoring (disclosed). Concerns
ambulatory oral therapy; none of it bears on intravenous or perioperative dosing. Several figures are from an
unpublished database. The reference list was not read for this card.
