# Park 1997: constrained longitudinal splines for sparse PK data

## Study
Methods paper. Constrained Longitudinal Spline (CLS) mixed-effects method (NONMEM) to estimate model-independent AUC, Cpeak and Tpeak from sparse, balanced, noisy population data, single-dose and steady state; compared with a Bateman (one-compartment, first-order absorption) model fit and with interpolation (pp. 618-623). Simulations: 500 replicates x 20 individuals x 5 samples; three models (unimodal, model misspecification with absorption shoulder, bimodal); proportional error CV 25% (p. 622). Real example: 42 HIV patients on saquinavir 600 mg tid at steady state, about 7 samples each (p. 615-616, Fig. 1). Constraints: nonnegativity, decreasing tail, typical Tpeak at the modal observed Tpeak, end-point conditions (pp. 619-620).

## Model and parameters
Individual curve = template spline (fixed effects) x individual distortion spline (random effects); number of distortion breakpoints chosen by twofold cross-validation (p. 621). Saquinavir typical values, Table I (printed p. 625; text layer scrambled, see open question): CLS cubic AUC 663 (SE 14%), Tpeak 3.6 h (8%), Cpeak 126 (15%), -2LL 2623.5, 8 parameters; CLS linear 643 (14%), 4.0 (0%), 125 (16%); Bateman 660 (20%), 2.5 (19%), 102 (22%), -2LL 2658.8, 6 parameters; interpolation 657 (14%), 3.8 (6%), 164 (14%). Units of AUC and Cpeak not given in the text.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Tpeak here is a plasma-concentration peak time, not an effect-site quantity. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0 or potency data.

## Caveats
Peak estimates depend on design-point placement; the cubic template can give bimodal Tpeak errors with bimodal data (p. 638). Designed, balanced sampling only.
