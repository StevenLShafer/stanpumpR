# Operto 2025: cenobamate concentrations in Italian responders

## Study
Retrospective multicentre Italian study with prospective follow-up, data May-November 2024 (p3). 53 adults with
drug-resistant focal epilepsy (Table 1, p4: 28 women, age 41 +/- 17 y, BMI 26 +/- 7) starting cenobamate at a stated
25 mg/day, titrated over about 20 weeks toward 200 mg/day. Responders = 50% or greater seizure reduction (26/53);
non-responders 27 (Table 2, p4: no significant differences except epilepsy duration, p 0.03). Plasma cenobamate (CNBp)
measured once, only in responders, at the first documented response; no sample in non-responders (p3). EDTA plasma,
UHPLC-MS/MS (Charlier 2022; Molteni 2024). Time of sampling relative to the dose is not reported. Analysis in R 4.1.2:
correlations, multiple linear regression (age, sex, BMI), Cohen's d per co-medication.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: 50% seizure reduction (monthly diaries). No concentration-response threshold was established; Figure 3
(p6) shows a modelled probability-of-response curve with "mean effective concentrations" of 18.7 (men) and 37.7 ug/mL
(women), not significant (p 0.126). 37.7 exceeds the highest measured CNBp (29.6), which the paper does not explain.

## Covariates
BMI (non-normalized only), co-medication effect sizes; age and sex not significant.

## Caveats
Small, responders only (selection bias, authors), one sample per patient at first response, often during titration
and so not at steady state (response at weeks 2-14, Figure 1); sampling time after dose unreported. Starting dose
stated as 25 mg/day yet two responders were on 12.5 mg. Mean CNBp at 200 mg/day in Figure 1 (about 27.5 ug/mL) is
well above the trial troughs at that dose; the paper does not discuss this.
