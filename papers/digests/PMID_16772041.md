# Hendrickx 2006: classical compartment models fitted to Gas Man output

## Study
- Pure computer simulation; no patients. Gas Man (Med Man Simulations) generated end-expired (FA) curves for halothane 0.75%,
  isoflurane 1.15%, sevoflurane 2%, desflurane 6% inspired; 115 min wash-in then 115 min wash-out; fresh gas 8 L/min, alveolar
  ventilation 4 L/min regardless of weight; Gas Man default circuit and tissue composition (Methods).
- 9 runs per agent: 70 kg with cardiac output 2, 3, 5, 8, 10 L/min; CO 5 L/min with weights 40, 55, 70, 85, 100 kg.
- Two- and three-compartment mammillary models fitted in NONMEM, with weight and CO as covariates (-2LL criterion, then backward
  deletion). Dose entered as FI x duration.
- Prospective test: 62 kg, CO 4.5 L/min, wash-in/out of 10, 60, 180 min each, FI halothane 1.5%, isoflurane 1.5%, sevoflurane 4%,
  desflurane 12%; converted with CONVERT.XLS.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None; no effect site, MAC or ke0 estimated. Mentions MAC and MAC-awake as the clinical links.

## Covariates
Weight and cardiac output, linear centred on 70 kg and 5 L/min (Table 1). Relations are not proportional: V2 falls as CO rises,
V3 vs CO is near-hyperbolic (Figures 1-2), so distribution volumes and clearances cannot be read as tissue volumes and flows.

## Caveats
Derived from Gas Man output, not patients; Gas Man itself is described as clinically unvalidated. High-flow open circuit only.
The paper does **not** list Gas Man's tissue volumes, blood flows or partition coefficients; the Appendix only describes the
structure: patient as four compartments (lung, vessel-rich group, muscle, fat) plus the circuit as a fifth, perfusion-limited
uptake, Euler integration, "standard values" for organ volumes and flows.
