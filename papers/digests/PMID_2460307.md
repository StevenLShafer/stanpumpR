# Nattel 1988: Recent advances in the pharmacology of amiodarone (review)

## Study
Narrative "Leading Article" review (11 pages, no new data). Covers sodium-, calcium- and potassium-channel
actions, the pharmacology of desethylamiodarone (DEA), thyroid interactions, drug interactions, pharmacokinetics
and plasma concentration-response relations. Much of the metabolite evidence is the authors' own canine and rat
work (Nattel 1986; Talajic 1987; Nattel et al. Circulation 1988;77:200-208).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Relative potency of DEA vs amiodarone depends on the end point (p. 124):
  - Ventricular arrhythmia suppression in dogs after myocardial infarction: DEA **3.2 times as potent** per plasma
    concentration (plasma concentration for 50% of maximal efficacy 3.2-fold higher for amiodarone; Nattel et al. 1988).
    Using Heger's human concentrations, about 70% of the long-term ventricular premature complex suppression is
    attributed to DEA (estimate, assumes the canine ratio holds in humans).
  - Myocardial DEA concentration averaged 3.68 times myocardial amiodarone at equal plasma concentration (dogs, IV).
  - Calcium-channel (slow channel) action: DEA about **one-ninth** as potent at displacing 3H-nitrendipine (Nokin 1986).
  - Vmax depression in vitro: similar degree for both (Yabek 1986).
- Therapeutic concentrations (p. 128): parent concentrations above 1 to 1.5 mg/L generally required for ventricular
  antiarrhythmic effect; a "therapeutic" 1 to 2.5 mg/L guarantees nothing; toxicity risk rises above 2.5 mg/L
  (Haffajee 1983, Rotmensch 1984). 8 patients recurred when plasma fell below 1 mg/L (p. 127). Regimens giving 2-3 mg/L
  produced prompt suppression (Mostow 1986). Assay: total serum/plasma (HPLC); not free.
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis model; single-dose QTc unchanged in dogs and humans despite comparable
  concentrations, so QT effect is time-related rather than concentration-related (p. 127).

## Covariates
None quantified.

## Caveats
Review; potency figures for DEA are canine (and sheep binding), not human. Several statements are from abstracts or
preliminary experiments. "Therapeutic range" explicitly described as a probabilistic indicator only.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Equal-potency claim (amiodarone.R:98-99; desethylamiodarone.R:49-50; amiodarone.md:22; desethylamiodarone.md:20):
  this review does not say the two are equipotent. It gives DEA 3.2 times the parent's potency per total plasma
  concentration for canine ventricular arrhythmia suppression, about one-ninth for calcium-channel binding, and similar
  Vmax depression in vitro. The repo's wording is attributed to Pollak's citation, so it may faithfully repeat Pollak,
  but the cited source does not support "equal potency". **discrepancy** (wording/attribution; no numeric parameter
  depends on it because neither drug has an effect site).
- No ke0 for either compound (amiodarone.R:100-101): consistent; the review reports none. **matches**.
- 1.0-2.5 mg/L window (amiodarone.R:110-113, attributed to the monograph/Pollak): consistent with this review's
  1-2.5 mg/L and >2.5 mg/L toxicity statement. **matches** (supporting, not the cited source).
- DEA/parent steady-state ratio: repo gives 0.90 from Pollak's clearances (desethylamiodarone.R:56-57); this review
  quotes 0.78 (Heger 1983). Different sources; noted only. **cannot verify** (not a claim the repo attributes here).
