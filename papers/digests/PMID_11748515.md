# Hudson 2001: Sufentanil pharmacokinetics before bypass in CABG patients

## Study
Naive-pooled NONMEM V population PK (log-normal error) of sufentanil in 103 elective CABG patients with good left ventricular function, from three prior pharmacodynamic studies; 529 arterial serum concentrations, pre-cardiopulmonary-bypass only (PDF pages 1-3, printed pp. 693-695).
- Group 1 (n=38): lorazepam 60 ug/kg oral; TCI effect-site targets 1.5, 3.0, 4.5 ng/mL (loading 2, 4, 6 ug/kg; 100-min totals 6.6, 13.2, 19.8 ug/kg). Group 2 (n=32): lorazepam plus propofol 1 mg/kg; targets 0.4, 0.8, 1.2 ng/mL (loading 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 ug/kg; totals 1.8, 3.6, 5.4 ug/kg). Group 3 (n=33): premedication with clonidine 5 ug/kg, lorazepam, or morphine 0.1 mg/kg plus scopolamine; 2.0 ug/kg over 12.5 min then 0.75 ng/mL target.
- TCI was run in StanPump, using the abdominal-aortic-surgery parameters (Hudson 1989) and ke0 from Scott 1991; the ke0 value is not printed in this paper.
- Demographics, all patients (Table 1): age 65.1 +- 8.3 yr, height 168 +- 9 cm, weight 78.8 +- 12.9 kg, 79 M/24 F (read from the text layer, Table 1 columns scrambled; group means in text order 66.5/64.6/63.9 yr, 77.3/77.8/81.5 kg).
- Assay: serum, radioimmunoassay (Janssen), duplicate; intra-sample CV 2.26%, error 1.29% at 0.2 ng/mL (total drug, serum, arterial).
- Model selection: 2- vs 3-compartment (3-comp gained 370.7 log-likelihood), then gender, weight-normalisation, premedication and propofol covariates; a covariate was kept only if log-likelihood rose >2 and prediction error improved.

## Model and parameters
Final model: simple 3-compartment, no covariates (Table 2, printed p. 696, confirmed on the page image). Gender, per-kg normalisation, premedication and propofol did not improve prediction. Volumes and clearances are absolute (L, L/min), not per kg.

Prediction (Table 2): first 30 min, median PE 1.3% (25th-75th -17.9, 20.3) vs -10.2% (-35.0, 18.5) for Gepts (p=0.007); median APE 19.0% (10.0, 35.5) vs 26.0% (14.0, 42.0) (p=0.019). Overall, median PE -0.4% (-16.3, 20.9) vs -2.3% (-20.4, 21.4); median APE 18.5% (9.7, 31.4) vs 20.7% (10.9, 34.5), not significantly different. The abstract gives overall APE 18.4%, the table 18.5% (rounding difference in the paper). PE = (measured - predicted)/predicted, so negative = overprediction by the model.

Before refitting, the earlier aortic-surgery parameters (Hudson 1989) used in the TCI gave median PE and APE 69.1% (25th 42.5, 75th 103.5): measured concentrations were consistently above target (p. 695).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Audit for this opioid paper:
- MEAC / analgesic concentration: none determined. The discussion (printed p. 698) states that sufentanil concentrations >=0.7 ng/mL give near-maximal opioid effect in CABG patients, citing Thomson 1998 (ref 1), not measured here; the end point and the matrix (serum vs plasma, total vs free) for that figure are not given in this paper.
- ke0 / t1/2 ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported. ke0 used in StanPump TCI was Scott 1991 (EEG-derived, per reference 6 title), value not printed.
- Equianalgesic or potency ratio: none.
- Effect-site target concentrations used in the TCI: 0.4-4.5 ng/mL across groups; Fig. 5 (printed p. 698) simulates a target of 0.8 ng/mL, approximated by a 0.5 ug/kg load plus 0.8 ug/kg/h for 78.8 kg.

## Covariates
Gender, weight (per-kg normalisation), premedication (lorazepam, morphine-scopolamine, clonidine) and propofol coinduction were tested and rejected: log-likelihood gains were below 5.7 for single-parameter variants (9 models) and below 4.1 for two-parameter variants (8 models), and none improved PE or APE (p=1.0).

## Caveats
Only pre-bypass samples, so elimination (gamma) phase and the effect of bypass are not characterised; do not use during or after bypass (authors, p. 698). Excludes severe LV dysfunction and morbid obesity. Naive pooled analysis, no inter-individual variance reported. V1 5.7 vs 14.3 L for Gepts is attributed to frequent early sampling under TCI and shorter distribution half-lives (authors, p. 698). Serum, not plasma or whole blood; concentrations are from patients on isoflurane.
