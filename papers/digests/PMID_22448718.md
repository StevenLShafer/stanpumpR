# Kokki 2012: intravenous oxycodone in first-stage labour

## Study
Prospective, open-label pilot with an opioid-free control group (newborns only for outcome). Kuopio, Finland,
2009-2010. 15 healthy nulliparous women at term (gestational weeks 38-42), Table 1 (PDF p3, printed 184): weight
82 kg [56-103], height 161 cm [150-178], age 29 y [18-38], gestational age 40 wk [38-43] (median [range]). 30
control newborns from mothers without systemic opioids.

Drug: Oxanest (Leiras), oxycodone hydrochloride trihydrate, IV boluses of 1 mg (stated equal to 0.78 mg oxycodone),
up to 5 mg cumulative, given when contraction pain was ≥5/10 NRS, repeated after 5 min if pain fell by ≤1 point.
Total dose median 5.0 mg [2-5]; one woman was given five 2-mg boluses (10 mg total) by mistake, and is reported
separately in the efficacy and neonatal results but appears to be included in the n = 15 population model (Table 3
header says n = 15).

Sampling: venous plasma 5 min after each bolus, 10, 30, 60 min after the last dose, then hourly to birth, plus a
maternal sample just after birth and umbilical arterial and venous samples after clamping. EDTA plasma.
Assay: LC-MS/MS (Neuvonen 2008), LLOQ 0.1 µg/L oxycodone and oxymorphone, 0.25 µg/L noroxycodone and
noroxymorphone; day-to-day CV <10%.

Model: NONMEM VI, FOCE-I; two-compartment, first-order elimination; bootstrap SEs (200 datasets in Methods, 500 in
the evaluation paragraph) and a numerical predictive check (Fig. 3). No covariates reported.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC / analgesic concentration**: none measured. The Discussion (PDF p5, printed 186) cites a "suggested
  analgesic concentration" of plasma oxycodone of **12 µg/L** from Olkkola 1994 (ref 21; postoperative children).
  In 10 of 14 parturients that was exceeded at 10 min after the last dose, and their pain ratings were lower than
  those below 12 µg/L (data not shown). Matrix: venous plasma, total (not free) oxycodone, LC-MS/MS. Population:
  labouring women; end point contraction pain NRS.
- Efficacy (PDF p3): baseline NRS median 7/10 (5-10); 10 min after the last dose 6/10 (3-10), p = 0.01; 4 women
  ≤5/10. All needed further analgesia; epidural at 89 min [15-309] after the last dose (the authors take this as
  analgesia duration).
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis**: none reported.
- **Potency ratio**: none. Hodge 1965 (rectal oxycodone in labour) is cited only qualitatively, with no
  concentrations or doses for the comparators.
- Adverse effects: sedation 6, light-headedness 2, nausea 1, desaturation to 93% 1. Neonatal Apgar and NACS
  (Table 4, PDF p5) did not differ from controls (NACS 34 vs 34); NACS was not correlated with cord oxycodone
  (r = -0.48, p = 0.068 venous).

## Covariates
None tested or reported.

## Caveats
- n = 15, pilot, no sample-size calculation; low doses (≤5 mg, 1-mg boluses); healthy nulliparae only.
- The comparative claim (shorter t½, higher CL, smaller V) is against **non-pregnant healthy volunteers in
  Grönlund 2011** (ref 18; t½ 3.8 h after IV oxycodone), not against a control group in this study. The abstract
  calls the comparator "non-pregnant women"; the Discussion says "non-pregnant healthy volunteers".
- t½ 2.6 h is a median of per-subject terminal half-lives from the post-dose elimination curves (Fig. 2), not a
  model output, though the model gives a similar 2.7 h (derived).
- Dose basis of the model (trihydrate mg, base mg, or other) is not stated; CYP2D6 genotype not assessed.
- Concurrent epidurals (12 of 15) and oxytocin (7) during sampling.

- **Ruling (Shafer, 2026-10-29), closing the dose-basis question:** "it isn't clear whether they calculated clearance based on dosing the trihydrate or the base. On the app's anhydrous-hydrochloride basis that is about 0.80 L/min (x 351.82/405.87), pending verification. The Oxanest-salt [decision] question stays open for that session.
- **Ruling (Shafer, 2026-10-29), closing the Oxanest question:** "Oxanest is a brand name for an injectable formulation of oxycodone hydrochloride trihydrate. So it is the trihydrate throughout." Pöyhiä 1991, Kirvelä 1996 and this paper all gave the trihydrate. R/drugs_oxycodone.R's DOSE BASIS, which treats Pöyhiä's and Kirvelä's Oxanest as the anhydrous hydrochloride and rescales them to the Oxanest dose, is to be corrected to the app's anhydrous-HCl basis by the session "Work out oxycodone dose-form molar equivalents".
- **Source supplied (Shafer, 2026-10-29), closing the 12 ug/L question:** reference 21 is Olkkola KT, Hamunen K, Seppala T, Maunuksela E-L, Br J Clin Pharmacol 1994;38:71-76. Its Results give "the theoretical concentration of oxycodone at the time of administering the rescue analgesic, based on the fitted function describing the pharmacokinetics ... 12 +/- 8 ng/ml (median 10, range 3-32)", which the Discussion calls the concentration "at the site of action". Caveats: children after ophthalmic surgery, mostly asleep; the rescue was IV diclofenac when the pain score exceeded 2 (closer to an onset-of-pain MEC than a MEAC); the value is the fitted plasma concentration. The library's oxycodone MEAC is also 12 ng/mL, set for consistency with morphine (R/drugs_oxycodone.R MEAC section), which does not cite this paper; adding it as corroboration is proposed to Shafer.
