# Faisal 2019: enalapril and enalaprilat, joint serum and urine population PK

## Study
24 healthy ADULTS (Table 2, PDF p7: age mean 28.00, median 24.40, range 22.08-47.16 y; weight 69.76, 67.60,
51.80-95.60 kg; height 174.5 cm; total body water 42.06 L), two-period crossover, single 10 mg enalapril maleate as the
reference tablet (Renitec, two 5 mg tablets) or ten 1 mg orodispersible mini-tablets (ODMT), each with 240 mL water
(PDF p2-3). Part of the EU FP7 LENA project. Serum at 0.17-48 h (20 times), urine 0-2 ... 36-48 h (PDF p3); 2,208 serum
and urine concentrations (PDF p5-6). LC-MS/MS, serum ranges 0.195-200 ng/mL enalapril and 0.180-188 ng/mL enalaprilat
(PDF p3). NONMEM 7.4.0, ADVAN6, FOCE+I; validated by VPC (1000 simulations), bootstrap (200) and SIR (PDF p4-5).

## Model and parameters
Figure 1 (PDF p5, rendered): Dose box, then Transit 1 ... Transit N=8 with MTT1 = (N+1)/KTR, then KA into the enalapril
central compartment (VC), eliminated by KREN to urine and KM to a metabolite chain ("Transit N=2", MTT2 = (N+1)/KTR1) into
the enalaprilat central compartment (VM) with a peripheral compartment (KQ1 out, KQ2 back) and KMEL (= KME) to urine.
Text (PDF p4): KTR is the rate through the transits, KA the transfer rate from the last transit into the central
compartment; F1 estimated because serum and urine of both moieties close the mass balance, assuming both are cleared only
renally and enalaprilat is not further metabolised. One compartment chosen for enalapril (two gave a significant
objective-function drop but higher RSEs; PDF p6, p8); two for enalaprilat (three rejected, PDF p9). Eight transits for
reference and pooled data, six for the ODMT alone (PDF p6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None fitted: no ACE inhibition, no BP. Observed summaries (PDF p6): enalapril Cmax in almost 1 h, gone from serum in
almost 10 h; enalaprilat Cmax in around 3-4 h, biphasic decline. ODMT absorbed about 5 min earlier (Wilcoxon on
separate fits, MTT1 p = 0.03; Table 3, PDF p9); no other formulation difference. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Equation 6 (PDF p4): TV = theta x (WT/WTref)^theta, WTref = study mean weight, exponent fixed 1 for volumes and 0.75 for
clearances. Retained: weight on enalapril VC (OFV -18.2; backward elimination confirmed) and formulation on MTT1 (OFV
-6.51) (PDF p7). Base vs full parameter change < 25%, called clinically unimportant (PDF p7). Age, height, sex and total
body water were "available"; the text describes testing only weight and formulation.

## Caveats
- Healthy adults only, despite the paediatric journal and programme; no renal covariate possible.
- Internal inconsistencies: the Discussion (PDF p8) quotes KA 6.03 /h (Table 6.010), total parent elimination 0.93 /h
  (KREN+KM = 0.993) and KME 0.175 /h (Table 0.184).
- MTT1 = (N+1)/KTR with KA out of the last transit cannot hold as a mean transit time for every reading of the chain.
- Dose basis: doses are labelled enalapril maleate strength; the text does not say whether F1 includes the salt fraction.

  the repo's own "reading 1" (`test-drugs-enalapril.R:230-231`), the one it rejected. The figure does not label the
  Dose-to-Transit-1 rate, so the question is unresolved, not settled against the repo; but the claim that the text
  fixes the structure "completely" overstates the source. Impact per the repo's own test: parent tmax 0.95 vs 0.88 h,
  peaks within 6%.
- `docs/antihypertensives.md:1729-1732` puts in quotation marks a sentence that splices two separate clauses of PDF p4

- One compartment for enalapril because two gave "higher standard errors with no better fit" (`drugs_enalapril.R:32-33`):
