# Wang 2017: dexmedetomidine during caesarean section under epidural anaesthesia, placental transfer

## Study
Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial (NCT02715154), First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical
University. 40 full-term parturients, singleton, ASA I-II, 23-41 y, 61-92 kg; 20 dexmedetomidine (Dex), 20 saline.
Table 1 (PDF p4, rendered), Dex vs NS: age 31.3 ± 5.3 vs 28.4 ± 3.8 y, weight 77.2 ± 10.9 vs 72.8 ± 8.7 kg, GA
39.1 weeks, operation time 57.3 ± 6.8 vs 56.2 ± 3.9 min. Epidural L2-3 (3 mL lidocaine 2% test, 10-20 mL ropivacaine
0.75%). Dex group: dexmedetomidine IV 0.5 µg/kg over 10 min after the block, then 0.5 µg/kg/h until abdominal closure
(abstract PDF p1 and Methods PDF p3, rendered; the text layer renders µg as "mg"). At delivery: maternal venous (MV),
umbilical venous (UV) and arterial (UA) blood; HPLC-MS/MS. Power analysis on HR. No pharmacokinetic model.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
HR lower than saline at T1 (10 min after the start), delivery and end of surgery; SBP and DBP lower at the end of
surgery (Dex T0 vs T3: SBP 116 vs 111, DBP 74 vs 66 mmHg, HR 91 vs 71 /min; abstract, Fig. 1). Ramsay score higher
at skin incision and 10 min after delivery (Dex 3-4). No difference in umbilical blood gases (Table 2) or Apgar
(9.9 and 10 at 1 and 5 min, Table 3). No concentration-effect analysis, no ke0.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
One time point per woman (delivery), so no time course or PK estimates; ratio of venous concentrations only, not
free drug; small sample; protein binding not measured. Authors attribute the lower ratio than under general
anaesthesia to reduced placental perfusion under epidural (speculative).
