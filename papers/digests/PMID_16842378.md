# Ralph 2006: validity of the epirubicin population model

## Study
Internal and external validation of the earlier epirubicin population model (Ralph 2003, clearance set by serum AST). Development set: 105 women with advanced breast cancer (69 with liver metastases), Guy's Hospital, epirubicin alone 12.5-120 mg/m2 as an IV injection (median 3 min, range 0.4-19 min), 8-18 samples per patient to about 48 h after cycle 1 (PDF p. 2). External set: 79 patients, Uppsala (28 early/locally advanced, 51 advanced; 16 with liver metastases), FEC regimen (fluorouracil/epirubicin/cyclophosphamide, epirubicin 60 or 75 mg/m2) given as an IV infusion (median 1 h, range 5 min-2.3 h), median 3 samples (2-6) between 4 min and 25 h (PDF p. 2). NONMEM FOCE-INTER. Internal methods: 105 jackknife samples, likelihood-based influence of each patient on AST selection, 5-fold cross-validation (5 groups of 21), +/-10% perturbation of AST or of concentrations. External: population predictions (etas and residual error zero) and MAP Bayesian clearance in the new patients.

## Model and parameters
Structural model, three compartments (Table 4 footnote, PDF p. 6): TVCL (L/h) = theta1 x (1 - theta7 x ln AST), V1 = theta2, Q2 = theta3, V2 = theta4, Q3 = theta5, V3 = theta6; log-normal IIV on CL, Q3 and V3. AST in U/L, doses in mg, concentrations in ng/mL.

Training-set fits (Table 2) were within 10% of the complete-data estimates. Jackknife structural estimates were within 7.5% of the final ones (p. 4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not a PD study; no effect-site, ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency data. Epirubicin is not an opioid. The model's therapeutic target is AUC (Dose/CL), said to predict toxicity and outcome (Discussion, p. 7); no concentration range is given.

Validation results (the paper's key output):
- External concentrations: rmse (imprecision) 102% (95% CI 96.4-108); bias (me) +81.4% (68.5-94.2), i.e. over-prediction, worst at < 100 ng/mL in the elimination phase (p. 5).
- External clearance (population CL vs MAP Bayesian CL): rmse 43% (37.8-48.0); bias -27.9% (-35.2 to -20.5), i.e. population CL under-estimates the Bayesian CL (p. 5).
- Cross-validation, Table 3 (p. 6): rmse of population CL, basic vs final model, validation sets 1-5: 45.1/43.6, 49.7/61.6, 56.9/44.4, 94.1/47.1, 71.1/30.9; combined 65.8/46.6 (delta -19.2, P = 0.062). Significant gain from AST only where AST > 150 U/L (P < 0.01).
- Median clearance in validation patients with normal liver function 62 L/h, vs 47-87 L/h in the literature; median in the development set 38 L/h (Discussion, p. 7).

## Covariates
AST is the only covariate in the model (log-linear on CL). Sensitivity: +/-10% change in AST left estimates essentially unchanged (Table 4, theta7 0.135 vs 0.136).

## Caveats
Authors' explanations for the external failure: differences in dose, infusion length, sampling, proportion with liver dysfunction (only about 20% of the validation group had AST above normal, six patients above 150 U/L), laboratory assay, FEC co-medication versus single agent, and health status (p. 7-8). Validation "true" clearances are themselves sparse-data MAP estimates. Table 1 development AST mean is 125 U/L vs 58 in the validation group (PDF p. 4). Tables 2 and 4 disagree (open question). Table 1's printed means (weight 63 kg dev, 68 kg validation; height 158 vs 165 cm; BSA 1.64 vs 1.74 m2) are on page image.
