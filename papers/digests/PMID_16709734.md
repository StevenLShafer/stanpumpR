# Curran-Everett 2006: the alveolar gas equation (teaching essay on Fenn, Rahn, Otis 1946)

## Study
A teaching essay, not a data paper. It describes the 1946 paper of Fenn, Rahn and Otis (Am J Physiol 146:637-653), a theoretical study of alveolar gas at altitude, and offers seven discussion questions with answers. No subjects, doses, assay or fitted model. PDF pages 1-5 = printed pp. 58-62. The PDF is not in the stanpumpR context file as cited anywhere (`cited_in_repo: false`, no locations).

## Model and parameters
No fitted parameters. Equations given (symbols of Pappenheimer 1950; PDF p. 2-3, printed pp. 59-60):
- Basic alveolar gas equation: PAO2 = PIO2 - PACO2/R, R = VCO2/VO2 (Eq. 1).
- Entire equation (Eq. 3, with FIO2): PAO2 = PIO2 + PACO2*FIO2*(1-R)/R - PACO2/R. The middle term vanishes when R = 1; it is largest at low R and high PACO2 (Fig. 2, up to about 7 mmHg at R 0.6, PACO2 50, air).
- Ventilation form (Eq. S4): PAO2 = PIO2 - (PB - 47)*VO2/VA, with PACO2 = (PB - 47)*VCO2/VA (S3). 47 is the water vapour pressure in mmHg.
- Inspired PO2 = (PB - 47)*FIO2. Pure O2 at any altitude: PAO2 + PACO2 = PB - 47.
- Worked values (Table 2, printed p. 60, air, mmHg): e.g. PACO2 40, R 0.8: PAO2 52 at 10,000 ft, 32 at 15,000 ft. PACO2 20, R 1.0: 80 / 60 / 43 / 29 at 10,000 / 15,000 / 20,000 / 25,000 ft. Values below 20 mmHg are blank (truncated). Pressures used: PB about 428 mmHg at 15,000 ft (text, printed p. 62); the 10,000 ft value is garbled in the text layer (PIO2 80 mmHg is legible) and is not recorded here.
