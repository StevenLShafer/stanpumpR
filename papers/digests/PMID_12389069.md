# Variol 2002: simultaneous oral/IV population PK model for vinorelbine

PDF page n = printed page 466 + n (PDF p1 = printed 467).

## Study
- Pooled data from five phase I and two phase II trials (Table 1, PDF p2) of vinorelbine monotherapy in adults with
  solid tumours or lymphoma: 175 patients, 419 PK courses in all.
- Phase I (model building): 111 patients (74 oral, 64 IV), 187 courses (88 oral, 99 IV). Rich sampling, mean 10
  samples (range 5–13) per course, 2217 concentrations in total, over 0–120 h after IV and 0–72 h after oral dosing.
- Phase II (assessment, then refit): 64 patients on oral drug, 232 courses (3–11 per patient), sampled at 1.5, 3 and
  24 h after dosing (909 concentrations).
- Doses: IV 20–45 mg/m² (zero-order input) and oral soft-gel capsules 60–100 mg/m². Usual doses were 25–30 mg/m² IV
  and 60–80 mg/m² oral.
- Demographics (Table 2, PDF p4; mean ± SD and range): age 57 ± 11 y (21–77), weight 68 ± 13 kg (37–125), BSA
  1.74 ± 0.17 m² (1.32–2.34), Cockcroft–Gault CrCl 80 ± 22 mL/min (34–180), platelets 298 ± 110 ×10⁹/L (90–883).
  M/F 91/84; liver metastases in 38/137 patients.
- Assay: **whole blood** concentrations, measured by HPLC-UV (LLOQ 2.5 ng/mL; bias < 10%; within- and between-day
  reproducibility < 12%). The paper does not report plasma concentrations or protein binding.
- Analysis: NONMEM V, FO method, with FOCE-I used to confirm the base and final runs. ADVAN12 TRANS4. Exponential
  IIV and inter-occasion variability (IOV). Covariates were screened by S-PLUS tree regression plus ETA plots, then
  added forward (ΔOFV > 7.9) and removed backward (ΔOFV > 10.9).

## Model and parameters
Three-compartment model with first-order absorption and a lag time; oral and IV data were fitted together.
Compartment 2 is the central compartment (ADVAN12 numbering), so K23/K32 and K24/K42 are the two peripheral
exchanges. The residual error was a constant CV of 24% (PDF p4).

Table 3 (PDF p5) gives mean ± SE. Its heading prints "interindividual variability" twice. From the text, the
bracketed IOV values are the intra-individual (inter-occasion) variability:

| Parameter | Mean ± SE | IIV CV, covariate-free | IIV CV, final |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl (L/h) | 41.9 ± 2.8 | 35% (IOV 18%) | 23% (IOV 12%) |
| F (%) | 36.0 ± 3.1 | 20% (IOV 19%) | 16% (IOV 19%, fixed) |
| Vc (L) | 22.2 ± 1.5 | 29% | 28% |
| K23 (h⁻¹) | 1.39 ± 0.16 | 32% | 29% |
| K24 (h⁻¹) | 2.46 ± 0.48 | 38% | 29% |
| K32 (h⁻¹) | 0.0337 ± 0.00247 | – | – |
| K42 (h⁻¹) | 0.402 ± 0.0351 | – | – |
| Tlag (h) | 0.15 ± 0.01 | 70% fixed | 70% fixed |
| Ka (h⁻¹) | 0.481 ± 0.091 | 61% | 46% |

- ETAs on Cl and K23 were correlated (R² = 0.76).
- F = 36% has a 95% CI of 30–42% (PDF p8). The authors note this is lower than the 43% found in the phase I crossover
  bioavailability study (ref 12, Marty 2001).
- Absorption half-life 1.4 h (stated, PDF p9; consistent with ln2/0.481).

Final covariate model, phase I data (Eq. 1–4, PDF p5; relative SE in brackets):
- Cl (L/h) = 30.5 (5%) · BSA · (1 − 0.22 (17%)·CrCl) · (1 − 0.12 (31%)·Transa) · exp[−0.00194 (15%)·(Plt − 400)]
  - Eq. 1 is printed with an unbalanced bracket, "Plt − 400)]". The refined Eq. 5 prints (Plt − 400).
- F = 0.36 (6%) · (1 + 0.23 (39%)·ALP)
- Vc (L) = 12.3 (4%) · BSA
- Alag (h) = 0.15 (7%) · (1 + 0.90 (14%)·Fed)

Refined model fitted to the pooled phase I + II data (Eq. 5–6, PDF p7):
- Cl = 30.2 (5%) · BSA · (1 − 0.18 (19%)·CrCl) · (1 − 0.10 (40%)·Transa) · exp[−0.00160 (13%)·(Plt − 400)]
- F = 0.37 (5%) · (1 + 0.23 (39%)·ALP)
- Intra-individual variability in F was about 20% (RSE 24%).
- The FOCE-I run for this model did not converge successfully. The authors report its estimates agreed with the FO
  estimates.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- None. The paper has no PD model, ke0, effect-site, exposure-toxicity or exposure-efficacy relationship, and no
  therapeutic or toxic concentration.
- Variability comparison (Table 5, PDF p6), using individual Bayesian CL (IV) or CL/F (oral) from phase I:
  - Usual doses: CV 26% IV (n = 59) and 33% oral (n = 63).
  - All doses: CV 27% IV (n = 99) and 36% oral (n = 81).
  - Seven oral courses were excluded for poor fits. With them included, the oral CV is 43% at usual doses and 53% at
    all doses.

## Covariates
Coding of the covariates in the clearance and bioavailability equations:
- BSA: in m², with a direct proportional effect on both CL and Vc.
- CrCl: 1 if Cockcroft–Gault clearance is < 70 mL/min, otherwise 0.
- Transa: 1 if SGOT and/or SGPT is above the normal limit.
- Plt: platelet count in ×10⁹/L when above 400, otherwise set to 400.
- ALP: 1 if alkaline phosphatase is above the normal limit (effect on F, imprecise, RSE 39%).
- Fed: 1 if the patient was fed (effect on the lag time).

Other effects, not given as equations:
- Gender and platelets act on K23; BSA acts on K24 (Results, PDF p5).

No effect was found for the following:
- GGT, LDH, total protein, liver metastases or age on the PK (abstract, PDF p1).
- Early vomiting (< 3 h after the oral dose) on Ka, F or Tlag (PDF p9).

## Caveats
- The measurements are whole-blood concentrations. They cannot be used directly as plasma concentrations.
- Derived check (not reconciled in the paper): at the median BSA of 1.74 m², with no covariate penalties, Eq. 1
  gives CL = 30.5 × 1.74 = 53.1 L/h. Table 3 gives 41.9 L/h.
  - Table 3 likely reports the covariate-free run, but the paper does not say so.
  - The patients' mean platelet count and the transaminase/CrCl flags lower the Eq. 1 value only partly.
  - Vc from Eq. 3 (12.3 × 1.74 = 21.4 L, derived) is close to Table 3's 22.2 L.
- Estimation was mostly by FO, and the refined FOCE-I run did not converge.
- The IIV on Tlag was fixed at 70%.
- The study enrolled cancer patients with near-normal organ function: CrCl ≥ 34 mL/min, and transaminase rises were
  limited to five times the normal limit.
- Only 11 patients had platelets > 500 ×10⁹/L, so the platelet effect rests on few data.
- The authors work for the sponsor (Institut de Recherche Pierre Fabre).
