# Gentilini 2001: BIS-guided closed-loop isoflurane

## Study
Control-engineering paper with a volunteer PK-PD identification study and two patient closed-loop cases. Volunteers: 20 ASA I, age 25 ± 3 y, weight 68 ± 13 kg (PDF p.2, printed 875). Each had seven combinations of constant alfentanil predicted plasma concentration (STANPUMP-driven infusion) and constant end-tidal isoflurane, ~20 min each, with painful stimuli between steady states. Two groups: alfentanil varied with isoflurane fixed, or isoflurane varied with alfentanil fixed. Induction was sevoflurane 7 % in 60 % N2O; mivacurium 0.15 mg/kg. Isoflurane was given by a Cicero closed-circuit system, fresh gas flow 6 L/min. BIS came from an Aspect A-1000 (5 s sampling); gases from a Datex AS/3. Arterial isoflurane and alfentanil samples were drawn, but the paper reports no assay. Alfentanil target concentrations are not given in the text. Stimulus-response analysis is deferred to another paper.
Patient cases (PDF p.13-14): 58-year-old man (hernia repair, ~4 h, BIS target 40-50, a reference of 30 for a period) and 63-year-old woman (BIS electrode disconnection at ~158 min). Both received propofol induction and manual alfentanil TCI.

## Model and parameters
Three parts: a respiratory-circuit mass balance (well-stirred tank, eq. 1); the Yasuda isoflurane mammillary 5-compartment PK model with the central compartment taken as end-tidal/alveolar (eqs. 2-4, Tables I-II, PDF p.4, printed 877); and an effect compartment with an Emax (sigmoid) BIS model (eqs. 5-8). The Yasuda PK is adopted from the literature, not fitted here; it was checked open loop against the volunteers' end-tidal data (Fig. 3, visual agreement only).
Effect-compartment model: dCe/dt = ke0 (C1 − Ce); ΔBIS = ΔBISmax · Ce^γ / (Ce^γ + EC50^γ), with BIS0 = 100 and BISmax = 0 assumed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: BIS (Aspect A-1000), against end-tidal isoflurane. A clockwise hysteresis against end-tidal and a counter-clockwise one against the 2nd-compartment concentration show BIS lies between them (Fig. 5).
- ke0 pooled 0.3853 /min, i.e. t½ke0 = ln2/0.3853 = 1.80 min (derived: not stated in the paper). Individual ke0 spans 0.02477 to 5.000 /min; the pooled value is described as more robust than the mean. The CV of ke0 (1.1426) is an order of magnitude above the PK-parameter CVs. No tPeak is reported.
- EC50 pooled 0.7478 vol % (population mean 0.7495); γ pooled 1.534. In the patient case, ~0.7 % end-tidal held BIS near 50 (PDF p.13).
- Comparison cited: Olofsen & Dahan (patients, end-tidal 0.6-1.6 %) gave ke0 0.217 /min; the authors' volunteers spanned 0.4-2.2 % and gave a higher ke0, which they speculate could partly reflect alfentanil. See open question.
- Alfentanil: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio reported. In this data set alfentanil neither consistently raised nor lowered BIS (Fig. 8), so it was excluded from the control model. No alfentanil assay values or concentrations are given.
- MAC / equipotency: none stated.

## Covariates
None. Parameters are per-subject or pooled; no weight, age or sex dependence.

## Caveats
- Healthy volunteers with muscle relaxant, a limited-quality BIS signal during stimuli (stimulation windows excluded).
- Three volunteers (10, 19, 20) hit the upper constraints (no BIS change, or constantly low BIS) and were dropped from the averages; volunteers 4, 5 and 7 remain and give outlying high ke0 (3.027, 3.489, 2.895).
- "Limited-quality" BIS: stimulation periods were excluded rather than modelled.
- The PK model is borrowed (Yasuda), validated only graphically on end-tidal data.
- Closed-loop evidence is two patients.
- ke0 here is relative to end-tidal (alveolar) concentration, not to a plasma concentration.
