# Cheung 1990: IV bolus nicardipine in awake mild-to-moderate hypertension

## Study
11 patients completed (10 men, 1 woman; mean age 56.5 +/- 4 y), mild-to-moderate hypertension (supine DBP 95-114
mmHg, HR < 90), antihypertensives stopped; serum creatinine > 1.5 mg/dL excluded (p1-p2). Single IV doses of
0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 3, 5 or 7 mg in 2 mL D5W given over 2 min on separate days (1-10 days apart), dose escalated
or halved from a 1 mg start until HR rose 20 bpm or SBP fell below 90; placebo (diluent) also tested (p2). The
methods also say the drug line was regulated at 30 mL/h, which does not fit 2 mL in 2 min; unresolved. BP and HR
by mercury sphygmomanometer at 1-min intervals for 5 min before, during the 2-min injection and for 15 min after,
then 5-min intervals to 30 min, 10-min to 60, 15-min to 90, hourly to 6 h (p2-p3). Plasma nicardipine (assay not
described; "serum" and "plasma" both used) drawn from the other arm at 1-min intervals during and at 1, 2, 5, 7.5,
10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60 min after, then hourly. Data mean +/- SEM.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model or parameters: concentrations are graphed only (Fig 1). Peak plasma levels 1 min after
the end of injection; peak level linear in dose (r = 0.98, Fig 1B); rapid fall over 15 min; at low doses levels
fell to 5 ng/mL in 1-4 h but stayed detectable 3-6 h (p4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: cuff SBP/DBP and HR. Abstract: peak average decreases in SBP 20-25 and DBP 15-44 mmHg, occurring
**2.5 +/- 0.6 min after infusion**; HR up 12-40 bpm at peak; mean duration of action 24 +/- 5 min. Results (p4):
pressures fell during the 2 min of injection and reached a nadir "at 2 minutes after injection"; 1 mg: SBP -21.4
+/- 6.6, DBP -20.3 +/- 2.6 mmHg; 7 mg: SBP -40.3 +/- 5.2, DBP -25.8 +/- 3.5 mmHg; HR rise 7.0 +/- 2.9 (0.125 mg) to
16.8 +/- 4.2 bpm (7 mg). The abstract's SBP range (20-25) does not include the 7 mg result in the Results (40.3);
abstract and text also differ on the time of the nadir (2.5 vs 2 min). Figs 2-3 (time axis -2 to 20 min) show
nadirs around 0-2 min on that axis; the axis origin is not stated, so which reading is right cannot be settled
from the figures. Clinically significant falls at concentrations as low as 50 ng/mL (p5). Correlations of SBP, DBP,
HR with level over 6 h r = -0.63, -0.68, 0.71 (Fig 5). No ke0, no EC50, no MEAC, no potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n = 11, non-randomised dose escalation within patient, cuff pressure at 1-min resolution, summary statistics in
SEM, assay unspecified, the abstract/text inconsistencies above.
