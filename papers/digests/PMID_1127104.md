# Klotz 1975: age and liver disease on diazepam disposition

## Study
33 healthy adults (27 M, 6 F), 15-82 y, 13 heavy smokers. 20 normals (17 M, 3 F) received 0.1 mg/kg IV over 2 min into an
antecubital vein; the rest (and some patients) 10 mg oral tablet fasted. Patients: 9 men with alcoholic cirrhosis
(45.7 +/- 3.7 y), 8 acute viral hepatitis (26.1 y), 4 chronic active hepatitis (30.0 y). Venous plasma from 15 min, then
frequent for hours and every 12 h for 3 days (normals) or 4 days (patients). GC with electron-capture detection,
calibration 1-50 ng/mL; binding by equilibrium dialysis. Two-compartment open model per subject (SAAM-23, CV weight 0.05);
clearance reported as k10 x V1 (agreed with dose/AUC).

## Model and parameters
Normals (Abstract, Results, Table II):
- Terminal t1/2: about 20 h at 20 y rising linearly to about 90 h at 80 y; regression t1/2 = 1.085 x age + 1.98 h
  (n = 35 studies, r = 0.83). Smoking no effect.
- Plasma clearance: most subjects 20-32 mL/min (one 43; four aged 56-63 about half); regression CL = 32.52 - 0.186 x age
  mL/min (n = 20, P = 0.089, not significant).
- V1/kg = 0.0061 x age + 0.08 L/kg; Vss/kg = 0.0172 x age + 0.42 L/kg (n = 16, both significant); k21/k12 ratio
  constant; k10 falls with age.
- Plasma binding 97.4 +/- 1.2%; blood/plasma ratio 0.58 +/- 0.16; no age effect.
- Oral 10 mg: peak 221-440 ng/mL at 1 h; bioavailability "only about 75%"; urinary and biliary excretion of
  unchanged drug negligible.
Cirrhosis vs age-matched controls (Table III; abstract): t1/2 105.6 +/- 15.2 vs 46.6 +/- 14.2 h; plasma CL 13.8 +/- 2.4
vs 26.6 +/- 4.1 mL/min; Vss 1.74 +/- 0.21 vs 1.13 +/- 0.28 L/kg; V1/kg 0.42 vs 0.31 (P = 0.054); k10 0.030 vs about
0.075 /h; binding 95.3 vs 97.8%; blood CL 24.8 vs 44.8 mL/min. Acute viral hepatitis t1/2 74.5 +/- 27.5 h; chronic
active hepatitis 59.7 +/- 23.0 h, vs controls 32.7 +/- 8.9 h.
Chronic active hepatitis, IV (Abstract; Results PDF p7, printed p353): only **two** patients had IV diazepam; their
plasma clearances were 13.0 and 17.9 mL/min (individual values, n = 2). Most hepatitis data gave only t1/2.
Acute viral hepatitis recovery (Results p353; Table IV, PDF p9, printed p355): five patients restudied when liver
tests had just returned to normal; t1/2 shorter in all (acute 59.6-94.2 h, recovery 47.4-62.2 h) but still above
the normal upper limit (Table IV: 45.4 h); one patient (J.H.) restudied 4 months later was in the normal range
(text 33.0 h, Table IV 36.0 h: the source disagrees with itself). The abstract summarises this as a return almost
to normal.
Liver-function tests (Abstract; Results p353): no statistically significant correlation between t1/2 (cirrhosis
or hepatitis), or clearance in cirrhosis, and any routine liver-function test.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No ke0.

## Covariates
Age (V1, Vss, t1/2; not clearance), liver disease (clearance, Vss, binding). Smoking none.

## Caveats
First sample at 15 min, so V1 is not an early-phase estimate; venous sampling; old GC assay; clearance by age not
significant but four older subjects had low values; small patient groups. Liver-disease clearance outside cirrhosis
rests on two chronic-active-hepatitis patients; the hepatitis groups otherwise give t1/2 only. Routine liver-function
tests did not predict diazepam t1/2 or clearance, so a hepatic covariate cannot be built from them on this evidence.
