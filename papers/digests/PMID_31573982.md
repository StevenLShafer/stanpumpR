# Sleigh 2019: editorial on remifentanil-hypnotic interaction

## Study
Three-page editorial (PDF pp. 1-3; printed 1199-1201) commenting on Kuizenga et al. 2019 (Anesthesiology 131:1223-38), a within-subject volunteer study of propofol vs sevoflurane, with and without remifentanil, using the Patient State Index (PSI) and a clinical response score. No new data, no PK model, no assay.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
All values are the editor's reading of Kuizenga's Fig. 2 (reproduced as Fig. 1, PDF p. 2, printed 1200); the end point is stated per item:
- Remifentanil 2 ng/mL (editor equates it to a steady-state infusion of about 0.1 ng/kg/min) lowers the probability of response to the tetanic stimulus at propofol EC50 2.8 ug/mL from 50% to under 5%. End point: quantal behavioural response. Read from the figure by the editor; not a MEAC.
- Remifentanil 4 ng/mL was the highest concentration studied; the editor says more than 2 ng/mL gives diminishing benefit against movement (tetanic stimulus, weaker than incision).
- Remifentanil shifts the propofol-PSI curve slightly left, with almost no effect on the sevoflurane-PSI curve. End point: PSI. Citing other work (ref 5, Wiczling 2016, propofol-fentanyl), the editor puts the EC50 of the remifentanil EEG concentration-response curve at 5-10 ng/mL (second-hand; assay and measure not stated).
- PSI 60 vs 30: most patients respond at 60 and most do not at 30 for verbal command; remifentanil or sevoflurane shifts that curve about 20 PSI points left.

Opioid audit: no MEAC, no ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis, no equianalgesic ratio, no assay type reported.
