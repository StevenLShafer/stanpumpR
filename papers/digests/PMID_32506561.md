# Van Hese 2020: Marsh and Minto predictions against measured plasma and brain concentrations

## Study
- Prospective observational study, 38 adults (20 M / 18 F; mean (SD) age 53 (17) y, BMI 25.8 (4.7) kg/m2) having elective neurosurgery under TCI propofol + remifentanil (PDF p4). Brain tissue kept from 29 tumour and 9 epilepsy resections; only the 9 epilepsy samples were analysed (tumours excluded as they may change brain PK).
- Induction: sufentanil 0.25-0.5 mcg/kg IV bolus plus TCI propofol; rocuronium 1 mg/kg. Maintenance: Alaris PK pumps, **Marsh** propofol (original) and **Minto** remifentanil. Plasma or effect-site targeting at the anaesthetist's discretion; BIS 40-60 aimed for (PDF p3).
- One arterial blood sample (4 mL) and one brain sample per patient, taken together when predicted blood and effect-site concentrations had been stable more than 10 min ("pseudo-steady state", assumed, not shown; PDF p3). Samples frozen at -80 C at once.
- Assay: whole blood and homogenised brain, solid-phase extraction; remifentanil by LC-MS/MS, propofol by GC-MS, deuterated internal standards. Whole-blood results were converted to plasma with red-cell/plasma partition coefficients 1.13 (propofol, sheep data) and 0.89 (remifentanil, dog data) (PDF p3). Brain results are per gram of tissue (ng/g, ug/g) and were compared with predicted concentrations in mL (Table 1).
- Performance error PE (%) = (Cmeas - Cpred)/Cpred x 100 (PDF p4). Cpred read by hand off the pump display (plasma and effect-site).
- Remifentanil: 5 patients with paired plasma + brain. Propofol: 5 patients with paired samples; one patient (38 y M, 92 kg) is in both sets (4 + 4 + 1 = 9 brain samples).

## Model and parameters
No model was fitted; the paper only tests Marsh (propofol) and Minto (remifentanil) as implemented in the pump. Their parameters are not given.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, EC50 or hysteresis was estimated. BIS at sampling is listed in Table 2 (28-43; two propofol patients have no BIS) but not analysed against concentration.
- The authors note the Marsh model was originally PK-only and its ke0 came from another study (PDF p2); neither the Marsh ke0 nor the Minto ke0 is stated.
- The paper argues that the usual "collapse the loop" ke0 method assumes plasma and effect-site concentrations are equal at steady state, which these 9 brain samples contradict (PDF p6-7). This is the authors' interpretation of a handful of single samples, not a measured ke0.
- **Opioid audit (remifentanil):** MEAC / analgesic concentration: none. ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported (BIS only recorded). Potency / equianalgesic ratio: none. Measured whole-blood remifentanil converted to plasma (partition 0.89); brain is tissue (ng/g), not free concentration.

## Covariates
None modelled. The discussion attributes plasma mis-prediction to obesity, sarcopenia, drug interactions (propofol with remifentanil, anti-epileptics, steroids) and case mix, citing other studies; none was tested here.

## Caveats
- Only 5 patients per drug with paired brain samples, one sample each, at an uncontrolled time in surgery, with steady state assumed rather than shown. Epilepsy patients only, usually on anti-epileptic drugs.
- Brain ng/g compared with a predicted ng/mL; tissue density taken as about 1 (not stated).
- Remifentanil is unstable in blood and tissue by esterases; samples frozen at once, but the interval from sampling to freezing is not stated.
- The text says propofol brain concentration was higher than plasma in "five out of five" patients (PDF p6), then "four out of the five" (PDF p7); Table 1 supports four (patient with plasma 14.71 has brain 5.64, lower).
- The 14.71 ug/mL plasma propofol (PE 267.8%) and 20.01 ng/mL remifentanil (PE 233.5%) are outliers that move the group picture; medians are used by the authors.
- Whole blood to plasma conversion used animal red-cell partition coefficients.
