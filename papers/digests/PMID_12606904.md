# Bruhn 2003: EEG indices vs OAA/S depth, propofol + remifentanil

## Study
- Healthy volunteers, 10 men and 10 women, age 33.5 yr (median, range 20-43), weight 69.3 kg (median, range 50-120) (p621 text).
- Randomised, open-label. Propofol and remifentanil by target-controlled infusion (Harvard pump driven by STANPUMP). Propofol PK: Schnider 1998 (median subject). Remifentanil PK: Minto 1997 covariate-adjusted set (p622). Targets were plasma concentrations. The text calls the first-drug fall "effect compartment" in one place, so the target compartment is not fully clear (see Caveats).
- Phase 1: each volunteer got propofol or remifentanil alone, stepped up until PECO2 > 65 mmHg or apnoea > 60 s, then allowed to fall passively to 1 ug/ml propofol or 1 ng/ml remifentanil (the single-drug respiratory part is the Bouillon 2003 study, see PMID_14508307).
- Phase 2: stepwise up-down infusion of the first drug while the second drug was held constant (Tables 2 and 3). Propofol constant levels 0-4 ug/ml with remifentanil varied up to 24-40 ng/ml; remifentanil constant levels 0-4 ng/ml with propofol varied up to 12 ug/ml. Peak concentrations per volunteer are in Tables 2 and 3. Volunteer 15's remifentanil target was 2 ng/ml instead of the planned 1 ng/ml (Table 2 footnote).
- Each target held 10 min, then a modified OAA/S responsiveness scale (Table 1): 5 responds to soft voice, 4 to loud name, 3 to shaking and shouting, 2 only after laryngeal mask insertion, 1 only after laryngoscopy, 0 no response to laryngoscopy. Stimuli at 15-s intervals, one assessor.
- EEG: frontomastoid bipolar, Aspect A-1000 (BIS version 3.22, SEF95), raw EEG 128 Hz; approximate entropy computed off-line (N 1024 points per 8 s epoch, m 2, r 0.2 x SD). Indices averaged over the 60 s before assessment.
- Assays: propofol GC-MS (LOQ 0.1 ug/ml, linear 0.1-15), remifentanil GC-MS in whole blood (LOQ 0.25 ng/ml, linear 0.25-30). Interday CV 4.0-10.4% propofol, 8.5-9.3% remifentanil (p623-624).
- Analysis: Minto 2000 response-surface interaction model fitted in Excel Solver (maximising R2 against OAA/S); prediction probability (PK) with jackknife SE; significance set at 0.01 with Bonferroni.

## Model and parameters
Interaction model (Table 4, PDF p5, printed 625). The paper fits the fractional Emax form E = E0 (1 - U^g / (1 + U^g)), with U = (Uremi + Uprop) / U50(theta) and U50(theta) = 1 - b2 theta + b2 theta^2; theta = Uprop / (Uremi + Uprop).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: the modified OAA/S level (responsiveness to graded stimuli, up to laryngeal mask insertion and laryngoscopy), and EEG indices (BIS, SEF95, approximate entropy). Not analgesia scored on a pain scale and not ventilation.
- Interaction: synergistic, U50(0.5) 0.64 (target) and 0.74 (measured) (Table 4).
- Opioid audit: no MEAC or minimum effective analgesic concentration. No remifentanil ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak reported (the study used plasma-targeted TCI with published PK sets; no hysteresis fit). The C50 values above are OAA/S-model parameters, not an analgesic concentration; their unit is doubtful (see open_questions). No equianalgesic or potency ratio.
- Predictors performed worse for airway manipulation (PK 0.60-0.71) than for consciousness (0.92-0.95); differences between indicators were not significant (p626).

## Covariates
None modelled. Age and weight ranges are given for the cohort only.

## Caveats
- Healthy young volunteers only; the authors say the interaction parameters were optimised on this data set, so PK values are likely optimistic (p626).
- Fit to target and to measured concentrations give different parameters (gamma 23.1 vs 12.5).
- Concentration step order was fixed by tolerance, not fully randomised across steps; only the second-drug level was randomised.
