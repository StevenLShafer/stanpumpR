## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Concentrations are serum or plasma digoxin in ng/mL (assay not specified), end point mortality/toxicity rather than ventricular rate (p. 63, section 7.2.2, supportive text 2):
- Recommendation 2 (COR 2a, LOE B-NR): when serum digoxin is measured in AF, target <1.2 ng/mL.
- Toxicity (electrocardiographic) in a small early series above 2.0 ng/mL, almost certainly above 3.0 ng/mL; the "narrow therapeutic range" is usually cited as 0.8-2.0 ng/mL, varying by laboratory.
- DIG-trial post hoc analyses: 0.5-0.9 ng/mL associated with lower mortality and hospitalisation than >=1.0; >1.2 ng/mL harmful, particularly in women; in AF, <0.9 no excess death, 0.9-1.1 non-significant excess, >=1.2 a 56% increase in death; 0.5-0.8 ng/mL "safest" in HFrEF.
- Recommendations: IV digoxin 2a (B-R) for acute rate control when beta blockers and calcium channel blockers are ineffective or contraindicated (p. 60, Figure 17 on p. 62); long-term, 2a (B-R) in AF with HF symptoms (p. 63). Acute diltiazem lowered heart rate within 5 min against 3 h for IV digoxin in one 30-patient RCT (p. 61).
No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Renal elimination noted (Table 21). Pregnancy: serum digoxin levels may be unreliable because digoxin-like immunoreactive substances cross-react with digoxin antibodies (text layer, section on pregnancy; not read on the rendered page).

## Caveats
Guideline recommendations summarise clinical trials; the concentration thresholds come from post hoc analyses of DIG and cohort data. Only about 3 of 156 pages were read as images.
