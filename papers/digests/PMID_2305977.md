# Santos 1990: ropivacaine pharmacokinetics in nonpregnant and pregnant ewes

PDF pages 1-5 = printed pages 262-266.

## Study
- Animal study: 9 nonpregnant ewes and 8 pregnant ewes near term (gestational age 133 ± 1 days, term 148 days),
  chronically instrumented (femoral or jugular/carotid catheters; fetal carotid and jugular catheters in the pregnant
  ewes). Weight 64.5 ± 1.7 kg pregnant, 56.6 ± 3.0 kg nonpregnant (p. 263).
- Drug: 0.5% ropivacaine HCl IV. 3 nonpregnant and 1 pregnant ewe got 2.44 ± 0.08 mg/kg over 2 min. They developed
  tremors, so the remaining animals got the infusion over 4 min: 2.85 ± 0.13 mg/kg (nonpregnant, n = 6) and
  2.86 ± 0.09 mg/kg (pregnant, n = 7) (p. 263). The protocol doses were 2.5 and 3.0 mg/kg. The paper does not say
  whether the doses were HCl salt or base.
- Arterial blood at the end of infusion, then at 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 240 and 300 min; plasma;
  urine from 15 min. Gas chromatography (after Tucker), calibrated down to 5 ng/mL; day-to-day CV 9% at 0.05 µg/mL
  and 2% at 1 µg/mL (p. 263).
- Two-compartment model. Initial estimates came from the method of residuals, with an infusion correction (Loo and
  Riegelman). Final fit by SIMPLEX (Nelder-Mead) with Ottaway weighting. Groups compared with an unpaired t-test.
  Results are given as mean ± SEM. The 2-min and 4-min groups were pooled (p. 263-264).

The microconstants have no printed units; presumably they are per minute. The P values for Vdβ and Vdss were 0.053
and 0.056 (power 50% and 40%, p. 264). Only Cl differed significantly.

Peak plasma concentration was reached at the end of infusion: 4.48 ± 0.94 µg/mL (nonpregnant, 2-min infusion),
4.18 ± 0.45 µg/mL (nonpregnant, 4-min infusion), 7.84 µg/mL (the one pregnant ewe given the 2-min infusion) and
5.45 ± 0.57 µg/mL (pregnant, 4-min infusion) (p. 263). Peak fetal concentration came 1-2 min after the end of the
maternal infusion, 0.74 ± 0.04 µg/mL at 1 min (n = 4).

Urinary excretion of unchanged drug (Table 3) was 0.53 ± 0.13 mg (0.36 ± 0.10% of the dose) in nonpregnant ewes and
0.19 ± 0.05 mg (0.10 ± 0.02%) in pregnant ewes. The text layer of the Results reads "0.01%", but the rendered
Table 3 says 0.10.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, EC50 or concentration-effect relationship is reported.
- At about 2.4 mg/kg over 2 min, early local anaesthetic toxicity (tremors) appeared. Peak plasma concentrations in
  those animals were 4.48 µg/mL (nonpregnant) and 7.84 µg/mL (one pregnant ewe). No toxic threshold was defined.
- The paper found no significant change in maternal or fetal MABP, heart rate, pH or blood gases (Table 1).

## Covariates
- Pregnancy is the only covariate, tested as a group comparison. All parameters are per kg of total body weight.

## Caveats
- The animals are sheep, so species differences apply. The groups are small (9 and 8), and the 2-min and 4-min
  dosing regimens were pooled.
- The volume differences did not reach significance and the study had low power for them.
- Comparator: the pregnant ewes were compared with the study's own nonpregnant ewes, not with humans.
- Results are reported as SEM, not SD.
- The authors suggest a pregnancy-related inhibition of hepatic metabolism, but this is speculation (p. 264).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:17** says that in ewes, pregnancy halved total clearance (21.6 vs 45.1 mL/min/kg) and
  halved the terminal volume with an unchanged half-life, and that this animal finding is not transferable.
  - The paper agrees with all of this: Table 2 gives Cl 21.6 vs 45.1, Vdβ 2.03 vs 4.32 L/kg and t½β 74.7 vs 64.4 min.
  - One nuance is missing from the row: the volume reduction was not significant (P = 0.053).
  - No factor is applied (cl_factor and v_factor are NA), so no number enters a model. Verdict: **matches**.
- The row's `notes` say that the sheep data run opposite to the human hepatic-enzyme expectation, citing CYP1A2 and
  CYP3A4. The paper says nothing about CYP isoforms. A falling CYP1A2 would predict lower clearance, which is the
  same direction as these sheep, so the word "opposite" holds only for the CYP3A4 half of the argument. This is the
  registry's own reasoning, not a transcription error.
- Comparison with the library model (`R/drugs_ropivacaine.R:17`, Simon 2006 human IV tracer): the model has
  CL = 0.300 L/min, about 4.3 mL/min/kg at 70 kg (derived). The sheep values (45 and 22 mL/min/kg) are about 5-10 times
  higher. The species difference makes them useless for checking the model. The registry rightly does not apply them.
