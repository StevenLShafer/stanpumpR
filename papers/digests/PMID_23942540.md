# Contin 2013: lacosamide TDM and enzyme-inducing comedication

## Study
Prospective therapeutic-drug-monitoring cohort, Laboratory of Clinical Neuropharmacology, Bologna,
March 2011 - July 2012. 75 of 87 consecutive patients on lacosamide met the inclusion criteria: age >= 16 y,
>= 1 month on lacosamide, no dose change (lacosamide or comedication) in the preceding 3 weeks, venous
sample 08:00-09:00 before the morning dose with the last evening dose 20:00-21:00, and no non-antiepileptic
drug known to affect metabolism (printed p 850, PDF p 2). The study is a comparison of two comedication
groups, not a pharmacokinetic model: no compartmental analysis, no clearance, no volume, no half-life is
estimated here.

- **Group A (n = 33)**: on a strong CYP inducer - 11 carbamazepine, 10 phenobarbital, 11 phenytoin, 1
  carbamazepine + phenobarbital (printed p 850).
- **Group B (n = 42)**: no strong inducer - predominantly oxcarbazepine alone (n = 6) or with valproate
  (n = 4), valproate (n = 12), levetiracetam (n = 13), lamotrigine (n = 7).
- Groups comparable for age (36 +/- 16 vs 35 +/- 18 y), sex, weight (70 +/- 17 vs 68 +/- 13 kg), lacosamide
  daily dose (283 +/- 110 vs 286 +/- 101 mg/d), dose per kg (4.1 +/- 1.6 vs 4.3 +/- 1.6 mg/kg/d) and dosing
  frequency (median 2/day in both); all NS (Table 1, printed p 850).
- Assay: HPLC with spectrophotometric detection (Chromsystems kit); laboratory in the LGC Standards
  external quality scheme.
- Statistics: Mann-Whitney rank sum (non-normal data), Pearson correlation for concentration against dose.

Within group A, median L/D was **comparable among the carbamazepine, phenytoin and phenobarbital
subgroups**, which were then **pooled** for the main analysis (printed p 850, Results). Within group B,
valproate alone (L/D 1.30, 1.05-1.44, n = 12) did not differ from the non-inducer/non-inhibitor pool
(1.41, 1.23-1.99, n = 30), so group B was pooled too (printed p 851, PDF p 3).

## Model and parameters
The measured quantity is the morning trough plasma **concentration-to-weight-adjusted-dose ratio**
`L/D [(mg/L)/(mg/kg/d)]`.

- Group A (strong inducers): **0.94** (25th-75th percentile 0.75-1.27)
- Group B (no strong inducer): **1.35** (1.16-1.74)
- P < 0.001, Mann-Whitney (printed p 851, Figure 2 and Results text; confirmed on the rendered page).

Derived ratio **1.35 / 0.94 = 1.436**, i.e. inducers raise apparent lacosamide clearance ~1.44-fold
(equivalently L/D falls ~30%). The authors state the practical consequence as a **30% higher lacosamide
dose** on carbamazepine, phenobarbital or phenytoin (Conclusions, printed p 852).

Plasma lacosamide was linearly related to dose/kg within each group over 75-600 mg/d: group A r = 0.628,
group B r = 0.563, both P < 0.001 (Figure 1, printed p 851). No sex difference in L/D, overall or within
either group (printed p 851).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No seizure, EEG or any other pharmacodynamic end point; no effect site, ke0, tPeak or
concentration-effect relation. The paper is pharmacokinetic (trough concentration) only, which matches the
library's plasma-only antiseizure drugs.

## Covariates
Only comedication class (strong inducer vs not) is treated as a covariate, and dose is normalised by body
weight in the L/D definition. Age, sex, weight and dosing frequency were checked as potential confounders
and were comparable between groups; sex was separately shown not to affect L/D. No renal, hepatic or
pharmacogenetic covariate.

## Caveats
- The authors' own limitation: a relatively small sample with heterogeneous comedication, which "hampered
  meaningful statistical analyses of individual drug contributions" (printed p 852). The per-inducer effect
  is therefore **not** separately estimated; the x1.44 is a **pooled** any-inducer factor derived from three
  subgroups that were only shown to be *comparable* (a negative finding on n = 10-11 each), not shown equal.
- Large interpatient variability within each group (Figure 1), and the groups are observational, not
  randomised, although PubMed's publication type lists "Randomized Controlled Trial" (a PubMed indexing
  artefact: the Methods describe a prospective observational TDM cohort).
- L/D is a ratio of trough concentration to dose, so it tracks apparent oral clearance only under linear
  kinetics and unchanged bioavailability; the paper shows linearity over 75-600 mg/d but cannot separate F
  from CL.
- Lower comparator figures exist: the paper itself cites an unpublished population analysis of two phase 3
  trials giving ~25% lower exposure (ref 10, printed p 851), below this cohort's ~30%.
- Protein binding of lacosamide is called controversial (15% initially reported, ~90% in one patient study,
  refs 6-7, printed p 849); the plotted and measured quantity is total plasma drug either way.
