# Lien 1996: cisatracurium and metabolite PK under N2O/opioid/barbiturate

## Study
20 ASA 1-2 adults, 23-65 y, elective minor surgery, New York Hospital-Cornell (PDF p2). Randomised to a single rapid IV
bolus of 0.1 mg/kg (2 x ED95; n = 10; 36.8 +/- 12.3 y, 73 +/- 13 kg, 170 +/- 12 cm, 6 M / 4 F) or 0.2 mg/kg (4 x ED95;
n = 10; 38.9 +/- 7.5 y, 87 +/- 10 kg, 181 +/- 5 cm, 10 M) (Table 1, PDF p3). Midazolam, fentanyl, thiopental; 70% N2O;
oesophageal temperature 35.1-36.4 C; no volatile agent. Venous samples at 2-480 min (17 times); EDTA tubes, acidified
within 3 min, HPLC-fluorescence for cisatracurium, laudanosine and the monoquaternary alcohol (LLOQ 10 ng/mL; CV < 14%,
10-2000 ng/mL); monoquaternary acid assessed qualitatively. Noncompartmental (log-linear terminal slope, trapezoidal AUC,
Vss from AUMC) and a two-compartment NONLIN fit with elimination from both compartments, k20 fixed at the in vitro
plasma degradation rate 0.0237 /min (PDF p3, Eqs 1-2).

No dose differences were significant; noncompartmental Vss underestimated by 17.2 +/- 11.9% and 20.2 +/- 14.4% (PDF p5).
AUC 21.07 +/- 4.05 and 46.81 +/- 6.66 min.mcg/mL (ratio 2.23, dose-normalised P = 0.17). Cmax (2 min) 1,400 +/- 663 and
3,519 +/- 929 ng/mL (Fig 2 caption); dose-normalised 1,400 and 1,760 ng/mL (PDF p4). Individual V1, k10, k12, k21 not
tabulated.

Metabolites (Tables 3-4, PDF p6): laudanosine Cmax 38 +/- 21 and 103 +/- 34 ng/mL, t1/2 3.6 +/- 2.6 and 4.3 +/- 1.6 h,
AUC ratio to parent 0.47 and 0.54; monoquaternary alcohol Cmax 101 +/- 27 and 253 +/- 51 ng/mL (Table 4 prints the unit
as mg/mL; the text says ng/mL), t1/2 34.4 and 41.4 min. Monoquaternary acid not found, so ester hydrolysis is minor.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None modelled: no ke0, C50 or twitch data analysed. ED95 0.05 mg/kg quoted from earlier work (PDF p1).

## Covariates
None tested beyond dose group.

## Caveats
n = 10 per arm; the 0.2 mg/kg arm heavier, taller and all male; venous sampling; temperature slightly low; the two-
compartment model rests on assuming in vivo peripheral degradation equals the in vitro plasma rate (authors, PDF p6-7).
