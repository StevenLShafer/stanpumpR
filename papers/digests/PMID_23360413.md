# Tomson 2013: antiseizure drug pharmacokinetics in pregnancy (review)

## Study
Narrative review (Epilepsia critical review). PubMed search 1966 to July 2012, English, human studies, plus authors' files and
reference lists (PDF p. 2, printed 406). No new data and no pooled analysis; no individual-patient modelling. Numbers below
are the review's summaries of other studies, usually changes in total serum concentration from preconception or postpartum
baseline in the same women.

Lamotrigine (Table 2, PDF p. 5; text p. 408): concentrations fall on average 50-60% (monotherapy), most in mid-to-third
trimester; dose/concentration ratio up 295% by mid-gestation on monotherapy versus 60% with valproate; return to baseline
within 2-3 weeks postpartum. Table 2 is badly scrambled in the text layer and was not rendered; its per-study values are not
recorded here.

Physiology (p. 407): GFR rises "up to 50-80% of the prepregnancy rate" (the review's wording; read as a 50-80% increase),
starting soon after conception and declining late in the third trimester.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK-PD. Clinical end point is seizure control: in Pennell 2008 (36 lamotrigine monotherapy pregnancies) seizure risk rose
when concentrations fell by more than 35% from the individual's optimal preconception level (p. 411). No therapeutic ranges
are given; the authors prefer each woman's own preconception concentration as the reference.

## Covariates
Not modelled. Mechanisms named: induction of UGT (lamotrigine, MHD, valproate) and CYP; increased renal clearance
(gabapentin, levetiracetam, pregabalin, topiramate); falling albumin lowering total but not unbound concentrations
(phenytoin, valproate). Valproate co-medication blunts the lamotrigine change.

## Caveats
- Review; numbers are ranges from heterogeneous small studies, mostly total concentrations, not clearances, and not always
  dose-normalised. A concentration decline of x% implies a clearance factor of 1/(1 − x) only at constant dose and
  bioavailability (derived conversions below use that assumption).
- Pregabalin, lacosamide, tiagabine, vigabatrin, felbamate, retigabine, perampanel and eslicarbazepine: no pregnancy PK data
  (p. 411); the review only predicts a decline for renally cleared drugs.
