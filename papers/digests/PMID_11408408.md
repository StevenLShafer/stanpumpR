# Peyton 2001: V/Q inhomogeneity, N2O and oxygenation (gas exchange model)

## Study
Computer-modelling paper, no patients. A multi-compartment lung model of simultaneous exchange of O2, CO2 and inert gases (N2O, N2), with a log-normal distribution of ventilation or blood flow across compartments (log-e SD 0 to 1.75; 10 compartments suffice, 50 needed when collapse of low VAI/Q units is modelled; PDF p 2). Two ventilation conventions: a distribution of expired alveolar ventilation (VAE, "constant outflow") or of inspired ventilation (VAI, "constant inflow", with collapse of compartments whose VAE would be negative, their perfusion becoming shunt). Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction included after Dantzker (equation in the Appendix, PDF p 8).

Scenario (PDF p 2): maintenance anaesthesia, inspired 30% O2 with 70% N2O or 70% N2; VAE 4.1 L/min, cardiac output (Q) 4.8 L/min, VO2 250 mL/min, CO2 output 200 mL/min, N2O uptake 100 mL/min, all held constant; PaCO2 held at 45 Torr in the washout cases. Washout scenarios: inspired N2O 0, N2O elimination fixed at 400 or 100 mL/min. Dry barometric pressure 713 mmHg, 37 C, STPD volumes (Appendix).

## Model and parameters
Mass balance per compartment (Appendix, PDF p 7-9): VAI*PIG/PB - VAE*PAG/PB = 863*Q*(Cc'G - CvG) for each gas (Eq A1); for an inert gas with partition coefficient lambda, PAG = (VAI/Q*PIG + lambda*PvG)/(VAE/Q + lambda) (Eq A2); the partial pressures sum to PB (A4); PACO2 = 863*(CvCO2 - Cc'CO2)/(VAE/Q) in the VAE model (A5). O2 and CO2 dissociation by Kelman routines; base excess equations of Siggaard-Andersen. Perfusion with HPV: Q = Qmax*(1 - 0.6218*exp(-0.0146*PO2)) per compartment (printed form; the PO2 variable placement is garbled in the text layer, so the exact equation is not confirmed).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, or potency ratio. No drug effect is modelled; the end point is arterial PO2.
- PaO2 falls progressively as the log SD of Q rises from 0 to 1.75, less steeply with 70% N2O than 70% N2 (results, PDF p 2-3).
- The N2O benefit is smallest at low SD (about 4 Torr in a homogeneous lung, Discussion PDF p 3) and greatest, an increase of about 50%, at severe inhomogeneity (Fig 1).
- The benefit is largest at log SD of Q 1.0-1.5, which the authors say is the range seen in healthy anaesthetised patients (Discussion, PDF p 5, citing MIGET studies).
- The constant-inflow (VAI) model predicts a much smaller N2O benefit than the constant-outflow (VAE) model even with identical gas uptakes (abstract; PDF p 3, 5).
- Washout: PaO2 lower with N2O than N2 at all inhomogeneity levels, most at mild spread with 400 mL/min elimination, modestly with 100 mL/min (Fig 2, results PDF p 3).
- The authors state that an effect on arterial oxygenation is not expected below about 50% inspired N2O (Farhi and Olszowska, cited; Discussion, PDF p 5).
- Clinical comparison: Nunn's spontaneous-ventilation groups had PaO2 107 vs 81 Torr with N2O vs N2, cited and re-analysed by the authors (PDF p 6); controlled-ventilation groups showed no difference, which fits the constant-inflow model.

## Covariates
None; the independent variable is the log SD of the Q (or ventilation) distribution, with shunt and HPV as options.

## Caveats
Theoretical; no new patient data. Steady-state gas exchange is modelled; no other soluble agent in the inspired mixture; N2O uptake fixed rather than computed; the choice of VAE or VAI distribution determines the answer, and the authors infer from literature that spontaneous ventilation follows the VAE model and controlled ventilation the VAI model. The model was not validated against its own patient data in this paper.
