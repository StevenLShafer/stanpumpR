# Keranen 1994: entacapone single-dose PK (oral 5-800 mg, IV 25 mg) and red-cell S-COMT inhibition

## Study
Open, non-randomised, ascending-dose study in 12 healthy young men (mean age 24 y, range 22-28; mean height 179 cm; mean weight 73 kg, range 66-80), page 2 (printed 152). Single oral doses 5, 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, 800 mg (tablets, 200 mL water, fasting), given in increasing order with 2 days (5-100 mg) or at least 1 week (200-800 mg) washout. A 25 mg IV dose (1 mg/mL concentrate diluted to 25 mg/100 mL, infused at constant rate over 15 min) was given after the oral phase; 11 subjects completed it. Venous plasma sampled 10 min to 12 h after oral doses and to 8 h after IV. Assay: reversed-phase HPLC with electrochemical detection, linear 10-4000 ng/mL, LOQ 10 ng/mL, intra-assay CV <11% at LOQ and <6% above (plasma, EDTA, total drug). S-COMT activity in red cells was measured in 6 subjects after oral doses. Software: PCNONLIN (Metzler and Weiner), weights 1/C^2 (oral) and 1/C (IV); infusion-time correction by Loo-Riegelman.

## Model and parameters
Oral: one-compartment fit for 5-50 mg, two-compartment fit for 100-800 mg (PDF p.3-4). IV: two-compartment. No absorption rate constant (ka) or lag time is reported; absorption is described only by tmax and Cmax. Bioavailability f = AUCpo x Div / (AUCiv x Dpo). CL = Div/AUCiv.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. End point: inhibition of soluble COMT activity in red blood cells (pmol product/mg protein/min, 3,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid substrate), percent change from pre-dose baseline, n = 6 (PDF p.3-4). Reported: a fall of up to 17% after 5 mg; a maximum of 82% after 800 mg; the maximum occurred within 1 h; activity recovered within 4-8 h at all doses (Fig. 2, PDF p.3). Maximum inhibition versus log(dose): Y = 1.613 + 12.848 x log(dose), r2 = 0.989 (Fig. 3; log base not stated in the text). The authors state that the dependence of inhibition on plasma concentration was analysed by regression but give no result and no EC50, Emax, ke0 or hysteresis value. No MEAC, no effect-site rate constant and no potency ratio are reported. The authors caution that red-cell COMT may not track liver, gut-wall or kidney inhibition (animal data suggest stronger and longer gut inhibition).

## Covariates
None analysed (12 young men, one weight range). The authors speculate on age, because entacapone AUC was lower here than in parkinsonian patients in another study (Myllyla 1993).

## Caveats
Small n, young healthy men only, ascending fixed order (no randomisation), IV given in a later phase; terminal phase not captured at low oral doses and after IV; F and CL therefore uncertain; COMT inhibition measured in red cells only, in 6 of 12 subjects. Tablet dissolution may explain the tmax rise with dose (100 mg tablets used for higher doses).
