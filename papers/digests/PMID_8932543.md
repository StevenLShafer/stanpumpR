# James 1996: famotidine PK/PD in children

## Study
- 12 critically ill children (PICU/cardiac ICU, Arkansas Children's), 8 boys, 4 girls, 1.1-12.9 yr (mean 6.1 +/- 4.9), 7.0-74.0 kg (mean 27.6 +/- 21.2), receiving famotidine for stress-ulcer prophylaxis. Exclusions: oliguria, creatinine >1.5 mg/dL, albumin <2.5, ALT/GGT >300, hypotension, CYP inhibitors/inducers.
- Single dose famotidine 0.5 mg/kg IV over 15 min (per kg dosing). Arterial blood at 0.083, 0.167, 0.25, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 h after the end of infusion; urine in 4-h intervals to 24 h.
- Assay: HPLC (Kroemer and Klotz), plasma 0.4 mL, linear 25-1000 ng/mL, LLOD 10 ng/mL (urine 1.0 ug/mL), CV <9%. Serum/plasma total concentration.
- Fitting: Siphar/Base, weighting 1/calculated concentration, AIC model choice; biexponential best for each subject and for the mean curve (Fig. 1). PD: Siphar/D sigmoid Emax.
- Intragastric pH by nasogastric pH probe, read at the PK sampling times; effect = change in pH from the mean of three pre-dose readings.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: change in intragastric pH from pre-dose baseline (pH units), measured by nasogastric pH probe.
- 9 of 12 were responders (change >=2 pH units): maximal change 5.0 +/- 1.0 units at 3.3 +/- 1.5 h; duration of change 8.2 +/- 7.3 h (2.5-23.7 h). Pre-dose pH 1.9 +/- 0.5 in responders vs 6.1 +/- 0.8 in the 3 non-responders (patients 2, 3, 6); non-responders showed no change. No response when baseline pH >4.
- Sigmoid Emax acceptable in 5 of 9 responders (Table II, PDF p.5, printed p.52). Emax = incremental pH change; EC50 in ng/mL serum (total) famotidine:

```yaml
parameters:
  - {name: EC50, value: 25.97, unit: ng/mL, source: "Table II mean (SD 13.22), n=5", note: individual 21.46, 12.17, 32.46, 18.15, 45.6}
  - {name: Emax, value: 5.46, unit: pH units, source: "Table II mean (SD 1.12), n=5", note: individual 5.47, 5.74, 5.13, 7.05, 3.93}
```

- ke0: no value reported. The authors state that in the other 4 responders the fit could not collapse the counterclockwise hysteresis loop, so keo could not be estimated. No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak; hysteresis was recognised but not quantified. Gamma (Hill exponent) is not reported.
- Adult comparators quoted from refs 1 and 7 (not this study): EC50 26.0 and 32.6 ng/mL (healthy adults, adults with GI bleeding), Emax 7.3 and 7.8; EC for pH 4 25.4 ng/mL.
- Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Age and weight tested against Vdss and Cl by nonlinear regression: no significant relationship (small n, wide age range). No equations. Baseline gastric pH was the only determinant of PD response.

## Caveats
- n=12 critically ill children, no neonates or infants <1 yr; heterogeneous disease; one dose.
- EC50/Emax from only 5 patients with unquantified hysteresis; no ke0.
- Comparator for "higher": the authors say Cl (0.70) and Cl_R were "somewhat higher" than adult values from the literature (0.19-0.43 and 0.16-0.27 L/h/kg) and Vdss larger than healthy adults (0.94-1.4 L/kg); against two other pediatric studies (Kraus Cl 0.30, Nagita 0.48 L/h/kg) it is also higher. The comparator is published adult values, not a stanpumpR model.
- Mean Cl_R 0.45 in Table I vs 0.43 in text: internal inconsistency.
- Vdss mean is driven by patients 11 and 12 (3.94, 7.07 L/kg).
