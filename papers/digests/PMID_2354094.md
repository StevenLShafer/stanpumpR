# Pihlajamäki 1990: epidural bupivacaine at term against non-pregnant women

## Study
The study is a cross-sectional comparison of three groups of six women each, in Turku (p557).

| Group | Who | Age, y (mean, range) | Weight, kg (mean, range) | Height, cm | Bupivacaine, mg/kg | Total dose, mg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | healthy term pregnancy, caesarean section, no medication | 27.0 (23-35) | 71.3 (60-85) | 162.1 | 1.78 (SD 0.27) | 112.2 (18.7) |
| 2 | non-pregnant, under 65 y, uterine prolapse repair | 47.5 (38-60) | 70.5 (60-92) | 158.3 | 1.58 (0.13) | 99.4 (19.0) |
| 3 | non-pregnant, over 65 y, same surgery | 74.0 (67-85) | 72.4 (57-94) | 158.1 | 1.50 | 94.5 (16.6) |

- **Drug:** plain bupivacaine. Half was injected through the Tuohy needle at L2-3 and half 10 min later through the
  epidural catheter.
- **Fluids:** Ringer's lactate 1000 mL before and 1000 mL during the procedure.
- **Other drugs:**
  - Group 1: two women had ephedrine 3 mg. After delivery, women received diazepam 5 mg (n = 2) or fentanyl 50-100
    µg with droperidol (n = 4), and IM oxycodone postoperatively.
  - Groups 2-3: diazepam 5 mg. In group 3, one woman also had droperidol and one pethidine.
- **Sampling:** serum for 24 h. Urine was collected for 12 h in group 1 and 24 h in groups 2-3.
- **Assay:** HPLC for bupivacaine, N-desbutyl-bupivacaine (DBB) and 4'-hydroxy-bupivacaine (4-OH-B). Detection
  limit 10 µg/L; CV 1.8-7.4%. Conjugates were measured after Glusulase.
- **Analysis:** non-compartmental (Cmax, Tmax, terminal t½, trapezoidal AUC 0-24 h). Groups compared by
  repeated-measures ANOVA and t test.

- **No clearance or volume is estimated.** The authors say slow epidural absorption probably inflates the terminal
  t½ (12-15 h here, against 2-3 h after IV injection).
- **Urine:**
  - Less than 1% of the dose was excreted as unchanged bupivacaine (Table II).
  - Total urinary bupivacaine plus metabolites and conjugates was 2.5% (0-12 h) in group 1, and 3.2% and 2.6%
    (0-24 h) in groups 2 and 3 (Table V).
  - The Summary gives the range as 2.46-3.22%.
- **Metabolites:**
  - Serum DBB was significantly higher in the pregnant women (Fig. 2).
  - Unconjugated 4-OH-B was undetectable in their serum and urine.
  - Conjugated 4-OH-B in urine was similar across the groups (Table IV).
- **Serum bupivacaine time course** (Fig. 1): group 1 was higher than groups 2-3 at about 30-60 min (P < 0.05 and
  < 0.01).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured: no block assessment and no concentration-effect analysis.

## Covariates
Pregnancy and age, compared by group only. The conclusion is that pregnancy, but not age, affects disposition "to a
small extent".

## Caveats
- **n = 6 per group.** The groups differ in age (27 against 47.5 against 74 y), surgery and dose.
- **Normalisation not stated:** the "(n)" values are not simply value ÷ mg/kg (derived: 8.29/1.78 = 4.66 against a
  tabulated 5.16). They are probably means of individual normalised values.
- **Wording conflict:** the Discussion (p561) says there was no significant difference between the groups and that
  absorption was similar. Table I flags the raw Cmax of group 1 (P < 0.05), and Fig. 1 flags 30-60 min. The
  "no difference" holds for Tmax, t½ and the normalised values, not for raw Cmax.
- **Short AUC:** a 24-h AUC against a 12-15 h apparent t½ cannot give clearance.
- **Total drug only:** serum total bupivacaine, with no protein binding measured.
