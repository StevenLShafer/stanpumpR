# Caritis 2017: dosing frequency of sublingual buprenorphine in pregnancy

## Study
Three data sources, no new PK parameters (PDF pp1-2, printed 459.e1-e2):
1. **Re-analysis of an earlier PK study** (Bastian 2017, ref 5): 14 pregnant women on stable twice-daily (BID)
   sublingual buprenorphine for at least 7 days, 11 samples over a 12-h dosing interval, studied in the second
   and/or third trimester and again postpartum. Plasma LC-MS/MS, LLOQ 0.05 ng/mL, linear 0.05-50 ng/mL.
   Concentrations were dose-normalised (ng/mL per mg), the **median** dose-normalised curve multiplied by 4, 8 or
   12 mg to draw Figure 1. Table 1 (PDF p3, page image): second trimester n 7, age 27 ± 5, BMI 29 ± 4, 22 ± 2.5 wk,
   BID dose 8 ± 3.1 mg (2-12); third trimester n 11, 28 ± 5 y, BMI 29 ± 4, 34 ± 2.2 wk, 10 ± 3.7 mg (2-16);
   postpartum n 10, 29 ± 6 y, BMI 25 ± 5, 7 ± 4.2 wk postpartum, 8 ± 0 mg. Weight not given.
2. **Clinic data** (Magee-Womens Pregnancy Recovery Center, July 2014-April 2015): 62 pregnant women choosing their
   own dosing frequency; total daily dose 4-32 mg, median 16 mg (PDF p3). Figure 2 (PDF p4): QD 10 (16%), BID 10
   (16%), TID 28 (45%), QID 14 (23%).
3. **PBPK simulation** (Simcyp 15.1) of **nonpregnant** subjects, 100 virtual subjects, 6 mg/day given as 6 mg QD,
   3 mg BID or 2 mg TID (model from Kalluri/Zhang, ref 11; sublingual built on Ciraulo 2006, validated against
   Harris, McAleer, Compton, Greenwald; predicted AUC within 75-125% of observed for 4-24 mg) (PDF p2).

## Model and parameters
No PK parameters (no CL, V, t½, ka) are reported; the PBPK model is described elsewhere (ref 11). Numbers given:
- Table 2 (PDF p4, printed 459.e4, page image): hours with median dose-normalised concentration below 1 ng/mL in a
  12-h BID interval at steady state — second trimester 10 / 8 / 8 h (4 / 8 / 12 mg); third trimester 10 / 4 / 0;
  pregnancy average 10 / 6 / 4; postpartum 8 / 0 / 0.
- Table 3 (PDF p5, printed 459.e5): PBPK nonpregnant, hours below 1 ng/mL per 24 h — 6 mg QD 16.3; 3 mg BID 14.4;
  2 mg TID 10.8. Text (PDF p4): 6 mg QD above 1 ng/mL for the first 7.7 h; 24-h AUC stated as "26 ng/mL/h"
  (unit as printed; presumably ng·h/mL).
- Figure 3 (page image): simulated mean peak about 2.9 ng/mL (6 mg QD), about 1.9 (3 mg BID), about 1.55 (2 mg TID);
  read off the graph, approximate.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Threshold (MEAC-like, for withdrawal, not analgesia): 1 ng/mL total plasma buprenorphine**, taken from
  Greenwald 2003/2007 (refs 8, 9), described as the concentration needed to prevent withdrawal / sustain
  satiety via mu-opioid receptor occupancy in **nonpregnant** opioid-dependent volunteers (PDF p1, p4). Not
  measured in this study; assay matrix for the threshold is plasma (total). The authors state no equivalent
  pregnancy data exist and the pregnant threshold may be higher or lower (PDF pp4-5).
- Receptors become increasingly available about 4 h after a dose and withdrawal begins (citing Greenwald; PDF p5).
- Cited, not measured: SL half-life in nonpregnant subjects 24-69 h in one study (ref 6); after 24 mg once daily,
  nonpregnant concentrations fall below 1 ng/mL within 12 h (Ciraulo, ref 12) (PDF p5).
- No ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, analgesic MEAC or equianalgesic/potency ratio.

## Covariates
Pregnancy state only (second trimester, third trimester, postpartum), descriptive. The comparison is
pregnant against **postpartum** (largely the same women; 9 of 10 postpartum women were studied in pregnancy),
not against nonpregnant controls; the PBPK arm is nonpregnant and is not compared quantitatively with pregnancy.

## Caveats
Small n (7/11/10); median curves from dose-normalised data assume linearity; doses 4/8/12 mg are scaled, not
observed per group; no clearance ratio is given. Internal inconsistency: text says third-trimester 12 mg median
concentrations dipped below 1 ng/mL briefly around hour 10, but Table 2 gives 0 h. Text p3 says PBPK
nonpregnant subjects "have higher plasma concentrations than nonpregnant subjects" (an evident slip for
pregnant). The 1 ng/mL threshold is borrowed from nonpregnant data and assumed.
