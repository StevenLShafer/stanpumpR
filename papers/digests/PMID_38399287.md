# Luo 2024: semi-PBPK model of CES1 substrates in cirrhosis

## Study
Simulation study (no new patients). A semi-PBPK model (stomach, intestinal wall and lumen, portal vein, liver, kidney, systemic compartment; coded in WinNonlin 8.1) was built for 9 CES1 substrates: enalapril, benazepril, cilazapril, perindopril, temocapril, oseltamivir (prodrugs), flumazenil, pethidine, remimazolam (direct-acting) and the active metabolites. Verified first on healthy-subject literature profiles, then extrapolated to Child-Pugh A/B/C by changing physiology (PDF p. 5, Table 1). 1000 virtual individuals per population; parameters varied randomly within 80-120% of typical (p. 7). Success criterion: simulated AUC or Cmax within 0.5-2-fold of observed, or observations within the 5th-95th percentile band (p. 8). Observed data are 42 literature studies listed in Table 3 (pp. 8-9; doses, n 3-59).

## Model and parameters
Hepatic CES1 clearance in cirrhosis: CLint,CI = CLint,HT x fCES1 x fliver (eq. 1, p. 4), where fCES1 = CES1 content ratio and fliver = liver volume ratio. Free fraction: fu,p,CI = 1/(1 + ((1-fu,p,HT)/(fu,p,HT)) x (Pprot,CI/Pprot,HT)) (eq. 3). Vsys,CI = Vsys,HT x fu,p,CI/fu,p,HT (eq. 4, free apparent volume assumed unchanged). Renal CLint,K,CI = CLint,K,HT x GFR,CI/GFR,HT (eq. 5). Peff,CI = Peff,HT x (lactulose/rhamnose ratio CI/HT) (eq. 6). Healthy reference subject is a 70 kg "ideal" adult (p. 22).

Physiology, Table 1 (p. 5), normal / CP-A / CP-B / CP-C: hepatic arterial flow 300 / 390 / 486.9 / 1020 mL/min; portal vein flow 1150 / 1046.5 / 690 / 636.3 mL/min; liver volume 1690 / 1368.9 / 1098.5 / 895.7 mL; GFR 105 / 82 / 82 / 82 mL/min; albumin 44.7 / 36.2 / 30.4 / 26.3 g/L; alpha1-acid glycoprotein 0.8 / 0.57 / 0.52 / 0.46 g/L; CES1 2.45 / 2.45 / 1.715 / 0.735 mg/g liver (i.e. CP-B 70%, CP-C 30% of normal, CP-A unchanged, p. 2); CYP2B6 17 / 17 / 15.3 / 13.6 pmol/mg.

Drug parameters are in Table 2 (p. 6, a rotated table the text layer scrambles; per-drug values below are from the text where it is unambiguous only): pethidine free fraction in healthy plasma 0.418 (p. 11), oral bioavailability 48-56% (p. 11), CES1-mediated CLint 1.56 or 5.382 and CYP2B6-mediated CLint 261 or 356 (two sources, units uncertain in the scrambled table); remimazolam protein binding about 92% (p. 10); flumazenil protein binding about 40% (p. 10).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD, no effect-site model: no ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, EC50, MEAC or analgesic concentration, and no equianalgesic or potency ratio is reported. (Pethidine is an opioid; the paper only says cirrhosis raises its plasma level and cites the clinical observation of increased CNS toxicity, p. 22.) The paper notes that for enalapril/perindopril, ACE inhibition was similar in cirrhotic and healthy subjects despite higher parent-drug levels (pp. 21-22).

## Covariates
Child-Pugh class only (via Table 1). No sex, weight, age or race effects; the authors list that as a limitation (p. 22).

## Caveats
- AUCR (cirrhosis/healthy) was poorly predicted for benazepril, temocaprilat, perindopril, perindoprilat; predicted AUCR for flumazenil and pethidine was lower than observed (p. 21).
- Pethidine: the oral prediction in cirrhosis fell outside the band (p. 17). Healthy pethidine clearance was under-predicted: Table 11 observed/predicted CL ratios 1.13-1.67 (observed 0.56-1.32 L/min, predicted about 0.50-0.85 L/min; pp. 15) (table scrambled, ranges approximate).
- Flumazenil healthy CL observed 0.72-0.90 L/min vs predicted about 0.45-0.50 (Table 10, p. 15, obs/pre 1.50-2.01 in healthy rows) (scrambled).
- Remimazolam: the authors expected AUCR of 0.76 (CP-B) and 0.61 (CP-C) from the model, i.e. LOWER exposure in cirrhosis, because higher hepatic arterial flow and free fraction offset reduced CES1 (p. 22); no observed cirrhosis AUCR could be computed.
- Simulations assume etiology-independent "ideal" patients; CES1 genetics and sex differences ignored (p. 22).

## Verification against stanpumpR
No repo location cites this paper, so there is nothing to check for transcription. Independent comparisons with models the library has:
- **pethidine** (R/drugs_pethidine.R:7-9, Bjorkman 2003 kinetics): clearance cl1 = V1ref x k10 = 18.1 x 0.042131 = 0.76 L/min for a 70 kg adult (derived). That lies inside the paper's healthy predicted-and-observed range (about 0.50-0.85 predicted, 0.56-1.32 observed, Table 11, scrambled), so the library is consistent with it. The library has no hepatic-impairment factor for pethidine.
- **remimazolam** (R/drugs_remimazolam.R:17-44, Eleveld 2025): the file deliberately ignores the Pugh-Child effect (a V3 increase, lines 21-23). This paper's mechanism is different (CES1 content down to 70%/30% in CP-B/C) and it predicts lower remimazolam exposure; it is not a validated clearance factor and was not compared with Eleveld's terms. cannot verify.
- **flumazenil** (R/drugs_flumazenil.R:86-92, Fiset 1995): cl1 1.12 L/min; paper reports healthy observed total CL about 0.7-0.9 L/min (scrambled Table 10), a different population and method; no discrepancy asserted.
- enalapril, enalaprilat and benazepril have library models (R/drugs_enalapril.R, drugs_enalaprilat.R, drugs_benazepril.R) from other sources; this paper's cirrhosis CES1 ratios could inform a hepatic-impairment adjustment but none is implemented here.
