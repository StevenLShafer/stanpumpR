# Petrenaite 2009: oxcarbazepine MHD concentration/dose ratio and seizures in pregnancy

## Study
- Short communication; retrospective chart review, two Copenhagen-area epilepsy clinics (Glostrup n = 8, Rigshospitalet
  n = 5), pregnancies 1999-2004 (p. 246, PDF p2).
- 16 consecutive pregnancies in 13 women screened; entry required a pre-pregnancy MHD level plus levels in at least two of
  three trimesters, so two patients were excluded: **11 women, 14 pregnancies** (three women had two pregnancies,
  Table 2 footnote). Mean age 28.1 y (range 25-37). Weight not reported.
- 10 women on oxcarbazepine (OXC) monotherapy; 1 (pregnancy 14) on OXC + topiramate 300 mg/day, unchanged in pregnancy.
- OXC pre-pregnancy mean dose 1650 mg/day (range 600-3900); doses were raised in 12 of 14 pregnancies, on average 1.5-fold,
  aiming to hold the pre-pregnancy MHD level (p. 246-247).
- Samples: steady-state trough, before the morning dose; pre-pregnancy, each trimester (TM1 < 14 g.w., TM2 14-28, TM3
  29-42) and once 4-8 weeks postpartum; several samples in one period were averaged.
- Assay: **total** plasma MHD (10-monohydroxy derivative, racemic; enantiomers not reported) by HPLC (Volosov 2000).
  Concentrations in µmol/L.
- Concentration-to-dose ratio CDR = MHD (µmol/L) × 1000 / OXC dose (mg). Paired Student's t-test.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model; non-compartmental CDR comparisons against each woman's own pre-pregnancy CDR.

Results (text p. 247, PDF p3, confirmed on the rendered page; Fig. 2 on PDF p4 shows them as bars with SD):

| Period | CDR change vs pre-pregnancy | p |
|---|---|---|
| TM1 | −26.2% | < 0.01 |
| TM2 | −36.5% | < 0.01 |
| TM3 | −38.2% | < 0.01 |
| Postpartum (4-8 wk) | +7.3% vs pre-pregnancy, n.s. | |

TM1 vs TM3 and TM3 vs postpartum also significant (p < 0.05, p < 0.01). Despite the dose increases, mean MHD
**concentration** fell 17% (TM1), 20% (TM2), 12% (TM3) from pre-pregnancy (p. 247). CDR was almost back to baseline
4-8 weeks after delivery.

Table 1 (PDF p2, rotated; read on the rendered page) gives per-pregnancy doses and MHD concentrations. Re-computing the TM3
CDR ratio per pregnancy from Table 1 (derived: (C_TM3/D_TM3)/(C_PreP/D_PreP), 14 pregnancies) gives ratios 0.48-0.87,
mean 0.616, i.e. a 38.4% fall, reproducing the paper's 38.2%. The mean of the reciprocals (a clearance ratio) is 1.66
(derived), geometric mean 1.64 (derived).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. End point: seizure frequency from records, trichotomised (no change, < 50% rise, > 50% rise).
Seizures worsened in 9 of 14 pregnancies (64.3%) in 8 women; frequency at least doubled in 7 (50%). Mean MHD fall
was 13% where there was no deterioration, 20% with < 50% rise and 22% with > 50% rise: a trend, not significant
(Fig. 1, PDF p3). No therapeutic MHD range, no ke0, no MEAC, no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Pregnancy trimester only. No weight, renal function or co-medication analysis (one topiramate patient included).

## Caveats
- Retrospective, small (14 pregnancies), records-based seizure counts; doses were changed during pregnancy, hence CDR.
- Total racemic MHD only; the S-enantiomer (eslicarbazepine) was not measured separately.
- Trough samples; CDR in the units above is not a clearance, but under linear kinetics and stable dosing interval its
  reciprocal ratio approximates a CL/F ratio.
- The 38.2% is a mean of per-pregnancy percentage changes (my recomputation agrees), so 1/(1 − 0.382) = 1.62 is the
  reciprocal of a mean, slightly below the mean per-pregnancy clearance ratio (1.66, derived).
- Postpartum sample only 4-8 weeks after delivery; three pregnancies had toxicity symptoms postpartum.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Header, 38.2% lower in TM3, 14 pregnancies** (`R/drugs_eslicarbazepine.R:64-65`): paper p. 247 (rendered PDF p3) gives
  CDR −38.2% in TM3 vs pre-pregnancy, 14 pregnancies in 11 women. **Matches.** "Dose-corrected" is the right description
  (CDR, not concentration; the raw concentration fell only 12% because doses were raised).
- **Factor arithmetic** (`:71`): 1/(1 − 0.382) = 1.618, written 1.62. **Matches.** Assumes CL/F ∝ 1/CDR (linear kinetics,
  same dosing interval), which is reasonable for MHD; the per-pregnancy mean clearance ratio would be 1.66 (derived),
  immaterial against the chosen 1.6.
- **Comparator**: the paper's baseline is each woman's own pre-pregnancy oxcarbazepine CDR. The repo applies the ratio to
  Falcao's non-pregnant eslicarbazepine-acetate model as a surrogate; the header and registry already say this is a
  surrogate from racemic MHD with low confidence. The paper measured total racemic MHD, not the S-enantiomer; the
  surrogacy rests on Mazzucchelli 2006, not on this paper. No discrepancy, but the paper itself cannot confirm that
  eslicarbazepine changes in the same proportion.
- **Mechanism statements** (`:74-76`): glucuronidation and ~40% binding appear in this paper's discussion (p. 248) as
  literature statements. Consistent.
- **Pregnancy reference string** (`:150-152`): citation correct (Epilepsy Res 2009;84:245-249).
- **Registry row** (`pregnancyRegistry.csv:108`): finding, n = 14, comparator and design "paired NCA" consistent with the
  paper. `evidence_read` says "abstract"; the full text has now been read and supports the row.
- **Help** (`inst/help/drugs/eslicarbazepine.md:15`): that line is the therapeutic band paragraph; the help page in this
  checkout neither cites Petrenaite nor describes the pregnancy factor. Not a defect in the numbers; the location
  appears stale.
- No library oxcarbazepine model exists (`R/drugs_*` and `drugDefaults_global.csv` list eslicarbazepine only), so there
  is no MHD model to compare against.
