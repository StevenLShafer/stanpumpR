# Lopes 2026: ESL pregnancy outcomes, global safety database

## Study
Descriptive pharmacovigilance review by the manufacturer (Bial). Global safety database (Argus Safety), clinical-trial (CT) and post-marketing (PMS) cases with eslicarbazepine acetate (ESL; Zebinix, Exalief, Aptiom, BIA 2-093) exposure, data cut-off 30 September 2025, plus an EMBASE/MEDLINE literature search from 1 May 2009 (launch) to 30 September 2025. Cases selected by pregnancy, neonate age group and MedDRA HLGT/HLT codes; excluded: no ESL use, no pregnancy exposure, cumulative literature, EURAP-reported cases (data property), breastfeeding-only, paternal exposure (one case, discussed only). Duplicate maternal and neonatal reports counted once. Classification per EMA GVP guidance (PDF p.2).
Exposure estimate: worldwide ex-factory sales 21 April 2009 to 30 September 2025, 1 tablet = 1 patient-day (dose strength ignored); text prints "1018,386 patient-years" (see open question) plus about 6000 subjects in clinical development (PDF p.3).
No pharmacokinetic, concentration or dose-adjustment data of any kind.

## Model and parameters
None. Counts only (Fig. 1, PDF p.2, read on the page image):
```yaml
parameters: []   # no PK or PD parameters; the counts below are case numbers, not model values
```
- 7134 initial cases (970 CT + 6164 PMS); 253 after selection criteria; 94 further exclusions (54 EURAP, 13 duplicates, 10 non-valid or cumulative literature, 8 not related to exposure, 3 CT screening failure due to pregnancy, 3 no temporal relationship, 3 CT carbamazepine exposure only, 1 breastfeeding only); 159 included.
- 91 with known outcome, 68 unknown: 51 normal live births (56%), 13 terminations (14%), 27 adverse outcomes (30%) = 13 miscarriages, 8 congenital anomalies, 4 neonatal adverse effects, 2 premature births (Fig. 1B percentages 14, 9, 4, 2).
- Of the 27 adverse outcomes, 16 were add-on to other antiseizure medicines rather than ESL monotherapy (abstract). Congenital anomalies: one monotherapy case, five polytherapy, two not reported (PDF p.6). Miscarriages: four no ASM information, three monotherapy, six polytherapy; all but one in the first trimester (PDF p.6). 43% of cases could not be classified for missing information (Discussion).
- Table 1 (PDF pp.3-5) lists per-case ESL doses in mg/day, with some cases flagged as overdose; its cell layout is garbled in the text layer, so no per-case dose is recorded here.
- Authors state that only 18 ESL exposures are documented across the six major pregnancy registries (Discussion).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None reported (no ke0, concentration, EC50 or effect measurement). Opioid audit: not applicable.

## Covariates
None modelled; descriptive text notes polytherapy and overdose as additional risk factors. No dose-response or exposure-response analysis.

## Caveats
Spontaneous reports with missing data (43% unclassifiable), no denominator, possible under- or selective reporting, no comparator group, EURAP-reported cases excluded, so no incidence, causality or comparative risk can be derived. 56% normal live births is not a rate. The authors say no consistent pattern of congenital anomalies was seen; this is not a pharmacokinetic finding. The sponsor wrote and funded the analysis (all authors employed by Bial).
