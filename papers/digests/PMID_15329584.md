# Shibutani 2004: fentanyl PK model accuracy and "pharmacokinetic mass" in lean and obese patients

## Study
Retrospective model evaluation (PDF p.2-3). 109 adults having major elective surgery: Group L n = 70 (BMI < 30, TBW < 85 kg; mean 69 kg, BMI 24), Group O n = 39 (BMI > 30, TBW >= 85 kg; mean 125 kg, BMI 44). 465 measured/predicted pairs (277 L, 188 O). Fentanyl 1-2 ug/kg induction, then 0.05-0.07 ug/kg/min for 60-75 min, 0.03-0.05 for 1-2 h, 0.02-0.03 afterwards; postoperative 0.5-2.0 ug/kg/h, up to 31 h. Residual arterial blood-gas samples (4 stages: <2 h, 2-5 h, 5-10 h, 10-31 h). Assay: radioimmunoassay of plasma/serum, LLOQ 0.1 ng/mL, CV 8.42% at 0.25 ng/mL. Predictions from STANPUMP with the Shafer 1990 and Scott and Stanski 1987 parameter sets. PE = (Cpm - Cp)/Cp x 100 (negative = over-prediction). Nonlinear regression (PK Analyst) of PE on TBW; random training/testing split (250/215 samples). Steady-state clearance from postoperative constant infusions in 16 lean and 10 obese patients: Cl (mL/kg/min) = infusion (ug/kg/min) / steady-state concentration (ug/mL), times TBW.

Pharmacokinetic mass (suggested dosing weight) = 52 / [1 + PE-Shafer-reg/100] (Shafer; reference 52 kg where PE is about 0) or 82 / [1 + PE-Scott-reg/100] (Scott; reference 82 kg). Table 6 (p.611, image-confirmed), Shafer-derived, TBW kg: PE-Shafer-reg %, correction factor, PK mass kg:

(The printed table lists 1/correction factor as 1.00, 1.23, 1.59, 1.79, 1.92, 2.00, 2.04, 2.08.) Slope of PK mass vs TBW is about 0.65 from 52 to 100 kg; the curve flattens above 140 kg.

Table 4 (p.609, image-confirmed) accuracy, Group L (n 277) / Group O (n 188):
- Cp-Shafer: mean PE -17 / -45, median PE -19 / -45, SD 23 / 20, within 30%: 64 / 24, median APE 23 / 45.
- Cp-Shafer-reg (corrected): mean PE 1 / -3, median -4 / -7, within 30%: 75 / 55, median APE 18 / 28.
- Cp-Scott: mean PE 15 / -23, median 11 / -25, within 30%: 66 / 49, median APE 22 / 31.
- Cp-Scott-reg: mean PE -1 / -3, median -3 / -9, within 30%: 76 / 51, median APE 18 / 29.
Shafer model PE is zero at about 52 kg; Scott's at about 82 kg. Mean PE-Shafer by stage (Table 3, text layer only) is more negative in stage 4 (10-31 h) than stages 1 and 3.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic data. No MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration, no ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak or hysteresis, and no equianalgesic or potency ratio are reported (checked the whole text). Concentrations are plasma/serum (arterial, total, radioimmunoassay). The Discussion cites Scott and Stanski 1987 as finding age affects fentanyl PD more than PK; that is a citation, not a result here.

## Covariates
Total body weight, BMI and BSA each show a strong nonlinear relation with the Shafer model's PE (R2 0.689, 0.697, 0.656); lean body mass (r -0.234; Bouillon-Shafer cap applied above the LBM peak) and ideal body weight (r 0.014) show weak or no relation. Age (r 0.134) and sex (Group L P 0.086, Group O P 0.923) had no significant effect on PE-Shafer.

## Caveats
Authors' own: sampling times were set by clinical blood-gas needs, not PK design; no early (induction) data, so the effect of obesity on early distribution is not established; major surgery (aortic cross-clamp, blood loss) may alter kinetics. Also: clearance data come from only 26 patients at about 24 h postoperatively and steady state is assumed from simulation; PK mass is built from the bias of a model rather than fitted PK; the exponential PE fit has a floor (about -54%) that fixes the plateau at about 108 kg; Group O is both heavier and younger (47 vs 60 y) and differently sexed. Predictions used STANPUMP's unscaled Shafer and Scott parameter sets, not the library's current model.

## Verification against stanpumpR
Not applicable (not cited). For information, R/drugs_fentanyl.R now uses Bae 2020 (V1 10.1, V2 26.5, V3 206 L; CL 0.704, Q2 2.38, Q3 1.49 L/min at 70 kg; volumes x (WT/70)^1.23, clearances x (WT/70)^0.313; header lines 27-37, constants lines 77-85) evaluated at the pharmacokinetic weight (lines 99-104). Comparison with Table 5 (derived, with the total-weight switch off, i.e. wt = TBW): lean 68.5 kg gives CL 0.704 x (68.5/70)^0.313 = about 0.70 L/min, against Shibutani's 718 mL/min (about 3% different); obese 117.3 kg gives about 0.83 L/min against 986 mL/min (about 16% lower in the library). With the default switch on (pkWeight from fat-free mass, which depends on height and sex not given per patient here) the library's value for an obese patient will be lower still, so the library probably under-predicts clearance relative to Shibutani's obese group, i.e. over-predicts concentrations, the same direction as the finding. Not computed for specific patients; this is a direction check, not a verdict. The paper's unscaled Shafer 1990 model is what the library used until the 2019 pooled set and is not the current model, so the headline finding (over-prediction in obesity) applies to the old unscaled model, not directly to Bae. Library MEAC 0.6 ng/mL (line 114) and tPeak 3.6 min (line 117) have no counterpart in this paper.
