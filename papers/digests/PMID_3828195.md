# van den Ouweland 1987: steady-state naproxen, total and unbound, in active RA vs healthy men

## Study
- Patients: 8 in-patients with active RA (6 F, 2 M), age 62 +/- 3 y, serum albumin 28 +/- 2 g/L, creatinine
  clearance 85 +/- 22 mL/min, on naproxen 500 mg bid (Naprosyn 250 mg tablets) >= 14 days; various comedication
  (Table 1).
- Volunteers: 8 healthy men, 24 +/- 3 y, albumin 45 +/- 2 g/L, naproxen 500 mg bid at 08:30 and 20:30, studied on
  day 4.
- Morning dose with breakfast; samples 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 8, 12 h; urine 0-12 h.
- Assay: HPLC with spectrofluorimetric detection (total, CV 1.2% over 20-120 mg/L); unbound by equilibrium
  dialysis 5.5 h at 37 C, pH 7.4 (CV 4.2% over 0.03-0.3 mg/L).
- Noncompartmental: AUC(0,12) trapezoidal; CL/F = 500 mg / AUC(0,12); lambda from last three points; V/F =
  (CL/F)/lambda per kg.

(derived: average steady-state total concentration in volunteers 896/12 = 74.7 mg/L; unbound fraction ~0.08% in
volunteers, ~0.3% in patients from AUCu/AUC.)

V/F (r = -0.85) and CL/F (r = -0.76) inversely correlated with serum albumin. Urinary recovery of naproxen +
desmethylnaproxen conjugates over 12 h: 54% (patients) and 59% (volunteers) of the dose, implying unchanged
absorption. Authors cite Runkel 1974/1976 for less-than-proportional AUC at high doses (albumin saturation).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured; authors speculate the higher unbound concentrations may matter if effect follows unbound drug.

## Covariates
Serum albumin (correlation only); disease, age and sex confounded between groups.

## Caveats
n = 8 per group; groups differ in age, sex and disease; t1/2 from the last three points of a 12 h interval
underestimates a longer terminal phase; patients' comedication.
