# Allott 1973: Variation with temperature of inhaled-anaesthetic solubilities

## Study
Literature analysis (Cardiff/Aberdeen) of the temperature dependence of Ostwald solubility coefficients, from over 50 papers giving nearly 200 temperature coefficients for 16 of 18 agents in water, saline, olive oil, blood and some tissues of various species. A coefficient was calculated only if one investigator measured the agent in one medium at two or more temperatures spanning at least 10 C and including 37 C; the two most widely separated temperatures within 0-45 C were used. Results are in per cent change of Ostwald coefficient per degree C at 37 C (mostly negative: solubility falls as temperature rises). Albumin-solution results were excluded as anomalous. Aqueous basis: 54 coefficients for water, 31 blood, 16 other liquids (saline, serum, plasma, CSF), 4 brain, 2 muscle (PDF p3, printed 296); no systematic difference between these media was found (paired t-tests), so water's coefficient is assumed to apply to all aqueous media, though the authors say data for brain, heart, kidney, liver and muscle are very much wanted.

## Model and parameters
Table II (PDF p2, printed 295; confirmed on page image): mean temperature coefficient (%/C at 37 C) in water and aqueous media, with 95% limits and degrees of freedom, and in oil and fat; Ostwald coefficients at 37 C. Nitrous oxide: water/aqueous -2.33 (limits +/-0.63, 4 df), oil and fat: no measured value (blank), Ostwald water 0.47, oil 1.4. Other rows (aqueous / oil): chloroform -3.76/-4.54; cyclopropane -2.11/-2.18; diethyl ether -4.89/-3.39; halothane -4.01/-4.36; methoxyflurane -4.18/-4.58; xenon -2.17/-0.96; krypton -1.72/-0.40; nitrogen -1.12/+0.28; fluroxene -3.88/-3.61; trichloroethylene -3.94/-4.53; ethylene -1.94 aqueous; ethyl chloride -3.29 aqueous (1 df); enflurane and Forane (isoflurane) have no measured coefficient.

Regressions of coefficient k (%/C) on log10 of the Ostwald coefficient lambda (Figure 1, PDF p4 text): kw = -1.2750 log10(lambda_w) - 3.3601 (water); ko = -1.2897 log10(lambda_o) - 0.9389 (oil and fat). Quadratic/cubic terms not significant (no curvature); P<0.000005 for the fit.

Table III (PDF p3, printed 296; image): coefficients predicted from these regressions, %/C: divinyl ether water -3.55; enflurane water -3.22, oil -3.51; ethylene oil -1.07; Forane (isoflurane) water -3.10, oil -3.50; nitrous oxide oil -1.13 (water blank, as measured).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None; a physical-chemistry paper. No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio. (The authors link the coefficients to potency changes in hypothermia only as motivation.)

## Covariates
Temperature (about 15-40 C range of the underlying data), medium (aqueous vs oil/fat), agent solubility.

## Caveats
Wide scatter between authors for a single agent and solvent (example Table I, diethyl ether); oil values for several agents are extrapolated from regression, not measured; brain and muscle coefficients rest on 4 and 2 values; high albumin concentrations behave differently; extrapolation outside the underlying range is open to question.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Aqueous 2.33%/C, 95% limits 0.63: **matches** Table II (image). Sign and direction: coefficient is negative (solubility falls with temperature), code uses exp(-slope x (T-37)) so solubility rises as the body cools: correct. Percent-per-degree is applied as a log-slope (exp), a small approximation the Appendix allows for few-degree changes; the paper's own large-change form is also exponential.
- 'Mean of four measured coefficients': the table gives 4 degrees of freedom, not a count; probably five values. Unresolved (open question); no effect on the value.
- Fat 1.13%/C: **matches** Table III (image), correctly described in the code as predicted, not measured.
- Blood, vessel-rich group and muscle take the aqueous value: supported for blood (31 blood coefficients show no difference from water) and the authors' general assumption for aqueous media, but only 4 brain and 2 muscle coefficients exist across all agents, and none is nitrous-oxide-specific: an assumption the paper endorses with reservations, not a measurement.
- Independent comparison for the volatile agents (not cited): Zhou's blood slopes in R/physiology.R:593-595 (desflurane 3.02, sevoflurane 2.95, isoflurane 3.82 %/C) sit in the range of Allott's aqueous values for related agents (halothane -4.01 measured, Forane -3.10 predicted, enflurane -3.22 predicted, methoxyflurane -4.18). Desflurane and sevoflurane are not in this paper. No discrepancy claimed.
