# Rave 2001: diabetic nephropathy and SC regular insulin vs lispro, PK and glucose clamp

## Study
- Double-blind, randomised, two-way crossover, 8-h euglycaemic (5 mmol/L) glucose clamp (Biostator), arterialised
  venous blood (hand box at 55 °C) (PDF p2, printed 887).
- 12 type 1 diabetic patients with overt nephropathy (NP; proteinuria >500 mg/24 h and/or serum creatinine >1.5 mg/dL
  [122 µmol/L]) and 12 type 1 diabetic controls with normal renal function (DC); age 20-50 y inclusion; negative
  insulin-antibody screen. Table 1 (PDF p2), means ± SD:

| Sex F/M | 6/6 | 3/9 | <0.05 |
| Age (y) | 41 ± 5 | 34 ± 10 | <0.01 |
| Weight (kg) | 72 ± 16 | 76 ± 10 | 0.45 |

| Proteinuria (mg/24 h) | 1382 ± 864 | 34 ± 12 | <0.01 |
| Daily insulin (U/day) | 43 ± 15 | 49 ± 9 | 0.39 |

- Dose: 0.2 U/kg SC into an abdominal skinfold, regular human insulin (Humulin U100) or insulin lispro (Humalog U100);
  NP received 14.3 ± 3.3 U, DC 15.4 ± 2.0 U (PDF p2).
- 4-h run-in with IV insulin to 5.0 mmol/L; injection ~12:00; mean clamp glucose 5.0-5.2 mmol/L in all arms.
- Assay: free regular insulin and free lispro by RIA (DPC Coat-A-Count) after PEG precipitation of antibody-bound
  insulin; inter-/intra-assay CV 15.0 / 6.6%; the assay is cited as validated for free human insulin and free lispro
  (ref 15). Gastric emptying screened with a ¹³C-octanoic acid breath test (no exclusions).
- Analysis: sixth-order polynomial fitted to each individual's GIR and insulin profile; non-compartmental summary
  measures; AUC by trapezoid rule; Wilcoxon / Mann-Whitney; StatView 5.0. No PK model.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: glucose infusion rate at an 8-h euglycaemic clamp (Table 2 above). No ke0, EC50 or Emax.
- Lispro: faster tGIRmax (102-105 vs 172-191 min) and shorter late tGIR50% than regular in both groups.
- Nephropathy lowered regular insulin's GIRmax (3.4 vs 5.9 mg/kg/min) and AUC GIR 0-8 h (967 vs 1510 mg/kg) despite
  higher insulin levels; lispro's overall effect did not differ between groups (Table 2, p4).
- Authors' derived claim (p4): a regular-insulin dose about 50% higher would be needed in nephropathy for the same
  metabolic effect (comparator: the diabetic controls in this study).
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Nephropathy status (categorical), adjusted in multiple regression for diabetes duration and HbA1c. The authors infer a
30-40% lower insulin clearance in nephropathy from the higher insulin levels (p4); that comparator is **diabetic
patients without nephropathy in this study**, and it is inferred from AUCs after SC dosing, not measured clearance.
Late tINS50% (the decline) did not differ between groups. No equations.

## Caveats
- n = 12 per group, groups unbalanced for age, sex, diabetes duration and HbA1c; exploratory, no multiplicity correction.
- Polynomial smoothing, non-compartmental; AUC unit printed without a time unit.
- The nephropathy group mixes macroproteinuria with normal GFR and raised creatinine (10/12 proteinuric, 7/12
  creatinine >1.5 mg/dL); subgroup analysis found no difference (p5).
- Free insulin after PEG precipitation in type 1 patients; endogenous insulin not subtracted (none expected in type 1).
- Funded in part by Lilly Deutschland.

## Verification against stanpumpR
**R/insulinEffectSite.R:45 (checked).** The file's own table gives Rave's regular-insulin GIR peak as 172-191 min
after 0.2 U/kg SC against the model's 2.7 h (162 min), so the model peaks about 6-18% earlier than the window this
paper reports -- which the header already treats as agreement, INSULIN_KE0 having been solved from Howey 1994's
23-min intravenous peak and checked against SC clamp peaks rather than fitted to them. Nothing to correct.

Independent comparison with the library's Tham 2017 model (its parameters at R/insulinTham.R:196-200, THAM_DISPOSITION; regular V/F 178 L x (BW/80)^1.62, CL/F 127 L/h x (Dose/10)^-0.282,

order with the rest zero-order over 1.06 h), simulated at the control group's means (76 kg, 15.4 U) with 1 mcU/mL = 6
pmol/L (the conversion R/insulinTham.R:6 states) (derived: Euler simulation in the session scratchpad):

|---|---|---|---|
| Regular Cmax | 144 pmol/L | 193 pmol/L | 1.3 |
| Regular tmax | 153 min | 94 min | 0.6 |

- Regular insulin: model and paper agree within ~1.3x on exposure; the model peaks earlier. Reasonable given type 1
  patients against Tham's mostly healthy volunteers.
- Lispro: the model gives about 4.4x the exposure Rave measured, and Rave's lispro/regular AUC0-8h ratio (1.11 in
  controls, 1.19 in nephropathy) is far from Tham's implied ratio (~3.8 at this dose). The Humalog label's 20 U healthy
  Cmax of 909 pmol/L (~700 pmol/L scaled to 15.4 U, derived) is closer to Tham than to Rave, so the gap may lie in
  Rave's RIA rather than in the model; left as a `[source]` question.
- Nephropathy: R/insulinTham.R has no renal covariate (grep for renal/creatinine found none). Rave suggests ~1.4x
  higher exposure to both insulins in overt nephropathy (derived from AUC 0-8 h, NP/DC: regular 51.3/37.3 = 1.38,
  lispro 61.0/41.5 = 1.47) against diabetic controls, not against Tham's population. Not a discrepancy, as the library
  makes no renal claim.
