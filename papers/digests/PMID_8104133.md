# Cook 1993: pharmacokinetics of smoked S-(+)-methamphetamine hydrochloride

## Study
Six healthy male paid volunteers experienced with amphetamines; age 26.7 ± 1.7 (SE) y, weight 83.8 ± 5.1 kg,
height 176.4 ± 1.5 cm (PDF p1, printed 717). Each smoked S-(+)-methamphetamine HCl (about 30 mg loaded) from a
glass pipe heated to 300-305 °C, inhaling and holding 15 s at 1-min intervals for about 4 min, and on another
occasion received 15.4-15.5 mg S-(+)-methamphetamine HCl iv over 1 min (PDF p2). Inhaled dose = amount loaded
minus the residue rinsed from the pipe (26.9 ± 1.0% left): 21.8 ± 0.33 mg HCl = 17.5 ± 0.26 mg base. (In vitro,
the pipe recovered 101.9 ± 2.7% of 40 mg: 57.0% as trapped vapour, 44.8% left in the pipe.) Plasma to 48 h,
urine to 72 h, saliva to 24 h. GC with nitrogen-selective detection; results as free base. Model-independent
analysis (RS/1, SAS); urinary excretion fitted with ADAPT II. Mean ± SE throughout.

Half-life range 8.3-18.2 h (PDF p2). Bioavailability of the smoked drug, 90.3%, is relative to the dose removed
from the pipe ("inhaled dose"), not the pipe load (PDF p2, p4).

**Oral bioavailability** 67.2 ± 3.1% (abstract, PDF p1; Discussion, PDF p4, printed 720): calculated by the
authors from this study's IV data and their 1992 oral study (ref 10), not measured within either alone.

Urine (Table 2, PDF p3): parent excreted 36.8 ± 4.3% (smoked) and 45.0 ± 9.5% (iv) of dose over 72 h;
k_u 0.0677 and 0.0673 /h; renal clearance 6.68 and 6.95 L/h; metabolic clearance 57.9% and 55.0% of total.
Amphetamine about 7% of the molar dose in urine; plasma amphetamine Cmax 4.0 ± 0.63 ng/mL at 17 ± 3.3 h (iv),
4.2 ± 0.56 ng/mL at 12 ± 2.3 h (smoked). Renal clearance regression (PDF p4): log CL = -0.51 pH + 0.42 log flow
+ 4.58 (r² 0.688; L/h).

**Two-input absorption model** (eq. 2, PDF p6, printed 722; rendered page checked): one elimination exponent
with a fast and a slow first-order input, fitted to smoked plasma data in 4 of 6 subjects. Absorption rate
constants 36.9 ± 7.5 /h and 0.693 ± 0.102 /h (stated as half-lives of 1.1 and 60 min); elimination rate
0.0617 ± 0.0030 /h; C1 23.1 ± 8.8 and C2 22.9 ± 4.8 (units not printed; ng/mL implied). One further subject fitted
one compartment with a single input, ka 1.35 /h; one fitted neither. Fig. 7 (subject 4) gives
Cp = 47.0 e^(-0.0600t) - 18.5 e^(-43.0t) - 28.6 e^(-0.853t) against a one-input fit 35.6 (e^(-0.0428t) - e^(-10.9t)).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, EC50 or effective concentration. Subjective "high" (0-100 visual scale relative to the subject's
highest-ever amphetamine experience) peaked at 38 ± 9.8% (smoked) and 41.5 ± 20.1% (iv), at 18 ± 2.1 min and
17.4 ± 1.1 min (PDF p4, printed 720). Peak heart-rate change at 11.8 ± 1.6 min (smoked) and 12.6 ± 2.8 min (iv);
cardiovascular measures returned to baseline quickly (Fig. 4). Fig. 3 (effect against plasma concentration): loop
clockwise for smoking and essentially counter-clockwise for iv by the authors' description; effects subsided
while plasma stayed high, which the authors read as acute tolerance. Not an opioid; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Renal clearance depends on urine pH (inverse) and log urine flow (above). Across oral, smoked and iv data the
authors report λ decreasing with bioavailable dose (λ = -0.128·(f·dose) + 0.0895, r² 0.70, p 0.037) and the urinary
fraction falling with absorbed dose (Fig. 5), suggesting dose-dependent kinetics. Weight only as per-kg scaling.

## Caveats
- n = 6 men; smoked dose about 0.2 mg/kg, far below typical abuse doses (authors note dose dependence matters more
  at higher doses).
- Urine pH not controlled.
- Smoked bioavailability is relative to the dose removed from the pipe, not to what was absorbed or exhaled.
- The oral 67.2% combines data from two separate studies.
- The two-input model fitted only 4 of 6 subjects; its rate constants are means over those 4.
- MRT in the abstract (11.5, 11.3 h) disagrees with Table 1 (16.7, 17.4 h).
