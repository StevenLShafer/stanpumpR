# Loo 1970: bolus-equivalent intercepts from post-infusion curves

## Study
Methods paper with two small demonstrations (PDF p3, printed p55):
- **Theory** (PDF p1-2, printed pp53-54): for any linear, constant-coefficient compartmental system, a bolus curve
  Cp = sum A_i e^(-k_i t) (Eq. 1) and a constant-rate infusion of the same dose over tau give, after the infusion
  stops (t' = t - tau), Cp_post = sum A_i (1 - e^(-k_i tau)) / (k_i tau) e^(-k_i t') (Eq. 9). The exponents k_i are
  unchanged; only the intercepts change. The bolus-equivalent intercept is recovered from the post-infusion intercept
  A_i' by **A_i = k_i tau / (1 - e^(-k_i tau)) x A_i'** (Eq. 12; Eq. 13 substitutes it into Eq. 1). Limits: short
  tau gives Eq. 10 (the bolus curve itself); long tau gives Eq. 11 (A_i/(k_i tau)). k0 is the fractional infusion
  rate, 1/tau (footnote 2). A general solution; the two-compartment case is credited to Rescigno and Segre (footnote 3).
- **Griseofulvin**: one healthy adult man, 142 mg over 3 min, and several weeks later 153.6 mg over 2 h at constant
  rate; intact drug assayed (modified Bedford method).
- **Sulfisoxazole**: one male rabbit (4.5 kg), ear-vein infusions of 10 s, 30 min and 60 min; unconjugated
  sulfonamide by Bratton-Marshall.
- Biexponential fits: analog computer (E.A.I. T.R. 48) for initial estimates, refitted by nonlinear least squares
  (BMD X85) on an IBM 360/50.

## Caveats
- n = 1 for each demonstration (one man, one rabbit); the 2-h griseofulvin dose (153.6 mg) differed from the 3-min
  dose (142 mg), yet A' in ug/mL is compared without dose normalisation.
- Misprints visible on the page images: the text says "Substituting tau for k0" before Eq. 9 where footnote 2
  defines k0 = 1/tau; Eq. 15 prints the second exponential as e^(+k2 t'), and the discussion writes
  A2(-e^(k2 tau))/(k2 tau); both lack minus signs. Eq. 9 and Eq. 12 themselves are printed correctly.
- The intercept correction needs only linearity and a constant-rate (zero-order) infusion. The authors add that the
  intrinsic compartmental constants follow by their earlier methods (refs 2, 3) when the curve is of "free drug"; for
  metabolite or total-drug curves other procedures are needed. In context (footnote 1, the unconjugated-sulfonamide
  assay) "free" appears to mean unchanged parent drug rather than protein-unbound drug; the paper does not define it.
- Footnote 4 corrects the Vd(ss) equation of their earlier paper (ref 3), as pointed out by Gibaldi.
- The authors advise short infusions, since curvature (and so the fast exponent) is harder to define after long ones.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Which paper is meant**: Fagan 1982's own text (archive text layer `_build/text/PMID_7066173.txt`, line 115)
  cites "Loo & Riegelman (1970)", i.e. this paper. The repo's short citation is correctly identified. **matches**.
- **Is the method what the code says?** Yes: Eq. 12 converts post-infusion intercepts to the intercepts a bolus of
  the same dose would give, and leaves the exponents unchanged, which is what "converted to bolus equivalents"
  means. The library does not apply the correction itself; it uses Fagan's already-converted coefficients, so no
  conversion is duplicated or missing in code. **matches**.
- Size of the correction for Fagan's regimen (derived: 0.05 mg/kg at 1 mg/min, 3.8 mg mean dose, so tau about
  3.8 min; factor k tau / (1 - e^(-k tau))): pi 0.856/min gives 3.25/0.961 = 3.38; alpha 0.0548/min gives 1.11;
  beta 0.203/h gives about 1.006. So Fagan's P (317 ng/mL) is about 3.4 times the fitted post-infusion intercept,
  and V1 (11.06 L in the model) depends strongly on the correction having been made with each subject's own tau.
  Whether Fagan applied it correctly cannot be checked from this paper; Fagan's own card (`PMID_7066173.md`)
  covers Table 1.
- Applicability: Eq. 12 assumes linear kinetics and a zero-order infusion; Fagan used a constant 1 mg/min infusion
  of a drug the model treats as linear, and measured parent propranolol (GC-MS), so the method's conditions are met.
- Note: the context lists only `R/drugs_propranolol.R:19`; the citation spans lines 19-20. No other repo location
  names Loo and Riegelman (searched the checkout; other digests mention the different Loo-Riegelman absorption
  method, which is a separate 1968 paper).
