# Jia 2015: unfractionated heparin (anti-IIa) during and after cardiopulmonary bypass

## Study
- **Design:** observational population PK study at Beijing Anzhen Hospital (Capital Medical University), in
  cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) (p2).
- **Population:** 41 enrolled, after excluding kidney, liver or coagulation dysfunction; 32 completed (p7).
  Table 1 (p2): weight 66 (41-82) kg, age 53.4 (18-74) years, 19 women and 13 men (these sum to 32, not 41),
  CPB time 2.04 (0.95-3.29) h.
- **Dosing (p2):**
  - Initial IV UFH 375 IU/kg (3 mg/kg; 1 mg = 125 IU) before CPB, plus 1 mg/kg (125 IU/kg) in the 1500 mL prime.
  - Further irregular doses by coagulation status.
  - Mean doses (Table 1): first dose 24,805 IU, prime 8,516 IU, total 34,023 IU (25,000-45,000). The text gives
    about 33,000 IU per surgery (p7).
- **Bypass conditions:** flow 2.2-2.4 L/min/m², temperature drifting to 32 °C, no ultrafiltration (p2).
- **Sampling (p2):** first sample 10 min after the initial dose, then every 30 min until protamine; after
  neutralisation at 2, 4, 8, 12 and 24 h, using actual times.
- **Assay:** anti-factor IIa activity (Heparin Chromogenic Activity Kit 820, American Diagnostica, on an
  ACL-TOP), measurable range 0.0-0.6 IU/mL. Samples before protamine were diluted 1:29 in pooled normal plasma
  (p2).
- **Software:** NONMEM VII, FOCE; PsN; covariate inclusion at p < 0.01, retention at p < 0.001 (pp3, 6-7).

## Model and parameters
**Structure (pp3-4):** two compartments, first-order elimination, IV doses superposed.
- **Protamine:** at neutralisation (Tneu) the central amount is set to zero while the peripheral amount carries
  over. The return from the peripheral compartment then produces the post-protamine rebound (Eqs 11-14).
- **Parameters and variability:** estimated as clearances and volumes (p7). IIV exponential; residual error
  proportional only (additive fixed to 0). Age, weight and sex were tested and none was retained (p7), so the
  parameters are absolute.

  and β = 0.0185 h⁻¹ (t½ 37.4 h).
- **Internal inconsistency:** the Methods (p7) describe bootstrap 90% intervals, but Table 2's heading says 95%
  CI. The bootstrap covariance step succeeded in 573 of 1000 runs and minimisation in 982 of 1000.

**Reported levels (pp7-8):**
- Median plateau anti-IIa during CPB was 2-19 IU/mL. The median at the end of CPB was 4.8 IU/mL, neutralised a
  mean 2.04 h after CPB started.
- The rebound peaked at 0.04 IU/mL 8 h after the end of CPB and stayed above 0.02 IU/mL at 24 h.
- The background (p1) gives a plasma half-life of 1-2 h depending on dose (ref 2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. The end point is anti-IIa activity in IU/mL; neither ACT nor aPTT was modelled.
- **Protamine simulations** (Fig 6, p10; typical individual, protamine 25 mg/h for 6-24 h after CPB): a 24 h
  infusion controlled the rebound better than a 6 h one.
- The protamine effect is represented only as the instantaneous emptying of the central compartment; there is no
  heparin-protamine binding model.

## Covariates
None retained (age, weight, sex tested; p7).

## Caveats
- Only anti-IIa was measured; heparin fractions and metabolites were not studied, and concomitant drugs may have
  affected coagulation (p9).
- Small sample (32 completers), Chinese adults, one centre. Pre-protamine samples were measured after a 1:29
  dilution because the assay range is only 0-0.6 IU/mL.
- V2 is poorly determined: RSE 31.8%, bootstrap 95% range 2.63-27.6 L. The 37 h terminal phase rests mainly on
  post-protamine samples at 0.02-0.04 IU/mL.
- Linear kinetics fitted under hemodilution and hypothermia; the dose-dependent half-life of the background is
  not represented.
