# Vaughan 1976: ideal IV input for a constant plasma concentration (lidocaine example)

## Study
Theoretical paper, no subjects. Derives, by Laplace transform of the convolution integral, the IV input that
produces and holds a constant plasma concentration Cpss for any linear system whose unit-impulse response is a sum
of N exponentials (sum Ai e^(-alpha_i t)). Explicitly makes no assumption about compartment structure or site of
elimination (elimination from a peripheral compartment or perfusion models allowed); requires only linearity /
superposition. States that Kruger-Thiemer's (1968) mammillary-model dosage flow is analogous to their result.

Result (eq 7, 20): a bolus D1 = Cpss / sum(Ai), plus a constant-rate infusion D1*R, plus N-1 exponentially
declining infusions (rate constants ci, initial reservoir amounts -D1*Ui). Closed forms given for N = 2
(eq 23-25) and N = 3 (eq 28-31); N = 4 needs a cubic solved by computer.

(derived check: c1 = (A1 alpha2 + A2 alpha1)/(A1 + A2) = 0.03386 /min; R = alpha1 alpha2 / c1 = 0.0244 /min,
D1 R = 1.00 mg/min, consistent with the text.)

Step approximation (p 439, Fig 3-4): the exponential input replaced by 2.40 mg/min for 0-15 min, 1.25 mg/min for
15-39 min, 0.55 mg/min for 39-87 min, on top of the bolus and 1 mg/min; concentration deviates -8.6% to +5.0%
from the ideal.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD modelling. Cites a lidocaine therapeutic blood range of 1.2 to 5.5 ug/mL (Gianelly 1967, Foldes 1960,
Bellet 1971) for ventricular dysrhythmias after myocardial infarction; usual clinical regimen cited as 1-2 mg/kg
bolus + 1-4 mg/min. No ke0, no effect site.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Theoretical; parameters are a literature mean. Several internal typographical inconsistencies (A2 0.084 vs
0.0084; c1 0.03368 vs 0.03386; reservoir 86.3 vs 86.6 mg). Plasma (not effect-site) targeting only.
