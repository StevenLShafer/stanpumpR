# Fischler 1986: droperidol three-compartment PK in anaesthetised adults

## Study
10 ASA I-II adults (6 F, 4 M), nonhaemorrhagic abdominal, orthopaedic or gynaecological surgery (Table 1):
age 32-63 yr (mean 49.8; reported "SD" 3.8), height 167.4 +/- 7.6 cm, weight 47-80 kg, mean 66.4 +/- 7.7 kg.
Premedication diazepam 10 mg + atropine 0.5 mg IM; thiopental induction, N2O 60%, fentanyl 5 mcg/kg,
pancuronium; controlled ventilation. Droperidol 150 mcg/kg IV bolus after induction. Venous plasma at 2, 4, 6,
10, 20, 40, 60, 80, 120, 180, 240, 300, 360 min. Radioimmunoassay using benperidol antibodies (Janssen),
LOQ 0.2 ng/mL. Individual triexponential fits, SAS nonlinear least squares (Marquardt), weight 1/predicted^2.
Mathematical analysis by R. Gasparini (Janssen).

## Model and parameters
Three-compartment, individual fits; means +/- SD of 10 (Tables 2 and 3).

About 90% of the dose leaves plasma within 1 h. Authors compare CL with hepatic plasma flow (900 mL/min) and
call droperidol a high-extraction drug.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. Authors note the short half-life does not fit droperidol's long clinical action and suggest
slow receptor dissociation or brain retention (speculative). No ke0. (Not an opioid paper.)

## Covariates
No relation found between age, sex, weight or body area and the PK parameters (n = 10).

## Caveats
n = 10, sampling to 6 h only (terminal phase may be underestimated), anaesthetised with fentanyl/N2O. Table 1
"SD" for age (3.8) is implausibly small for a 32-63 yr range (probably SEM or misprint); Table 3 SDs for Vc and
k10 look misprinted. Grunwald 1993 later misquotes this paper's Vss as 2.4 L/kg (Fischler's Vss is 1.40;
Vdbeta 2.04).
