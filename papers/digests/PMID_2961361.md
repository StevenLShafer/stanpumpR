# Martin 1987: venous bupivacaine after lower abdominal field block

## Study
16 women, ASA I, 25-35 y, laparoscopic sterilisation under GA (thiopentone, suxamethonium, intubated, N2O/halothane,
spontaneous ventilation; procedure about 20 min). Bilateral ilioinguinal/iliohypogastric and modified iliac crest
blocks plus bilateral posterior rectus sheath injection: **40 mL 0.5% bupivacaine = 200 mg**, plain (n = 8) or with
adrenaline 1:200,000 (n = 8). Table I (mean ± SEM): plain 64 ± 6.2 kg, 28 ± 3 y, 3.38 ± 0.127 mg/kg; adrenaline
60 ± 2.2 kg, 30 ± 4 y, 3.29 ± 0.22 mg/kg (row assignment of weight/age inferred from the text layer). Venous plasma
(contralateral arm) at 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120 min after the block was completed; total bupivacaine by
GLC. No PK model.

Plain significantly higher from 5 to 45 min. 11 individual plain-group values lay in 2.3-5.0 µg/mL (Fig 4); no
adrenaline value exceeded 1.5 µg/mL. Peak reached sooner with plain (Fig 3; times not tabulated).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Sensory loss to pinprick over the lower abdomen and upper thigh after recovery. No toxicity. Thresholds quoted from
others: 4 µg/mL "danger level" (Jorfeldt 1968, dog extrapolation); mild toxicity 1.6-2 µg/mL and serious 2.3-5 µg/mL
(Reynolds 1987 review); arterial 10-20% above venous (Moore 1976). Protein binding about 90% up to 5 µg/mL (Tucker
and Mather 1975). Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
n = 8 per group, young women only, GA during early sampling, venous total concentrations, sampling to 120 min.
