# Bührer 1990 (part I): choosing an EEG measure of midazolam effect

## Study
Six men (Table I): volunteers S (36 y, 85 kg), L (40 y, 70 kg), N (40 y, 78 kg), P (36 y, 78 kg); patients before
knee arthroscopy G (43 y, 74 kg), D (33 y, 81 kg). Ages 33-43, weights 70-85 kg. No premedication, fasted 8 h.
Three substudies:
- Baseline (S, N, P): 4 h EEG without drug (2 h stimulated, 2 h allowed to sleep).
- Single dose (S, L, N, G, D): midazolam 15 mg IV at 5 mg/min (3 min).
- Multiple dose (S: 3.75, 7.5, 15, 25 mg; L: 7.5, 15; N: 3.75, 7.5, 15, 25), random order, > 4 weeks apart, all at
  5 mg/min.
Radial arterial line for BP and blood gases; ventilation assisted (PaCO2 41-51 mm Hg at peak effect); O2 by mask.
EEG: bipolar Fp1-O1, Fp2-O2, Cz-O1, Cz-O2. FFT (200 Hz, 2.56 s epochs; total power, SEF95, SEF50) vs aperiodic
analysis (960 Hz, 1 s epochs; waves/s and voltage/s, 0.5-30 Hz, 60 s moving average). Responsiveness to "move your
toes" every 15 s during infusion.
**No plasma concentrations, sampling schedule or pharmacokinetics are reported in this paper** (part II, 48:555-567,
holds the PK/PD).

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Conclusion: aperiodic **voltage per second** is the best EEG measure (stable at baseline, rises and
recovers with dose, ceiling effect, least between-subject variability). FFT power behaved similarly but was more
variable; spectral edges moved inconsistently (biphasic alpha-to-beta then delta shift).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: EEG aperiodic voltage per second (Fp-O leads), plus loss/return of response to verbal command.
No ke0, t1/2ke0, EC50 or concentration data in this paper (all in part II). Observed time to peak EEG effect after
the non-ceiling 3.75 mg dose: 2.9 min (S) and 2.2 min (N), Table II.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n = 6 men (3 in dose ranging), no women, no elderly; ceiling effect makes peak time a poor ke0 surrogate at doses
>= 7.5 mg; voltage absolute values not comparable with FFT power; marked inter- and intra-individual variability in
recovery.
