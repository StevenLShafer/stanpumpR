# Bourke 1987: ketamine-morphine respiratory interaction

## Study
Six healthy male volunteers aged 18-30 y, each studied three times (>= 1 week apart); a seventh withdrew after one session. Blinded IV injection of placebo, morphine sulfate 0.2 mg/kg, or 0.4 mg/kg, then 15 min later five ketamine IV boluses 10 min apart: 0.39, 0.26, 0.43, 0.72, 1.20 mg/kg (cumulative 0.39, 0.65, 1.08, 1.80, 3.00 mg/kg). Measured: steady-state CO2 response curves (0, 3, 5, 7% inspired CO2) before and after, and isohypercapnic minute ventilation (end-tidal CO2 49.8 ± 2.4 mmHg, 6.1 mmHg above resting), measured in minutes 8-10 after each ketamine dose; inspired O2 about 40% to remove hypoxic drive. No drug concentrations were measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: isohypercapnic minute ventilation (VE) and the CO2 ventilatory response curve (slope and PETCO2 at VE 15 L/min). Control VE 23.2 ± 1.8 L/min (mean ± SD).
- Morphine alone: VE fell 8.2 ± 1.2 L/min at 0.2 mg/kg; a further 6.6 ± 1.8 L/min on doubling to 0.4 mg/kg (abstract, Figure 1, PDF p.2). Curve shifted right about 10 mmHg after ketamine on top of either morphine dose (Table 2, PETCO2 at VE 15: placebo-ketamine 49.7, with morphine 59.0 and 58.2); slopes unchanged (Table 1, p.155).
- Ketamine alone: log-linear dose-related VE fall, 1.6 ± 0.3 L/min per dose doubling over 0.39-3.0 mg/kg; first significant fall 4.9 ± 1.1 L/min at the third dose (1.08 mg/kg cumulative). Curve displaced about 2 mmHg rightward (P = 0.057, not significant) with no slope change. All subjects unresponsive to voice after 1.8 mg/kg cumulative; two after 1.08 mg/kg.
- Effects were additive; persisted about 2 h after first ketamine dose and 1.5 h after last.
- Opioid audit (morphine): no MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration, no ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis, no concentration data, assay none. Only dose-based potency statements: ketamine loses 1.6 L/min per doubling against morphine's 7 L/min (the morphine figure is from unpublished data in the discussion); ketamine is described as similar to but less potent than morphine for respiration yet about equipotent for loss of consciousness. No formal equianalgesic ratio.

## Covariates
None (all male, healthy, young).

## Caveats
n = 6 crossover, males only, bolus doses by mg/kg (no concentrations, so no PD parameters usable in a PKPD model); a mouthpiece circuit failed in several subjects after the fourth ketamine dose (a mask substituted). Morphine effect size reflects an unspecified time after injection (15 min). The comparative "less potent than morphine" is by dose (mg/kg bolus), not concentration. Speculation about opioid-receptor mediation is not tested.
