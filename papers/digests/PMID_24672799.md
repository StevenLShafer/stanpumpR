# van Hasselt 2014: free and total cefazolin popPK in pregnancy, empirical vs semiphysiological

## Study
- Pooled analysis (p. 2): one prospective Leuven study plus two literature datasets; 94 patients in total (Table 1).
  - Leuven: 41 pregnant women having in utero surgery, GA median 25 (17-34) weeks, 153 plasma samples, free
    concentration also measured in 84% of them; cefazolin 2 g IV every 8 h for 2 days. Assay described in refs 3, 9
    (Allegaert 2009).
  - Fiore Mitchell 2001: 1 g IV bolus before elective caesarean, 24 samples, mean 1.85 h after dose; GA fixed at 40 weeks.
  - Brown 1990: 10 fetal interventions in 7 women, mean GA 27 weeks, single 2 g IV bolus, mean sampling 0.5 h after dose.
- Table 1 (page image): GA median 33 (17-40) weeks; age 31 (20-42) y; weight 72 (54-99) kg; serum creatinine
  0.64 (0.33-0.88) mg/dL (all median, range). (The text layer misaligns these rows; the image values are given here.)
- Missing data (p. 3): weight missing in 46%, age in 55%, imputed with the pooled median; some serum creatinine
  values missing in 58% of patients, imputed by median (empirical) or by the typical CrCL change (semiphysiological).
- CrCL by Cockcroft-Gault with total body weight (p. 2).
- NONMEM 7.1, FOCE with interaction (p. 3). No bootstrap (data too heterogeneous); VPC and goodness-of-fit plots.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment model with linear (constant) protein binding, C_total = C_free / f_u (eq. 7, p. 4). Nonlinear
binding and nonlinear elimination were tried and not supported. The paper does not say explicitly whether CL and the
volumes refer to free or total drug; the fitted structure (free concentrations linked to total via f_u) suggests the
parameters belong to free drug, but this is not stated.

Covariate equations (Methods eq. 3 and 6; text p. 4):
- Empirical: CL = θCL0 + θCL Preg × (1 + GA/40).
- Semiphysiological: CL_i = θCL0 + θCL Preg × (CrCL_i(t) / CrCL_i0).
- The Table 2 footnotes print these the other way round (footnote a, "Empirical", carries the CrCL formula; footnote
  b, "semiphysiological", carries the GA formula). The Methods and Results show the footnotes are swapped, so
  this is a typesetting error, not an ambiguity.

Table 3 (p. 4, page image), CrCL time-course model, CrCL(t) = CrCL0 + CrCLMAX × t / (CrCL50 + t) (eq. 4), structural

printed "—"; additive to CrCL0, so presumably mL/min) (12.48%), CrCL50 13.3 weeks (37.59%). Estimated IIV: 31.4 (16),
35.1 (17), 111.8 (121) CV%; proportional residual variance 0.0291 (43).

Derived (not in the paper):
- CrCL at 40 weeks = 97.83 + 83.83 × 40/53.3 = 160.7 mL/min, i.e. 1.64 × baseline.
- Typical semiphysiological CL: non-pregnant 0.142 + 0.212 = 0.354 L/min; at 40 weeks 0.142 + 0.212 × 1.64 =
  0.490 L/min (×1.38).
- Typical empirical CL: GA 0 gives 0.336 L/min; GA 40 gives 0.553 L/min (×1.65).
- Base CL 0.49 L/min = 29.4 L/h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. Simulations used a coagulase-negative staphylococcal MIC90 of 0.5-4 mg/L and the time free cefazolin
stays above 4 mg/L (p. 3, Figure 4). The authors report that 2 g every 6 h (instead of 8 h) prolonged the time above
4 mg/L (p. 6). No ke0; not an opioid.

## Covariates
- Empirical model: only a linear CL–GA relation was significant (dOFV −9.6). CL–CrCL was not significant (dOFV −0.9,
  slope RSE 130%). GA had no significant effect on Vc, Vp or f_u, and albumin had none on f_u (pp. 4-5). Power models
  did no better than linear ones.
- Semiphysiological model: once CL was driven by the individual predicted CrCL change, no extra GA effect improved the
  fit. The authors conclude the pregnancy effect was fully explained by the CrCL predictions (p. 5, 3.3.2).

## Caveats
- Sparse, pooled, heterogeneous data, with extensive imputation of age, weight and creatinine. The authors think
  imputation is why GA beat CrCL in the empirical model (p. 7).
- Clearance poorly estimated (RSE 58% and 44%); ωV1 101.5% in the semiphysiological model.
- The CrCL time course is not learned from these data: it is fixed from a meta-analysis of old studies (Davison
  1974, Lebaudy 2008). So "explained entirely by CrCL" partly reflects that imposed prior.
- Table 2 covariate footnotes are swapped (see above). CrCLMAX unit printed as "—".
- No non-pregnant comparison group: baseline CL is an extrapolation to GA 0 / CrCL0.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv:49` (also `data-raw/pregnancyRegistry.R:576`). The finding text says
  "van Hasselt 2014 explained the gestational effect entirely through the rise in creatinine clearance." The paper
  says the same in its semiphysiological model (section 3.3.2, p. 5): no GA effect remained once CL was linked to
  the predicted CrCL change. **matches**. Context worth keeping: the paper's *empirical* model found GA, not
  measured CrCL, to be the significant covariate. The CrCL trajectory was fixed from a prior meta-analysis, so the
  result shows that the data are *consistent with* a CrCL-driven explanation; it is not an independent estimate.
- The row's applied factor (creatinine clearance × 1.5 when creatinine is blank; unbound clearance × 1.27) is not
  attributed to this paper and no van Hasselt number is used in code. For comparison (derived from Table 3): the
  fixed CrCL model gives ×1.64 at 40 weeks and about ×1.59 at 30 weeks (97.83 + 83.83 × 30/43.3 = 155.9 mL/min).
  Typical semiphysiological CL rises ×1.38 at term. These are consistent with the row's 1.5 creatinine-clearance
  factor. No discrepancy.
