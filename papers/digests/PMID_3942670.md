# Hibbard 1986: Placental transfer of naloxone

## Study
- Pregnant women, uncomplicated singleton pregnancies of 38-41 weeks, normal labour and vaginal delivery, pethidine 50-300 mg for labour analgesia (PDF p1).
- Naloxone 400 ug during the second stage of labour: i.v. in 30 mothers, i.m. in 7 mothers. Maternal venous and umbilical venous blood were taken at cord clamping. The injection-to-clamping interval was 1-29 min (i.v.) and 5-31 min (i.m.). Women could breathe 50% nitrous oxide.
- Five non-pregnant adult volunteers (65-75 kg): naloxone 400 ug i.v., then at least a week later 2 mg i.m. Samples from 1 min to 48 h.
- Assay: serum naloxone by radioimmunoassay (Berkowitz method), lower limit 0.02 ng/mL with 50 uL samples. Serum, not plasma; total drug as measured by RIA (cross-reactivity with metabolites is not discussed). No compartmental model was fitted.

Results by route:
- i.v., mothers: scattered values; the highest were 2-10 min after injection. All maternal values exceeded 2.5 ng/mL (Discussion, PDF p3-4). Three mothers were below 3 ng/mL (see open question). Three samples were haemolysed (>80 ng/mL, excluded from the mean). About half the fetal samples were below 2.5 ng/mL; the one exception that exceeded maternal values was haemolysed. The authors conclude transfer is rapid.
- i.m., mothers: neonatal concentrations were variable and judged too variable for therapeutic use.
- Neonatal half-life 3 h and adult half-life roughly half that are quoted from other work (Moreland 1980, Berkowitz 1976), not measured here.
- Pethidine-treated babies: two had 1-min Apgar of 5 and 6 after i.v. naloxone; one had 4 after i.m.; all were fine at 5 min or in good condition otherwise.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD was measured. No ke0, tPeak or hysteresis, no MEAC, no equianalgesic ratio (naloxone is an antagonist, not an opioid agonist). The authors state the therapeutic serum concentration is not known with certainty and use 2.5 ng/mL as a working threshold (Discussion, PDF p3-4); no outcome was related to concentration. Dose context quoted from earlier neonatal studies: 60-65 ug/kg. The adult dose 400 ug is described as about 5.7 ug/kg.

## Covariates
None analysed. The authors suggest the lower volunteer-versus-mother concentrations may reflect the larger distribution volume in pregnancy (untested).

## Caveats
Single time point per mother (one sample, a cross-sectional design across intervals); haemolysed samples; small i.m. group; mothers had received pethidine and nitrous oxide; volunteers were few and non-pregnant, studied at a different dose route than some mothers (i.m. 2 mg vs 400 ug). Table I's concentration header reads "ng mg-1" in the text layer, an evident misprint for ng/mL.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Registry row (finding, notes, source): states the paper gives "rapid transfer after IV, variable after IM" and that there is no pregnant-vs-nonpregnant disposition comparison. **Matches** the paper: rapid placental transfer after i.v. and variable after i.m.; the volunteer arm was a separate non-pregnant group, but at different doses and routes, and gave no formal comparison. Citation (BJA 1986;58:45-48, PMID 3942670, DOI) matches.
- The registry marks evidence as read from the abstract, n = 0 and no factors; consistent with this card (no clearance or volume ratio in the paper).
- Comparison with the library's model (R/drugs_naloxone.R, Dowling 2008; header lines 2-24), derived by me, unvalidated: for a 70 kg, 170 cm man (CL 75.4 L/h, Vc 2.87 L, Vp1 1.49 L, Q1 5.66 L/h, Vp2 33.6 L, Q2 29.8 L/h) a 400 ug bolus gives about 73 ng/mL at 1 min and 0.84 ng/mL at 30 min, against Hibbard's volunteers' 146.3 and 3.6 ng/mL (Fig 1). Hibbard's serum values are higher at both times, by about 2x and 4x. The assay, volunteers' sex and weight (65-75 kg, sex not stated), and sampling site (venous) differ. This is not a discrepancy in a citation, since the code does not cite Hibbard for disposition. It is raised as an open question.
