# Graves 1989: felbamate population PK from a phase II trial

## Study
- Phase II randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial at the University of Minnesota (PDF p2, printed 373): stabilisation, baseline 8 wk, treatment I 12 wk, treatment II 12 wk; 7-day inpatient stay at the start of each treatment period.
- 32 adults with refractory partial seizures (12 F, 20 M), age 18-55 y, weight 51.8-110 kg (mean 68.6 kg) (p1, printed 372; rendered). All took **phenytoin and carbamazepine** (concentrations >10 and >4 ug/mL). 3 excluded from NONMEM (2 incomplete, 1 non-compliant), so 29 analysed (derived: 32 - 3).
- Felbamate oral, titrated over 5 days to 3000 mg/day (first 4 patients) or 2600 mg/day, given TID at 8 A.M., 6 P.M., 10 P.M. Phenytoin dose cut 20% a priori when felbamate started; carbamazepine not changed a priori (p2).
- 300 serum felbamate concentrations, mean 10.3 per patient (range 8-13); 65% troughs; none between 6 and 10 P.M.; few in the absorption phase (p2, p5).
- Assay: HPLC (Wallace Laboratories), quantitative range 0.5-50 ug/mL (p2).
- Analysis: NONMEM version 2 level 1 on IBM 3081; one-compartment, first-order absorption and elimination; F fixed 1; **ka fixed at 1 h^-1** (taken from Wilensky 1985, ref 2), not estimated (p3, printed 374; rendered).

## Model and parameters
Final model (p4, printed 375; rendered; SE in parentheses):
- CL (L/h) = 2.43 (0.168) + 0.429 (0.160) * CDCR/240, where CDCR is the carbamazepine dose:concentration ratio; 240 is roughly its mean, used to scale it about 1 (p3). Units of CDCR not stated.
- Vd = 51 L (4.96). IIV on Vd estimated <1e-9 and removed.
- IIV additive on CL: CLj = CL + eta, var(eta) = 0.117 (0.0324) L^2/h^2 — the paper calls it a CV of about 12% at CL 2.86 L/h (CDCR = 240).
- Residual: Cij = Cmij + (1 - P3 + P3*Cmij^2)^1/2 * eps, P3 = 0.0066 (0.00205), var(eps) = 4.71 (1.22); CV 47% at 5 mg/L, about 20% above 20 mg/L.
- Correlations: P1 with P4 0.89; P3 with sigma^2 0.88.
- Alternative residual models gave Vd 65.4 L (proportional only) and 58.9 L (additive only) (p4).
- Derived: k = 2.86/51 = 0.056 h^-1, t1/2 about 12.4 h at CDCR 240 (derived; not stated in the paper).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No efficacy or concentration-effect analysis, no therapeutic range, no effect-site term. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
- Tested and not significant (p>0.05): weight (power on WT/70), sex, folic acid, phenytoin dose, phenytoin dose:concentration ratio (p3-p4).
- Carbamazepine dose (p<0.05) and CDCR (p<0.005) significant; CDCR kept, linear additive on CL. Removing it raised the objective function by 9.3.
- Comparisons the authors make (p4-p5): CL agrees with Wilensky 1985 in 8 patients on phenytoin or carbamazepine (3.47 L/h after one 200 mg dose, 2.75 L/h after long-term dosing) and with healthy volunteers taking at most 1200 mg/day. Vd is 15-20% smaller than the about 65 L (volunteers) and about 59 L (patients) of ref 2.

## Caveats
- Small, homogeneous adult population, all on two enzyme inducers; the authors read the low CL variability as a result of that.
- Absorption not identifiable (ka fixed); sampling at similar post-dose times.
- No monotherapy data; the intercept 2.43 L/h at CDCR = 0 lies outside the data.
- CDCR units not given. No renal or hepatic data.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **One compartment, first-order, apparent (oral)**: matches (p3).
- **V 51 L**: matches (p4).
- **CL 2.43 L/h**: **discrepancy** in meaning. The transcription is right, but 2.43 L/h is the intercept at CDCR = 0. The paper's typical clearance for its population is 2.86 L/h at CDCR 240 (about 2.9 L/h in the Discussion), 18% higher (derived: 2.86/2.43). The note does not mention the carbamazepine covariate. See open questions.
- **"no published absorption rate"**: matches: ka was fixed at 1 h^-1 from an earlier paper, not estimated.
- **"~30% renal"**: cannot verify; not in this paper.
- Nothing is plotted from this paper (the row is `proposed_unestimated`), so the error has no effect on a curve today.
