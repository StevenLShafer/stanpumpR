# Anderson 2019: ibuprofen clearance maturation and an analgesic target concentration

## Study
- Meta-analysis of published ibuprofen clearance estimates (n = 389 data): 3 premature-neonate publications (individual estimates), 3 infant (population), 11 children 2-15 y (1 individual Bayesian set from Hannam 2018, 9 population), 13 adult (1 individual, 12 population). IV and oral assumed bioequivalent.
- Model: CL = CLstd x (W/70)^0.75 x MF, MF = PMA^Hill / (TM50^Hill + PMA^Hill). NONMEM 7.3; bootstrap CIs.
- Target concentration from Hannam 2018's PD; dose = CL x TC (maintenance), V x TC (initial). Simulations in Berkeley Madonna.

Maturation: 90% of adult at 44 weeks PMA (1 month after term), 98% at 53 weeks (3 months). (Derived check with Hill 11.9: MF 0.894 at 44 wk, 0.987 at 53 wk.) Volume: mean 26.4 (SD 27.8) L/70 kg in premature neonates vs 16.5 (SD 6.0) L/70 kg in children and adults; geometric means 15.9 and 15.5.
Table 2 predicted three-times-daily doses, e.g. 1 month 45 mg (10 mg/kg), 12 months 93 mg (9.3), 6 y 156 mg (7.8), 16 y 350 mg (6.0).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Uses Hannam 2018 tonsillectomy PD: Emax 6.5 pain units (VAS 0-10), Ce50 3.95 mg/L, Hill quoted as 1.45 (Hannam 2018 Table 2 gives 1.48). Target effect: 4-unit pain reduction. TC = 6.3 mg/L effect site. The printed equation lost its layout; 6.3 is reproduced by Ce50 x TE/(Emax - TE) = 3.95 x 4/2.5 = 6.32, i.e. without the 1/Hill exponent (derived: with Hill 1.45-1.48 the inverse Emax gives ~5.4-5.5 mg/L). Simulated 400 mg 8-hourly in a 70 kg adult (Hannam 2018 PK): effect-compartment trough 6.2 mg/L after the first dose, 7.2 mg/L at steady state. No ke0 estimated here. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Weight (allometry ^0.75), postmenstrual age (Hill maturation).

## Caveats
- Pooled literature estimates of mixed quality; few data between term birth and 1 year.
- PD from children after tonsillectomy; target effect of 4 units is an assumption.
- The 6.3 mg/L appears to omit the Hill exponent (see above); Hill 11.9 in Table 1 vs 11.5 in abstract.
