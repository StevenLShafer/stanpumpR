# Guilhaumou 2019: SFPT-SFAR guidelines for beta-lactams in the ICU

## Study
A joint guideline of the French Society of Pharmacology and Therapeutics and the French Society of Anaesthesia and Intensive Care Medicine. Consensus committee of 18 experts; PICO questions; literature January 2000 to January 2018 (PubMed, Tripdatabase, PROSPERO, clinicaltrials.gov) reviewed by PRISMA and graded by GRADE; rating 1-9, "strong agreement" = at least 70% concordant and under 20% discordant (p. 3). Because few studies used mortality as the outcome, every recommendation is "optional" ("we suggest"). Result: 21 optional recommendations, all with strong agreement, in four areas (PK variability, PK-PD target, administration, TDM), plus a care algorithm (Fig. 2, p. 14). Population: critically ill (ICU) adults throughout; obesity explicitly out of scope (p. 4). No industry funding.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. The guideline's quantitative content:

Derived check of Table 2's method (stated in its footnote): free target 4-8 x MIC divided by the free fraction. Cefazolin: 4-8 x 2 = 8-16 mg/L free, / 0.20 = 40-80 mg/L total (matches). Ceftriaxone: 4 x 0.5 = 2 mg/L free, / 0.10 = 20 mg/L total, the lower bound; the 100 mg/L upper bound is the toxicity threshold, not 8 x MIC (which would give 40 mg/L).

Toxicity thresholds quoted (p. 6-7): cefepime trough > 22 mg/L (intermittent) or Css > 35 mg/L (continuous) with neurotoxicity in 50%; meropenem trough > 64, flucloxacillin > 125, piperacillin > 360 mg/L (without tazobactam); piperacillin-tazobactam Css > 157 mg/L (specificity 97%, sensitivity 52%); fCmin/MIC (P. aeruginosa breakpoint) > 8 associated with neurological deterioration in about half (piperacillin) and two thirds (meropenem).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Basis of R2.2 (p. 6): DALI (361 ICU patients, 8 beta-lactams): 100% fT > MIC associated with better outcome than 50% fT > MIC (OR 1.56, 1.15-2.13 vs 1.02, 1.01-1.04); cefepime fCmin/MIC > 7.6 with eradication in all patients (33% below); clinical cure needing 4-6 x MIC in other studies; in vitro maximal kill and suppression of resistance at 4-8 x MIC. The margin over 100% fT > MIC is justified by MIC and assay imprecision (up to +/-15%) and poor tissue penetration.
- Other recommendations: creatinine clearance measured by urine collection (U x V / P), not estimated, in ICU patients (R1.2, p. 4); albumin at the start and with each TDM (R1.3); higher initial doses (R3.1); prolonged or continuous infusion for high-MIC bacteria, severe illness, lower respiratory infection and non-fermenting Gram-negatives (R3.2-R3.5); a loading dose equal to one intermittent dose (R3.6); TDM at 24-48 h, trough for intermittent and steady state for continuous (R4.1-R4.7); dose-adjustment rules (R4.8-R4.9).
- No surgical-prophylaxis target is given.

## Covariates
Qualitative: renal function (ARC, AKI, renal replacement therapy), hypoalbuminaemia (higher free fraction, particularly for cefazolin, ceftriaxone and ertapenem), inflammation, fluids and vasopressors, obesity (not covered).

## Caveats
All recommendations are optional, built largely on PK-PD target attainment rather than mortality. Free fractions in Table 2 are approximate class values for patients with normal binding; ICU binding is variable. The 4-8 x MIC range is a consensus target for critically ill patients, not for prophylaxis or healthy patients. Table 2 thresholds are empirical-phase MICs (ECOFFs) chosen to cover the usual pathogens; the documented-infection target uses the measured MIC.
