# Swart 2004: lorazepam and midazolam population PK in long-term ICU sedation

## Study
- Learning group: open-label randomized parallel study in mechanically ventilated medical ICU patients. 66 were enrolled and 49 were evaluable: lorazepam n = 28, midazolam n = 21.
- Evaluation group: a separate earlier study, lorazepam n = 31, midazolam n = 33, sparse samples.
- Lorazepam learning group (Table 1): 17 M / 11 F; age 58 ± 17 (21-84) y; weight 80 ± 25 (40-175) kg; APACHE II 18 ± 7.
- Dosing (Table 2):
  - Lorazepam 0.16 mg/mL infusion, titrated to the Addenbrooke scale (maximum 5.3 mg/h). Boluses of 2 mL when needed.
  - Duration 149 ± 157 h (24-572) in the learning group and 141 ± 117 h (24-583) in the evaluation group.
  - Dose 18 ± 13.9 mg/day.
  - Midazolam durations reached 715 h.
- Samples at 0, 15, 30 and 45 min and 1, 2, 4 and 8 h, then daily. After the infusion stopped: 0, 15 and 30 min and 1, 2, 8 and 24 h.
- Assay: HPLC-UV, LLOQ 10 ng/mL; range 10-1000 ng/mL.
- Analysis: NONMEM V, two-compartment, proportional residual error.
- Fentanyl was given for analgesia.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- None modelled. Sedation was titrated clinically on the Addenbrooke scale.
- Elimination half-lives in this population: lorazepam about 30 h (CV 61%), midazolam 22.7 h (CV 149%).

## Covariates
- Lorazepam: PEEP on CL (linear), alcohol abuse (more than 6 units/day) on CL, age on Vss.
- Midazolam: age and alcohol abuse on CL, APACHE II on Q.
- Albumin, renal and hepatic indices were not significant.

## Caveats
- Infusion data with few early samples, so the central volume is poorly identified (CV 124%).
- Midazolam's volumes are even less certain: interindividual CVs of 225% on V and 355% on Vss (Table 5), so its
  V 7.15 L and Vss 431 L are poorly identified typical values (Vss 364 L without covariates; Barr's 159 L).
- Lower concentrations were systematically overpredicted.
- Critically ill patients with low albumin (about 19 g/L).
- The CV of 389% on alcohol-abuse clearance shows how few patients informed it.
