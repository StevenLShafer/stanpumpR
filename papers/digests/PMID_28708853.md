# Westin 2017: SSRIs and venlafaxine in pregnancy (paired TDM)

## Study
- **Design:** retrospective, naturalistic, paired. Routine therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) records from the two
  largest Norwegian psychotropic TDM services (Trondheim and Oslo), October 1999 to December 2011, linked to the
  Medical Birth Registry of Norway. Each pregnant sample is compared with the same woman's own non-pregnant
  ("baseline") samples, taken before or after pregnancy (pp2-4).
- **Data:** 196,726 analyses from 54,393 women of reproductive age, of which 3206 analyses from 1226 women fell in
  pregnancy. After selecting SSRIs plus venlafaxine and applying exclusions: 367 analyses in pregnancy from 290
  pregnancies in 281 women, plus 420 baseline analyses from the same women (Table 1, p4). Another 22 samples from
  the first 12 weeks after delivery were shown only in Fig 2, not modelled.
- **Exclusions (p4):** no drug detected (19 zero results), intoxication, sampling <8 h or >30 h after the last
  dose, or a moderate/major interacting co-medication. Time after dose was not standardised to 12 h (p12).
- **Per drug (Table 1, p4; analyses in pregnancy / baseline / pregnancies / women):**

| Drug | In pregnancy | 0-12 wk postpartum | Baseline | Pregnancies | Women |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Escitalopram | 110 | 3 | 161 | 97 | 95 |
| Citalopram | 78 | 3 | 80 | 58 | 58 |
| Fluoxetine | 53 | 2 | 49 | 43 | 41 |
| Sertraline | 56 | 5 | 52 | 37 | 34 |
| Venlafaxine | 36 | 1 | 44 | 33 | 33 |
| Paroxetine | 29 | 6 | 31 | 20 | 19 |
| Fluvoxamine | 5 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 |

- **Assay (p5):** serum, LC-MS or LC-MS/MS, total (bound + unbound) concentrations; inter-day CV mostly <10%.
  Racemic citalopram and fluoxetine were measured as totals; no chiral analysis.
- **Analysis (pp5-6):** each concentration was divided by the woman's daily dose and multiplied by the WHO defined
  daily dose (DDD), so every value is a dose-adjusted concentration at the DDD. log-concentration was fitted by a
  linear mixed model (random intercept per woman, fixed linear slope per gestational week, baseline = week 0),
  maximum likelihood, STATA 13 `mixed`. Trimester values are model predictions at gestational weeks 6, 20 and 34.
- No weights, ages, CYP genotypes or clinical outcomes are reported.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. The results are model-estimated dose-adjusted serum concentrations (Table 2, p5; ng/mL at the DDD).
For fluoxetine and venlafaxine the values are the active moiety (parent + norfluoxetine or + O-desmethylvenlafaxine).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. The end point is the dose-adjusted serum concentration; no clinical scores, no reference/therapeutic
range analysis beyond plotting the percentiles of a female reference population (Fig 2). The authors' advice (p12):
roughly double paroxetine and fluvoxamine doses in the third trimester; no routine change for escitalopram,
citalopram, fluoxetine, venlafaxine or sertraline. For citalopram they attribute most of the fall to the inactive
R-enantiomer (CYP2D6, induced) rather than S-citalopram (CYP2C19) (pp9-10; inference, no chiral assay). For
sertraline they suspect CYP2C19 inhibition and still advise against a reflex dose reduction (p11).

## Covariates
Gestational week only (log-linear). No genotype; the discussion (p10) cites Ververs 2009 for paroxetine falling
0.3 ng/mL per week in CYP2D6 extensive/ultrarapid metabolisers and rising 0.6 ng/mL per week in intermediate/poor
metabolisers.

## Caveats
- Retrospective TDM: adherence, indication for sampling and exact dose-to-sample time unknown (pp11-12); no
  systematic difference in post-dose interval between pregnant and non-pregnant samples (S3 Table, not seen).
- The mixed model forces a linear-in-log change with gestational week; the "third trimester" value is a week-34
  prediction, not a measured trimester mean.
- Total concentrations only; protein binding not addressed. Fluvoxamine has n = 3.
- Converting a concentration change to a clearance factor (1/ratio) is an inference that assumes steady state and
  unchanged bioavailability; the paper does not do it.
