# Pisani 1984: valproate inhibits ethosuximide clearance (single-dose study)

## Study
- Six drug-free healthy volunteers, 4 men and 2 women, 18-42 y, 62-77 kg (PDF p1, printed 229). Subjects 1 and 2
  are sisters (Table 1 footnote).
- Fasting single oral dose of ethosuximide 500 mg (Zarontin, 2 x 250 mg tablets) at 8 a.m. Serum samples at 0, 2, 4,
  6, 10, 16, 24, 32, 48, 72, 84, 96 h; 24 h urine collections.
- Then sodium valproate (Depakine 200 mg tablets) 800-1,600 mg/day in three divided doses, continued to the last
  sample. The second 500 mg ethosuximide dose was given 9 days after valproate started (5 days after valproate was
  assumed to reach steady state at 4 days) (PDF p2, printed 230).
- Assay: GLC, in duplicate; a substitute internal standard (n-dipropylacetamide) in four subjects because of an
  interfering peak. Valproate measured in serum at 48, 72, 84, 96 h after the second ethosuximide dose.
- Analysis: model-independent. Kel from the log-linear tail, t1/2 = 0.693/Kel; AUC 0-96 by trapezoids plus C96/Kel;
  Vd = D·F/(AUC·Kel) and CL = D·F/AUC with F assumed 1; renal clearance from urine. Paired t test.

the rendered page (PDF p3, printed 231). Mean (SD), n = 6:

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. A pharmacokinetic interaction study; no effect measure, no ke0, no therapeutic concentration. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled. The authors suggest valproate concentration (competitive inhibition), co-medication and saturation of
ethosuximide metabolism as determinants of whether the interaction appears, and possibly genetics (Discussion, p3-4).

## Caveats
- n = 6, healthy adults, single-dose ethosuximide; 2 of 6 showed no change. Comparator for "decreased clearance" is
  each subject's own pre-valproate single dose (paired), not chronic dosing or another population.
- F assumed complete, so CL and Vd are apparent (CL/F, V/F).
- The authors themselves note (citing Bauer 1982) that under chronic ethosuximide, with metabolism saturated, valproate
  produced no further inhibition; their effect may be specific to the unsaturated single-dose state.
- Valproate dispersion "± SEM 11.3" is implausible for a range of 66.8-95.0 with n = 6 (an SEM that size implies an
  SD of about 28); probably an SD. Does not affect any repo number.
- Internal standard changed in four subjects.
