# Regardh 1980: Clinical pharmacokinetics of metoprolol

## Study
Narrative review (Clin Pharmacokinet, 13 pages) by the AB Hassle group that developed metoprolol. It pools the
authors' own studies (about 50 healthy subjects, per the text on printed p561) and others: absorption and
bioavailability, distribution, metabolism, renal and hepatic disease, age, pregnancy, interactions, and
dose/concentration/effect in healthy subjects, hypertension and angina. No new data, no compartmental model, no parameter
table. Most numbers are cited to Regardh 1974/1980a, Jordo 1980a/b, Johnsson 1975a. Assay not described (review).
Concentrations are plasma unless stated; the review says whole-blood concentration is about 10% higher than plasma in the
therapeutic range (printed p559).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t½ke0 or tPeak is reported, and no hysteresis analysis. Not an opioid; no MEAC is defined. What the review gives:

- Beta-blockade end point is exercise heart rate (also resting HR). Effect is linearly related to the log of plasma concentration, with a slope that varies greatly between individuals (printed 565, PDF p9).
- Clear-cut effect on exercise heart rate at plasma 5 to 10 ng/mL (text: "about 20 to 40 nmol/ml", probably nmol/L; see open question), healthy volunteers (PDF p9). Equal plasma concentrations after IV (10-20 mg) and oral (50-100 mg) doses reduce exercise HR equally, so no clinically important active metabolite (PDF p9).
- Time course (healthy volunteers): maximal oral effect 1-2 h after dosing; IV effect seemed immediate. Exercise HR fell 12, 17, 22% after 20, 50, 100 mg oral and 10, 17% after 5, 20 mg IV (PDF p9). Resting HR fell 4, 10, 14% after 20, 50, 100 mg oral. Effect decay is zero-order: effect halved about 5 h after 50 mg oral, 6-8 h after 100 mg; mean duration of blockade 13.2, 16.9, 19.2 h after 50, 100, 200 mg oral (Kendall 1977a) (PDF p9).
- Potency: IV:oral equipotent dose ratio 1 to 2.5, matching bioavailability (PDF p9). Maximal exercise-HR reduction 45 beats/min was reached with average daily doses of metoprolol 240 mg, pindolol 20 mg, propranolol 240 mg in 9 volunteers (Gugler 1979); metoprolol and propranolol are equipotent beta-1 blockers by mouth and IV (PDF p9-10).
- Antianginal: a significant therapeutic effect at plasma about 50 ng/mL (about 200 nmol/L; derived check 50 ng/mL / 267 = 187 nmol/L), 1 to 3 h after dosing (Ekelund 1976; PDF p11, printed 567). Dose-dependent effect for acute doses of 50 mg and more.
- Antihypertensive effect: no clear-cut correlation between concentration and effect in individuals; groups on 50, 100, 150 mg twice daily had similar mean plasma levels (PDF p10). Routine plasma monitoring of limited value.
- Metabolites: n-hydroxy (about 50% of parent plasma level after oral; needs about 10x parent concentration for the same blockade, so about 5% of the effect) and O-demethyl metabolites are weakly active; acid metabolites inactive (printed 560, PDF p4).

## Covariates
- Cirrhosis (PDF p7, printed 563): bioavailability up (mean 0.84 vs 0.50, p<0.05), CL 0.61 vs 0.80 L/min and half-life 7.2 vs 4.2 h, not significant; portocaval shunt patients up to 15.3 h half-life (PDF p7-8, printed 563-564).
- Renal failure: parent PK essentially unaffected (GFR 5-55 mL/min, F 59% vs 55%); metabolite half-life rises about 5 h to 20 h (GFR 20) to 60 h (GFR 5) (PDF p6-7, printed 562-563).
- Age 20-65: PK virtually unaffected; elderly data inconsistent (PDF p6, printed 562).
- Hyperthyroidism: AUC lower (enhanced metabolism) (PDF p8, printed 564).
- Pregnancy: crosses placenta, umbilical about equal to maternal blood concentration (Sandstrom 1978); no clearance data for pregnancy are given here (PDF p6, printed 562).
- Food: AUC about +50% with a standard breakfast (Melander 1977). Chronic dosing: AUC rises 10-148% across studies (PDF p2-3).
- Genetic: occasional poor metabolisers (about 40% of dose excreted unchanged, half-life 9.5 h) (PDF p4-5, printed 560-561).

## Caveats
Review, secondary reporting; the exercise-HR concentration/effect numbers are from small healthy-volunteer studies and the review itself says the correlation with therapeutic effect is poor. Pre-CYP2D6-polymorphism era. The text layer is poor (OCR-like), so every number above that is not marked image-confirmed rests on it.
