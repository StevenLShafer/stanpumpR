# Wu 2025: is fondaparinux safe in pregnancy? (narrative review)

Page convention: printed pp. 1461-1471 = PDF pp. 1-11 (PDF page = printed page − 1460). Author names are printed
surname first (Wu Yuexiao, ...).

## Study
Narrative "state-of-the-art" review (West China Second University Hospital); no search strategy, no PRISMA, no
quantitative synthesis. Sections: mechanism, PK and guideline recommendations; adverse events in pregnancy
(bleeding, HIT, teratogenicity and placental transfer, liver injury, allergy, osteopenia); special situations
(renal insufficiency, obesity); monitoring. 97 references.

Dosing summarised: 2.5 mg daily prophylaxis (also used for prophylaxis in pregnancy); 7.5 mg (5 mg <50 kg, 10 mg
>100 kg) for treatment; 1.5 mg daily in moderate renal impairment (CrCl 20-50 mL/min, ref 83); 2.5 mg every 48 h
proposed in severe impairment (ref 84) (printed pp. 1462, 1466).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD values for pregnancy. Placental transfer: ex vivo placenta models show no transfer (refs 62, 63), but in
vivo cord-blood anti-Xa activity was measurable in four women on prophylactic doses with no neonatal adverse
events (ref 64; printed p. 1465). Neuraxial timing: RCOG 36-42 h after the last dose; ASIPP/ASRA at least 4 days
for moderate/high-risk procedures; Queensland 5 days before birth; most reviewed studies stopped 24 h before
delivery; the authors suggest regional anaesthesia 3 days after the last prophylactic dose (printed p. 1463).
Monitoring: no consensus therapeutic range; anti-Xa or TEG may help near delivery, in renal impairment and at
extremes of weight (printed p. 1467).

## Covariates
Renal function: fondaparinux is mainly excreted unchanged in urine; accumulation and bleeding risk rise with renal
impairment; CrCl rises about 50% by the end of the first trimester and stays raised, which could raise
fondaparinux clearance, but the authors find no evidence for dose adjustment in pregnancy and say pregnancy PK
(Vd, t1/2) has not been reported in detail (printed pp. 1466-1467). Weight: lower weight gives higher anti-Xa.

## Caveats
Narrative, unsystematic; evidence mostly case reports and small series, many from Asia. It reports no measured
fondaparinux PK in pregnant women and no pregnant/nonpregnant ratio.
