# Palatini 1993: dose-dependent GHB absorption and elimination

## Study
- 8 healthy non-smoking men, 22-26 y, 66-85 kg (mean 79.2, SD 7.5) (p. 1).
- Single oral doses of 12.5, 25 and 50 mg/kg in random order, 3-day washout, after an overnight fast at 08:00; "GHB
  dissolved in a black cherry syrup" diluted to 150 mL total fluid; light meal at 4 h (p. 2). The paper does not say
  whether the mg/kg are of the free acid or of the sodium salt.
- Samples 0, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45 min, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 6 h. GC-MS, linear 2-200 ug/mL, detection limit 0.2 ug/mL.
- Protein binding by equilibrium dialysis (25 ug/mL in each subject; 3-300 ug/mL in one subject).
- Non-compartmental analysis; CLo = dose/AUC; ANOVA, Wilcoxon for tmax.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. Adverse effects (p. 3): after 12.5 mg/kg, slight dizziness in 3 subjects around tmax for about 15 min;
after 25 and 50 mg/kg, dizziness and/or drowsiness in all, resolving within 20-60 min; nausea in 3 at 50 mg/kg, whose
peak concentrations were 56-98 ug/mL. No ke0 or threshold.

## Covariates
None (dose only). Parameters did not differ from an earlier study in alcohol-dependent patients with compensated liver
disease (p. 3).

## Caveats
- n = 8, single sex. The concentration-time profile was biphasic with a convex terminal limb increasing with dose,
  read as a distributive phase plus non-linear elimination.
- Saturable absorption is the authors' interpretation of rising tmax and falling normalised Cmax; they note saturable
  elimination could mask it. Liechti 2016 could not confirm it at 20 and 35 mg/kg.
- Terminal t1/2 changed little (15%) while CLo halved; the authors attribute this to the terminal slope reflecting
  unsaturated concentrations.
