# Bastian 2017: sublingual buprenorphine exposure lower during than after pregnancy

## Study
- Prospective observational PK study, University of Pittsburgh (Magee-Womens Hospital), enrolled June-Nov 2014 (p2).
- 17 consented; 14 pregnant women analysed (abstract says n = 13); all on twice-daily sublingual buprenorphine
  tablets for opioid use disorder, at steady state on a stable dose for at least 7 days (p2-3).
- Visits: PK-2 at 18-25 wk (n = 7 valid), PK-3 at 31-37 wk (n = 11), PK-P at 4-18 wk postpartum (n = 10).
  35 studies done, 7 excluded (C0 much higher than C12 suggesting non-adherence, 2 switched to Suboxone film,
  1 once-daily dosing), leaving 28; only 6 women were studied at all three visits; all but 1 postpartum
  participant was also studied at least once in pregnancy (p3).
- Table 1 (PDF p3, printed 64.e3), mean ± SD, PK-2 / PK-3 / PK-P:
  age 27.3 ± 5.0 / 27.9 ± 5.2 / 28.6 ± 5.6 y; weight 73.7 ± 7.6 / 74.1 ± 8.3 / 66.5 ± 9.4 kg;
  BMI 28.5 / 28.6 / 25.3 kg/m²; albumin 3.4 / 3.2 / 4.0 g/dL; total protein 5.9 / 6.0 / 6.6 g/dL;
  twice-daily dose 8.0 ± 3.1 (2-12) / 10.0 ± 3.7 (2-16) / 8.0 ± 0.0 mg; GA 22.0 ± 2.5 / 34.0 ± 2.2 wk;
  postpartum 7.1 ± 4.2 wk; smokers ~70% in each group.
  (The text on p3 gives 33.9 wk and 7.4 wk postpartum, not the table's 34.0 and 7.1.)
- After an overnight fast, observed morning dose; blood at 0 (pre-dose), 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 8,
  10, 12 h (Methods p2; the abstract omits 10 h). Sodium-heparin plasma.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS, total plasma buprenorphine; LLOQ 0.05 ng/mL, linear 0.05-50 ng/mL, CV < 15%, bias < 5% (p2).
- Analysis: noncompartmental in Excel (observed Cmax, C0, C12, Tmax; AUC0-12 by trapezoidal rule); log-transformed
  linear mixed models in SAS 9.4, Sidak-adjusted P (p3). Terminal half-life not estimable (12 h interval).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No MEAC or minimum effective concentration; no analgesic or withdrawal end point was measured.
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis.
- No equianalgesic or potency ratio.
- Clinical point only (p5-6): the authors argue that pregnant women may need higher doses; no target
  concentration is given.

## Covariates
Pregnancy state only (PK-2, PK-3, PK-P as a categorical fixed effect). PK-2 and PK-3 did not differ. Trough C0
correlated with AUC0-12 (r² 0.87; 0.57 without 2 outliers; Figure 2, p5). Salivary pH 6.2 / 6.4 / 6.5, no
difference between groups and no correlation with AUC, Cmax, Tmax or dissolution time (p5).

## Caveats
- Small: 7, 11 and 10 valid studies; only partly paired (6 women at all three visits).
- Doses differed by visit (P = .0276), and dose-normalisation assumes linearity.
- Tablets were often broken up by participants (not controlled); adherence was inferred, and 4 studies were
  excluded on the C0 > C12 criterion.
- The comparator is early postpartum (mean about 7 wk), not non-pregnant women.
- Arithmetic means of skewed data (SDs near the means); Figure 1's legend calls the groups PK-1a, PK-1b and PK-2.
- Total plasma buprenorphine; no free fraction, although albumin was lower in pregnancy (Table 1).
- Norbuprenorphine and glucuronides were assayed but not reported.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **AUC about 50% lower in pregnancy** (`R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:246-247`; registry row): the paper says so (p5),
  and Table 2 gives 2.05 vs 4.0 ng·h/mL/mg (derived ratio 0.51). **Matches.**
- **Time to peak and salivary pH unchanged** (`:247`): Tmax P = .8411 (Table 2); pH did not differ (p5). **Matches.**
  The paper did not measure absorption itself; "absorption was unchanged" (`:255`) rests on these surrogates and on Liu 2020.
- **"13-14 women on maintenance, each studied in pregnancy and again after delivery"** (`:245-246`): **discrepancy (wording).**
  14 women were analysed but only 10 valid postpartum studies exist, 6 women were studied at all three
  visits, and one postpartum woman had no valid pregnancy study (p3). The registry's "within-woman pregnancy vs
  postpartum" is likewise only partly paired; a mixed model was used.
- **Registry "AUC p<0.003, Cmax p<0.018"** (`pregnancyRegistry.csv:36`): these are the abstract's values; Table 2 gives
  .0068 and .0365. Both are in the paper. **Matches (abstract).** The registry calls it "Bastian/Caritis 2016": that is the online year;
  the issue is 2017.
- **Factor 1.83** (`R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:321`, `docs/pregnancy.md:77`): the code takes it from Zhu 2025 (218/119), not
  from this paper. As an independent check, Bastian's PK-P/PK-3 ratio is 4.0/2.2 = 1.82 (derived) and the
  whole-pregnancy ratio 1.95, so the paper supports the magnitude. **Consistent (cannot verify the Zhu numbers here).**
- **Baseline of the comparison** (open question): Bastian compared pregnancy with the same cohort early postpartum. The library
  multiplies Bjornsson 2023's non-pregnant clearance. As an uncited cross-check (derived; Bjornsson CL at each group's mean age
  and weight, power-law F, no FFM scaling), the library's AUC0-inf/dose is 3.43 ng·h/mL/mg at the PK-P group's
  covariates against the measured 4.0, and with the 1.83 factor 1.78 (PK-2, 8 mg) and 1.64 (PK-3, 10 mg) against 1.9
  and 2.2. So the library runs 6-25% below these mean exposures, with the largest gap in the third trimester. These are
  arithmetic means of skewed data, so they sit above a typical value. The shortfall is in the same direction in
  pregnancy and postpartum.
- **Unit check**: Bastian's dose-normalised values are per mg (Figure 1 axis) and per 12 h interval at steady state. That
  equals single-dose AUC0-inf only at true steady state. The library's sublingual dose is mg and its concentrations
  are ng/mL; no conversion problem.
- Verdict **partly-verified**: the qualitative claims match. The "each studied ... after delivery" wording overstates the
  pairing. The mapping of the ratio onto CL1 against a non-pregnant baseline, with dose-dependent F, is left open.
