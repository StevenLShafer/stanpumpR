# Lima 2021: CPIC guideline for CYP2C19 and proton pump inhibitors

## Study
Clinical practice guideline (CPIC), systematic literature review with graded evidence (Supplement), for CYP2C19
genotype-guided dosing of omeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole and dexlansoprazole; esomeprazole and rabeprazole
receive no recommendation (CPIC level C). Not about antidepressants.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Genotype-to-phenotype standard (Table 1):

| Predicted phenotype | Definition | Example diplotypes |
|---|---|---|
| Ultrarapid metabolizer (UM) | two increased-function alleles | *17/*17 |
| Rapid metabolizer (RM) | one normal + one increased-function | *1/*17 |
| Normal metabolizer (NM) | two normal-function alleles | *1/*1 |
| Likely intermediate | normal + decreased, increased + decreased, or two decreased | *1/*9, *9/*17, *9/*9 |
| Intermediate metabolizer (IM) | normal + no-function, or increased + no-function | *1/*2, *1/*3, *2/*17, *3/*17 |
| Likely poor | decreased + no-function | *2/*9, *3/*9 |
| Poor metabolizer (PM) | two no-function alleles | *2/*2, *3/*3, *2/*3 |
| Indeterminate | one or two uncertain-function alleles | *1/*12, *2/*12, *12/*14 |

Allele frequencies (Background): *2 about 15% (Europeans, Africans), 25-30% (Asians), about 60% (Oceanians); *3 2-7% in
Asians, 15% Oceanians; *17 about 20% in African, European and Near Eastern populations, low in Asians.
PPI exposure: IM/PM AUC 3-14-fold and Cmax 2-6-fold higher than NM after standard doses.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Table 2 dosing: UM increase starting dose 100%; RM and NM standard dose (consider +50-100% for H. pylori / erosive
esophagitis); IM and PM standard start, consider 50% reduction after > 12 weeks once efficacy achieved; "likely"
phenotypes follow their confirmed counterparts. End points: acid suppression, H. pylori eradication, esophagitis
healing. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
CYP2C19 diplotype only; pediatric considerations (children > 1 yr) included.

## Caveats
PPI-specific; most PPI evidence from Asian populations with little *17; RM/UM recommendations extrapolated from PK.
The phenotype vocabulary (Table 1) is the generic CPIC CYP2C19 standard used across its guidelines.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Five terms ultrarapid / rapid / normal / intermediate / poor: Table 1. **matches**. CPIC also defines "likely
  intermediate", "likely poor" and "indeterminate"; Table 2 footnote says the likely phenotypes take the confirmed
  phenotype's recommendation, so collapsing them into five is consistent with the guideline. "Indeterminate" has no
  stanpumpR equivalent (the default, normal, would be used).
- Citation details (journal, volume, pages 1417-1423, year 2021) correct. **matches**.
- Mapping to the escitalopram source (Liu 2022): Liu's EM (*1/*1), IM (*1/*2, *1/*3) and PM (*2/*2, *2/*3) coincide
  with CPIC NM, IM and PM; Liu did not genotype *17, so CPIC RM/UM have no estimate there, as R/drugs_escitalopram.R
  states.
- Note: this is the PPI guideline; the CPIC guideline specific to SSRIs (Hicks 2015, updated Bousman 2023) uses the
  same phenotype terms and would be the more natural citation for escitalopram/citalopram dosing context.
