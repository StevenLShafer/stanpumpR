# Short 1994: midazolam and flumazenil PK given together

## Study
12 ASA I men (mean 31 y, range 25-41; 70 kg, 63-85; 172 cm, 160-185). Each had midazolam 0.15 mg/kg IV and, immediately after, flumazenil 0.015 mg/kg IV, each over 10 s into separate arms. Venous plasma at 2.5-360 min (15 samples). HPLC assay of both drugs together (linear 10-500 ng/mL midazolam, 1-50 ng/mL flumazenil). Protein binding by equilibrium dialysis. Software SIPHAR; 2- vs 3-compartment chosen by Schwartz criterion; biexponential fitted best in every subject. Psychomotor tests (critical flicker fusion, two 10-cm linear analogue scales) to 360 min. Free concentrations of both drugs went into a receptor-occupancy model (affinity constant 1 nmol for both drugs, quoted from Amrein). A 60-min midazolam infusion at a constant 500 ng/mL followed by flumazenil 0.015 mg/kg 5 min later was simulated from the group mean kinetics.

Per-kg values; no absolute conversion given. Midazolam Vc ranges 0.084-0.812 L/kg (subject 11 is 0.084, with t½alpha 1.0 min), so Vc is poorly determined (authors admit sparse early sampling, four samples in 15 min). Within-person correlation of the two drugs (Table 3, Kendall): Cltot P<0.001; t½alpha 0.075; t½beta 0.34; Vc 0.27; Vss 0.58; binding 0.25. Individual midazolam t½beta was 0.49 to 3.17 times flumazenil's.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper; no MEAC. No ke0 was fitted. For context the authors quote an EEG t½ke0 for midazolam of 2.2 (SD 1.3) min from Breimer (their ref 7, 15 mg IV); flumazenil's was not known to them. Effect: receptor occupancy R = C/(C+Ki) for the agonist, C/(C+Ki(1+Ci/Ki)) in the presence of the antagonist, on free concentrations. Correlation of calculated midazolam occupancy with percent change from baseline: CFF r=0.50 (P<0.01), LAS alert/drowsy r=0.56, LAS relaxed/anxious r=0.54 (P<0.005). Psychomotor tests returned to within 10% of baseline at 240 min (CFF, drowsy) and 360 min (relaxed/anxious). Occupancy range tested was narrow. Table 4 lists past flumazenil reversal trials (doses, not PK).

## Covariates
None tested (healthy men, one cohort). Protein binding is the authors' proposed source of variation in free midazolam (free fraction 2.5 times higher in the lowest-bound subject than in the highest).

## Caveats
Venous sampling; sparse early samples; simultaneous bolus dosing is not clinical practice; plasma free-concentration assumed equal to brain; metabolites ignored; occupancy-effect correlation is modest. Subject 9 and 10 binding values are missing or shifted in the text layer; the page image shows subject 9 blank.
