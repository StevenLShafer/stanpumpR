# McEwan 1993: isoflurane MAC reduction by steady-state fentanyl

## Study
Randomized study at Duke. 77 unpremedicated ASA 1-2 adults aged 20-60 for elective surgery (exclusions: inhalational
induction contraindicated, significant organ disease, MAC-altering drugs, alcohol or drug abuse, head/neck or prone
surgery). Allocated to no fentanyl or target plasma fentanyl 0.8, 1.5 or 3.0 ng/mL, plus 8 patients added at the
end for targets above 3 ng/mL (Fig. 1 shows 4.0 and 6.0); each also randomized to a preset end-tidal isoflurane
(PDF p2, printed p865). Fentanyl by CACI (computer-assisted continuous infusion) using the McClain and Hug
pharmacokinetic set; isoflurane-in-oxygen induction, succinylcholine 1 mg/kg for intubation, neuromuscular function
checked before incision, mechanical ventilation to normocapnia, temperature above 35.5 C. End-tidal isoflurane held
at least 15 min, and at least 20 min from start of infusion to incision. Venous fentanyl sampled 10 min after
start, 5 min before and immediately after incision; RIA (Janssen kit), LOD 0.2 ng/mL, CV < 10%. End point: gross
purposeful movement within 60 s of skin incision (cough, chewing, swallowing excluded). Patients whose pre- and
post-incision fentanyl differed by more than 30% and 0.5 ng/mL were excluded (4), leaving 73: 30 men, 43 women,
age 36 +/- 10 yr (20-57), weight 75 +/- 15.0 kg (46-123) (PDF p3, printed p866). Measured concentrations tended to
exceed targets; pre-incision values (used in the analysis) ranged 0-10.6 ng/mL. Analysis: maximum-likelihood
logistic regression (Appendix).

## Model and parameters
Logistic model (Appendix, PDF p6): ln(P/(1-P)) = b0 + b1 x1 + b2 x2 + b12 x1 x2, P = probability of response,
with x1 stated as ln(1 + isoflurane %) and x2 = ln(1 + fentanyl ng/mL). Estimates b0 = 0.6673, b1 = -3.6952,
b2 = -2.5982, b12 = 0 (confirmed on the rendered page).

Internal consistency (derived, my arithmetic): with x1 = ln(1 + isoflurane) as the Appendix states, the estimates
give a MAC at zero fentanyl of exp(0.6673/3.6952) - 1 = 0.20%, not Table 1's 1.20%. With x1 = ln(isoflurane) they
reproduce Table 1: MAC0 = exp(0.1806) = 1.198%, 0.736% at 1 ng/mL, 0.452% at 3 ng/mL, and a 50% reduction at
fentanyl 1.67 ng/mL. The printed isoflurane transform is therefore inconsistent with the printed estimates and
results; the paper does not say which is intended. The two MAC-alone values (1.23 from the isoflurane-only group,
1.20 from the model at fentanyl 0) are both printed and come from different calculations.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: movement at skin incision (MAC). Plasma fentanyl 1.67 ng/mL (1.11-2.38) halves isoflurane MAC; 1
  ng/mL gives 39%, 3 ng/mL 63%, and a ceiling with 10 ng/mL giving only a further 19% (abstract; Table 1).
- No MEAC is reported. ke0: not measured; the Discussion quotes a fentanyl T1/2 ke0 of 6.4 +/- 1.3 min from Scott
  1985 (EEG end point) to justify the 20-min (about three half-lives) equilibration (PDF p4).
- Potency: the authors propose the concentration giving a 50% MAC reduction as a measure of opioid potency, with
  concentrations giving equal reduction considered equipotent; no ratio to another opioid is given.
- Dosing note (Discussion): 3 mcg/kg over 5 min then 1 mcg/kg/h gives about 1 ng/mL; doubling doubles it. Above 2
  ng/mL may bring significant respiratory depression (cited).

## Covariates
None examined (age, sex and weight not modelled).

## Caveats
- Venous, not arterial, fentanyl (authors cite minimal arteriovenous difference after 10 min of CACI).
- Few patients above 3 ng/mL (8 added at the end); the ceiling rests on them.
- Isoflurane only; the authors caution against applying the values to other volatiles (enflurane in dogs needs
  more fentanyl).
- Appendix transform inconsistent with the estimates (above).
