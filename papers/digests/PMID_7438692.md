# Reece 1980: intravenous and oral hydralazine by a specific assay, slow and fast acetylators

PDF page n = printed page 768 + n.

## Study
Ten healthy volunteers, five slow and five fast acetylators of sulfamethazine (Table I, PDF p3, rendered): nine
men and one woman (Y.C., fast); ages 22-39 y (means 29 and 25); weights 57-86 kg (means 76 and 70; pooled mean
72.9, derived). Each received IV and oral hydralazine at least 1 week apart, fasted.
- IV: 0.375 mg/kg "HP, Apresoline" diluted in 20 mL water, constant-rate infusion over 5 min into an antecubital
  vein; samples from the contralateral forearm before, at 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 40 min and 1-10 h after (p2).
- Oral: 1.0 mg/kg (fast) or 0.5 mg/kg (slow) as 25/50 mg tablets to the nearest 5 mg; samples 0-10 h (p2-3).
- Assay: HPLC-fluorescence, specific for hydralazine (HP), its pyruvic acid hydrazone (HPPAH) and MTP; samples
  iced, centrifuged and treated immediately (p3). "Apparent HP" (non-specific) also measured.
- Analysis: individual post-infusion data fitted to a two-compartment model with infusion terms (eq 1-3),
  initial estimates by a calculator program, final fit by SPSS NONLINEAR, weighting 1/Cp; Vss and Varea by
  Wagner's equations; AUC by trapezoids plus Clast/beta; Student's t test (p3, p4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect model, no ke0. IV: within 15 to 30 min of the infusion diastolic pressure fell 23 +/- 3
(slow) and 26 +/- 7 (fast) mm Hg from 72 and 73; heart rate rose 28 and 26 bpm (PDF p7, rendered). Oral: diastolic
fall 17 and 20 mm Hg within 0.75-1.25 h. Flushing began 5-10 min after the infusion; all had headache, three
vomited. The authors find no correlation between the HP time course (t1/2 25-40 min) and the hours-long
hypotensive effect, and list an active long-lived metabolite or binding to vessel walls as proposed explanations
(Discussion, p9). Duration of action not determined (no placebo control). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Acetylator phenotype: no IV parameter differed (Table II); oral F and dose-corrected AUC 2.2 and 2.5 times higher
in slow acetylators. No weight, age or renal covariate analysed.

## Caveats
n = 10, young, almost all male, healthy; no pooled estimates; doses as "HP" with no salt stated (the product is
the hydrochloride); assay limits HP to the first 1-2.5 h (Fig 2), so the terminal phase is short; oral half-life
shorter than IV, unexplained by the authors.
