# Osborne 1991: patient-controlled propofol sedation vs midazolam + fentanyl

## Study
Randomised, non-blinded comparison, 40 fasted, unpremedicated ASA 1 day-surgery patients (n=20 per group; 3 males in group 1, 1 in group 2; Table 1: age about 21 y in both groups) having third-molar extraction under local analgesia, one surgeon.
- Group 1: anaesthetist-titrated IV midazolam and fentanyl to half to full eyelid closure with prompt response to voice; fentanyl capped at 100 ug total.
- Group 2: patient-controlled sedation (PCS) with propofol. An initial 0.7 mg/kg bolus from the anaesthetist, then self-administered 0.7 mg/kg boluses delivered at 16.7 mg/min, with a 1 min lockout (text p554; modified PCA pump).
- Mean doses (p554): propofol 5.29 mg/kg (SD 2.68, range 2.10-12.04); midazolam 0.11 mg/kg (SD 0.04, 0.05-0.16); fentanyl 1.78 ug/kg (SD 0.28, 1.00-2.30). PCS group averaged 6.7 (SD 3.8) successful and 31.6 (SD 53.6) unsuccessful demands; effective lockout about 3.5 min because each bolus took about 2.5 min to deliver.
- Tests: FAST index (computerised choice-of-flashing-lights test, accuracy % correct) and p-deletion, pre-op, about 30 min after recovery arrival and at discharge; telephone follow-up day 1.
- No blood sampling, no drug assay, no PK modelling.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio. End points are depth of sedation (observer scale), patient satisfaction, amnesia, FAST index and p-deletion.
- No PCS propofol patient was deeper than full eyelid closure with response to voice; 3 patients in group 1 needed mild physical stimulus (p554).
- Satisfaction (Tables 2-3): "good" 19/20 propofol vs 11/20 midazolam-fentanyl (p<0.01); would repeat 20/20 vs 15/20 (p<0.05).
- Amnesia for recovery-room events (Table 4): 0/20 vs 6/20 (p<0.05).
- Propofol dose correlated with procedure duration (r=0.441, p=0.052) and surgical difficulty (rs=0.553, p=0.016); midazolam did not.
- Fitted FAST accuracy, group 1: 42.03 + 0.65(predrug) - 186.93 x midazolam dose (mg/kg); group 2: 28.61 + 0.65(predrug); group 2 about 6 percentage points higher at about 0.1 mg/kg midazolam (p555-556).
- Opioid audit: fentanyl was given only as an adjunct in group 1; no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Surgeon, sex, age and surgical difficulty were tested and nonsignificant. Predrug score, test time and midazolam/propofol dose (mg/kg) were included; only predrug score and midazolam dose (and recovery time for p-deletion) were retained.

## Caveats
Not blinded; groups differ in drug and in mode of delivery, so the effects cannot be separated (authors say so); healthy young patients; the authors call the p-deletion test not very sensitive.
