# Backes 1985: group B streptococcal endocarditis, five Mayo Clinic cases

## Study
Case series, Mayo Clinic, 1970-1983: 5 adults (3 M, 2 F; mean age 50.4 y, range 18-72) with group B streptococcal
infective endocarditis (aortic 4, mitral 1) (PDF p1 = printed p693; Table 1, PDF p2 = printed p694). MIC and MBC of
each isolate for penicillin, cefazolin, vancomycin, clindamycin, streptomycin and gentamicin, by methods referenced
elsewhere (refs 14, 15; inoculum and medium not stated in this paper). Treatment (Table 1): penicillin G 12-20 x 10^6
units/day for 7-28 days, most with streptomycin or gentamicin; one case vancomycin then penicillin. Four cured,
one died (brain-stem embolus, day 28). No cefazolin was given to any patient. No pharmacokinetics.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
In-vitro MIC and MBC only. Abstract and results (p1) state cefazolin's in-vitro activity against group B streptococci
was similar to penicillin's, and that penicillin and cefazolin were the most active agents. Penicillin-streptomycin
synergy in vitro in 3 of 5 strains; rabbit endocarditis model (strains 4, 5) killing increased by adding streptomycin
(P < .01, unpublished). Authors suggest cefazolin or vancomycin for 4 weeks in penicillin-intolerant patients (p3).
Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Five isolates; MIC/MBC method not described here; 1970-83 strains. "Similar" is a qualitative judgement: cefazolin
MICs (0.06-0.13) are 1.4-4x the penicillin MICs (0.0325-0.09) (derived) and one strain was cefazolin-tolerant by MBC.
Text/table inconsistency on vancomycin MIC in case 1 (above).
