# Smith 2012: 72-h clevidipine infusion in mild to moderate essential hypertension

## Study
- Randomised, single-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study (Methods, p1386). Sponsored by The Medicines
  Company.
- 61 adults aged 18-80 with essential hypertension, off antihypertensives for 7 days: SBP 140-200 and/or DBP
  95-115 mmHg, HR < 120 bpm.
- Four cohorts with target doses of 2, 4, 8 and 16 mg/h. Dosing started at 2 mg/h and doubled every 3 min to the
  target, then held for 72 h. Placebo was Intralipid.
- Table 1 (ITT, rendered p5), clevidipine n = 10, 10, 18 and 10 by cohort, placebo n = 13:
  - Mean weight 87.4, 105.3, 93.6 and 99.9 kg (placebo 88.1); BMI 30-34.
  - Baseline SBP 152, 143, 150 and 155 mmHg (placebo 151); baseline HR differed between cohorts (P = 0.0033).
- Per-protocol population: 40 clevidipine (10 per cohort) and 13 placebo. Seven subjects were excluded for
  incorrect blood sampling and one withdrew.
- Blood clevidipine by LC/MS/MS, LLOQ 0.2 ng/mL, sampled 0.5-72 h on drug and 2-60 min after the stop.
- PK: NCA in WinNonlin 5.0.1. Css is the mean at 54-72 h.
- Dose-response: a random-coefficient regression of %ΔSBP on the infusion rate, with a squared term.

Notes on the PK results:
- **Less than dose-proportional:** for an 8-fold increase in dose, Cmax rose about 4.7-fold, Css about 6.7-fold and
  AUC about 5.9-fold (p1390). Mean clearance was nonetheless "similar" across doses.
- (Derived: rate/mean Css gives 24.3 L/min at 2 mg/h and 29.0 at 16 mg/h. Per kg at about 95 kg that is about
  0.3 L/min/kg, roughly 2-2.5 times the Ericsson venous-blood estimates.)
- No samples were taken in the first 2 min after the stop, so the authors read the half-lives as the second and
  terminal phases. They are not the initial phase of about 1 min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **End point:** % change from baseline in SBP (automated cuff). The driver is the dose rate.
- **Table 3 predictions (PP), % change in SBP (95% CI):**
  - 2 mg/h: +0.83 (-1.16, 2.82)
  - 4 mg/h: -3.99 (-6.29, -1.69)
  - 8 mg/h: -11.20 (-14.61, -7.80)
  - 10 mg/h: -13.59 (-17.23, -9.95)
  - 16 mg/h: -15.93 (-22.32, -9.53)
- Each 1 mg/h added roughly a 1-1.5% further fall in SBP, or 2-4 mmHg for 1-2 mg/h (p1392).
- **Maximal effect:** an inconsistency in the source (p1391-1393).
  - The text says the maximal predicted change "was estimated at -22%" at 16 mg/h, but quotes the point estimate as
    -15.93% with -22.32% as the lower CI limit. The ITT version is "-25%" with a point estimate of -16.77%.
  - The Discussion gives a maximal effect of 25% of baseline SBP, with half of it at about 10 mg/h.
  - The headline figures appear to be CI limits rather than point estimates. Unresolved.
- **Concentration-response:** Css against %ΔSBP was shallow with no plateau (Fig 4). No model was fitted, and no
  EC50 or ke0 is reported.
- No hysteresis or tolerance over 72 h, judged visually in the 16 mg/h cohort. No rebound at 4 or 6 h after the
  stop. There was a non-significant trend to above-baseline pressure at 8 h; placebo was below baseline at 6-8 h.

## Covariates
None modelled.

## Caveats
- NCA on sparse post-infusion samples. Later-phase half-lives rest on n = 3-9 with SDs near the means.
- Large between-subject variability in clearance.
- The 2 mg/h cohort fell more than the 4 mg/h cohort, which the authors attribute to its higher baseline SBP.
- Rare triglyceride rises above 500 mg/dL.
