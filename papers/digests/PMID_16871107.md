# Chen 2006: racial and ethnic differences in febrile-infant UTI

## Study
Retrospective chart review of one urban paediatric emergency department, 5/2002 to 1/2003. 465 infants aged 1 to 24 months with a urine culture as part of a fever work-up (rectal temperature above 37.9 C). Race/ethnicity as reported by parents: white 207 (45%), Hispanic 125 (27%), African American 95 (20%), Asian 18 (3.9%), other 20 (4.3%). 97% of samples by urethral catheterisation. A positive culture was a single pathogen above 10,000 CFU/mL (catheter), 1,000 CFU/mL (suprapubic) or 100,000 CFU/mL (bag). Logistic regression, SPSS 11.5.

Results (Table 1, p. 486): 64 UTIs, overall prevalence 14% (95% CI 11-17%). UTI rate by group: Asian 22%, white 16%, Hispanic 16%, other 11%, African American 4.0% (P = 0.007 versus the general population). Odds ratios against African American infants: white 4.4 (95% CI 1.5-12.6), Hispanic 4.6 (1.5-13.9), Asian 6.5 (1.5-29); unchanged after adjustment for sex, age and fever height.

## Covariates

## Caveats
