# Crevoisier 1983: midazolam plasma concentration and psychomotor effect, oral and IV

## Study
Double-blind, double-dummy crossover in six healthy volunteers (medical students; Table 1: ages 22-27, five men, one woman, 55-77 kg, 171-184 cm). Each had midazolam 0.15 mg/kg IV, 20 mg orally and placebo; four also had 10 mg orally and two had 40 mg orally, in fasting subjects at 14-day intervals. Plasma sampled over 12 h (pharmacodynamic tests at baseline, 15, 30, 45 min, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 24 h). Assay: unchanged midazolam and free alpha-hydroxymidazolam in plasma by gas chromatography with electron-capture detection (Heizmann and von Alten 1981); concentrations are plasma, parent drug total (not unbound). Effects: tracing test (errors counted), Vienna reaction time (mean of 20 responses, ms), subject self-rating scales, investigator's rating. Hill equation fitted by non-linear regression; hysteresis loops drawn per Holford and Sheiner 1981. The pharmacokinetics themselves are in Heizmann 1983 (cited as a separate paper), not here.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic parameters are given. The paper's model is the sigmoid Hill equation, E = E0 + Emax.CE^gamma / (CE50^gamma + CE^gamma), with the plasma concentration used directly as CE. Table 2 (PDF p7, printed 57S), confirmed on the page image:

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak is reported. No hysteresis loop was demonstrable for reaction time against plasma concentration (Figure 4, subject R.H., IV and oral 20 mg; PDF p6), from which the authors conclude plasma-to-receptor equilibration is rapid and the effect tracks the central-compartment concentration. The first pharmacodynamic sample is at 15 min, so equilibration faster than that could not be resolved (derived from the sampling schedule). Peak plasma concentration and maximum effect both came within 30 min; maximum deviation from placebo on the tracing test at 15 min. Duration: baseline reaction time regained at about 1.5 h after IV 0.15 mg/kg and 2 h after oral 10 mg; 4 h after oral 20 mg, when plasma concentrations of 20-75 ng/ml were still measurable. Spearman rank correlation of log concentration with reaction time 0.70 (n = 198) and with tracing errors 0.78 (n = 200), both P < 0.001. At the same plasma midazolam concentration, the oral dose gave a larger effect than IV (Figure 5; CE50 100 oral against 165 IV in subject R.H.), attributed to the active alpha-hydroxy metabolite formed by first-pass metabolism (about 50% first-pass, from Heizmann 1983); using total (parent plus metabolite) concentration gave a closer relation (rs 0.97 against 0.94 for parent alone in R.H.) and the IV and oral curves approximated each other (Figure 6, CE50 157). Minimum effective plasma concentration: 30 to 100 ng/ml across the six volunteers, midazolam plasma, GC-ECD, healthy volunteers, psychomotor tests plus subjective and investigator ratings; it is the concentration below which no effect was demonstrable by subjective or objective methods (the paper does not give a threshold definition). The two 40-mg subjects had concentrations up to 1400 ng/ml and could not be tested for the first 75 min, so the upper curve above 400 ng/ml is not plotted. No equianalgesic or other potency ratio (midazolam is not an analgesic); only the oral-versus-IV shift above.

## Covariates
None modelled. Route (and hence alpha-hydroxymidazolam exposure) shifts the parent-drug concentration-effect curve; subject R.H. only for the route-specific fits.

## Caveats
Six healthy young volunteers; Hill parameters for individual figures come from one subject (R.H., 11 pairs per route, 21 for the total-concentration curve). Parent-drug CE50 is route dependent, so a single plasma CE50 transfers poorly from oral to IV data. Psychomotor tests (reaction time, tracing), not sedation scores or EEG. Concentrations are total plasma, unbound fraction not measured here. The data above 400 ng/ml are missing.
