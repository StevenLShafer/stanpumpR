# Shore-Lesserson 2018: STS/SCA/AmSECT guideline on anticoagulation during bypass

## Study
Joint clinical practice guideline of the Society of Thoracic Surgeons, the Society of Cardiovascular
Anesthesiologists and AmSECT (co-published in Ann Thorac Surg and J Extra Corpor Technol). Systematic PubMed
search, January 2000 to December 2015: 833 abstracts, 286 full papers reviewed, 96 included, plus 17 pre-2000
"sentinel" papers (PDF p. 1-2, printed 413-414). Recommendations graded by ACCF/AHA class and level of evidence;
more than two-thirds agreement required. Adult evidence only for almost all topics. Three areas: heparin dosing
and monitoring; heparin contraindications and alternatives (HIT, bivalirudin); reversal (protamine). No primary
data, no PK modelling.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model or parameter estimates. Dosing and target figures quoted in the text (all from cited
studies, not new data):

- Heparin initial bolus: fixed weight-based, e.g. 300 IU/kg (PDF p. 3, printed 415; rendered page).
- Grima: 100 IU/kg x 3 pre-CPB doses vs a single 300 IU/kg bolus (p. 3).
- Neema: 6 of 100 patients given 300 IU/kg had post-heparin ACT < 350 s (p. 3); redosing one third of the bolus at
  90 min of CPB then every 60 min (p. 4, printed 416).
- Garvin: 3,880 patients; initial bolus vs first post-heparin ACT r^2 = 0.03 (p. 3).
- Despotis: total heparin 612 +/- 147 vs 462 +/- 114 U/kg (concentration-based vs ACT-based, ACT > 480 s) (p. 4).
- Gravlee: subclinical coagulation despite heparin concentration > 4.1 IU/mL; 400 IU/kg with concentration
  > 4 IU/mL vs 200 IU/kg plus top-ups for ACT < 400 s (p. 4).
- Residual heparin after protamine: mean 0.18 IU/mL, chromogenic anti-Xa (p. 4).
- Heparin dosing above 400 IU/kg can cause heparin rebound; 10% to 15% have detectable heparin 2 h after
  protamine (p. 8, printed 420).
- Protamine: limit to < 2.6 mg protamine per 100 units heparin (Class IIa, LOE C); platelet function impaired
  above 5:1 (p. 8). Post-CPB protamine infusion 25 mg/h for up to 6 h for rebound (Class IIb, LOE C; RCT of 300) (p. 8).
- Bivalirudin: half-life about 25 min with normal renal function (p. 6, printed 418; and Conclusion p. 9).
  CHOOSE-ON/EVOLUTION-ON dosing: load 1.0 mg/kg, infusion 2.5 mg/kg/h, pump prime 50 mg, target ACT 2.5 x baseline
  (PDF p. 7, printed 419; rendered page). ECT vs bivalirudin concentration R^2 = 0.91; celite-ACT vs ECT-determined
  concentration R^2 = 0.93 (p. 6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is the activated clotting time (whole blood), with anti-Xa activity as the laboratory reference.

- Class IIa (LOE C): reasonable to keep ACT above 480 s during CPB; the threshold is an approximation and varies
  with instrument bias; for maximally activated whole-blood or microcuvette instruments, values above 400 s are
  frequently considered therapeutic (PDF p. 3, printed 415; confirmed on the rendered page).
- History of the threshold (p. 3): Bull (1970s) no circuit clot with ACT > 300 s; Young raised it to 400 s (rhesus
  monkeys, 5 children); 480 s became the standard "to maintain a margin of safety" above 400 s, on limited evidence.
- No upper ACT limit is given anywhere in the guideline.
- Patteril: a new ACT device gave mean 557 vs 618 s (p < 0.05) and needed more heparin to reach 480 s (p. 5,
  printed 417).
- Bivalirudin: ACT 2.5 x baseline (p. 7). No concentration target in mcg/mL is stated.
- No ke0, EC50 or Emax for heparin are reported. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Text notes antithrombin III level, hypothermia, hemodilution, hypofibrinogenemia, thrombocytosis
and infective endocarditis alter heparin response or the ACT (p. 3-4); renal function governs bivalirudin
clearance (p. 7).

## Caveats
Almost all recommendations are Level of Evidence B or C; the 480 s figure itself is described as resting on
limited evidence. Adult evidence only. ACT values are instrument-specific and not interchangeable. Literature
search ended December 2015. Heparin concentrations quoted are a mix of whole-blood (protamine titration) and plasma
anti-Xa measurements and are not directly comparable to each other.
