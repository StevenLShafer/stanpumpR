# Itthipanichpong 2005: celecoxib PK in healthy Thai men

## Study
- Single-dose, open study, 18 healthy Thai men (resident in Thailand at least two generations), age 19-23 y (mean 20.94, SD 1.21), weight 54.5-71.5 kg (mean 63.00, SD 5.17), height 165-187 cm (mean 173.22), BMI mean 20.95 (Table 1, PDF p 4-5).
- 200 mg celecoxib capsule orally with 200 mL water after overnight fast. Blood (sodium heparin) before dosing and at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 24 and 48 h. 48 h samples all <LOQ (Table 2).
- Assay: plasma celecoxib (total), reversed-phase HPLC, fluorescence detection, ibuprofen internal standard; calibration 50-3200 ng/mL, recovery 90-110%, CV <15% (PDF p 3-4).
- Analysis: non-compartmental (trapezoidal AUC, kel from terminal log-linear slope, t1/2 = 0.693/kel). No bioavailability exists, so the authors state CL means CL/F and Vd means Vd/F; Vd = CL/kel (terminal-phase volume). No population model, no software named.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect measurement, no ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak, no MEAC or analgesic concentration. Not an opioid. Nothing to audit.

## Covariates
None analysed (single sex, narrow age and weight range).

## Caveats
- Men only, aged 19-23, one 200 mg fasting dose; no food, no CYP2C9 genotyping (CYP2C9 is cited as the main route), no variability model.
- Terminal half-life is highly variable (3.5-19.9 h) and several subjects show double peaks in Table 2 (e.g. subjects 1, 8, 12, 17), consistent with the slow, irregular absorption of a poorly soluble drug; kel and Vd from a late slope on 24 h data are uncertain (AUC extrapolated using Ct/kel).
- Misprints: unit of Vd and CL in abstract and text; Cmax 686 vs 686.83; Tmax 2.5 vs 2.55 (Discussion).
- Total plasma concentration; unbound not measured.
