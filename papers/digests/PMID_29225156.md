# Lee 2018: Schnider-model TCI propofol on cardiopulmonary bypass (pilot)

## Study
Prospective predictive-performance (validation) study, Singapore General Hospital; IRB approval, consent waived because remnant samples were used. N = 10 adults (8 men, 2 women), age 46-81 y (mean 60), BMI 21.0-27.8 (mean 24.5) (PDF p5, printed 727), elective CABG with or without valve replacement needing CPB, ejection fraction at least 50%. Excluded: BMI over 35, chronic sedative or opioid use, drug or alcohol history, drug allergy, severe physiological impairment. Single cardiac anaesthesiologist.

Anaesthesia: fentanyl 100 mcg bolus, propofol by TCI with the Schnider PK model on a Fresenius Kabi Injectomat TIVA Agilia pump, initial effect-site target Ce 5 mcg/mL, then titrated to BIS 30-60 in steps of 0.1 mcg/mL Ce; atracurium bolus and infusion; morphine up to 1.5 mg/kg in titrated boluses. CPB circuit primed 1.3 or 1.4 L (PDF p2).

Sampling: 5 mL arterial blood at 5 time points: 5 min before CPB, 15 min after starting CPB, 60 min after starting CPB, 20 min after rewarming, and after coming off CPB; taken at TCI steady state, when the pump's calculated plasma and effect-site concentrations equalled the Ce target (PDF p2). 50 samples. Assay: liquid-liquid extraction, GC-MS, total propofol in whole blood, validated in house; bias -7.8% to 5.3%, lower limit of detection 0.1 mcg/mL (PDF p3-4 text).

Analysis: Varvel measures, PE = (Cm - Cp)/Cp x 100% (positive = under-prediction), MDPE and MDAPE per patient and overall; Bland-Altman (Cp - Cm) against the mean of Cp and Cm; GLLAMM (Stata 13) for PE before, on and after bypass; Pearson correlations of BIS and MAP with Cp and Cm.

## Model and parameters
No model is fitted; the paper tests the Schnider 1998 propofol model as implemented on the pump and gives no model parameters.

Results:
- Overall MDPE 45% and MDAPE 45% over all samples (PDF p4-5, text and abstract). Per patient MDPE 5% to 160%, MDAPE 13% to 160%; 20% of patients had MDPE below 20%, 30% had MDAPE below 35% (PDF p5). Only 4% of samples had a negative PE.
- Per-patient MDPE (Table 1, PDF p3-4, image; the printed value equals the median of the 5 listed PEs where I checked, subjects 1-4): subject 1 11.11, 2 120, 3 5, 4 25, 5 160, 6 43.33, 7 66.67, 8 80, 9 46.67, 10 53.33. The continuation of Table 1 on PDF p4 is rotated and its column headers read "Subject 1" and "Subject 2" although the rows are subjects 9 and 10 (their Ce and Cm on PDF p3 give PE 82.14 and 83.33 at Time 1). Table 1 target Ce ranged 1.3-4 mcg/mL; measured Cm 1.6-11 mcg/mL.
- Bland-Altman, all samples (Fig 1, PDF p5, image): bias (Cp - Cm) -1.76 mcg/mL (95% CI -2.29 to -1.22); limits of agreement -5.56 (-6.48 to -4.63) and 2.04 (1.12 to 2.97) mcg/mL. Bias grew more negative with higher mean concentration, r = -0.838. For mean concentration below 4.5 mcg/mL (Fig 2): bias -1.09 (-1.32 to -0.87), limits -2.69 (-3.08 to -2.30) and 0.51 (-0.42 to 1.43), t test p = 0.03.
- Mean PE by phase (Table 2, GLLAMM, image): pre-bypass 135.7% (95% CI 82.8-188.6, reference); on bypass 45.6% (9.2-82.1), coefficient -90.1 (-144.3 to -35.8), p = 0.001; post-bypass 97.1% (44.2-150), coefficient -38.6 (-105 to 27.8), p = 0.282. Mean measured propofol 5.39 (pre), 3.77 (on bypass), 4.35 mcg/mL (post) (Fig 3).
- BMI: the three patients with BMI below 22 (21.0-21.96) had the highest MDPE and MDAPE, all above 70%; GLLAMM coefficient for BMI above 22 -92.2 (-140.9 to -43.5), p < 0.001 (PDF p5). The Discussion (PDF p7) says "below 23" for the same three patients.
- BIS and MAP not correlated with Cp or Cm (r = 0.06 to 0.12).
- Worked example in the paper: target 3 mcg/mL corresponds to an expected measured mean 4.76 mcg/mL, range 0.96-8.56 (from the bias and limits of agreement, PDF p6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
BIS was the titration end point (target 30-60) and had no correlation with predicted or measured concentration. No ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio reported; no opioid-propofol interaction analysis (fentanyl and morphine were given and are discussed as possible causes of under-prediction). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
BMI (above vs below 22) and bypass phase only, as above. No other covariate modelled; drugs other than fentanyl and morphine were not recorded (PDF p8).

## Caveats
- Pilot: 10 patients, 5 samples each, convenience sample; the authors say it may be underpowered.
- The comparator is the pump's Schnider prediction at a target that was set as an effect-site concentration; the "predicted" value Cp is the pump's calculated plasma concentration, equal to Ce at the sampling moment, so PE measures plasma accuracy only.
- Bias is plausibly confounded by fentanyl, heparin (unbound-fraction rise), hypothermia, haemodilution and circuit sequestration, which the paper discusses but cannot separate; there is no control group off CPB. Pre-bypass samples (before CPB, after fentanyl and propofol induction at Ce 5) are the worst (mean PE 136%), which argues against the circuit as the main cause.
- Arterial sampling and a whole-blood total-propofol assay, compared with the model's plasma concentration; the paper does not discuss blood-versus-plasma differences.
- Mixed prediction-error conventions: PE uses (Cm - Cp)/Cp, Bland-Altman uses Cp - Cm, so a positive PE corresponds to a negative difference.
- Two small internal inconsistencies noted: BMI 22 vs 23 (above), and the Discussion refers to a study "involving 19 patients (4 of whom were eventually excluded)" while this study enrolled 10.
- Comparator for "under-prediction": measured concentrations against the Schnider model's own predictions, in these 10 cardiac patients; not against Marsh or Eleveld.

For context only: the library's Schnider model (`R/alternativeModels.R:193-229`, `propofolSchnider`, "as STANPUMP ran it", with the 1.6 min time to peak effect from Schnider 1999) is the model tested here, shown as the optional "Minto / Schnider" companion; the default propofol model is a different one (`R/drugs_propofol.R`; Eleveld is named there), and the library has no cardiopulmonary-bypass propofol set (no mention of bypass in the propofol files or help). The paper's finding, an average 45% under-prediction of whole-blood propofol with Schnider on CPB, was measured on a pump's Schnider implementation in a 10-patient cohort; it is evidence about the Schnider companion in cardiac surgery and does not bear on the default model.
