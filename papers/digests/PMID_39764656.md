# Karanam 2025: MONEAD population model of lamotrigine clearance in pregnancy and postpartum

## Study
- MONEAD: prospective observational cohort, 20 US sites, December 2012 to February 2016 (abstract; p. 34). Women with epilepsy aged 14-45 y, IQ > 70, on lamotrigine; pregnant women recruited before 20 weeks' gestation. Seven visits: pregnant women at three pregnancy visits, delivery and 3, 6, 9 months postpartum; nonpregnant controls at matched intervals over 18 months. Doses managed clinically, not by protocol.
- Table 1 (rendered, PDF p. 4): 170 pregnant women (936 samples) and 51 nonpregnant (291 samples), 1227 samples in all (derived sum). Median age 31.0 (20-43) and 29.0 (16-40) y. Mostly White, non-Hispanic (146/170, 47/51). GA 5-42 weeks; postpartum 0-48 weeks. Median weight 65.5 / 73.9 / 76.5 kg in trimesters 1-3, 70.5 kg postpartum, 68.6 kg nonpregnant. Median daily dose 400 / 500 / 600 mg (T1-T3), 400 mg postpartum, 350 mg nonpregnant. Enzyme inducers 7 and 2; combination hormonal 9 and 13, progestin-only 20 and 4 (the nonpregnant hormonal rows sum to 50, not 51).
- Plasma lamotrigine by validated LC-MS (MONEAD PK Core, Minnesota); total concentrations.
- NONMEM 7.4.2, FOCE-I. A one-compartment model and a steady-state infusion model (C = daily dose rate / (CL/F)) were both fitted; V/F was not identifiable (68.3, 130 or 162 L depending on the residual model; sensitivity analysis with V/F fixed at 70-180 L left CL/F at 2.9-3.1 L/h), so the steady-state model was chosen (p. 36-37). CL/F (and V/F) allometrically scaled on the last nonpregnant weight (last postpartum visit); the exponent and reference weight are not given in the main text.
- Change in CL/F with GA tested as linear, quadratic and Emax; Emax chosen. Mixture model for subpopulations (dOFV -40, P < 0.001). Covariates: age, ethnicity, smoking, oestrogen-containing hormonal drugs, enzyme-inducing ASMs. Bootstrap 1000.

## Model and parameters
Delta CL/F = Emax x GA / (EC50 + GA) during pregnancy in the high-change subpopulation; exponential return to baseline after delivery (Methods p. 35; equations in Table S1, not archived).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No seizure or concentration-effect analysis.

## Covariates
GA (Emax, mixture of two subpopulations: about 91% rise, about 9% no estimable change); postpartum weeks; oestrogen-containing medication (+33.3%) and enzyme-inducing ASMs (+83.9%) on nonpregnant CL/F; allometric weight (last nonpregnant weight). Post hoc analysis found no variable (weight, race, ethnicity, age, smoking, alcohol, newborn sex, estrone, estradiol, progesterone) that separated the subpopulations (Figure S1, not archived).

## Caveats
- Authors: V/F and its gestational change not identifiable; subpopulation aetiology unknown; mostly White, non-Hispanic US population.
- Kdeg fixed, so the postpartum time course is not independently estimated here. The Methods call the unestimable quantity a "zero-order rate", while Table 2 and its footnote describe Kdeg as first-order (a wording slip).
- Steady-state model with sparse, non-protocol samples; CL/F is apparent (F unknown).
- The abstract's "275%" is the maximum CL/F relative to baseline (2.76 x), not a 275% increase; the Discussion's "275% change" can be misread.
