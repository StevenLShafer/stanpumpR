# Weber 1988: IV fenoldopam PK and blood-pressure response in essential hypertension

## Study
10 adults with essential hypertension (supine DBP > 95 and < 115 mmHg on three visits; untreated or off drugs
>= 2 weeks); 4 M / 6 F, 9 black, 1 white; mean supine BP 151 +/- 3 / 102 +/- 2 mmHg; mean weight 77.1 kg (61.8-103.6);
mean age 39 y (26-48) (PDF p. 2, printed p. 18). Day 1, dose ranging: IV fenoldopam in 5% dextrose at 0.025,
0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.375, 0.5 and 1.0 ug/kg/min, 15 min each, escalated until DBP fell 10-20 mmHg; plasma at 14 min
of each step; washout samples before and 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 40 min after stopping the highest rate reached. Day 2 (one
week later): 2 h infusion at the rate that had lowered DBP > 10 and < 20 mmHg; plasma at 30, 60, 120, 180, 240
min. Assay HPLC with electrochemical detection (Boppana 1984), inter/intra-assay CV 4% / 8.6%; plasma (heparin,
ascorbic acid added). Analysis (PDF pp. 2-3): t1/2,z from a weighted (1/y) biexponential fit of the washout
(program PHARM); Css = mean of 1 h and 2 h levels on day 2; CL = k0/Css; Vss = (k0*t - CL*AUC(0-t))/Css.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: mean arterial pressure (MAP = DBP + (SBP - DBP)/3) by automated cuff (Dinamap). Reduction in MAP was
linear in log infusion rate (r = 0.71, P < 0.001) and in log plasma concentration (r = 0.69, P < 0.001)
(Figure 1a/b, PDF p. 3, n = 9, means +/- SE by dose step); **no slope, intercept, EC50 or Emax is reported**.
Figure 1b spans about 0.7 to 15 ng/mL and roughly -3 to -19 mmHg (read from the figure; not digitised).
Day 2: MAP fell 12 mmHg (10%) at 15 min and 15 mmHg (12%) at 20 min, the maximum during the 2 h infusion; MAP 124 ->
114 mmHg within 15 min (Discussion); heart rate rose by 17 beats/min on average; BP returned to baseline soon
after stopping (Figure 3); no tachyphylaxis over 2 h (and an ongoing study up to 18 h). No ke0, tPeak or
hysteresis analysis. Asymptomatic T-wave flattening in several patients; one transient flush at 0.5 ug/kg/min.

## Covariates
None analysed. Authors state PK similar to healthy male volunteers (Allison 1987), so mild hypertension does
not alter disposition (that comparison is to another study, not tested here).

## Caveats
- n = 10 (t1/2 in 8); individual biexponential fits to a 40-min washout after a stepwise day-1 infusion of 15-min
  steps (not at steady state), while CL and Vss come from day 2.
- t1/2,z (9.8 min) is shorter than ln2*Vss/CL (13.3 min); for linear mammillary kinetics the terminal half-life
  cannot be shorter than ln2*MRT, so the three means are not jointly consistent with any linear compartment
  model (derived). Different days/designs or the crude Vss formula may explain it.
- Spreads given as SE, not SD. Washout figure axis is log-scaled; the lower tick reads "25.5" (likely 0.25 or
  axis artefact) on the rendered page.
