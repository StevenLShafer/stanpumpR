# Modi 1993: system-analysis PK/PD of IV nicardipine in healthy men

## Study
Eight healthy male volunteers enrolled (mean age 22.6 y, range 19-28; mean weight 73.3 kg, range 58.8-87.5 kg),
Syntex Research Centre, Edinburgh; 7 completed (one dropped out, reason judged unrelated to drug) (p3). Each
received, in random order, four 30-min IV infusions of nicardipine hydrochloride, total 70, 140, 210 and 280
ug/kg, at least 6 days apart. Venous blood at 5, 10, 20, 30, 35, 40, 50, 70 min and 1.5, 2, 2.5, 4.5, 6.5, 8.5,
12.5, 16.5, 20.5, 24.5 h after the start. Supine BP and HR by Nippon Colin sphygmomanometer at the same times (two
readings averaged). Plasma nicardipine by HPLC-UV (254 nm), linear 5-150 ng/mL, sensitivity 5 ng/mL (p3).
Analysis: a sum-of-exponentials unit impulse response fitted simultaneously to all four doses per subject
(FUNFIT); number of exponentials chosen by AIC; disposition parameters from the unit impulse response by the
Appendix formulas (p6). Linearity: within-subject slope of dose-normalised AUC vs dose, two-sided t-test, null
not rejected at 0.05 over 70-280 ug/kg (p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points: change from predrug baseline in supine HR and MAP (cuff), fitted by a population model with two
antagonistic sigmoid transduction functions: a central biophase equal to plasma concentration and a peripheral
biophase reached through a biexponential conduction function (eqs 6-11). No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak is reported.
The central transduction lowers MAP and raises HR; the peripheral one opposes both (Table II, III footnotes),
giving a tachycardia peaking near the end of infusion (about 0.5 h) then a HR undershoot below baseline, and a MAP
fall during infusion followed by an overshoot above baseline lasting hours (Figs 2-3; observed falls in MAP are
a few mmHg in these normotensive men).

Means across the 7 leave-one-out fits (Table II, HR, PDF p5): central Imax 43.8 bpm (SD 10.5), IC50 58.0 ug/L
(12.7), n 1.24 (0.22); peripheral Imax 20.4 bpm (8.3), IC50 32.0 ug/L (83.8), m 1.57 (2.35). Table III (MAP, PDF
p6): central Imax 72.1 mmHg (SD 148), IC50 35.1 ug/L (16.8), n 1.23 (0.60); peripheral Imax 51.0 mmHg (90.6),
IC50 168 ug/L (268), m 0.481 (0.169). The MAP means are dominated by the fit with subject 2 dropped (central Imax
408 mmHg, IC50 1.56e-3 ug/L, n 0.048). Conduction-function parameters span orders of magnitude between fits.
Cross-validation (Tables IV, V): mean RPQ 90.3% (HR) and 95.7% (MAP); mean prediction r 0.865 (HR) and 0.773
(MAP) vs fitting r 0.774 and 0.751. The text cites these as "Tables III and IV" (a mislabel; they are IV and V).
No MEAC, no potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Parameters are per kg of total body weight.

## Caveats
- n = 7 young healthy men; no women, no patients.
- Sampling to 24.5 h with a 5 ng/mL limit: the authors' model is biexponential; the label (Cardene IV) reports a
  14.4-h terminal phase detectable only after long infusions, which this design could not see.
- CL 0.920 L/h/kg is high relative to other reports the authors cite (Higuchi 0.42-0.54, Graham 1985 0.494;
  range 0.31-0.91 from a review) (p3).
- Authors note no placebo arm, so circadian baseline drift cannot be excluded, and a baroreflex explanation of
  the overshoot cannot be distinguished from the two-biophase model (p7).
- Subject 3 CL typo (see above); abstract volume 0.275 L/kg unexplained.
