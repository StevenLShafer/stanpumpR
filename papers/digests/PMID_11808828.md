# Yukawa 2002: clonazepam relative clearance and antiepileptic co-medication

## Study
- Retrospective routine TDM, Japan: 259 serum clonazepam concentrations from 137 epileptic patients, mostly children (Table II age ranges 0.3-28 y; group means 8.1-12.6 y; weights 5.5-75 kg).
- Clonazepam tablets or fine granules two to three times daily, > 1 month; samples 2-6 h after the morning dose (not troughs). Normal renal and hepatic function.
- Groups: monotherapy (53 obs), plus one AED (valproate 49, carbamazepine 46), plus two or more AEDs (Table I).
- Assay: HPLC (Nonaka), CV < 10%. NONMEM IV; steady-state model Css = D/(CL·τ), so CL is CL/F and "relative" (sampling 2-6 h post dose).

## Model and parameters
Final model (Eq. 9, Table III model 4): CL (mL/kg/h) = 152 · TBW^-0.181 · DIF.

- Abstract: carbamazepine +22%, valproate +12%, two or more AEDs +23% to 75% (weight-dependent, largest in small children, Figure 4). Gender not significant. Monotherapy mean CL 69-100 mL/kg/h over 10-80 kg (p 84).
- Introduction quotes target ranges 13-72 ng/mL and 20-70 ng/mL (cited, not derived here).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Figure 5: dose to reach 30 ng/mL at 2-6 h post dose. Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0 or potency.

## Covariates
Total body weight (power on per-kg CL), co-medication (DIF). Age and dose significant alone but not with weight.

## Caveats
Mostly paediatric; Japanese; sparse TDM samples at 2-6 h (not average Css); CL/F only; compliance not guaranteed; valproate is not an inducer, yet raised CL; one-AED factor pools CBZ and VPA; the abstract says "more than two" where Table III says "otherwise" (two or more).
