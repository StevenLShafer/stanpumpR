# Sng 2014: closed-loop phenylephrine/ephedrine vs manual boluses at caesarean section

## Study
- Randomised, double-blinded trial, KK Women's and Children's Hospital, Singapore. 216 randomised, 213 analysed (106 automated, 107 control; 3 failed spinals) (Fig. 2, PDF p4).
- Healthy ASA 1-2 women, term singleton, elective caesarean under spinal: age 21-45, weight 40-99 kg, height 145-170 cm. Baseline mean (SD): age 32.4 (5.4) automated vs 33.5 (4.6) control; weight 72.4 (12.3) vs 70.9 (11.7) kg; height 158.5 vs 158.2 cm (Table 1, PDF p4; column labels scrambled in the text layer, row order as listed).
- Spinal: 11 mg hyperbaric bupivacaine 0.5% with fentanyl 15 mcg and morphine 100 mcg; 10-15 mL/kg Ringer's lactate co-hydration.
- Automated arm: continuous non-invasive arterial pressure (CNAP) integrated over 10 s; if systolic BP < 90% of baseline, phenylephrine 50 mcg over 10 s (10 s lockout; 30 s cycle); if also HR <= 60 /min, ephedrine 4 mg instead. Syringes: phenylephrine 100 mcg/mL, ephedrine 8 mg/mL (Fig. 1, PDF p3).
- Control arm: anaesthetist gave phenylephrine 100 mcg or ephedrine 8 mg boluses, at most one per minute, on the same thresholds from 1-min CNAP readings.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: systolic BP relative to baseline; performance error (PE) with Varvel's MDPE, MDAPE and Wobble, pooled-data method (PDF p5).
- Results (Table 2, PDF p5, printed 41; automated vs control): any beat-to-beat SBP < 80% of baseline in 37/106 (34.9%) vs 63/107 (58.9%), p = 0.001 (abstract says p < 0.001); SBP > 120% of baseline 8/106 (7.5%) vs 14/107 (13.1%), p value not confirmed (the text layer scrambles the p column; the abstract gives no p); MDAPE 8.5% vs 9.8%, p = 0.013; MDPE -5.7% vs -7.5%; Wobble 6.3% vs 6.2%.
- Total phenylephrine median 400 mcg (IQR 250-650, range 0-1600) vs 400 mcg (300-700, 0-1550), p = 0.947 (Table 3, PDF p6); ephedrine median 0 mg in both (range 0-44 vs 0-20 mg); phenylephrine used in 99.1% vs 94.4%, ephedrine in 16.0% vs 15.9%. Nausea 1 (0.9%) vs 11 (10.3%), p = 0.005. Neonatal pH, lactate, Apgar similar (Table 4).
- No ke0, no effect-site concentration, no MEAC, no onset or peak time reported (opioid audit: not an opioid paper; the intrathecal fentanyl and morphine were background only). No time to peak effect is measured.

## Covariates
None analysed; groups matched on age, weight, height, baseline BP/HR, block height.

## Caveats
- Healthy term women, single centre, controlled by a particular device (CNAP); the 90% threshold was chosen to be permissive and the authors say a threshold nearer baseline should help.
- The hypotension definition (any single beat < 80% of baseline) is stricter than clinical intermittent monitoring would detect.
- A dose-per-bolus difference confounds the comparison: 50 mcg every 30 s automated vs 100 mcg every 60 s manual.
