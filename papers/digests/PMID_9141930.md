# Veselis 1997: performance of computer-assisted continuous infusion at sedative concentrations

## Study
41 healthy volunteers (mean ± SEM: age 27.7 ± 0.9 y, weight 67.9 ± 1.9 kg, BMI 23.0 ± 0.5), each given one drug by computer-assisted continuous infusion (CACI) targeting effect-site concentration: midazolam n=11, propofol n=10, thiopental n=10, fentanyl n=10 (with ondansetron). Five sequential constant targets (three rising, two falling), each held 45-70 min, below loss of consciousness; initial bolus to reach the effect-site target fast. Targets varied by about 10-20% between subjects. Sampling began only after predicted plasma/effect-site equilibration (3 min propofol, 6 min fentanyl, p1051), so predicted = target concentration. Three blood samples per target. 26 subjects arterial, 15 venous (no subject both). Assays: HPLC for midazolam, propofol, thiopental; radioimmunoassay for fentanyl (plasma; Appendix p1055-56).
Performance measures (Varvel 1992): performance error PE, MDPE (bias), MDAPE (inaccuracy), wobble, divergence, and a new median absolute constancy error (MDACE), the scatter about the mean measured value at one target.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit (fentanyl):
- MEAC / analgesic concentration: **none reported**. Targets were sedative-range concentrations, with psychomotor tests, EEG and event-related potentials recorded; those results are "reported in a separate publication" (p1051), not here.
- ke0 / t1/2 ke0 / tPeak: **none reported**. The software targeted effect site and waited the predicted equilibration time of about 6 min for fentanyl and 3 min for propofol (p1051); the ke0 or PK set behind those times is not given in this paper.
- Potency ratio: none.
- Arterial vs venous plasma fentanyl at pseudo-steady state: no significant difference in concentrations (power only about 0.2). MDAPE differed between arterial and venous sampling for fentanyl (arterial larger error, P<0.05).

## Covariates
CACI took age, weight and gender as inputs (p1049); no covariate analysis. Bias partly attributed by the authors to PK sets derived from other designs (bolus vs infusion, venous vs arterial sampling, different assays), and to midazolam's assay being less sensitive than Greenblatt's (p1054).

## Caveats
Small groups; no simultaneous A-V sampling (power low); healthy young volunteers; some targets near assay detection limits (thiopental 1.6 mcg/mL limit, midazolam), which inflates percentage error; the paper shows CACI misses the target by a median of tens of percent (midazolam up to 82%) while holding the concentration constant (MDACE about 6-11%). Paper-level conclusion: targets were not accurate at sedative concentrations for the published parameter sets, but constancy was good.
