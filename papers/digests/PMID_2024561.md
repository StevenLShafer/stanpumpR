# Wulf 1991: plasma protein binding of bupivacaine in pregnant women at term

## Study
In vitro plasma protein-binding study (Kiel, Germany). Venous plasma (5 mL samples, stored at -20 °C) from:
- Group I: 13 healthy male volunteers, 23-27 years.
- Group II: 20 pregnant women, 17-34 years, 36th-40th week of gestation, no infection, no pre-eclampsia/eclampsia.
- Group III: 12 non-pregnant female volunteers, 23-32 years, no oral contraceptives, sampled in the follicular phase.

Bupivacaine was added in vitro (1 µg/mL for the group comparisons). Protein binding was measured by equilibrium
dialysis with ³H-bupivacaine at pH 7.4, 16 h, with a volume-shift correction (CV 0.57% at 95% binding). AAG was measured
by radial immunodiffusion (CV 3.6% at 0.9 g/L) and progesterone by RIA. Bupivacaine plasma concentrations were measured by HPLC.
The paper describes its statistics as "median ± s.d." with nonparametric tests (p. 130). It does not say whether
each headline figure is a median or a mean.

Validation (Table 1, p. 130): in 7 surgical patients (34-80 years) given 75-100 mg of epidural bupivacaine, binding ex vivo
(sample at 15-20 min) and binding after in vitro addition to their pre-block plasma did not differ. Medians were 93.3% and
92.9%, and r = 0.89.

## Model and parameters
There is no pharmacokinetic model. The binding results:

| AAG (g/L) | 0.42 | 1.01 | not stated numerically (Fig. 1 bar ~0.8) | Abstract; Fig. 1 |
| Progesterone (ng/mL) | median 156 (65-312) | median 0.4 (0.2-0.9) | all <0.1 | Results p. 130 |

- In the abstract, the 1.01 g/L AAG is set against the 5.4% free fraction, so it is taken to be the female volunteers.
  The paper says the female and male volunteers did not differ.
- Adding 100-400 ng/mL progesterone to volunteers' plasma had no effect on free fraction (Fig. 2, 8 male volunteers). The
  free fraction did not correlate with progesterone (r = -0.23 non-pregnant, 0.06 pregnant).
- The free fraction correlated with AAG overall (r = 0.72), but only weakly within the parturients (r = 0.4).
- Adding 0.2 g/L AAG to the plasma of 10 parturients lowered the free fraction to the volunteers' level (Fig. 3; values
  only shown graphically, roughly 8% to 5.5%).
- In pooled parturient plasma, binding fell with concentration (Fig. 4, read from the graph): about 92% at 1 mg/L, 91% at
  2 mg/L and 88% at 5 mg/L. Tucker 1970's non-pregnant values in the same figure, which were measured by ultrafiltration,
  were about 96%, 95.5% and 88%. **At 5 mg/L the two groups converge.**
- Discussion (p. 131): the hepatic extraction ratio is about 0.32 and the free fraction about 0.05. Both are quoted
  background figures, not results of this study.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not a PD study: no toxic threshold, ke0 or concentration-effect data. The authors argue that unbound concentration is what drives
systemic toxicity, citing references 17 and 18.

## Covariates
Pregnancy at term, with AAG as the mechanism. Progesterone was tested and had no effect. Total bupivacaine concentration
was tested in pooled plasma only (Fig. 4).

## Caveats
- All the binding measurements were made in vitro at a fixed 1 µg/mL. The ex vivo validation was in non-pregnant surgical
  patients, not parturients.
- The concentration study (Fig. 4) used pooled plasma, and it compares against another study (Tucker 1970) that used a different method
  (ultrafiltration).
- The authors note that a higher free fraction does not necessarily mean a higher free concentration, because the volume of
  distribution can also change.
- No measures of dispersion are given numerically for the headline values; they appear only as figure error bars.
- No pharmacokinetic data are given (no clearance or volumes).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:16, finding column.** The registry gives "8.2% against 5.4% in non-pregnant volunteers (ratio
  1.52), with AAG 0.42 against 1.01 g/L". The paper gives these values (abstract; Results p. 130), and 8.2/5.4 = 1.52.
  **Matches.** The comparator is specifically the non-pregnant *female* volunteers; males were 5.7%, for a ratio of
  1.44 (derived). The value was measured in vitro at 1 µg/mL by equilibrium dialysis.
- **The 1.5x unbound note.** This holds at 1 µg/mL. The paper's own Fig. 4 shows the difference shrinking at higher total
  concentration, with both groups at about 88% bound at 5 mg/L, though the comparator there comes from Tucker 1970 by a
  different method. The registry note attributes this concentration dependence to Denson 1984 (<10 mcg/mL). Wulf 1991
  suggests the 1.5x may narrow at the toxic concentrations the note mentions. This is a qualifier, not an error.
- **Group "unchanged", no factor applied.** This is consistent: Wulf reports binding only, not clearance or volume. The library's
  bupivacaine model (`R/drugs_bupivacaine.R:4-6, 58-67`) plots total plasma concentration and states that protein binding
  and the unbound fraction are not modelled. That is consistent with how the registry row uses this paper.
- There is no PK in the paper to compare with the library's Burm 1987 disposition.
