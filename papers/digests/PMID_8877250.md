# Spina, Pisani & Perucca 1996: carbamazepine interactions (review)

## Study
Narrative review of pharmacokinetic interactions involving carbamazepine, in two halves: section 2, drugs
that affect carbamazepine (absorption, protein binding, inhibition, induction), and section 3, carbamazepine
as the perpetrator on other drugs. No primary data; every figure is quoted from a cited study.

PDF page N = printed page 197 + N (chunk/page headers 200, 202, 204, 206, ... at PDF 3, 5, 7, 9).

## Model and parameters
No model. The quantitative statements relevant to the stanpumpR antiseizure module:

**Carbamazepine autoinduction** (section 1, printed p 199): as a result of autoinduction the half-life of
carbamazepine "is reduced by 50% after 3 weeks of drug administration" - i.e. a clearance roughly doubled,
on a half-life basis.

**Enzyme-inducing anticonvulsants on carbamazepine** (section 2.4.1, printed p 203-204): phenytoin,
phenobarbital and primidone lower plasma carbamazepine, the magnitude correlating with the inducer's dose
(Figure 2 shows carbamazepine alone vs carbamazepine + phenytoin at matched carbamazepine dosage). In
polytherapy the inducing effects "can be additive". Patients on inducers show a higher epoxide:parent ratio,
and phenytoin and barbiturates stimulate the CYP3A4-mediated conversion to the epoxide. No numeric factor is
given for this interaction.

**Felbamate on carbamazepine** (section 2.4.2, printed p 204): plasma carbamazepine "may decrease by 10 to
42%"; in a crossover study mean carbamazepine fell 7.5 -> 6.1 mg/L on felbamate while the epoxide rose
1.8 -> 2.4 mg/L.

**Carbamazepine on phenytoin** (section 3.1, printed p 205): "The effect of carbamazepine on the
pharmacokinetics of phenytoin is controversial and probably inconsistent." Both shortened half-life /
reduced steady-state concentrations and prolonged half-life / raised concentrations are reported; Zielinski
found an increase in half the patients and no change in the rest. The authors conclude carbamazepine may
both induce and inhibit phenytoin biotransformation, the prevailing effect varying between patients.

**Carbamazepine on ethosuximide and valproate** (section 3.1, printed p 205): carbamazepine "has been
reported to increase the metabolic clearance and to decrease the plasma concentration of ethosuximide and
valproic acid, both interactions being clinically significant". For valproate the induced pathways are
glucuronidation, omega and omega-1 oxidation, and the formation clearance of the hepatotoxic 4-ene
metabolite may double. **No numeric factor for either.**

**Carbamazepine on lamotrigine** (section 3.2, printed p 205-206): carbamazepine "accelerates the hepatic
glucuronidation of lamotrigine and reduces lamotrigine half-life from **24 +/- 6 hours** in healthy
volunteers to **14 +/- 7 hours** in carbamazepine-treated patients" (refs 42, 157, 158). Patients on
carbamazepine need larger lamotrigine doses; withdrawing carbamazepine risks a marked rise in lamotrigine.
Derived ratio 24/14 = **1.714** (mine - a half-life ratio, see Caveats).

**Carbamazepine on zonisamide** (section 3.2, printed p 206): "plasma zonisamide concentrations after
zonisamide 400 mg dose were found to be **40% higher** in patients comedicated with carbamazepine compared
with patients comedicated with phenytoin" (ref 164). So phenytoin is the stronger zonisamide inducer of the
two. Zonisamide's own metabolism "can be accelerated by enzyme-inducing drugs" but "has been little
investigated".

Other carbamazepine victims named without numbers: felbamate, topiramate, tiagabine, stiripentol,
remacemide (printed p 206); no effect on vigabatrin or gabapentin (renally eliminated). Benzodiazepines:
clobazam falls a mean 61% with norclobazam up 44% on two weeks of carbamazepine (printed p 206).

Zonisamide as a perpetrator on carbamazepine is called controversial - reported to increase, not alter, or
even decrease plasma carbamazepine (section 2.3.2, printed p 202).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None quantitative. The review notes that carbamazepine + lamotrigine adverse events may be a
pharmacodynamic interaction rather than the epoxide rise, and that the clinical significance of each
pharmacokinetic change must be judged on monitoring - no concentration-effect model anywhere.

## Covariates
None fitted. The review states that the inducing effect correlates with the inducer's dose and that
multiple inducers can be additive (section 2.4.1) - the dose dependence the stanpumpR model represents with
a saturable `fold(C)`, and the additivity assumption the model's additive-excess rule mirrors.

## Caveats
- **Secondary source**: no primary data, no n or variance for most figures, and the key lamotrigine numbers
  are a cross-study comparison (healthy volunteers against carbamazepine-treated patients) rather than a
  paired design.
- **The 24 -> 14 h figure is a half-life ratio across two different populations.** By the repository's own
  rule (AGENTS.md, "Comparing sources: only clearance is real"), a terminal half-life carries the artefacts
  of sampling duration and assay limit, and the ratio equals a clearance ratio only if the volume of
  distribution is the same in both groups - which is not reported. Treat x1.71 as an order-of-magnitude
  cross-check, not a measurement.
- The zonisamide "40% higher" figure is a **carbamazepine-vs-phenytoin** contrast with no uninduced control,
  so it bounds the *difference* between two inducers, not either one's absolute effect.
- Several statements are explicitly unresolved by the authors (carbamazepine -> phenytoin; zonisamide ->
  carbamazepine), and the review correctly declines to quantify them.
- 1996 vintage: no lacosamide, eslicarbazepine, cenobamate or modern population models.
