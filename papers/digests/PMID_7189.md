# Westerman 1976: josamycin against aerobic Gram-positive cocci and anaerobes

## Study
In-vitro susceptibility study, Methodist Hospital / Baylor College of Medicine, Houston. Josamycin (a new macrolide)
compared with ampicillin, erythromycin and clindamycin. Clinical isolates: 25 each of pneumococci, enterococci,
S. aureus, S. epidermidis and non-enterococcal haemolytic streptococci (11 alpha-haemolytic and 14 group A
beta-haemolytic, pooled), and 25 anaerobes (Table 1, PDF p2: 10 B. fragilis, 1 B. capillosus, 1 Acidaminococcus
fermentans, 1 Fusobacterium symbiosum, 3 Peptococcus saccharolyticus, 1 P. asaccharolyticus, 5 Clostridium
perfringens, 1 C. tertium, 1 Clostridium sp., 1 Peptostreptococcus micros).

Method (PDF p1-2, printed p988-989): aerobes by serial twofold tube dilution in Mueller-Hinton broth (no serum, so
free-drug values); inoculum about 10^5 organisms/mL (enterococci and staphylococci averaged 2.3 x 10^5/mL), 0.1 mL
into 1 mL of drug dilution; MIC = lowest concentration without turbidity after overnight incubation. **MBC = lowest
concentration giving 95% kill or fewer than five colonies on subculture of 0.03 mL from each clear tube onto blood
agar.** Anaerobes: agar dilution only (Steers replicator, plates with 5% laked sheep blood and menadione, GasPak jars,
37 °C, read at 48 h): **MIC only, no MBC for anaerobes.**

Text (PDF p2, printed p989): clindamycin at 0.05 µg/mL killed all pneumococci; at 0.2 µg/mL killed all haemolytic
streptococci. Background (p1): josamycin's average serum peak 1 to 2 µg/mL after 600 mg orally (cited, not measured).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points are in-vitro inhibition and killing (95% kill / <5 colonies). The authors note that macrolide activity
falls at low pH and high CO2, which applied to their anaerobe testing. No serum concentrations, PK/PD index or
time-kill data. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None (organism only).

## Caveats
- 25 isolates per group (10 B. fragilis); each step is 4% (10% for B. fragilis).
- MBC end point is 95% kill or <5 colonies, laxer than the 99.9% kill used in the stanpumpR help.
- Alpha- and group A beta-haemolytic streptococci are pooled. 1975 isolates and methods.
- Anaerobe MICs on blood-containing agar under raised CO2; no anaerobe MBC.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Citation** (`antibiotics.md:58`): journal, year, volume and pages match. **matches**.
- **"Against streptococci and anaerobes"**: the paper gives clindamycin MBCs for streptococci and pneumococci but only
  MICs for anaerobes. **discrepancy** (the line implies anaerobe MBCs).
- **Principal row, `R/antibioticMbc.R:256-265`** (tonsillitis isolates, MBC50 = MBC90 = 0.25 mg/L, free): Westerman's
  streptococcal MBC90 is 0.20 and MBC50 0.05 (derived), with a laxer end point. Same order as 0.25, slightly lower,
  in line with the row's note that 0.25 is probably the lowest concentration tested. The row's note "The one measured
  MBC found" (line 259) is no longer true. **consistent values; wording discrepancy**.
- **Pneumococcus row, `R/antibioticMbc.R:266-271`** (no MBC; serum bactericidal titres): this paper measured one
  (100% killed at 0.05 µg/mL). **discrepancy** (statement), see open question.
- **B. fragilis row, `R/antibioticMbc.R:272-286`** (MBC90 1.6 from Levison's 80% kill): this paper gives MICs only
  (MIC90 0.78, derived, 10 isolates), so it cannot test the MBC. **cannot verify**.
- **S. aureus exclusion, `R/antibioticMbc.R:293-303`**: Table 3 supports it: MIC90 0.20 against MBC50 1.56 and MBC90
  6.25 (derived), the MBC many times the MIC. **matches**.
- **Group G and other-anaerobe exclusions** (lines 287-292): no group G strains; anaerobes have no MBC here. Not
  tested.
- Units: µg/mL = mg/L; broth MBCs are free-drug values, as the help (`antibiotics.md:22`) assumes before placing them
  on clindamycin's total-drug curve through its binding fit.
