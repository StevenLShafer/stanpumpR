# Shafer 2012: All models are wrong (response surface editorial)

## Study
Two-page editorial (PDF pp. 1-2; printed 240-241) accompanying Heyse B, et al., Anesthesiology 2012;116:311-23, which compared several response surface models of the sevoflurane-remifentanil interaction for the probability of response to tracheal intubation. No new data. The DOI is not printed in the file and is left blank rather than guessed.

## Model and parameters
No parameter values. Fig. 1 shows the surface Heyse et al. selected: x and y axes are sevoflurane and remifentanil concentrations, z is probability of nonresponse to intubation; isoboles at 5, 20, 50, 80 and 95% nonresponse. Fig. 2 is the same surface from above. The best-performing models assume opioids attenuate the noxious stimulus while hypnotics suppress the response circuitry (Glass 1998 concept). The editorial lists published response surface models (Minto 2000, Nieuwenhuijs 2003, Mertens 2003, Bouillon 2004, Manyam 2006, Kern 2004, Fidler and Kern 2006, Schumacher 2009).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: quantal response to tracheal intubation. Qualitative only: sevoflurane alone can give nonresponse; remifentanil alone cannot reliably do so (no concentration-response relationship at sevoflurane = 0).
