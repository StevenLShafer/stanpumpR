# Mertens 2003: predictive performance of remifentanil TCI during propofol anaesthesia

## Study
- Retrospective evaluation of blood remifentanil concentrations from an earlier propofol-remifentanil interaction study (ref 6 of the paper, Eur J Anaesthesiol 2001 abstract).
- 30 female patients, ASA I-II, age 20-65 yr (mean 37), weight 66.9 (SD 11.1) kg, height 1.66 (0.09) m, LBM 47.5 (6.0) kg, anaesthesia 192 (59) min; lower abdominal surgery (p. 136).
- Propofol TCI held at 2, 4 or 6 ug/mL (Gepts parameters); remifentanil TCI (Minto 1997 set on the device, Hull algorithm, pump updated every 5 s), target changed with clinical signs of inadequate anaesthesia (p. 133).
- 444 arterial whole-blood remifentanil samples (median 15 per patient, range 5-20); measured 0.1-19.6 ng/mL. Remifentanil by LC-MS/MS, quantitation limit 0.1 ng/mL, intra-assay CV 10.0, 7.8, 2.5, 2.7% at 0.1, 0.2, 30, 50 ng/mL; esterases inactivated with citric acid (p. 134). Blood propofol 0.5-11 ug/mL by HPLC.
- Predicted concentrations for five published parameter sets were computed off-line from the infusion record actually delivered, then compared with measured (PE, MDPE, MDAPE, divergence, wobble after Varvel 1992).

Table 3: divergence (% per h) 5, 6, 3, 8, 6 and wobble (%) 16, 21, 19, 17, 12 for Minto, Egan 1996, Egan 1993, Egan 1998, Drover (rendered page; the text layer scrambles the rows). LBM (female) = 1.07 x weight - 148 x (weight/height)^2 (Eq 1; height unit not stated in the equation).
Measured-vs-predicted regressions (Fig 2 and 3 captions): Minto Cm = 0.76 Cp + 0.36 (r 0.93); Egan 1996 0.86 Cp + 0.42 (r 0.91); Egan 1993 0.86 Cp + 0.35 (r 0.92); Egan 1998 0.79 Cp + 0.47 (r 0.92); Drover 0.64 Cp + 0.45 (r 0.93).
Negative PE means measured below predicted: the Minto set over-predicted blood remifentanil by about 15% during propofol anaesthesia in these women. Propofol concentration did not affect remifentanil PE (PE = 3.16 Cprop - 18.4, R2 0.02, Fig 6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: none reported. No MEAC or analgesic concentration, no ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis, and no equianalgesic ratio. The paper only remarks that blood remifentanil has a short blood-effect site equilibration half-time and that literature dosing guidelines by blood concentration should be applied with the 15% bias in mind (p. 140).

## Covariates
Within the evaluated sets: age and LBM (Minto), LBM (Egan 1998, Drover). Mertens tested only blood propofol concentration on PE (no effect). Their population (mechanically ventilated, abdominal surgery, propofol, all female) differs from every source population (Table 1); the authors list perfusion, sex and surgical conditions as possible reasons for the PE differences.

## Caveats
- Predictions use the delivered infusion record, not targets. True performance (measured vs target) is only known for the Minto set that ran on the device (p. 139).
- Only ASA I-II women, n = 30, blood (not plasma) samples; very high PEs come from the 0.1-0.2 ng/mL samples near the quantitation limit (Fig 4 caption).
- Sponsor: GlaxoSmithKline.
- Conclusion: the Egan sets had least bias; the authors still suggest a population set like Minto's may suit heterogeneous patients.
