# Jung 1988: ketorolac IV / IM / oral crossover

## Study
- 15 healthy volunteers, 22-40 y (mean 32), 52-108 kg (mean 72 kg); sex not reported (p. 423).
- Randomized three-way crossover, 1-week washout: 10 mg ketorolac **tromethamine (salt)** IV over 4 min, IM
  (gluteal), or oral tablet with 200 mL water; 10 h fast, fasting until 4 h post-dose (p. 423).
- Venous plasma at 0, 5 and 10 min (IV only), 0.33, 0.67, 1, 2, 4, 8, 24 h. HPLC-UV, detection limit 10 ng/mL.
  Racemic ketorolac (no enantiomer separation) measured as ketorolac anion, not the salt (p. 424-425).
- Analysis: individual IV curves fitted to 2- or 3-exponential functions (ESTRIP initial estimates, PCNONLIN,
  1/C^2 weighting); triexponential best in 10 subjects, biexponential in 5. Vss, CL, MRT, MAT by statistical
  moments; Vbeta = CL/beta; f = AUC ratio vs IV; Cmax/tmax observed (p. 424).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No ke0, EC50 or analgesic concentration reported. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None examined. CL and volumes reported per kg.

## Caveats
- Small n (15), healthy fasting young adults; sex not given; single 10 mg dose.
- Racemic (total) ketorolac; no enantiomer data.
- f > 100 % for IM reflects variability; the authors conclude bioavailability is essentially complete.
- Authors contrast an earlier oral-solution study (n = 4) giving 81 % (range 69-90 %).
