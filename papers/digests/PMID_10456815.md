# Persson 1999: ketamine and alfentanil-induced hypoventilation

## Study
Randomised, placebo-controlled crossover, 8 healthy men, 24-43 y, 79-90 kg, two study days (p.1-2, journal pp.744-745). Five 40-min periods: period 1 baseline; periods 2-5 alfentanil target; periods 3-5 racemic ketamine (or saline) added at target plasma 50, 100, 200 ng/mL. Alfentanil: computer-controlled infusion (STANPUMP, Scott's weight-adjusted parameters) aiming at an effect-site concentration of 50 ng/mL in period 2; then fixed-rate infusions of 0.2125 mg/kg/min (period 3) and 0.16 mg/kg/min (periods 4-5), set from a STANPUMP simulation (units as printed; these rates look 1000x too high for mg/kg/min of alfentanil and probably are ug/kg/min, see open question below). Ketamine: STANPUMP with Domino's weight-adjusted parameters. Alfentanil assay GC-NPD (CV 4-6.4%); ketamine/norketamine enantiomers HPLC (limit 2 ng/mL, CV <= 8%). Ventilation: face-mask with occlusion valve, six 30-s CO2 pulses per period, P0.1, minute ventilation, end-tidal CO2, SpO2. ANOVA within subjects.

## Model and parameters
No PK model fitted. Measured plasma concentrations (Table 2, ng/mL, min-mean-max; PDF page 5):
- Alfentanil, placebo runs: period 2 21-42-59; period 3 41-55-81; period 4 38-52-68; period 5 46-53-63.
- Alfentanil, ketamine runs: period 2 21-39-56; period 3 43-58-74; period 4 40-57-73; period 5 40-59-88.
- S-ketamine: period 3 17-24-33; 4 41-55-88; 5 71-96-128. R-ketamine: 18-27-35; 46-59-89; 75-106-136.
- Sum S+R means 51, 105, 202 ng/mL against targets 50, 100, 200 (text, journal p.752). R-ketamine higher than S: regression slope 1.079 (norketamine 1.518). Up to 3-fold between-subject alfentanil variation; alfentanil run-to-run correlation r = 0.55.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is ventilation (not analgesia). Alfentanil plasma about 40-55 ng/mL (total plasma, GC, racemic-free drug not separated) reduced resting minute ventilation, respiratory rate, Ti/Ttot and SpO2 and raised end-tidal CO2 in all subjects; P0.1 (drive), Vt and Ti were unaffected; the CO2-pulse response was unchanged. Ketamine added at plasma 50 ng/mL did not change this; at 200 ng/mL (sum of S+R) minute ventilation, RR, Ti/Ttot, SpO2 and end-tidal CO2 returned to baseline (period 1), mainly via respiratory rate. Concentration-dependent trend, not fitted.
Opioid audit: no MEAC / analgesic concentration measured. No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis reported (effect-site targeting used Scott's parameters, values not printed). No potency or equianalgesic ratio. Authors say the concentration range used is within that required for postoperative analgesia, citing other work (refs 8, 9, 25), not measured here. Ketamine analgesic threshold not measured.
Side effects (Table 1): ketamine-related effects in 2/8 at 50 ng/mL, 7/8 at 100, 8/8 at 200; one subject withdrew at 200 ng/mL (anxiety), so n=7 in period 5.

## Covariates
None analysed; weight-adjusted dosing parameters only.

## Caveats
n=8 male volunteers; alfentanil concentrations lower than target in period 2 and up to 2-fold different within subject between days; period effects confounded with order (fixed escalation); CO2 stimulus brief so mainly peripheral chemoreceptors; sedation/arousal from ketamine side effects may stimulate breathing indirectly.
