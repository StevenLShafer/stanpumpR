# Shafer 2004 (editorial): shock and anaesthetic dose; plus Ebert and Maze 2004 (editorial): high-dose dexmedetomidine

Both are opinion pieces accompanying other papers; no new data tables. Numbers below are the editorials' own statements.

## Study
- **Shafer, "Shock values" (PDF p1-2):** accompanies Johnson KB et al., Anesthesiology 2004;101:647-59 (porcine haemorrhage then crystalloid resuscitation, propofol PK/PD). Author ran simulations with the PK/PD models of the Johnson/Egan series (fentanyl, remifentanil, etomidate, propofol in the pig) to give the percent dose reduction to reach the same effect with a bolus or a 10-min infusion in haemorrhage vs control (Figure 1).
- **Ebert and Maze (PDF p2-4):** accompanies Ramsay and Luterman, Anesthesiology 2004;101:787-90 (three case reports of high-dose dexmedetomidine as sole anaesthetic). Narrative review of alpha2 pharmacology and cautions.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Shafer (PDF p1-2):
- Propofol: stated reduction in dose of 80 to 90% for haemorrhagic shock, same whether bolus or 10-min infusion; after fluid resuscitation about half the control dose. Figure 1 (read from image, approximate): propofol about 85-88%; propofol with resuscitation about 40-50%; etomidate about -10% (a slight increase); remifentanil about 50%; fentanyl about 45-50% (bolus and infusion bars similar). Text states fentanyl/remifentanil should be reduced by no more than about half in trauma.
- Mechanism as stated: shock reduces central volume and clearance (fentanyl, remifentanil), central and peripheral volumes (etomidate, ~20% higher levels), intercompartmental clearance (propofol); brain sensitivity rises only for propofol (not etomidate, remifentanil). Reference BIS: 4 mg/kg propofol gives BIS 50 in control pigs but BIS 0 (isoelectric) after haemorrhage with resuscitation.
- The comparator for every reduction is the control (non-bleeding) animal, from porcine models, not human.
- No ke0, t1/2 ke0 or tPeak, no MEAC, no equipotent ratio for fentanyl or remifentanil is stated (opioid audit: none).

Ebert and Maze (PDF p2-4):
- Ebert 2000 volunteers targeted to 8 ng/mL dexmedetomidine could not be aroused (this is a target plasma concentration from ref 2, end point unrousability); the editorial says plasma levels 8-10-fold above the clinical range gave unarousable volunteers who kept ventilatory drive (PaCO2 43 to 47 mm Hg).
- Blood pressure biphasic with concentration: falls in the clinical range, rises at higher plasma levels (peripheral vasoconstriction); bradycardia possible. No numeric clinical-range concentration is given in the text; no ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Not applicable. (Shafer: haemorrhagic shock, with or without crystalloid resuscitation, as the covariate on dose.)

## Caveats
- Editorials; the simulated reductions come from animal (pig) models and the author states that compartment volumes and clearances have little physiological "truth" beyond systemic clearance. Dose reductions are not validated in humans.
- Figure values here are visual readings.
- Ebert and Maze authors disclose Abbott/Orion consultancy (Maze).
