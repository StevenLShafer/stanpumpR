# Han 2019: NAT2 genotype and oral hydralazine pharmacokinetics in pregnancy

## Study
- Prospective steady-state PK study in **12 pregnant women** with hypertension on **oral** hydralazine
  hydrochloride (Teva tablets) 5-25 mg every 6 h, titrated clinically without regard to the study (PDF pp. 3-4).
  13 consented, 12 completed at least one study day. 7 non-Hispanic White, 3 Asian, 2 Native Hawaiian/Pacific
  Islander (PDF p. 7).
- 13 study days: mid-pregnancy (22-26 weeks) n = 5, late pregnancy (30-38 weeks) n = 8; one woman (a rapid
  acetylator, 10 mg q6h) studied in both (PDF pp. 4, 7, 8).
- Mean height 163 ± 6 cm, **weight 113 ± 38 kg** (Table 1 individual weights 75.0-192.6 kg; mid 121.7 (40.5),
  late 107.7 (37.5) kg; Table 1, PDF p. 21). Dose RA 10.7 ± 6.7 vs SA 17.5 ± 8.2 mg (p = .1). **All took atenolol**
  (12.5-100 mg twice daily, Table 1).
- Fasted ≥5 h before and 1 h after the study dose. Blood from an indwelling venous catheter predose and 0.5, 1,
  1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6 h (one dosing interval) (PDF p. 4).
- Assay: **whole-blood** hydralazine, derivatised at bedside with p-nitrobenzaldehyde at pH 6 (avoids release of
  "apparent" hydralazine from acid-labile hydrazones), LC-MS/MS; LOD 0.02, **LOQ 0.04 ng/mL**. Plasma MTP by
  LC-MS/MS, LOQ 1.5 ng/mL (PDF pp. 4-6).
- NAT2 genotype by 4 SNPs (buccal DNA): 6 rapid (RA: *4/*5 ×2, *4/*6 ×3, *4/*7), 6 slow (SA: *5/*5, *5/*6 ×2,
  *5/*7, *6/*6 ×2) (PDF p. 7). No intermediate category: one functional allele counted as rapid (PDF p. 6).
- Non-compartmental (Phoenix WinNonlin): AUCτ,ss linear-up/log-down 0-6 h, CL/F = dose/AUCτ,ss, Vz/F = (CL/F)/kel;
  actual body weight for per-kg values; AUC and Cmax dose-normalised to 10 mg q6h (PDF p. 6). The dose is the
  hydrochloride as prescribed (PDF p. 3); the paper does not say whether CL/F used salt or base mg.
- **No nonpregnant or postpartum control.** No intravenous dosing.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Not an opioid paper. **No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or concentration-effect relation**; no therapeutic concentration.
- Clinical Doppler haemodynamics (timing relative to dose unspecified, PDF p. 3): no RA/SA difference in TPR,
  MAP or HR, at baseline or on hydralazine (e.g. change in MAP −9 ± 12 vs −1 ± 9 mm Hg, p = .3; PDF p. 7). The
  authors call these confounded (clinical titration, atenolol co-therapy, unpowered; PDF p. 9).

## Covariates
- NAT2 acetylator genotype: strong effect on CL/F, Vz/F and MTP ratio (regression above).
- Gestational age (mid vs late) and dose (5-25 mg): no significant effect. Weight used only to normalise.

## Caveats
- **No control group.** The authors' "comparable to non-pregnant" judgement (PDF p. 11) is against historical oral
  studies in non-pregnant subjects (refs 44, 64: plasma assays, oral solution, 3-h sampling), scaled by a
  blood/plasma ratio of 1.65. Their own ranges do not overlap: CL/F 3903-16,292 (pregnant RA) vs 18,750-37,500 L/h
  (non-pregnant RA), 800-2849 vs 3260-7500 L/h (SA); dose-normalised Cmax is also lower in pregnancy (0.3-1.8 vs 4-5;
  1.4-9.4 vs 7-50 ng/mL). Lower CL/F with lower Cmax runs in opposite directions, so the comparison cannot settle the
  direction of any pregnancy effect.
- Oral CL/F mixes NAT2 first-pass extraction (F ~16% RA, ~35% SA in non-pregnant subjects, a textbook value,
  PDF p. 3) with systemic clearance; F was not measured.
- Small n (6 + 6), heavy and variable body weight (75-193 kg), all on atenolol, 0-6 h sampling only.
- The abstract (PDF p. 1) gives weight-adjusted CL/F as "L/h" and Cmax as 0.77/4.04 ng/mL; Table 2 gives L/h/kg and
  0.8/4.0. The abstract's unit is a slip; Table 2 is the source used here.
- Accepted manuscript: tables could change at proof; numbers here are from the manuscript's PDF pages only.
