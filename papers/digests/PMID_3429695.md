# Cheymol 1987: IV propranolol in obese and normal-weight volunteers

## Study
- 12 obese subjects (9 F, 3 M), 41-58 y, 110.3 +/- 20.4 kg, 198.7 +/- 32.5% of ideal body weight; 12 normal-weight
  controls (3 F, 9 M), 20-25 y, 66.7 +/- 6.8 kg, 94.5 +/- 7.8% IBW (PDF p1-2). Done as two trials: group 1 (5 obese F vs
  6 controls, 3F/3M) and group 2 (7 obese, 4F/3M, vs 6 male controls), Table I (PDF p2, rendered). Mean ages: controls
  23.0 and 22.5 y; obese 49.7 and 52.4 y. BMI controls 20.8 and 22.1; obese 43.1 and 40.6.
- IBW = 45.5 (F) or 50.0 (M) kg + 2.3 kg per 2.5 cm over 152 cm (PDF p2).
- Single 8 mg IV dl-propranolol hydrochloride over 10 min by syringe pump, supine, fasting (PDF p2).
- Venous samples from the opposite arm at 5 min during, and 0, 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60 min and 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10 h
  after the infusion (PDF p2).
- HPLC, limit 1 ng/mL plasma. Binding by equilibrium dialysis with 3H-propranolol, and AAG by nephelometry, in group 2
  only (PDF p3).
- Open two-compartment model, unweighted nonlinear least squares; AUC by trapezoids; CL = dose/AUC; Vss = central +
  peripheral; Vbeta = CL/beta; Student's t test (PDF p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Heart rate only, in obese subjects: reduction maximal 30 min after propranolol (19 +/- 0.9% of control values), 15 +/- 4%
at 1 h, 11 +/- 7% at 2.5 h, regressing by 5 h (8 +/- 7%) (PDF p3, rendered). No heart-rate data for controls, no
concentration-effect model, no ke0. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Obesity compared by group only (no regression). Binding (88.3 vs 90.1%) and AAG (519.8 vs 582.0 mg/L) did not differ;
albumin lower in obese (42.5 vs 35.7 g/L, P < 0.01) (Table I). The authors propose altered hepatic function and tissue
blood flow, not lipophilicity or binding, as the explanation for lower CL and Vss (abstract and discussion, PDF p1, p4-5).

## Caveats
- **Internal inconsistency in Table II (obese total row)**: AUC 161.0 +/- 67.0 and t1/2 3.5 +/- 0.9 are identical to obese
  group 2, although obese group 1 is 142.9 and 2.3 h. The n-weighted means of the groups would be about 153.5 h ug/L and
  3.0 h (derived); the other obese total columns are the weighted means. The abstract and Results text repeat 161.0 and
  3.5. Which value is right cannot be decided from the paper.
- Obese and control groups differ in age (about 50 vs 23 y) and sex mix (mostly F vs mostly M); the authors argue age
  did not bias the result (PDF p4).
- Two-compartment fit, but the coefficients and distribution rate are not printed.
- Small groups (5-7 per subgroup).
