# Cooper 2015: methylprednisolone in breast milk after high-dose IV pulses

## Study
Single case report (InfantRisk Center, Texas Tech). A 36-year-old lactating woman, 84 kg, relapsing multiple sclerosis,
given 1000 mg IV methylprednisolone sodium succinate over 2 h on 3 consecutive days; only other medicine prenatal
vitamins. Her 5-month-old daughter (5.4 kg) did not breastfeed during the course. Milk from both breasts at 0, 1, 2, 4,
8 and 12 h "following methylprednisolone therapy" each day (PDF p2 / printed p238). No maternal plasma samples.
Assay: LC-MS (ESI, SIM m/z 357.2), calibration 0.06-6 ug/mL in the patient's own 0-h milk; standards and samples in
triplicate; RSD 0.9-29.1%, intraday accuracy 59.3-105.9%, extraction efficiency 56.2-112.2%.

Background figures cited from other sources (p1): protein binding 75-82%, apparent volume of distribution 1.5 L/kg,
and an earlier case (oral 6 mg, milk peaks about 7 ng/mL, RID 1.28%).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No infant outcome (the infant did not feed); no maternal adverse events reported. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None (n = 1).

## Caveats
- One woman, one course; milk only, no plasma, so no milk/plasma ratio.
- Time zero is not defined relative to the 2-h infusion ("following ... therapy"); BLQ at 0 h every day suggests time
  is counted from before or at the start of the infusion, but the paper does not say. The repo does not use these times.
- Assay accuracy as low as 59.3% and RSD up to 29.1%.
- RID arithmetic (derived): 0.207 mg/kg/day over the mother's 1000 mg / 84 kg = 11.9 mg/kg/day gives 1.74%, not
  1.45%; the published RIDs correspond to a 70 kg maternal weight (derived). The direction does not change the
  conclusion (all well below 10%).
- Dose stated as methylprednisolone sodium succinate 1000 mg (label convention is methylprednisolone equivalents);
  not settled by the paper.
- Printed acceptance date (January 13, 2014) precedes the submission date (August 20, 2014): a misprint.
