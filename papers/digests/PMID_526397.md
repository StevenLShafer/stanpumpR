# McNeil 1979: IV and oral labetalol in hypertensive patients

## Study
Twelve patients with mild-to-moderate hypertension (7 M, 5 F; 21-65 y, mean 49.9; 55-99 kg, mean 76.6), normal hepatic
and renal function, on placebo or untreated for at least 8 weeks (PDF p1). Labetalol 100 mg IV over 2 min (20 mL of
5 mg/mL) on the first occasion, then 100 mg (patients 1-6) or 200 mg (7-12) orally; fasting 8 h (PDF p1-2). Venous
samples 5 min to 8 h after the end of infusion (5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60 min, 1.5-8 h). Spectrofluorimetric assay,
sensitivity about 20 ng/mL. Post-infusion data fitted per patient with AUTOAN to two or three exponentials, corrected
to bolus intercepts (Loo-Riegelman); V1, Vd-beta, terminal half-life and CL from the coefficients (PDF p2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- IV 100 mg: SBP/DBP fell within 5 min of the infusion from 152/99.4 and kept falling to a minimum of 131/85 mmHg at
  3 h; a substantial effect persisted to 8 h (PDF p4; Fig. 2).
- No correlation between AUC and the area under the BP-fall curve; inverse correlation between the 5-45 min plasma
  levels and maximum DBP fall (r 0.61-0.77) (PDF p4).
- Authors raise a deep tissue compartment or an active metabolite as the explanation (abstract; PDF p5).
- No ke0, no effect-compartment model, no concentration-effect parameters.

## Covariates
None modelled; volume correlated poorly with weight and surface area (PDF p3).

## Caveats
Twelve patients, single IV dose, mixed bi/triexponential fits with no reported rate constants; spectrofluorimetric
assay; secondary peaks after food on oral dosing prevented an oral terminal phase.
