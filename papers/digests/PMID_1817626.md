# Robison 1991: plasma bupivacaine after sciatic + femoral "3 in 1" block, with and without adrenaline

## Study
Randomised, 22 adults for total knee arthroplasty under GA (diazepam premed; methohexitone or thiopentone;
N2O/O2/enflurane; spontaneous ventilation). Combined sciatic (15 mL, supine Raj approach; start of injection = time 0)
plus femoral "3 in 1" (30 mL) block: **45 mL 0.375% bupivacaine = 168.75 mg**, plain (n = 11, 5 male) or with
adrenaline 1:200,000 (n = 11, 4 male). Age median 65 (25-71) plain, 65 (38-75) adrenaline; weight mean 62 (SD 12) and
66 (14) kg (Table I). Thigh tourniquet 400 mmHg inflated about 32-36 min after the block. Antecubital venous plasma at
0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45, 60, 120 min; HPLC-UV, LOD 0.05 µg/mL; total drug. No PK model.

Adrenaline significantly lower at 10, 15, 20 min. Peak inversely related to weight with adrenaline (P < 0.005), not
with plain; no age relation. Five plain and two adrenaline patients exceeded 2 µg/mL; none exceeded 4 µg/mL; no
toxicity (under GA).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured (analgesia not assessed). Toxicity thresholds quoted from others: 4 µg/mL (from dog data), symptoms
at mean peak 2.24 µg/mL in awake volunteers (Scott 1975). Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0.

## Covariates
Weight correlation only (Spearman).

## Caveats
Small groups; sampling only to 120 min (adrenaline peaks possibly missed); tourniquet inflated during absorption;
venous samples; total concentration; GA masks CNS signs.
