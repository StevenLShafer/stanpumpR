# Mitchell 2003: SROM versus methadone in methadone-maintained patients

## Study
- Open-label randomised crossover study (only the order of the methadone assessment was randomised), Adelaide. 18 methadone-maintenance patients were recruited and 14 completed: 11 men and 3 women, all Caucasian, age 35 ± 8.3 (23-47) y. Seven were self-reported "holders" and 7 "non-holders" (withdrawal between doses). PDF p4-5.
- Racemic methadone oral solution against once-daily slow-release oral morphine sulphate (Kapanol, pellets emptied into liquid and drunk through a straw). Subjects were switched to SROM at about 3.5:1 morphine:methadone (based on an oral equivalence of 4:1, Sherman 1996), titrated, and kept on it about 6 weeks. Assessment came after ≥4 weeks on a stable dose. PDF p3.
- Doses at assessment: methadone 78 ± 32 mg (25-120); SROM 349 ± 137 mg (120-570) of morphine sulphate (PDF p5, image confirmed). Per kg: 1.10 (methadone) and 4.96 mg/kg (SROM) (Table 2).
- Venous plasma before dosing and at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11 and 23 h at steady state. HPLC for (R)-methadone and morphine base (Doverty 2001; Foster 2000). QC precision and inaccuracy <10%. Total (not free) plasma concentrations; morphine glucuronides were not measured.
- PD measures at 0-23 h: 16-item Methadone Symptoms Checklist, pupil diameter (video, 65 lux) and respiratory rate (observed covertly).
- Non-compartmental analysis: AUC0-23 by linear trapezoid, Css = AUC/23 h, and Cmax/Cmin/Tmax observed. AUC, Css, Cmax and Cmin are **normalised to 70 mg racemic methadone and 300 mg SROM**. Methadone is reported for (R)-methadone only.
- Additional drug use (Table 1): during methadone, plasma morphine was found in 3 subjects (5.3 ± 4.3 ng/mL, 1.2-11.4). Benzodiazepines and cannabinoids were common.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC / minimum effective concentration:** none reported. This is a maintenance (withdrawal-suppression) population, not an analgesia study, and the paper defines no concentration threshold.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis:** none estimated. The authors describe withdrawal, pupil diameter and respiratory rate as inversely related to plasma concentration over the dosing interval (Fig. 1). They fitted no concentration-effect model and did not analyse hysteresis.
- **Potency / equivalence ratio** (oral, steady state, opioid-tolerant): morphine sulphate to racemic methadone was 4.64 ± 1.0 (3.82-8.00) (PDF p5). In the Discussion, all but one subject were between 3.8:1 and 5:1, and that subject was at 8:1 (PDF p8). The starting conversion was about 3.5:1, from an assumed 4:1. The authors contrast this with equianalgesic tables (1:1 to 4:1, single-dose, opioid-naive) and with chronic pain switching (5:1 to 20:1) (PDF p8). This is a withdrawal-suppression equivalence, not an analgesic one.
- The overall magnitude of withdrawal, pupil and respiratory effects did not differ between drugs (ANOVA main effects P = 0.58, 0.60, 0.70). The time courses did differ (drug × time interactions).

## Covariates
None modelled. Holding status: non-holders had a shorter (R)-methadone Tmax (1.7 vs 3.4 h) and more withdrawal at 0 h and 23 h on methadone. There was no PK difference by holding status on SROM.

## Caveats
- n = 14, open-label, mostly men, tolerant maintenance patients with concurrent benzodiazepine and cannabis use.
- Concentrations are dose-normalised means, not observed individual values, and there are no individual ranges.
- Methadone is (R)-enantiomer only. Morphine is total plasma parent; M3G and M6G were not measured.
- The formulation is once-daily Kapanol (slow-release), not immediate release.
- AUC was over 0-23 h, not a full 24 h interval.
