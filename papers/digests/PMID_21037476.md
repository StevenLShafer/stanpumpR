# Sahinovic 2010: administration and monitoring of IV anesthetics

## Study
Narrative review (Current Opinion in Anesthesiology, printed pp 734-740, PDF pp 1-7). Topics: target-controlled infusion (TCI) and effect-site targeting; propofol PK model comparisons; delivery-system errors; monitoring hypnosis (EEG indices) and analgesia (surgical stress index, RIII reflex, noxious stimulation response index); PK and PD interactions between hypnotics and opioids; advisory displays; closed-loop control.

## Model and parameters
No parameters are given. The paper reports no PK table. The statements relevant to models:

```yaml
parameters: []   # review: no model parameters or PK values
```

- Masui et al. (ref 10, summarized p 735): of Marsh, Schnider, Schuttler and a physiologically based recirculatory model, the Schnider model performed best overall for TCI and advisory displays.
- Masui et al. (ref 11): early-phase propofol PK depends on infusion rate (10-160 mg/kg/h) and age is a covariate for lag time; PD was not infusion-rate dependent.
- Vuyk et al. (ref 47): midazolam lowers propofol metabolic and distribution clearances.
- Caution (p 735): most IV PK-PD models come from healthy volunteers, so use in children, the elderly and the morbidly obese is limited.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, EC50, Emax, MEAC or analgesic concentration values are reported. No opioid potency or equianalgesic ratio. Opioid audit (remifentanil is discussed): none.
- Qualitative: effect-compartment-controlled TCI may control the dose-response relation better than plasma targeting because plasma is not the effect site (p 734). Response-surface interaction work is cited (refs 48-55): propofol-remifentanil, sevoflurane-remifentanil synergy; propofol-sevoflurane additive (ref 54). Propofol EC50 for adequate anesthesia in children unaffected by dexmedetomidine (ref 55). Remifentanil and propofol effect-site concentrations feed the noxious stimulation response index (ref 46), which predicted the analgesic component better than EEG indices.
