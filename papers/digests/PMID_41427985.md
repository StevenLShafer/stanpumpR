# Ter Horst 2025: lacosamide concentration/dose ratios in pregnancy, six-patient case series

## Study
- Retrospective case series from a Dutch specialised epilepsy clinic (SEIN, Zwolle) with TDM at Isala; samples January 2017 to September 2025 (PDF p2).
- Inclusion: women 18-45 y with lacosamide levels in pregnancy plus a baseline level within 6 months before pregnancy or, failing that, at least 2 months after delivery (so the comparator is the same woman, pre-pregnancy or postpartum).
- n = 6 women, ages 22-42; 5 focal, 1 generalised epilepsy; all on another antiseizure drug (carbamazepine 1; levetiracetam 3; lamotrigine + brivaracetam 1; lamotrigine + oxcarbazepine 1) plus folic acid and multivitamins (Table 1, PDF p3). Doses (mg/day as printed): 300, 2000, 250, 200, 150, 400. Doses did not change during pregnancy (Results, p3). Weights not reported.
- Assay: UPLC-MS/MS (Waters), deuterated internal standard; LLOQ 0.5 mg/L, upper limit 11.2 mg/L, within/between-day variation <4.7% (p2). Plasma; trough by local protocol, but sampling time could not be controlled (Discussion, p5).
- C/D ratio from the mean plasma concentration per trimester divided by dose; missing trimesters filled by multiple imputation. RTC = concentration in pregnancy / pre-pregnancy concentration, per month. Linear mixed models (lmerTest) and GEE (geepack) in R.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model; no clearance, volume or half-life is estimated.

Results (PDF p3-4; confirmed on page images):
- C/D ratios in the trimesters ranged from 48% to 146% of baseline (text p3; Fig. 1). Discussion (p5) describes the largest third-trimester fall as almost 50%.
- Mean difference pre-pregnancy to third trimester −0.6% (95% CI −0.2% to −0.06%, p = 0.224), as printed; the point estimate lies outside its own CI, so the unit or the numbers are garbled.
- RTCs ranged from 46% to 204% across months (text p4); Fig. 2's y-axis stops at 1.50, so the 204% point is not visible.
- Fig. 1 (box plot, C/D ratio, units not stated; axis 0.01-0.04): medians read from the figure about 0.024 pre-pregnancy, 0.021 first, 0.021 second, 0.0175 third trimester (approximate, read by eye). Derived: third-trimester median about 0.73 of pre-pregnancy, i.e. apparent CL/F about 1.37x, from figure reading only.
- Seizures increased in 5 of 6 women (Table 2, p5). No association of seizure change with C/D (B −36, 95% CI −81 to 8.5, p 0.10) or RTC (B −0.37, −1.3 to 0.54, p 0.40).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: seizure-frequency category (increased/stable/decreased) per trimester. No concentration-effect relationship, no therapeutic range derived. Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Seizure type, prior seizure freedom and polytherapy were planned as GEE covariates; the authors say n was too small for covariate analysis.

## Caveats
- n = 6, retrospective, all on polytherapy (one on carbamazepine, an inducer of lacosamide clearance); adherence and sample timing uncontrolled; imputation of missing trimesters.
- Baseline is pre-pregnancy or postpartum (≥2 months), mixed across women.
- Internal inconsistencies: the −0.6% mean difference and its CI; patient 2's dose; the RTC range vs Fig. 2's axis; C/D units not given.
- The comparison "lower C/D than Schoretsanitis" and "0.6% vs MONEAD's 39.9%" are against other studies' populations, not a pregnant-vs-library baseline.

- Units: the paper's concentrations are mg/L, the same as the library's mcg/mL; lacosamide in the library is total plasma, as here.
