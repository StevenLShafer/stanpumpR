# Moulin 1991: subcutaneous vs intravenous hydromorphone infusion in cancer pain

## Study
Randomised double-blind (double-dummy) crossover in inpatients with severe cancer pain, 1988-1990. 20 enrolled
(10 M, 10 F; age 61.9 ± 11.8 y; weight mean 61.0 ± 18.3, median 53.6 kg); efficacy data in 15. Titrated first on
SC morphine (18) or hydromorphone (2); morphine converted at one-fifth the rate. Each patient: 48 h hydromorphone
infusion SC or IV, 24 h morphine washout, then 48 h at the same dose by the other route. SC sites on anterior chest
wall or abdomen. Rates 1-35 mg/h (mean 6.1, median 4.0). Breakthrough: SC morphine. VAS pain intensity, relief, mood,
sedation at 4, 8, 24, 32, 48 h. Plasma at 24 and 48 h of each infusion, HPLC-ECD, limit 2.5 ng/mL; Css from
trapezoidal area 24-48 h, normalised per mg/h.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Steady state reached by 24 h (no difference 24 vs 48 h). Normalised mean Css during SC infusion was
**78% of that during IV infusion** (P = 0.02), taken by the authors as the SC bioavailability; they suggest it may be
conservative and approach 80% or more with continued infusion. Linear correlation of IV rate with Css r = 0.85.
Elimination half-life cited as about 2.6 h (Vallner 1981), not measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No significant difference in VAS pain intensity, relief, mood or sedation between routes (P > 0.1). Breakthrough
morphine injections 4.8 (SD 4.5) IV vs 5.3 (5.6) SC.
- MEAC: none reported.
- ke0 / tPeak: none.
- Potency: hydromorphone 5-6 times morphine (used for the 1:5 conversion; Foley 1985) and at least 3 times
  diamorphine; cited, not measured.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Small n (15 analysable); opioid-tolerant cancer patients; Css ratio at steady state is a relative bioavailability
for continuous SC infusion, not a bolus; only two samples per infusion; the 48 h difference just reached
significance (P = 0.05). Linear kinetics assumed for normalisation.
