# Albert 1975: pharmacokinetics of diphenhydramine in man

## Study
Pilot study, two healthy men, both 27 y, 76 and 95 kg (PDF p2). Each had three single 50-mg diphenhydramine HCl doses 2 weeks apart: IV infusion, oral solution, and a commercial capsule (Benadryl Kapseal). IV: 240 mL infused at 4 mL/min over 1 h (Table I footnote a). Assayed doses as free base: 43.7 mg (both subjects, IV), 0.572 and 0.462 mg/kg (Table II). Fasted. Plasma by a GLC assay specific for unchanged drug; times 0.25-24 h; urine to 48 h. Whole-blood/plasma ratio and plasma binding by tritiated drug (ultrafiltration). Fitting with AUTOAN (polyexponential) and trapezoidal areas. The IV data are not shown as an infusion model: each subject's post-infusion data were fitted separately.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model was chosen: the authors say the data cannot support the choice (PDF p6). Individual post-infusion biexponential fits (Table III, PDF p7, time in h, t measured from the start so the fit starts at t = 1 h):
- Subject 1: C = 122 e^(-3.32(t-1)) + 138 e^(-0.166(t-1)) ng/mL, r2 0.996.
- Subject 2: C = 128 e^(-0.437(t-1)) + 65.5 e^(-0.0996(t-1)) ng/mL, r2 0.988.
(derived: subject 1 fast exponent 3.32/h = half-life 12.5 min; subject 2 fast exponent 0.437/h = half-life 1.6 h; slow phases 4.2 h and 7.0 h.)

IV plasma concentrations, ng/mL (Table II, PDF p4, observed, average of two determinations):

| h | 0.25 | 0.5 | 1 | 1.5 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 12 | 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| subject 1 | 120 | 222 | 258 | 150 | 132 | 83.8 | 61.6 | 19.4 | 6.49 |
| subject 2 | 31.0 | 89.1 | 179 | 170 | 147 | 80.3 | 41.5 | 18.2 | 8.19 |

AUC 0-inf of the IV infusion, ng/mL x h (Table IV, PDF p8): subject 1 1181 (trapezoid) and 1046 (polyexponential); subject 2 1062 and 1036.
Apparent clearance dose/AUC on the base (footnote 5, PDF p6): 37.0 L/h (0.48 L/h/kg) subject 1, 41.1 L/h (0.44 L/h/kg) subject 2. No volume of distribution is reported.
Apparent elimination half-life (Table VII, PDF p11): IV, from the 12 and 24 h levels 7.59 h (subject 1) and 10.4 h (subject 2); from the smallest rate constant 4.17 and 6.96 h. The authors judge the true half-life to be 4-7 h and call the data inadequate (no samples below 5 ng/mL or beyond 24 h). Oral data give about 15 h on average (compare 18 h of Glazko, ref 3).
Oral: bioavailability of the solution about 0.5 (0.53 by equation 1, 0.49 by Rowland's equation 2; Table V, PDF p9), i.e. a large first-pass effect; capsule relative to solution 0.83 (subject 1) and 1.0 (subject 2). Unchanged drug in urine 1.77% and 3.37% of the IV dose (Table II).
Blood/plasma ratio 0.82 mean, independent of concentration (113 and 904 ng/mL; Table VI). Unbound fraction 1.5% and 1.3% (subject 1), 1.8% and 1.7% (subject 2) at 113 and 1130 ng/mL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0 and no effect-site modelling. The only pharmacodynamic observation is clinical: both men went to sleep shortly after the 1-h IV infusion ended but were only drowsy after oral doses. Peak IV/oral-solution plasma ratio was 3.7 (subject 1) and 2.8 (subject 2); the authors suggest sleep may occur above about 70 ng/mL plasma (PDF p10-11; total, not free, drug; end point: observed sleep, not scored). Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None analysed (n = 2). Doses given per subject (mg/kg 0.572 and 0.462).

## Caveats
Two subjects only, sampling too sparse for a model (the first sample after the 1-h infusion ended is at 1.5 h, none beyond 24 h, assay sensitivity about 5 ng/mL at the tail). The IV fits are of post-infusion data only; terminal half-life is unreliable. Doses and clearances are on the free-base basis (HCl is 291.82/255.36 = 1.143 times heavier; derived). Concentration basis is not stated.
