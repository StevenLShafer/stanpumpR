# Gehr 1993: fosinoprilat PK and ACE-inhibition PD in haemodialysis patients

## Study
- **Patients:** six hypertensive haemodialysis patients, 4 men and 2 women (4 black, 2 white). Mean age 40 (13) y and
  weight 73 (16) kg.
- **Design:** open-label and randomised, with three periods at least 14 days apart:
  - 7.5 mg ¹⁴C-fosinoprilat IV (infusion duration not stated);
  - 10 mg ¹⁴C-fosinopril sodium orally, both with sampling to 96 h before dialysis resumed;
  - 10 mg unlabelled fosinopril sodium orally, 6 h before a 4-h dialysis.
- **Sampling:** plasma at 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 16, 24, 48, 72 and 96 h; faeces for 120 h. Dialysate and
  arterial and venous samples in 4 patients.
- **Assays:** TLRC (LOD 2 ng/mL) and HPLC for parent and metabolites; RIA for unlabelled fosinoprilat (LOD 1 ng/mL);
  a serum ACE radioassay.
- **Analysis:** noncompartmental, with area and moment analysis. The PD model was Emax on % ACE inhibition. Bristol-
  Myers Squibb authors (Grasela, Duchin).

- **Derived checks:**
  - Vss/CL = 11.0 L / 0.678 L/h = 16.2 h, against MRT 16.3. So the MRT carries no information beyond Vss and CL.
  - 7.5 mg / 11804 ng·h/mL = 10.6 mL/min, against a mean CL of 11.3 (mean of individual values).
  - Dose-normalised mean AUCs with a fosinopril-sodium-to-fosinoprilat molar correction give about 27%, against
    29.2%. The paper does not state its molar handling, so this is only an approximate check.
- **Binding and metabolites:**
  - Plasma protein binding of radioactivity was 96.5% (93.2-98.4).
  - Fosinoprilat was about 75% of circulating radioactivity after oral dosing. The glucuronide was 9-15% and
    p-hydroxy fosinoprilat 2-6%.
  - Faecal recovery was 37.9% (IV) and 27.5% (oral), likely incomplete because of constipation.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **ACE inhibition:** serum ACE activity was inhibited for a prolonged time. An Emax model fitted only some data sets:
  "three patients" in the summary, and 2 IV plus 2 oral fits in the text (p4). Individual Emax was 95.3-102.5% and
  IC50 2.58-4.15 ng/mL. There is no population estimate.
- **Blood pressure:**
  - The maximum fall was 30/15 mmHg at 3 h after IV, to 114/71.
  - It was 22/13 mmHg at 6 h after oral, to 119/71.
- **Hormones:** PRA rose by up to 246% (IV) and 173% (oral). Aldosterone fell by up to 23% and 42%.
- **Not reported:** ke0 and hysteresis analysis.

## Covariates
None modelled (n=6). The authors compare these patients with patients with renal impairment from other work (Hui
1991), finding CL similar and about half that of normal subjects.

## Caveats
- Six patients and noncompartmental means only.
- The IV infusion duration is not stated.
- Units differ between tables: IV AUC in ng·h/mL, oral AUC in µg·h/mL.
- Source typo: the summary (p1, image) gives the IV Cmax as "2,042 µg·ml⁻¹". Table 1 gives 2042.1 ng/mL. A µg/mL
  value is physically impossible for a 7.5 mg dose (it implies a 3.7 mL volume).
- The summary rounds the faecal metabolite fractions (about 15% and 2%). The text gives 17.0% and 2.4% after the
  oral dose.
