# Chatelut 1996: population PK of carboplatin in children

## Study
Population PK (NONMEM IV, PREDPP ADVAN3 TRANS4, proportional residual error) in 57 children, 58 cycles (one child studied twice), Toulouse and Newcastle. Median age 5 y (0.17-18), weight 17 kg (3.6-85), height 110 cm (45-200), BSA 0.71 m2 (0.23-1.9, Dubois), serum creatinine 44 umol/L (27-268; below 100 for 55 patients, Jaffe), GFR median 54 mL/min (6-176) (Table I, PDF p. 2). 35 male, 22 female; 7 with unilateral nephrectomy; 11 previous cisplatin (Table II, as read from the garbled text; counts for diagnoses not reliably legible). Carboplatin 37.5-1020 mg as 60 min to 24 h IV infusions in 5% dextrose; 5-12 plasma samples per patient over 24 h after the infusion (482 concentrations). Plasma ultrafiltrate platinum by flameless atomic absorption: the analyte is ultrafilterable platinum, reported as ultrafilterable carboplatin.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, first-order elimination. Mean individual parameters, Table III (58 cycles; Bayesian posthoc): CL 61.5 mL/min (SD 38.2, range 9-168); CL per BSA 74.6 mL/min/m2 (SD 23.8, 17-131); Vss 10.6 L (SD 9.1, 2-54); Vss per BSA 11.9 L/m2 (SD 4.1; the range printed "10-18" is not a plausible range for that mean and SD).

Final model (printed p. 439-440, 95% CI): CL (mL/min) = th1 x weight x (1 - th2 x SCr) x (1 - th3 x Np) + th4; Vss (L) = th5 x weight, with V1 = th5a x weight and V2 = th5b x weight. SCr in umol/L, weight in kg, Np = 1 for previous unilateral nephrectomy else 0. Interindividual CV of CL falls from 74% (no covariate) to 49% (weight) and 29% (final; 95% CI 22-34%). Interindividual variability of Vss is not stated.

Predictive performance: predicted AUC as % of observed, median 107% (range 50-191%; 25th-75th percentile 87-125%); r = 0.90 between predicted and observed CL (p. 440).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD. Carboplatin AUC is related to toxicity in the introduction only; no target AUC is derived. Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Univariate screening (Table IV, change in NONMEM objective function): CL vs weight 122, BSA 153, Gehan-George BSA 148, height 98, age 85, sex 34, SCr 93, nephrectomy 117, cisplatin 28, location 1 (not significant). Vss vs weight 161, BSA 157, height 127, age 144, sex 41. Backward elimination (Table V): age, sex, cisplatin pretreatment dropped from the CL model (change 0, 0, 2); SCr (38) and nephrectomy (41) kept; replacing weight x th1 by a constant raised the objective function by 270. Weight replaced BSA in the final model (objective function 7.0 better, p < 0.01) once the nonrenal constant th4 was added; the authors prefer weight because height is hard to measure in small children and Dubois's formula rests on 9 subjects. SCr enters by subtraction in children (the authors' adult formula divides weight by SCr).

## Caveats
Equation CL is for ultrafilterable platinum, not total platinum. Mixed assays across two centres. Two children with SCr above 100 umol/L (maximum 268) drive the SCr slope (th2 constrained positive in the full fit; the refit above doubles it to 0.00775). 19 of 57 patients appeared in Newell 1993. Prediction error is wide (AUC 50-191% of observed). The authors call for prospective validation.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No repo claim rests on this paper. Independent comparison (derived: the formula applied to a child of 17 kg and SCr 44 umol/L, the study's medians, no nephrectomy):
- Chatelut 1996: CL = 2.85 x 17 x (1 - 0.00357 x 44) + 8.73 = 49.6 mL/min = 2.98 L/h; Vss = 0.49 x 17 = 8.3 L.
- carboplatinPediatric (Duong 2019, R/drugs_carboplatinPediatric.R:82-89): CL 2.7 x (17/15.5)^0.75 = 2.89 L/h (3% below Chatelut); Vss (8.3 + 0.74) x 17/15.5 = 9.9 L (19% above).
- carboplatinPeng (Peng 1995, R/drugs_carboplatinPeng.R:59, per m2; BSA taken as about 0.71 m2, the study's median): CL 6.31 x 0.71 = 4.5 L/h (about 50% above Chatelut); the Peng header already predicts this (40-50% above Duong).
