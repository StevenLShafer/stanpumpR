# Juma 2026: lumefantrine population PK in pregnant vs non-pregnant women, Kenya

## Study
- Open PK study at Ahero County Hospital, Kisumu, western Kenya, Aug 2013 to Apr 2014 (PDF p3, p5). 75 women with uncomplicated P. falciparum malaria: 25 non-pregnant, 30 second-trimester, 20 third-trimester (abstract, PDF p1).
- Dose: artemether/lumefantrine 80/480 mg (four 20/120 mg Coartem tablets) at 0, 8, 24, 36, 48 and 60 h, with water then 250 mL milk or a light meal (PDF p3). Lumefantrine 480 mg per dose.
- Sampling: dense venous plasma, two randomised sampling arms of 12 times each over 72 h, plus single samples on days 4, 5, 6, 7 and 14 (PDF p3-4). 1151 concentrations; 86 (7.4%) below the LLOQ of 10 ng/mL, omitted (M1) (PDF p4-5).
- Assay: protein precipitation then LC-MS, plasma (total), CVs 6.10%, 4.69%, 2.50% at 25, 1600, 3200 ng/mL (PDF p4).
- Software: NONMEM 7.4 FOCE-I, PsN, SIR for uncertainty (PDF p4).
- Demographics (Table 1, PDF p6, rendered): median (range) age 20.2 (18.0-35.3) y pregnant vs 25.2 (18.1-35.6) non-pregnant; weight 62.0 (40.0-86.6) vs 59.5 (45.0-89.0) kg; height 164 vs 165 cm; gestational age 26 (13-40) weeks. Day 28 PCR-corrected cure 44/44 and 22/22 (100%).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment disposition, flexible transit absorption (3 transit compartments, fixed) plus first-order Ka, first-order elimination (Figure 1, PDF p6). Allometric body weight on all CL and volumes a priori, exponents 0.75 and 1, centred on 70 kg (Eqs 2-3, PDF p5). F fixed to 100% for the population with Box-Cox IIV, and a dose-saturation term F = 100% x (1 - Dose/(Dose50 + Dose)), Dose50 3.84 mg/kg fixed from Kloprogge 2018 (Eq 4, PDF p5). Final covariate: pregnancy status (categorical, proportional, theta = theta_TV x (1 + theta_cov)) on CL and Vc; it beat gestational age and trimester on OFV (PDF p6-7). Table 2 (PDF p9, printed p919) values are for a typical non-pregnant 70 kg woman.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. The study uses day-7 lumefantrine concentration against a 200 ng/mL threshold "previously associated" with treatment failure (Discussion, printed p920, PDF p10): 44% of pregnant women fell below it against 20% of non-pregnant women. Plasma (total) concentration; the threshold comes from other studies, not fitted here. Parasite clearance half-life 3.24 h pregnant vs 2.87 h non-pregnant, p = .06 (PDF p5). Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
- Body weight, allometric on all CL and volumes (fixed exponents 0.75, 1; reference 70 kg).
- Pregnancy status: CL x 1.232, Vc x 1.281 (Table 2). The full-covariate approach (500 bootstraps) agreed; pregnancy did not change MTT or F (Figure 2, PDF p6, not read as an image). Gestational age was also significant (p < .01 on CL) but the categorical covariate fit better; trimester not significant (PDF p7).
- Age, baseline parasitaemia and temperature: not significant.

## Caveats
- Authors: under-powered for gastrointestinal effects; control group half the size of the pregnant groups; pregnant women were younger, had lower parasite density and less fever than the non-pregnant women (Discussion, PDF p10).
- Desbutyl-lumefantrine not modelled; Dose50 fixed, not estimated; absorption IOV and Ka shrinkage 60%.
- The 'higher CL and V' is against the study's own non-pregnant Kenyan women, whose clearance (4.03 L/h at 70 kg) is not the same population as any other model's.
- Text layer gives a different weight for non-pregnant women in the Results prose (median 62, range 40-87) from Table 1 (59.5, 45.0-89.0); the page image shows the table values. Unused by stanpumpR.
