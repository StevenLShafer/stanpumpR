# Lebaudy 2008: enoxaparin pharmacokinetics through pregnancy (anti-Xa, population model)

## Study
Prospective population PK, Pitie-Salpetriere and Hotel-Dieu, Paris. 75 pregnant women (Table 1, PDF p2:
age 31 (5) y, height 165 (7) cm, weight 63 (13) kg; mostly antiphospholipid syndrome 52%) on SC enoxaparin,
prophylactic 4000-6000 IU once daily or therapeutic 100 IU (1 mg)/kg twice daily (initial regimens Table 1), and 38
non-pregnant controls (age 35 +/- 7 y, weight 62 +/- 16 kg, Scr 78 +/- 27 umol/L; 100 IU/kg b.i.d. treatment or
4000 IU/day prophylaxis). Sparse routine sampling: 343 anti-Xa values in pregnancy (1-49 h after the dose; 52% at
3-4 h), 118 in controls, 80 postpartum from 32 women. Anti-Xa by chromogenic assay (STA-Rotachrom Heparin,
Stago), IU/mL, LLOQ 0.01 IU/mL, interassay CV 6-8%. NONMEM; bootstrap (1,300 sets) validation.
Covariates by gestational stage in Table 2 (p2): weight 65 -> 75 kg from first trimester to ninth month; Scr
62 (16) -> 55-56 umol/L; creatinine clearance 7.5 -> 9.3-9.4 L/h (method: from Scr, age, weight; formula not named).

## Model and parameters
One compartment, first-order absorption, exponential IIV on CL/F and V/F, additive residual error (p3).
All parameters apparent (CL/F, V/F); anti-Xa activity is the "concentration".

Stage summaries (p3, rendered):
- Non-pregnant controls: CL/F 0.52 +/- 0.03 L/h, V/F 7.03 +/- 1.05 L, ka 0.71 +/- 0.18 /h.
- All pregnant women pooled: CL/F 0.78 +/- 0.04 L/h (abstract: +/- 0.03), V/F 10.5 +/- 1.3 L, ka 0.63 +/- 0.17 /h;
  CL/F +48% and V/F +49% vs the non-pregnant controls (t-test, P < 0.001); ka not different.
- Covariate model: CL/F = 0.781 x [(body weight/serum creatinine)/1.27]^0.423 (Scr presumably umol/L, since
  WT/Scr ~ 1.2 at Table 2 values; units not stated); IIV on CL/F 30.4 -> 20.3%.
- V/F = 7.81 x (body weight/70) x 1.41 at >31 weeks of amenorrhoea; IIV on V/F 35.9 -> 26.0%.
- Postpartum: CL/F 0.71 +/- 0.07 L/h, ka 0.62 +/- 0.32 /h; V/F 11.9 +/- 2.6 L up to day 4, 5.58 +/- 1 L after day 4.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not a PD study; the measured quantity is anti-Xa activity itself. No ke0. Observed peak anti-Xa 4 h after
40 mg/day: 0.47 +/- 0.13 IU/mL first trimester vs 0.32 +/- 0.08 third (P = 0.002); therapeutic regimen 0.86 +/- 0.24
vs 0.84 +/- 0.23 (p5). A target of 0.1-0.2 IU/mL during pregnancy is quoted from another source (ref 35, p6), not
derived here. Simulated 4000 IU/day peak 0.42 (first trimester) vs 0.32 IU/mL (ninth month) (p4).

## Covariates
CL/F: body weight / serum creatinine, power 0.423, no gestational-age effect after the first-trimester rise.
V/F: body weight (linear, /70 kg) and a step x1.41 after 31 weeks. Postpartum V/F returns below baseline
after day 4.

## Caveats
Routine, opportunistic sampling (late points mostly before epidural at delivery); 52% of samples at peak, so V/F and ka are
better informed than the terminal phase. Pregnancy vs control comparison is between groups (different women,
different indications; controls older, with higher serum creatinine). Assay is anti-Xa activity, not drug mass.
The two-step V/F is empirical; the authors speculate on mechanism. Creatinine-clearance formula and Scr units in
the CL equation are not stated. Text and Table 4 give slightly different CL/F typical values (0.781 vs 0.81
bootstrap median).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- CL factor 1.5 (R/drugs_enoxaparin.R:69, 79-80, 117-120): 0.78/0.52 = 1.50 (derived), the paper's own
  "+48%" (p3). Table 3 stage ratios run 1.40 (10.7 wk) to 1.56 (34 wk) (derived). **Matches** the paper as a
  ratio. The quoted "0.78 +/- 0.03" is the abstract's SD; the Results give +/- 0.04: immaterial.
- Comparator: the paper's ratio is against its own young non-pregnant controls (0.52 L/h). stanpumpR applies it
  to Berges' elderly model, whose non-pregnant value for a 30-y woman is already 1.27x those controls (derived;
  see open questions). Direction of the error, if any: the library's pregnant CL/F is above Lebaudy's measured
  pregnant 0.78 L/h. Not a transcription error; flagged as **[decision]**.
- V factor 1.4 (lines 70-71, 80-83, 121): Table 4 theta5 = 1.41 (1.04-1.86), applied after 31 weeks on top of a
  linear weight term. The header's quotation ("an additional increase of 41% during the last 2 months of
  pregnancy, independent of changes in weight") matches the abstract. Applying it to the central volume of a
  two-compartment model, with Berges' WT^1.25 standing in for Lebaudy's linear weight term, is the repo's
  structural choice. **Matches** for the late-pregnancy state; overstated before 31 weeks (open question).
- "Stage having no influence" on CL (header line 68): matches abstract and Table 3 after the first trimester.
- Renal term (lines 84-88): the paper attributes the CL rise to the ~50% GFR rise (p5) but models CL on WT/Scr;
  the repo's direct application, with Zarek's non-renal argument, is the repo's interpretation, not this paper's.
- Header line 90 and registry note say the Lebaudy numbers came "from the abstracts": now confirmed against the
  full text (p3, Tables 3-4).
- tests/testthat/test-drugs-enoxaparin.R:88-89 pin the ratios to the constants and lines 96-97 the reference:
  consistent with the code.
- docs/pregnancy.md:111 (1.500, 1.40, Lebaudy 2008, high) and pregnancyRegistry.csv:144 (cl 1.5, v 1.4,
  trimester third, comparator non-pregnant women): **match** the code. Registry n = 123 is Patel 2013's, not
  Lebaudy's 75 + 38.
- Independent comparison with the library's Berges model (not citing this paper for these values): Lebaudy
  non-pregnant CL/F 0.52 L/h, V/F 7.03 L (one compartment), ka 0.71 /h vs Berges 0.70 L/h at 65 kg/CLcr 69,
  central 6.43 + peripheral 8.18 L, ka 0.63 /h. Lebaudy's young controls clear less than Berges' elderly
  reference patient; ka agrees within the CIs.
