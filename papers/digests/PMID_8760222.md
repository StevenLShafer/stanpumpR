# Wastney 1996: calcium kinetics in adolescent girls vs young women

## Study
- 14 healthy white girls, 11-14 y (13 ± 1 y; 54 ± 12 kg; 158 ± 5 cm; 0.7 ± 1 y post-menarche) and 11 healthy white
  women, 19-31 y (22 ± 4 y; 64 ± 9 kg; 167 ± 7 cm); serum Ca 10.6 mg/dL in both (Table 1, PDF p. 2, printed R209).
- Housed 21 days at a campus residence: 7 days' adjustment to a diet of 1,330 mg Ca/day (250 mg breakfast, 500-600 mg at
  lunch and dinner), then a 14-day kinetic study.
- Tracers: oral ⁴⁴Ca (~38 mg, CaCO₃ capsule with breakfast) and, 1 h later, intravenous ⁴²Ca (~40 mg as CaCl₂ in 10 mL
  saline), stable isotopes.
- Sampling: blood at 1, 1.08, 1.17, 1.33, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12, 24 h then daily to 14 days (some to 21 days);
  first post-IV sample at 5 min. Complete urine and faecal collections.
- Assay: serum (total) calcium by atomic absorption; isotope enrichment by fast-atom-bombardment mass spectrometry.
- Model: SAAM compartmental model (Fig. 1), steady state assumed; oral and IV data fitted together; population values by
  extended multiple-studies analysis.

## Model and parameters
Three exchangeable compartments (1 = contains blood; 2 = "soft tissue"; 3 = exchangeable bone calcium), bone deposition as
loss L(0,3), bone resorption as input to compartment 1, urine (6), gut compartments 7-9 and faeces (5). The initial
distribution space was poorly determined and **fixed at 10 L** ("five times vascular space", PDF p. 2).
Transfer coefficients are **fractions per day**, masses in **g Ca** (Table 2, PDF p. 5, printed R212; mean ± SD):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. End points are tracer enrichment and total calcium in serum, urine and faeces; no ionized calcium, no effect
measure. Not an opioid paper (no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio).

## Covariates
Group (girls vs women) and post-menarcheal age: M(2), M(3), bone deposition and resorption fall exponentially with
post-menarcheal age, Y = P1·exp(−P2·T) + P3 (Fig. 6; P values not tabulated). M(1) and L(0,3) do not change.

## Caveats
- Healthy young females only; physiological steady state, tracer doses (~40 mg) that do not perturb serum calcium.
- Total, not ionized, serum calcium.
- First IV sample at 5 min, so the initial space was fixed (10 L), not estimated.
- **Internal inconsistency**: the abstract (PDF p. 1) gives the first pool as 1.6 g; Table 2 gives M(1) 1.06 g and Fig. 5
  1.0 ± 0.03 g. The table is presumably right (1.06 g in 10 L = 10.6 mg/dL, the measured serum Ca; derived).
- Women's L(1,2) and L(3,2) SDs exceed their means (poorly identified in some subjects).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **calcium.md:15**: "21 days" matches the 21-day protocol; "steady-state" matches the constant-diet steady-state
  assumption; "long-term" matches 14-day kinetic sampling. "Oral dosing" is incomplete: the kinetics rest on an IV ⁴²Ca
  tracer as well as the oral tracer (see open question). The point the help makes is supported: these are tracer kinetics
  of total calcium over days, with time constants in fractions per day, not the first-hour ionized-calcium response to a
  pharmacological IV dose. Whether Ansari meant this Wastney paper cannot be checked from the repo. **cannot verify**
  (citation identity) / **matches** (substance).
- Independent comparison (derived, illustrative only): the fixed 10 L initial space is close to Ansari's central volume
  of 10.9 L quoted at calcium.md:11. Elimination from compartment 1 here (urine + endogenous faecal, women
  (0.19 + 0.11)/day × 10 L ≈ 3 L/day ≈ 0.002 L/min) is about 1/90 of Ansari's 0.183 L/min clearance, consistent with the
  help's own statement (calcium.md:37) that the Ansari "clearance" reflects first-hour redistribution and regulation and
  that longer sampling would show a slower terminal phase. Different end points (total tracer vs change in ionized Ca),
  so not a discrepancy.
