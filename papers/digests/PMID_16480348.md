# Stonell 2006: effect-site targeted patient-controlled propofol sedation for colonoscopy

## Study
Randomised, double-blind trial, 40 adults aged 18-75, ASA I-III, elective colonoscopy at one Australian hospital (20 per group, 32 M / 8 F). Both groups got fentanyl 1.0 mcg/kg IV (per kg) first. PCS group: propofol effect-site TCI controlled by the patient (RUGLOOP II, Asena GH MkIII pump), start target 0.8 mcg/mL, +0.1 mcg/mL per press, 3 min lockout, target lowered by 0.1 mcg/mL after 5 min without a press (PDF p2). Control (AAS): anaesthetist boluses to OAA/S 3; concentrations simulated afterwards in RUGLOOP II. Primary end point: patient satisfaction VAS. Sample size 16/group (mean VAS 90 vs 80, SD 10). Weight PCS 79 (14) vs AAS 84 (29) kg; age 46 (13) vs 47 (13) y.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper (fentanyl was a fixed co-medication; no fentanyl concentration, MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio reported). Propofol end points: OAA/S and BIS. Reported (Table 3, from scrambled text layer, unconfirmed on image): time to sedation 3 (1.1) AAS vs 13 (7.1) PCS min; peak predicted effect-site concentration 4.3 (1.5) AAS vs 2.2 (0.7) PCS mcg/mL; minimum BIS 58 (15) vs 71 (16); median BIS 78 (11) vs 88 (9); median OAA/S 3 vs 4; minimum OAA/S 2 vs 3. Total propofol 288 (213) vs 238 (143) mg. Peak Ce above 3 mcg/mL in two PCS patients who struggled to press the button. No EC50 or gamma. The authors discuss ke0 qualitatively: a faster ke0 gives less overshoot of plasma concentration and a slower induction.

## Covariates
None modelled. Schnider covariates (age, lean body mass) are those of the source model.

## Caveats
Low recruitment (38%), male-dominated, tertiary-educated sample; hand-set design difficult for some. PCS sedated more slowly and less deeply than AAS; AAS had more hypotension (Table 2). Effect-site concentrations in AAS were simulated, not measured. No plasma assay.
