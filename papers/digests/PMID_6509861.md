# Grasela 1984: one-compartment procainamide population model from routine data

PDF page n = printed page 544 + n.

## Study
39 UCSF inpatients on procainamide for ventricular arrhythmias (Table I: 34 men, 24 with CHF, mostly NYHA I; 19 on
Procan SR; age 59.7 ± 13.7 y; weight 72.4 ± 13.5 kg; height 175.1 ± 9.1 cm, missing in 7; SCr 1.22 ± 0.34 mg/dL;
plasma procainamide 7.9 ± 5.3 mg/L). 116 plasma concentrations, 14 timed urine collections (procainamide and
NAPA). HPLC. Oral (Pronestyl capsules, Procan SR) and IV dosing, every input modelled as a zero-order infusion
over the time to peak (or infusion duration) (eq 1-2). One compartment; renal (CLR), acetylation (CLA, from urinary
NAPA, eq 4) and other (CLo) clearances; NONMEM (extended least squares); log-transformed residuals.

## Model and parameters
SIZE = (WT/70)^θW (HT/175)^θH (eq 5); FR = (140 - AGE) / [(113 + 20 GEN) SCr], GEN = 1 for a man (eq 7, PDF p4
rendered), the fraction of the renal function (120 mL/min) of a healthy 70 kg 20-year-old man;
CLR = CLR,std SIZE FR (1 - θCHF1 CHF); CLo = CLo,std SIZE; CLA = CLA,std SIZE (1 - θCHF2 CHF);
Vd = Vd,std SIZE (1 - θCHF3 CHF) (eq 8-12). Standard individual: 70 kg, 170 cm (p548; eq 5 normalises to 175),
CLcr 120.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None.

## Covariates
Weight (linear), Eq 7 renal fraction (age, sex, SCr), CHF (11% on CLR and CLA). Age enters only through Eq 7.

## Caveats
39 patients, 5 women; Eq 7's sex term as printed gives a woman 133/113 = 1.18 times a man's FR at the same SCr,
opposite to Cockcroft-Gault; the paper does not comment. CHF fall SEM 0.15 (Table III) is hard to square with
p < 0.01 (Table II). Acetylator bimodality not visible. Mostly oral, no distribution phase. Standard height 170
vs 175 cm normaliser (irrelevant: θH = 0). Dose basis: the text equates 1000 mg Pronestyl with 850 mg base
(1000 mg of the hydrochloride contains 866 mg base) and F(Pronestyl) is also 0.85, so whether the model's doses
are base or hydrochloride is not stated explicitly.

  the stated rationale only).
  **Resolved, Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-27:** the dose basis is base, settled by the text (PDF p9: "1000mg of
  Pronestyl (850mg procainamide base)"), which agrees with the legend; procainamide exists only as the
  hydrochloride, so 850 mg base and 1000 mg hydrochloride are one dose. The model's F for Pronestyl is 0.85
  (Table II, PDF p7), so its CL and Vd are absolute and the code's base conversion is correct. Figure 2 matches the
  model when 850 mg enters it (8.63 / 3.17). Shafer's account (first-hand: he knew Sheiner well): Sheiner and
  Grasela would never have folded the salt-to-base difference into bioavailability; the likely cause is that the
  figure was drawn with the salt dose, 1000, instead of 850 base, then x F 0.85 = 850 mg, which reproduces the
  printed curve exactly. This is provenance from Shafer, not something the paper states. It is a slip in the
  paper's illustration, not an error in stanpumpR's parameters. The repo's comment and test misread the figure (7.3 / 2.7) and cite it as
  the evidence; the evidence is the text. No change to computed values.
