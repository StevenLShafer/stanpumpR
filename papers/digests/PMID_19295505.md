# Hebert 2009: glyburide PK in gestational diabetes vs nonpregnant type 2 diabetes

The archived file is the NIH Public Access author manuscript (18 pages, manuscript page numbers = PDF pages), not
the journal typeset version (printed pp. 607-614).

## Study
Obstetric-Fetal Pharmacology Research Unit Network, five US sites. Steady-state PK in 40 women with GDM at 28-38
weeks (mean 34.6 +/- 2.9) on stable glyburide monotherapy >= 1 week, and 26 nonpregnant women with T2DM on glyburide
(age <= 55); MMTT also in 40 gestational-age-matched healthy pregnant women (Methods, p. 6). Table 1 (p. 16, rendered):
GDM age 32 +/- 6, weight 96 +/- 22 kg, BMI 36 +/- 6, dose 3.4 +/- 2.4 mg twice daily (1.25-10); T2DM age 41 +/- 8,
100 +/- 19 kg, BMI 37 +/- 4, dose 3.8 +/- 3.4 mg twice daily (1.25-10); CYP2C9 *1/*1 in 34/40 GDM and 19/26 T2DM.
All GDM and 25 T2DM women dosed twice daily, one T2DM woman once daily. Investigators supplied drug for the week before
the study. Samples pre-dose and 0.5-12 h (13 samples; 24 h for the once-daily subject); one urine interval for M1.
HPLC-MS, LOQ 0.25 ng/mL; protein binding by ultrafiltration of Cmax and 12-h samples. NCA (linear trapezoid,
CL/F = dose/AUC0-tau) and a compartmental model: one compartment, first-order elimination, absorption by an inverse
Gaussian density (MAT, CV2), F fixed to 1 (apparent values). Statistics: Wilcoxon signed rank, means +/- SD.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
MMTT minimal-model indices (Table 3, p. 18, rendered), GDM vs healthy pregnant vs T2DM (p vs GDM): insulin
sensitivity SI 1.2 +/- 0.9 vs 5.8 +/- 3.9 (< 0.0001) vs 3.0 +/- 3.3 (< 0.01) x 10^-4 min^-1 per uU/mL; Phi_total
140.6 +/- 99.9 vs 124.0 +/- 53.1 (0.7) vs 54.8 +/- 48.6 (< 0.0001) x 10^-9 min^-1; Phi_d 1720 vs 3120 vs 1535;
disposition index 193.1 +/- 225.5 vs 698.8 +/- 540.7 (< 0.0001) vs 165.6 +/- 232.6 (0.3) x 10^-13 min^-2 per uU/mL;
peak post-MMTT glucose 167 +/- 33 vs 122 +/- 16 vs 187 +/- 70 (unit not printed). No glyburide
concentration-effect model. The authors cite a report of no
further insulin secretion above about 200 nM (about 100 ng/mL) in healthy volunteers.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (GDM vs nonpregnant T2DM), CYP2C9 genotype (the rise was independent of genotype). Parameters adjusted for
actual body weight in statistics.

## Caveats
- Cross-sectional: different women in the two groups, T2DM older (41 vs 32 y); no postpartum within-subject
  comparison; GDM vs T2DM confounds disease with pregnancy.
- V/F difference is weak (p = 0.04, large SDs) and from a one-compartment fit.
- The compartmental values are given only in the text, not in a table.
- The authors attribute the rise to increased intestinal and/or hepatic metabolism (likely CYP2C9 and CYP3A
  induction), since unbound CL/F and M1 formation clearance rose while binding and M1 urinary recovery did not change.
