# Vierke 2020/2021: buprenorphine-cannabis interaction in opioid maintenance therapy

## Study
Retrospective therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) analysis, University Medical Center Göttingen, 2012-2018
(PDF p2, printed 848). Patients in buprenorphine or buprenorphine/naloxone opioid maintenance therapy (OMT) for at
least 5 years, clinically stable, stable dose with take-home prescription, morning dosing only; excluded: other
illicit drugs (for at least 1 year), HIV, active hepatitis with hepatic impairment, CYP3A4/CYP2C8-interacting
medicines, renal impairment, abnormal transaminases, bilirubin, INR or γ-GT (PDF p2-3).

- 32 eligible measurements from 79 (PDF p4, printed 850): control, 5 patients / 17 measurements, all male, 3 on
  buprenorphine/naloxone; cannabis group, 5 patients / 15 measurements (40% of samples from women), all on
  buprenorphine alone. Tables give n as measurements (17 and 15), not patients.
- Mean age 36.9 ± 9.9 y (control), 33.7 ± 3.3 y (cannabis). Weight not reported. All were tobacco smokers.
- Dose (sublingual; route implied by the formulations named, not stated as a column): control 8.8 ± 3.9 mg/day
  (range 2.4-16), cannabis 8.6 ± 0.9 mg/day (range 2-10) (Table 2; PDF p5 text).
- Sampling: trough, blood drawn 30 min before the next supervised dose (PDF p2). Time since last intake, documented
  for 53% of patients: 24.2 ± 2.1 h (control), 21.6 ± 5.1 h (cannabis), p = 0.18 (PDF p4).
- Assay: serum, UPLC-MS/MS (Waters Acquity / TQ-S), deuterated internal standards; calibration 0.1-10 ng/mL; LOQ
  0.1 ng/mL buprenorphine, 0.2 ng/mL norbuprenorphine; control-sample CVs 6.8-15.9% (n = 27) (PDF p3). Total
  (not free) serum concentrations.
- Cannabis exposure: urine immunoassay (THC cutoff 50 ng/mL, Table 1), urine GC/MS, and serum THC/metabolites in 3
  patients. Statistics: Student's t (Holm-Bonferroni α = 0.00625), Mann-Whitney U, ANOVA; Statistica 13.3.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- **MEAC / effective concentration**: none. No analgesic or minimum effective concentration; doses were titrated
  clinically (withdrawal score, well-being without a "high"), not to a concentration. The paper calls for TDM but
  proposes no target range.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis**: none reported.
- **Potency ratio**: none numerical; norbuprenorphine described only qualitatively as a much less potent analgesic
  (PDF p1).
- Case report (PDF p6): after stopping cannabis, trough fell from 6.3 to 0.3 ng/mL (norbuprenorphine 1.0 to
  0.3) with withdrawal signs and craving; at 12 mg/day 14 days later, 2.1 ng/mL.

## Covariates
Cannabis use (raises buprenorphine trough about 2.7-fold, lowers metabolite/parent ratio); sex tested within the
cannabis group, not significant; duration of treatment not significant (ANOVA, Fig. 1). Naloxone combination not
associated with different levels (p = 0.522). No equations.

## Caveats
- Very small: 5 + 5 patients; measurements (not patients) treated as the unit of analysis; controls all male.
- Internal inconsistencies: text gives cannabis C/D 0.63 ± 0.54 vs Table 2 ± 0.26; Table 3 (PDF p5) gives
  metabolite/parent mean 0.423 (control) and 0.18 (cannabis), median 0.93 and 0.278, which do not match Table 2's
  0.98 and 0.39; text calls the ratio difference not significant while Table 2's Mann-Whitney p = 0.009171 is starred.
- Serum THC values printed as mg/L (14.29 mg/L THC) are implausible for cannabis users (the case report uses
  ng/mL); probably a unit misprint. Not used by stanpumpR.
- The control trough mean (2.00) is inflated by a skewed distribution (IQR 0.4-1.4, max 12.6).
- Retrospective, single centre, timing of last dose documented for only 53%.
