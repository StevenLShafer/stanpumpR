# Bouillon 1999: PK-PD model of alfentanil respiratory depression (PaCO2)

## Study
14 men, ASA I-II, median age 42 y (range printed 20-71 in the abstract and 19-72 in the text; PDF p1 vs p3), median weight 82.5 kg (68-108), before major urologic surgery, unpremedicated. Alfentanil infusion 2.3 µg/kg/min over a tight CPAP mask, FiO2 0.5, stopped at 70 µg/kg cumulative, PE'CO2 > 65 mmHg, or apnoea > 60 s. Infusion stopped early in 6 of 14. Arterial samples at 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 60 min for plasma alfentanil (HPLC-UV, LOQ 10 µg/L, CV 2.95%) and PaCO2. Sequential NONMEM IV (FOCE with interaction): PK first, then PD with the individual Bayesian PK estimates fixed. The concentrations are plasma (not whole blood, not free). Two-compartment PK, the PK fitted only for the 60-min study period.

## Model and parameters
Table 1 (PDF p5, printed 148; population mean, %CV; read on the page image): V1 5.35 L (26), V2 10.4 L (no CV), CL1 (elimination) 0.35 L/min (42), CL2 (intercompartmental) 0.54 L/min (53). Residual error 34.4% without and 16.5% with IIV. The authors say the PK serve only as input to the PD model and must not be extrapolated beyond the study period (PDF p7).

PD structure: one-compartment CO2 turnover (PaCO2 normalised to 760 mmHg), constant CO2 production, opioid reduces CO2 elimination by a fractional-Emax term c/(EC50+c) (slope n fixed at 1, since the fractional Emax model beat the linear model by AIC), CO2 stimulates elimination as (PaCO2/PaCO2(0))^F. Steady state: Valv/Valv(0) = (1 - c/(EC50+c)) * (PaCO2ss/PaCO2(0))^F (Eq. 16, PDF p5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: arterial PaCO2 (mmHg), not minute ventilation, not EEG, not analgesia. Alveolar ventilation is derived, not measured (baseline not measured).
- EC50 60.3 µg/L (CV 32%), plasma, population mean, Table 2 PDF p7. Defined as the concentration giving a 50% reduction of alveolar ventilation (CO2 elimination) when the CO2 term equals 1 (PDF p4). Population: 14 men.
- ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak: NOT estimated. Authors state the effect-site equilibration half-time of alfentanil is 1.1 min (no reference in the sentence; EEG source implied), far shorter than the ~5 min needed for a 50% PaCO2 change, so keo could not be estimated and plasma concentration was used directly (PDF p9). Hysteresis: counter-clockwise PaCO2 vs plasma concentration (Fig. 3, PDF p6), explained entirely by the inertia of the indirect model, not by an effect-site delay.
- kel_CO2 0.088 /min (Table 2, PDF p7); the model's maximal PaCO2 rise rate 3.6 mmHg/min is checked by the authors against 3-6 mmHg/min in the literature (PDF p9).
- Steady-state predictions, median (Table 3, PDF p9, read on the page image): alfentanil 0 / 50 / 100 / 150 / 200 / 250 µg/L give PaCO2 40.8 / 46.7 / 50.4 / 52.9 / 54.9 / 56.7 mmHg and fractional alveolar ventilation 1.00 / 0.89 / 0.83 / 0.78 / 0.75 / 0.72. At 50 µg/L ventilation falls by under 23% from baseline in 97.5% of patients, PaCO2 under 53.6 mmHg (PDF p11, printed 154).
- MEAC: none measured here. The authors cite postoperative-analgesia MEACs of 15-60 µg/L from other studies (their refs 34, 35, Lehmann 1990 and van den Nieuwenhuyzen 1993) and conclude 50 µg/L is attractive; assay and matrix are not stated in this paper.
- Potency ratio / equianalgesic: none.
- Patients with profound hypercapnia were at most lightly sedated (clinical scale + EEG), PDF p5-p9.

## Covariates
Weight, age and infusion completion were tested; none entered the model (PDF p3). A trend to lower F in the 6 patients whose infusion stopped early was not significant.

## Caveats
Males only, n = 14, unpremedicated, no surgical stimulation or other drugs; pain would blunt the depression. CO2 production assumed constant (literature shows 12-19% fall at 40 µg/L in one study, no change at 80 µg/L in another). F was fixed, not estimated (EC50 and F not jointly identifiable). Single-compartment CO2 body model; PK is a 60-min description only; no post-study samples; ke0 not identifiable.
