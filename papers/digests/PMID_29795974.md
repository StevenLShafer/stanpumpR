# Johnson 2018: absolute bioavailability of oral hydrocortisone on total, unbound and salivary measures

## Study
- Randomised 2-period crossover, 14 healthy men, median (IQR) age 28 (25-36) y, weight 83 (75-90) kg, BMI 25.3 (23.1-26.3) kg/m2 (p 2). Subjects as previously reported by Debono 2016 (ref 6). EudraCT 2013-000259-42.
- Endogenous cortisol suppressed with dexamethasone 1 mg at ~22:00 day 1 and ~06:00 and ~12:00 day 2 (p 3).
- Hydrocortisone 20 mg orally ("by tablet", abstract) and 20 mg IV, each at 07:00.
- Sampling: serum and saliva at -10 min, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90 min, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 h.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS (Waters Xevo TQ-MS); serum cortisol LLOQ 12.5 nmol/L, inter-assay CV 6-8%. Salivary cortisol/cortisone LOD 0.80/0.50 nmol/L.
- Unbound serum cortisol was NOT measured: it was calculated from total cortisol with a polynomial fu(Ctotal) fitted to literature data (Lentjes and Romijn 1999).
- Baseline (-10 min) subtracted; non-compartmental analysis (WinNonlin Phoenix) over 0-12 h; F from log AUC0-inf by a fixed-effects GLM (sequence, treatment); geometric mean and 95% CI.

## Caveats
- Unbound cortisol is model-calculated from a literature binding curve, not measured; the authors note this may add variability.
- 2 x 2 design; n = 14 men only.
- Authors argue unbound/salivary F is the better estimand because saturable CBG binding raises free fraction and clearance after IV; the unbound 0.88 is nevertheless "not statistically different" from 1.
- Formulation: abstract says tablet; Bindellini 2024 (PMID 38977635) describes what appears to be the same 14-subject, identical-schedule study (Debono 2016 reference, 20 mg oral vs IV) as using immediate-release granules. Not resolvable from this paper.
- RJR and MJW are Diurnal Ltd directors.
