# Gaiser 1996: Rocuronium in a pregnant patient on magnesium (case report)

## Study
Single case report (PDF p1-2). A 31-year-old woman, 28 weeks pregnant, 162 cm, 80 kg, with an open eye injury, receiving magnesium sulphate for preterm labour: 4 g loading dose then 2 g/h i.v. Magnesium concentration before induction 5.8 mmol/litre (stated therapeutic range 4-8 mmol/litre). Rapid-sequence induction: fentanyl 200 ug, thiopentone 400 mg, rocuronium 80 mg (0.9 mg/kg, stated as 3 x ED95). Maintenance with isoflurane and fentanyl (450 ug total). Neuromuscular monitoring with a peripheral nerve stimulator (train-of-four). No drug concentrations were measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: train-of-four count and post-tetanic response, a clinical monitor read by the anaesthetists. First twitch at 215 min was described as a roughly four-fold prolongation relative to the 53 min expected (summary) and "more than six times" in the Discussion (against a different comparator, the lower-dose figure; the two comparisons use different baselines). No ke0, tPeak, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio. Onset at 0.9 mg/kg quoted as 135 +/- 79 s versus 271 +/- 129 s at 0.6 mg/kg (from ref 17, not this patient). The authors give isoflurane as a possible contributor but argue it cannot explain the size of the effect.

## Covariates
Magnesium infusion (serum 5.8 mmol/litre) is the proposed cause; pregnancy and isoflurane are confounders in this patient. No equation.

## Caveats
n = 1, no control or crossover, no rocuronium or magnesium concentration time course beyond one magnesium value, no information on whether pregnancy itself changed the response (the paper says it did not enhance block in an earlier caesarean study, Abouleish 1994). Dose was above the usual 0.6 mg/kg. Concurrent isoflurane and fentanyl.
