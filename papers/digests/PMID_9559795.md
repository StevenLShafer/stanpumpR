# Tod 1998: population pharmacokinetics of amikacin in febrile neutropenic adults

## Study
- **Patients**: 57 adults (ages over 18) with febrile, severe neutropenia (neutrophil count below 500/mm^3)
  under treatment for a primary haematological disorder, Haematology Unit, Avicenne Hospital, Bobigny, France.
  Expected duration of neutropenia over 7 days; all had a central venous catheter. Pregnant women and
  HIV-infected patients were excluded (PDF p2, printed 850).
  - Diagnoses: acute myeloblastic leukaemia 18, non-Hodgkin's lymphoma 21, acute lymphoblastic leukaemia 8,
    myeloma 7, Hodgkin's lymphoma 1, agranulocytosis 1, aplastic anaemia 1 (PDF p3, printed 851).
- **Design**: two consecutive periods, not randomised.
  - January 1993 to December 1994: amikacin 7.5 mg/kg **b.i.d.** with piperacillin 4 g t.i.d. (n = 29).
  - January to December 1995: amikacin 20 mg/kg **o.d.** with piperacillin 4 g plus tazobactam 0.5 g t.i.d.
    (n = 28).
  - Amikacin was given into a peripheral vein over 30 min.
- **Demographics** (Table 1, PDF p2, printed 850):

| Group | n | Wt mean (SD) | Wt median (range) | Age mean (SD) | Age median (range) | M/F | CLCR mean (SD) | CLCR median (range) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| o.d. | 28 | 65.8 (13.0) kg | 64 (44-95) | 50.2 (16.8) y | 51 (19-85) | 16/12 | 104 (39) mL/min | 107 (25-213) |
| b.i.d. | 29 | 68.1 (13.1) kg | 66 (49-106) | 51.3 (16.0) y | 50 (18-74) | 19/10 | 91 (36) mL/min | 101 (20-150) |

  Combined, weighting by n: 57 patients, **mean weight 66.97 kg, mean age 50.76 y, 35 men and 22 women**
  (derived from Table 1). **No height is reported.** CLCR is Cockcroft-Gault. The b.i.d. group's mean CLCR is
  12.5% below the o.d. group's but the difference is not significant, and the medians differ by 6%
  (PDF p3, printed 851; both figures reproduce from Table 1, derived).
- **Sampling**: 278 serum samples, of which 93 peak, 117 trough and 68 intermediate; 1 to 14 per patient,
  median 4, over 1 to 47 administrations. Drawn from the central venous catheter at 1 h (the peak, i.e. 0.5 h
  after the end of the infusion), 12 h or 24 h (trough) after the start of the first infusion, then every
  3 days; most patients also gave samples at 2 and 8 h in the first dosing interval (PDF p2, printed 850).
- **Assay**: enzyme-multiplied immunoassay (Cobas, Roche). Limit of quantification 2.5 mg/L, precision better
  than 6% over the 2.5 to 50 mg/L calibration range; samples above 50 mg/L were diluted. Values below the
  limit were recorded as measured, neither zeroed nor dropped. Total serum amikacin.
- **Dosing target during the study**: 1-h peak above 40 mg/L and 24-h trough below 5 mg/L.
- **Model and software**: open two-compartment model with zero-order input, NONMEM IV, first-order conditional
  estimation with eta-epsilon interaction. Interindividual variability log-normal. Residual error is the
  power-variance model of the Methods, C = Chat + eps x Chat^b, with b estimated; the retained model is
  Table 3's **step 19** (the same clearance model with the FOCE eta-epsilon interaction method, OBFV 1,248,
  comment sigma^2 eps = 0.189, which is Table 4's estimate). Step 20 added a second, additive residual term
  (C = Chat + eps1 Chat^b + eps2 with var(eps2) fixed to 0.25), reached OBFV 1,258 and is marked **"Not
  better than step 19"**, so it was tried and **not** retained (Table 3, PDF p3, printed 851, read on the
  rendered page). Simulations with the authors' POPSIM; covariate regressions in SPSS 6.1.

### Observed concentrations (Table 2, PDF p3, printed 851)

| Dosage | Time | n samples | Mean | Median | SD | Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 mg/kg o.d., first day | 1-h peak | 23 | 75.4 | 66.7 | 28.9 | 43-170 mg/L |
| 20 mg/kg o.d., first day | 24-h trough | 24 | 2.1 | 1.0 | 2.8 | 0.5-14.5 mg/L |
| 7.5 mg/kg b.i.d., first day | 1-h peak | 23 | 36.0 | 30.4 | 14.8 | 19.4-66.0 mg/L |
| 7.5 mg/kg b.i.d., first day | 12-h trough | 29 | 4.7 | 3.8 | 3.9 | 1.0-17.0 mg/L |

Over all administrations the o.d. peaks were mean 74.1, median 75.5 (n = 43) and the b.i.d. peaks mean 39.3,
median 36.6 (n = 50). The dose-normalised mean peaks of the two groups differed significantly (P below
0.0001), but the population model found no difference in any parameter between the regimens, so the authors
conclude the kinetics are **linear from 7.5 to 20 mg/kg** (Table 3 steps 7-14; PDF p4, printed 852).

Interindividual variability is log-normal on each of CL, V1, CLD and Vt; allowing covariance between the etas
did not improve the fit. The % IIV is a coefficient of variation.

**Clearance.** The paper gives two forms, and the second supersedes the first.

1. *First form* (PDF p3, printed 851), with CLCR the Cockcroft-Gault creatinine clearance in L/h:
   `CL = a x CLCR + b`, with **a = 0.367 and b = 1.40**.
2. *Final form* (PDF p4, printed 852), after a significant reduction in the objective function value was
   obtained by replacing the estimated CLCR with its own covariates (sex, age, body weight and serum
   creatinine) in a formula the authors call similar to Cockcroft and Gault's:

   **CL = theta_i x 6 x (theta3 - age/100) x bw/SCR + theta4**, i = 1 for men and 2 for women.

   Units: age in years, bw in kg, **SCR in umol/L**, CL in L/h. The umol/L reading comes from the
   simulation's own covariate distribution, a serum creatinine log-normally distributed with mean and SD of
   85 and 37 umol (PDF p5, printed 853), and from Table 6's footnote c. Removing sex from this model gave a
   significantly poorer fit although theta1 and theta2 are close.

### Derived values by simulation (Table 5, PDF p6, printed 854)

1,000 simulated individuals with age 51 +/- 16 y (normal), body weight 67 +/- 13 kg (normal) and
SCR 85 +/- 37 umol/L (log-normal).

| Value | CL men | CL women | t1/2beta men | t1/2beta women | Vss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean | 3.82 L/h | 3.40 L/h | 5.6 h | 6.0 h | 20.6 L |
| SD | 1.52 | 1.28 | 2.0 | 2.2 | 3.4 |
| Median | 3.60 | 3.25 | 5.2 | 5.6 | 20.1 |
| 5th and 95th percentiles | 2.0-6.5 | 1.8-5.7 | 2.9-9.2 | 3.2-10.0 | 15.8-26.4 |

Three internal checks (all derived):
- V1 + Vt = 20.32 L, against the simulated mean Vss of 20.6 L.
- Evaluating the final clearance equation at age 51, bw 67 kg and the **median** of that log-normal serum
  creatinine (85 / sqrt(1 + (37/85)^2) = 77.9 umol/L) gives **3.61 L/h for men and 3.23 for women**, against
  Table 5's medians of 3.60 and 3.25. This is what confirms that theta1 belongs to men and that theta4 is the
  1.66 L/h intercept, which the text layer alone would have got wrong.
- The sex term acts on the renal part only, so theta2/theta1 = 0.803 becomes an 11% lower total clearance in
  women (3.40 against 3.82), which is the roughly 10% the authors report on PDF p5, printed 853.

### Proposed dosing (Table 7, PDF p7, printed 855)

Chosen to give a first 1-h peak above 60 mg/L in 90% of patients and a steady-state predose trough below
5 mg/L in 95%.

| CLCR (mL/min) | Dose (mg/kg) | Interval (h) |
|---|---|---|
| 80-130 | 20 | 24 |
| 60-80 | 22 | 36 |
| 40-60 | 20 | 36 |
| 20-40 | 20 | 48 |
| 10-20 | 17 | 48 |

The 60 mg/L target is raised from the 40 mg/L used during the study: the authors argue that a peak/MIC ratio
above 6 is wanted, that the MIC90 for susceptible strains is below 8 mg/L, and that neutropenic patients need
higher peaks than non-neutropenic ones (PDF p7, printed 855).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
**None.** This is a pharmacokinetic study only: no effect compartment, no ke0, no tPeak, no
concentration-effect model. The concentration targets above are clinical breakpoints taken from other
people's outcome studies (Beaucaire 1991 for the peak, Ter Braak for netilmicin troughs), not fitted here.
No MIC or MBC is measured.

## Covariates
- **Clearance**: sex, age, body weight and serum creatinine, through the final equation above. The authors
  first used estimated Cockcroft-Gault CLCR and then replaced it by its own covariates, which fitted better.
  CLCR explains 57% of the variability in amikacin clearance; 21% interindividual variability remains.
- **Dosing regimen** (o.d. against b.i.d.) was tested on each of CL, V1, CLD and Vt and on the etas
  (Table 3 steps 7-15) and was **not** significant: the kinetics are linear from 7.5 to 20 mg/kg.
- **Length of therapy** was tested as a hyperbolic rise with time, on Vt (Table 3 step 15) and on V1 (step 16),
  and was not significant; the parameters do not change during treatment.
- **Body weight on V1 and Vt** was tested (Table 3 step 17: V1 = theta5 x (bw/65) + theta6; step 18:
  Vt = theta7 (bw/65) + theta8) and neither was significant; the volumes are fixed values.
- Leukocyte and neutrophil counts, and serum albumin, were recorded and did **not** correlate with any
  parameter.

## Caveats
- The authors' own: the two dosing groups are consecutive periods, not randomised, and had different
  beta-lactam partners (piperacillin against piperacillin-tazobactam).
- Their clearance is about **half** that of the earlier amikacin studies and the volume similar, so the
  half-life is nearly doubled. The comparison is against the six studies in Table 6 (PDF p6, printed 854):
  four in neutropenic patients given 7.5 mg/kg b.i.d. (CL 5.7 to 9.2 L/h, Vss 0.38 to 0.45 L/kg, t1/2beta
  2.1 to 3.8 h) and two in healthy volunteers (CL 6.7 and 7.6 L/h, Vss 0.34 and 0.30 L/kg, t1/2beta 2.3 and
  1.9 h), against this study's 3.4 to 3.8 L/h and 5.6 to 6.0 h. **Name the baseline when quoting the lower
  clearance: it is those studies, not an untreated or non-neutropenic norm.** The authors attribute much of
  the gap to method, the earlier studies having mostly fitted one compartment to a peak and a trough, or
  sampled only to 8 h, either of which shortens a terminal half-life and cannot lengthen one.
- A high **proportion (40%)** of amikacin clearance was not associated with CLCR in this cohort (PDF p6,
  printed 854), which the authors call surprising for a drug cleared almost entirely by filtration; they offer the imprecision of Cockcroft-Gault, tubular
  secretion of creatinine, and day-to-day variation in CLCR (one creatinine a day) as explanations.
- Sparse sampling: a median of 4 samples per patient, and only three observed concentrations above
  150 mg/L, which is where the authors note the predicted-against-observed plot may depart from the identity
  line.
- No height is reported, so the cohort has no BMI and no BSA.
- Serum creatinine is in umol/L throughout, never mg/dL; any model coding the final equation must convert.
