# Karara 1999: food effect on nateglinide pharmacokinetics and insulin response

## Study
Open-label, randomised, single-dose, four-period crossover (4 x 4 replicated Latin square). 13 healthy men entered,
12 completed (one withdrawn for rising liver function tests); age 20-38 y (mean 32.6), weight 74.5 +/- 11.4 kg
(53.6-91), height 172.6 +/- 6.3 cm; 38% African American, 31% Caucasian, 23% Hispanic, 8% Oriental (Table I and
text, PDF pp 3-4; demographic table read from the text layer). Treatments: 30 mg and 60 mg film-coated tablets
fasted; 60 mg 10 min before and immediately after a standard high-fat FDA breakfast. Fasted arms received IV D5W 200
mL/h from 30 min before to 4 h after dosing to prevent hypoglycaemia. Plasma at 0-10 h (13 samples); urine to 24 h
after the 60 mg fasted and fed arms. Assay: HPLC-UV 210 nm, LLOQ 50 ng/mL (plasma and urine). Non-compartmental
analysis (linear trapezoid, t1/2 = 0.693/beta). Insulin by ELISA (IMx), glucose by glucose oxidase (YSI 2700).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Non-compartmental values, mean +/- SD, n = 12.

Table III (urine, 60 mg; PDF p 5, printed 176): % dose unchanged 0-10 h 12.5 +/- 2.07 fasted, 13.1 +/- 3.02 fed;
0-24 h 13.0 +/- 2.07 and 14.0 +/- 3.41 (range 7.64-20.4%); CLR 20.7 +/- 5.47 and 21.1 +/- 5.06 mL/min.

Table IV (fasted, dose-normalised; PDF p 5): AUC/dose 0.117 +/- 0.029 (30 mg) and 0.115 +/- 0.033 (60 mg)
ug.h/mL/mg; Cmax/dose 0.050 +/- 0.025 and 0.051 +/- 0.027 ug/mL/mg; tmax 1.69 +/- 1.07 and 1.91 +/- 1.41 h; t1/2
1.38 +/- 0.38 and 1.44 +/- 0.42 h. Dose proportional, differences < 13% and not significant.

Background (introduction, from unpublished data): absolute oral bioavailability about 72%; metabolised by CYP3A4 and
CYP2C9; 99% plasma protein bound (discussion).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points: plasma immunoreactive insulin and glucose (Figs 4-5). Fasted 30 mg: insulin up +6.0 +/- 2.5 uU/mL at 45
min; 60 mg: raised from 20 to 60 min, +17.3 +/- 4.1 uU/mL at 30 min. With 60 mg fasted, incremental insulin AUC
correlated with nateglinide AUC (r² 0.528) and peak insulin with Cmax (r² 0.664). With a meal, insulin AUC(0-210 min)
5160 (before meal) and 4920 uU.min/mL (after); peak 103 +/- 18 vs 70 +/- 10 uU/mL (p < 0.09). Dosing before the meal
abolished the meal glucose excursion. No EC50, Emax, ke0 or concentration-effect model. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Food timing only (above). Dose (30 vs 60 mg) had no effect on dose-normalised parameters.

## Caveats
- Healthy young men, n = 12, single doses, fasted arms under IV glucose.
- Secondary plasma peak in 9 of 12 fasted subjects, which inflates fasted tmax variability.
- Internal inconsistencies: text fasting AUC 3.11 and 6.44 ug.h/mL vs Table IV (0.117 x 30 = 3.51) and Table II
  (6.93); discussion gives CLR 20.9 mL/min against the table's 20.7 and 21.1 (perhaps pooled). See open question.
- Absolute bioavailability (72%) and binding (99%) are stated without data here.
