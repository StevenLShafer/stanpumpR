# Bilbao-Meseguer 2021: levetiracetam in critically ill patients with normal or augmented renal clearance

## Study
Prospective, open-label, two ICUs in Spain (Araba, Doce de Octubre), 2019-2020. 27 critically ill adults
with **measured urinary CrCl > 50 mL/min** (no renal impairment). Table 1 (median, range; reassembled
from the garbled layer): 18 male / 9 female; ARC (CrCl > 130) 10 (37%); haemorrhagic stroke 10, trauma
8, other 9; age 60 y (23-81), weight 80 kg (58-115), height 168 cm (148-189), BSA 1.9 m2, APACHE II 18
(5-35), urinary CrCl 117 mL/min (54-239), albumin 3.4 g/dL. Levetiracetam 500, 1000 or 1500 mg q12h
as 30-min IV infusion (18/27 on 500 mg q12h). Samples pre-dose, 0.5 h, 1-2, 3-5, 6-8 and 12 h; 158
samples (median 6/patient). HPLC-UV, linear 2-100 mg/L. NONMEM 7.4, FOCE-I; bootstrap 2000; pcVPC.

## Model and parameters
Two compartments, linear; IIV on CL and V1; proportional residual error. Table 3, final model (RSE %):

Clearance: **CL (L/h) = 3.5 + (CrCl/120)^2.5** with CrCl in mL/min (urinary). The printed equation is
lost in the extracted text; the power form is confirmed by the Discussion's figures (derived: 3.5 + 1 =
4.5 L/h at 120 mL/min and 3.5 + 2^2.5 = 9.2 L/h at 240 mL/min, both stated; 4.1 L/h at 100 mL/min, also
stated). A linear form (3.5 + 2.5 x CrCl/120) would give 6.0 L/h at 120 and does not fit. Base-model CL
4.6 L/h. Total V 54.9 L (stated). No weight term on any parameter.

NCA (Table 2, mean (SD)): no-ARC CL 4.28 (1.40) L/h, t1/2 8.86 (6.13) h, Vz 54.41 L; ARC CL 6.51 (2.65)
L/h, t1/2 7.25 (4.11) h, Vz 61.09 L.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured. Targets used for simulation: ILAE trough 12-46 mg/L, plus a lower > 6 mg/L target.
Probability of target attainment (Tables 4-5): for trough > 12 mg/L, q12h needs 1500 mg (CrCl 80) and
2000 mg (CrCl 120); q8h 1500 mg (CrCl 160), 2000 mg (CrCl 200); CrCl 240 not reached even with 2000 mg
q8h (59%, 67% with a 2-h infusion).

## Covariates
Urinary CrCl on CL only (power term above an intercept). ARC (categorical) also significant but less
IIV reduction; trauma and APACHE II on V1 dropped at backward elimination. Sex, age, height, albumin,
labs tested.

## Caveats
n = 27; no external validation. Only CrCl > 50 mL/min, so the model says nothing about renal
impairment. Measured urinary CrCl, not Cockcroft-Gault. Large CrCl-independent intercept (3.5 L/h,
78% of CL at 120 mL/min). The "p > 0.05" for ARC differences in the NCA text is presumably a typo
for < 0.05.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Citation details (code 58-59): **matches**.
- Claim (code 24-26) that this model shows a **proportional** fall in clearance with renal function:
  **discrepancy** in support, not in numbers. The paper's model is CL = 3.5 + (CrCl/120)^2.5 L/h,
  fitted only above CrCl 50 mL/min: a large fixed intercept with a steep power term, so CL falls only
  from 4.5 to 3.68 L/h (derived) when CrCl halves from 120 to 60 mL/min, far less than proportional;
  the dependence is mainly an increase above 120 mL/min (ARC). The paper does show CL rising with
  CrCl, so it supports "clearance depends on renal function", but not proportionality or the 0.66/0.34
  split, which come from the label/mass-balance (66% unchanged in urine, 24% hydrolysis, as this
  paper's Introduction also states). The code's own wording ("without importing an intensive-care
  intercept") acknowledges the intercept; suggest rewording "proportional" for this citation.
- Not this paper, noted in passing: R/drugs_levetiracetam.R:32-33 says the reference patient's
  Cockcroft-Gault clearance is 107 mL/min; with SCR_ASSUMED_MALE 1.0 the code computes (140-40) x 70 /
  72 = 97.2 mL/min (crclRef, line 86). The code is self-consistent (it normalises to its own crclRef),
  so only the comment is wrong. Same stale 107 appears in R/drugs_lacosamide.R:37.
