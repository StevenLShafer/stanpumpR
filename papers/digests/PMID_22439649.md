# McLeay 2012: drug clearance and body size, systematic review and meta-analysis

## Study
Systematic review of population PK papers 2000-2007 (MEDLINE search Sept 2008; human, English). 2384 articles identified, 458 included, 484 studies, 487 CL models (Fig. 2). Excluded: monoclonal antibodies, simultaneous parent-metabolite models, PBPK. Data abstraction in Access; analysis in Excel and R 2.8.0. Meta-analysis on 121 CL models that included adults and reported N, mean and SD of total body weight (TBW): each study's CL computed across its TBW range at 1 kg steps, normalised, binned every 10 kg and weighted by N; candidate fixed and estimated size relationships compared by mean error and RMSE for TBW > 40 kg. LBW = Janmahasatian 2005 (i.e. fat-free mass), IBW = Devine, BSA = Du Bois, typical male height 1.75 m.

## Model and parameters
Descriptive (p. 323-324): body size screened in 95.7% of studies; included in 58.3% of those that screened it; 56% of all CL models contained size; 52% of those were nonlinear in TBW (allometric TBW estimated exponent 39.2%, fixed 0.75 26.4%, BSA 22.2%, linear LBW 4.2%). Estimated TBW exponents ranged -1.17 to 2.17, median 0.65 (Fig. 6, 56 studies). **No single size descriptor was more successful than the others** (abstract; Fig. 5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
The subject of the paper: LBW with an exponent of about 2/3 best described the average CL-TBW relationship (abstract; Discussion 4.1), uniting the LBW hypothesis and allometry; the authors question Kleiber's 3/4 for drug CL. Recommend Janmahasatian over James LBW equations, and standardising covariates to a 70 kg, LBW 55 kg, 1.75 m male (Conclusion). Wider TBW range in a study raised the chance of finding a nonlinear CL-TBW relation (Fig. 8).

## Caveats
Meta-analysis of published model equations, not raw data; uniform weighting over each study's weight range; 2000-2007 literature only; Janmahasatian LBW unvalidated in children so pediatric scaling not assessed; the difference between exponents 2/3 and 3/4 on LBW is small (RMSE 0.323 vs 0.325).
