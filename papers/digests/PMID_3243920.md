# Liem 1988: time course of procainamide's QRS effect in ventricular tachyarrhythmias

## Study
- **Population:** 9 patients (4 men, 5 women), age 52 ± 14 y, undergoing electropharmacologic study (Table I,
  p985).
  - Arrhythmias: sustained VT, non-sustained VT or VF.
  - Heart disease: CAD in 5, LVH in 2, MVP in 1, none in 1.
  - Excluded: LVEF below 25%, NYHA III-IV, acute MI, and significant liver or renal disease.
  - Concurrent drugs included digoxin, diltiazem, β-blockers and diuretics.
- **Dosing:** procainamide by constant infusion through a femoral venous catheter (p985).
  - All patients: 0.27 ± 0.05 mg/kg/min (range 0.22-0.33) for 55.6 ± 5.8 min, total 15.5 ± 4.4 mg/kg (9.92-19.81).
  - Four patients: 0.22 mg/kg/min, total 11.0 ± 0.88 mg/kg.
  - Five patients: 0.30-0.33 mg/kg/min, total 19.1 ± 0.96 mg/kg.
  - Weights are not reported.
- **Effect end point:** QRS duration at a paced cycle length of 500 ms (pulse width 2 ms, twice diastolic
  threshold), averaged over 5 complexes (p985).
- **Recording times:** every 5 min during the infusion, every 2 min for 10 min, every 10 min for 50 min, then
  every 30 min for 180 min.
- **Plasma:** jugular venous samples drawn with each ECG, assayed by HPLC. No NAPA was measurable (p989).
- **Analysis:** linear regression of QRS on concentration. For the three patients with hysteresis, the paper
  instead fitted a linear-effect pharmacodynamic model after Sheiner 1979 (pp986-987).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model is fitted. The text says only that the decline looked biexponential (p986).

**Internal inconsistencies** (all confirmed on the rendered pages):
- **SD of the slope:** the abstract gives 3.2 ± 1.1, the text and Table III 3.2 ± 1.6. The six tabled slopes give
  SD 1.04 (derived), so the abstract's 1.1 agrees with the data and 1.6 does not.
- **Lower-dose maximum effect:** printed "19.8 ± 40.0%" (p986). The four values (22, 24, 15, 18) give 19.8 ± 4.0
  (derived), so 40.0 is a typo.
- **Patient 7's infusion:** the text calls it a "17.9 mg/kg/min infusion" (p986). Table II gives 0.30 mg/kg/min and
  a total of 17.99 mg/kg.
- **Hysteresis slopes:** the text (p987) gives the slopes for patients 1, 2 and 7 as 6.7, 1.0 and 1.4. Table III
  gives 1.0, 6.7 and 1.5. The order of the first two is swapped and the third differs. Fig. 3 (p988) shows patient
  2 with a steep relation (QRS 170 to 210 ms over about 6 µg/mL) and patient 1 with a shallow one (180 to 220 ms
  over about 17 µg/mL), which supports Table III.
- **Order of the delays:** the abstract lists 3, 10 and 18 min. The Results (p986) give 3, 18 and 10 min for
  patients 1, 2 and 7.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **End point:** paced QRS duration, as a percentage of baseline or in ms.
- **No ke0 or t1/2ke0 is reported**, although a linear-effect model of the Sheiner type was fitted to patients 1,
  2 and 7.
- **Timing:** in 6 of 9 patients the peak effect fell within 2 min of the end of the infusion. In patients 1, 2 and
  7 it came 3, 18 and 10 min after the end, with counterclockwise hysteresis (Fig. 3).
- **Earlier delays quoted** (refs 1-2, both in 4 healthy volunteers): 7 min on QT (Galeazzi), 10 min on QS2I
  (Boudoulas).
- The authors advise taking electropharmacologic measurements at the peak level and again 5-10 min later
  (p989).
- No toxicity was seen. The paper gives no therapeutic or toxic concentration.

## Covariates
None analysed. Two dose groups only.

## Caveats
- 9 patients, a single infusion each, and no PK model. QRS widening may not track the antiarrhythmic effect, an
  assumption the authors name (p989).
- The cause of the delay in 3 patients is unknown. No NAPA was detected.
- The abstract and the text disagree on the slope SD, the hysteresis slopes and the order of the delays (above).
