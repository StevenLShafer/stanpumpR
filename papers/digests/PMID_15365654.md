# Cavalli 2004: lidocaine and MEGX after perineal (pudendal) block in parturients

## Study
- 23 healthy term parturients with singleton vaginal delivery, Ribeirão Preto, Brazil. Table 1 (PDF p3, medians (P25-P75)):
  age 23.0 (21.5-27.5) y, gestational age 278.0 (271.5-284.5) days, weight 68.5 (61.0-76.5) kg, height 1.61 m,
  BMI 26.4 kg/m2.
- Drug: 20 mL 2% lidocaine hydrochloride (400 mg) without vasoconstrictor, perineal injection for pudendal nerve
  block in the second stage of labour (PDF p2). 14 of the 23 also had epidural bupivacaine and fentanyl through a
  catheter during labour; the authors say PK did not differ and pooled them.
- Maternal venous plasma at 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 60, 90, 120, 240, 360 min; umbilical venous plasma at delivery.
- Assay: HPLC-UV (205 nm); calibration 0.5-5 µg/mL lidocaine, 8-80 ng/mL MEGX (PDF p2). Total (bound + free)
  plasma concentrations; protein binding not measured.
- Analysis: Kinetica 4.0; lidocaine "bicompartmental" fit, MEGX one-compartment; AUC by trapezoids plus Ct/β;
  CL/F = dose/AUC0-∞, Vd/F = CL/β (i.e. Varea/F). Results are medians of individual estimates.

## Model and parameters
Table 2 (PDF p4, lidocaine, median (P25-P75)): Cmax 3.22 (2.47-3.85) µg/mL; tmax 15.0 (10.0-20.0) min;
t½α 24.0 (20.0-30.0) min; t½β 180.0 (150.0-195.0) min; AUC0-∞ 460.2 (407.9-498.7) µg·min/mL;
CL/F 12.2 (11.1-14.5) mL/min/kg; Vd/F 3.1 (2.8-3.6) L/kg.
(The text layer misaligns Table 2 by one row; the rendered page gives the values above.)
Table 3 (PDF p4, MEGX): Cmax 229.0 (187.0-270.0) ng/mL; tmax 90.0 (90.0-90.0) min; t½ 240.0 (185.0-270.0) min;
AUC0-∞ 82.4 (61.1-90.8) µg·min/mL.
Table 4 (placental transfer at delivery; latency 11.0 (8.0-15.5) min): lidocaine maternal 2.7 (2.2-3.1), fetal
1.3 (0.8-1.6) µg/mL, F/M 0.46 (0.32-0.59); MEGX maternal 39.0 (26.0-95.0), fetal 67.9 (38.0-96.0) ng/mL,
F/M 1.07 (0.78-1.60).
Derived check: 400 mg / 460.2 µg·min/mL = 0.87 L/min = 12.7 mL/min/kg at 68.5 kg (derived: medians, so consistent
with the reported 12.2); 12.2 × 180 / 0.693 = 3.17 L/kg (derived: consistent with Vd/F 3.1).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, ke0 or effect concentration. Toxic thresholds are quoted from other sources only (PDF p4): toxicity
signs from 5 µg/mL (ref 16), occasional toxicity 6-10 and frequent above 10 µg/mL (ref 14); two of 23 women peaked
above 5.0 µg/mL. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None analysed. No non-pregnant control group; comparisons with epidural or IV studies are to other papers'
populations (Ramanathan 1986, Downing 1997, Nattel 1987), not to this study.

## Caveats
- All clearance and volume values are apparent (perineal absorption, bioavailability unknown, as the authors say).
- Sampling only to 360 min with t½β 180 min; absorption may still be ongoing (flip-flop not excluded).
- 14/23 also received epidural bupivacaine/fentanyl; pooled.
- Medians only; no IIV. Total plasma concentrations; no binding measured.
- The discussion (PDF p5) attributes Vd/F 0.98 L/kg and CL/F 6.1 mL/min/kg after 420 mg peridural lidocaine to its
  ref 13 (Philipson 1984), which is a perineal-infiltration paper; the attribution looks garbled in the source.
