# Shafer 2025: plasma PK of IV and intranasal oxytocin (LC/MS)

## Study
Two single-centre studies (Wake Forest) in healthy ASA 1-2 adults, BMI <40, venous EDTA plasma on ice, assayed by LC/MS (limit 2 pg/mL, 350 uL aliquot) (PDF page 3). Duplicate Study 1 samples also assayed by ELISA (Enzo ADI-900-153A, limit 15 pg/mL, intra-assay CV 11.6%).
- Study 1 (n = 11, May-July 2019): 16.7 mcg IV over 60 s (first 3 subjects) or 10 min (other 8), sampled to 240 min after the infusion. Mean age 38 (22-58) y, 55% female, weight 86 (SD 11) kg, height 173 (SD 6.5) cm (PDF page 3).
- Study 2 (n = 24, 2023, crossover, two visits >=5 days apart): 13.7 mcg IV over 30 min, sampled to 90 min; separate day 100 mcg intranasal (TNX-1900, 25 mcg per spray, one spray in each nostril at 0 and again at 5 min), sampled to 60 min. Age 40 (21-68) y, 50% female, weight 82 (SD 10) kg, height 171 (SD 7.9) cm, max BMI 33.6, five class 1 obese, all White non-Hispanic.
- Whether Study 1 and Study 2 subjects are distinct people is not stated.
- NONMEM 7.5, first-order conditional estimation, lognormal IIV, additive+proportional residual. Intranasal fit: volumes/clearances taken from each subject's IV fit, bioavailability estimated first, then fixed at the population value while ka and disposition re-estimated (ADVAN4). Six first intranasal samples below quantitation imputed at 1 pg/mL. Bootstrap 3000 iterations; jackknife.

Doses are in micrograms (the text layer prints the micro sign as "m"). Concentrations in ng/mL (Fig 1).
Fit quality (PDF pages 4-5, Fig 2): IV bias 0%, median inaccuracy (MDAPE) 18%, the same out of sample by jackknife; no-covariate model 22% / 0%. Intranasal bias +9%, MDAPE 47%. Intranasal volumes and ka had non-parabolic likelihood profiles (poorly identified, Fig 4); no covariate improved the intranasal model.
Concentrations after 100 mcg intranasal were about an order of magnitude below those after 16.7 mcg IV (Fig 1).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, EC50 or effect measure is reported (sensory testing was done in the studies but no PD results are given; the paper cites Minto 2003 only as a method for tPeak). No MEAC or therapeutic concentration. Not an opioid; no equipotency ratio.
Assay comparison (Study 1, Fig 5, PDF page 8): ELISA read about 40% lower than simultaneous LC/MS and lost quantifiable signal about 1 h after dosing whereas LC/MS measured over 2 h. Cause (standard curves vs metabolite cross-reactivity) not determined.

## Covariates
Model D: weight (all volumes and clearances proportional to Wt/80) and age on CL1 only. Age, weight, height, BMI, sex tested (supplement). The text says age, weight and BMI improved the IV fit "modestly"; none improved the intranasal fit. The simulator on the web used Model G of the supplement (PDF page 8) rather than Model D.

## Caveats
Single centre; no severe obesity; no race/ethnic diversity; not representative of parturients (the authors cite near-undetectable levels in third-stage labour, and faster oxytocin metabolism in the third trimester, citing Oliver 2023). Intranasal fit is inaccurate (MDAPE 47%) with high IIV. IV Study 2 sampling stopped at 90 min, Study 1 at 240 min. The intranasal bioavailability (0.66%) is compared by the authors with 11% from Martins et al., who used ELISA; their own comparison with Gajewska-Knapik (inhaled/IM) is derived from the paper's model: 8.4 mcg IV over 30 s or 5 min peaked at 1000 and 700 pg/mL in that study vs 900 and 680 simulated here.

## Used by stanpumpR
Context file lists no locations (the archive found no citation by stem), but `R/drugs_oxytocin.R` cites it by title/PMID: IV model (Model D, Table 1) is the library's human oxytocin model; intranasal not offered; `inst/app-help/drugs/oxytocin.md` repeats it. Unit potency 600 U/mg: the paper gives the injection's strength as 16.7 mcg/mL (PDF page 2) and does not state its units; 10 units/mL is the US labels' standardisation (Pitocin, NDA 018261, papers/labels/), and 16.7 mcg = 10 U is 600 U/mg. The paper's own "100 mcg (60 IU)" (Introduction) is its report of another group's study (ref 17), consistent with the same 600 U/mg, not a statement about its own injection.
