# Waldmeier 1991: 14C-benazepril mass balance in healthy men

## Study
Ciba-Geigy study run at Clinical Pharmacology Associates, Miami. 4 healthy white men, mean 29 y (24-33), 82 kg (71-89).
[14C]benazepril HCl 20 mg (49 uCi, 43.4 umol) orally in gelatin capsules after an overnight fast on day 1; unlabelled
20 mg once daily days 5-9; a second labelled 20 mg dose on day 10 (presumed steady state). Plasma 0.25-96 h; urine and
faeces collected quantitatively days 1-13. Total 14C by liquid scintillation; benazepril and benazeprilat by GC-MS
(Kaiser 1987; LOQ in plasma 0.03 and 0.05 nmol/g); urinary metabolite pattern by HPLC-radiometry on 0-48 h pools
(semiquantitative). Non-compartmental: trapezoidal AUC, Ae, CLR = Ae/AUC. Protein binding by equilibrium dialysis.
Concentrations are in **nmol/g** of plasma (molar, mass of plasma).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Table 1 (PDF p.7, printed 256; rendered, rotated table), means +/- SD, n = 4 unless noted:

The abstract prints total-14C AUC as 9.7 +/- 1.1 (nmol/g)h, Table 1 9.65 +/- 1.12 (rounding).

Excretion, Table 2 (PDF p.8, printed 257), % of dose after the first dose (n = 4): urine 0-8 h 29.1 +/- 5.0; urine
0-216 h 37.0 +/- 6.0; faeces 0-216 h 59.8 +/- 6.4; total 96.8 +/- 0.5. Second dose (n = 3, 0-96 h): urine 31.1 +/- 3.9,
faeces 59.8 +/- 7.5, total 90.8 +/- 5.5.

Half-lives (text, p.254, n = 4): total 14C apparent alpha t1/2 2.7 h, terminal "of the order of" 50 h (24-96 h);
benazeprilat alpha 3.2 h, terminal about 20 h (24-72 h; limit of detection reached at 72-96 h); benazepril 0.7 h.
Repeated dosing: benazepril Cmax and AUC fell 38% and 21% (P < 0.025); benazeprilat and 14C unchanged.

Urinary metabolites (0-48 h pool, HPLC, semiquantitative; p.257): benazeprilat about 56% of urinary radioactivity (20% of
dose); metabolite A (benazepril acyl glucuronide, tentative) about 11% (4% of dose); B (benazeprilat acyl glucuronide)
about 22% (8% of dose); benazepril about 1% (0.4%). Protein binding (p.258): benazepril 97% (2 umol/L), benazeprilat 95%
(0.2-20 umol/L); to albumin 93% and 94%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. No ACE inhibition or blood pressure measured; no ke0, EC50 or therapeutic range. The Discussion
estimates that ACE binding capacity (600-900 nmol, from other papers) is 5-8% of the at least 7400 nmol of benazeprilat
systemically available, so saturable binding should contribute little (p.260).

## Covariates
None (4 men).

## Caveats
- n = 4 men, one dose (20 mg); faeces missing for one subject after the second dose.
- Absorption is bounded only from below (37%); biliary excretion in man is unquantified here.
- Metabolite percentages are semiquantitative (incomplete HPLC separation); A and B identified tentatively as acyl
  glucuronides, which hydrolyse at pH >= 9.
- Concentrations per gram of plasma.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **37% as the upper bound of fm (R/drugs_benazepril.R:83-84). discrepancy (wording).** The paper's 37% is total
  urinary 14C, and it calls it the minimum absorption; with 59.8% in faeces and reported biliary benazeprilat excretion,
  absorption and hence fm can exceed 37%. The conclusion the code draws (fm too poorly bounded to use; parameters
  apparent in fm) stands and is if anything stronger. The ~25% lower bound is not stated in the paper; it is reproducible
  as urinary benazeprilat 17% (GC-MS) or 20% (HPLC) plus benazeprilat glucuronide 8% = 25-28% of dose (derived). See
  open_questions.
- **"A third of the radioactivity leaving as glucuronides" (docs/antihypertensives.md:1520). matches, imprecisely.**
  11% + 22% = 33% of the *urinary* radioactivity in 0-48 h (about 12% of the dose), not of all radioactivity excreted.
  The point it supports (urinary benazeprilat underestimates formation) holds. The "biliary elimination" in the same
  sentence is attributed to a class review, not this paper; this paper cites only unpublished Sioufi data for it.
- **Independent check of the library model (Kaiser 1989 derivation; not cited for this).** Doubling Kaiser's 10 mg
  benazeprilat values to 20 mg (assumes linearity; the header notes accumulation falls with dose): Kaiser AUC0-inf
  3185 pmol/g.h -> 6.37 nmol/g.h against Waldmeier's AUC0-96 4.68 (ratio 0.73); Kaiser Cmax 195 ng/mL = 0.49 nmol/g
  -> 0.98 against 0.84 (0.86); the model's own peak, 156 ng/mL per 10 mg -> 0.79 nmol/g at 20 mg, sits 6% below
  Waldmeier's 0.84 (all derived; nmol/g taken as nmol/mL, MW 396.44). Waldmeier's Tmax 1.0 h (1.5 h at steady state)
  brackets the model's 1.5 h target. Benazepril t1/2 0.7 h vs Kaiser's 0.6 h; benazeprilat alpha 3.2 h vs 2.7 h,
  terminal ~20 h vs 22.3 h. So Waldmeier lies on the LOW side of the model's area by about a quarter, the opposite
  direction to Sun 1994 (1.34x higher); with n = 4 and a 20 mg dose this widens the scale uncertainty the header already
  records rather than changing it.
