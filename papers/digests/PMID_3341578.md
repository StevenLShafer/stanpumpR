# Shafer 1988: Testing computer-controlled infusion pumps by simulation

## Study
Stanford / Palo Alto VA laboratory report. Pump: IMED 929 driven by an Apple II over RS-232; software a highly modified version of the original CACI software (Reves, Alvis; Pascal, UCSD p-system). Three-compartment fentanyl model of McClain and Hug 1980, elimination from the central compartment. Undiluted fentanyl 50 ug/mL into a simulated 70-kg patient for 1 h unless stated.

## Model and parameters
- Differential equations (eqs 1-3) solved by **Euler's method** over T = 15 s (eqs 4-6); Cp = a1/V1 (eq 7).
- Rate rule (eq 8): u[n] = (k1 a1 - k21 a2 - k31 a3) + (Cd[n+1] V1 - a1)/(3T); dividing the shortfall by 3T damped the oscillation seen with T.
- Exact analytic solution (eqs 9-11, adapted from Maitre et al.) used as the reference, recreating the actual infusion record.
- Simulations A-J: hold 2, 5, 10 ng/mL; step 5 -> 10 and 10 -> 5; recover after a 10-min "air-in-line" stop; diluted fentanyl 10 ug/mL; 10-kg child; hold 10 ng/mL for 24 h; 500-ug bolus decline over 24 h; alternate 10/5 ng/mL; original CACI vs revised software alternating 5/10 every 10 min.

Recovery after pump stop within 2.25 min; 5% overshoot then within 3%. Euler error fell to 0.0021 ng/mL (I) and < 0.0001 ng/mL (G, H) at 24 h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None; plasma targeting only. No ke0, no MEAC. (Fentanyl paper, but no PD or analgesic concentrations reported.)

## Covariates
Weight only (70 vs 10 kg).

## Caveats
Simulated patient follows the model exactly; pharmacokinetic variability not addressed. Oscillation attributed to IMED 929 under-delivery and its integer-mL volume reporting. Original CACI results said to be no longer clinically relevant (software since corrected). Addendum: exact-solution analysis software in C for 80286/MS-DOS available free from Shafer.
