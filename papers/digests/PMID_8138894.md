# Karlsson 1993: modeling interoccasion variability (IOV)

## Study
Methods paper (NONMEM IV, FOCE and FO). Simulations: one-compartment IV bolus, CL=10, V=100 (units not given), IIV (omega) 0.1/0.3/0.5, IOV (pi) 0 or 0.3, residual proportional error SD 0.1; 32 subjects x 2 occasions; 30 replicates per condition (p. 738). Three sampling designs: full (4 samples/occasion at 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3 half-lives), sparse balanced, sparse unbalanced. Analyses: no-IOV model, each occasion as a separate subject, full IIV+IOV model. Two real examples: Drug A (broad-spectrum antibiotic, 74 critically ill patients, 1-h infusions twice daily) and Drug B (antihypertensive, 64 patients, first-order input) (pp. 739-740).

## Model and parameters
P_ij = P_typ * exp(eta_i + kappa_ij), with eta (IIV) and kappa (IOV) independent normal, mean 0 (Eq. 1-2, p. 737). In NONMEM, IOV is coded as a separate eta per occasion with a shared variance (SAME block; Appendix, pp. 748-749). No drug PK parameter is reported for use. Tables I and II (pp. 744-745) give Drug A and B estimates with and without IOV; drugs unnamed.

## Caveats
Ignoring IOV inflates residual error and IIV, biases some fixed effects (direction varies with design), and produces spurious period effects (about 30-50+% of data sets; p. 742). With IOV unrecognised, TDM value is overstated (p. 747).
