# Woodhouse 1999: morphine, pethidine, fentanyl PCA, within-patient crossover

## Study
Randomized, double-blind, three-way crossover of IV PCA in 82 post-operative patients (PDF p.1-2, printed 545-546). Age 19-69 yr (mean 47, SD 16), within 15% of ideal body weight, ASA 1-3, normal renal and liver function. Surgery: 35 orthopaedic, 53 lower abdominal, 4 upper abdominal, 10 had two procedures. Each patient received one 60 mL syringe of each opioid in random order, then a fourth syringe of the opioid given first; syringes changed every 8 h over a 32 h trial, with no washout. Syringe contents: morphine sulphate pentahydrate 60 mg, pethidine HCl 600 mg, or fentanyl 0.6 mg (as citrate), each in 60 mL saline, so 1 mL = 1 mg morphine, 10 mg pethidine, 10 mcg fentanyl (derived: 0.6 mg / 60 mL; the text prints "10 mg fentanyl" per mL, which is a misprint for 10 mcg given the syringe content and the stated 0.01 mg ratio). Graseby 1205-0002 pumps, bolus 1 or 1.5 mL (up to 2 mL if needed), lockout 5 min, no background infusion, no maximum dose rate. Outcomes: side-effect questionnaire at each syringe change, patient satisfaction questionnaire, 11-point NRS pain at rest every 2 h, hourly dose and demand counts. No drug concentrations measured.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model. Study-level numbers (all measured, not fitted):
- Equianalgesic dose ratio assumed for the design: 1 mg morphine = 10 mg pethidine = 0.01 mg fentanyl, attributed to Lehmann 1988 (p.2, printed 546).
- Opioid use in morphine-equivalent mg per 8 h syringe, mean +/- SD (Table 3, p.4 printed 548): syringe 1 31.6 +/- 16.9; syringe 2 22.8 +/- 15.5; syringe 3 23.1 +/- 14.5; syringe 4 19.6 +/- 13.6.
- NRS pain (0-10) per syringe, mean +/- SD (Table 3): 4.3 +/- 2.2; 3.1 +/- 1.9; 3.2 +/- 1.9; 3.0 +/- 2.1.
- Morphine-equivalent use by opioid, syringes 1 and 4 combined (text p.4): pethidine 26.8 mg (SD 19.5), morphine 35.4 mg (SD 25.8), fentanyl 35.0 mg (SD 24.4); P = 0.005.
- Mean NRS by opioid (text, printed 549): morphine 3.4 (SD 1.9), pethidine 3.0 (SD 1.7), fentanyl 3.5 (SD 2.0); no significant difference.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- MEAC / minimum effective analgesic concentration: none measured or reported (no assay).
- ke0 / t1/2 ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported.
- Equianalgesic / potency ratio: 1 mg morphine = 10 mg pethidine = 0.01 mg fentanyl, IV, assumed from Lehmann 1988 (p.2 printed 546), not derived here. The observed PCA self-administration (morphine equivalents) was lower for pethidine (26.8 mg) than for morphine (35.4 mg) or fentanyl (35.0 mg), i.e. on these ratios patients took about 24% less pethidine than morphine (derived: 26.8/35.4 = 0.76) with equal pain scores; authors attribute this possibly to pethidine's lower clearance (citing Mather and Woodhouse 1997) or demand behaviour.
- End points: NRS pain at rest, PCA dose, side-effect incidence, satisfaction. Satisfaction correlated inversely with mean NRS pain on each syringe (r = -0.31, -0.26, -0.28, -0.36; page 4-5).

Side effects (Table 1, patients of 82 with the effect on each opioid): nausea 40 morphine / 53 pethidine / 50 fentanyl; vomiting 7 / 12 / 11; pruritus 34 / 36 / 25; drowsiness 11 / 7 / 1; other 13 / 15 / 15. Only sedation differed significantly (fewer with fentanyl, P = 0.02). By syringe order (Table 2): pruritus 16, 20, 28, 31, rising over time (P = 0.03); nausea 37, 39, 35, 32.

## Covariates
None analysed for PK. Surgery type was not considered an important variable by design.

## Caveats
No washout between opioids (authors argue 8 h periods and half-lives of a few hours limit carry-over). Dose-ratio assumed, no concentrations, so the data cannot support a potency ratio. Pain only at rest. Side effects by questionnaire (pruritus may be suggested by repeated questioning, per authors). Patients' retrospective recall of differences between syringes was inaccurate. The text's "10 mg fentanyl" per mL is inconsistent with the syringe content (see Study).
