# Eisenach 2003: cephalad CSF movement of intrathecal morphine and fentanyl

## Study
- 8 healthy volunteers (4 men, 4 women), ASA I-II, age 18-50 y (mean 32 +/- 3.3), height 176 +/- 4.7 cm, weight 79 +/- 4.9 kg (PDF p2, printed p167).
- Single intrathecal injection of fentanyl 50 mcg + morphine 50 mcg in 2 mL saline over 5 s through a No. 27 needle at a lower lumbar interspace (L5-S1 or L4-L5); slightly hypobaric solution.
- A second No. 27 needle at L2-L3; CSF (0.4 mL) sampled from it at 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 45, 60 min (first 4 volunteers) and additionally 75, 90, 105, 120 min (last 4). One sample taken from the caudad (injection) needle at the end. Tip separation measured by x-ray: 8.8 +/- 1.6 cm (Table 2).
- CSF volume (T10 to end of thecal sac) measured by MRI in the first 4 volunteers only: mean 25 +/- 4.0 mL.
- Assay: GC-MS with deuterated internal standards; linear 5-1500 ng/mL; minimum detectable 5 ng/mL (CSF, so neither plasma nor whole blood; total drug).
- Modelling: NONMEM, FOCE, log-normal IIV, constant-CV residual error; individual values are post hoc Bayes estimates.
- No pharmacodynamic measures; safety monitoring only. Two volunteers became drowsy from 45 min, SpO2 never below 95%, RR never below 12/min.

## Model and parameters
Two compartments of identical volume V (caudad = injection site, cephalad = sampling site), exchange rate constant kmix between them, and first-order elimination kel from each compartment (kfen for fentanyl, kmor for morphine), fitted to both drugs and both sites at once (Fig 3, PDF p4). Four parameters; extra mixing, spacer, or distribution compartments did not improve the fit significantly.

Table 2 (PDF p5, printed p170), read from the rendered page:
- kmix (min^-1): individuals 0.010, 0.053, 0.008, 0.001, 0.015, 1.010, 0.007, 0.004; median 0.009; typical 0.469; IIV 430%.
- V (L): 0.119, 0.062, 0.051, 0.043, 0.086, 0.033, 0.071, 0.051; median 0.057; typical 0.033; IIV 43%.
- kfen (min^-1): 0.017, 0.019, 0.020, 0.014, 0.017, 0.022, 0.014, 0.015; median 0.017; typical 0.015; IIV 24%.
- kmor (min^-1): 0.0141, 0.0093, 0.0040, 0.0092, 0.0027, 0.0184, 0.0029, 0.0115; median 0.0092; typical 0.0106; IIV 106%.
- Text states median kfen 0.017 and kmor 0.0092 min^-1 correspond to 40 and 75 min. (derived: ln2/k gives 40.8 and 75.3 min, so these are half-lives, not time constants.)

Observed (not model) CSF variables at the cephalad site (Table 1, PDF p4, printed p169, and text):
- Tmax 41 +/- 13 min fentanyl, 57 +/- 12 min morphine; Cmax 225 +/- 145 ng/mL fentanyl, 275 +/- 77 ng/mL morphine (mean +/- SEM; no difference between drugs). These are observed values, not model-derived.
- Time to first detectable drug: median 4 min (range 2-45) fentanyl, 2 min (range 2-30) morphine.
- Morphine:fentanyl concentration ratio at the cephalad site rose linearly with time: intercept 0.91, slope 0.03 per min, r = 0.67, P < 0.0001; above 2:1 by 36 min and 4:1 by 103 min (Fig 2, text p168).
- Injection-site to cephalad-site gradient at the end of sampling: median 3.1:1 fentanyl, 1.8:1 morphine (P = 0.15); excluding volunteer 4, 2.5 +/- 0.4 and 1.6 +/- 0.3.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic end point was measured, so there is no ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, MEAC or analgesic concentration. The only "peak" is the observed CSF Tmax above (a CSF concentration at a distant site, not an effect). No equianalgesic or potency ratio is given; the same mass (50 mcg) of each drug was injected, and the morphine:fentanyl ratio quoted is a CSF concentration ratio. The discussion infers a ratio of about 1.5:1 at 20 min (peak fentanyl respiratory depression) and about 10:1 at 6 h (peak morphine respiratory depression) by extrapolating the model; this is an inference, not an observation.

Audit: opioid paper; MEAC none; ke0/tPeak none; potency ratio none.

## Covariates
Height, weight, interneedle distance and CSF volume were tested; no rate constant correlated with any of them. V was inversely correlated with measured CSF volume (r^2 = 0.64) in only the 4 volunteers who had MRI; the authors call this unexplained and possibly a small-sample artifact. CSF concentrations did not correlate with weight, height, or lumbosacral CSF volume.

## Caveats
- n = 8, one sampling site per person, short needle separation (about 9 cm), up to 120 min.
- Tmax is the observed maximum and may be early where concentrations were still rising at the last sample; 2 volunteers showed long delays before drug appeared (volunteer 4 had a 33:1 gradient for fentanyl at 60 min).
- Fentanyl and morphine were co-injected, so a drug-drug interaction on CSF kinetics cannot be excluded; the model assumes the same V and kmix for both.
- Caudad morphine was slightly under-predicted (Fig 5). Mixing is extremely variable (IIV 430%) and its physiology is unexplained.
- Table 2's median V (0.057 L) disagrees with the 28 mL stated in the text (see open_questions).
- CSF is not the effect site; the authors note drug concentrations in CSF may not parallel clinical effect.
