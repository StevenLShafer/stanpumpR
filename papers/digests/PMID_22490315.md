# Altermatt 2012: IV lidocaine lowers propofol TIVA requirement (BIS-guided)

## Study
Randomised, blinded, saline-controlled trial; 40 ASA I-II adults for elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy (BMI <=35); 39 analysed (one lidocaine patient excluded for a TCI device fault). Control n=20, lidocaine n=19 (Table 1, p.981): age 36.47 (range 20-60) vs 42.10 (24-62) yr; M/F 5/15 vs 7/12; weight 70.14 (SD 10.78) vs 70.84 (13.77) kg; height 160.85 vs 163.05 cm; BMI 26.98 vs 26.54; ASA I/II 10/10 vs 7/12.
Induction fentanyl 3 mcg/kg; 3 min later propofol by effect-site TCI with the Schnider model (Orchestra Base Primea), initial Ce 4.5-5.0 mcg/mL; atracurium 0.5 mg/kg. Lidocaine 1% 1.5 mg/kg over 5 min then 2 mg/kg/h (control: equal volume saline), started 5 min after intubation, stopped at skin closure (T0). Maintenance: Ce changed by 0.5 mcg/mL to hold BIS 40-60; floor Ce 2.5 mcg/mL; fentanyl 25-50 mcg boluses as needed.
Arterial samples every 10 min; propofol serial levels in 5 random patients per group; end-of-infusion propofol in both groups. Propofol: HPLC, linear 0.1-10 mcg/mL, LOQ 0.1 mcg/mL (plasma; sample type arterial plasma). Lidocaine: total plasma, LOQ 0.1 mcg/mL. Performance error PE% = (measured - predicted)/predicted x 100; MDPE = bias.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: BIS (target 40-60), not a movement or analgesia end point. No EC50, ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis reported for propofol or lidocaine. The only effect-site values are the Schnider-model Ce used by the TCI (floor 2.5 mcg/mL; Fig 2 plots targeted Ce, mean and SD, over ~80 min).
- No MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration reported. Not an opioid paper (fentanyl was a background drug: 6.55 (1.52) vs 6.49 (1.77) mcg/kg, Table 2).
- BIS at end of infusion 46.4 (9.4) control vs 49.3 (11.27) lidocaine (P=0.47).
- Effect: propofol maintenance dose 17% lower with lidocaine (derived: 6.00/7.25 = 0.83; the authors say 15-20%), with equal end-of-infusion plasma propofol, so the authors rule out a propofol-lidocaine PK interaction; they suggest the sparing is mainly during surgical stimulation (antinociceptive) and speculate (not tested) about a lidocaine-fentanyl interaction.
- Time to extubation (T0 to extubation, no stimulation): median 11.0 min (range 10.0-21.0) lidocaine vs 8.3 (5.5-12.5) control, P=0.02.
- Measured/predicted: measured propofol exceeded Schnider-predicted by a median 25-35% at end of infusion in both groups (Schnider under-predicts in these patients).

## Covariates
None modelled. Groups matched on age, weight, height, BMI (Table 1).

## Caveats
Authors: Ce floor of 2.5 mcg/mL may have blunted the sparing effect; surgery only 1-1.5 h. Notice: serial propofol levels in only 5 per group; end-of-infusion sample only for the PK-interaction test; Table 1 age is a mean with range (not SD) while other rows are mean (SD); the lidocaine group's median extubation range overlaps the control's. Lidocaine was not sampled serially in the text beyond the end-of-infusion mean.
