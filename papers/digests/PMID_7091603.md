# James 1982: Nitrous oxide analgesia and altitude

## Study
Blinded Latin-square crossover in a pressure chamber (Salisbury, Zimbabwe). n = 20 healthy adult volunteers, both sexes, all normally resident at 1460 m. Three barometric pressures: sea level 101.5 kPa (Table 1; Methods say 100 kPa), 1460 m 85.0 kPa (Table 1; Methods say 84), simulated 3300 m 69.0 kPa. At each pressure subjects breathed air, gas A (50% N2O in O2) and gas B (50% N2O in O2-enriched air, PO2 held at 21 kPa) from a Mapleson A system, 10 min each before testing. Pain threshold: pressure on the bare tibia with a spring balance (Dundee and Moore method), 1 lb = 1 arbitrary "pain unit". Order of altitude and gas varied by Latin square; volunteers and tester unaware. Repeated-measures ANOVA. Expired PCO2 by Beckman infrared.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Table 1 (PDF p.2; image read), partial pressures in kPa at 101.5 / 85.0 / 69.0 kPa: gas A N2O = O2 = 50.5 / 42.5 / 34.5. Gas B N2O 50.5 / 42.5 / 34.5, O2 21.3 / 21.3 / 21.3, N2 29.2 / 21.2 / 13.2. The N2O partial pressures equal about 50, 42 and 34% of a sea-level atmosphere (the paper's wording).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: pain threshold (tibial pressure, pain units), 10 min after starting inhalation. Table 2 (image read), mean (SEM) pain units at sea level / 1460 m / 3300 m: air 8.6 (4.26), 8.8 (3.13), 9.0 (3.59); gas A 14.8 (3.01), 12.3 (3.12), 10.5 (2.03); gas B 14.7 (3.51), 12.4 (2.25), 10.9 (2.40). Increase over air: gas A +6.2 (p<0.01), +3.5 (p<0.01), +1.5 (p>0.05); gas B +6.1 (p<0.01), +3.6 (p<0.01), +1.9 (p>0.05). Mean % increase 71.5 / 40.0 / 19.0. Sea level vs the other two p<0.01; 1460 m vs 3300 m p<0.05. Gas A = gas B at every altitude (O2 and N2 tension not relevant). Mean expired PCO2 4.59 / 4.51 / 4.59 kPa. Nausea in 3 and semi-comatose state in 2 only at sea level. The authors say 50% N2O at sea level gave slightly more analgesia than morphine 0.2 mg/kg in an earlier study with the same apparatus. No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or hysteresis reported (maximum effect said to be reached by 10 min, citing earlier work); no plasma or alveolar concentration measured.
Opioid audit: not an opioid paper; the only potency statement is the qualitative 50% N2O at sea level slightly greater than morphine 0.2 mg/kg on pain threshold (Discussion, PDF p.3).

## Covariates
Barometric pressure (N2O partial pressure). Subjects were acclimatised to 1460 m; the authors note that chronic adaptation at each altitude might give different results.

## Caveats
Acute exposure in acclimatised subjects; three pressures, one nominal concentration; large between-subject variation in resting threshold; the 3300 m effect not significant vs air; "% increase" is of the group mean threshold.
