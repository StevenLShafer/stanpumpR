# Camu 2002: Pharmacology of systemic analgesics

## Study
Narrative review (14 pp.) of systemic analgesics for acute postoperative pain: opioids (morphine and pure mu agonists, mixed and partial agonists, weak opioids), NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors, dipyrone and paracetamol, and ketamine. No original data; Tables 1 and 2 are compiled from unstated sources; references are numbered, with 68 listed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit (all values are from other authors' work, cited only as a list or not at all):
- **t1/2ke0** (Table 1, p. 3): morphine 30 min, fentanyl 6.4 min, alfentanil 0.9 min, sufentanil 6.2 min, pethidine blank. Defined as equilibration half-time between plasma and brain concentrations; the end point (EEG, analgesia) and the source are not given. No tPeak or hysteresis loop is reported. (derived: ke0 = ln2/t1/2 = 0.023, 0.108, 0.77, 0.112 /min.)
- **MEAC** (Table 2, p. 3, ng/mL, plasma by implication; the text says "effective plasma concentration" and that it varies four- to fivefold between patients, p. 3): morphine 15-40, pethidine 455, alfentanil 40-60, fentanyl 0.6-1, sufentanil 0.03-0.04. MEAC defined in the text as the concentration eliciting analgesia at steady state during constant-rate infusion, when plasma and effect site have equilibrated; MEC as the concentration at the time re-medication is required. Assay (total vs free, plasma vs whole blood) and population not stated. Methadone MEC 30-58 ng/mL depending on regimen (p. 4, ref 4, Gourlay 1984). Fentanyl Css50 0.63 +/- 0.25 ng/mL for postoperative pain (p. 4, ref 6); respiratory depression seldom important below 3 ng/mL.
- **Potency**: IV potency ratios in Table 1 (above). Text on p. 4 gives different ratios (sufentanil 625x morphine, 12x fentanyl; fentanyl 60x alfentanil); piritramide about two-thirds of morphine; tramadol about one-tenth of morphine; M6G 20-40x morphine; peak sufentanil effect about 8 min.
- Non-opioids (p. 6-9, printed 480-483): aspirin t1/2ke0 20 min, Css50 5.3 ug/mL, slope 1.35 (ref 13); t1/2ke0 for analgesia 7-13 min for paracetamol, 20 for aspirin, more than 60 min for ibuprofen (the text layer reads this as 460; the page image shows ">60"); diflunisal 40 ug/mL for experimental pain; ibuprofen Css50 30 ug/mL, 60 ug/mL for 90% pain free (dental surgery); paracetamol analgesia associated with a plasma target of at least 10 ug/mL (ref 47), effect depends on peak concentration.
- Ketamine (p. 11, printed 485): inhibition of pain at serum concentrations below 1 uM; analgesia at 1/10 to 1/5 of an anaesthetic dose (ref 55, Eide 1995, post-herpetic neuralgia).

## Covariates
Age lowers volume and clearance of opioids; weight-based dosing is said to lack evidence in adults (p. 3); renal failure for M6G; hepatic disease; phenytoin and phenobarbital raise fentanyl, sufentanil and pethidine clearance, cimetidine lowers it. No equations.

## Caveats
Narrative review; Tables 1 and 2 are uncited compilations; the opioid tables give ranges with no populations; t1/2ke0 end points are unstated; internal inconsistency in potency ratios (see open questions); MEAC is for plasma concentrations of mixed assays.

- Pethidine: MEAC 460 ng/mL (R/drugs_pethidine.R:25) vs Camu 455: **matches** (both likely Austin 1980). tPeak 10 min (:14); Camu gives no t1/2ke0.

- Aspirin: library has no effect site (R/drugs_aspirin.R:170-171) versus Camu t1/2ke0 20 min, Css50 5.3 ug/mL (ref 13); not used by the library.
- Ketamine: library tPeak 3 min is assumed and MEAC is 0 (R/drugs_ketamine.R:52-53); Camu's 1 uM (about 0.24 ug/mL) analgesic ceiling is not used.
