# Puhto 2020: single-dose epidural hydromorphone in labour, maternal NCA and cord concentrations

## Study
Pilot, Oulu University Hospital, Feb 2017-Feb 2018 (EudraCT 2016-000903-10). Seven healthy term parturients
(ASA I/II, 37-41 weeks), age 22-34 y, weight 63-94 kg (Table 1, PDF p4). After a 10 mL lidocaine 5 mg/mL test dose
with epinephrine 10 µg/mL, one epidural dose of hydromorphone hydrochloride in 10 mL saline: 1.5 mg (1 woman), 0.75
mg (2), 0.5 mg (4), equal to 1.33, 0.665 and 0.443 mg free base; the dose was lowered because of protracted nausea.
Rescue: levobupivacaine 1.25 mg/mL 10 mL on request. Venous samples at 0, 15, 30, 45 min, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 h
or until birth, plus at birth; umbilical artery and vein at birth. UHPLC-MS/MS, linear 0.1-25 ng/mL, LLOQ 0.1 ng/mL;
the sample preparation uses 0.5 mL whole blood while the Results speak of plasma concentrations (matrix not
reconciled in the paper). NCA (WinNonlin 6.3), results as free base.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No MEAC measured; no ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak; no potency ratio. Cited analgesic concentrations of IV hydromorphone:
4.7 µg/L (children with mucositis, Collins 1996) and mean 4.1 µg/L serum (postoperative, Jeleazcov 2016) (PDF p6);
the authors say these are not comparable with epidural plasma levels and read the low Cmax as evidence of a spinal
site of action. Analgesia end point: first levobupivacaine request a median 163 (19-303) min after hydromorphone;
NRS curves in Fig. 1. Adverse events: nausea 5, vomiting 3, pruritus 3, hypotension 1; no desaturation below 97%.

Neonatal (Table 3, PDF p7, rendered): maternal at delivery 2.34, 0.48, 0.29, 0.11 µg/L (IDs 1, 3, 4, 5; others
below LLOQ or no vaginal birth); umbilical venous/maternal 0.79, 1.27, 1.1, 1.55; umbilical arterial/maternal 0.69,
1.23, 1.03. Apgar 7-10, NACS 30-39 (Table 3's footnote calls Ua/Uv "uterine", evidently umbilical).

## Covariates
None (n = 7).

## Caveats
Pilot n = 7, three dose levels, sampling stopped at birth (terminal phase 1.5-3.3 half-lives), no IV arm, epidural
epinephrine test dose possibly slowing absorption, matrix wording inconsistent. Authors recommend a maximum
cumulative 0.5 mg hydrochloride with local anaesthetic.
