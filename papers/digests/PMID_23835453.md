# Rinehart 2013: closed-loop fluid (LIR) vs anesthesiologists in pigs

## Study
Randomised in vivo animal study, 16 Yorkshire cross pigs (55-70 kg; 17 enrolled, 1 excluded for ventricular fibrillation during line placement), 8 per group: anesthesia practitioners (AP) vs the Learning Intravenous Resuscitator closed-loop (CL) fluid controller (pumps driven by LiDCO Rapid CO/SV/PPV). Anesthesia: ketamine/xylazine IM, pentobarbital and fentanyl 1-2 ug/kg IV for intubation, then isoflurane 3%, fentanyl 1.5 ug/kg/h and rocuronium 0.01 mg/kg/min (PDF p. 2, printed 1120). Protocol (Fig. 1): 30 min baseline, bleed 20 mL/min x 30 min (600 mL, ~15% blood volume), 45 min rest, bleed 40 mL/min x 30 min (1200 mL, ~30%), 30 min rest. Baseline lactated Ringer 3 mL/kg/h in both groups. Analysis: GEE over 34 five-minute points per pig; Mann-Whitney for fluid totals and CoV.

## Model and parameters
No PK/PD model. Results (median [IQR], Table 3, PDF p. 5, printed 1123): LiDCO cardiac index 3.7 [3.4-4.1] CL vs 3.5 [3.2-3.9] AP L/min/m2 (P<0.0005); SV index 40 [34-45] vs 36 [31-38] mL/m2 (P<0.0005); total fluid 3685 [3230-4418] vs 3253 [2735-3926] mL (P=0.28); CoV of CI 11% vs 22% (P=0.02), of SVI 11% vs 17% (P=0.04). End Hgb lower in CL, 6.0 vs 7.3 g/dL (P=0.04). The text layer scrambles part of Table 3 (colloid, crystalloid and blood cells), so those are not recorded.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None (not an opioid paper). End points are hemodynamic (CI, SVI, MAP, HR, CoV). Fentanyl appears only as background anesthesia (1.5 ug/kg/h); no concentration or effect data.

## Covariates
Porcine body surface area from height and weight (square root of height x weight / 3600); used only to index CO and SV.

## Caveats
Small n; pigs and a hemorrhage model; LiDCO not designed for animals (calibrated to thermodilution at baseline); CL gave more colloid; AP group had no mandated protocol; authors hold equity in a closed-loop company; no follow-up; possible occult anemia in the CL group.
