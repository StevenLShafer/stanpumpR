# Gershuny 2025: midazolam plus oxycodone and hypercapnic ventilation

## Study
Randomised, double-blind, four-period crossover trial (NCT 04310579), US FDA-run, at one clinical pharmacology unit. 20 healthy non-smoking adults, 18-50 y; 54 screened, all 20 randomised and completed (Fig 1, PDF p.4). Table 1 (PDF p.5, read on the image): median age 36 y (range 22-47); 7 female (35%); 14 Black (70%), 6 White (30%); 2 Hispanic/Latino (10%); weight median 80 kg (43-92); height 1.76 m (1.46-1.92); BMI 27.4 (20.5-29.6); resting minute ventilation 8.0 L/min (5.3-18.0), frequency 15.3 bpm (8.2-21.3), end-tidal PCO2 5.3 kPa (3.8-5.9), SpO2 96.4% (93.9-98.9).

Four treatments, 3-day washout (Fig 1b): oral oxycodone 10 mg (immediate release) + i.v. placebo; oral placebo + i.v. midazolam; oxycodone + midazolam; both placebo. Oxycodone/placebo at 0 h; midazolam i.v. over 2 min starting at 1.92 h (115 min) so that its peak coincided with oxycodone's, during the final 2 min of the 100% O2 phase before rebreathing. Midazolam dose 0.0375 mg/kg in the first 5 participants, raised to 0.075 mg/kg for the other 15 after an interim analysis (sedation and SpO2 < 90% in the first two participants given the higher dose led to supplemental O2 by nasal cannula). Ondansetron 4 mg orally 30 min before oxycodone.

Ventilation: modified Read rebreathing at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 24 h; primary outcome minute ventilation at end-tidal PCO2 7.3 kPa, interpolated from an individual linear fit, at 2 h, midazolam 0.075 mg/kg subjects only. One-sided alpha 0.049 (O'Brien-Fleming for two looks). Linear mixed model (participant random; order, period, baseline fixed). Power: n = 20, 90%, to detect a 4 L/min decrease, SD 5.

PK: plasma oxycodone, oxymorphone and midazolam by LC-MS/MS at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24 h; noncompartmental analysis in R; values below the LLOQ set to zero. No population model.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit. End point: hypercapnic ventilatory response (minute ventilation at end-tidal PCO2 7.3 kPa), plus slope and x-axis intercept of the ventilation-PCO2 line, tidal volume, frequency, sedation VAS.
- MEAC / analgesic concentration: none reported (no analgesia measured).
- ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none fitted. The only timing statement is the design assumption that oxycodone and midazolam concentrations peak together near 2 h; no plasma-effect model was fitted. Fig 2a plots ventilation over 0-6 h with oxycodone 10 mg reducing ventilation vs placebo at 2 h.
- Potency/equianalgesic ratios: none.

Table 2 (PDF p.6, printed 1175, read on the image), minute ventilation at 7.3 kPa, 2 h, L/min, mean (95% CI): placebo 32.2 (28.6-35.9), n = 20; oxycodone 25.2 (21.5-28.9), n = 20; midazolam 0.075 mg/kg 30.8 (26.8-34.8), n = 15; oxycodone + midazolam 23.5 (19.4-27.6), n = 15. Differences (one-sided 95% CI): oxycodone vs placebo -7.0 (-inf to -4.0), P < 0.001; midazolam vs placebo -1.4 (-inf to 1.8), P = 0.24; oxycodone + midazolam vs oxycodone -1.7 (-inf to 1.6), P = 0.21 (primary, not significant); vs placebo -8.7; vs midazolam -7.3.
Other Table 2 rows (mean): HCVR slope L/min/kPa: placebo 10.3, oxycodone 9.6, midazolam 15.0, combination 12.1. X-axis intercept kPa: placebo 4.8, oxycodone 5.2, midazolam 5.9, combination 6.1. Frequency at 7.3 kPa, bpm: 14.0, 12.8, 20.7, 16.7. Tidal volume at 7.3 kPa, mL: 2100, 1841, 1214, 1030.
Resting end-tidal PCO2 (supplement S4): combination higher than oxycodone alone by 0.2 kPa (95% CI 0.0-0.4), 5.8 vs 5.6 kPa (abstract). Sedation VAS and sleep/snoring: 9/15 (60%) asleep or snoring on combination, 6/15 (40%) on midazolam alone, 0/20 on oxycodone or placebo (Fig 4, p.1178).
Oxycodone 10 mg depressed hypercapnic ventilation; midazolam alone and the added midazolam did not at 7.3 kPa, but reduced tidal volume and raised frequency.

## Covariates
None analysed beyond order, period and baseline ventilation. No age, sex, weight or CYP2D6 effects reported in the main text.

## Caveats
Authors' limitations (PDF p.7, printed 1176): midazolam given i.v. 2 min before rebreathing while oxycodone was oral, so exposures were not matched or sustained; by 3 h resting differences had gone; clinicians roused sleeping participants during testing; only 10 subjects had no missing data for the primary analysis, so it is underpowered; healthy volunteers; midazolam dose not maximal. Only 15 participants had the 0.075 mg/kg dose. CIs other than the primary are unadjusted. Text-layer Table 1 is scrambled (columns misaligned); the values above are from the image.

## Verification against stanpumpR
The paper is not cited in the checkout (searched R/, inst/extdata, docs). It reports PK for two drugs the library models, so an independent comparison was attempted:
- Oxycodone (R/drugs_oxycodone.R): the model's oral CL/F is 1.44 L/min for 15 mg (Lalovic 2006), with miosis t½ke0 11 min, EC50 30 ng/mL (header lines 32-35). Gershuny's 10 mg dose and AUCinf 132.2 ng/mL*h give CL/F about 1.26 L/min (derived: dose / AUC, ignoring HCl vs base), about 12% lower than the model's value, within the 37% CV. Cannot verify further (open question 1). Cmax 21.8 ng/mL for 10 mg is consistent in scale with Lalovic's 38 ng/mL for 15 mg (derived: 38 x 10/15 = 25 ng/mL), direction-wise 13% lower. The paper has no ke0 or EC50, so it neither supports nor contradicts the model's effect-site values.
- Midazolam (R/drugs_midazolam.R, Zomorodi 1998 three-compartment): cannot compare Cmax without the sampling time (open question 2). The paper's finding that oxycodone does not change midazolam PK is consistent with the library having no oxycodone-midazolam PK interaction (no such term was looked for exhaustively).
Verdict: **cannot verify** against repo values; no discrepancy found.
