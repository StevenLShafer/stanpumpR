# Antila 2004: levosimendan and its metabolites after a 7-day infusion

## Study
Open, non-randomised phase II study at two centres (Helsinki, Tallinn); 24 patients with congestive heart failure (all but two male; NYHA III except 4 in class IV, p3). Levosimendan IV infusion for 7 days, 12 patients at 0.05 ug/kg/min and 12 at 0.1 ug/kg/min (PDF p2, abstract; the text layer prints "mg", the rendered page prints ug). Follow-up 10 days (6 patients on 0.1) or 13-15 days (the rest). Renal function by baseline creatinine clearance: normal 14, mild impairment 5, moderate 3, severe 2. Plasma samples (3 mL) at 0, 2, 4, 8, 12 h, then daily to day 7, then 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 12, 24, 36 h after the infusion and daily 10-15 days. Plasma, LC-MS/MS; LOQ 0.2 ng/mL levosimendan, 1.0 ng/mL OR-1855 (plasma), 0.2 ng/mL OR-1896. Noncompartmental analysis (WinNonlin 1.5); too few distribution-phase samples for compartmental fitting. Protein binding by ultracentrifugation, lower-dose group only.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured here. No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, EC50 or MEAC. The paper says OR-1896 is pharmacologically similar to the parent (citing a rabbit myocardium study, ref 4) and less protein bound, and that it may explain the long haemodynamic effect (p415). Not an opioid; no potency ratio. The metabolite tmax (144-314 h) is after the end of infusion (day 7 = 168 h), so the metabolites' peaks follow termination.

## Covariates
Creatinine clearance did not correlate with levosimendan clearance (P = 0.259) or dose-normalised metabolite AUC (P = 0.9137 OR-1855, 0.8913 OR-1896) (p414). Ten of 24 had mild to severe renal impairment; authors warn the sample is small. Weight appears only through per-kg reporting.

## Caveats
Noncompartmental only; metabolite half-lives of about 80 h against a 7-day infusion mean steady state was not reached and terminal half-lives rest on 10-15 days of follow-up. Protein binding only at the low dose. Patients with severe heart failure, nearly all male. Sponsor (Orion) study. Metabolite concentrations are per ng/mL of each metabolite, not parent-equivalent. DOI in the page header reads 2004.02043.x, the page footer 2003.02043.x; the footer form is used above.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No citation to verify. As an independent check on the library's levosimendan model (R/drugs_levosimendan.R:79-84, Jonsson 2003: CL 13.3 L/h at 76 kg, CL slope 1.5 %/kg, V1 16.8 L, Q 2.33 L/h, V2 30 L; units L/min after /60 at line 79, consistent with L/h):
- Clearance agrees. Antila mean CL 0.21 L/h/kg (low dose) times 76 kg gives 16.0 L/h and 0.18 L/h/kg gives 13.7 L/h (derived), bracketing 13.3 L/h; the absolute values 17.9 and 14.4 L/h are also close. Direction: Antila's low-dose group is somewhat higher than the model, the high-dose group close to it; the dose groups differ by P = 0.14, so no real trend.
- Volume: Antila Vz 27.8-28.2 L (0.33-0.34 L/kg, about 25.5 L at 76 kg, derived) is smaller than the model's V1+V2 = 46.8 L. Vz is a terminal-phase volume from a noncompartmental analysis, normally at least as large as Vss, so the model's central-plus-peripheral volume exceeding it is a genuine difference in kind (heart-failure patients, 190 vs 24). See open question.
- Half-life 1.1-1.4 h (Table 1) is the terminal value; the model's two-compartment terminal half-life was not computed here.
- The model carries the parent only; the model header and registry say the metabolite OR-1896 is not modelled. Antila supplies its t1/2 (about 81 h), Cmax 9.9 and 17.1 ng/mL and tmax 170-180 h, and free fractions (61%) as reference values for a later metabolite model. Verdict: not-applicable (no citation), comparison consistent for clearance.
