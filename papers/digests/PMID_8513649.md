# Greenblatt and Wright 1993: review of alprazolam clinical pharmacokinetics

## Study
Narrative review (Tufts / Upjohn) of alprazolam metabolism, receptor binding, assays, single- and multiple-dose
kinetics, covariates, interactions, PK-PD and concentration-response. Summary tables: protein binding (Table I),
assays (Table II), single-dose studies (Table III), interactions (Table IV).

Metabolites: alpha-hydroxy and 4-hydroxy alprazolam below 10% of parent in plasma; receptor affinities 77%, 14%,
10% of parent for alpha-OH, 4-OH and alpha,4-diOH (section 1.2). CYP3A4 likely.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Concentration-response (section 8.3): steady-state concentrations below 20 ug/L have minimal efficacy; 20-40 ug/L
  give clear efficacy for anxiety associated with panic disorder; higher concentrations (> 60 ug/L) may be needed for
  situational panic attacks, with more side effects. End point: clinical efficacy in panic-disorder trials.
- Time of maximum CNS effect roughly at tmax; some studies show counterclockwise hysteresis (delayed CNS entry).
  Maximum clinical effect 1 to 1.5 h after an oral dose (section 8.1). Tolerance develops with chronic use.
- No ke0, EC50 or MEAC.

## Covariates
- Sex: 2 of 3 studies show no effect; Kristjansson 1991 found higher CL in young women. Summary: most studies show no
  significant influence of gender.
- Age: lower CL in elderly men (0.64 vs 1.33 mL/min/kg; t1/2 19 vs 11 h, Greenblatt 1983a); less clear in women.
- Obesity (Abernethy 1984): Vd 114 vs 73 L, CL 66 vs 88 mL/min (not significant), t1/2 22 vs 11 h.
- Cirrhosis lowers CL; renal failure raises free fraction; smoking trend to higher CL; no ethnic, panic-disorder,
  alcoholism or menstrual effects. Cimetidine, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, propoxyphene impair CL.

## Caveats
Review; values are ranges of study means, mostly healthy young men; concentration-response evidence is from panic
disorder trials and labelled by the authors as not clearly established. Co-author from the manufacturer.
