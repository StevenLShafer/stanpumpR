# Berk 1992: sublingual vs intravenous verapamil, PK and PR interval

PDF page n is printed page 32 + n. DOI from the PDF's download banner (PubMed lists none).

## Study
University of Illinois at Chicago. Dosage-form development in dogs (3 mongrels for HCl forms with induced AF; 2
anaesthetised dogs given 40 mg sublingual base, AV nodal ERP measured; Table 1, PDF p3). Human study: seven healthy
male volunteers, 25-30 years; weights not reported. Sequential, non-randomised: verapamil HCl 5 mg IV infused over
1 min, then after at least a week a sublingual dose of verapamil **free base** in PEG 400 (40 mg in four subjects,
60 mg in three), not swallowed for an hour, supine for 3 h. ECG lead II rhythm strips and blood at baseline and
5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90, 120, 150, 180, 240, 360 and 480 min after each dose (PDF p3). PR interval: mean of
three blinded readers. **Serum** racemic verapamil and norverapamil by HPLC with fluorescence (CV < 11% over
6.5-65 ng/mL; LOD 1.0 ng/mL). Non-compartmental PK: terminal ke by regression, AUC trapezoidal, normalised to 5 mg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: percentage change in PR interval (AV nodal conduction); no heart rate data in humans. One subject had
almost no PR change after either form, with an estimated time to maximum effect of about 3 h. Excluding him (n = 6):
maximum PR prolongation 20.6 ± 6.4% after IV vs 14.8 ± 5.5% after SL (p < 0.05); time to maximum PR prolongation
28.3 ± 15.7 min after IV vs 57.0 ± 17.5 min after SL (p < 0.05). All seven: 18.5 ± 8.2% vs 14.7 ± 5.0% (NS) and
50.0 ± 59.1 vs 57.0 ± 17.5 min (NS) (PDF p4). Figure 3: PR change vs serum concentration lies higher or further
left for IV than SL in six of seven, i.e. less effect per concentration after SL. Figure 2 (PDF p4) shows the mean IV
PR change near its highest from about 15 to 30 min. No ke0, EC50 or hysteresis model fitted. In dogs, ventricular
rate in induced AF fell 30-60 min after sublingual HCl forms.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Seven young healthy men, nonrandomised order, two SL doses pooled; SL drug partly swallowed (norverapamil formed).
Serum, not plasma. Weights not reported, so CL and Vss cannot be put per kg. Peak-effect time depends on the
sampling grid (5, 10, 15, 30, 45 min...). All subjects reported bitter taste, numbness and salivation after SL.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Peak PR time** (code `:87-89`, help `:19`, registry): 28.3 ± 15.7 min in six of seven healthy men after 5 mg
  over 1 min, the seventh about 3 h. **Matches.**
- **PK numbers** (registry): CL 89.0 ± 35.5 L/h, Vss 277.2 ± 91.5 L, t1/2 2.83 h, serum, 7 men, SL base. **Matches.**
- **Sampling description**: **discrepancy (minor).** The code says the PR was read at 5, 10, 15, 30 and 45 min and
  the registry at 5, 10, 15, 30, 45 and 60 min; the paper (PDF p3) took ECG strips at 14 times, 5 through 480 min
  (5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90, 120, 150, 180, 240, 360, 480). The lists in the repo are truncated; the conclusion
  (the peak lies on a coarse grid) still holds.
- **"Kates model gives 56 L/h and 262 L"** (registry): **cannot verify.** Berk reports no weights. The Kates
  per-kg model gives 60.8 L/h and 283 L at 81 kg; 56 and 262 correspond to about 75 kg (derived); the repo does
  not say which weight it used.
- **tPeak not taken from this paper** (registry): consistent with the paper, whose end point is PR, not heart rate.
