# van den Berg 2017: review of PK and PD interactions in anaesthesia

## Study
Narrative review, no new data. Sections: PK interactions (absorption, distribution, elimination, with cardiac-output effects on propofol clearance), PD interactions (isoboles, response-surface models, additivity/supra-additivity/infra-additivity, criss-cross design), clinically relevant end points (responsiveness, BIS/entropy/other EEG indices, qNOX, CVI), volatile-opioid, IV-opioid, hypnotic-hypnotic and alpha2-agonist interactions, then teaching simulators (Table 1) and advisory displays (SmartPilot View, Navigator; Fig 5).

## Model and parameters
No PK parameter tables. Statements of note:
- Propofol volume of distribution about 300 L or more (printed p.45; cites Gepts and Schüttler).
- Commonly used clinical models named: Schnider and Marsh (propofol), Minto (remifentanil), Gepts (sufentanil); the Eleveld generalized propofol model is mentioned (printed p.47).
- Experts' criterion: MDAPE should not exceed 20% for a useful TCI system (printed p.47). Prospective validation (Short and colleagues, ref 64) of the Bouillon propofol-remifentanil BIS model: MDPE 8% (SD 24%), MDAPE 25% (SD 13%) (printed p.47).
- Navigator uses the Minto model for propofol-opioid interaction and the Greco model for volatile-opioid; SmartPilot View uses the hierarchical Bouillon model for both (printed p.53).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit. No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis value is reported for any drug. No MEAC or minimum analgesic concentration is defined. No equianalgesic dose ratio is stated. The review does give concentration values that reduce MAC or shift an isobole (all from cited primary studies; printed p.49 and p.50, confirmed on PDF pages 6-7; assay, population and matrix are not stated in this review):
- Desflurane MAC50 for skin incision reduced by 50% with fentanyl 0.5 ng/mL (ref 81).
- Isoflurane MAC reduced by 50% (stated as an equivalent reduction) by fentanyl 1.67 ng/mL (ref 82), alfentanil 28.8 ng/mL (ref 83), remifentanil 1.37 ng/mL (ref 84), sufentanil 0.15 ng/mL (ref 85). These imply concentration ratios relative to fentanyl but the paper does not state any ratio (derived: fentanyl:alfentanil:remifentanil:sufentanil about 1 : 17 : 0.82 : 0.09 on this end point, a coarse comparison across different studies).
- Sevoflurane MAC reduced by 50% by fentanyl 1.8 ng/mL (ref 86) or remifentanil 1.69 ng/mL (ref 41; laryngoscopy as the stimulus).
- Propofol-fentanyl LOC isobole: maximal reduction of the 50% LOC probability reached at fentanyl 3 ng/mL (ref 95).
- Propofol 1.2 µg/mL plus sufentanil 0.456 ng/mL used for conscious sedation in burn dressing changes (ref 99), satisfaction scores good in 95%.
- Other (non-opioid) values: dexmedetomidine 0.66 ng/mL (TCI) lowers the propofol EC50 for motor response to electrical stimulation from 6.63 to 3.89 µg/mL (ref 121); oral clonidine 5.0 µg/kg 90 min earlier lowers the propofol EC50 for response to verbal command from 2.67 to 0.91 µg/mL, about 65% (ref 124).
Qualitative interaction findings: propofol-fentanyl, -alfentanil, -remifentanil supra-additive for noxious stimuli; propofol-sufentanil on LOC closer to additive; remifentanil-propofol on BIS inconsistent (none, additive, supra-additive); propofol-sevoflurane additive; midazolam-propofol variable.

## Covariates
None quantified. Mentions cardiac output as a determinant of propofol concentration and clearance (reduced by remifentanil; sheep data), and CYP induction by anti-epileptics lowering fentanyl, methadone and pethidine concentrations.

## Caveats
Review: values are secondary and carry only citation numbers. Prediction errors in interaction models are mostly population variability, so individual adjustment is needed (printed p.47).
