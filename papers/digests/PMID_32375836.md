# Schulz 2020: therapeutic and toxic blood concentrations of >1100 drugs (revisited)

## Study
Narrative review / data compilation (pubtype Review), an update of the authors' 2012 compilation (Crit Care
2012;16:R136, ref 4). More than 200 substances added since 2012, including new psychoactive substances. Data
abstracted from original papers, textbooks, earlier compilations (mainly refs 5-25: Baselt, Hiemke/AGNP 2017,
Regenthal, Winek, Musshoff and others) and the authors' own forensic and clinical toxicology laboratory cases,
collected over 30+ years (PDF p1-2). No new subjects, no assay or PK modelling.

Categories defined (PDF p2):
- **Therapeutic** ("normal"): blood (plasma or serum) concentrations after therapeutically effective doses with no
  or minimal side effects.
- **Toxic**: plasma/serum concentrations producing toxicity or clinically relevant adverse effects.
- **Comatose-fatal**: plasma/serum for comatose, whole blood for fatal; whether fatal values are ante- or
  post-mortem (femoral or heart blood) is often unknown.

Units of the table: mg/L unless stated otherwise (Additional file 1 legend, PDF p3). Half-lives are generally
terminal elimination half-lives, mostly from healthy subjects at low doses (PDF p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: the article text gives no MEAC or minimum effective/analgesic concentration, no ke0 / t½ke0 /
tPeak / hysteresis, and no equianalgesic or potency ratio for oxycodone or any opioid. It notes only (PDF p3)
that tolerance, especially for all opioids, makes the therapeutic/toxic threshold impossible to fix for a given
patient. Fig. 1 (PDF p3) is a summary graphic of drug classes covered.

## Covariates
None reported.

## Caveats
- Authors: inter-individual variation is high; values are orientation only (PDF p3).
- Comatose-fatal values deliberately lean towards the low concentrations seen in fatal cases; many patients
  survive higher concentrations (PDF p3).
- Mixed matrices (plasma, serum, whole blood) and unknown sampling times; post-mortem redistribution not
  separated.
- Therapeutic ranges for opioids describe patients on therapy (often chronic, tolerant), not acute
  opioid-naive analgesia.
