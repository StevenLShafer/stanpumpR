# Grujić 2010: ceftriaxone vs cefazolin 2 g prophylaxis at elective caesarean

## Study
Prospective comparison, Clinical Centre of Vojvodina, Novi Sad (PDF p1-2, printed pp. 600-601). 90 healthy term
pregnant women for elective caesarean and 8 non-pregnant women for gynaecological surgery.
- Group A: 45 pregnant, ceftriaxone 2 g IV 10 min before surgery; group C: 4 non-pregnant, same.
- Group B: 45 pregnant, cefazolin 2 g IV 10 min before surgery; group D: 4 non-pregnant, same.
- Concentrations measured immediately after and 6 h after the operation "in 36 pregnant and 8 non-pregnant
  women" (Methods, PDF p2), i.e. not in all 90 pregnant women; the split by drug is not stated. Also amniotic fluid
  and umbilical-cord blood. Assay: liquid chromatography (ref 6, an HPLC method for cefotaxime/ceftriaxone in rat
  plasma). Matrix (serum vs plasma) and total vs free not stated.
- Table 1 (PDF p2, image), pregnant groups only: Group 1 / Group 2 age 30.8±5.7 / 28.9±6.1 y, weight
  83.0±12.6 / 80.0±11.9 kg, height 166.2±6.8 / 167.6±6.9 cm, BMI 30.2±5.1 / 28.4±4.1. Non-pregnant
  demographics not given.
- Table 2: operative time 31.9±11.2 / 30.0±9.4 min; blood loss 391.1±211.9 / 424.4±213.8 mL; infection
  morbidity 2 (4.4%) in each group.
- PK method (one-compartment, NCA or fitted) is not described in this paper.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD model. End points: concentrations against the MIC breakpoints in Tables 3-4, and clinical infection
(4.4% per group, Table 2). Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Pregnancy only (pregnant caesarean vs non-pregnant gynaecological surgery, different subjects). Doses were fixed 2 g;
the authors note the per-kg dose was lower in the heavier pregnant women (PDF p3).

## Caveats
- Non-pregnant comparator n = 4 per drug; huge SDs (ceftriaxone Vd SD ≈ mean, Cl SD > mean).
- Ceftriaxone volumes (190-234 L) and clearances (24-48 L/h) are 20-40 times usual values (derived comparison:
  healthy adults ~1.2 L/h, ~9 L), and a 22.7 μg/mL mean post-operative level after 2 g is about a tenth of
  expected peaks. Something in the assay or calculation is off; the absolute numbers should not be used.
- The PK numbers are identical to Popovic 2007 (ref 13, same group), so this is not independent evidence.
- Sampling only two time points (end of surgery, 6 h) for each patient; PK method not described.
- The authors attribute the pregnancy effect to albumin change/saturable binding without measuring it.
- Non-pregnant women had different surgery (gynaecological) with no demographic table.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:52, ceftriaxone**: the finding says Grujic 2010 measured higher post-operative
  ceftriaxone concentrations after 2 g "in 45 pregnant than in 4 non-pregnant women". Direction **matches**
  Table 3 (22.7 vs 12.0 μg/mL after surgery; 13.2 vs 5.7 at 6 h). The n is **overstated**: 45 women per group
  received the drug, but concentrations were measured in 36 pregnant women in total across both drugs (Methods,
  PDF p2), so at most 36 (plausibly 18, as in Popovic 2007) for ceftriaxone. Minor wording discrepancy; no
  numeric factor depends on it, since nothing is applied.
- The row presents Grujic 2010 and Popovic 2007 as separate sources; Table 5 here is the same data (ke
  0.11 vs 0.18 /h, Cl 24.42 vs 48.24 L/h, matching the registry's Popovic numbers 0.1109 vs 0.1817 and 24.4 vs
  48.2 to rounding). The two are one cohort, so they count as one piece of evidence (see open question).
- Comparator: the "higher" concentrations are against 4 non-pregnant women having gynaecological surgery, not
  against the library's model (Sanz-Codina 2023, six healthy men, `R/drugs_ceftriaxone.R:9-12, 88-92`). Against
  that model the absolute values here are implausible (CL 24-48 vs 1.21 L/h; V 190-234 vs 8.73 L), so the paper
  supports at most a qualitative direction, and the registry's decision to apply nothing is consistent with it.
- Cefazolin (`pregnancyRegistry.csv:49`) does not cite this paper; its Table 5 shows no pregnancy effect on
  cefazolin ke (0.10 vs 0.11 /h), in line with Popovic 2007 as the card PMID_18021337 records. The library's
  cefazolin model was not compared here because these absolute values share the ceftriaxone doubts.
