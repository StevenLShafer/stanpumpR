# Jonsson 2002: model-based statistical tests for clinical trials

## Study
Commentary with a worked example, not a drug PK/PD study. A failed phase 3 trial of an unnamed drug in a rare, uniformly fatal disease (261 patients, 1:1:1 to placebo 87 / low dose 88 / high dose 86, up to 9 months, monthly visits, Symptom Severity Index, SSI; only 115 remained at the last visit; PDF p.2 / printed p.604). Three tests of the same data are compared: change-from-baseline t test with LOCF (P=.06), individual-slope t test (P=.02), and a log-likelihood-ratio (LLR) test of a linear-progression mixed-effects model referenced to a 10,000-permutation randomization distribution (P=.01; observed LLR 10.2; printed p.605-608). Power by simulation (1000 data sets, 100 permutations each): change-from-baseline t test 0.55; the slope and LLR tests gained less than theory predicted (LLR about +18%, Fig 6, printed p.611-612).

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Trajectory model 2F: SSI = theta1*exp(eta1) + theta2*j*exp(eta2), plus theta3*j for treated patients, monthly visit j. Simulation-alternative fit (printed p.610): theta1 68.3, theta2 0.12, theta3 -0.021, theta4 -0.04, var(eta1) 0.04, var(eta2) 0.0008, var(eps) 17.6 (SSI units, not drug units).
