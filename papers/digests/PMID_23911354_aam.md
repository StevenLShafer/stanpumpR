# Reisinger 2013: antiepileptic drug clearance and seizure frequency in pregnancy

## Study
Retrospective chart review, Emory Epilepsy Center, February 1999 to February 2012 (PDF p2). Of 135 women with
epilepsy taking AEDs in pregnancy, 115 pregnancies in 95 women were kept: at least one AED blood level (ABL) in
each trimester, and no AED added or removed during the pregnancy. 113 singleton pregnancies, 2 twin pregnancies.
Table 1 (PDF p14): mean age 30.3 y (15-43); mean gravida 1.85 (1-6); Caucasian 89 (77%), African-American 19 (17%),
Asian 7 (6%); localization-related epilepsy 72 (63%), generalized 37 (32%), unknown 6 (5%). Body weight is not
reported. 100 pregnancies on monotherapy, 15 on two AEDs, 1 on three (PDF p4).

AED use (Table 1; total / monotherapy / polytherapy): lamotrigine 80/69/11, levetiracetam 27/15/12,
carbamazepine 7/6/1, topiramate 7/3/4, oxcarbazepine 4/2/2, phenytoin 2/1/1, valproate 2/1/1, zonisamide 2/1/1,
ethosuximide 1/1/0.

Sampling: usually monthly ABLs in pregnancy, more often after seizures; drawn at Emory or by the obstetrician;
**timing relative to the last dose not standardized** (PDF p2). Assay: routine clinical laboratories, plasma,
**total** concentrations (free not consistently available); for oxcarbazepine, the 10-monohydroxy metabolite (MHD).
Doses were adjusted to hold each woman's individualized target level. Gestational age from LMP or ultrasound;
trimesters: 1st <14 wk, 2nd 14-28 wk, 3rd >28 wk to delivery. Baseline = preconception ABL or, failing that, one
>6 weeks postpartum (76 preconception baselines, 24 postpartum; 15 without a baseline excluded from the clearance
comparison; PDF p3-4). Statistics: SPSS 20, general linear model (baseline vs maximum pregnancy Cl), two-way ANOVA,
t-tests.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Apparent oral clearance for each ABL: Cl = daily dose (mg/kg) / serum concentration
(µg/mL), with weight from the most recent clinic visit (PDF p3). The paper prints no unit for Cl; the formula gives
L/kg/day (derived: mg/kg/day ÷ mg/L). Table 2 (PDF p15, confirmed on the rendered page) reports, per trimester, the
**mean of each patient's peak (maximum) Cl** in that trimester; "% increase" is against the nonpregnant mean, and
checks as (mean peak / nonpregnant mean − 1) (derived; e.g. LTG 2.53/0.87 = 2.91, +191%).

Text results (PDF p4-5): maximum pregnancy Cl vs nonpregnant baseline, LTG monotherapy +191% (p<0.001), LEV
monotherapy +207% (p=0.006), other monotherapies p=0.048; CBZ "virtually no change". Excluding twins and repeat
pregnancies: LTG +185% (p<0.001), LEV +224% (p=0.014). The two twin pregnancies (both LTG) rose 360%; LTG

across two pregnancies, mean 3.64 then 2.63 (−28%), individual change −69% to +76%.

Dose rose for most AEDs (Figure 1, Table e-1, supplement not archived); only 4 patients had no dose change. Mean ABL
fell for every AED, nadir mostly in the 2nd trimester; smallest falls for CBZ, ESM and VPA (Table e-2, not archived).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK-PD model, ke0, EC50 or therapeutic range. Not an opioid paper. End point: seizure frequency by trimester
against the 12 months before conception (PDF p5-6). 38.3% (44/115) had more seizures in some trimester, 44.3% no
change, 17.4% fewer. Of women seizure-free in the preconception year (49%), 78.8% stayed seizure-free; 20% of the
others did. Worsening was more likely with seizures in the prior year (p<0.001) and with localization-related
epilepsy (p=0.005). The ratio of trimester ABL to baseline did not separate worsened from non-worsened patients, but
a fall of >35% below preconception ABL was associated with more seizures in the 2nd trimester (p<0.001 with prior
seizures; p=0.030 if seizure-free before). Increased seizures: LTG 38%, LEV 47%, polytherapy 50%, CBZ 0/6 (PDF p7).

## Covariates
Clearance is per kg (weight at the latest clinic visit). Race: no effect on Cl change (all drugs p=0.872; LTG
p=0.404). Monotherapy vs polytherapy (LTG): no difference.

## Caveats
Retrospective; untimed samples (so dose/concentration is not a steady-state average clearance); total, not free,
concentrations (matters for CBZ, PHT, VPA); compliance not monitored; "peak" per trimester is a maximum of a
variable number of samples, which biases it upward against a baseline that is a single or few values; doses changed
throughout; n of 1-3 for most non-LTG/LEV drugs. Footnote b says oxcarbazepine n=3 in monotherapy, but Table 1,
Table 2 and Figure 1 give 2. Table 2's "676" is very likely a misprint (open_questions). Accepted manuscript: tables
may differ in the published version.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/drugs_levetiracetam.R:79**: "Reisinger 2013 a mean PEAK clearance 207% higher (n = 15)". Table 2: LEV
  monotherapy n 15, 2nd-trimester mean peak 3.35 vs 1.09, +207%. **Matches** (207% higher = 3.07-fold, and the
  header's wording keeps that sense). The comparator is the same women's nonpregnant (preconception or >6 wk
  postpartum) per-kg apparent clearance, not the library's model. Reisinger is not one of the five studies behind
  LEVETIRACETAM_PREGNANCY_CL_FACTOR = 1.6 (line 140), so nothing is computed from it. As an independent check,
  Reisinger's 3rd-trimester ratio is 1.97 (derived, 2.15/1.09) and is per kg, so in absolute terms larger still with
  pregnancy weight gain: the paper sits above the factor (see open_questions). Its nonpregnant mean, 1.09 L/kg/day
  (derived; 3.2 L/h at 70 kg), is 0.81x the model's 3.9 L/h at the 70 kg reference (derived: 3.9 × 24 / 70 = 1.34
  L/kg/day); untimed samples make that comparison loose.
