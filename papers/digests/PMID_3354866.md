# Gourlay 1988: fentanyl MEC by PCA after abdominal surgery

## Study
- 30 adults (17 F, 13 M), upper (15) or lower (15) abdominal incision; age 47 +/- 13.9 y (23-74), weight 76 +/- 15 kg
  (49-120) (Table 1). Exclusions: significant renal/hepatic impairment, chronic respiratory disease, opioid
  hypersensitivity, drug abuse.
- Premed diazepam 10 mg PO; thiopental 4-5 mg/kg, succinylcholine, fentanyl 200 ug IV, N2O/O2 2:1, pancuronium,
  enflurane 0.5-1% as needed.
- Postop IV fentanyl by Janssen ODAC: basal 20 ug/h, demand 20 ug, lockout 5 min, max 180 ug/h.
- 5-mL blood samples via a central venous catheter (tip near subclavian vein) drawn immediately before
  patient-initiated demands (not before every demand); heparinised whole blood, -20 C; GLC with nitrogen-phosphorus
  detection (ref 26). Mean 22 samples/patient (12-45), mean duration 50 h (24-72); 657 MEC determinations.
- Regression (PRESS model selection) of log MEC and maintenance dose on anthropometric and psychological scores.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEC (the paper's term; stanpumpR's MEAC)**: mean 0.63 +/- 0.25 ng/mL (mean +/- SD of the 30 patient means),
  five-fold range 0.23-1.18 ng/mL (Table 2, abstract). Defined as the fentanyl concentration in **whole blood**
  drawn immediately before a PCA demand; total (not free) drug; GLC-NPD. Population: adult postoperative abdominal
  surgery patients over 24-72 h. The authors note it marks the concentration at or below which patients request
  more analgesia, not a level above which pain is abolished (p. 334).
- Intrapatient CV of MEC 30.2 +/- 7.7% (16-46%); MEC stable over 48 h while dose rate fell; MEC log-normally
  distributed (Fig 2). Example patient 7: 0.55 +/- 0.13 ng/mL (0.32-0.76).
- No ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis estimated.
- Concentrations cited from others (introduction/discussion, not measured here): loss of consciousness 34 +/- 7,
  EEG 6.9 +/- 1.5, respiratory depression 1-5 ng/mL and "approximately 2 ng/mL" for clinically significant
  depression (Cartwright 1983), intubation response 102 +/- 50, extubation 3, surgical BP <15, Lehmann's ~1 ng/mL
  for postoperative analgesia. No respiratory depression observed in this study.
- No equianalgesic/potency ratio stated.

## Covariates
Best model: log(MEC) = -0.73 + 0.10 x IBQ2 (disease conviction score), adjusted R2 0.08 (log base garbled in the
text layer; derived: natural log fits a mean of 0.63 for plausible IBQ2 scores). Dose requirement (ug/h) = 71 -
5 x IBQ1 + 9 x IBQ2 - 15 x SEX (1 male, 2 female; operator before 5 x IBQ1 partly garbled), adjusted R2 0.19.
Age, weight and incision site not predictive.

## Caveats
Whole-blood, not plasma, concentrations; samples not before every demand (fewer at night); concomitant
benzodiazepines and other drugs in some patients (Table 1); single centre, n = 30; PCA demand reflects pain,
psychology and incident pain, not a fixed effect level.
