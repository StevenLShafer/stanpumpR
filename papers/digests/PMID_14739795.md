# Bouillon 2004: propofol intrinsic ventilatory depression, indirect-response model

## Study
- 10 healthy volunteers (5 men, 5 women), median age 33 y (20-41), weight 70.3 kg (52-120). A subset of the 20-volunteer propofol-remifentanil study of PMID_12459659. Fasted at least 6 h, arterial line, CPAP-style tight mask with pressure-differential spirometer and gas analyser; Bispectral Index recorded (PDF pp. 2-3).
- Step 1: CO2 rebreathing (Read-type, bags initially CO2-free) to define each person's CO2 response, with PaCO2 drawn every 1-2 min and PETCO2 and minute ventilation (VE) every 5 s.
- Step 2 (20 min later): propofol by TCI (STANPUMP, Schnider 1998 kinetics, targeting effect-site concentration) in ascending steps of 15 min each (volunteer 3: 20 min), stopped at PETCO2 > 65 mmHg or apnea > 60 s, then allowed to fall passively to 1 ug/ml. FiO2 1.0. Table 1 (PDF p. 2, printed p. 241), read on the page image: peak target 6-12 ug/ml (6 in two subjects, 8 in two, 9 in four, 12 in two); steps from 1, 2, 4, 8 up to 3, 6, 9, 12 ug/ml; final concentration 1 ug/ml.
- Arterial propofol (plasma) and PaCO2 sampled at 2, 5, 10, 15 min from the start, before each step, and at 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 min after the fall. PaCO2 by i-STAT within 20 min on ice.
- Analysis: NONMEM V FOCE, additive residual error; propofol kinetics taken as Bayesian estimates from PMID_12459659 (V1 3.8 L, V2 31.6 L, V3 209.0 L, Cl1 3.04, Cl2 3.25, Cl3 1.09 L/min; these match Table 3 of that paper). The subject-specific CO2 response parameters (F, ke0,CO2) were estimated first in drug-free rebreathing and then fixed as known in the drug model.

## Model and parameters
Model (PDF pp. 3-4): CO2 mass balance, d PaCO2/dt = [Valv(0) PaCO2(0) (production term) - Valv(t) PaCO2(t)] / VdCO2. Effect-site CO2: dPecCO2/dt = ke0,CO2 (PETCO2 - PecCO2). Nonlinear CO2 drive, VE = VE(0) (PecCO2(t)/PecCO2(0))^F. Propofol effect compartment dCe/dt = ke0,prop (Cp - Ce). Alveolar ventilation falls by the fractional sigmoid Emax term 1 - Ce^g/(C50^g + Ce^g), multiplied by the CO2 drive; CO2 production falls with a negative sigmoid Emax using the same C50 and gamma, to a minimum of 0.7 of baseline (fixed, from Hausmann et al.). A power-function alternative was 79 objective-function points worse and rejected; leaving out the CO2-production term gave a difference of 2.77 (indistinguishable).

Table 2 (PDF p. 5, printed p. 244), CO2 response in the drug-free state, nonlinear model: ke0,CO2 0.95 /min (SE 0.19, CV 59.8%); F 4.37 (SE 0.53, CV 36.7%); VE(0) 6.45 L/min (SE 0.76, CV 36.3%); baseline PETCO2 40.9 mmHg (measured, CV 12.8%). Table 3, linear model (not used): ke0,CO2 0.98 /min; SL 0.99 L/min/mmHg; VE(0) 6.01 L/min. Nonlinear model fit better by 340 objective-function points; mean prediction error 1.44 vs 1.67 L/min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: arterial PaCO2 (mmHg), through the model's fractional alveolar ventilation. VE and PaCO2 were both collected, but only PaCO2 was fitted in the drug model.
- ke0,prop 0.27 /min (Table 4) on the PaCO2 / ventilation end point. The authors state a t1/2 of equilibration of 2.6 min (printed p. 247); ln 2 / 0.27 = 2.57 min (derived), which agrees. No tPeak is reported.
- ke0,CO2 0.95 /min (Table 2) is the PETCO2-to-effect-site CO2 equilibration, t1/2 0.73 min (printed p. 249); it is a CO2 constant, not a drug constant.
- C50 1.33 ug/ml (effect-site, drug in plasma by Schnider-based Bayesian prediction), gamma 1.68. Defined as the effect-site propofol concentration that halves alveolar ventilation at constant PaCO2 (the "intrinsic" potency), not the steady-state clinical effect.
- Steady state without clamped CO2 (printed pp. 247-248): with F = 4.37 the fall in VE at the C50 is only 12.1% if CO2 production is unchanged, and 23% if production falls (equation 18). Concentrations of up to 5 times the C50 were reached in the study.
- Simulation (Fig. 5): a 100 mg bolus depresses ventilation more than an infusion that reaches the same peak, because PaCO2 rises too slowly to oppose the fast onset. The authors advise against extrapolating to untested PaCO2 ranges.
- Sensitivity analysis (Fig. 4): the fit improves with a larger ke0,CO2 and a smaller F; F and C50 are not separable from ventilatory data alone, and ke0,CO2 and F could each change 30-40% without a significant objective-function change.
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, analgesic concentration or opioid potency ratio. No BIS-based ke0 is reported (BIS was recorded but not modelled here).

## Covariates
None modelled for propofol potency. CO2 response parameters are individual Bayesian estimates from the rebreathing step. Interindividual variability is large (CVs 28-60%).

## Caveats
- n = 10 healthy volunteers; wide interindividual variability, so population predictions are poor for individuals (authors, printed p. 245).
- CO2 response curves come from rebreathing, which has been criticised as biased; room air, not oxygen, was given after rebreathing (authors consider the effect negligible by simulation). ke0,CO2 and F were not re-optimised in the drug model.
- Assumes PaCO2 equals effect-site CO2 at baseline (physiologically wrong, authors say) and no hypoxic drive (FiO2 1.0 in the drug phase).
- Propofol plasma concentrations are Bayesian predictions from the PMID_12459659 model, not measured values, in the PD fit.
- The Table 4 footnote contains a typographical error: it calls the fractional sigmoid Emax models "fractional sigmoid maximum ventricular elastance models"; the text makes the intended meaning clear.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- The paper's pharmacokinetics used for the fit are the Schnider 1998 set that the library carries as its companion (alternativeModels.R:219-225: V1 4.27 L, V3 238 L, Cl3 0.836 L/min, with age and size terms for V2, Cl1, Cl2). The typical values this paper quotes (V1 3.8, V2 31.6, V3 209.0, Cl1 3.04, Cl2 3.25, Cl3 1.09; individual Bayesian estimates from that model drove the fit) come from the same laboratory's PMID_12459659 data, not from the Schnider set, so the library's model is not the model used for the PD fit; any use of C50 1.33 ug/ml or ke0,prop 0.27 /min with the library's propofol model would transfer a potency fitted to a different disposition.
- ke0: the library fixes tPeak 1.6 min for propofol (drugs_propofol.R:276). This paper's ke0,prop 0.27 /min (t1/2 2.6 min) is for ventilation/PaCO2; the tPeak it corresponds to on the library's disposition was not computed here, so whether it is faster or slower than 1.6 min is not established by this card.
- No code applies a propofol ventilatory C50, so there is nothing to transcribe. Verdict: not-applicable.
