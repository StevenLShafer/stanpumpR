# FDA 2008: lacosamide Clinical Pharmacology review, NDA 22-253/22-254, Part 3

## Study
A regulatory document, not a single study. This PDF (100 scanned pages) holds three pieces:
1. PDF pp 1-20 (sponsor-file pages 251-270 of the DPN-indication review): the end of the QT Interdisciplinary Review Team
   report on the thorough QT study SP640. Healthy volunteers, n = 247 randomized (placebo 62, lacosamide 400 mg/day 60,
   800 mg/day 71, moxifloxacin 54); mean age 24.7 y, weight 72.53 kg, 54.7% female (Table 4, p 252). No 1-sided 95%
   upper bound for lacosamide minus placebo change in QTcI exceeded 10 ms (p 260). Signed 25 Jul 2007 (p 270).
2. PDF pp 21-24: OCP filing/review form for NDAs 22-253/22-254 (DPN and partial-onset seizures; reviewer E O Fadiran);
   BCS class 1; 33 studies.
3. PDF pp 25-100: review pages 1-76 of a 174-page Clinical Pharmacology/Biopharmaceutics Review for the partial-seizure
   indication (reviewer V Tandon; team leader R Uppoor; pharmacometrics H Zhu / J Gobburu). Submission dates 9/28/07,
   11/26/07, 12/13/07. Many summary items are carried over from the Fadiran/Zhang DPN reviews.
Assay: LC/MS/MS in plasma, LLOQ 0.01 ug/mL for lacosamide and SPM 12809, linear 0.01-10 ug/mL (SP757 synopsis, review
p 113, PDF p 100). Concentrations are total plasma; protein binding < 15% (review p 10).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Exposure-response for efficacy (Dr Zhu's pharmacometric review, summarised review pp 6, 21-22): responder population
  of SP667, SP754, SP755 (subjects with no evaluable baseline slope or baseline-slope R^2 < 0.95 excluded). Exposure =
  steady-state AUC over the 12-h dosing interval; response = change from baseline in average daily number of partial
  seizures; an Emax model fitted by nonlinear least squares (P < 0.0001). Only the fitted curves are shown (review p 22,
  end of titration and end of maintenance; y axis about 0 to -70, x axis AUC 0-250); **no Emax, EC50/AUC50 or baseline
  coefficients are printed.** Conclusion: the curve flattens beyond the median 400 mg exposure; 600 mg adds no benefit.
- Median percent seizure reduction per 28 days (review p 22): E1 placebo 10%, 200/400/600 mg 26/39/40%; E2 placebo 20.8%,
  400/600 37.3/37.8%; E3 placebo 20.5%, 200/400 35.3/36.4%.
- Primary FDA end point: change in partial seizure frequency per 28 days, baseline to maintenance (review p 20).
- QT: no QTc prolongation signal at 400 or 800 mg/day; sponsor reported a small concentration-related PR increase
  (figure redacted, p 260).
- No ke0, effect-site, therapeutic range, MEAC or toxic concentration is given; not an opioid.

## Covariates
- Renal (NCA, review pp 34-37): AUC +27% / +23% / +59% in mild / moderate / severe impairment; CL/F 2.13 (normal), 1.68,
  1.74, 1.34 L/h; CLR 0.59 to 0.14 L/h; t1/2 13.22 to 18.30 h. SPM 12809 AUC 4.6-fold in severe. Hemodialysis removes
  ~50% (extraction 57.4%, CLdial ~140 mL/min). Highest doses in severe impairment to be reduced (fraction redacted).
- Hepatic, moderate (Child-Pugh B): AUC 161% of normal, t1/2 24.1 vs 14.8 h (review p 38).
- Age: elderly ~30% higher AUC (20-25% after weight); PopPK shows no age trend in patients (p 39-40).
- Sex: elderly women ~15% higher AUC than elderly men, gone after weight normalisation; PopPK: sex does not influence
  exposure in partial-seizure patients (review p 40).
- Race: Asian/Black ~10% higher AUC, similar after weight normalisation (p 41).
- CYP2C19 PM vs EM: lacosamide AUC +6 to +10%, SPM 12809 -76 to -81% (p 26).
- Co-medication: carbamazepine, valproate formal DDI no effect; sponsor PopPK 15-20% lower exposure with carbamazepine,
  phenytoin, phenobarbital, which the pharmacometric reviewer judged confounded and not clinically meaningful (p 9, 46).

## Caveats
- Scanned, heavily redacted (b(4)) pages; parts of the BE section and labeling are struck out.
- The pharmacometric (Zhu) review itself is not in this part; the Emax model appears only as a figure.
- PopPK values are by-study typical values without a covariate model; healthy NCA half-life (13 h) differs from patient
  PopPK half-life (21-24 h).
