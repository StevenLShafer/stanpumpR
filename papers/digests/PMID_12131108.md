# Heier 2002: neostigmine pharmacokinetics and time course in normothermic and hypothermic volunteers

## Study
- 7 healthy, non-obese, unpremedicated volunteers (1 woman, 6 men), 23-39 y, 58-79 kg, each studied twice 1 week apart: normothermia (>36.5 °C) and mild hypothermia (<34.5 °C), random order (UCSF).
- Anaesthesia: alfentanil 30 ug/kg + propofol 3 mg/kg; N2O 60-70% + isoflurane 0.7-0.9% end-tidal.
- Vecuronium 0.03 mg/kg bolus then infusion titrated to steady T1 5 ± 1% (adductor pollicis, mechanomyography, TOF every 12 s), held constant.
- Neostigmine **70 ug/kg over 2 min** (after glycopyrrolate 14 ug/kg). Venous samples before and at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 30, 60, 90, 120, 240 min. HPLC, LOQ 10 ng/mL, linear 10-1000 ng/mL, CV 5%.
- NONMEM population model (2 vs 3 compartments, weight-normalized or not, eta structure, temperature effects on Cl, Q, V1, Vss).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Table 1, median (range), normothermia vs hypothermia:
- Onset (time from injection of neostigmine to maximum T1 effect): **4.6 min** (4.0-5.8) vs 5.6 (4.8-6.1), P < 0.05.
- Onset for TOF ratio: 4.3 vs 5.5 min.
- Maximum T1 effect 77% vs 85%; maximum TOF ratio 54 vs 51 (n.s.).
- Duration (to 50% decline of maximum effect), T1: 66 vs 70 min.
- End point: antagonism of a steady vecuronium block (T1 5%), adductor pollicis mechanomyography. No ke0, EC50 or concentration-effect model fitted. Effective vecuronium concentration for 95% block 272 ± 92 (normo) vs 236 ± 80 ng/mL (hypo).
- Not an opioid; no MEAC; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Temperature on V1 (−38%). Weight tested and not retained.

## Caveats
n = 7; venous sampling with first sample at 1 min; large dose likely produced maximal reversal; steady-state block (not spontaneous recovery); "onset" measured from injection of a 2-min infusion.
