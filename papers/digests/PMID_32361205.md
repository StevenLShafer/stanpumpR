# Moseley 2020: drug-drug interactions of brivaracetam (review)

## Study
Narrative review (UCB Pharma-funded; four of six authors UCB employees; medical-writing support paid by UCB) of
in vitro and clinical drug-drug interaction data for brivaracetam (BRV), mostly summarising sponsor studies. No new
data; no PK model fitted. Printed pages = PDF pages (article number 106327, pp. 1-8).

Background PK summarised (p. 2, from cited sources): nearly complete oral bioavailability; protein binding < 20%;
V 0.49 ± 0.05 L/kg; > 90% metabolised to inactive metabolites: about 56% by amidase hydrolysis (carboxylic acid),
28% by CYP2C19 hydroxylation (hydroxy-BRV); ~95% of a dose renally excreted in 72 h, 9% as parent; linear PK
10-600 mg single and 100-400 mg bid multiple doses; oral 50/100 mg tablets bioequivalent to 100 mg IV bolus.
Children ≥ 4 y: 2.0 mg/kg bid gives exposure like 100 mg bid in adults. Severe renal impairment AUC +21% (no
adjustment); hepatic impairment (any severity) exposure +50-60%, dose reduction.

Gemfibrozil: no effect on BRV. Oral contraceptives: no effect on BRV; BRV 400 mg/day lowered ethinylestradiol and
levonorgestrel AUC by 27% and 23%, no effect at 100 mg/day (Table 2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No concentration-effect, therapeutic range or ke0 reported. Clinical relevance of the inducer effect stated
as no significant influence on clinical response (pop-PK study cited). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
CYP2C19 genotype (PM exposure x1.4, p. 4); co-medication with inducers (above); hepatic impairment (+50-60%).

## Caveats
- Sponsor-authored review; nearly all numbers are second-hand from cited UCB studies, several "data on file".
- Table 1 reports the inducer effects as "↓ AUC" whereas the text says "decreased brivaracetam levels"; the source
  is a population-PK covariate analysis, so these are concentration/CL-derived, not AUC from dedicated studies.
- The 26% is carbamazepine alone; phenytoin is 21% and phenobarbital/primidone 19%.
- The text attributes the carbamazepine effect to induced CYP activity in general; only the rifampin effect is
  attributed specifically to CYP2C19 induction.
