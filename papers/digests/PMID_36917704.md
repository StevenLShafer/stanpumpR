# Krzyzanski 2023: hybrid minimal PBPK model of dexamethasone and betamethasone in pregnancy

## Study
A top-down meta-analysis of literature data on women at term. The authors searched for maternal and fetal data on
dexamethasone (DEX) and betamethasone (BET) in parturient women, digitised the published curves and pooled them
naively, without individual information. The data were:
- maternal plasma time courses: n = 71 observations for DEX and 333 for BET;
- single umbilical-vein concentrations at birth: n = 14 for DEX and 232 for BET (PDF pp 3-4).

The sources, formulations, routes and regimens are listed in Table S1, which is not in the PDF. The DEX doses were
dexamethasone phosphate (DEX-P), given IV, IM and orally (8 mg PO, 6 mg IM and 8 mg IV/IM appear in the text). BET
was given as the phosphate (BET-P) or as a 1:1 phosphate/acetate mixture (BET-PA).

The model was fitted in NONMEM 7.4 (FOCE) to log-transformed concentrations, with additive residual error set
separately for maternal and fetal DEX and BET. The global sensitivity analysis was run in MATLAB SimBiology.

## Model and parameters
The model has three parts:
- Mother: a two-tissue minimal PBPK model (adapted from Song and Jusko's across-species DEX mPBPK model) with
  plasma/blood, tissue 1 (well perfused) and tissue 2, and one tissue-to-plasma partition coefficient Kpm.
- Fetus: a one-tissue mPBPK model (fetal blood and fetus).
- Placenta: a single compartment linking the two (based on Szeto), with a placental efflux clearance CLp
  (permeability plus P-gp).

Physiological values are taken from the literature for gestational age 39 weeks (Table S2). Blood-to-plasma ratios
are fixed at 1. Fetal clearance is fixed as a fraction of maternal clearance, from a 33.8-fold ratio of CYP3A4
(maternal) to CYP3A7 (fetal) intrinsic clearance. The other parameters are estimated jointly for both drugs, and
fdm1, Ftm1 and Kpm are shared between DEX and BET.

Simulated, model-derived quantities (Results, PDF pp 5-6; Table 2, PDF p 10):
- 8 mg DEX-P IV: maternal Cmax 145.2 ng/mL at 0 h. Fetal concentration has an immediate peak of 24.7 ng/mL and a
  second peak of 28.0 ng/mL at 0.4 h.
- 8 mg DEX-P IM: maternal Cmax 61.3 ng/mL at 0.9 h; fetal 17.0 ng/mL at 1.6 h.
- 8 mg DEX-P PO: maternal 41.8 ng/mL at 1.6 h; fetal 12.1 ng/mL at 2.0 h.
- Terminal half-lives come from the simulated slope at t = 200 h: DEX-P 8.3 h, BET-P 16.5 h, BET-PA 117 h, the same
  in mother and fetus.
- Fetal:maternal ratio for DEX-P peaks at 0.40 (IV, 2.4 h) and 0.33 (IM, 5 h), then plateaus at 0.311 from about
  20 h. BET-P plateaus at 0.282. Fetal:maternal AUC0-72 is about 0.28 for every regimen.
- Table 2, WHO regimens: DEX-P 6 mg IM 4 x 12 h gives maternal Cmax 50.8, fetal Cmax 14.2, maternal trough 5.2 and
  fetal trough 1.6 ng/mL, maternal AUC0-72 879 and fetal AUC0-72 266 ng/mL·h. The BET-P 12 mg 2 x 24 h row is 92.0,
  25.3, 17.6, 5.0, 2390 and 676. Against nonpregnant women given the same regimens, simulated maternal Cmax is
  20-50% lower and AUC0-72 is 40-60% lower (PDF p 11).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model is fitted here. The paper mentions:
- a suggested target of fetal BET above 1 ng/mL for 48 h;
- the group's earlier IC50s in nonpregnant women, by which DEX was on average 1.8 times more potent than BET (cortisol,
  lymphocyte, neutrophil and glucose responses; PDF p 11).

There is no ke0 and no MEAC.

## Covariates
None. All parameters are for term pregnancy (GA 39 weeks). The authors say GA could be added as a covariate.

## Caveats
- The pooled data are naive pooled and digitised. DEX data are limited (71 maternal and 14 fetal observations), and
  DEX maternal concentrations are modestly underpredicted after 6 mg IM and 8 mg PO (PDF p 5).
- Two pairs of parameters are highly correlated, about 0.9: Kpm with CLp_DEX, and ka_IMa with F_IMa.
- Possible DEX autoinduction of CYP3A4 is not modelled.
- F was fixed at the upper bound of 1.
- The higher Kpm (2.37 against a "previous" 1.07) is offered by the authors with alternative explanations: less
  plasma binding, more tissue binding, transporters, or an oversimplified structure. The source of the 1.07 is not
  cited in that sentence.
- The comparator clearances for nonpregnant women, DEX 9.5 L/h and BET 6.5 L/h, come from a separate study by the
  same group (ref 27, Jobe 2020, IM and oral dosing in Indian women of reproductive age), not from this fit.
- The authors set their DEX clearance (26.7 L/h) beside Tsuei's 33.5 L/h in parturients (p 9).
- The text compares the oral ka (0.446/h) with a nonpregnant value of 0.936/h, but the reference number given (28) is
  Giaginis's placental-transfer QSAR paper. The IM comparator (0.460/h) is cited to Krzyzanski 2021 (ref 31).
- The main text does not say whether doses were entered as phosphate or as base.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **26.7 L/h, 9.5 L/h, Kpm 2.37 vs 1.07** (`drugs_dexamethasone.R:57-60`, registry :58): **matches** Table 1 and the
  Discussion (PDF pp 8-9). 26.7/9.5 = 2.81 is derived, not printed in the paper. The paper itself says the volume of
  distribution increases in pregnancy (p 9).
- **"Krzyzanski's 2.81 within-study ratio"** (`drugs_dexamethasone.R:64`): **discrepancy (wording)**. The 9.5 L/h is
  not from this study. It is the nonpregnant value from a separate study by the same group (ref 27), so 2.81 is a
  cross-study ratio. The registry says this correctly ("separate non-pregnant study by the same group"). The factor
  of 2 itself is not affected.
- **Absorption figures not applied** (`drugs_dexamethasone.R:77-79`, registry :58): **matches** (0.893, 0.460, 0.446,
  0.936/h). The 0.936/h comparator is attributed to ref 28 in this paper, a reference mismatch noted under Caveats.
  The library uses the value from Krzyzanski 2021, which was not checked here.
- **"F about 1"** (registry :58): **matches**, with the qualification that F was fixed at 1 after the estimates reached
  the bound.
- **Absolute ratio 1.48 against Hong's 18.1 L/h** (registry :58): arithmetic **matches** (26.7/18.1 = 1.475). The 18.1
  L/h is from Hong 2007 and cannot be verified from this paper.
- **The library's x2 CL and x2 V, half the nonpregnant concentration at every time**: consistent with the paper's
  simulated 40-60% lower AUC0-72 and 20-50% lower Cmax for WHO regimens (p 11). Note, though, that this model's
  simulated terminal half-life for DEX-P is 8.3 h, a deep-tissue phase evaluated at 200 h. That is not the 3.68 h
  (Hong) or the 142 min (Tsuei) the header relies on. The header does not cite Krzyzanski for half-life, so this is
  context rather than a discrepancy.
- **Reference entry** (`drugs_dexamethasone.R:100-102`): authors, journal, 2023;12:668-680 and the DOI **match**.
