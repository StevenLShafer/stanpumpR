# Weiss 2004: slow receptor binding explains digoxin's inotropic delay

## Study
Reanalysis of published mean data (no new subjects). (1) Kramer 1979: 1.0 mg IV bolus in 12 healthy men; 23 serum samples and 15 measurements of the heart-rate-corrected shortening of electromechanical systole (delta QS2c) over 4 days. (2) Weiss 1983 concentration clamp: six healthy men, a 100 mcg bolus plus constant and biexponential infusions holding about 4.2 ng/mL for 4 h; 8 concentrations and 16 responses. Plasma curves used as forcing functions: Kramer's refitted as C(t) = 57.3 e^-0.164t + 9.99 e^-0.011t + 0.74 e^-0.00024t (ng/mL, t in min) (PDF p3 / printed 233). ADAPT II, maximum likelihood, simultaneous fit of the two mean data sets; AIC.

## Model and parameters
Mechanistic heart model (Fig 1): vascular and interstitial compartments and saturable single-site receptor binding; effect E = e x DR. Fixed from anatomy or rat-heart work: Vvas 0.06 mL/g, heart 300 g, functional flow QD about 100 mL/min (plasma flow x fu 75%), kvi 41.2 /min, kiv 8.91 /min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: inotropy, measured as shortening of electromechanical systole (delta QS2c, ms), mean data, healthy men. The empirical effect-compartment (link) model fitted to the bolus gives tau 18.7 h, i.e. **t1/2ke0 about 13 h** (agrees with Holford and Sheiner's 14 h on the same data; Kelman and Whiting's 4 h came from 12-h sampling). Under the concentration clamp the link model's tau falls tenfold (1.5 h), so the link model's delay depends on the dosing schedule. The mechanistic model attributes the delay to slow receptor binding, not to transport (Fig 5). Its time constant for a step is 1/(kon x Dis,0 + koff), dose-dependent, and approaches 1/koff (about 34 h, derived) at low concentrations. At 0.5-1.5 ng/mL the mechanistic model predicts 65-85% of maximal inotropic effect (p4). A linear receptor-binding model fitted worse (dAIC 45). No ventricular-rate end point.

## Covariates
None (mean data).

## Caveats
- Fitted to mean data from two small historical studies of healthy men; four parameters fixed from rat-heart or anatomical values; Rtot not identifiable (fixed). The authors call their approach forward modelling, aimed at insight rather than exact estimates.
- Failing hearts (down-regulated Na/K-ATPase) may differ (Discussion).
