# Biswas 2013: closed-loop (CLADS) vs manual propofol in paediatric cardiac surgery

## Study
Randomised, open, two-arm trial (NCT01600287), PGIMER Chandigarh. 40 children aged 5-18 y, ASA II-III, elective open-heart surgery on CPB, 20 closed-loop (CL) and 20 manual (Fig 2). Table 1: age 11.1±5.1 (CL) vs 12±4.1 y; M:F 9:11 vs 11:9 (column placement of the other rows is scrambled in the text layer). Premedication midazolam 0.25 mg/kg; fentanyl 3 ug/kg before induction, then 1 ug/kg/h plus 1 ug/kg boluses; vecuronium 0.1 mg/kg. Target BIS 50; end of induction = two consecutive BIS <=55. The controller (CLADS) is described as a PK-PD model-based adaptive PID system on BIS with a paediatric algorithm (authors say higher V and clearance in children give higher doses and maximum rates); no PK parameters are given. A safety cutoff stops propofol when MAP/HR fall below set limits.

Manual-group rate changes: median 18/h (range 8-29); CL none. Pilot: manual control kept BIS 50±10 for 65% of the time; sample size 32 (80% power) was based on a 15-point gain. BIS within ±10 was similar between groups (Table 2; per-period values not readable in the text layer).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
BIS (target 50) only. No ke0, EC50 or concentration-effect parameters estimated. The authors cite Munoz 2006 and Rigouzzo for BIS correlating with estimated propofol concentration in children over 2 y.

## Covariates
Age, weight, height and risk class (low, high, very high, paediatric) are controller inputs; no covariate equations given.

## Caveats
n=20 per arm, unblinded, possible Hawthorne effect in the manual arm; CPB and hypothermia alter propofol PK/PD; dose basis mg/kg (weight type not stated); fentanyl co-administered; surrogate safety outcomes only.
