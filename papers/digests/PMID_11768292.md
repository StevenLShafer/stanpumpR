# Beal 2001: fitting PK models with BQL data

## Study
Simulation methods paper, no drug and no patients. Compares seven ways of handling concentrations reported below the quantification limit (BQL), for single-subject and population data (NONMEM; first-order conditional estimation for M1, M5-M7, Laplacian for M2-M4).

- M1: discard BQL observations.
- M2: discard them, but maximise the likelihood conditional on being above QL (Eqs. 1-2, pp. 484-485).
- M3: BQL treated as censored, likelihood = P(below QL) (Eqs. 3-6, p. 485).
- M4: as M3 but conditioned on the value being positive (Eqs. 7-10, p. 486).
- M5: BQL replaced by QL/2.
- M6: as M5, but later consecutive BQL values are discarded.
- M7: BQL replaced by 0.

Simulation (pp. 488-492): single dose, one-compartment, f(t) = (1/Vd)exp(-CL t), CL = 0.693 and Vd = 1 (time in half-lives); observations at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3; QL 0.125 (about 3 half-lives) or 0.25 (a serious mismatch). Four simulation error models SI1-SI4 (truncated-normal or lognormal, constant SD 0.02 / 0.05 or constant CV 15%), two analysis variance models (homogeneous DA1, constant CV DA2). Assay CV at QL is at most 20% (the FDA guidance). Single-subject: 250 data sets per combination. Population: 20 individuals per set, 150 sets, geometric SD of CL 0.2 or 0.4 and of Vd 0.2.

Conclusions (pp. 503-504): M7 (substitute 0) should not be used; M3 and M4 are somewhat better than M1 and M2 (M3 and M4 results are identical at the displayed precision, p. 494); M5/M6 can improve the typical-value estimate but badly degrade estimates of the population variance of a parameter; when the BQL fraction is small, discarding BQL data (M1) does about as well; the extra gain of censored-likelihood methods is often small, and M2-M4 sometimes failed to converge in population fits (about 5-10% of replicates in some cases, pp. 496-497).

## Caveats
Authors' limits (pp. 502-503): one-compartment model only, moderate data per individual (6 samples), assay CV at QL assumed at most 20%, no structural misspecification, little work with few samples per individual; M2-M4 depend on a normality assumption that was not tested against non-normal errors.
