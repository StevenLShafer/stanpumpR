# FDA 2008: lacosamide NDA 22-253/22-254 Clin Pharm review, part 4 (incl. pharmacometric review pp. 1-22)

## Study
A regulatory review, not a single study. What this part contains:
- **Pharmacometric review (PM pp. 1-22)**. Indications: adjunctive therapy for partial-onset seizures, age 16 and older (tablets, NDA 22253), and diabetic neuropathic pain (DPN). The proposed regimen starts at 100 mg/day BID and rises by 100 mg/day each week to 100-400 mg/day. The maximum dose and the IV infusion time are redacted (b(4)). Three efficacy trials in partial seizures:
  - SP667 (phase IIb): 200/400/600 mg/day; 421 randomized and dosed.
  - SP754 and SP755: SP755 tested placebo, 200 and 400 mg/day, with 485 randomized, an 8-week baseline, 4-week titration, 12-week maintenance and a 2-week transition or taper.
  - Three DPN trials: SP742 (200/400/600 mg/day, 370 dosed), SP743 (400/600 mg/day) and SP768 (469 randomized).
- **Population PK reports** (Table 1, PM p. 18): SP640 and SP620 (healthy), SP754 and SP755 (partial seizures), SP665, SP742 and SP743 (DPN). E-R reports: SPM927 (seizures, from SP667/754/755) and SPM929 (DPN, SP742).
- **SP640 (PM pp. 19-22)**: phase 1 thorough-QT trial, 400 or 800 mg/day (200 or 400 mg BID) for 6 days. PopPK used 1701 concentrations from 123 subjects (median 15 samples each). Assay LC/MS, LOQ 0.1 µg/mL. One-compartment model with first-order absorption (ADVAN2), NONMEM IV, FO method.
- **Clin Pharm review pages**:
  - IV vs oral bridging in patients: 10-, 15-, 30- and 60-min infusions.
  - Ascending single IV doses of 50-300 mg over 10 min in healthy subjects.
  - DDI studies with valproate and with carbamazepine (healthy, 200 mg BID).

## Model and parameters
SP640 base model (Table 2, PM p. 21-22): one compartment, Ka fixed at 4.00 /h, log-normal IIV on Ke and V/f, proportional residual error. The final covariate model (Table 3) is on pages not in this file.

Covariates screened (PM p. 20): age, sex, height, weight, BMI, LBW, CLcr, liver tests. LBW and CLcr equations:
- LBW, men: 1.10·WT − 0.0128·BMI·WT
- LBW, women: 1.07·WT − 0.0148·BMI·WT
- CLcr: Cockcroft-Gault (140 − age)·WT/(72·Scr), ×0.85 for women. The printed denominator reads "72 − S" (a typo).

Selection: forward inclusion at ΔOBF > 3.841, backward elimination at ΔOBF ≥ 7.88.

IV vs oral (review pp. 123, 130). With dose- and weight-normalized means, the IV peak is higher and earlier: Cmax at the end of a 30- or 60-min infusion, versus 2 h after an oral dose. From 4 to 12 h the oral concentrations are slightly higher. The reviewers judged the two routes similar overall.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Seizure exposure-response** (PM pp. 6-7, Figure 3). The FDA reviewer fitted an Emax model by nonlinear least squares:
  - Exposure: steady-state AUC over the 12-h dosing interval.
  - Response: change from baseline in the average daily number of partial seizures, in per cent.
  - Data: SP667, SP754 and SP755. Only "responders" were kept. The sponsor's rule dropped patients with no evaluable baseline slope or baseline R² < 0.95, and also dropped placebo.
  - The curves are steep and flatten beyond the median exposure at 400 mg. The plateau, read off the figure, is about −65 to −68% at the end of titration and about −55% at the end of maintenance. The arrows for 200, 400 and 600 mg sit near AUC12 of about 45, 90 and 120 (read from the figure; the axis has no unit).
  - **No Emax, EC50 or Hill coefficients are printed in this part.** The reviewer's detailed E-R section (4.1) falls after p. 22, outside this file.
  - Conclusion: 600 mg adds no benefit over 400 mg.
- **DPN exposure-response**: redacted (b(4)) throughout, including the executive summary and question 3.
- **Peak saccadic velocity** (CBZ DDI, review pp. 165-167): lacosamide 200 mg BID alone had no significant effect. With carbamazepine, the largest mean fall was 11% on day 8 (−55.5 deg/s, p = 0.064).
- No ke0, no effect-site model, no therapeutic range. Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
- **Age and sex** (PM pp. 5-6, Figs 1-2): no trend in the IIV of V or Ke against age or sex (SP754, SP755). Exposure is not affected.
- **Enzyme inducers** (PM pp. 8-9): the sponsor's popPK found lower exposure with carbamazepine, phenobarbital or phenytoin. The FDA reviewer found this hard to interpret:
  - carbamazepine alone was not significant;
  - 55 of the 69 SP754 patients on carbamazepine also took other AEDs;
  - the healthy-volunteer DDI study showed no change.
  - The worst case was about 20% lower exposure, judged not clinically meaningful.
- **Elimination**: about 40% renal as parent drug. Lacosamide is a CYP2C19 substrate.

## Caveats
- This is part 4 of a multi-part file. The SP640 final model (Table 3), the patient popPK results (SP754/755, DPN) and the reviewer's full E-R analysis come later in the review and are not here.
- The base model is from healthy subjects only. FO estimation.
- The Emax analysis is limited to "responders", which biases the response upward.
- The AUC axis in Figure 3 has no unit.
