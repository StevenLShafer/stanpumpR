# Samer 2010 (PD paper): CYP2D6/CYP3A and oxycodone analgesia, miosis and toxicity

## Study
Same 10 healthy men and 5-arm crossover as the companion PK paper (PMID 20590587): placebo, oxycodone 0.2 mg/kg oral
(mean 15.3 mg) alone or after quinidine 100 mg, ketoconazole 400 mg, or both; naloxone 0.8 mg IV at 90 min in every
session. Table 1: ages 21-48 (mean 26.2), 65-90 kg (76.4); CYP2D6 6 EM, 2 UM (*41/*41xN, *1/*2xN), 1 IM (*5/*41),
1 PM (*4/*4); IM and PM analysed together as "PM". PD at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 6 h: nociceptive flexion reflex (sural
nerve electrical stimulation), subjective pain threshold (SPT), cold pressor test (≤ 2 min), thermode thresholds,
dark-adapted pupil (NeurOptics PLR-100), DSST, sedation, SpO2, spontaneous ADRs. AUEC90 and Emax; Spearman and linear
mixed-effect models relating PD to PK.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No MEAC, no EC50, no ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak (naloxone at 90 min truncates the time course).
- CYP2D6 phenotype: cold pressor Emax and AUEC90 correlated with CYP2D6 activity (rS 0.816, 0.612); UM 40% higher pain
  tolerance than EM (AUEC90 3735 vs 2710 s·min; Emax 61 vs 43 s); SPT peak 34.6 mA UM vs 14.1 EM; PM showed trivial
  antinociception. Pupil: no significant effect in PM; AUEC90 -167 vs -106 mm·min UM vs EM (p = 0.038).
- Oxymorphone Cmax correlated with SPT (rS 0.7, abstract); oxymorphone AUC90 was the only independent predictor of SPT
  AUEC90 (estimate 17.9) and saturation AUEC90 (-1.218); pupil AUEC90 predicted by oxycodone (-0.02) and oxymorphone
  (-0.55).
- Quinidine: SPT reduced by 30%, similar to placebo; ketoconazole: SPT +15%, sedation AUEC90 +80%, NFR +15%.
- ADRs (Table 2): 60 in total; all UM reported ADRs after oxycodone alone; none in PM; most after ketoconazole.
- Potency statements (introduction, cited): oxymorphone 14-fold more potent than oxycodone; µ affinity 40× oxycodone,
  3× morphine; noroxymorphone 3× oxycodone and 10× noroxycodone.

## Covariates
CYP2D6 phenotype; CYP3A/CYP2D6 inhibition.

## Caveats
n = 10 (2 UM, 1 PM + 1 IM); men only; experimental pain; naloxone at 90 min in every arm; AUEC90 only; multiple
comparisons; conclusions about oxymorphone as the active species conflict with Lalovic 2006's miosis PK-PD (the
authors discuss this).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- R/drugs_oxycodone.R:195-198 attributes to "Samer 2010" that oxymorphone peak concentration was 62% lower in poor than
  extensive and 75% lower than ultrarapid metabolisers. This PD paper reports no oxymorphone Cmax values or percentages
  (it refers to the PK paper for them). The numbers are in the companion paper, Br J Pharmacol 2010;160:907-918
  (PMID 20590587, doi 10.1111/j.1476-5381.2010.00673.x). The reference block at R/drugs_oxycodone.R:216-217 therefore
  cites the wrong one of the two papers. **discrepancy** (citation only; the values themselves are correct, see
  PMID_20590587.md).
- tests/testthat/test-drugs-oxymorphone.R:130 "Samer 2010" short form: ambiguous between the two papers. **cannot verify**
  against this paper.
- Not used by the model, but relevant to the opioid audit: this paper argues oxymorphone drives analgesia, whereas the
  model (following Lalovic 2006) gives oxycodone its own effect site and treats oxymorphone as a separate drug row.
