# Irestedt 1997: ropivacaine 7.5 mg/mL epidural for elective caesarean section, 150 vs 187.5 mg

## Study
- Non-randomised, open, consecutive-group pilot; 16 healthy full-term women, elective caesarean under epidural. 8 received 20 mL (150 mg, "LD"), then 8 received 25 mL (187.5 mg, "HD") of ropivacaine HCl 7.5 mg/mL, injected over 5 min via an L3-L4 catheter after a 4 mL lidocaine-epinephrine test dose.
- Demographics (patient characteristics table, text layer): age 34 (6) and 34 (5) y; height 169 (4) and 166 (4) cm; weight 79 (9) and 76 (14) kg; 6 multiparous per group. Mean (SD).
- Maternal venous samples before the dose, 10, 20, 30, 40, 60 min and 2, 4, 8, 12 h after the end of injection, and at delivery; umbilical vein and artery at delivery. Time from injection to delivery 41 (5) min LD and 48 (16) min HD.
- Assays: total ropivacaine base in plasma by GC with nitrogen detector, LOQ 10 ug/L (CV 6%); free (unbound) by ultrafiltration and coupled-column LC-UV, LOQ 3 ug/L; AAG by radioimmunodiffusion. Samples adjusted to pH 7.4 (stated in Discussion).
- PK: individual non-compartmental analysis. Terminal t1/2 by log-linear regression; CL = dose/AUC, AUC by linear trapezoid to the last point plus extrapolation. Dose used was ropivacaine base 132.4 mg or 165.5 mg (equal to 150 or 187.5 mg HCl). fu = Cfree/Ctotal.

Other: AAG about 12 umol/L maternal before and 12 h after dosing, 4-5 umol/L in umbilical samples. UV/MV total 0.19-0.76 (LD), 0.20-0.61 (HD); UV(u)/MV(u) 0.59-1.18 and 0.69-0.97; UA/UV 0.64-1.12 and 0.59-1.02, rising with time from injection to delivery (P<0.01; Figure 4). CV of PK variables about 20-30% (Discussion).
Clinical (text): max sensory level T5-T2 with LD; HD four blocks above T1 (C8 x3, C7 x1), all four needed ephedrine 10-20 mg (derived: 4 of 8 HD patients); no ephedrine or hypotension in LD. Pain: one LD and two HD patients reported any pain; fentanyl rescue to one patient in each group. Apgar, umbilical pH and NACS normal in both groups. Motor block regression about 4 h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, tPeak for an effect site, EC50 or MEAC. Clinical end points were sensory block height (cold stimulation), Bromage motor score and NRS 0-100 for pain and discomfort; no concentration-effect relation fitted. The authors compare maternal free concentrations (peak 0.11 and 0.14 mg/L) with a reported CNS-toxicity threshold of 0.6 mg/L (range 0.3-0.9; ref 25, intravenous infusion in volunteers; presumably unbound, not stated here). Not an opioid; fentanyl is only rescue.

## Covariates
Dose (150 vs 187.5 mg) and time to delivery on umbilical/maternal ratios by ANOVA; no covariate model. Pregnancy (term) and surgery are the setting, with no non-pregnant control group.

## Caveats
n = 8 per group, non-randomised, consecutive groups; no intravenous reference, so CL and t1/2 are apparent (absorption-limited: terminal t1/2 6-7 h against about 2 h after IV, ref 20, per the authors). Total concentration reported; free fraction measured at pH 7.4 which may underestimate neonatal free exposure.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Registry text says parturient studies, including Irestedt 1997 n=16, report only that clearance was "similar to values reported previously". **Matches**: n = 16 (8+8), and the Discussion says apparent CL was in the range reported after epidural dosing in non-pregnant (ref 22) and pregnant patients (ref 6). No pregnant vs non-pregnant comparison within the paper. The registry's n 36 is the three studies combined.
- Registry says "unbound fraction likely higher at term (lower AAG); library plots total". Paper: AAG about 12 umol/L in the mothers at term and 4-5 in neonates; fu at Cmax 9.5-9.6%; neonatal fu 22-27%. Direction supported for neonates; the paper has no non-pregnant AAG or fu comparator, so "higher at term" in the mother is not tested here (that rests on Porter 2001, not read).
- Registry evidence_read "abstract" and 'confidence low': the full text agrees with what is cited.
- Uncited cross-check against `R/drugs_ropivacaine.R`: library CL 0.300 L/min (older-adult anchor from Simon 2006, then FFM-scaled; the paper's CL 330/291 mL/min is apparent epidural CL on base dose with F assumed 1) is the same size, consistent with the paper's own remark that CL lay in the earlier range. The library's pure intravenous t1/2 (1.7 h cited) differs from the 5.7-7.1 h here because this is epidural absorption-limited decline; the library's first-order depot ka_RA 0.004749/min (header lines 39-45) implies absorption half-time 146 min, a different scale from this study's tmax of 35-45 min after epidural injection (Simon's two-process epidural input, header lines 51-53, is what this paper's fast peak resembles). Library ka was fitted to axillary block, not epidural. Dose basis (HCl vs base) is an open question above.
