# Drover 2004: propofol-remifentanil interaction in children (EGD)

## Study
- Single-centre (Stanford) clinical dose-finding study, 32 children aged 3-10 y (Table 1, PDF p2) scheduled for esophagogastroduodenoscopy. Exclusions: obesity (weight-for-height >95th percentile), significant cardiac, metabolic, hepatic or renal disease, propofol allergy. No premedication; EMLA at the IV site; no pharyngeal topical anaesthesia.
- Groups (consecutive enrolment): group 1 propofol alone (n=12, the Hammer 2001 data, reported before); group 2 remifentanil 25 ng/kg/min (n=7); group 3 50 ng/kg/min (n=7); group 4 100 ng/kg/min (n=6). Counts from Table 1 (derived: counted rows).
- Propofol by target-controlled infusion (STANPUMP) using the Kataria paediatric pharmacokinetic model (ref 9, children 3-11 y), target = plasma concentration. Remifentanil by fixed-rate infusion (not TCI, not bolus). Both started together, 5 min before endoscope insertion, "to allow equilibration".
- Up-and-down (Dixon) design: next patient's propofol target moved 0.1 log interval down after no response, up after response. Starting targets 1.0 (group 1), 2.0 (group 2), 2.5 mcg/mL (groups 3, 4). Targets used in Table 1 run 1.0-5.0 mcg/mL.
- End point: response vs no response to the procedure's stimulation; response = excessive movement (more than gentle restraint) or heart rate or blood pressure >120% of baseline. All observed responses were movement; haemodynamics stayed within 120% of baseline.
- No plasma concentrations measured (neither drug); concentrations are model-predicted.
- Analysis: NONMEM V, first-order method, logistic/sigmoid Emax on quantal response; models compared by likelihood ratio.

## Model and parameters
Sigmoid Emax (Eq. 1, printed p1384): E = E0 + (Emax - E0) C^g / (C50^g + C^g). Best model: one curve for propofol alone, a second for propofol with any remifentanil dose, one shared steepness.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t1/2 ke0 or tPeak was estimated for either drug. The paper states only that remifentanil reaches effect-site equilibrium rapidly and that a fixed infusion is expected to give a consistent effect-site concentration by 5 min (Discussion p1385, citing refs 22-24); a steady state is not reached at 5 min.
- Remifentanil: no MEAC or analgesic concentration; no concentration measured or modelled. Doses are infusion rates only: 25, 50, 100 ng/kg/min (= 0.025, 0.05, 0.1 mcg/kg/min, derived).
- Interaction: remifentanil lowered propofol C50 from 3.7 to 2.8 mcg/mL (derived: 24% lower). The three remifentanil doses were not significantly different from each other (P>0.05), so no dose-response of the interaction. The surface-response interaction model showed synergy by SE criteria but was not significantly better than the two-curve model.
- Adverse effects: three patients in group 4 (100 ng/kg/min) desaturated (SpO2 <94%) and needed positive-pressure ventilation before the endoscope; no bradycardia or hypotension. Authors suggest 25 ng/kg/min as the starting dose.
- No equianalgesic or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Age 3-10 y and weight 9.7-57.4 kg tabulated (Table 1) but not used as covariates.

## Caveats
- Predicted, not measured, concentrations (authors acknowledge; they cite Ropcke 2001, who found no difference between measured, predicted and infusion-rate analyses).
- Propofol C50 is a population value for a tight, one-procedure stimulus, in children 3-10 y with the Kataria model; it is not a BIS or hypnotic C50.
- Small groups (6-7) per remifentanil dose; the pooling to one "with remifentanil" curve rests on non-significant differences.
- Remifentanil effect-site concentration at the 5 min stimulus time was not at steady state.
- Table 1 shows group 1 containing only propofol-alone targets with a data-dependent up-and-down sequence, so patient 1's row (target 1.0 mcg/mL) is plain response data.
