# Eichelbaum 1984: IV kinetics of (+)-, (-)- and racemic verapamil

PDF page n is printed page 452 + n. PMC1463390.

## Study
Bonn. Five healthy male volunteers, 23-27 years, weight 68-91 kg in the Methods (Table 1 lists 70, 68, 70, 92 and
76 kg: subject 4 is 92 kg in the table, outside the stated range; Table 2's CL/kg for subject 4 implies about
91-92 kg). Normal renal, hepatic and ECG findings; two smokers; no other drugs; no alcohol or coffee from 24 h before.
Each received, in random order, 5 mg (-)- or (+)-verapamil in 30 mL saline over 5 min; three (subjects 1-3) also
received 10 mg racemic verapamil; two (subjects 4 and 5) also 7.5 and 10 mg (-) and 25 and 50 mg (+). Venous
samples at end of infusion and 5-720 min after. Assay: GC mass fragmentography (capillary column, d7-verapamil
internal standard), detection limit about 0.2 ng/mL; enantiomers given separately, so total verapamil was measured.
Protein binding by equilibrium dialysis. Multi-exponential fit with the infusion term (LSNLR, weights 1/C²);
derived V, Vss, Vz, CL, t1/2z (Wagner 1976). The salt form of the doses is not stated; ampoules were a gift from
Knoll.

No dose dependency of either isomer over 5-10 mg (-) and 5-50 mg (+) in two subjects. With liver blood flow 1500
mL/min and blood:plasma 0.9 assumed, the authors predict oral bioavailability of (-) about half that of (+).

**Table 1 against Table 2 (derived, mine).** Dose × 10⁶ / Σ(Ci/λi) (the AUC of the fitted function) divided by
Table 2's CL in mL/min: subject 1 racemate 1.080, and 17 of the 21 rows lie between 1.046 and 1.096 (median about
1.078). Four rows do not: subject 2 (+) 0.625, subject 2 (-) 1.126, subject 3 (±) 0.893 (the biexponential fit) and
subject 5 (+) 50 mg 1.155. The ratio of verapamil hydrochloride to base molecular weight is 491.07/454.60 = 1.080.
The near-constant 8% for most rows is consistent with doses entered as the hydrochloride and Table 2 computed as
base, but the paper never says so, and the outlying rows show the relation is not exact in every subject.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured here. Stated from the group's other work: (-)-verapamil about 10 times as potent as (+) on AV
conduction and echocardiographic parameters ("manuscript in preparation", PDF p4-5); after oral racemate, 2-3 times
higher plasma levels were needed for the same P-R prolongation as after IV (Eichelbaum 1980), and the slope ratio
2.2. No ke0 or time to peak effect.

## Covariates
None modelled. Isomer is the main source of difference.

## Caveats
Five young healthy men; racemate in only three; enantiomers given on separate days, so between-day variation
confounds the isomer comparison (the authors say so). Biexponential in one subject. Subject 4's weight conflicts
between Methods and Table 1. Exponent ordering in Table 1 subject 2 (+) and (-) rows is irregular (λ1 0.03 and 0.05
below or near λ2), which may explain their outlying Table 1/Table 2 ratios; I did not resolve it.
