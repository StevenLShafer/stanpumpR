# Bartels 2007: nicardipine in pre-eclampsia, placental transfer and breast milk

## Study
Short communication, Erasmus MC Rotterdam, 2004-2005. Ten women with severe, early-onset pre-eclampsia (Table 1:
median age 28 y (17-36); GA at start of nicardipine 28-4/7 weeks; 2 HELLP at admission; 9 caesarean, 1 vaginal)
given continuous IV nicardipine after failure of IV ketanserin or dihydralazine: start 3 mg/h, reduced at target
(intra-arterial DAP < 100, < 90 with HELLP), titrated in 0.5-1 mg/h steps to a maximum 10 mg/h (p1-p2). Maternal
blood just before delivery, umbilical arterial and venous blood just after; breast milk collected until 24 h after
nicardipine stopped postpartum, with a maternal sample after each collection. In-house HPLC-UV, LOQ 1.4 ng/mL
plasma and 5.0 ng/mL milk (p2). Maternal weight not reported.

## Model and parameters
No model and no clearance. Results (p3): dose at delivery 1-7 mg/h; cumulative dose to delivery 4.5-450 mg; median
treatment to delivery 55 h (1.5-100); maximum dose 1-9 mg/h. Eight women had the same dose for 6 h before
delivery; one (3 -> 1.5 mg/h for the last 2 h of 4 h) was included as near steady state; one (3 mg/h for 1.5 h,
stopped just before delivery, U/P 0.5) was excluded as not at steady state. Nine analysed.

Breast milk: 34 samples from 7 women; nicardipine undetectable (< 5 ng/mL) in 82%, so no M/P ratio; highest 18.5
ng/mL at 5.5 mg/h; maximum neonatal exposure < 300 ng/day (0.015-0.004% of a therapeutic dose in a 1-kg neonate)
(p3). Maternal dose at delivery correlated with umbilical arterial and venous concentrations (rs 0.857, P 0.007;
rs 0.800, P 0.005); cumulative dose did not correlate with U/P (Fig 1, p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None quantified against concentration. Authors cite 70-100 ng/mL as the adult therapeutic range (ref 8) (p4). The
introduction's "short elimination half-life (2-5 minute)" is a background statement, not a measurement.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Ten patients; single paired sample per woman; concentrations reported as ranges only; the abstract's "highest
umbilical cord concentration ... 18 ng/ml" after 4.5 mg/h vs Results venous maximum 18.6 ng/mL (rounding). The
6-h constant dose does not by itself guarantee steady state for a drug with a long terminal phase, which the authors
do not discuss.
