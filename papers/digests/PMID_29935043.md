# Maagdenberg 2018: pediatric acenocoumarol dosing algorithm (CAPS)

## Study
Multicentre retrospective follow-up study (four Dutch academic children's hospitals plus the Leiden
anticoagulation clinic), children aged ≤ 18 years who took acenocoumarol for > 1 month between 1995 and 2014
(PDF p2, printed 1733). 175 patients enrolled; 128 had a stable period, 123 with dose information, 86 with valid
weight and height (clinical cohort), 80 with all SNP genotypes (genetic cohort) (Fig. 1, PDF p4, printed 1735).
Clinical cohort: 48.8% female, median age 8.9 (IQR 4.2-13.3) years, only 2 (2.4%) under 1 year, 84.9% European;
indications Fontan circulation 30.2%, prosthetic valve 27.9%, DVT/PE 20.9%; median BSA (Haycock) 0.98
(0.67-1.38) m² (Table 1, PDF p5, printed 1736). Outcome: mean daily dose (mg) during the first stable period
(≥ 3 consecutive INRs in the patient's target range over ≥ 3 weeks). Genotyping from saliva (TaqMan): VKORC1
rs9934438, CYP2C9*2/*3, CYP4F2, CYP3A4*22 and *1B, CYP2C18 rs1998591. Analysis: forward stepwise linear
regression of log10 mean stable dose, SPSS 23. No concentrations were measured; this is a dose-requirement
regression, not a PK or PK/PD model.

## Model and parameters
Outcome is log10(daily dose, mg). Table 3 (PDF p7, printed 1738), confirmed on the rendered page:

- Genetic algorithm (n = 80): log dose = 0.105 + 0.316·BSA(m²) − 0.102·Fontan − 0.120·(VKORC1 variant alleles)
  − 0.084·(CYP2C18 variant alleles) − 0.090·(CYP2C9*2 and *3 variant alleles); adjusted R² 61.8% (unadjusted 64.3%).
- Clinical algorithm (n = 86): log dose = −0.061 + 0.319·BSA − 0.149·Fontan; adjusted R² 45.0% (unadjusted 46.3%).

Observed median daily doses by VKORC1 (Table 2, PDF p6, printed 1737): GG 2.52 (1.70-3.64) mg, AG 1.66
(1.14-2.29), AA 1.00 (0.71-1.68). Fontan patients needed less (mean difference 1.10 mg, p < 0.001; PDF p4).
Sensitivity analyses: stricter stable period (n = 47) gave BSA + VKORC1 + CYP2C9, adjusted R² 62.2%; age instead of
BSA gave adjusted R² 54.4% (genetic, n = 116) and 37.9% (clinical, n = 123) (PDF p5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is the stable maintenance dose needed to hold INR in the target range; target ranges varied (Table 1:
standard 2.5-3.5 in 50%, high 3.0-4.0 in 25.6%). Target range was not significantly associated with dose
(R² 4.2%, p = 0.058). No concentration, EC50, ke0, MEAC or potency ratio is reported. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
BSA (Haycock), Fontan circulation, VKORC1, CYP2C18 and CYP2C9*2/*3 variant-allele counts, as in the equations
above. Age, height, weight and BSA were collinear (all r > 0.872), so BSA alone was offered. CYP4F2, CYP3A4*1B, sex
and ethnicity were not associated (p ≥ 0.2).

## Caveats
Retrospective; doses and INRs may be incomplete; no concomitant drugs or vitamin K intake. Only 80 in the genetic
cohort against a planned 110. Very few infants (2). The genetic model overestimated dose in two obese children
(BMI > 30). No external validation. VKORC1 rs9934438 is reported as GG/AG/AA in Table 2 rather than the usual
C/T notation for 1173C>T; the allele naming is the paper's own. Population is Dutch children dosed in the
Netherlands, mostly with cardiac indications, and the outcome is a dose, not a concentration or INR time course.
