# Gin 1990: propofol pharmacokinetics at elective caesarean section

## Study
Hong Kong (Prince of Wales Hospital). 10 Chinese women, singleton pregnancy, elective caesarean section, versus 6
nonpregnant Chinese women for laparoscopic sterilization; all ASA I with normal haematology, biochemistry and liver
tests (PDF p2, printed p149). Caesarean group: ranitidine and sodium citrate, propofol 2.0 mg/kg over 20 s,
suxamethonium 1.5 mg/kg, 50% N2O + 1.0% enflurane, atracurium; oxytocin infusion and morphine 0.2 mg/kg IV after
delivery. Laparoscopy group: no premedication, propofol 2.0 mg/kg over 20 s, atracurium 0.5 mg/kg, 70% N2O + 1.0%
enflurane, no opioids. Venous samples before and at 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 30, 60, 120, 240, 360, 480 min after injection;
controls also at 2, 4, 6, 8 min. Whole-blood propofol by HPLC with fluorescence (modified Plummer), linear 2-3000
ng/mL, between-batch CV 6.7% at 50 and 4.8% at 3000 ng/mL, LOD about 2 ng/mL. Analysis: non-compartmental
(statistical moments, BITRI program); controls also by compartmental fit. Demographics, mean (SD) (PDF p3):
caesarean 29.4 (2.8) yr, 65.3 (7.6) kg, anaesthesia 44.0 (13.7) min; laparoscopy 33.3 (2.7) yr, 52.8 (4.4) kg,
24.2 (6.6) min (all P < 0.05). Height not reported. Mann-Whitney between groups.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None: no effect measure, no ke0. Apgar scores (Table II) 8-10 at 1 min and 8-10 at 5 min; induction-delivery
times 5.6-13.6 min. No awareness or dreaming reported.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (term caesarean) as a group effect only. Groups differed in age, weight and anaesthetic duration.

## Caveats
- Small groups (10 vs 6); the caesarean group had sparser early sampling (the authors found extra points did not
  change the control-group results).
- Different anaesthetics: morphine after delivery and 50% N2O in the caesarean group; no opioid and 70% N2O in
  controls.
- Whole-blood, not plasma, concentrations; sampling only to 480 min, which shortens apparent Vd and half-life (the
  authors note smaller Vss and half-life than other studies).
- The authors offer blood loss and delivery of fetus and placenta, or increased extrahepatic clearance, as causes
  of the higher clearance; they do not resolve which.
