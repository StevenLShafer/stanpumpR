# Liu 2025: LC-MS/MS of remifentanil, etomidate and rocuronium in maternal and cord serum

## Study
This paper validates an assay and then applies it to a small pilot sample. The assay is one UPLC-MS/MS method
(Waters Xevo TQ-S, positive ESI, MRM) for remifentanil, etomidate and rocuronium in 10 µL of serum. It uses protein
precipitation with acetonitrile and a 4-min run. The internal standards are sufentanil, metomidate and vecuronium.
Calibrators and QCs were made up in serum from pregnant women who had not been exposed.

**Application** (Methods p5, Table 6 p4):
- 20 maternal-newborn pairs at caesarean section under general anaesthesia, at one hospital in Chongqing.
- Induction: remifentanil TCI target 5 ng/mL, sevoflurane 5% (stopped when the eyelash reflex was lost), etomidate
  0.25 mg/kg IV, then rocuronium bromide 0.6 mg/kg IV.
- Sampling: once only, immediately after cord ligation. Samples were taken from the maternal radial artery, the
  umbilical vein and the umbilical artery.
- Not reported: maternal age, weight, gestation, induction-to-delivery interval, or the TCI model.
- Registered as ChiCTR2100046547.

**Validation results:**
- Linearity R² 0.994 / 0.997 / 0.992 (Table 1).
- LLOQ (Table 2): remifentanil 0.15 ± 0.02, etomidate 16.87 ± 0.51, rocuronium 106.73 ± 8.63 ng/mL.
- Imprecision under 15% (Table 3), recovery 92.9-114.3% (Table 4), and carry-over residual between -1.66% and
  -0.11% (Table 5).

## Model and parameters
There is no PK model. The only result is a single-time-point concentration ratio, reported as mean ± SD with the
range, from Table 6 (n = 20 pairs).

- Umbilical vein (Table 6): remifentanil 2.43 ± 0.13 ng/mL, etomidate 302.15 ± 29.03 ng/mL, rocuronium 0.86 ± 0.16
  µg/mL.
- Umbilical artery (Table 6): 1.33 ± 0.15 ng/mL, 166.24 ± 21.53 ng/mL and 0.44 ± 0.77 µg/mL respectively.
- The UV/MA ratios differ between the drugs (P < 0.001, one-way ANOVA).
- Ratios of the means (derived: 2.43/4.75 = 0.51, 302.15/412.71 = 0.73, 0.86/7.08 = 0.12) agree with the
  tabulated means of the ratios.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD was measured.
- Opioid audit (remifentanil): no MEAC, no ke0, t½ke0 or tPeak, and no potency ratio.
- The maternal arterial remifentanil of 4.75 ng/mL is said to agree with the 5 ng/mL TCI target.
- The Discussion cites earlier remifentanil UV/MA ratios: 0.88 for remifentanil alone (Kan 1998) and 0.64 with
  propofol.

## Covariates
None studied.

## Caveats
**Internal inconsistencies:**
- LLOQ: the abstract and Table 2 give 0.15 (remifentanil) and 16.87 ng/mL (etomidate). The Results text (p2) gives
  0.08 and 18.69. The Discussion uses 0.08.
- Carry-over: the abstract says under 5%. The Results text says under 15%.
- Table 3 intra-day QC means do not match the nominal QC levels in Methods. For remifentanil they are 0.54, 1.49
  and 2.55 against nominal 0.5, 2.0 and 4.0. The inter-day high rocuronium is 8188 against an intra-day 4488. These
  are unresolved.

**SDs look too small for the ranges:**
- Remifentanil maternal arterial is 4.75 ± 0.19 over a range of 2.77-5.79, and rocuronium UA is 0.44 ± 0.77.
- My inference, not the paper's: the "SD" may be an SEM. The arithmetic would fit (0.19 × √20 = 0.85).

**Other points:**
- "Nearly 4000 times more concentrated" (Discussion) holds for rocuronium against remifentanil (derived: about
  1500×). It does not hold against etomidate (about 17×).
- One sample per pair, at an undefined time after induction: the ratio is a snapshot, not a transfer rate.
- No demographics are reported. "Placental transfer rate" is the paper's name for the UV/MA ratio.
- Serum was used, not plasma, with no esterase stabilisation for remifentanil.
