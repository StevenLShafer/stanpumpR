# Minto 2000: response surface model for anesthetic drug interactions

Note: the archived file is the article itself (14 pages, Anesthesiology 2000;92:1603-16). The earlier card `PMID_10839909_sup0001.md` covers the web supplement (Appendices 1 and 2), which this file does not contain.

## Study
A modelling paper with a worked example, not a new trial. Data are from Short, Plummer, Chui 1992 (ref 4): 400 women having elective gynecologic surgery; hypnosis defined as failure to open the eyes to verbal command, assessed 4 min after midazolam and 2 min after propofol or alfentanil IV bolus (the approximate times of peak effect). Midazolam, when part of a combination, was given 2 min before the other drugs. Doses (Table 2, PDF p. 7, mg/kg, text layer only): midazolam 0.1-0.2, propofol 0.7-2.5, alfentanil 0.05-0.15 alone, plus paired and triple combinations; 10 subjects per dose line. Fits used NONMEM maximizing the binomial log-likelihood across all 400 observations (E0 = 0, Emax = 1 fixed).

Model: the probability of effect is a sigmoid in the sum of drug potency units, U = C/C50 (equations 1-5, PDF p. 3-5). The ratio theta = UB/(UA+UB) defines a "drug" for each fixed ratio, and U50(theta), Emax(theta) and gamma(theta) are polynomials in theta (fourth order, constrained to equal the single-drug values at theta = 0 and 1). The usual quadratic form is U50(theta) = 1 - b2*theta + b2*theta^2 (equation 9; a positive b2 means synergy, negative means infra-additive). A three-drug version has U50(thetaB, thetaC); its full equations are in the web supplement.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: loss of response to voice (failure to open eyes), a quantal hypnosis end point at one assessment time per drug, not analgesia, EEG or pupil.
- Alfentanil opioid audit: D50 0.0930 mg/kg bolus (Table 4); C50 for hypnosis 1020 ng/mL, derived by the authors as the predicted effect-site concentration at the assessment time (2 min) after the D50 bolus using Scott and Stanski PK (Computer Simulations, PDF p. 8, printed 1610); population = 50-kg 30-yr woman covariates; concentration basis not stated whether total or free, blood or plasma (the PK source's assay is not described here). t1/2 ke0 0.9 min (Table 6), from Scott and Stanski; end point for that ke0 (EEG in the original) not stated in this paper. No MEAC, no analgesic concentration, no tPeak, no hysteresis discussion.
- Potency comparison, hypnosis by D50 (Table 4): alfentanil 0.0930 mg/kg, midazolam 0.144, propofol 0.988; ratio propofol:alfentanil about 10.6:1, midazolam:alfentanil about 1.5:1 (derived). By C50: alfentanil 1020 ng/mL, midazolam 229, propofol 2660 (Table 6). No equianalgesic ratio.
- Interaction: effect-site equilibration included in simulations only (t1/2 ke0 above); the response surface itself was fitted to doses. Authors note PK interactions between the drugs were not modeled.
- A single dose of midazolam abolished response to voice in only 80% of subjects at the highest dose, so the 100% Emax was assumed (text, discussion).

## Covariates
None in the surface. Simulations use fixed covariates (female, 30 yr, 50 kg, 165 cm) inside the cited PK models. The authors say the fit uses doses in mg/kg (Table 2-4); Table 5 doses are in mg for 50 kg (derived from mg/kg times 50 for the D50 values: 0.144 x 50 = 7.2 mg; the 95% doses in Table 5 are larger as expected).

## Caveats
Empirical model, no mechanism; polynomials can extrapolate to unrealistic shapes (authors say U50 and gamma must be kept positive); data come from one study of women at one assessment time; pharmacokinetic interactions ignored; midazolam never reached 100%; results for hypnosis do not tell about response to incision. The paper's own labels (Figure 9 cites "table 7"; Table 4 refers to "table 1") are inconsistent.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No repo location cites this paper, so there is nothing to transcribe-check; the comparisons below are independent cross-checks against library models for the same drugs.
- Alfentanil (R/drugs_alfentanil.R): library MEAC 39 (ng/mL) and tPeak 1.4 min; Minto's C50 for hypnosis is 1020 ng/mL and t1/2 ke0 0.9 min (ke0 = 0.693/0.9 = 0.77/min, derived). These are different end points (the library MEAC is analgesic; Minto's is loss of response to voice), so the 26-fold difference is not a discrepancy. A t1/2 ke0 of 0.9 min is consistent in magnitude with a tPeak of about 1.4 min but the tPeak was not recomputed here. Minto's alfentanil V1 1.56 L and CL1 0.142 L/min for a 50 kg woman are lower than the library's 70 kg values (2.1853 L, 0.1989 L/min, lines 29-32), the direction a smaller patient would give; whether the covariate scaling accounts for all of it is an open question.
- Midazolam (R/drugs_midazolam.R): library tPeak 3.0 min against t1/2 ke0 4.8 min here; Minto's CL1 0.535 L/min (50 kg woman) equals the 0.5352 the library's comment gives for the 70 kg reference man, so the two sets differ in body size basis; see open question.
- Propofol: Minto's PK for propofol are Schnider's (Table 6); the library models propofol separately (R/alternativeModels.R companion Schnider model) and was not compared here.
Verdict: partly-verified (no citation to check; cross-checks consistent with the library's direction, but two source questions open).
