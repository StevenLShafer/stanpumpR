# Minto 2000 (web supplement): drug interaction models in anesthesia

## Study
No data. Methods supplement: Appendix 1 reviews existing interaction models; Appendix 2 derives a three-drug extension of the Minto response surface model (PDF p. 1-12), followed by Figures 1-2 (p. 13-15), Table 1 (p. 16) and references (p. 17).

## Model and parameters
```yaml
parameters: []  # no parameter values; methods only
```
- Appendix 1: an additive isobole is a straight line; a single 50% isobole cannot say anything about other effect levels (e.g. 95%) without the steepness. The "many isobole" approach has too many parameters.
- Logistic-regression interaction models are criticized: with concentration (not log) the single-drug limit is inconsistent and the model predicts effect with no drug; with log concentrations the 50% isobole is a hyperbola implying profound synergy and needs an arbitrary constant.
- Greco-type models (non-additivity parameter alpha) cannot give a maximum greater than either drug alone or asymmetric isoboles.
- Table 1 (PDF p. 16): seven criteria for an ideal interaction model (additive isobole consistency; valid for any exposure measure; estimable from reasonable-size studies; flexible for synergy/antagonism and unequal steepness or Emax; whole-range prediction for one to three drugs; no effect with no drug; correct reduction when a drug is absent).
- Appendix 2: for three drugs A, B, C, with proportions theta_B and theta_C (theta_A = 1 - theta_B - theta_C), at a fixed ratio there is a sigmoid concentration-response. Emax, U50 and gamma are quadratic polynomials in theta_B and theta_C, built so that each pair reduces to its two-drug interaction. The single free three-drug parameter is the triple term q_ABC (p. 12). Criteria: reduces to the two-drug model when a drug is absent; no interaction gives U50 = 1 everywhere; a sham experiment with B and C the same drug reproduces the A-B interaction; symmetric in A, B, C.
