# Wolff 2005: methadone clearance across pregnancy

## Study
Longitudinal within-subject observational study, Leeds Addiction Unit (UK outpatients). n = 9 opioid-dependent
pregnant women on oral methadone maintenance (>3 months in treatment), aged 18-32 y (mean 22.8, median 23.0).
Heavy co-use: all smoked 5-25 cigarettes/day, 7 regular cannabis, 3 crack cocaine, 5 irregular heroin, 3 on
temazepam (Results, PDF p. 3). Sampling: trough venous sample just before the supervised daily dose, then 5-6 more
over 24 h, once per trimester (0-13, 14-27, 28-40 wk) and postpartum (44-60 wk). Data: 1st trimester n=4,
2nd n=4, 3rd n=8, postnatal n=3. Assay: plasma racemic methadone by HPLC with UV detection at 215 nm, benzhexol
internal standard (PDF p. 2). Analysis: non-compartmental; AUC by trapezoid over 24 h, then extrapolated to infinity
with the terminal rate constant; CL = dose / AUC(0-inf), divided by body weight for per-kg values (PDF p. 2).
This is an apparent oral clearance (CL/F); bioavailability was not measured. No enantiomers, no free fraction.

No volume, half-life, ka or bioavailability reported.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- **MEAC / minimum effective concentration:** none reported. The paper relates falling trough concentrations
  (0.115 to 0.07 mg/L, total racemic plasma, HPLC-UV) to withdrawal complaints (2 women in the 3rd trimester), but
  defines no threshold concentration.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis:** none reported. tmax (plasma, oral) 1.9-2.5 h is a PK quantity, not an effect
  end point.
- **Potency / equianalgesic ratio:** none.
- Secondary outcomes (PDF p. 4): mean gestation 39.7 wk; mean birth weight 3094 g (SD 368), none <2500 g; 5 neonates
  in special care 0.5-28 days; 4 of 9 showed neonatal withdrawal.

## Covariates
Body weight used only to normalise clearance. Trimester is the grouping variable; no covariate model.

## Caveats
- Very small and unbalanced: different women contribute to different trimesters (only 4 in the 1st and 2nd, 3
  postnatally); only two women gave all three trimester values (abstract). Fig. 1 legend shows 8 subjects, though
  the caption says nine.
- The comparator for "0.17 to 0.21 L/h/kg" is the **first trimester of the same cohort**, already pregnant, not
  non-pregnant women; the authors compare with non-pregnant literature values (their ref. 28) only in words.
  Derived 3rd/1st ratio of means 0.21/0.17 = 1.24.
- Postnatal values (n=3) imply lower clearance than any trimester, but the row is internally inconsistent (see
  open question) and cannot be used for a pregnant/postpartum ratio.
- CL computed as dose / AUC(0-inf) after a steady-state dose; at steady state dose / AUC(0-tau) is the correct
  estimator, so the absolute values carry a method error of unknown size.
- Doses were cut during pregnancy (dose-reduction programme), confounding trough and AUC falls.
- The discussion's "around 5% of the nadir" for 0.115 to 0.07 mg/L does not follow arithmetically (0.07/0.115 = 61%,
  derived).
- Heavy polysubstance use (smoking induces CYP1A2; cannabis, cocaine, benzodiazepines) and no genotyping.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:10** finding text: "Wolff 2005 (weight-adjusted CL 0.17 to 0.21 L/h/kg across pregnancy)".
  Paper: Table 1 means 0.17 (1st trimester) and 0.21 (3rd trimester) L/h/kg, also in abstract, results and
  discussion. Unit (L/h per kg) correct. **Matches.**
- The factor applied (CL × 24/14 = 1.71, `R/drugs_methadone.R:287-290`) is derived from Ansari 2016, not from this
  paper, and the registry says so. Wolff's within-pregnancy rise (×1.24, derived) is in the same direction but is
  measured against the first trimester, not against non-pregnant women; it neither establishes nor contradicts the
  1.71 magnitude. The registry's comparator column names Bogen and Pond for the non-pregnant comparison, so Wolff is
  not misapplied against the library's non-pregnant baseline. **Matches (as corroboration of direction only).**
- Independent comparison with the library's non-pregnant model (`R/drugs_methadone.R:19-25, 180-190`, Henthorn and
  Kharasch, IV): typical racemic systemic CL about 0.097 L/min base (derived as the harmonic mean of R 0.103 and S
  0.091 L/min) = 5.8 L/h; on an HCl-dose basis /0.8946 = 6.5 L/h; as oral CL/F with F 0.70, about 9.3 L/h
  (all derived, reference adult, no weight term on CL in the header). Wolff's first-trimester CL/F is 9.52 L/h (mean,
  54.9 kg) / 10.11 (median); third-trimester median 12.49 L/h is ×1.34 the model's non-pregnant value (derived),
  less than the library's ×1.71 pregnant value (about 15.9 L/h, derived). The salt/base basis of Wolff's numbers is
  not stated (open question), and the means are internally inconsistent, so this is indicative only.
