# Watanalumlerd 2007: GI transit makes Adderall XR's mixed pellets behave like sustained release

## Study
Modelling/simulation; no new subjects. Data: mean d-amphetamine curves for Adderall XR (fed 20 mg, fasted 30 mg)
and Adderall IR 10 mg x2 4 h apart (fed), from the PDR and the NDA 21-303 clinical pharmacology review.
In vitro dissolution of Adderall XR 20 mg (10 mg IR + 10 mg delayed-release pellets), USP apparatus II, 2 h
pH 1.4 then pH 6.0, UV 257 nm. Kinetica 2000 fits and deconvolution; Crystal Ball Monte Carlo, 500 trials.

## Model and parameters
One compartment, first-order absorption and elimination. IR pellets absorbed first order; enteric-coated (EC)
pellets enter the intestine by zero-order gastric emptying over tau (fed) or first-order emptying kem (fasted),
after an emptying lag; EC coat and release assumed instantaneous on reaching the intestine (closed forms,
Eq 1-8). Variability only in GI-transit times (lognormal).

Whether V/F is per mg salts or per mg d-base is not stated explicitly.

Results: deconvolved absorption of Adderall XR shows no double pulse, unlike two IR doses (Fig 5-6); it resembles
sustained release with a short lag (fed). In vitro: IR pellets fully released in 15 min; EC pellets in ~30 min
(30-45 min) after the shift to pH 6.0. Table 3: simulated vs observed Cmax, fed 20 mg 26.0 vs 25.3 ng/mL (2.8%),
fasted 30 mg 43.6 vs 40.2 ng/mL (8.4%). Contrast: an IR tablet + EC tablet capsule (melatonin, Hoffmann 1998)
does give two pulses, because a tablet empties as a single unit.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None.

## Covariates
None (GI-transit variability only).

## Caveats
Mean literature data; PK variability excluded; deconvolution used simulated IV data from the oral fit; transit
parameters from pellets < 2 mm; assumes the EC polymer dissolves in the proximal small intestine.
