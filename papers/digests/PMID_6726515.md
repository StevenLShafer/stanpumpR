# Schwartz 1984: GFR estimate (k = 0.45) in full-term infants

## Study
Albert Einstein College of Medicine, Bronx. 137 healthy full-term infants (birth weight > 2500 g, gestation 38-42 wk); plasma creatinine (Pcr) in 74 infants on days 1-3; endogenous creatinine clearance (Ccr, 2-3 timed urines over 2-6 h) in 63 infants aged 5 days to 12 months. 59 children (>= 3 y, Ccr > 90 mL/min/1.73 m2) as controls. Creatinine by modified Technicon AutoAnalyzer **Jaffe end-point** method with a blank between samples. BSA from the Haycock height-weight formula. Sexes not separated.

## Model and parameters
GFR (mL/min/1.73 m2) = k x L (cm) / Pcr (mg/dL), with k in mg creatinine/100 min/cm/1.73 m2.

Mean Ccr minus 0.45 L/Pcr = -0.4 +/- 3.7 (SE) mL/min/1.73 m2 (Fig. 4). With k 0.55 the estimate exceeded measured Ccr in infants by 24% (P < 0.01). Bedside shortcut GFR = 0.45 L/0.39 = 1.1 L.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not applicable (no drug). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Body length and plasma creatinine; age group selects k.

## Caveats
- k 0.45 probably overestimates GFR in preterm/low-birth-weight infants (footnote: 25-30% overestimate in 8 + 11 studies of premature infants, unpublished).
- Age at which k should change from 0.45 to 0.55 is called "rather arbitrary" (second to third year); no data in the second year of life.
- Jaffe end-point AutoAnalyzer creatinine; authors note other (kinetic Jaffe) methods read higher. Modern enzymatic/IDMS creatinine reads lower in children, so the k values do not transfer directly to enzymatic creatinine (later "bedside Schwartz" k = 0.413).
- First week of life only partly covered. Days 5-7 are represented: 9 full-term infants (median 5.1 days; Ccr 50.6 +/- 5.8 mL/min/1.73 m2, k 0.49 +/- 0.05; Tables I-II, printed p850) are among the 63 infants whose k averaged 0.46, and k did not differ among the infant age groups (F = 0.18, P = 0.95; printed p852). Days 1-4 have plasma creatinine only (74 infants, days 1-3), no clearances, and nothing was measured between day 7 and 1 month. Pcr fell from 0.8 mg/dL at birth to 0.5 mg/dL at 5-7 days and is expected to be stable at 0.4 only after about 8-9 days (t1/2 2.1 days; printed p853), so a single creatinine in the first week is not at steady state. k = 0.45 stands for the first year.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- k = 0.45 below one year (egfrSchwartz, renalFunction.R:212): **matches** the paper's infant constant (term infants 1-52 wk). Applying it from day 0 and to preterm infants goes beyond the paper (clearances from day 5, with 9 infants aged 5-7 days in the fit; none on days 1-4; not for preterm).
- k = 0.55 from one year: **matches** as the children's constant (Schwartz 1976, confirmed 0.56 here); the switch age of 1 year is a reasonable reading of an interval the paper calls arbitrary.
- Units mL/min/1.73 m2, height in cm, creatinine in mg/dL: **matches**.
- Caveat (not a transcription error): when creatinine is blank, the assumed normal infant creatinine is enzymatic (Boer, ~0.25 mg/dL; renalFunction.R:84-90), whereas Schwartz's 0.45 was fitted to Jaffe creatinine with an infant normal of 0.39 mg/dL. 0.45 L/0.25 gives ~1.8 L instead of Schwartz's normal ~1.1 L (derived). Pregabalin caps clearance at 96.4 mL/min/1.73 m2, which limits the effect. Whether Chan 2021 used the 1976/1984 k values with enzymatic creatinine is a question for that paper.
- Citation in repo: the repo names only "Schwartz" (context note); no full citation to check.
