# Kinnunen 2019: updated clinical PK and PD of oxycodone (review)

## Study
Narrative review (University of Eastern Finland / Kuopio; open access). MEDLINE search for oxycodone PK literature
from 2006 onward; no systematic-review methods, no meta-analysis, no new data. Sections: basic PK, CYP2D6
polymorphism, CNS penetration, renal/hepatic insufficiency, sex, pregnancy and lactation, children, elderly,
laparoscopic vs open surgery, cancer/cachexia, neuropathic pain, neuraxial, transmucosal, abuse-deterrent
formulations, drug interactions, ethanol.

## Model and parameters
No model of its own. Values quoted from cited studies (all confirmed on rendered pages):

- Healthy adults, IV (Pöyhiä 1991, ref 10; PDF p2 text and Table 1, PDF p4/printed 708): median Vdss 2.6 L/kg
  (1.8–3.7), CL 0.82 L/min (0.4–1.3; absolute, not per kg), t½ 3.0 h (1.8–9.7), NOR:OXY AUC ratio 0.3.
  Table 1's column header says IV 0.07 mg/kg.
- IM 0.14 mg/kg (ref 11): Cmax 34 (SD 10) ng/mL, Tmax 1.0 h, CL 0.78 L/min, t½ 4.9 h. Oral liquid 0.28 mg/kg:
  Cmax 38 ng/mL, t½ 5.1 h, F relative to IM 0.6. Oral IR capsule 0.15 mg/kg (Nieminen 2009, ref 12): Cmax 26 ng/mL,
  Tmax 1.5 h, t½ 3.8 h (SD 0.9), F 0.69 vs IV (PDF p2).
- Table 1 also: oral IR 5 mg tablet Cmax 15.6 ng/mL, Tmax 1.4 h, AUC 99; CR 10 mg Cmax 15.1, Tmax 3.2 h, AUC 104
  (AUC unit printed "ng/mL/h").
- Protein binding 38–45% (mainly albumin); metabolism CYP3A4/5 → noroxycodone 45%, CYP2D6 → oxymorphone 19%;
  urinary unchanged 9–11%; MW 351.83 g/mol (hydrochloride stated in the chemical name); pKa 8.5.
- **Labour** (Kokki 2012, ref 68; PDF p7, printed 711): IV 2–5 mg in early labour, Cmax 9–41 ng/mL; Vd 2.4 L/kg,
  described as smaller than in non-pregnant subjects, so t½ 1.8–2.8 h; at birth (median 7 h after last dose)
  umbilical plasma 0.1–14.8 ng/mL, F:M ratio 0.9–1.5. Pregnant ewes (ref 67, p6): CL 4.6–6.2 "L·h/kg" (sic),
  Vd 1.5–4.7 L/kg, F:M 0.8–2.0 IV; oxymorphone F:M median 2.1 IV.
- Elderly (p8): IV CL 0.5–0.8 vs 0.8–1.2 L/min young; Vd 3.2–3.7 vs 3.7 L/kg; t½ up to 5.0 vs 4.0 h.
- Renal (Kirvelä, ref 49; p5): t½ 3.9 vs 2.3 h, Vd 4.0 vs 2.4 L/kg, CL 0.8 vs 1.1 L/min. Hepatic transplant
  (ref 51; p6): CL 0.26 → 1.1 L/min, Vdss 3.1 → 2.0 L/kg.
- Children/neonates (p7–8): extremely preterm t½ 6.8–12.5 h; 6–24 months t½ 1.7–2.6 h. Buccal/sublingual
  0.1 mg/kg F 55%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEC / MEAC** (Table 2, PDF p10, printed 714; plasma, total oxycodone; assay matrix and free fraction not stated
  in the review; the review does not define MEC or MEAC beyond their names; adult postoperative patients):
  - Open heart surgery, after fentanyl infusion: MEC 5–6 ng/mL (ref 167).
  - Laparoscopic cholecystectomy, no adjunct: MEC 21–34, MEAC 47–55 ng/mL (Kokki 2012, ref 96). Text (p9, printed
    713) calls 50 ng/mL the proposed MEAC after laparoscopic surgery.
  - With IV acetaminophen 1/2 g: MEC 25/24, MEAC 70/62 (ref 168). With IV dexketoprofen 10/50 mg: MEC 60/52,
    MEAC 98/80 (ref 169).
  - Laparotomy: MEC 32–63, MEAC 74–76 (ref 170). Midline laparotomy with acetaminophen + rectus sheath block:
    MEC 18–27 at 24 h (ref 171).
  - Breast cancer surgery (fentanyl 1 µg/kg): MEC 22, MEAC 33; extensive surgery MEC 31, MEAC 51 (ref 103).
  - Footnote: MEC 20–60 and MEAC 50–90 ng/mL after laparoscopic surgery or laparotomy.
  - Children: proposed MEC 12 ng/mL (p8, printed 712; p11, printed 715); paediatric dosing target 10–50 ng/mL.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak**: none reported. Related timing: 50% equilibration to the deep brain compartment 7 min
  (experimental, ref 27); onset of analgesia 15 min IV in rats and 28 min IV in humans (refs 36, 37), end point
  analgesia; CSF after IV 0.1 mg/kg: Cmax 31 ng/mL, Tmax 1.1 h, t½ 4.0 h, AUC CSF:plasma 1.2 (ref 41) (PDF p5,
  printed 709). Unbound brain concentration 3–6-fold plasma (ref 25–27).
- **Potency ratios**: noroxycodone 5–10-fold less potent than oxycodone (µ affinity one-third); oxymorphone 8–30-fold
  more potent (10–45-fold affinity), oral equianalgesic oxymorphone:oxycodone 1:2; noroxymorphone 2-fold more potent
  but does not cross the BBB (PDF p2, printed 706). Intrathecal: oxycodone:morphine 1:14 (experimental); epidural
  oxycodone needs 2–10-fold the morphine dose; epidural 3 mg post-caesarean, oxycodone:morphine potency "less than
  1:1" (PDF p11, printed 715). Intrathecal ED50 (50% MPE) oxymorphone 0.63 µg/kg vs oxycodone 20 µg/kg (ref 46;
  species not stated). IV post-laparoscopic-hysterectomy 24 h consumption: oxycodone 13 mg vs morphine 22 mg
  (ref 100; p9).
- Ventilation: oral oxycodone 20 mg ± IV ethanol, MV baseline 8.0 → 5.8 L/min (oxycodone alone), 5.1/4.7 with
  ethanol 0.5/1.0 g/L (ref 94; p13, text layer only); elderly more affected.

## Covariates
Narrative only, no equations: age (elderly lower CL; start 50% of dose > 70 y, 30% > 80 y), neonatal maturation,
renal and hepatic impairment (start 50–75% / 50%), sex (lower oxymorphone formation in women), pregnancy/labour
(smaller Vd, shorter t½), cachexia (lower CYP3A), CYP2D6 phenotype (little clinical effect), CYP3A/2D6 interactions
(Table 3: e.g. itraconazole, ketoconazole, voriconazole, ritonavir raise oxycodone AUC; rifampin lowers it 53–86%).

## Caveats
- Secondary source; numbers are as transcribed by the authors from cited studies.
- Breast surgery MEAC conflicts within the paper: text (p9) gives 29 ng/mL without and 39 ng/mL with axillary
  evacuation, Table 2 (p10) gives MEAC 33 and 51 (MEC 22 and 31). Cajanus 2018 (ref 103) would settle it.
- Labour data: no median t½, no clearance, no non-pregnant comparator value stated.
- Sheep CL unit printed as "L·h/kg"; Table 1 AUC unit printed "ng/mL/h".

- Citation: source field "Clin Pharmacokinet 2019;58:705-725 PMID 30652261" matches. The finding text calls it
  "The 2018 Clin Pharmacokinet review"; the paper is the 2019 volume (published online 17 January 2019).
  **discrepancy** (minor, year label).
- "Summarises the same": the review does report the Kokki 2012 labour study and a t½ shorter than in non-pregnant
  subjects (1.8–2.8 h; PDF p7, printed 711, rendered). It does **not** give 2.6 h or 3.8 h, gives no clearance, and
  attributes the shorter t½ to a smaller Vd (2.4 L/kg). The repo applies the change entirely to clearance with
  volumes unchanged (`pregnancyRegistry.R:304`). (derived: against this review's own non-pregnant median Vdss of
  2.6 L/kg, 2.4/2.6 = 0.92, too small a change to account for the t½ difference alone, so the review's attribution
  does not by itself contradict a CL increase, but it does not support one either.) **cannot verify** the 1.46
  factor from this paper; see open questions.
- The phrase "the review attributes the faster clearance to all three [CYP3A4, CYP2D6, GFR]" appears to refer to
  Ansari 2016, not Kinnunen; Kinnunen mentions only general pregnancy changes in hepatic and renal function
  (p6, printed 710), not a clearance attribution for oxycodone.
- Notes field ("the review gives no metabolite figure"): consistent; this review gives no human maternal
  oxymorphone figure, only sheep F:M ratios (ref 67). **matches**.
- Units: the review gives CL in L/min absolute and V in L/kg; the repo uses a dimensionless ratio of half-lives,
  so no unit conversion is involved.
- **Both open questions are closed, and the discrepancy is resolved by removing the factor, not by changing it.**
  This card asked whether the clearance scaling should stay, move to volume, or both, and whether Kokki 2012
  reports a clearance at all. Kokki 2012's full text was then read (Table 3: CL 0.92 L/min, V1 95 L + V2 100 L, at
  the median 82 kg), which answers the second question -- it does report clearance -- and settles the first in a
  way neither option anticipated: against the library's own non-pregnant model for a woman of that size (CL 1.03
  L/min, Vss 213 L), labour gives **0.89x the clearance and 0.92x the volume**. Labouring women are not faster.
  Kokki's "clearance was higher" is relative to its reference 18, Gronlund 2011's volunteers (Clin Drug Investig
  31:143-153), not to this model, and the applied 1.46 factor was predicting 1.51 L/min for those women, **64%
  above what Kokki measured**. **Steven L. Shafer's ruling (2026-10-28): "drop the oxycodone factor"** -- and no
  factor goes on volume either; nothing is applied. Implemented on branch `claude/digest-review-decisions`:
  `oxycodone()` loses its `pregnant` argument and `OXYCODONE_PREGNANCY_CL_FACTOR`, the registry row moves from
  `applied` to `unchanged` with `cl_factor` NA and `evidence_read` full_text, and the test pins 1.03, 0.89 and
  0.92. The lesson worth keeping: the comparator a source means by "higher" is part of the claim, and this one
  was another study's volunteers, not the model the factor was being applied to.
