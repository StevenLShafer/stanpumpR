# Klepser 1997: clindamycin 300 mg q8h vs q12h, serum bactericidal titres

## Study
Randomized, open-label, four-way crossover in 12 healthy volunteers (6 M, 6 F; age 28.5 ± 8.1 yr, weight
75.3 ± 13.5 kg, mean ± SD; PDF p2). Clindamycin (Cleocin) 300 mg i.v. (in 100 mL D5W over 30 min) or p.o.
(one 300 mg capsule, fasted), every 8 h or every 12 h; 6-day washout. Three doses were given before sampling
(steady state assumed); samples before the third dose and at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 h (plus 10, 12 h for q12h).
Serum, HPLC-UV (198 nm), linear 0.5–50 µg/mL, CV ≤ 2.3%. Noncompartmental PK; peak/trough by inspection,
"standardized to 70 kg" (method not given). Serum bactericidal titres (SBT, NCCLS M21-P, 99.9% kill, serial
twofold dilution in pooled serum, inoculum ~10^6 CFU/mL) against two clinical isolates each of S. aureus,
S. pneumoniae (one penicillin-sensitive, one penicillin-resistant) and B. fragilis. Duration of activity = last
time with reciprocal SBT ≥ 2, as % of the dosing interval. Predicted duration = time ½ the serum concentration
stayed above the MIC.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio. Table 1 (PDF p2; image-confirmed), median MIC / MBC µg/mL:
S. aureus 1 0.25/0.25; S. aureus 30 0.125/0.125; S. pneumoniae 8 0.125/0.125 (penicillin MIC 4.0);
S. pneumoniae 23 0.125/0.125 (penicillin MIC 0.125); B. fragilis 11 0.5/0.5; B. fragilis 16 0.5/1.0.

Table 3 (PDF p4, printed p633; image-confirmed), mean ± SD % of dosing interval with measurable bactericidal
activity (predicted in parentheses):

| Regimen | S. aureus 1 | S. aureus 30 | S. pn 8 | S. pn 23 | B. frag 11 | B. frag 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| i.v. q8h | 87.5 ± 14.7 (75) | 100 (100) | 100 (100) | 100 (100) | 100 (50) | 97.9 ± 7.1 (50) |
| p.o. q8h | 92.7 ± 13.8 (75) | 100 (100) | 100 (100) | 100 (100) | 93.8 ± 13.3 (25) | 92.2 ± 17.2 (25) |
| i.v. q12h | 55.6 ± 12.7 (50) | 74.3 ± 16.3 (83) | 100 (83) | 100 (83) | 88.2 ± 18.0 (33) | 81.3 ± 19.8 (33) |
| p.o. q12h | 49.6 ± 21.2 (50) | 77.1 ± 14.6 (83) | 100 (83) | 100 (83) | 87.5 ± 18.6 (25) | 80.2 ± 22.3 (25) |

S. aureus: q8h > q12h (P < 0.001); no i.v. vs p.o. difference at the same interval (P > 0.05). Peak median
reciprocal SBTs reached about 32 for S. pneumoniae (Fig. 1C–D). Predicted duration underestimated measured
activity against B. fragilis by 50–75%. Authors cite ≥ 50% of the interval as adequate for concentration-independent
killing (ref. 4, Craig 1995).

## Covariates
None studied.

## Caveats
Healthy volunteers, n = 12; only two isolates per species, all clindamycin-susceptible, so MBC = MIC here is not a
population MBC. SBT is a whole-serum (total-drug, protein-binding-included) ex vivo measure. Troughs (0.21–0.49
µg/mL) are below the stated linear range (0.5–50 µg/mL). The oral F of 51–55% is low against the library's
0.876 (Bouazza 2012) and is a steady-state AUC ratio across different study periods. Weight standardization "to 70 kg" is not
explained. Authors note this was not a clinical-outcome trial.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **S. pneumoniae row (`R/antibioticMbc.R:268-271`)**: "serum after 300 mg every 8 hours was bactericidal for the
  whole dosing interval" — Table 3: 100% for both isolates, i.v. and p.o., q8h and also q12h. **Matches.** But the
  same row says "No MBC was found", while Table 1 of this cited paper gives MBC 0.125 µg/mL (= MIC) for both
  pneumococcal isolates. Only two isolates, so leaving the MBC fields empty is defensible, but the wording is
  inaccurate as to this source (open question).
- **S. aureus exclusion (`R/antibioticMbc.R:293-303`)**: the note says serum at usual doses "mostly does not kill"
  S. aureus and that the MBC is many times the MIC; Klepser is cited for "50 to 85% of the dosing interval".
  The paper's numbers: q8h 87.5–100%, q12h 49.6–77.1% (Table 3); the abstract's lower bounds are > 85% (q8h) and
  > 50% (q12h). So "50 to 85%" understates q8h, and for these two susceptible isolates MBC = MIC (Table 1). The
  paper concludes q8h gives adequate S. aureus coverage. It does not support "mostly does not kill"; it is the
  label and Dan 1997 (also cited) that carry that claim. **Discrepancy** (in what the citation supports; open
  question for Shafer).
- **PK, uncited comparison with `R/drugs_clindamycin.R` (Bouazza 2012, osteomyelitis patients)**: library CL
  15.2 L/h at 70 kg, V 66.2 L (t½ ≈ 3.0 h, derived: 0.693 × 66.2/15.2), oral F 0.876. Klepser's healthy volunteers
  have higher CL (18.5–19.0 L/h, about 1.25×), shorter t½ (2.2–2.7 h) and much lower F (0.51–0.55). The direction
  of CL/t½ is as expected for young volunteers versus patients; the low F is out of line with the library and with
  other sources and is not used anywhere. Not a discrepancy in the code, which does not cite this paper for PK.

  in master 06480c72.
