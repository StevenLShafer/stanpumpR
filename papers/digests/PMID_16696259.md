# Drews 2006: drug display as feedback in simulated anesthesia

## Study
Human-factors simulation experiment, University of Utah. 33 anesthesiologists enrolled, 30 analysed (3 lost to technical problems): novices (PGY1-2), intermediates (PGY3/chief), experts (faculty). Between-subjects factors: drug display vs none, and expertise; within-subject factor: complexity (low = scope incision, high = unplanned Bankart procedure at 20 min). High-fidelity Human Patient Simulator (METI), 62-yr, 80-kg male, CAD, controlled hypertension, family history of MH; TIVA. The experimental group saw a Utah drug display (effect-site concentration history 30 min back, 10 min forward, plus PD-model effect bars for sedation, analgesia, NMB; drugs shown: propofol, fentanyl, remifentanil, rocuronium; Fig 1, printed p.90). The display used PK/PD models (cites Shafer 1990 fentanyl PK and Shafer & Gregg 1992); the paper gives no parameters. Pumps and bar-coded syringes fed the display.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio. The simulator's PD models and the display's models are stated to match well (printed p.93). End points were behavioural: RMSE of heart rate and MAP from baseline during painful stimulation (Table 1, printed p.91, text layer only), time to eye opening (736 s display vs 862 s control, p=.05), time to respiratory rate 50% of normal (577 s vs 774 s, p<.01), total procedure time (2846 s vs 3215 s, p=.039), NASA-TLX subjective performance (5.9 vs 4.5, p=.045). HR RMSE: display 3.78 (low) / 5.7 (high) vs control 7.7 / 10.5 (p=.011). No expertise effect.

None modelled.

## Caveats
Simulator and display used matching models (authors' own limitation), so benefit under model mismatch is untested. Single scenario, small n, simulated patient. The text reports F(2, 25) for the MBP condition effect, which looks inconsistent with a 1-df factor; not checked on the page image.
