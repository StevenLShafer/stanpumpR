# Rincon 2021: rapid nitrocefin test for the cefazolin inoculum effect in MSSA

## Study
In vitro diagnostic-accuracy study. 689 bloodstream MSSA isolates: 640 from a 2011-2014 multicentre Latin
American surveillance study (nine countries) plus 2018-2019 isolates from one hospital in Pereira, Colombia,
and 49 archived U.S. isolates (Duke repository and the ICE endocarditis cohort) (PDF p2, printed p2).
Reference standard: cefazolin broth microdilution MIC at standard and high inoculum, in triplicate, read by three
investigators. The cefazolin inoculum effect (CzIE) was defined as MIC of 16 µg/mL or more at high inoculum
and 8 µg/mL or less at standard inoculum (p3). The abstract (p1, page image) gives the high inoculum as
10^7 CFU/mL; the Methods text layer reads it as 5 x 10^7 (and standard 5 x 10^5) CFU/mL (p2; page 2 not
rendered). All isolates were genome-sequenced and BlaZ typed (A-D) from residues 128 and 216.
Index test: ampicillin-induced supernatant mixed with nitrocefin (400 µM), read for a yellow-to-red change at
15 min to 2 h; about 3 h in all.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD index, ke0 or concentration-effect relationship. The PD-relevant end point is the high-inoculum
cefazolin MIC: CzIE cutoff 16 µg/mL or more, chosen because earlier work (ref 13, Miller 2018) linked it to
mortality; an alternative cutoff of 32 µg/mL or more is also analysed (Table 3). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
BlaZ type (A, B, C, D, nontypeable, none) and country of origin; no patient covariates.

## Caveats
- 93% of isolates are Latin American, where CzIE is common; U.S. isolates were few (49) and archived, and
  the authors say the U.S. frequency may be underestimated (p8).
- Bloodstream isolates only; no clinical outcomes assessed in this cohort; a research-laboratory test not
  yet validated in routine practice (p9).
- The 37% is a pooled figure across very different regional prevalences (10% U.S. to 55% Argentina).
