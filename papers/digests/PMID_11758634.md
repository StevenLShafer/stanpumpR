# Anderson 2001: acetaminophen analgesia and placebo after paediatric tonsillectomy

## Study
- Pooled NONMEM V analysis (FOCE-interaction, ADVAN4 TRANS1), n = 182 children undergoing outpatient
  tonsillectomy, ASA 1-2: new high-dose study (elixir 40 mg/kg, n = 12; 100 mg/kg, n = 20, given 0.5-1 h
  pre-op; age 9.0 +/- 3.0 y, weight 37.9 +/- 16.6 kg), placebo group (black-currant juice, n = 30, from an
  unpublished diclofenac study), and 120 children from the authors' 1999 study (oral elixir pre-op n = 20, rectal
  suppository at induction n = 100). Pooled: age 8.4 +/- 3.4 y, weight 34.9 +/- 17.3 kg, 87 M / 95 F.
- Validation group: 25 children, elixir 40 mg/kg + rectal 20 mg/kg at end of surgery.
- 698 concentrations, 761 pain scores (VAS 0-10). Serum acetaminophen by fluorescence polarisation immunoassay
  (Abbott TDx); LLOQ 1.5 mg/L. Anaesthesia: propofol or halothane/N2O induction, halothane/N2O maintenance; rescue
  morphine 0.05 mg/kg ended pain scoring in that child.
- Size: allometric (WT/70)^PWR with PWR 0.75 for clearance, 1 for volume, **0.25 for effect-site equilibration
  and placebo half-lives** (Eq. 4, p. 561); absorption half-life not scaled.

## Model and parameters

All parameters standardised to 70 kg. Table 1 (PK), Table 3 (PD), Table 5 (placebo); population (geometric mean)
estimates, %CV = between-subject variability.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: VAS 0-10 pain score after tonsillectomy (self-report), combined drug + placebo model
  (Effect = E0 x (1 - Effect_drug) x (1 - Effect_placebo)); fractional Emax model on effect-compartment
  concentration.
- Analgesic equilibration half-time teq 53 min (Table 3), i.e. ke0 = 0.0131 /min (derived: ln2/53), **for a 70 kg
  standardised person**; the paper's discussion restates it as 0.89 h. In a 35 kg child it would be about 45 min
  (derived: 53 x (35/70)^0.25). Previous (1999) estimate without placebo: 1.6 h (CV 131%).
- EC50 9.98 mg/L, Emax 5.17 units; target effect-compartment concentration 10 mg/L predicted to give a 2.6-unit
  reduction (abstract, discussion). EC50 compared with 9.7 mg/L for antipyresis in children.
- Placebo accounted for up to 5.6 units of pain reduction at 3 h.
- Combined prediction (Abstract Conclusions, PDF p1, printed p559): placebo response plus preoperative oral
  acetaminophen 40 mg/kg gives pain scores below 4 (VAS 0-10) for 5 h after surgery. This is a **model prediction**,
  not an observed comparison: the body does not restate it; it matches the simulated mean pain course of Fig. 7b
  (PDF p8, printed p566; 1000 simulated children from the fitted parameters, time 0 = end of surgery), where the
  40 mg/kg oral mean starts near 5, falls below 4 within the first hour and returns to about 4 at roughly 4-5 h
  (read from the figure, approximate). Discussion cites (background) pain < 4 as satisfactory in children.
- 100 mg/kg gave no more analgesia than 40 mg/kg and more vomiting (11/20 vs 3/12, P = 0.037); all vomiters had
  serum concentrations > 30 mg/L.
- No MEAC, no potency ratio (not an opioid).

## Covariates
Weight only (allometric, as above). Age and sex had no effect on PK or PD parameters.

## Caveats
- teq is very imprecise (CV 217%, SEM 31 min) and moves to 29 min when 5 placebo responders are excluded.
- Data are oral elixir and rectal suppository (100 of 182 children rectal); no IV data. Pain stimulus is
  post-tonsillectomy; placebo includes emergence and coping effects.
- Sparse early sampling after oral dosing (authors' own comment); immunoassay.
- teq is a 70-kg-standardised value with a 0.25 exponent; stanpumpR applies no size scaling to ke0.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **ke0** (`R/drugs_acetaminophen.R:94`): log(2)/53 /min = 0.01308 /min. Paper: teq 53 min (Table 3). Unit and
  conversion correct (half-time, not ke0, not tPeak). **Matches.** Test (`test-drugs-acetaminophen.R` ~line 81) pins
  log(2)/ke0 = 53. **Matches.**
- **Emax 5.17, EC50 9.98 mg/L, Hill 1, 10 mg/L -> 2.6 units** (header lines 62-64, 150-151; help line 17, 21;
  scenario lines 7, 11): all as Table 3 / abstract. Scenario arithmetic 5.17 x 3.1/(9.98+3.1) = 1.22 and
  5.17 x 6.2/16.18 = 1.98 (derived) agree. **Matches.**
- **Description of the population** (header lines 62-66; help line 17; scenario line 5): the code says the 53 min
  half-time is "in children (9 +/- 3 years)" and calls it "paediatric, oral-derived". Paper: 53 min is the value
  **standardised to 70 kg** with a 0.25 exponent (Methods Eq. 4; Table 1/3 captions), so it is not a raw paediatric
  half-time (a typical 35 kg child's would be about 45 min, derived); and the pooled data were mostly rectal
  (100 of 182) plus oral, not oral alone; 9.0 +/- 3.0 y describes only the 32-child high-dose subgroup (pooled
  8.4 +/- 3.4 y). Value unaffected; wording is inaccurate and understates that the paper itself scales to adult
  size. **Discrepancy (descriptive only).**
- `R/drugs_ibuprofen.R:75`: cites Anderson 2001 as precedent for applying a paediatric oral half-time to adults and
  IV; same descriptive caveat. **Matches** in substance.
- Not mentioned in repo but relevant: teq CV 217% and 29 min without placebo responders.
