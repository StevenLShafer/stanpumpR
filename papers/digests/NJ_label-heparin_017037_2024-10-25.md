# Heparin sodium injection US label (Hikma, NDA 017037), 2024

## Study
This is the current US prescribing information for unfractionated heparin sodium from porcine intestinal mucosa
(Hikma; initial US approval 1939). Page 1 is the Heparin Sodium Injection Highlights, as the file name says, so
there is no source file problem. Products: vials of 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 USP units/mL preserved with benzyl
alcohol, a 30 mL multiple-dose vial, and preservative-free prefilled syringes of 5,000 units/mL and 5,000
units/0.5 mL (section 3, pp7-8; 11, p14). Potency is by a USP biological assay, in units of activity per mg (p14).

**Dosage (section 2, pp5-7):**
- Adult full-dose regimens for a 68 kg patient (Table 1, p6):
  - Deep SC: 5,000 units IV, then 10,000-20,000 units SC; then 8,000-10,000 units q8h or 15,000-20,000 units q12h.
  - Intermittent IV: 10,000 units, then 5,000-10,000 units q4-6h.
  - Continuous IV: 5,000 units IV, then 20,000-40,000 units per 24 h.
- Monitoring (2.3): adequate when the aPTT is 1.5-2 times normal or the whole-blood clotting time is about 2.5-3
  times control. During infusion, check the aPTT about every 4 h at first. After SC dosing, sample 4-6 h after the
  injection.
- Pediatric (2.5): 75-100 units/kg IV over 10 min. Maintenance 25-30 units/kg/h in infants (average 28 units/kg/h
  under 2 months) and 18-20 units/kg/h in children over 1 year. Target aPTT 60-85 s, taken to reflect anti-Xa
  0.35-0.70.
- Cardiovascular surgery with total body perfusion (2.6): at least 150 units/kg. Often 300 units/kg for procedures
  under 60 min and 400 units/kg for longer ones.
- Prophylaxis (2.7): 5,000 units 2 h before surgery, then every 8-12 h.
- Dialysis (2.11): 25-30 units/kg, then 1,500-2,000 units/h.

- **Clearance (12.3 Excretion, p15):** biphasic. A rapid, saturable (zero-order) component from binding to
  proteins, endothelial cells and macrophages, then slower first-order elimination. Removal is mainly by hepatic and
  reticuloendothelial uptake; heparin is not degraded enzymatically.
- **Binding (12.3 Distribution):** highly bound to antithrombin, fibrinogens, globulins, serum proteases and
  lipoproteins.
- **Route (12.3 Absorption):** not absorbed orally. Peak and onset are immediate after IV dosing.
- The label gives no clearance value, no units for "plasma levels" and no assay.
- **Arithmetic (derived):** 0.07 L/kg is about 4.9 L at 70 kg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Mechanism (12.1):** heparin acts through antithrombin III. Small amounts inhibit factor Xa and larger amounts
  thrombin (IIa).
- **Clotting tests (12.2):** ACT, aPTT, PT and whole-blood clotting time are prolonged by full doses and mostly
  unaffected by low doses. Bleeding time is usually unaffected.
- **Targets:** the aPTT and clotting-time targets are given above (2.3, 2.5). There are no target anti-Xa or
  anti-IIa activity values for adults.

## Covariates
- **Age:** patients over 60 may have higher plasma levels and longer aPTTs on similar doses, and may need lower
  doses (12.3 Specific Populations, p15; 8.5, p13). Women over 60 have more bleeding.
- **Weight:** pediatric and surgical doses are per kg; the adult table assumes 68 kg.
- The label gives no renal or hepatic adjustment.

## Caveats
- The 0.5-2 h half-life and the 0.07 L/kg volume are bare label statements with no study, dose range, assay or
  population. The label does not say over which doses the half-life spans 0.5 to 2 h. It says only that the
  half-life is dose-dependent; that it lengthens with dose follows from the saturable component (inference).
- Pregnancy (8.1, p12): no human data to inform risk; the preservative-free product is recommended.
- Benzyl alcohol (8.4, p13): the preserved product must not be used in neonates and infants.
