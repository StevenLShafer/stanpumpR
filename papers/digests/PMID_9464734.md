# David 1998: IV nitroglycerin bolus at caesarean delivery (randomized, placebo-controlled)

## Study
- **Design:** a double-blind RCT at the Virchow and Charite hospitals, Berlin, June 1994 to July 1996 (pp1-2).
- **Patients:** 97 women at elective caesarean delivery after 34 weeks, under general anaesthesia (thiopental,
  succinylcholine, alcuronium, N2O/O2/isoflurane 0.3-0.4%).
- **Arms:** 0.25 mg nitroglycerin (n = 32), 0.5 mg (n = 34) or saline (n = 31). Each was given as an IV bolus at
  the uterine puncture incision. The product was Nitro Pohl Infus, diluted in 5 mL.
- **Mothers (Table 1, medians):** age 29-29.5 y, weight 74.8-82.5 kg, gestation 39-40 weeks.
- **Sampling (p2):** maternal **venous** blood at 1 and 5 min after the dose, "usually" from an antecubital
  catheter **in the same arm**. Umbilical arterial and venous blood was taken at cord clamping.
- **Assay:** GC-MS for GTN and 1,2- and 1,3-glyceryl dinitrate, at a reference lab. Umbilical samples were whole
  blood and maternal samples plasma.

**Checks (derived):**
- The dose response is close to proportional: 80.3/38.1 = 2.1 for twice the dose.
- A fall to one third over 4 min gives t1/2 = 4 ln2 / ln3 = 2.5 min, consistent with the stated 2-2.5 min.
- Table 4's ratios recompute from its medians: 0.095/38.137 = 0.0025 and 0.008/0.095 = 0.084.
- The paper prints no 5-minute concentrations, only the one-third statement.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Primary end point: no effect.** Surgeons' ease of fetal extraction showed no significant difference from
  placebo (Table 3; power 0.7). Uterine tone and bleeding also showed no significant difference.
- **Blood pressure:** it fell more with nitroglycerin.
  - Against placebo, the 0.5 mg arm was lower from 1 to 20 min: systolic by 26 to 10 mmHg, diastolic by 13 to 10
    mmHg.
  - The 0.5 mg arm was also lower than the 0.25 mg arm: systolic by a median of 11 mmHg at 5 min, diastolic by 8
    and 10 mmHg at 3 and 5 min.
  - Four patients needed a volume expander.
- **Neonates:** Apgar scores, umbilical pH, BP and pulse were not different from placebo.
- **Concentrations:** the paper gives no concentration-effect relation.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
- The 1-min samples were drawn from the same arm as the injection, so they may not be mixed-venous. This is my
  inference; the authors do not discuss it.
- Only medians are given, with no dispersion. There are only two sampling times and no model.
- Uterine relaxation was scored subjectively by surgeons.
- **Same trial as David 2000:** the 2000 paper (PMID 10764480) describes the same 97-patient trial, with the same
  centres, dates and arms. It cites this paper for the clinical results and reports the PK in a 62-patient
  subset. The two are two reports of one trial, not two trials.
