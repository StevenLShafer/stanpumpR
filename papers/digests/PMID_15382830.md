# Hovorka 2004: nonlinear MPC glucose-insulin model for type 1 diabetes (insulin lispro)

## Study
- A methods paper describing a nonlinear model-predictive controller for overnight or fasting glucose control in type 1 diabetes.
- Clinical evaluation: 15 experiments in 10 subjects with type 1 diabetes, each 8-10 h of fasting; five subjects took part twice (PDF p10).
- IV glucose was sampled every 15 min, and SC insulin lispro was given by pump with the rate updated every 15 min.
- One example subject is described: male, 59 y, 82 kg, basal insulin 27 U/day (Fig 6). No other demographics are given.
- Parameter priors come from earlier work, not from this paper's data (see Tables 1-2). The controller re-estimates parameters by Bayesian Marquardt minimisation over a 2, 4 or 12 h learning window.

## Model and parameters
- Structure (Fig 1 and Eq 1-7, PDF p4-5), confirmed on the rendered page:
  - Glucose: two compartments, Q1 (accessible) and Q2.
  - Insulin absorption: a two-compartment SC chain with equal rates 1/tmax,I, dS1/dt = u - S1/tmax,I and dS2/dt = S1/tmax,I - S2/tmax,I, with appearance UI = S2/tmax,I (Eq 5).
  - Plasma insulin: one compartment, dI/dt = UI/VI - ke·I (Eq 6).
  - Insulin action: three remote actions, dxi/dt = -kai·xi + kbi·I (Eq 7). Here kai are the deactivation and kbi the activation rate constants; the Fig 1 arrow labels are drawn the other way round.
  - Gut glucose absorption: UG = DG·AG·t·e^(-t/tmax,G)/tmax,G² (Eq 4).
  - F01 correction below 4.5 mmol/L (Eq 2), and renal loss FR = 0.003(G - 9)VG above 9 mmol/L (Eq 3).
- Table 1, model constants (PDF p6, printed 909):
  - ke and VI are taken from Hovorka 1993. k12, ka1-ka3 and VG are from Hovorka 2002, which studied healthy subjects. AG and tmax,G are from Livesey 1998.
- Table 2, model parameters, prior means for Bayesian estimation:
  - EGP0, F01 and the three sensitivities SIf are from Hovorka 2002. Their priors are multivariate log-normal (PDF p7).
  - The tmax,I prior is from Howey 1994 and Rave 1999.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- The end point is IV plasma glucose. Insulin acts through three first-order remote effect compartments rather than a ke0 effect site.
- The authors give deactivation half-times of about 30 min for disposal and EGP and about 200 min for transport (PDF p7).
- They state a delay of over 90 min from SC delivery to peak action (PDF p14).
- Prediction RMSE grows by 0.2 mmol/L per 15 min of horizon. Clarke grid: 95% zone A and 5% zone B at horizons up to 60 min (n = 1674); 66/33/1/0.1% in zones A/B/C/E at 240 min (n = 5256).
- No EC50, no ke0 and no potency ratio. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
- Volumes and fluxes are per kg of body weight. No other covariates.
- Individual parameters vary over time through Bayesian re-estimation. In the example, Fig 7 shows insulin sensitivities about doubling by 6.5 h after the meal.

## Caveats
- ke and VI are not estimated here; they come from Hovorka 1993, a five-compartment insulin study in NIDDM.
- The glucose kinetics and sensitivity priors come from healthy subjects (Hovorka 2002), not from type 1 diabetes.
- tmax,I is a literature prior.
- The clinical evaluation is described only briefly; the authors say detailed evaluation is outside the paper's scope.

- **Uncited PK comparison with the library's lispro model (Tham 2017, `R/insulinTham.R:31,169`; V/F 43.0 L and CL/F 30.5 L/h at 80 kg).**
  - Hovorka's plasma insulin CL = ke × VI = 0.0166 L/kg/min. At 80 kg that is 1.32 L/min = 79.5 L/h, with V 9.6 L and t½ 5.0 min (derived).
  - Hovorka's clearance is 2.6× Tham's CL/F, and its volume is 0.22× Tham's V/F.
  - The models are structurally different and both are expected:
    - Tham is an apparent one-compartment model. Its slow terminal phase reflects absorption (t½ about 59 min, derived), and it fits observed concentrations.
    - Hovorka splits a fast plasma elimination from a 55 min absorption chain and carries no bioavailability term.
  - Neither model's header predicts the other. No change is implied, since the Hovorka row is not plotted.
- **Units.** All rates are per minute, matching the engine's minutes. VI and VG are per kg. Insulin is in mU/L, which equals mcU/mL, the library's unit.
