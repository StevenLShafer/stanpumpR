# Lowe 2001: oral and intravenous thioguanine pharmacokinetics and CSF penetration in children with ALL

## Study
Pilot treatment trial (Pediatric Oncology Branch NCI with the Children's Cancer Group, Jan 1995 to Apr 1996) in
which thioguanine (TG) replaced mercaptopurine in a multiagent regimen. 58 children with previously untreated
standard-risk ALL were enrolled (median age 3 y, range 1 to 9 y; PDF p.3). Pharmacokinetic samples for oral and CIVI TG
came from 41 patients (21 male). No weight, height or BSA is given.
- Oral: 9 plasma samples (pre-dose, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 h) after the FIRST 60 mg/m2 oral TG dose of
  maintenance (PDF p.2 Methods).
- IV: first CIVI of 20 mg/m2/h for 24 h (480 mg/m2) during consolidation; CSF by lumbar puncture and plasma
  from a different site at steady state, near the end of the infusion.
- Assay: reverse-phase HPLC with UV detection of TG in plasma and CSF (LOQ below 0.05 uM). 8-OH-TG was
  estimated from the TG standard curve (no 8-OH-TG standard). RBC thioguanine nucleotides (TGN) measured 7 days after a CIVI course and before each maintenance cycle.
- Analysis: noncompartmental (trapezoidal AUC extrapolated to infinity, terminal half-life by log-linear regression); clearance = dose rate / Css. No model was fitted.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic model, no ke0, no effect site. Not an opioid. The authors compare plasma and CSF concentrations
with in vitro cytotoxicity: IC50 0.04-0.07 uM in three human lymphoblastic lines (cited from their reference 2),
minimal cytotoxic concentration 0.04 uM; plasma stayed above 0.04 uM for a mean 5.1 h (range 1-7.5 h) after the oral
dose, and exposures as short as 4 h were cytotoxic in cell lines. The CIVI target plasma concentration from the
phase I trial was 1 uM (Kitchen 1997). Those are in vitro thresholds, not clinical effect concentrations.

## Covariates
Oral AUC did not correlate with age (r = -0.12, P = 0.48) or differ by sex (P = 0.53) (Fig. 3, PDF p.4). Dosing is per m2 throughout.

## Caveats
Single first oral dose, no intrapatient variability studied; oral exposure varied 50-fold (derived: 9.5 / 0.18 = 53).
The oral AUC and the CIVI clearance come from different courses (oral at the start of maintenance, CIVI in consolidation).
The authors note dose-dependent TG kinetics and attribute the higher Cmax and AUC at 60 mg/m2, compared with a smaller study at 40 mg/m2 (median Cmax 0.1 uM, median AUC0-6 0.16 uM h, 5 children), to that. They suggest low oral bioavailability may come from first-pass aldehyde oxidase to 8-OH-TG. No food or fasting condition is stated in the text I read. No oral bioavailability was measured (no IV dose in the same child at the same dose level).
Internal inconsistency: mean Cmax 0.46 +/- 0.68 uM in the abstract and text versus 0.52 +/- 0.72 uM in Table 2.
