# Verret 2020: perioperative gabapentinoids for postoperative pain, meta-analysis

The archived PDF is the corrected article (PDF p. 1 notes "Corrected on November 13, 2020"); it already carries the
erratum's correction (control 32.6 mg, below). The one-page erratum itself is `PMID_32667154_erratum.pdf`. Page
numbers: PDF p. n = printed p. 264 + n. Supplemental digital content (SDC) is archived beside it; see the
"Supplemental digital content" section.

## Study
- Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials (PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42017067029). Medline,
  EMBASE, CENTRAL, Web of Science to January 2018, plus ClinicalTrials.gov and litigation-released unpublished
  reports (none eligible) (PDF pp. 2-3).
- Included: adults (>= 80% aged >= 18 y) having surgery; gabapentin or pregabalin started between 1 week before and
  12 h after surgery; comparator placebo, another analgesic regimen or usual care. Excluded: regional analgesia as
  comparator; patients already taking gabapentinoids (PDF p. 2).
- 322 trials (N = 28,465) in the review; 281 (N = 24,682) with quantitative data in the meta-analysis (PDF p. 3).
  Gabapentin 146 trials, pregabalin 122, both 13 (participant counts printed as 5,800 / 4,228 / 421, which do not sum
  to 24,682; as printed, PDF p. 5). Single dose in 68% of trials, multiple in 31%; given before surgery in 71%, after
  in 4%, both in 25%. Regional analgesia co-used in 9% of trials (PDF p. 5).
- Primary outcome: pain intensity (0-100) at 6, 12, 24, 48, 72 h (windows 0-6, 7-12, 13-24, 25-48, 49-72 h).
  Secondary: subacute pain (weeks 4-12), chronic pain (>= 3 months), cumulative IV morphine equivalent (0-24, 0-48,
  0-72 h; IV morphine taken as twice oral), persistent opioid use, lengths of stay, adverse events (PDF pp. 2-3).
- RevMan 5.3 random effects; GRADE; trial sequential analysis. Clinical significance judged against a minimally
  important difference (MID) of 10/100, with 20-30/100 "appreciable" and 50/100 "substantial" (PDF p. 3).
- Risk of bias: unclear 62% (174/281), low 11% (32), high 27% (75) (PDF p. 5).

Trial sequential analysis, SDC7 (rendered pages; O'Brien-Fleming, 5% two-sided): required information size 507
(6 h), 643 (12 h), 737 (24 h), 5,058 (48 h) and 8,536 (72 h) patients. The Z-curve crosses the benefit boundary at
6, 12, 24 and 48 h; at 72 h (4,134 patients accrued) it crosses neither the benefit nor the futility boundary, so
the 72 h analysis is inconclusive, which the article does not say (it lists only 6-48 h as needing no further
trials, PDF p. 7). The plots do not state the effect size the information size was computed for.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: patient-reported pain intensity on a 0-100 scale (other scales converted), not a concentration; this is
  dose-exposed vs control, with no concentrations, MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.
- The authors judge every acute-pain difference below the 10/100 MID (Abstract; Results PDF p. 5). The 6 h point
  estimate is -10 (CI -12 to -9), at the MID itself, and gabapentin alone at 6 h is -12 [-15 to -10] (Table 2); the
  "not clinically significant" conclusion also rests on Table 3 (PDF p. 8, rendered page): the proportion of patients
  reaching a difference greater than 10/100 differed by risk difference -0.26 [-0.31 to -0.20] at 6 h, -0.21 at 12 h,
  -0.16 at 24 h, -0.07 at 48 h, -0.05 at 72 h; at 20/100 only -0.14 (6 h) and -0.05 (12 h); 0 at 30 and 50/100 at
  every time. The text reads these as a slightly greater probability of a >10/100 effect at 6-24 h, none at 48-72 h,
  and no appreciable or substantial effect (PDF p. 5). (Sign convention of the risk difference not defined in the
  table; reported as printed.)
- Effect consistent by drug, dose regimen, timing, rest vs dynamic pain and comparator; heterogeneity partly from
  co-analgesia (regional or not) and risk of bias; low-risk-of-bias trials showed no or smaller effects (PDF p. 5).
- No effect on chronic pain; small, not clinically significant subacute effect (-6/100).
- Opioid sparing small (about 8 mg IV morphine in 24 h); the lower nausea/vomiting risk was not associated with the
  opioid reduction (Discussion, PDF p. 8).
- Trial sequential analysis: information size about seven times the required size; the authors conclude further
  acute-pain trials are not needed (PDF pp. 7, 9). By SDC7 this holds at 6-48 h; the 72 h analysis is inconclusive
  (see Supplemental digital content).
- The article's "not different with the type of drug" (PDF p. 5) sits beside subgroup-difference I2 of 79% (6 h)
  and 85% (12 h) in eTables 3-4, gabapentin showing the larger effect; no P values are printed for these tests.

## Covariates
Not applicable. Prespecified subgroups: funding, drug, dose (high = pregabalin >= 300 mg/day or gabapentin
>= 900 mg/day), inpatient vs ambulatory, regular opioids, risk of bias; post hoc: country income, pronociceptive
surgery (no effect modification) (PDF pp. 3, 7).

## Caveats
- Evidence quality for the primary outcome is low (risk of bias, inconsistency); I2 88-91% for acute pain at
  6-48 h; potential publication bias for opioid use, chronic pain and hospital stay (PDF pp. 7-8).
- Ataxia, delirium, respiratory depression, abuse and persistent opioid use could not be adequately assessed
  (few trials, imprecision); no trial assessed addiction (PDF pp. 7-8).
- Internal inconsistencies, as printed: dizziness CI 1.12-1.39 (Table 1) vs 1.13-1.39 (Results text, PDF p. 6);
  pregabalin 24 h pain n 5,169 (Table 2) vs 5,119 (Table 3); drug-level participant counts on PDF p. 5 do not sum to
  24,682.
- More, from the supplements: subacute pain gabapentinoid n is 750 in eTable 8, and 750 + 642 = 1,392, the total in
  the Results text (PDF p. 5) and in Table 2 (588 + 804); Table 1 prints 650, so its 650 looks like a typo; gabapentin 6 h MD -13
  [-15, -10] in eTable 3 vs -12 [-15 to -10] in Table 2; the post hoc country analysis is called not significant
  (PDF p. 7) while eTable 22 prints a subgroup-difference I2 of 77% (-6 vs -10), with no P value. eTable 16 prints
  an I2 of "415" for risk of bias (a typo).
- SDC2 (eTable 1, characteristics of each trial) is not archived.
