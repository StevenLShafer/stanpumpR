# West 1997: general model for allometric scaling

## Study
Theoretical paper (no subjects, no drug). It derives quarter-power allometry of metabolic rate B ∝ M^(3/4) from a space-filling fractal branching network with invariant terminal units (capillaries) and minimized energy dissipation. Printed pages 122-126 (PDF pages 1-5; page 6 is the publisher's cover sheet).

## Model and parameters
Y = Y0·M^b (Eq. 1). Key derived exponents (Table 1, printed p125, predicted vs observed from Schmidt-Nielsen and Peters):
- Metabolic rate B: predicted 3/4 (observed 0.75); O2 consumption 3/4 (observed 0.76).
- Blood volume Vb: 1 (observed 1.00); lung volume 1 (1.05); cardiac stroke volume 1 (1.03).
- Cardiac output 3/4 (0.74); cardiac frequency -1/4 (-0.25); respiratory frequency -1/4 (-0.26); circulation time 1/4 (0.25).
- Aorta radius 3/8 (0.36); aortic pressure and blood velocity 0 (0.032 and 0.07); total resistance -3/4 (-0.76).
- Number of capillaries 3/4; capillary density -1/12 (-0.095); O2 affinity P50 -1/12 (-0.089).
(The text layer scrambles Table 1's row alignment; the pairings above follow the paper's variable order and the stated 3/4, 1, -1/4 results, and the table would need a rendered page to confirm every row.)
Generalisation: a = D/(D+1) for a D-dimensional organism (D=3 gives 3/4). Networks: area-preserving branching (β = n^-1/2) in large pulsatile vessels, Poiseuille cubic branching (β = n^-1/3) in small vessels. Human estimates: n = 3, N ≈ 15 levels to the Poiseuille crossover ≈ 22 total (Nc ≈ 2×10^10 capillaries).

## Caveats
Idealised zeroth-order model (authors' words: ignores tapering, turbulence, nonlinear effects). Predictions for circulation time (3/8 predicted vs 0.25 observed), volume flow to lung (3/8 vs 0.80) and alveolar radius (1/4 vs 0.13) do not match well in Table 1. Observed small mammals deviate slightly above 3/4.
