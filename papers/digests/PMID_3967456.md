# Aoki 1985: oral amantadine kinetics in healthy elderly men

Amantadine is not a stanpumpR drug and the paper is not cited in the repository; `drugs` is empty.

## Study
- 10 healthy men aged 60-76 y (median 68), weight 45.5-91.5 kg (median 72.3), 24-h urine CLcr 43-89 mL/min (median 64);
  two subjects studied twice a year apart (1A, 3A) (Table I, PDF p2; Table IV, PDF p6). No other medication.
- Amantadine HCl syrup 10 mg/mL, single-blind, randomized to 0 (placebo), 25, 50 or 75 mg per dose: a single fasting
  dose, and after >= 2 weeks' washout 25-75 mg every 12 h for 21 doses (10.5 days) (PDF p1, p3). One subject received
  33.3 instead of 25 mg in the single-dose study (PDF p4). Eight subjects completed 9 single-dose and 10 multiple-dose
  studies.
- Plasma at 0-12, 24, 48, 72 h after the single dose; troughs on days 5, 10 and 11; 0-96 h after the last dose; urine
  over 120 h (single) and 7 days (after the last dose) (PDF p2-3).
- Gas chromatography (Biandrate), linear 10-1000 ng/mL, duplicates within 10% (PDF p3).
- One-compartment analysis: kel from log-linear regression of samples >= 9 h after dosing; Vd = f x D / (AUC x kel);
  Vdss = f x D / (T x Cpav x kel); plasma clearance and renal clearance (12-48 h urine) as defined on PDF p4.

Trough Cpss by dose (Table II, PDF p2): medians 229 ng/mL (25 mg bid), 297 (50 mg bid), 475 (75 mg bid); log trough
rose with dose (r = 0.68, PDF p5). Peak Cp after the first dose median 113 (25 mg) and 141 (50 mg) ng/mL. Amount in the
body at steady state (A, from urine) 160-400% of the maintenance dose (median 220%) (PDF p5).

Young vs elderly (Table IV, PDF p6; young from the authors' 1979 study): kel 0.047 vs 0.024 1/h; t1/2 14.7 vs 28.9 h;
Vd 10.4 vs 6.03 L/kg; CLrenal/CLcr 4.20 vs 2.07.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect modelling. Target trough Cpss 300 ng/mL, the trough of the effective, tolerated 200 mg/day dose
in young adults; the authors' lab found >500 ng/mL no more protective than placebo, so doses should avoid exceeding it
(PDF p1, p7). Suggested dose: about 0.7 mg/kg twice daily, or about 1.4 mg/kg once daily, in healthy elderly men
(PDF p1, p7). Symptom questionnaires showed no dose-related pattern (PDF p6). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
kel and t1/2 did not correlate with CLcr over 43-89 mL/min (PDF p5-6); in the pooled young and old data (22 subjects)
kel correlated more with age (r = 0.73) than with CLcr (r = 0.61) (PDF p7). Elimination was independent of dose.

## Caveats
- n = 10 men; healthy; no women; one-compartment, absorption still under way up to 9 h.
- **Half-life figures do not agree across the paper.** The abstract gives a median t1/2 of 28.9 h (range 18.5-45.0);
  Table III gives 25.9 h (18.5-40.8) after the single dose and 29.0 h (22.6-45.0) after the 21st dose; Table IV gives
  28.9 h (22.6-45.0). 28.9 h equals ln2 / 0.024 (derived), the median steady-state kel, and the abstract range joins the
  single-dose minimum to the steady-state maximum.
- **Time to peak**: abstract 4.0-8.0 h, Table II 4.0-8.0 h, text "4.0 h (range 4.0 to 9.0 hr)" (PDF p1, p2, p5).
- **Table IV elderly Vd 6.03 (5.73-7.87) L/kg, n = 3** cannot be traced to Table III, whose Vd values run 1.48-6.13 and
  contain neither 5.73 nor 7.87. Unresolved. Table IV's elderly Cpss 229 (212-334), n = 3, is the 25-mg-bid group of
  Table II.
- **Renal clearance exceeds the steady-state plasma clearance** in several subjects (e.g. subject 1A, renal 5.47 vs
  plasma 2.37 L/h; subject 6, 12.96 vs 10.53), and the medians are 7.71 vs 3.69 L/h. (Row alignment of the
  continuation table checked: renal clearance / Table I CLcr reproduces the printed ratio for 7 of 9 subjects, derived;
  the two re-studied subjects 1A and 3A do not reproduce, unresolved.) Since renal clearance cannot exceed
  total clearance, one of the two definitions or columns is off; the paper does not comment (unresolved).
- Abstract says trough Cp "varied <300%"; the text gives interindividual variation of 150-240% (PDF p5).
