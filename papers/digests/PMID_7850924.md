# Tracewell 1995: hydroxyurea population PK in cancer patients

## Study
- 54 cancer patients (28 men, 26 women) at Duke and Nebraska; mean weight 71.8 kg (SD 15.6; range 43.2-118), mean age 53 y (SD 13.5; 23-76). CrCl measured in 24 patients: mean 71.5 mL/min (24-117) (Patients, PDF p2).
- 46 patients received IV infusions of 48-72 h at 1.9-8.7 mmol/h (84-315 mg/m2/h) (UNMC, one sample at end of infusion, n=18) or, at DUMC (n=28), a 30 min loading infusion followed by a 24 or 48 h maintenance at 3.1-24.3 mmol/h (165-950 mg/m2/h); sampling at 15 and 30 min and 4, 8, 16, 20, 24 h (+32, 40, 48 h). Some patients had 2 or 3 treatment courses.
- 8 UNMC patients got oral hydroxyurea 20 mmol/m2 every 6 h (study dose after 1-8 prior doses), sampling every 30 min for 5 h then hourly to 8 h.
- Assay: colorimetric (iodine oxidation, nitrite diazotisation, 540 nm), plasma, sensitivity 1 ug/mL, CV 13% at 1 ug/mL and 4.8% at 5 ug/mL.
- NONMEM III / PREDPP II (ADVAN6). Between-patient variance proportional, residual error proportional.
- Infusions above 14 mmol/h (600 mg/m2/h) were excluded from fitting (11 patients, 16 treatments) because steady-state concentrations were extremely variable and the fit would not converge; 35 patients, 48 treatments remained.

## Model and parameters
One compartment; parallel Michaelis-Menten (saturable, presumably hepatic) elimination and first-order (presumed renal) elimination; first-order oral absorption with bioavailability F. Model 2 (parallel pathways) fitted slightly better than Michaelis-Menten alone (MVOF -426.443 vs -424.562, not statistically different) and was kept on external evidence.

Derived in the paper (p5): renal clearance = Ke x Vd = 90.8 mL/min, about 75% of normal GFR (this uses Vd 38.1 L: 0.143 x 38.1 = 5.45 L/h = 90.8 mL/min; with the 71.8 kg value 38.8 L it would be 92.5 mL/min, derived). Saturable pathway nonlinear between about 0.06 and 2.8 mmol/L (5-213 mg/L); above that the linear pathway dominates (p4 text). Only about 10% of an oral dose is removed on first pass (p5).

Abstract values in concentration units: Vmax 0.097 mmol/h/L, KM 0.323 mmol/L, ClR 90.8 mL/min, Vd = 0.186 x weight + 25.4 L, Ka 2.92 /h, F 0.792.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis. No ke0, no effect compartment, no therapeutic range; steady-state concentrations versus infusion rate (Fig 1) are the only exposure link. Not an opioid; opioid audit not applicable.

## Covariates
- Weight on Vd (combined data only, P < 0.05; slope 0.186 L/kg, intercept 25.4 L). Not significant in the IV-only fit; BSA not significant.
- Creatinine clearance on elimination at high infusion rates: inclusion just missed significance (P < 0.042 stated as failing), patients with high steady-state concentrations tended to have lower CrCl.
- Age, gender and dose rate were also tested (no effect reported).

## Caveats
- Single sample per patient in 18 IV patients; sparse sampling elsewhere; plasma colorimetric assay with 1 ug/mL sensitivity.
- IV rates above 14 mmol/h (600 mg/m2/h) excluded, so the model is not validated at the highest doses; the authors advise renal monitoring there.
- Model 2 was not statistically better than model 1; chosen on prior animal and human evidence.
- KM and Ke IIV were estimated near zero and not reported.
- Oral data: 8 patients, with high between-patient variance on Ka (omega^2 2.48).
