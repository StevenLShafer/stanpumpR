# Tanaka 2004: mechanisms of cardioprotection by volatile anesthetics

## Study
Narrative review (15 pages) of anesthetic-induced preconditioning (APC) of myocardium by halothane, enflurane, isoflurane, sevoflurane and desflurane. Covers history (the coronary-steal controversy), signalling (adenosine A1 and delta-1 opioid receptors, Gi proteins, PKC, tyrosine kinases, MAPK), sarcolemmal and mitochondrial KATP channels, reactive oxygen species, mitochondrial protection (Ca2+ overload, permeability transition, bioenergetics), and clinical evidence (human atrial trabeculae, PCI, CABG).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid review. Reported only in passing: morphine (mu agonist with some delta-1 activity) added to isoflurane reduced infarct size more than either alone, blocked by 5-HD and by naloxone (PDF p.5, printed 711, citing Ludwig 2003); delta-1 agonists TAN-67 and BW373U86 potentiated isoflurane protection. No MEAC, analgesic concentration, ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, or equianalgesic/potency ratio is given. Isoflurane preconditioning is described as dose-dependent in rats, with cardioprotection persisting 15-30 min after washout (isoflurane in rats, rabbits and dogs) but not 30 min after sevoflurane (PDF p.2, printed 708); no concentrations are tabulated.

## Covariates
None quantified (species and age differences mentioned qualitatively).

## Caveats
Narrative, not systematic; mostly animal and in vitro work; the authors state that clinical benefit is not yet shown by a large trial (PDF p.10-11, printed 715-716).
