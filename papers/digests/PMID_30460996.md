# Kharasch 2019: steady-state bioequivalence of brand and generic bupropion XL 300 mg in depressed adults

## Study
- Randomised, double-blind (over-encapsulated), four-period, four-sequence crossover (NCT02209597), 4-week lead-in,
  then four 6-week periods without washout: brand Wellbutrin XL 300 mg (Valeant) and generics from Par, Watson, Mylan.
- Adults with major depression in remission on bupropion XL 300 mg once daily for >= 4 months. PK n = 70 (median
  47 y [37-57], 76 kg [63-94], BMI 26 [23-33]; 53 women, 17 men); complete BE data n = 67. Adherence 98.6%.
- PK session 10-20 days into each product, after an overnight fast; samples 1-24 h post-dose; achiral LC-MS/MS of
  bupropion, hydroxy-, erythrohydro- and threohydrobupropion. NCA; BE by mixed-model ANOVA, 90% CI within 80-125%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Clinical end points: relapse (1 subject, on the Watson generic), MADRS (clinic and smartphone), ASEC side effects,
  patient perceptions; no differences among products. No concentration-response analysis, no EC50, no ke0/tPeak.
- Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
- Only XL 300 mg once daily; results are achiral; 76% women; patients, not volunteers.
- All three generics bioequivalent; hydroxybupropion Tmax ratio for Watson 84% (74-95).

- Observation for the auditor (not a stated claim in the repo): on 300 mg/day the model (SR-based, Ghimire) gives
  average steady-state bupropion 84 ng/mL and hydroxybupropion 1389 ng/mL (`inst/help/drugs/bupropion.md:37`).
  Kharasch's XL medians give 35 and 871 ng/mL (derived). Correcting the model for XL bioavailability
  (0.68/0.955 = 0.71, Connarn 2017) gives about 60 and 989 ng/mL (derived), still about 1.7x and 1.1x the observed
  values. The observed hydroxybupropion/bupropion exposure ratio is about 25 (paper) vs 16.5 in the model. Consistent
  with the help page's warning that steady-state concentrations were not checked against the model
  (`inst/help/drugs/hydroxybupropion.md:32`).
