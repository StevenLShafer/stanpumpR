# Doufas 2019: remifentanil ventilatory depression in OSA vs controls

## Study
Prospective observational cohort, Stanford, 50 awake surgical patients aged 18-70 (head and neck surgery), BMI < 35: 30 with moderate-to-severe OSA (AHI >= 15/h on polysomnography) and 20 controls (9 no OSA, 11 mild OSA; 6 controls had no PSG and were enrolled on STOP-Bang <= 2). Age 43 +/- 13 (OSA) vs 34 +/- 10 yr (controls); median weight 87 vs 78 kg (Table 1, printed p217, text layer only). No premedication; supine in the OR before induction, FiO2 0.5 by tight mask, poikilocapnic. 3 min baseline, then remifentanil 0.2 ug/kg **ideal body weight** (Devine)/min for 10 min, chosen to reach Ce about 4 ng/mL at the end (p214-215). Median total dose 144 ug (OSA) vs 146 ug (controls) (p218). No washout data. No remifentanil concentrations measured. Minute ventilation (VE) from the anesthesia-machine flow sensor (60-s moving window, read at 5-s intervals). Stopped early if SpO2 < 85% for > 10 s or apnea > 60 s. NONMEM 7.3 FOCE, PLT Tools, 1000 bootstraps.

## Model and parameters
Effect-site concentrations were not estimated: they were predicted with the published Minto remifentanil 3-compartment model and its age-adjusted EEG ke0 (refs 15,16; Discussion p222 gives ke0 0.52 /min), driven by the IBW-based infusion. VE = VE0 - (VE0 - VEmin) * Ce^g/(Ce50^g + Ce^g), multiplied by a Bouillon-type CO2 feedback term (eqs 1-3, p215-216); the CO2 term was needed because the sigmoid alone predicted more depression than observed at end-infusion.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: minute ventilation (VE) during a non-steady-state remifentanil infusion in awake, spontaneously breathing patients (no EEG, pupil or analgesia measured).
- **Ce50 (ventilation) 2.20 ng/mL** (Table 2): predicted effect-site concentration at half-maximal VE depression, VEmin fixed 0, CO2-stimulation term included. Not an assayed concentration, so total/free and blood/plasma do not apply. gamma 3.55.
- **ke0**: not estimated for ventilation (the infusion scheme did not allow it, p222); the EEG-derived Minto ke0 0.52 /min was borrowed. The authors cite other studies' ventilatory ke0 0.34-1.30 /min (refs 34-36) and EEG ke0 0.43 (Egan) and 0.52 (Minto). No tPeak, no hysteresis analysis.
- OSA did not change sensitivity: OSA effect on Ce50 +7% (99% CI -5 to +21%); AHI and minimum nocturnal SpO2 not significant. OSA lowered gamma (3.36 vs 3.81, OFV drop 100), judged clinically insignificant (p219).
- Other C50s the authors list (p223): Olofsen 1.6, Bouillon 0.92, Babenco 1.4, Nieuwenhuijs 0.7 ng/mL (CO2-aware); Nieuwenhuijs 3.3 and Dahan 2.6 (no CO2 term). EEG spectral edge C50 11.2 (Minto) and 19.9 (Egan) ng/mL.
- Observed: VE fell from about 6.5-7 to about 3 L/min by end infusion at Ce about 3.5-4 ng/mL (Fig. 2A, read from image); no SpO2 < 92% from remifentanil; OAA/S never < 4.
- MEAC / analgesic concentration: none reported. Potency ratio: none.

## Covariates
Tested on Ce50 (additive): OSA status, BMI, sex, age, AHI, minimum nocturnal SpO2, CPAP use. Only age reduced OFV (by 8) but worsened population fit, so none retained (p218). IIV on VE0 and Ce50 only.

## Caveats
Predicted, not measured, concentrations; Minto model derived in normal-weight patients though about 40% had BMI >= 30 (repeat with Kim 2017 PK gave nearly identical results, p221-222); ke0 borrowed from EEG; no washout data; anesthesia-machine flow sensor; 6 controls without PSG (AHI 3, SpO2 94 imputed); awake patients only, not asleep or sedated. The authors say they cannot tell whether OSA alters remifentanil concentrations. Ce50 depends on the CO2 model and the ke0 used, so it is not interchangeable with a steady-state C50.

Independent comparison with the library's remifentanil entry: R/drugs_remifentanil.R:116 sets tPeak 1.6 min (its comment converts this to ke0 0.577 /min, the library's value, not Eleveld's 1.09) and :117 MEAC 1 ng/mL (drugDefaults_global.csv: lower 0.8, upper 2, typical 1.2, endCe 1). Doufas's borrowed Minto ke0 0.52 /min is close to 0.577 /min (my comparison, not in the paper). The ventilatory Ce50 of 2.20 ng/mL is about 2x the library MEAC of 1 ng/mL and just above the upper-typical 2.0; expected, as half-maximal VE depression is a different end point from a minimum effective analgesic concentration. No discrepancy. Caution: Doufas's Ce50 is tied to Minto PK with IBW dosing, whereas the library kinetics are Eleveld/Kim.
