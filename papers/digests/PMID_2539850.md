# Thomson 1989: lisinopril population PK in elderly and renal-disease hypertensives

## Study
- **Data:** pooled from two Merck-supported multicentre trials of lisinopril in hypertension. Trial I was in patients
  aged 65 or over. Trial II was in renal impairment: CrCl 30-60, under 30, or haemodialysis.
- **Patients:** 140 had data, 79 were excluded (compliance, missing data and the like) plus one outlier, leaving 60.
  40 came from the elderly study and 20 from the renal one.
  - Mean age 65 y and mean weight 72 kg.
  - Age bins on Figure 1a run from 20-30 (2 patients) to 81-90. Weight 51-120 kg; creatinine up to about
    1000 µmol/L.
  - 13 had compensated cardiac failure, one was on haemodialysis, and 17 took a cardiac glycoside (Table 1).
- **Dosing:** 2.5-40 mg once daily (median 10 mg), titrated.
- **Sampling:** a steady-state profile at 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 12 h after at least 2 weeks on a constant dose. 381
  concentrations in all, about 6 per patient. Steady-state peaks ranged 6.4-343 ng/mL.
- **Assay:** serum radioimmunoassay.
- **Analysis:** NONMEM, steady-state one-compartment models with first-order or zero-order input. Log-additive IIV
  and residual error.

## Model and parameters
One compartment with first-order absorption, apparent in F (oral data only). The final model is model 11 for CL,
model 13 for V and model 19 for ka (Table 2). Table 3 is on PDF p6 (printed 62).

- **Equation 5 (p5):** prints the same model rounded: 0.25 × wt × (creatinine/70)^-0.89 × (age/65)^-0.45, times
  0.65 with cardiac failure.
- **Paper's check (p8):** at age 40, 70 kg and creatinine 70, CL/F is 21.8 L/h, against 15 L/h measured by Ajayi in
  young men. My recomputation with the Table 3 values gives 21.9, which confirms -0.451 and not -0.41.
- **Derived at 65 y, 70 kg, Cr 70:**
  - CL/F is 17.6 L/h.
  - kel = CL/V = 0.479 /h, against ka 0.104 /h, so absorption is the slower process: flip-flop. The paper itself
    does not remark on this.
- **Table 2 likelihood differences:**
  - Weight × Cr^θ4 against Cr alone: LLD 172.
  - Adding age: LLD 14 (p < 0.001).
  - Female: -41 (NS).
  - Cardiac failure: LLD 24, against 15 for cardiac glycoside.
  - BSA in place of weight did worse (-15).
  - Volume: weight LLD 1, BSA 1, weight power 0, creatinine 6 and age 0, all NS or NA.
- **Input:** zero-order input was worse (LLD -281), and its standard errors could not be obtained.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD model was fitted, and there is no therapeutic range. BP was not related to concentration.
- Adverse effects (Table 4) were more common with renal dysfunction, with no clear concentration relation. Peaks of
  200-350 ng/mL were tolerated by some patients.

## Covariates
- **CL/F:** serum creatinine (a power, on concentration; better than estimated CrCl), actual body weight (linear;
  ideal body weight did not improve it in a preliminary analysis), age (a power) and cardiac failure (× 0.645).
- **Not significant on CL/F:** sex, alkaline phosphatase and BSA.
- **V/F:** no covariate.

## Caveats
- The authors warn against extrapolating above 90 kg (only 4 obese patients) or below 40 y (only 2 patients under 40,
  both renal).
- Compliance was a likely major confounder, and CL/F IIV stayed at 52% after covariates.
- V's IIV was over 100% in every model. The authors say V's variability could not be established from steady-state
  data.
- Cardiac failure and glycoside use are confounded (all CHF patients took a glycoside).
- Saturable ACE binding (terminal phase) was assumed negligible at these doses.
- Steady-state sampling only, so ka is the least informed parameter. The earliest post-dose sample is at 1 h.
