# Müller 2022: pregabalin and the MAC of sevoflurane in women

## Study
Single-centre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (Vienna, Sept 2019-Feb 2021; EudraCT 2017-001439-37). 78 women recruited, 75 analysed (25 per group; 3 lost to laryngospasm needing IV anaesthetic). ASA 1-2, age 30-65 y, elective breast surgery; no sedative or analgesic premedication, no chronic pain. Groups: placebo, pregabalin 150 mg, pregabalin 300 mg, single oral capsule 1 h before induction (p. 2). Induction by 8 vol% sevoflurane inhalation only, LMA at BIS < 40; end-tidal sevoflurane then held at the target for at least 15 min before a 3-5 cm skin incision; positive = gross purposeful movement of head or limb within 1 min. Up-and-down design, first patient 1.6 vol%, step 0.2 vol% (pp. 2-3). MAC by isotonic regression, bootstrap CIs (5,000 resamples), 97.5% CIs for the two main comparisons (Bonferroni). Serum pregabalin from a venous sample just before incision, LC-MS/MS (MassTox kit). Sample size by simulation (placebo MAC 2, SD 0.3; 20% reduction to 1.6), 22 per group for 80.8% power, 25 enrolled.

Patient data (Table 1, p. 5, mean (SD)): the text layer scrambles the rows; age about 47-51 y, height 165-167 cm and a weight row of about 68-70 kg could be read, but the text states only that groups were similar (p. 4), and no value is relied on here.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: movement to skin incision under sevoflurane (MAC), humans. 300 mg lowered MAC by 33% (2.16 to 1.44 vol%; abstract/p. 4); 150 mg by 16%, not significant. 300 vs 150 mg difference 0.36 (CI -0.09 to 0.80), not significant. BIS at incision was higher with pregabalin (about 48-51 vs 38 in the placebo group, Table 3, scrambled) at the same 50% movement end point; the authors read this as a spinal rather than cortical effect. Secondary: recovery-unit pain NRS lower with pregabalin (0.7-1 vs 2.5), side effects 4%, 12%, 32% (dizziness 8/25 with 300 mg) (Table 4). No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis measured; no MEAC or EC50; no concentration-effect analysis, only mean concentration per dose group. Not an opioid; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
None analysed beyond group. Women only, ASA 1-2, one surgery type.

## Caveats
Authors: probably underpowered for 150 mg; women only; post-incision anaesthesia not standardised so secondary outcomes are hypothesis-generating; unclear whether the effect is on pain perception or motor response. Up-and-down MAC estimates depend on the start concentration (their ref 24). Funded by Chiesi Pharmaceuticals.
