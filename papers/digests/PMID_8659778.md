# Ornstein 1996: cisatracurium in elderly versus younger surgical patients

## Study
24 ASA 1-2 neurosurgical patients, Columbia University: 12 elderly (65-82 y; 72 +/- 5 y, 69.9 +/- 10.8 kg) and 12 younger
(30-49 y; 42 +/- 5 y, 77.6 +/- 16.2 kg) (Table 1, PDF p3); weight <= 150% of ideal. Thiopental, fentanyl, midazolam;
0.7% end-tidal isoflurane in 70% N2O. Cisatracurium 0.1 mg/kg (2 x ED95) over 5-10 s. Adductor pollicis T1 by force
transducer (TOF every 15 s). Arterial samples at 2-480 min (17 times), acidified, HPLC-fluorescence (sensitivity 10 ng/mL,
accuracy 9%, precision 13%). Bi- and triexponential fits by weighted least squares; two compartments chosen
(Boxenbaum); parameters from standard equations with elimination from the central compartment only (PDF p2).

No Q or V2 reported. Renal excretion of unchanged drug 9-15% (two elderly) and 11-24% (four young) of the dose; laudanosine
Cmax 22 +/- 5 vs 20 +/- 5 ng/mL, half-life 4.4-10.9 h (PDF p4). The authors note a 17% larger volume and a 19% (4 min)
longer half-life in the elderly (PDF p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD model, no ke0 or C50. Observed T1 data (Table 1): onset to 90% block 3.4 +/- 1.0 (elderly) vs 2.5 +/- 0.6 min
(P = 0.015); time to maximum 5.2 vs 4.2 min (NS); maximum block 99.0 vs 99.5%; recovery T5 44.8 vs 44.8, T25 61.1 vs
55.6, T75 78.2 vs 71.1, T95 88.6 vs 83.7 min, TOF 0.7 81.6 vs 82.5 min, recovery index 19.0 vs 16.1 min (all NS).
Recovery about a third longer than reports without volatile agents (PDF p4).

## Covariates
Age group only.

## Caveats
Small groups (12 and 11 for PK); central-elimination model underestimates Vss; isoflurane; one elderly patient on
diltiazem excluded from PD; two elderly patients given neostigmine before 90% recovery.
