# Schoretsanitis 2020: antidepressant concentrations in the third trimester, systematic review and meta-analysis

Published version of the work already digested from the accepted manuscript (`PMID_32238008_aam.md`). Every number
below was re-read from this file; where this card differs from the manuscript card, it says so.

## Study
PRISMA systematic review (Embase and Medline, searched August 2019) plus one unpublished trimipramine case from the
Trondheim TDM database (PDF pp2-4, printed 431-433). Inclusion: within-subject design; plasma/serum antidepressant
levels in the 3rd trimester (week 26 onward) and at baseline (before pregnancy, or more than 4 weeks after delivery);
treatment long enough for steady state; trough or presumed-trough samples. 169 records screened, 14 full texts,
11 studies included, 11 antidepressants (Figure 1, PDF p4).
- Primary outcome: **alteration ratio** = dose-adjusted concentration (ng/mL per mg/day) in the 3rd trimester divided
  by baseline (pooled pregravid and postpartum). Computed as a mean of individual patients' ratios where individual
  data existed, otherwise a sample-size-weighted mean of cohort ratios (PDF p3).
- Secondary: mean difference (MD), baseline minus 3rd trimester (positive = lower in pregnancy), random-effects
  DerSimonian-Laird with a fixed-effect check, R package `meta`; only where at least 3 studies existed (PDF pp3-4).
  AUCs were divided by the interval as a proxy for trough; DDD-adjusted data divided by the DDD; values below the
  LOQ set to LOQ/2; nmol/L converted to ng/mL with Hiemke 2018 factors.
- Co-author AA Westin supplied the raw data of the largest cohort (Westin 2017, ref [7]) (PDF p3).
- Quality: ClinPK checklist, mean score 13.7 (PDF p6).
- Active moiety (parent + active metabolite) for clomipramine, imipramine, fluoxetine and venlafaxine (Table 1
  footnote); citalopram and fluoxetine/norfluoxetine enantiomers summed where reported separately (PDF p4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD, no effect site. Reference ranges quoted from Hiemke 2018 (AGNP 2017; PDF pp7-8): fluoxetine active moiety
120-500, fluvoxamine 60-230, imipramine 175-300, nortriptyline 70-170, paroxetine 20-65, sertraline 10-150,
venlafaxine active moiety 100-400, clomipramine active moiety 230-450 ng/mL; trimipramine printed "150-300 mg/mL"
(sic, PDF p8, printed 437). Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Discussion (PDF pp7-8): CYP2D6 induced and CYP2C19/CYP1A2 reduced in pregnancy; the citalopram fall
attributed largely to inactive R-citalopram (two patients with enantiomer data, ref [6], supplementary Table 2);
paroxetine direction depends on CYP2D6 genotype (Ververs 2009, not pooled for lack of baseline).

## Caveats
- Not independent of Westin 2017: Westin is an author and supplied that cohort's raw data; [7] contributes to every
  SSRI and venlafaxine pooled value, and dominates the sertraline forest plot (85.7% of fixed-effect weight).
- Combined ratios are means of individual ratios, which large ratios inflate; inverse-variance MDs can disagree
  (sertraline: ratio 1.38 but MD null).
- Table 1 n is the cohort size; fewer women may enter the ratio (footnote). For sertraline the forest plot uses
  3 + 3 + 12 = 18, against Table 1's 8 + 6 + 12 = 26. Weighted by the forest-plot n the cohort ratios reproduce the
  combined value: (0.77×3 + 1.37×3 + 1.53×12)/18 = 1.377 (derived). The manuscript card (`PMID_32238008_aam.md`)
  weighted by Table 1's n, got 1.26, and called 1.38 unreproducible; that caveat is resolved here.
  Citalopram (0.58×11 + 1.23×2 + 0.73×12)/25 = 0.70, fluoxetine mean of 0.68, 1.03, 0.88 = 0.86 and venlafaxine
  (0.34 + 0.72×7 + 1.52×5)/13 = 1.00 also reproduce (derived).
- Escitalopram: the text says 13 patients (PDF p7), but the Table 1 cohorts add up to 14.
- Trough timing not standardised (8-30 h in one study), adherence not assessed, some co-medication, small n (PDF pp8-9).
