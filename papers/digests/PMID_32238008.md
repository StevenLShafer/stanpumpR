# Schoretsanitis 2020: pregnancy effect on antidepressant pharmacokinetics (meta-analysis)

## Study
Systematic review and individual-patient meta-analysis (PRISMA) of within-subject
studies measuring serum/plasma antidepressant concentrations in the 3rd trimester
(from week 26) versus a non-pregnant baseline (pregravid or >4 weeks postpartum).
Embase and Medline searched August 2019; 169 records screened, 14 full-text, 11
studies finally included, covering 11 antidepressants (Results, printed p433; PDF p4).
No new PK data were generated — this is a secondary analysis of trough,
dose-adjusted levels (concentration per mg/day, ng/mL per mg/day). One previously
unpublished trimipramine case came from a large TDM database (ref [7], Westin 2017).

Primary outcome: the **alteration ratio** = dose-adjusted 3rd-trimester concentration
÷ dose-adjusted baseline concentration; <1 means a lower 3rd-trimester level. Ratios
were pooled as means of individual-patient ratios (individual participant data
approach), or weighted by study sample size where only cohort data were available.
A separate random-effects meta-analysis (DerSimonian–Laird) estimated mean
differences (MDs) in dose-adjusted concentration; performed only where ≥3 studies
existed (Methods, printed p432-433).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio — this is a disposition (TDM) review, not a
PD study, and none of the six stanpumpR drugs here is an opioid. Therapeutic
reference ranges are quoted secondhand from the AGNP consensus (ref [16], Hiemke
2018), not measured here: e.g. sertraline 10–150, paroxetine 20–65, fluvoxamine
60–230, nortriptyline 70–170, imipramine 175–300, fluoxetine active moiety 120–500,
venlafaxine active moiety 100–400 ng/mL (Discussion, printed p436-438; trimipramine
"150–300 mg/mL" on p438 is a ng/mL typo).

Worked clinical examples given by the authors (Discussion): citalopram MD 0.78 means
a 20 mg/d dose gives ~15.6 ng/mL lower 3rd-trimester concentration (p436); fluoxetine
MD 2.59 means ~51.8 ng/mL lower active-moiety at 20 mg/d (p436).

## Covariates
Not a covariate model. Qualitative mechanisms discussed: citalopram total level falls
because CYP2D6 (inactive R-enantiomer) is induced while CYP2C19 (active S) is only
mildly down; sertraline rises are tentatively linked to CYP2C19 inhibition.

## Caveats
Authors' limitations (printed p437-438): most data are single cases or small cohorts
(n often 1–12 per drug); prominent heterogeneity and wide CIs (risk of Type II error);
post-dose intervals for "trough" samples not standardised (8–30 h in one study); two
case reports did not confirm samples were trough; no adherence data; co-medication
present in some patients. Combined-ratio and MD methods can disagree in sign (sertraline:
ratio 1.38 but MD null). Dose-adjusted concentration (C/D), not clearance, is the unit.

Notice also: several ratios are "mean of two measures" (footnote a) or active-moiety
sums (clomipramine, imipramine, fluoxetine, venlafaxine). The paroxetine 0.82 is a
single 4-patient cohort mean, printed in the "Alteration ratio" column with "NA" in
the "Combined alteration ratio" column — it is not a pooled meta-estimate.
