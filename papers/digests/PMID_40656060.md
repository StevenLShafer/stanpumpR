# Amann 2024: duloxetine therapeutic reference range revised (systematic review, meta-analysis)

## Study
Systematic review and meta-analysis by the AGNP TDM working group (PROSPERO CRD42021258633). Four databases
(MEDLINE, Web of Science, PsycINFO, Cochrane), searched October 2021, updated August 2023. 340 articles screened;
11 studies included in the qualitative synthesis (five concentration-effect, one later excluded for contradictory
data; four concentration studies; three PET studies); 7 studies (n = 331) in the quantitative analysis (PDF p3, p6).
Blood levels = serum or plasma duloxetine (total; free fraction not discussed). Random-effects meta-analysis in R
(metafor, meta). Quality and level of evidence by the authors' own instrument (Hart 2021 protocol). Adults only.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model is fitted. Concentration-dose figures it reports (PDF p6, Table 4 and §3.6; p8 §4.2):
- Mean C/D ratio used for Table 4: 0.43 [0.28, 0.58] (ng/mL)/(mg/day), taken from Hiemke 2018.
- Table 4 expected levels in somatically healthy populations: 30 mg 12.9 [8.4, 17.4]; 60 mg 25.8 [16.8, 34.8];
  90 mg 38.7 [25.2, 52.2]; 120 mg 51.6 [33.6, 69.6] ng/mL.
- C/D ratios 0.37 (monotherapy) to 1.38/1.39 (with fluvoxamine 25 mg/d; 3.7-fold) (Paulzen 2011, 2016).
  Smokers 0.27 (Fric 2008) and 0.38 (Augustin 2018); non-smokers 0.81 and 0.85.
- Pooled mean concentration 54 ng/mL, 95% CI [44; 64] at mean dose 70 mg/day (7 studies, n = 331, I² = 71.6%).
- Pooled median 44 ng/mL, IQR 22–72 ng/mL (4 studies, n = 223; mean dose 76 mg/day, mean 56 ng/mL).
- Mean level with potentially interfering comedication 38 ng/mL [18; 59] vs 57 [51; 62] without (p < 0.01).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or tPeak, and none reported. End points:
- Antidepressant response. De Donatis 2019 (fixed 60 mg/d, n = 66): bell-shaped quadratic with HAMD-21 improvement;
  maximum effect 35–120 ng/mL; responder IQR 72–116 (median 92) ng/mL. Waldschmitt 2009 (n = 103; monotherapy
  n = 36): responders (CGI = 1) median 93, IQR 65–123 ng/mL; non-responders median 47, IQR 23–54; ROC threshold
  58 ng/mL (p = 0.011). Volonteri 2010 (n = 45): HAMA optimum 40–100 ng/mL; no HAMD-21 correlation. Rovera 2016
  (≥65 y): negative correlation. Level of evidence C (PDF p3 Table 1, p4).
- Adverse effects: only Volonteri 2010 found irritability/anxiety at the highest levels (range 5–135 ng/mL);
  level C (PDF p4).
- Transporter occupancy (PET, healthy men): 80% SERT above 10–15 ng/mL; 50% NET at 58 ng/mL (PDF p5).
- Range derivation (PDF p8): lower limit from the expected concentration at 60 mg/day in real-world patients; upper
  limit from the 75th percentile in responders, read as an efficacy optimum rather than a toxicity bound.
- No potency ratio.

## Covariates
On blood levels (Table 3, PDF p6): smoking lowers levels (Augustin 2018: median 38.4% lower, C/D 53.6% lower);
fluvoxamine raises the C/D ratio 3.7-fold; lithium, clozapine and mirtazapine lower it (Waldschmitt 2009). No
effect of sex (5 studies) or age (3 studies); creatinine clearance not correlated (Rovera 2016).

## Caveats
Few, small, mostly naturalistic flexible-dose studies; no RCT with concentration-effect design; quadratic fits
criticised (a logistic function is preferred); heterogeneity high (I² 71.6% and 90.5%); PET in healthy men only;
adults only. Table 4 uses Hiemke's C/D ratio, not one from this review. Lead author reports AbbVie employment.
