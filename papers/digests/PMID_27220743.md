# Chermá 2017: methylphenidate blood concentrations on optimised doses

## Study
Naturalistic Swedish study (Linköping region). 59 outpatients aged 9-69 (76% male) with DSM-IV ADHD on chronic
extended-release methylphenidate at a clinically titrated dose giving good control (CGI-S level 2): 22 general
psychiatry (GP), 16 dependency clinic (DD, former substance abusers abstinent >= 3 months), 21 child and adolescent
psychiatry (CAP) (Table 1). Morning dose withheld at home; whole blood (Venosafe Na-fluoride/Na-heparin) and oral
fluid at arrival (trough, time 0), then 1 h and 6 h after the morning dose. UHPLC-MS/MS; blood MPH range 0.20-30
ng/mL, ritalinic acid (RA) 10-1500 ng/mL (PDF p3). 50 patients took OROS-MPH (Concerta) on the study day and only they
were analysed further; 9 took other ER products. Median daily dose about 1.0 mg/kg in all groups (range 0.18-2.2).

Inter-individual CV of dose-normalised MPH: 55% (1 h), 39% (6 h). Oral fluid MPH about fourfold blood, RA about
25-fold lower, OF/blood ratio CV 100-131%: oral fluid not recommended.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis: all patients were defined as well controlled. The authors call the concentrations
consistent with therapeutic ones elsewhere and suggest blood concentrations might guide dose optimisation. Not an
opioid.

## Covariates
Adults vs children: no difference in MPH concentration or dose per kg; RA and RA/MPH lower in children. No effect of
sex, caffeine, nicotine, alcohol, abuse history.

## Caveats
- **Which patients the interquartile ranges describe**: the sentence giving 5.4 (3.8-7.7) and 9.3 (6.6-13) follows the
  dose-stratified Table 4 subgroup (36 patients at 36, 54 or 72 mg; PDF p8), and its medians differ from Table 3's
  medians for all 50 OROS patients (5.8 and 10). The Key Points and Discussion present 5.4/9.3 as the OROS cohort's
  values. The paper does not resolve this; the 5.4/9.3 medians are consistent with the 36-patient subgroup (Table 4
  medians 4.8-5.8 and 9.2-11), not with Table 3's 50.
- Whole blood, not plasma; venous; many adults dosed more than once a day; non-randomised doses.
