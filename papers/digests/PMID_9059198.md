# van den Nieuwenhuyzen 1997: TCI alfentanil for postoperative analgesia

## Study
- 37 ASA I-II adults, 18-65 y, elective general / major orthopaedic / gynaecological surgery >1 h; exclusions: cardiovascular, pulmonary, hepatic or renal disease, opioids in the preceding month (PDF p2). Random allocation to intraoperative bolus opioid: fentanyl (F, n=14), sufentanil (S, n=12), alfentanil (A, n=10). One A patient excluded (surgical complication), leaving 36 (Table 1).
- Premedication temazepam 0.3 mg/kg PO; N2O 66% plus enflurane or isoflurane; pancuronium.
- Postoperative: all received target-controlled infusion of alfentanil (TCIA), 0.25 mg/mL syringe, pump limited to 100 mL/h. Software (Pascal, Atari Portfolio) used the Maitre 1987 population PK set (ref 7); infusion rate recalculated every 10 s. Initial target CT 20 ng/mL; every 5 min, if pain and Ramsay sedation <=3, CT raised by 10 ng/mL until no further request and VAS <=3.0; after loading (CT unchanged 30 min) step 5 ng/mL, lockout 5 min; CT lowered 5 ng/mL after 10 min with no demand.
- Arterial plasma alfentanil by capillary GC (detection limit 0.1 ng/mL, CV <5%), sampled before every CT change and 5 min after a new CT reached. Total plasma concentration (no protein-binding measurement in this paper).
- Logistic regression per patient: P = Cp^g / (EC50^g + Cp^g) on adequate (no demand and VAS <=3.0) vs inadequate analgesia against measured Cp. Patients who never complained of pain were assigned EC50 = 0.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: quantal analgesia (adequate = no request for more analgesia and VAS <=3.0), early postoperative period (first ~2 h in the recovery room, loading then maintenance). Not ventilation, EEG or pupil.
- EC50 (concentration for 50% probability of adequate analgesia; logistic, per patient, total plasma, arterial blood): group medians 26 / 39 / 52 ng/mL for F / S / A, individual range 0-151 (PDF p5). Excluding patients without pain: 47 / 44 / 52. Groups did not differ (Kruskal-Wallis).
- Individual EC50 not estimable in 2 F, 1 S, 3 A patients (overlap) and 1 S, 1 A (single inadequate observation).
- MEAC quoted from the authors' earlier study (ref 6, 1993): median 59 ng/mL (range 26-159) at start of therapy in 20 orthopaedic patients (PDF p6, printed 22). Not re-measured here. Plasma total concentration assumed (not stated in this paper).
- Measured Cp of alfentanil in A-group patients when they first reported pain: 24-97 ng/mL, median 56 (PDF p4). Simulated residual intraoperative opioid concentration at pain onset: 1.7 ng/mL fentanyl, 0.10 sufentanil, 44 (18-141) alfentanil.
- ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported. Equianalgesic or potency ratios: none reported. No respiratory depression at the step sizes used; sedation <=3 throughout.
- Time from last intraoperative bolus to pain: 68 (SD 19) min A vs 101 (52) F and 136 (29) S (Table 2, rendered, P<0.05 for A). Time to satisfactory analgesia median 18 / 15 / 14 min (F/S/A); loading period 72 / 55 / 56 min.

## Covariates
None analysed beyond the intraoperative opioid group. Weight and age are given only in Table 1 (age 37/43/42 y, weight 73/78/70 kg for F/S/A per the text layer, which is scrambled; not confirmed on the page image).

## Caveats
- Small groups (n=10 analysable per group); EC50 from within-patient logistic fits with few points, and no-pain patients set to 0 pull the group medians down.
- Residual volatile agent and intraoperative opioid contributed to analgesia.
- Maitre 1987 PK came from intraoperative studies; authors judge MDAPE 20-33% acceptable and bias absent (CIs include zero).
- Total, not unbound, concentration; the follow-up paper (PMID_10472227) shows unbound fraction varied 5-15%.
