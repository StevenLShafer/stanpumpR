# Stauble 2014: hydrocodone as a pro-drug for hydromorphone after Cesarean delivery

## Study
- Single-sample observational cohort, University of Louisville (PDF p2, printed p27). 156 women after Cesarean
  section, age 18-45, BMI under 40; 5 had general anaesthesia, the rest the same anaesthetic and analgesia; the
  first 40 did not receive intrathecal morphine (Duramorph), the rest did (PDF p3).
- Drug: oral hydrocodone as needed (product and unit dose not stated), plus ibuprofen 800 mg every 8 h for all.
  Median duration of hydrocodone use 49 h (range 4-79.5 h). Total dose mean 72.7-102.1 mg by phenotype (Table 1).
- One blood sample (EDTA plasma; the paper calls it "serum" and "plasma" interchangeably) on the morning of
  discharge, described as the 3rd day after delivery. Time since last dose not reported.
- Pain: VAS-type pain index (0-10 by the medians/ranges) at the same time; 13 side effects surveyed.
- CYP2D6 genotype: Luminex xTAG (17 variants, 2 rearrangements). Assay: LC-MS/MS at Mayo Clinic for hydrocodone,
  hydromorphone, norhydrocodone, dihydrocodeine (method in its ref 9); LLOQ, and whether conjugates were hydrolysed,
  not stated.
- Statistics: ANOVA by phenotype; ordered logistic and ordinary least-squares regression of pain on
  concentrations, adjusted for age, BMI, total dose, duration, intrathecal morphine (Stata).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Table 2 (PDF p3): regression of pain index on concentration, N = 156 in the title but footnote excludes 6 women
  taking CYP2D6 inhibitors. Hydromorphone beta -0.045 per ng/mL (SE 0.020, p = 0.023); hydrocodone -0.005 (SE
  0.008, p = 0.502); dihydrocodeine -0.065 (p = 0.561); norhydrocodone -0.016 (p = 0.664). Only total dose and
  duration were independently associated with pain at p < 0.01 (footnote).
- **MEAC**: none reported for hydrocodone or hydromorphone. No minimum effective or analgesic concentration,
  no concentration-response curve; single-time-point association only.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis**: none reported (single sample, timing relative to dose unknown).
- **Potency** (Introduction, PDF p1, cited, not measured): hydromorphone 7-10 times morphine's analgesic potency
  (citing Baselt 2011); mu-receptor Ki 0.36 for hydromorphone versus 41.58 for hydrocodone (citing Volpe 2011;
  unit not printed), i.e. over 100-fold affinity.
- Authors' conclusion: pain relief tracks hydromorphone, not hydrocodone; hydrocodone behaves as a pro-drug.

## Covariates
CYP2D6 phenotype (genotype-derived). Ancestry noted: 6 of 8 UM African-American, 5 of 7 PM Caucasian (PDF p3).
No pregnancy-versus-nonpregnant comparison: all women were postpartum.

## Caveats
- Single unscheduled sample, time since last dose unknown; concentrations reflect dose, timing and genotype mixed.
- Postpartum (day ~3) only; no pregnant or non-pregnant comparator, so nothing about pregnancy PK.
- Internal inconsistencies: Table 1 versus text for EM/IM hydromorphone; Fig. 2 versus Table 1 percentages;
  Table 1 footnote reads "p for trend > 0.05" while the text says the differences were significant (P < 0.05).
- Small PM (7) and UM (8) groups with SDs larger than means; the pain association is weak (authors' word).
- Free versus conjugated hydromorphone not stated (see open questions); hydrocodone formulation not stated.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:34** says Stauble is a postpartum post-Cesarean CYP2D6 genotype study that gives no
  pregnancy clearance ratio. The paper is exactly that: one postpartum sample, no pregnant or non-pregnant
  comparator. **matches.** The row's citation year "2013" is the online date (21 Nov 2013); the issue is 2014
  (Clin Chim Acta 429, 2014); pages 26-29 correct. Minor.
- **Independent check of R/drugs_hydrocodone.R (not cited there).** The library's hydrocodone model has a MEAC of
  12 ng/mL (`R/drugs_hydrocodone.R:214`) and tPeak 90 min (`:186`), both provisional; this paper offers no MEAC or
  tPeak to confirm or replace them. Its finding that analgesia tracks hydromorphone is the same claim the header
  attributes to Boswell 2013 (`:146-147`), from the same group.
  The model's CYP2D6 weights (`:221-226`: poor 0.121, intermediate 0.506, ultrarapid 1.502 relative to normal)
  compare with this paper's derived group-mean ratios of 0.090, 0.66 and 0.91. The poor-metaboliser floor agrees
  roughly; the ultrarapid group here shows no excess over EM. Single samples at unknown times with n = 7-8 make
  this a weak check, not a discrepancy.
  The absolute ratio (about 0.21 in EM) is ~18x the Kapil 2015 AUC ratio of 0.0117 used at `:300`; this cannot be
  judged until it is known whether the assay measured total (conjugated) hydromorphone, so it is an open question,
  not a finding. **cannot verify.**
