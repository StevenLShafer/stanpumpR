# Tsuda 2010: population pharmacokinetics of oral tamsulosin in children 2-16 years

## Study
- **Population** (Table 2, PDF p4; printed p91): 189 paediatric patients, 1082 plasma samples, from three
  Boehringer Ingelheim trials (Table 1A, p2). 45 with non-neuropathic bladder (trial 1), 144 with neuropathic
  bladder (trials 2 and 3). Step 2 data set: median age 8 (2-16) years, weight 25.0 (12.1-92.2) kg, height
  125 (63-175) cm, BMI 17.41, BSA 0.94 m2, serum creatinine 0.96 mg/dL, Cockcroft-Gault CLcr 46.85 mL/min,
  AAG 19.95 (9.76-74.53) uM; 105 male, 84 female; 87 White, 83 Asian.
- **Dosing**: oral tamsulosin HCl, fed. Trial 1 single doses 0.1-0.8 mg by weight band (Table 1B, p3);
  trials 2 and 3 once daily, capsule contents sprinkled on applesauce or yogurt, weight-banded low/medium/high
  doses 0.025-0.4 mg, i.e. about 0.001-0.008 mg/kg (Table 1C, p3), up-titrated weekly.
- **Sampling** (Table 1A): rich in trials 1 (pre-dose to 26 h) and 2 (to 33 h after the last dose); sparse in
  trial 3 (weeks 6 and 10).
- **Assay**: HPLC-MS/MS (APCI), lithium-heparin plasma, linear 0.100-50.0 ng/mL; QC imprecision < 9.2%,
  inaccuracy within +/- 6.7% (p3). Concentrations are of tamsulosin HCl, total (bound + unbound).
- **Model**: NONMEM V, FOCE-I. One compartment, first-order absorption with lag time (two compartments and
  zero-order or sequential absorption rejected, PDF p6; printed p93). Base model from trials 1-2; covariates with all three.
  Exponential IIV on CL/F, V/F, Ka with a CL-V covariance; combined proportional + additive residual error.

Derived: half-life at 70 kg, AAG 20 uM = ln2 x 37.5 / 2.28 = 11.4 h (the paper gives 12 h for a 73 kg patient,
PDF p12; printed p99). Shrinkage: CL 5.15%, V 18.5%, Ka 21.0%, epsilon 17.1% (PDF p6).

Simulated typical exposure after 0.1 mg, AAG 20 uM (Figure 4, p10; figure inset, text layer only): AUCss(0,tau)
159, 94.2, 57.9, 45.0 ng h/mL and Cmax,ss 11.2, 6.04, 3.41, 2.60 ng/mL at 12.5, 25, 50, 70 kg. At 25 kg,
AAG 10/20/30/40 uM: AUC 55.1/95.3/131/179, Cmax 3.61/6.09/8.25/10.9. With the high weight-based dose, median
paediatric AUCss was 56.5-133% and Cmax,ss 46.3-119% of adult values after 0.4 mg (Figure 5; p7 text).

Background quoted from other sources (Introduction, p2): adult IV clearance 48 mL/min; IV volume 16 L (PDF p11);
oral absorption > 90% fasted; food lowers AUC about 20% and Cmax 30-40%; tmax 6-7 h fed, 4-5 h fasted;
9-15% excreted unchanged in urine; metabolised by CYP3A4 and CYP2D6. These are not this study's measurements.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No efficacy or concentration-effect analysis, no ke0, no therapeutic range. The authors argue that
unbound concentration drives effect and that AAG changes total but not unbound exposure (Discussion, PDF p12).
Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Weight (allometric, fixed exponents 0.75 and 1, reference 70 kg) and AAG (power, reference 20.0 uM) on CL/F and
V/F. Tested and not retained: age, sex, race, height, BMI, BSA, creatinine, CLcr, liver tests, GGT (entered
forward, dropped backward), BMI on V/F (likewise), patient population, anticholinergics, dose.

## Caveats
- Children 2-16 years only; not valid under 2 years (no data). Only 5 patients > 70 kg; VPC underpredicted the
  4-12 h concentrations by up to 33% in the 50-100 kg group (n = 16).
- Apparent parameters (CL/F, V/F): bioavailability of the opened-capsule fed formulation is not identified.
- Dose and concentrations are expressed as tamsulosin **HCl**; an implementation must keep dose and
  concentration on the same (salt) basis.
- Sponsor-run analysis (Boehringer Ingelheim employees).
- Comparison with adults is against NCA from two adult healthy-volunteer studies (median 73 and 74 kg) after
  0.4 mg, not against an adult population model.
