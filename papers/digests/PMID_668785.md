# Moore 1978: diazepam disposition in pregnant women at parturition

## Study
18 women at 37-40 weeks being induced with IV oxytocin (Sydney), each given diazepam 10 mg as a rapid IV injection
within the 10 h before delivery (Table 1, PDF p2: age 19-36 y; weight at delivery 48-98 kg). Group I, uneventful
puerperium, n = 14 (12 vaginal, 2 caesarean); Group II, n = 4, vaginal delivery then tubal ligation under thiopental
and nitrous oxide on postnatal day 1 (L.A., J.B., R.R.) or day 2 (R.B.). Venous sampling for at least 3 days; whole
blood in 9. Plasma (and blood) diazepam by electron-capture GC (calibration 25-1000 ng); desmethyldiazepam seen only
in trace amounts, not quantified. Non-compartmental: terminal t1/2 from the log-linear tail (reached by about 30 h),
CLtp = dose / AUC0-inf (trapezoids plus tail); blood clearance = CLtp / blood:plasma ratio. Co-medication varied
(amobarbital, bupivacaine, pethidine and others; Table 1).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model: the postnatal rise in concentration prevented fitting exponentials or estimating a volume
(PDF p8). Table 2 (PDF p6), mean (SD):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD. Placental transfer (Table 4, PDF p7; 16 cases, dosing-to-delivery 0.1-9.5 h): umbilical/maternal venous plasma
ratio 0.91-1.69, mean 1.31, above 1 in 14 of 16; Apgar 6/9 to 10/10. Diazepam was in cord blood within 6 min (L.J.).
The Discussion (PDF p9) says maternal exceeded umbilical concentration in most cases, which contradicts Table 4 and the
abstract (umbilical greater in most cases); the table is the source of record.

## Covariates
None modelled. In 13 of 14 Group I women plasma and blood diazepam rose by an average of 50% within 18 h after
delivery, without a haematocrit change (Figure 1, PDF p4; PDF p7); the authors attribute the long half-life to this
redistribution around delivery and ambulation, not to reduced elimination. C.M. (highest CLtp, shortest t1/2, blood:
plasma 0.77) is suspected of reduced binding. Debendox in L.A. and R.B. (Group II) is a possible interaction.

## Caveats
Total (not free) concentrations; no free fraction measured. Non-stationary kinetics around delivery, so CLtp is a
time average and t1/2 is distorted. Historical, mixed-age literature controls; Group II n = 4 with co-medication.
Single 10 mg dose, assay calibration in ng amounts only.
