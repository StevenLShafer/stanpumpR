# Koup 1975: digoxin pharmacokinetics in renal failure and dosage design

## Study
Seven hospitalised adults with severe renal impairment (Table I, PDF p2-3, printed 10-11): ages 26-76 y,
weights 43.2-95.4 kg, BSA 1.36-2.10 m²; GFR (¹²⁵I-iothalamate) 0 to 13.0 and creatinine clearance 0 to 7.3
mL/min/1.73 m²; serum creatinine 5.0-10.3 mg/dL. Patients 3 and 4 anephric, 3-5 on intermittent haemodialysis
(studied between dialyses); patient 7 had congestive heart failure. None had received digoxin in the prior month.
Digoxin (Lanoxin) ~2.5 µg/kg lean body weight (patients 1-2) or ~5 µg/kg (others), in 9 mL saline,
**IV over 5 min**; time zero at the end of infusion. Serum at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8 h then about daily for 3-4 days;
daily urine collections. Radioimmunoassay (Clinical Assays) of serum and urine. Protein binding by equilibrium
dialysis with tritiated digoxin (15 normal, 15 renal failure sera). Serum and urinary excretion rate fitted
simultaneously to a biexponential (two-compartment) model, NONLIN; A and B corrected to bolus (Loo-Riegelman).
A second dataset (Jusko, Szefler and Goldfarb 1974; 16 chronically dosed patients, 17 observations, oral tablets)
tested six steady-state prediction methods.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None: no effect measure, no ke0 or time to peak effect, no therapeutic range stated. Serum digoxin by RIA only.

## Covariates
Body clearance against creatinine clearance (Eq 6) or measured digoxin renal clearance (Eq 7), with a reduced
nonrenal intercept in congestive heart failure. No relationship found between creatinine clearance and volume.

## Caveats
- n = 7 (5 analysed), severe renal failure only (CLcr ≤ 7.3 mL/min/1.73 m²); Eq 6/7 fitted on 6 of these plus 4
  normal subjects from another study.
- Internal inconsistencies: the abstract gives Vss 195-489 L/1.73 m², the table and text 189-481 L (absolute);
  Fig 6's caption gives slope 1.101 and intercept 36.0 for the creatinine-clearance plot, which are Eq 7's
  values, not Eq 6's (1.303, 41); ClB and ClM SDs are both printed 17.4.
- Clearances are per 1.73 m²; the per-kg conversions above are mine.
- RIA cross-reactivity with metabolites in azotemia acknowledged; low-concentration terminal data limit t½β.
- The text layer scrambles Tables I-II (e.g. patient 7's Vβ 194 appears as "4.1"); values above are from the
  rendered pages.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **drugs_digoxin.R:55** — calls Koup 1975 an intravenous digoxin study: the paper gave digoxin IV over 5 min with
  a two-compartment fit: **matches**. The comment that it is still to be obtained is stale (it is archived) and the
  library already has an IV model, `digoxinIV` (Cheng 1997; R/drugs_digoxinIV.R): see the open question.
- **vasoactiveRegistry.csv:56 / data-raw/vasoactiveRegistry.R:447** — citation, journal, volume and pages
  18:9-21: **match**.
- **Independent comparison with digoxinIV (Cheng 1997), not cited for it**: Cheng's CL = 0.0003·CLcr + 0.0676
  L/h/kg gives ~0.069 L/h/kg at Koup's creatinine clearances (3-7 mL/min). Koup's body clearances, converted to
  absolute and per kg (derived), are 0.039-0.062 L/h/kg in the four patients without heart failure and 0.020 in
  the one with it, i.e. 57-90% of Cheng's: Cheng's model would clear digoxin faster than Koup measured in severe
  renal failure. Vss per kg (2.8-5.8 L/kg, derived) brackets Cheng's 5.35 L/kg at CLcr 5. Koup's t½β 2.2-4.1 days
  compares with 0.693·Vss/CL = 2.2 days from Cheng's parameters at CLcr 5 (derived; a lower bound on
  the terminal half-life, which digoxinIV's own two-compartment solution would set). The digoxinIV header does not
  discuss this. The library's oral model (Yukawa, apparent /F) cannot be compared directly.
- No numeric value from this paper is used in code; no unit conversion to check.
