# Ornoy 2018: ADHD drugs in pregnancy and lactation (review)

## Study
Narrative expert review (Pharm Res, article 46, 11 pages), single author (Laboratory of Teratology, Hebrew
University). No stated search method, no meta-analysis, no tables. Covers methylphenidate and dexmethylphenidate,
amphetamines/methamphetamine/lisdexamfetamine, atomoxetine, clonidine, guanfacine and bupropion: teratogenicity,
perinatal outcome, neurodevelopment and breast feeding. Evidence is epidemiological (registries, cohorts,
case-control studies) and animal data; **no maternal pharmacokinetic data for any stimulant**.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic parameters. Facts stated (PDF page = printed page):

- Methylphenidate: duration of effect Ritalin IR about 4 h, SR about 6 h, LA about 8 h, Concerta 10-12 h;
  dexmethylphenidate 4-5 h, XR 6-8 h (p. 2). Placental transfer shown in mice; the author found no human
  placental-transfer data, and gives a molecular weight of 269.8 Da (p. 2). Secreted in small amounts in milk,
  generally undetected in infant blood (p. 3).
- Methylphenidate outcomes (p. 3): major anomalies 10/309 vs 13/358 controls; spontaneous abortion 54/382 vs
  10/382; Bro adjusted RR 1.55 (95% CI 1.03-2.36) for spontaneous abortion, also raised in untreated ADHD;
  cardiac anomalies 9/486 (1.85%) pooled Swedish data without controls.
- Amphetamines: dextroamphetamine 4-5 h and 6-8 h; lisdexamfetamine 10-12 h; Adderall 4-6 h, XR 8-12 h (p. 4).
  Swedish registry 132 first-trimester exposures, 4 malformed (3%) (p. 4). Amphetamine 20 mg/day gives measurable
  amphetamine in infant urine; breast feeding contraindicated (p. 5).
- Clonidine: crosses the placenta and "is cleared more rapidly from the blood during pregnancy" (refs 67, 68;
  comparator not stated in the review) (p. 6); infant blood about half of maternal on breast feeding (p. 6).
- Bupropion: nursing infant expected to receive about 2% of the maternal dose; bupropion and hydroxybupropion not
  detected in 2 infants' blood (p. 8).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No concentrations, ke0, EC50 or therapeutic ranges. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency
ratio. Durations of action (above) are clinical product durations, not PK quantities.

## Covariates
None quantified. Pregnancy is discussed only as an exposure for fetal outcome, not as a PK covariate.

## Caveats
Narrative, single author, no systematic search. Much amphetamine evidence comes from mothers misusing
methamphetamine, with alcohol, tobacco and other drugs as confounders (the author's own caveat, p. 4).
Confounding by indication for spontaneous abortion (p. 3). The 269.8 Da figure matches methylphenidate
hydrochloride rather than the base; the review does not say which. Nothing on CES1, CYP2D6 or
renal clearance of these drugs in pregnancy.
