# Brown 2017: oral psilocybin pharmacokinetics at 0.3, 0.45 and 0.6 mg/kg

## Study
- Open-label phase 1 study, University of Wisconsin-Madison, NCT02163707 (p1-2).
- 12 healthy adults with prior psychedelic experience: 10 male (83%), 11 White and 1 Native American
  (Table 1, p5). Table 2 (p5): age 43 (24-61) y, height 179.1 cm, weight mean 78.1 / median 71 (60.9-119.8) kg,
  BMI 24.2, BSA 1.96 m², CLCr 126 (71-177) mL/min. (Table 2's CLCr/1.73 m² mean of 73 is lower than the raw 126
  despite a BSA above 1.73; it is not explained.)
- Synthetic psilocybin, purity > 99%, corrected for water content, in capsules. Escalating single oral doses of
  0.3, 0.45 and 0.6 mg/kg actual body weight, at least 4 weeks apart, after a standard breakfast (p3).
  33 doses: 12 at 0.3, 11 at 0.45 and 10 at 0.6 mg/kg (Table 4).
- Sampling: K2-EDTA plasma at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 18 and 24 h; 24 h urine; ascorbate added to
  stabilise psilocin (p3).
- Assay: LC-MS/MS (Covance, GLP). Psilocin LLOQ 0.5 ng/mL in plasma and 5.0 ng/mL in urine. No parent psilocybin was found
  in any sample. There was no hydrolysis step, so it measured unconjugated psilocin; glucuronide was not assayed (p3, p8).
- NCA in WinNonlin 6.4 (linear-up/log-down AUC0-24). Population PK in NONMEM 7.3, FOCE-I, log-transformed data,
  with an M3 check of BLQ data. 353 measurable observations, plus 48 BLQ (p4-5).
- The dose was scaled to psilocin by F1 = 0.71863, the psilocin/psilocybin molar mass ratio, so CL/F and V/F are on a
  **psilocin-equivalent dose basis** (p4).

## Model and parameters
Final model: one psilocin compartment with first-order absorption and linear clearance. A reversible "psilocin
glucuronide" pool shares the central volume: formation K23, hydrolysis back to psilocin K32. It was not fitted to
glucuronide data. A plain two-compartment fit put the peripheral volume at over 20,000 L (p6), or 21,500 L (p7). IIV
on CL/F and V/F with a BLOCK(2) covariance. IIV on Ka and K32 was fixed to zero. There are no covariates: weight, renal function,
bilirubin, albumin and dose had bootstrap CIs that included null (p6).

The PK was linear over 0.3-0.6 mg/kg: the slopes of dose-normalised AUC and Cmax against dose did not differ from 0 (p6).
Simulation: a fixed 25 mg dose approximates the exposure of 0.3 mg/kg (Figs 3-5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD was modelled: no ke0, EC50 or Emax, and no concentration-effect relation. Effects are deferred to a later report (p11).
Safety: no serious adverse events; mild transient hypertension, tachycardia and late headache (p7). Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None in the final model. Weight did not predict CL/F; weight on V/F was dropped after the bootstrap (p6).
Creatinine clearance did not improve the fit.

## Caveats
- n = 12, mostly White men, and all had used psychedelics before. Only a twofold dose range.
- Ka was poorly identified. The glucuronide pool is a structural assumption with no glucuronide measurements, and
  glucuronide elimination is not in the model (p8).
- The authors say a two-compartment model was slightly favoured by the objective criteria and chose one compartment
  plus the glucuronide pool on inspection (p5, p7).
- The bootstrap CI for CL/F (29.0-224) is wide. The IIV column formats are unclear.
- The Table 4 unit labels and the duplicated row are probably errors. Its NCA exposure is not reconciled with Table 3's CL/F (open questions).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **V/F 298 L** (`docs/illicit-drugs-specification-review.md:110`): Table 3 gives 298 L (SE 20.2%), on a psilocin-equivalent dose
  basis. **Matches.** The contradiction by a 2025 review cannot be verified from this paper (open question). Note that Brown's
  V/F belongs to a model with a reversible glucuronide pool that shares the central volume. A one-compartment V/F from another
  study is not strictly the same quantity.
- **Glucuronide compartment inferred, not measured** (`R/drugs_psilocybin.R:72`, `psilocybin.md:27`, review doc `:111-112`): the paper says
  that glucuronide was not assayed and that the pool's kinetics were only indirectly estimated (p6, p8). **Matches.**
- **"Different apparent parameters"** from Holze 2023 (`R/drugs_psilocybin.R:72`): both studies use the psilocin-equivalent basis
  (Brown's F1 = 0.71863; the library's 0.718628, `:24`, `:112`), so they compare directly:
  - CL/F is 164 vs Holze 172 L/h (0.95x), so nearly the same.
  - V/F is 298 vs 448 L (0.67x).
  - Ka is 0.367 vs 0.89 /h (0.41x). Brown reports no lag; Holze's lag is 0.3 h.
  - The NCA half-life is 3 h vs Holze 1.8 h.
  The statement is true for V/F, ka and half-life, not for clearance. **Matches (qualitative).**
- **Uncited cross-check of the library model against Brown's NCA** (derived; library on the psilocybin-dose basis: CL/F 239.3 L/h,
  V/F 623.4 L, ka 0.89 /h, lag 18 min):
  - Peak: the library gives 0.85 ng/mL per mg psilocybin at 1.96 h. Brown's per-mg Cmax (Table 4, taken as per absolute mg)
    is 0.70-0.84 at a median Tmax of 2.03-2.05 h. The library is 0-20% higher at peak, with the same timing.
  - Exposure: the library's AUC is 4.18 ng·h/mL per mg. Brown's AUC0-24 per mg is 6.06-6.84, so Brown's NCA exposure is
    about 45-65% higher. Brown's own Table 3 CL/F would give 4.38 (164/0.71863 = 228 L/h), near the library's value. So
    the gap lies within Brown's paper (open question), not between Brown's model and the library's.
- **Unit checks**: µg/L = ng/mL. Brown's CL/F is in L/h (the library converts Holze's L/h to L/min, `:123`). No conversion
  error, because the library does not use Brown's numbers.
- Verdict **partly-verified**: every statement the repo makes about this paper is supported. The 2025-review contradiction is
  not checkable here, and the paper's internal inconsistencies are recorded as open questions.
