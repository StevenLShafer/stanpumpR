# Varvel 1989: Absorption of transdermal fentanyl

## Study
- 8 surgical patients (5 F, 3 M), major spine surgery; mean age 45 yr (33-57), weight 68 kg (52-100). Stanford / Palo Alto VA, with Alza.
- IV: fentanyl 150 ug/min for 5 min (one patient 6.5 min) before thiopental induction; N2O 70% + volatile. Arterial samples to 6-8 h, then venous, to 1440 min.
- Transdermal: TTS-fentanyl 100 (nominal 100 ug/h; content 10.2 +/- 0.4 mg) on the upper chest from postoperative day 1 for 24 h; samples to 72 h. Postoperative morphine or meperidine (PCA or IM) throughout.
- Serum fentanyl by GC/MS, sensitive to 0.2 ng/mL, CV 6.9% (0.2-68 ng/mL). Residual patch content by HPLC.
- Individual triexponential fits (PCNONLIN, weights 1/Cobs^2); Loo-Riegelman deconvolution of absorption; bioavailability by dose-normalised AUC and by Loo-Riegelman.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, tPeak, EC50 or MEAC measured. The 1.8 ng/mL plateau is described as appropriate for postoperative analgesia (no analgesic end point measured; patients also received other opioids). Predicted 90% range for a 90 ug/h IV infusion 1.4-5.6 ng/mL; observed TTS 90% range 0.5-3.1 ng/mL. No respiratory depression observed. No potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n = 8; two patients had bioavailability > 1 (estimation error in delivered dose and clearance). Clearance assumed unchanged between the IV (anesthetized) and TTS (postoperative) phases. Concurrent opioids.
