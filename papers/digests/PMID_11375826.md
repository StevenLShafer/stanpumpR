# Mortero 2001: small-dose ketamine added to propofol sedation

## Study
Randomized, double-blinded trial in 40 ASA I-III outpatients having elective ambulatory surgery under monitored anesthesia care (laparoscopic tubal occlusion, cervical conization, superficial surgery); 39 analyzed (Propofol n=20, Coadministration n=19; p.1 abstract and Table 1). Exclusions included morbid obesity (>100 lb over ideal weight), CNS-active drugs, psychiatric disease, chronic pain. Premedication midazolam 1-3 mg IV; fentanyl 50 ug IV on arrival, then 50 ug increments for pain. Study solutions: propofol 10 mg/mL, or propofol 9.8 mg/mL with ketamine 0.98 mg/mL (a 10:1 mixture). A 1-3 mL bolus, then infusion at 0.3-0.5 mL/kg/min, titrated to OAA/S = 4 (p.2). Outcomes: end-expiratory PCO2 (nasal prong), RR, SpO2, mood and perception VAS, MMSE, and pain/hydrocodone use by phone for 5 days. No drug concentrations were measured.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Infusion rates (abstract, p.1): propofol 38 +/- 24 ug/kg/min (Propofol group) vs 33 +/- 13 ug/kg/min plus ketamine 3.7 +/- 1.5 ug/kg/min (Coadministration). Total doses (Table 1, p.3): propofol 2.2 +/- 1.7 vs 2.0 +/- 1.1 mg/kg; fentanyl 1.1 +/- 0.5 vs 1.1 +/- 0.5 ug/kg; ketamine 0 vs 0.24 +/- 0.13 mg/kg.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Respiratory: end-expiratory PCO2 was lower with coadministration (P<0.0001, Fig 1, p.3); RR higher at 45 and 60 min (13.6 and 13.8/min Propofol vs 15.4 and 16.0/min Coadministration, P<0.05). No group difference in BP, HR, SpO2 or OAA/S.
- Recovery: mood VAS and MMSE higher with coadministration (MMSE total after surgery median 25 vs 30; Table 2, p.4). Pain at rest, activity, and hydrocodone use were lower after discharge (Table 3, p.5; log-rank P=0.0001 for most).
- Not reported: ke0, tPeak, EC50, MEAC, any concentration-effect relation, any equianalgesic ratio. Ketamine/propofol interaction is shown only as dose-level effects; no interaction model.

## Covariates
None modelled. The groups did not differ in age, weight, height, gender or duration of surgery.

## Caveats
Small n; two-drug mixture at a fixed 10:1 ratio, so the ketamine effect cannot be separated from a dose of either; midazolam and fentanyl given to both; sedation titrated to a fixed OAA/S so comparable depth is assumed. The Coadministration group n is given as 19 in the abstract and Table 1 but as 20 in the Fig 2 legend (p.3).
