# Bullingham 1981: sublingual buprenorphine after hip replacement

## Study
Ten patients for elective total hip replacement (Oxford), age 45–75 y and weight under 80 kg by entry criteria;
Table 1 (PDF p3, printed 119, rendered): age 59.8 ± 3.7 y, weight 68.8 ± 3.0 kg (mean ± SEM), 6 M : 4 F, surgery
99.9 ± 3.8 min, blood loss 510 ± 91.0 mL. Diazepam 10 mg premedication; thiopentone, pancuronium, N2O/O2,
halothane 0.5%. Buprenorphine 0.3 mg IV over 1 min after induction; 3 h later, postoperatively, 0.4 mg sublingual
(two 0.2 mg tablets). Arterial samples at 0–180 min after the sublingual dose. Plasma buprenorphine by specific
radioimmunoassay (Bartlett 1980); total plasma drug. Demand analgesia from 6 h after the first dose: diamorphine
0.25 mg IV per press, the only analgesic until the next morning.

PK method (PDF p2): the IV dose's contribution was subtracted using the mean terminal decay rate from Bullingham
1980; sublingual AUC 0–3 h by numerical integration, compared with a postoperative IV AUC(0–∞) from a separate
comparable group of ten (0.3 mg IV), scaled by 4/3 for dose.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC:** none reported. No concentration–effect analysis; the authors note analgesia despite low plasma levels.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis:** none estimated. Discussion (PDF p5) attributes the long effect to slow, avid
  receptor association (qualitative only).
- **Analgesia (demand, diamorphine 0.25 mg IV/press):** onset 15–45 min (observer estimate); median duration
  534 min from the sublingual dose, defined as time to the fifth demand (PDF p3, rendered); abstract: about 9 h,
  similar to 0.3 mg IV or IM in McQuay 1980. No difference in demands at 7 and 13 h between SL, IV and IM groups.
- **Sex:** men demanded more (8.3 ± 0.1 vs 1.5 ± 0.1 mg diamorphine × 10⁻³/patient/min, 60–600 min; P < 0.0001);
  plasma levels did not differ by sex.
- **Ventilation:** postoperative PaCO2 raised (Table 3, about 5.9–6.4 kPa), no sex difference.
- **Metabolic:** glucose fell 0.58 mmol/L over 3 h after the SL dose; cortisol did not rise further.
- **Potency:** no formal ratio; 0.4 mg SL gave analgesia of similar duration to 0.3 mg IV/IM (comparison groups).

## Covariates
None modelled. Sex affected analgesic demand, not plasma levels.

## Caveats
n = 10; sampling only to 3 h, so neither Cmax nor F is complete (F is a 3 h partial value); IV contribution removed
by an assumed mean decay, not measured per patient; IV reference AUC from a different group; SEMs (not SDs);
one patient vomited a tablet at 10 min and was kept in the analysis; RIA specificity per 1980 methods.
