# Barr 2001: propofol ICU sedation PK-PD model

## Study
30 adult male medical/surgical ICU patients (age 21-80 yr), intubated and ventilated for more than 24 h (PDF p1, p3). Propofol by target-controlled infusion (STANPUMP, Harvard Pump 22), target raised from 0.5 ug/mL in steps of 0.25-0.5 ug/mL every 5 min to Ramsay 5-6, then titrated; weaned by 0.25-0.5 ug/mL every 10 min to Ramsay 2. First 20 patients (learning group) were run on the Dyck pre-existing model (unpublished, Table 5 "Original"); last 10 (test group) on the revised model derived from the learning group. Fentanyl IV/epidural up to 200 ug/h allowed (11 learning, 9 test patients got it); not modelled. Samples: arterial, at baseline, before each target change, q4h on infusion and q4h to 5 days after. Assay: modification of the Plummer whole-blood HPLC method, fluorescence, run on plasma ("Plasma propofol"), range 2-200 ng/mL and 0.05-10 ug/mL, LOQ 2 ng/mL. Analysis: naive pooled data and mixed-effects (the "monocular estimate method").
Table 2 (mean (SD), learning n=20 / test n=10): height 176 (8)/174 (7) cm, weight 81 (21)/79 (16) kg, LBM 60 (9)/60 (7), FBM 21 (14)/20 (10), APACHE II 32 (6)/33 (5); age 61 (11)/79 (16) (see open question). LBM = 1.1 x weight - 128 x (weight/height^2) (men); FBM = weight - LBM.
Table 3 (median, range): duration 1,124 (90-21,085) / 918 (425-3,510) min; total dose 4,246 (182-71,056) / 2,288 (410-9,322) mg; 46 (2-1,421) / 35 (6-89) mg/kg; steady-state rate 2.6 (0.5-7.0) / 1.9 (0.8-4.4) mg/kg/h.

## Model and parameters
Three-compartment mammillary model, naive pooled fit in 19 of the learning patients (1,006 observations; 1 excluded), LBM and FBM as covariates. Table 5 (PDF p4, printed 327), checked on the page image:

```yaml
  - {name: V2, value: "6.34 x LBM", unit: L, source: "Table 5", note: "rapid peripheral, CV 26%"}
  - {name: V3, value: "32.4 x LBM + 107 x FBM", unit: L, source: "Table 5", note: "slow peripheral, CV 43%"}
  - {name: Cl1, value: "0.0502 x LBM - 0.0171 x FBM", unit: L/min, source: "Table 5", note: "metabolic, CV 28%"}
  - {name: Cl2, value: "0.0159 x LBM", unit: L/min, source: "Table 5", note: "CV 68%"}
  - {name: Cl3, value: "0.0251 x LBM", unit: L/min, source: "Table 5", note: "CV 68%"}
```
Original (Dyck 1991) in the same table: V1 9.64 - 0.0512 x age; V2 19.4; V3 571 - 1.66 x age; Cl1 0.652 + 0.0148 x wt; Cl2 1.68; Cl3 2.67 - 0.0145 x age. Performance (Table 5/6): improvement in -2LL 295; MDAWR 53% (original) vs 23.7% (revised, learning), 29.2% (test, n=10); MDWR -12% vs -3.1% (learning), 2.6% (test).
Derived for the mean Table 2 patient (LBM 60, FBM 21): V1 36 L, V2 380 L, V3 about 4,190 L, Cl1 2.65 L/min. Table 10 (PDF p6 text layer, not image-checked) compares ICU studies: Vdss here three to nine times larger than earlier ICU studies; clearance similar.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: modified Ramsay sedation score (Table 1, scores 0-6), plasma propofol. 643 observations in the 20 learning patients. Proportional-odds sigmoid P(SS >= n) = C^g/(C^g + C50,n^g). Table 7 (PDF p7, printed 330, image-checked), mixed-effects (MEM) vs naive pooled (NPD): C50 for SS>=2/3/4/5/6 = 0.13/0.50/0.74/1.48/2.34 ug/mL (MEM) and 0.04/0.38/0.73/2.39/5.21 (NPD); gamma 1.7 (MEM), 0.84 (NPD). MEM chosen. Correct predictions 30% learning, 38% test; within +/-1 SS 54% and 73%.
Table 8: C50,SS vs probability mode (concentration of most likely score): SS2 0.13 vs 0.25; SS3 0.50 vs 0.60; SS4 0.74 vs 1.00; SS5 1.48 vs 2.00 ug/mL. Abstract gives modes 0.25, 0.6, 1.0, 2.0 ug/mL.
No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak or hysteresis modelled: the PD is on plasma concentration at the time of the score, no effect-site compartment. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC or potency ratio. Fentanyl effect on sedation judged minimal (no difference in relationship with vs without fentanyl, p6 discussion), not quantified.
Table 9 (PDF p8, printed 331, image-checked), simulated for a 61-yr man, 81 kg, 176 cm: light sedation SS3 target 0.6 ug/mL, bolus 0.28 mg/kg; rates 2.26, 2.06, 1.92, 1.72, 1.54, 1.41, 1.29, 1.23, 1.23 mg/kg/h at 0, 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72 h, 7 d, 14 d; emergence (to 0.25 ug/mL) 0.12, 0.13, 0.17, 0.22, 0.32, 0.57, 3.30, 3.38 h (3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72 h, 7 d, 14 d). Deep sedation SS5 target 2 ug/mL, bolus 0.93 mg/kg; rates 7.52, 6.88, 6.38, 5.75, 5.14, 4.69, 4.28, 4.12, 4.12; emergence 0.38, 2.27, 9.77, 24.98, 48.53, 58.60, 71.47, 74.15 h. The abstract's emergence times for a typical subject after 24 h, 72 h, 7 d and 14 d of light sedation (13, 34, 198, 203 min) agree with Table 9 in h (0.22, 0.57, 3.30, 3.38).

## Covariates
LBM and FBM (James formula for men) on all volumes and clearances. Only men studied. No age term in the revised model.

## Caveats
All male, ICU (APACHE II about 32), mostly on fentanyl; n=19 for PK; naive pooled fit; PK sampling to 5 days gives very large peripheral volumes; PD derived from 20 patients; assay stated as whole-blood method modified for plasma. Prediction accuracy within one score 73%. Appendices 1-2 not in the PDF.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No citation to verify. Independent comparison with R/drugs_propofol.R (Eleveld 2018, tPeak 1.6 min): for a 61-yr male, 81 kg, 176 cm the Eleveld CL1 is about 1.7 L/min (derived, THETA 4, 0.75 weight power, age factor exp(-0.002857 x age)) against Barr Cl1 of about 2.65 L/min at the mean Table 2 LBM/FBM; Eleveld V3 is of order 100 L against Barr's about 4,190 L. Barr's volumes are far larger because of 5-day sampling; the direction (Barr larger) follows from its design and is not claimed to be an error in either. Barr's SS C50s (0.13-2.34 ug/mL) are plasma concentrations in ICU patients; the library does not use them for propofol (no hit in the checkout). Verdict: not-applicable.
