# Hong 2016: transit-compartment absorption for pregabalin

## Study
Five Korean bioequivalence/food PK studies; 88 healthy young men (27.2 +/- 5.0 y, 68.3 +/- 7.8 kg, CLcr 120.0 +/- 21.6 mL/min; Table 1). Pregabalin 150 mg single or twice-daily multiple doses, fasted or 30 min/4 h after regular or high-fat meals; dense sampling to 24 h after last dose; 1,615 concentrations. NONMEM 7.3, FOCE-I.

## Model and parameters
Table 4 model comparison (OFV; CL/F; V/F total): 1-cmt first-order 18,670.37, 6.53 L/h; 1-cmt + lag 18,211.86, 6.36 L/h, 47.3 L; 2-cmt + lag 18,137.27, 6.43; zero-order 18,089.94; Erlang (4 compartments) 17,648.44; transit + 2-cmt 17,635.99, 6.25 L/h (final). Transit model improved AIC by 497.3 vs first-order and 8.5 vs Erlang.

No CLcr breakpoint found (unlike Bockbrader 2011). Typical CL/F at CLCR 107 = 5.89 L/h vs 4.96 in the Pfizer model.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None estimated. Discussion notes a pregabalin exposure-response delay (CSF Tmax lag; rat effect-compartment model). Not an opioid; no MEAC/ke0.

## Covariates
CLcr power on CL/F; food on MTT and Ka.

## Caveats
Young healthy Korean men only, single 150 mg dose level. Van Esdonk 2018 (16 volunteers, sparser absorption sampling) did not find transit compartments superior to first-order with lag.
