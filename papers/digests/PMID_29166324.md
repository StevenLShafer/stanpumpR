# Gambus 2018: Artificial intelligence for everyone

## Study
Editorial accompanying Lee et al. (Anesthesiology 2018;128:492-501, digest `PMID_28953500`). It is a narrative piece on machine learning and neural networks (TensorFlow, Keras, GPUs). No data, no model, no parameters. It describes Lee et al.'s neural network, trained on more than 2 million one-second samples (propofol rate, remifentanil rate, BIS) from 231 patients, as predicting BIS better than the published propofol PK, remifentanil PK and interaction models, judged with the Varvel performance metrics (ref 14).

```yaml
parameters: []   # editorial; no parameters reported
```

It cites the Schnider propofol model (ref 15) and the Minto remifentanil model (ref 16), and notes that both controlled infusions for many hours with reasonable accuracy in Lee et al. It cites the Bouillon response-surface interaction model (ref 17).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None reported. The only end point mentioned is BIS (from Lee et al.). No ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency values (not an opioid PK/PD paper, though remifentanil is mentioned).

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Opinion. The authors say the neural network contains no pharmacokinetic knowledge and probably cannot extrapolate (for example to doubled doses, or a different propofol:remifentanil ratio, or extra boluses). The editorial also says neural networks will probably displace PK/PD modelling eventually, but not yet.
