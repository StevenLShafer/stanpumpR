# Moisés 2005: epidural fentanyl pharmacokinetics and placental transfer at caesarean section

## Study
- Ten clinically normal term parturients (one primigravida) having elective caesarean section under epidural
  anaesthesia, Ribeirão Preto, Brazil; non-randomised, no control group. Table 1 (PDF p.3, printed 519), medians
  (P25–P75): age 28.5 (23.8–31.8) y, gestational age 271.5 (267.3–282.3) days, weight 69.3 (68.1–76.5) kg, height
  1.6 (1.6–1.7) m, BMI 27.2 (24.8–29.1) kg/m².
- Drug: 2 mL fentanyl citrate 0.05 mg/mL (Fentanest) epidurally, together with 15 mL 0.5% bupivacaine with
  1:200,000 epinephrine and 10 mL 2% lidocaine plain; 5 mL 2% lidocaine for skin infiltration (PDF p.2).
  Whether 0.05 mg/mL refers to base or salt is not stated (see open question).
- Sampling: maternal venous plasma at 1, 5, 15, 30, 45, 60, 120, 240, 360, 480, 600, 720, 840 min; umbilical vein at
  birth.
- Assay: GC-MS (SIM, m/z 245.2 fentanyl, clobazam internal standard), plasma; calibration 0.025–1.0 ng/mL;
  LOQ 0.025 ng/mL (precision 18%, accuracy −12.5%); intra/inter-assay CV < 15%. Total (not free) concentration.
- Analysis: PK Solutions 2.0, noncompartmental plus method of residuals; bi-exponential in 6 patients,
  tri-exponential in 4. CL/F = dose/AUC0–∞; Vd/F = dose/(AUC·terminal rate constant) (i.e. Varea/F).

Two patients (9 and 10) were excluded from Table 3 because their umbilical concentrations were judged a collection
error (PDF p.3). Cmax/tmax were said to be read by inspection but are not tabulated; Fig. 1 shows medians peaking
near 0.4–0.5 ng/mL within the first minutes (read from the figure, approximate).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No MEAC, minimum effective or analgesic concentration measured or stated.
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis measured. The introduction cites a textbook (Goodman & Gilman, ref. 2) for a time
  to peak action of about 5 min, and ref. 3 for epidural onset three- to fivefold slower than IV; neither is
  measured here and no end point is given.
- No equianalgesic or potency ratio.
- Neonatal outcome only: all 10 newborns had Apgar 10 at 5 min (PDF p.3).

## Covariates
None analysed. CL/F and Vd/F are also reported per kilogram (total body weight at term).

## Caveats
- Epidural route with epinephrine-containing bupivacaine: F is unknown, so CL/F and Vd/F are apparent; the authors
  could not resolve an absorption phase (PDF p.4–5).
- Single 100 ug (citrate, or base; unstated) dose, n = 10, medians only, no non-pregnant comparator in the study.
- Comparisons in the discussion (PDF p.4, printed 520) are to literature values in non-pregnant women given IV
  fentanyl (Scholz 1996: V 4.0 L/kg, CL 0.78 L/h/kg; Tegeder 1999: V 200–560 L, CL 600–1000 mL/min) and to epidural
  infusion after thoracotomy (Sandler 1992: CL 0.95 L/h/kg); the authors attribute differences to route, dosing and
  population and draw no pregnant-vs-non-pregnant conclusion.
- Terminal half-life (620 min) is estimated from data ending at 840 min.
- Variable bi- vs tri-exponential fits across patients.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:4** — the registry quotes Moisés 2005 CL/F 6.875 mL/min/kg and describes the data as
  epidural/apparent. The abstract (PDF p.1, rendered) gives 6.875 mL/min/kg; Table 2 gives the median 6.9. The
  citation (Eur J Clin Pharmacol 2005;61:517-522, PMID 16021436) is correct. The row applies no factor
  (`cl_factor` NA), and the paper indeed has no non-pregnant arm and yields no clearance ratio, so nothing is
  claimed that the paper does not support. **matches**.
- Independent comparison with the library's fentanyl model (`R/drugs_fentanyl.R:27,30`: V1 12.1 L, CL 0.632 L/min
  for the reference size; not cited to this paper): Moisés' median CL/F 0.465 L/min at 69.3 kg is 0.74x the model's
  0.632 L/min (derived; about 6.7 vs 9.0 mL/min/kg at 70 kg, derived). This cannot be read as a pregnancy effect:
  it is apparent clearance after an epidural dose (true CL = F x CL/F, F ≤ 1 would lower it further, though
  incomplete sampling of the terminal phase and the unknown dose basis cut the other way), from 10 women, and the
  dose basis is unresolved (open question). The registry rightly leaves the factor unquantified.
- Opioid audit: no MEAC, ke0/tPeak or potency ratio in this paper; nothing in the library is attributed to it for
  pharmacodynamics.
