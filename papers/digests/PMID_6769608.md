# Armstrong 1980: IV nitroglycerin kinetics and haemodynamics in heart failure

## Study
- 16 patients with congestive cardiac failure (Table 1, PDF p2): 9 M / 7 F, age 38-68 y, weight 45.0-84.9 kg
  (Table 2); arteriosclerotic heart disease in 15, cardiomyopathy in 1; three within 48 h of myocardial
  infarction, the rest NYHA III-IV and refractory; 6 had taken isosorbide dinitrate; 6 diabetic.
- IV glyceryl trinitrate (GTN), 100 mcg/mL in 5% dextrose in glass bottles, started at 15-20 mcg/min and raised
  by 50% every 10-15 min until PCWP fell by ≥ 25% or the rate exceeded ten times the start; held at the peak rate
  ≥ 15 min, then stopped (PDF p3). Peak rates 15-440 mcg/min (Table 2). Infusion tubing material not stated.
- Sampling: **radial arterial** blood at control and at the peak rate; in 11 patients also 2, 5 and 10 min after
  the stop. Samples centrifuged at 5 °C, **plasma** frozen and assayed by GLC (isosorbide dinitrate internal
  standard). The paper labels the result "blood GTN"; the assayed matrix is arterial plasma. Limit of detection
  quoted as 0.5 ng/mL (Table 2 footnote, Results).
- PK: clearance = peak rate / concentration (assumed steady state); Vd = CL × t½ / 0.693 (PDF p3).

## Model and parameters
Non-compartmental; individual values in Table 2 (PDF p4, printed 163, confirmed on page image).

- Group 1 (n = 8; arterial GTN ≤ 11.1 ng/mL; all reached the PCWP end point): rates 15-94 mcg/min, GTN 1.2-11.1
  ng/mL, clearance 7.0-24.7 L/min, **mean 13.8 ± 5.8 (SD) L/min**.
- Group 2 (n = 8; GTN > 11.1 ng/mL; 3 reached the end point): rates 59-440 mcg/min, GTN 18.7-481.3 ng/mL,
  clearance 0.9-6.4 L/min, **mean 3.6 ± 1.8 L/min**; p < 0.005 vs group 1.
- **Half-life after the stop, 1.9 min**: the slope of one regression line (r = 0.95) through percent-of-peak
  concentrations of five patients pooled over 0-10 min (Figure 3, PDF p4). Individual t½ in Table 2: 1.8, 2.7,
  0.9, 1.2, 1.7 min (patients HA, BK, BR, CA, MO; derived mean 1.66 min). **All five are group-2 patients**
  (clearance 3.9, 4.9, 2.2, 2.5, 0.9 L/min; derived mean 2.9 L/min).
- Vd in those five: 10.1, 19.2, 3.0, 4.4, 2.2 L. Printed group-2 mean 9.9 ± 6.7 L, but the five printed values
  average 7.8 L (derived); each individual value is consistent with CL × t½ / 0.693 (derived), so the printed
  mean is the odd one out. Not used by stanpumpR.
- Six further patients (asterisked: RA, LI, MT, KE, CR of group 1, and JO) had GTN < 0.5 ng/mL within 5 min of
  stopping, too fast to fit. In every patient GTN was undetectable or < 5% of peak 10 min after the stop.
- Earlier sublingual study in volunteers (their ref. 7, quoted p6): t½ 4.4 min, CL 28 L/min, Vd 179 L (the
  text prints "1/min" for Vd), which the authors think overestimated.
- Whole human blood in vitro at 37 °C: t½ 6.4 ± 1.9 min (ref. 27, quoted p6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: ≥ 25% fall in PCWP (PCWP chosen as the most predictable measure, p6).
- **Minimum effective concentration 1.2 ng/mL** (arterial plasma GTN, GLC) at 15 mcg/min, defined from the
  authors' experience that haemodynamic change is rare below 15 mcg/min (Abstract; Discussion p6).
- 1.2-11.1 ng/mL described as the apparent therapeutic range (group 1). Above 11.1 ng/mL the response was often
  attenuated without toxicity; up to 481.3 ng/mL with no adverse effects.
- Hysteresis: concentrations fell within minutes but haemodynamics took about 15 min to return to control
  (Results p5; patient CA, Figure 4: 5.9 ng/mL at 10 min with only partial recovery). No ke0 reported.
- Whole group at peak rate: MAP 90→83 mm Hg, PCWP 28→20 (-27%), RAP 14→9, SI 23→27, TPR 24→19 (Figure 1, 2).
- No potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Group 2 had higher RAP (19 ± 4 vs 10 ± 4 mm Hg, p < 0.001) and lower clearance; the authors
attribute the low clearance partly to hepatic congestion and poor perfusion (comparator: group 1 of this study).

## Caveats
- Single "steady-state" sample per patient for clearance; steady state assumed, not shown (as McNiff 1981 notes).
- The 1.9-min half-life comes from five group-2 patients with low clearance and very high concentrations; the
  high-clearance patients fell below the assay limit too fast to estimate.
- Arterial sampling; small n; congested, mostly post-infarction patients; tubing material and adsorption not
  addressed.
- Group-2 Vd mean inconsistent with the individual values (above).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Half-life 1.9 min** (R:176, test:8, help:25, docs:13): the paper's pooled post-infusion half-life is 1.9 min
  (Figure 3; Results). Unit minutes in both. Transcription **matches**.
- **Sampling site**: the code's curve is "arterial plasma" (R:140-145, reference string); this paper sampled
  radial arterial blood and assayed plasma. **Matches**.
- **Citation** (R:38-39, test:7): Armstrong PW, Armstrong JA, Marks GS, Circulation 1980;62:160-166, PMID 6769608.
  **Matches**.
- **Volume 17.0 L**: not a number in this paper; it is the repo's derivation from Armstrong 1983's CL 6.2 L/min
  and this half-life (6.2 × 1.9 / 0.6931 = 17.0, derived; the test's 16.99 agrees). Note for the reader: the 1.9
  min was measured in group-2 patients whose own clearance averaged about 2.9 L/min and whose own Vd was 2.2-19.2
  L; the model applies the half-life, not their volume, to a 6.2 L/min clearance. For an infusion-only
  one-compartment model the half-life and clearance fully determine the curve, so this is a coherent choice, and
  the help page (help:25-27) states it as such. **Matches** (as a use of the reported half-life).
- **help:25** "measured half-lives of 1.1 to 2.8 minutes" belongs to Armstrong 1983, not this paper (whose
  individual values are 0.9-2.7 min); the sentence attributes it correctly to "Armstrong's group" and to the
  later study. **Matches**.
- MEC 1.2 ng/mL (arterial plasma): the library sets `MEAC = 0` and draws no band for nitroglycerin (R:221,
  test:60-66); the paper's MEC and 1.2-11.1 ng/mL range are not used. Not a discrepancy; recorded for reference.
