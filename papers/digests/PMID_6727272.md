# Ochs 1984: Pharmacokinetics and absolute bioavailability of diltiazem

## Study
- Single-dose, three-way crossover, 6 healthy male volunteers aged 22-31 y, at least 10 days between occasions (PDF p.1).
- (a) IV diltiazem hydrochloride 20 mg by constant-rate pump into an antecubital vein, given over 20 min per the Methods (the Summary says 10 min; see open question); (b) 120 mg hydrochloride oral, in 50 mL aqueous solution; (c) 120 mg as two 60-mg sustained-release tablets. Oral doses after an overnight fast and 4 h more.
- Venous blood into heparinised tubes; IV sampling before and at 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 min (end of infusion), 22 min, 25, 30, 45 min, 1 h, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 36 h. Oral samples 0.25 h to 36 h.
- Assay: electron-capture GLC, butyryl analogue as internal standard, plasma, sensitivity limit about 5 ng/mL (PDF p.2). Total (not free) plasma concentration.
- IV analysis: post-infusion concentrations fitted by iterative nonlinear least squares to a sum of three exponentials; Vd by the area method, t1/2, CL from the fitted function. Oral: trapezoidal AUC with extrapolation; F = AUC(oral)/AUC(IV) corrected for dose.

The solution vs tablet AUC difference was not significant. Discussion: plasma protein binding about 80%, octanol:buffer partition ratio 158, hepatic clearance about 50% of hepatic blood flow, about 50% dose increase advised on conversion from IV to oral. Body weights are not given.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect measure, ke0, tPeak, MEAC or therapeutic concentration is reported. (Not an opioid paper.)

## Covariates
None; six healthy men, weight not reported.

## Caveats
- Healthy young men only; the authors warn kinetics may differ in cardiovascular disease and with other drugs.
- Infusion time stated inconsistently (10 vs 20 min).
- Vd 11.1 L/kg and the 11.2 h half-life are far larger than the same group's alternative-model Vss 5.2 L/kg and 9.8 h, and than Hoglund 1988; the 36-h sampling and a GLC assay with a 5 ng/mL limit make the terminal phase sensitive to the fitting method.
- Doses are diltiazem hydrochloride; the concentrations are of diltiazem (base presumed, not stated).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Registry row says three half-lives, V1, Vss and CL fix a three-compartment model, and that the companion paper gives a different infusion time. This paper supplies the three half-lives (0.1, 2.1, 9.8 h), Vss 5.2 L/kg and CL 11.5 mL/min/kg but no V1, so V1 must come from Kolle. The infusion-time remark is supported, since 10 and 20 min both appear (open question). **Matches** in substance; V1 **cannot verify** from this paper.
- Context location `:64` points at a digoxin row; the diltiazem row is line 76 (stale line number, not a repo error).
- Comparison with the implemented model (not citing this paper): R/drugs_diltiazem.R:111-115 CL 0.992 L/min (59.5 L/h), V1 119 L, Vss 209 L, beta t1/2 2.77 h (Hoglund, n=10, mean 71.9 kg). Ochs CL 11.5 mL/min/kg is about 0.83 L/min for 72 kg (derived: 11.5 x 71.9 / 1000), about 17% below Hoglund. Ochs Vss 5.2 L/kg is about 374 L at 72 kg (derived), about 1.8x Hoglund's 209 L; Ochs's terminal t1/2 (9.8-11.2 h) is about 4x Hoglund's beta 2.77 h. Ochs is in the direction of a lower clearance and much larger, slower tissue distribution; Hoglund's header does not discuss Ochs.
- Dose basis: Ochs gives hydrochloride doses; the library converts hydrochloride to base (R/drugs_diltiazem.R:110, factor 1.088). Whether Ochs's concentrations are base is not stated.
- Oral values (F 0.44, tmax 38 min, Cmax 251 ng/mL after 120 mg) are not used by any library model; diltiazem is IV only.
