# Zarek 2025: pregnancy-associated PK changes of APS medications (systematic review)

Page convention: printed pp. 11-22 = PDF pp. 1-12 (PDF page = printed page − 10).

## Study
Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42020209543, PRISMA), MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL, Web of Science, Pharmaceutical
Abstracts, inception to 2 June 2020, updated to 1 April 2024 (printed p. 13 / PDF p. 3). Included: original PK in
pregnant women with a nonpregnant comparison (nonpregnant women or the same women before pregnancy or postpartum).
Drugs: low-dose aspirin, unfractionated heparin, LMWH (only enoxaparin and dalteparin were found), chloroquine and
hydroxychloroquine. 3,460 records screened, 12 publications analysed. No meta-analysis; each parameter expressed
as % pregnancy / nonpregnancy (%P/NP); where doses rose in pregnancy the authors dose-standardised; CL/F was
derived as dose/AUC where not reported. Reporting quality by the ClinPK checklist (median 84% conformity).
**No fondaparinux study is included**; fondaparinux is not discussed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point throughout is anti-factor Xa activity (heparins). Aleidan: subtherapeutic anti-Xa (Cmax <0.6 U/mL) in
30-36% of pregnant vs 6-12% of nonpregnant (printed p. 19 / PDF p. 9). No ke0, no efficacy model.

## Covariates
Not modelled. Mechanism discussion (printed p. 19 / PDF p. 9): renal CL is 3% (dalteparin) to 15-20% (enoxaparin)
of total LMWH CL (ref 37); a renal cause alone would need a 3- to 10-fold rise in renal CL against a 1.5-fold rise
in renal perfusion, so non-renal (reticuloendothelial, stabilin-2) clearance must rise too.

## Caveats
Small studies with heterogeneous comparators (postpartum, prepregnancy, other patients, men in the aspirin study),
dose escalation, and APS inflammation as a possible confounder. Postpartum recovery profiles not reported in any
study. Patel 2013 (no nonpregnant comparator) is not among the included studies.

Summary statements and where they come from: "about 1.5-fold" CL increase on average for all drugs (highlights,
printed p. 12) and for the two enoxaparin studies (text printed p. 15), whose table values are 132% and 148%
(mean 140%, derived); AUC reduction about 30% (text p. 15; table 78% and 68%); V expansion 30-50% (text p. 19;
table 124% and 149%); Cmax/Dose and AUC/Dose 20-30% lower (p. 19). Across all drugs CL/F ranged from about 120% to
more than 250% (printed p. 15).
