# Kaiser 1989: benazepril/benazeprilat PK in the elderly, renal impairment and cirrhosis

## Study
A summary of several Ciba-Geigy studies. Oral benazepril hydrochloride, single or multiple doses of 5-20 mg. Unchanged benazepril and benazeprilat measured in plasma, reported in pmol/g and nmol/g·h; the assay is not described here (ref 3 is an enzymic method). Non-compartmental throughout. Detailed results are deferred to later papers (p1).
- **Healthy reference** (from ref 6, the Biopharm Drug Dispos paper): benazepril tmax 30 min, elimination virtually complete by 4 h; benazeprilat tmax 1.5 h, initial half-life about 3 h, terminal about 22 h from 24 h onward (p1, printed 746). Fig. 2: healthy men, 10 mg, n = 35 (benazepril) and 61 (benazeprilat).
- **Elderly**: 15 healthy volunteers (6 F, 9 M), aged 65-80, 10 mg single dose, compared with younger subjects aged 19-32 (n = 50 in Fig. 3).
- **Renal impairment**: three studies, 44 patients, CLcr 6-149 mL/min, 18 of them with CLcr < 30. A single 10 mg dose, plus once-daily 10 mg multiple dosing in two studies. Two further studies: 10 ESRD patients on haemodialysis given 5 or 10 mg singly, or two 10 mg doses 42 h apart (p2, printed 747).
- **Cirrhosis**: 12 men, a single 20 mg dose, compared with healthy volunteers given 10 mg (n = 50), dose-normalised (p5, printed 750).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No ACE inhibition or blood-pressure data. No ke0. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Renal function (CLcr), descriptively. Benazeprilat renal clearance falls linearly with CLcr across the whole range (Fig. 6). AUC changes little above 30 mL/min and rises steeply below it. Nonrenal clearance partly compensates in ESRD. Age has a small effect, attributed to declining renal function. Cirrhosis does not affect benazeprilat.

## Caveats
- A summary paper: no assay, demographics or individual data, and no total clearance or half-life values for the renal groups. Details are deferred to unpublished work.
- The "<30 mL/min" threshold is the authors' dosing conclusion. Their own Fig. 6 shows a continuous linear relation, not a step.
- ESRD patients were plotted at an assumed CLcr of 0.
- The discussion (p5-6) questions whether AUC(0-∞) reflects accumulation, because benazeprilat binds ACE. The author answers that the 24 h AUC at steady state gives the same conclusion.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **inst/help/drugs/benazepril.md:52**: "clearance is reduced below a creatinine clearance of 30 mL/min". **Supported in substance.** The paper reports slowed benazeprilat elimination, a 4.3-fold higher mean AUC (implying a proportionally lower CL/F, derived) and an accumulation ratio of 1.98 vs 1.36-1.45 when CLcr < 30. The repo's comparator matches the paper's (patients with normal function). The paper reports AUC and renal clearance, not total clearance. Renal clearance declines continuously with CLcr (Fig. 6) rather than only below 30. The citation is unspecific ("companion papers", plural, no reference), so it **cannot be confirmed** that this is the paper meant (open question).
- **Model (R/drugs_benazepril.R, not citing this paper)**: the library has no renal covariate (R/drugs_benazepril.R:149-155), as the help page says. That is consistent with this paper only for CLcr > 30.
