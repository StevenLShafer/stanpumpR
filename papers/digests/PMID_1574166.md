# Yerby 1992: total and free antiepileptic drug levels through pregnancy

## Study
- Prospective cohort, Oregon Comprehensive Epilepsy Program. Women with epilepsy enrolled before conception (about
  one third) or by the end of the first trimester, then seen monthly through the 8th postpartum week (PDF p2).
- **Baseline = the 8th postpartum week**, not preconception. The authors say the few preconception values they had
  did not differ significantly (PDF p2).
- n = 51 women, 20-37 y (mean 28), 98% white, 74% on monotherapy (PDF p2). Table 1 (PDF p2): carbamazepine 22
  (18 mono / 4 poly), phenytoin 14 (10/4), phenobarbital 11 (7/4), **valproic acid 9 (3 mono / 6 poly)**,
  ethosuximide, primidone and clonazepam 1 each (polytherapy). Table 5 gives phenytoin n = 18.
- Typical sampling per woman: 2 first-trimester, 4 second, 3 third, 1 at delivery and 2 postpartum observations
  (PDF p2). Free and total AED and metabolites measured at each visit. **The assay, the free-drug separation method
  and the doses are not reported.** Weights were recorded but are not reported.
- Statistics: Student's t test of each trimester mean against baseline (PDF p2).
- **Doses were changed in 38% of patients**, and the analysis does not control for dose or weight (PDF p4).
- Mother-infant pairs at delivery (Table 7), with 3 phenytoin pairs added from a pilot study.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. No therapeutic range, seizure-frequency analysis or concentration-effect relation is reported.
The authors argue that free levels matter more than totals and recommend monitoring free drug.

## Covariates
None modelled. The authors list dose, body weight and protein binding as drivers of the levels but did not adjust
for dose or weight (PDF p4).

## Caveats
- The comparator is the **same women at 8 weeks postpartum**, not before pregnancy.
- Doses changed in 38% of women and the analysis does not allow for it, so the concentration ratios are not
  clearance ratios. Doses and weights are not reported.
- Valproate: n = 9, and 6 of the 9 were on polytherapy. The co-medications are not named, but enzyme inducers
  would raise valproate clearance. No significance is shown for any valproate free value or free fraction.
- The paper is internally inconsistent for free valproate. Table 3 and the text give +25 at delivery, Table 2's
  means give +33%, and Table 4 calls free valproate a steady decline. The third-trimester free value (5.6) is
  below baseline.
- Table 6's unit ("mg/h·kg") is wrong as printed, and the valproate clearances are rounded to integers.
- The free-drug method and the assay are not described. Earlier Yerby papers presumably hold them.
- The carbamazepine, phenytoin and phenobarbital clearances and free fractions in Tables 5-6 are the same numbers
  the registry quotes from Yerby 1990 (Epilepsy Res 5:223, Table VII), so this paper is not independent of that
  one for those drugs. Valproate is the new content here.
- Comparatives: "free valproate increased 25%" is delivery against 8 weeks postpartum in the same women.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **The 25% rise (R/drugs_valproate.R:86-87): matches as worded.** The code says "by delivery", and that is what the
  paper reports (text PDF p2; Table 3 "+25").
- **The factor's arithmetic (R/drugs_valproate.R:147-150, 196; registry :98 applied): discrepancy in the time
  point.** The factor is applied to late (antepartum, third-trimester) pregnancy, but Yerby's +25% is a delivery
  value, which is also the time point that the header (:131-133) says inflates Nau and Krauer's fu. In the third
  trimester Yerby's free valproate was 0.78x baseline (5.6 vs 7.2) and the free fraction 1.18x (13 vs 11%). The
  total fell to 0.673x baseline (39.7/59.0), which would be a total CL ratio of about 1.49 at an unchanged dose
  (derived; doses not controlled). At delivery the total ratio is 0.607, giving about 1.65 (derived). So Yerby's
  own totals put the antepartum ratio at about 1.5 and the delivery ratio at 1.65, close to 1.6. The value is not
  contradicted, but the stated route to it pairs two delivery-time numbers. Raised under open_questions.
- **R/drugs_valproate.R:128** ("Voinescu's free-clearance ratio 0.820 agrees with Yerby's 25% rise"): **discrepancy.**
  Yerby's own free-clearance ratio, third trimester over baseline, is 19/31 = 0.61 (Table 6, derived), and the
  third-trimester free concentration fell. The 0.8 is the reciprocal of the delivery-time +25%, not a measured
  Yerby clearance.
- **Comparator.** Yerby's baseline is the same women at 8 weeks postpartum. The library applies the factor against
  Teixeira-da-Silva's non-pregnant population model. The direction carries over, but the 1.25 is not a ratio
  against that model.
- **inst/help/drugs/valproate.md:19** ("the unbound, active concentration does not fall"): **matches** for Yerby in
  the third trimester (5.6 vs 7.2, not significant), though Yerby's mean is 22% lower, not unchanged.
- **docs/pregnancy.md:225-226, registry :98 to_obtain** ("per trimester with doses"): **discrepancy.** The paper has
  per-trimester mean totals and frees but no doses or weights, so it cannot give a dose-normalised ratio. Its
  Table 6 clearances (valproate total 2 to 3, ratio 1.5) are integers in a unit printed wrongly.
- **Phenytoin (registry :96, source list only):** Yerby 1992's phenytoin clearances and free fractions (Tables 5-6)
  match the numbers the registry attributes to Yerby 1990's Table VII: free fraction 10.1 vs 7.7 (1.31) and 13 at
  delivery, total CL 40 vs 23, free CL 392 vs 281. **Matches.** The phenytoin model is not built from this paper.
  Its Table 2 phenytoin third-trimester ratios (total 0.51, free 0.62, derived, doses varying) show a smaller total
  fall and a larger free fall than the Tomson numbers the model is calibrated to (total 0.39, unbound 0.82).
  The model's header does not cite this paper.
- **Carbamazepine and phenobarbital (registry :97, :99, cited there as Yerby 1990):** this paper's numbers are the
  same (CBZ CL 85/76, free 319/352, fu 25.5 vs 21.4; PB CL 5/4, free 9/7, fu 59.3 vs 52). Consistent.
- **tests/testthat/test-drugs-valproate.R:31-45:** tests the 1.6 factor, which is not a Yerby value. Nothing to
  transcribe. Consistent.
- Units: the library carries valproate in mcg/mL (mg/L), the same scale as Table 2's ug/mL. No unit conversion is
  involved.
