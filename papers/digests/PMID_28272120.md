# Sindrup 2017: serum concentration and efficacy of imipramine and pregabalin in polyneuropathy

## Study
Post hoc, explorative concentration-effect analysis (authors say the plan was formulated post hoc; p3,
printed 1049) of a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 4-way crossover trial (Holbech 2015, ref 10).
- Population: 47 of the 48 per-protocol completers; painful polyneuropathy of any cause (unknown 20, diabetes
  10, alcohol 7, drug-induced 4, six others); median age 59 y (29-82); male:female 29:18 (p3). Weight and
  renal function not reported.
- Arms: placebo, imipramine 75 mg once daily, pregabalin 150 mg twice daily (300 mg/day), and the
  combination; titrated over the first week. Patients 70 y and over got imipramine 25 mg once daily and
  pregabalin 75 mg twice daily; CYP2D6/2C19 poor metabolisers got imipramine 25 mg (p2). Five poor
  metabolisers and six patients >=70 y (p3).
- Periods: 5 weeks each with 1-week washouts (Methods p2, Fig 1, Table 1 title). The abstract says 4 weeks
  (p1); the body and figure say 5.
- End point: median of daily 0-10 NRS total pain in the last treatment week; responder = >=30% reduction
  from baseline (also >=50%).
- Sampling: serum drawn 440-1560 min after the pregabalin dose in monotherapy and 95-3260 min in combination;
  each concentration was corrected by log-linear regression to 720 min (12 h) after the last dose, and the
  corrected values were analysed (p3).
- Assay: pregabalin by LC-MS/MS (Filadelfia Hospital), CV about 5%, LOQ 0.5 µM (p2; the text layer drops
  the micro sign). Imipramine and desipramine by LC-MS/MS, LOQ 50 nM.
- Analysis: general linear model; Pearson correlation; t test between responder groups.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: weekly median NRS pain, >=30% reduction from baseline defines a responder.
- Pregabalin: pain reduction vs serum concentration, r = 0.283, P = 0.057 (monotherapy, Fig 2, p3-4);
  responder vs nonresponder means 9.8 and 11.7 µmol/L (abstract order "responders and nonresponders"),
  P = 0.178. No difference for the >=50% criterion (data not shown).
- Imipramine + desipramine: r = 0.17, P = 0.247; imipramine alone r = 0.149, desipramine alone r = 0.136.
- Interaction with concentration category (Table 2, p5; high/low counts; additional change with high
  concentration): pregabalin monotherapy 22/25, -0.43 (-1.20 to 0.35), P = 0.282; imipramine monotherapy
  30/17, -0.22, P = 0.610; in combination P = 0.161-0.797. None significant.
- Isobolograms (Figs 4-5): no synergy.
- No therapeutic or minimum effective concentration, no ke0, tPeak or potency ratio is given. Authors
  conclude the relation for pregabalin is weak and does not justify concentration measurement; they suggest a
  ceiling effect at the doses used and low power.
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC.

## Covariates
Dose reduced by age (>=70 y) and, for imipramine, CYP2D6/2C19 genotype. No covariate model.

## Caveats
Post hoc and explorative; n = 47; a single dose level per patient (300 mg/day or 150 mg/day), so the
concentration range is narrow; concentrations back-extrapolated to 12 h from samples taken 7-26 h after
the dose; paracetamol escape up to 3 g/day allowed; abstract/body disagreement on period length (4 vs 5
weeks). Text layer renders µmol/L as "mmol/L"; the rendered pages read µmol/L.
