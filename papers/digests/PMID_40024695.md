# Yano 2025: PBPK of risperidone and paliperidone in the perinatal period

## Study
Japanese-language review (Kobe University Hospital) of the author's group's Simcyp ADME simulator v21 PBPK work. Steps: compound models for risperidone and paliperidone, built on 5 published clinical datasets; checked against 7 external datasets; extrapolated to pregnant and paediatric physiology; then fitted to one pregnant woman on risperidone 1 mg/day and her neonate (PDF p3-4). Table 3 is reprinted from Mahdy WYB et al., Clin Transl Sci 2023;16:618-630 (ref 10). The paper is secondary: no new data other than the one case.

Text (p3-4): mean paliperidone Cmax after risperidone 0.5 mg twice daily falls from 7.1 ng/mL (non-pregnant) to 5.0 ng/mL (40 wk), model estimate. Neonate: paliperidone 0.99 ng/mL on the day of birth, then 1.03, 0.83, 0.82 ng/mL on days 1-3, undetectable from day 4; risperidone undetectable. Maternal risperidone was below the assay limit at every sample (1 mg/day), so only paliperidone was checked against the model.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio (not an opioid).

## Covariates
Gestational age (0, 20, 40 weeks) acting through CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 activity, protein binding, GFR/renal clearance and hepatic blood flow. Ten neonatal renal-function models were compared; the Flanders metadata equation predicted neonatal paliperidone best (p4).

## Caveats
Simulated (PBPK) values, not measured. One pregnant woman and one neonate. Maternal risperidone not measurable. Table 3 is a reprint of Mahdy 2023, so this is not an independent source. The Table 3 text layer scrambles columns and was read from the image.
