# Bogen 2013: enantiomer-specific methadone kinetics in late pregnancy and postpartum

PDF pages 1-11 = printed pages 441-451. Published online 2012, in print 2013 (vol 225); the repo cites it as 2012.

## Study
- Prospective longitudinal cohort, single community methadone maintenance programme (Pittsburgh). 51 screened,
  31 consented, **25** completed both third-trimester visits, 18 the postpartum visit (p. 445).
- Women 18-40 y (mean 26.8, SD 6.06), 88% white, all Medicaid, 76% smokers, 56% hepatitis C positive (Table 1, p. 445).
  Concomitant psychotropics in 4 at enrolment, 9 at delivery. Not healthy volunteers.
- Oral racemic methadone, once daily (3 on split dosing excluded from PK/clearance). Dose at delivery 102.6 (SD 44.3)
  mg/day, range 22-200; late postpartum 105.9 (42.1), n=22 (Table 1). The salt is not stated (US methadone is
  dispensed as the hydrochloride); "dose per isomer" = half the racemic dose.
- L/D arm: 24-h trough samples at two third-trimester visits (27-39 weeks, 1-15 days apart) and one postpartum visit
  (mean 153 days after delivery, range 123-242; p. 446). Steady state required (same dose >= 1 week).
- PK arm (n=5): samples pre-dose and 0.5, 1.5, 3, 4.5, 6, 24, 26, 28 h after a dose (second-day dose delayed 4-6 h)
  (p. 443).
- Assay: chiral HPLC/UV (modified Foster 2000), plasma, **total** (bound + free) R- and S-methadone; LOD 5 ng/mL;
  CV 8.6-12.7% (p. 443).
- Analysis: non-compartmental (WinNonlin), AUC 0-infinity by trapezoid; terminal slope from the last three points.
  Clearance from L/D: **CL = Dose/(Css x 24 h), f assumed 1**, with Css = the 24-h (trough) concentration (p. 444).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **No MEAC** or analgesic concentration (patients were on maintenance for opioid dependence; dose titrated to
  withdrawal symptoms, Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale used only to check the delayed dose, p. 443).
- **No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis.** No PD end point measured; postpartum sedation is an anecdotal motive
  for the protocol change (p. 443).
- Potency: R-enantiomer described as ten times more active than S (p. 442, citing Kristensen 1995 and Crettol 2005);
  no equianalgesic ratio to another opioid.

## Covariates
- Pregnancy vs late postpartum (same women): clearance larger in pregnancy for R, S, rac.
- CYP2B6 Q172H (rs3745274) GT: higher S- and rac-methadone L/D in both pregnancy and postpartum than GG
  (p=0.001, 0.006). Other CYP2B6 SNPs (G-8207C, T-2320C, C15582T) interact with time; CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 SNPs
  not significant (pp. 446-447, Fig. 3). No equations.
- Three women sampled weekly after delivery: L/D rose within 1-2 weeks of delivery (Fig. 2).

## Caveats
- Small (PK n=5; paired clearance n=14), pilot data by the authors' own account (p. 449).
- "Clearance" from the L/D ratio uses the 24-h trough as Css, so it overstates CL/F relative to an average
  steady-state concentration (my observation; the PK arm's AUC-based rac CL 0.172 is well below the L/D mean 0.377).
  f = 1 assumed, so all clearances are apparent oral CL/F.
- Half-life "about half" of non-pregnant is against **other populations**, not this cohort's postpartum values:
  Kristensen 1996 chronic pain patients, and Eap 2002's review range 22-54 h for rac (p. 447).
  The half-lives are from a 6-28 h window after a steady-state dose, not a terminal phase after a single dose.
- Smoking, benzodiazepines, other psychotropics, hepatitis C, partial breastfeeding not controlled.
- Salt basis of dose not stated.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Factor origin.** `R/drugs_methadone.R:215-229` and the registry's `applied` text derive 1.714 from Ansari 2016
  (24 h non-pregnant over the 14 h midpoint of 8-20 h), not from this paper. Bogen does not state a factor. The paired
  Table 4 data give a median pregnancy/postpartum ratio of 1.717 (ratio of means 1.753; derived), so the paper
  **supports** the factor's size, with the comparator being the same women about five months postpartum.
  Verdict: **matches** (corroboration, as the registry says).
- **Registry finding text** (`pregnancyRegistry.csv:10`): n=25 (matches p. 445); R half-lives 14.7-24.9 h and S
  8.02-18.9 h (match Table 3 on the rendered page); clearances of R/S/rac significantly larger than postpartum
  (matches p. 446); rac half-life 11-21 h in pregnancy (matches p. 447; Table 3 range 11.3-21.2). "22-54 h
  non-pregnant" and "about half of non-pregnant" are from the literature the authors cite (Eap 2002, Kristensen 1996),
  not measured in this study; the registry's `comparator` field ("postpartum / non-pregnant (same women, Bogen ...)")
  is right for the clearance comparison but not for the half-life one. Verdict: **matches** (comparator wording only).
- **Citation year**: registry and docs say 2012 (online date); the print issue is 2013. Not a numerical issue.
- **Against the library's own model** (Henthorn 2026, `R/drugs_methadone.R:19-25`, 205): racemic systemic CL at 70 kg
  per HCl dose is 0.0926 L/h/kg, i.e. CL/F 0.132 L/h/kg at F 0.70, and 0.227 when pregnant (x 24/14) (derived).
  Bogen's pregnant CL/F is 0.172 (AUC, n=5) to 0.377 (trough L/D, n=22), and postpartum 0.215 (trough L/D). The
  library's non-pregnant value sits below Bogen's postpartum and its pregnant value between Bogen's two pregnant
  estimates; with the trough bias of the L/D method and the unknown salt basis, the absolute values are consistent
  within the method's uncertainty. The relative factor is what the library uses, and it agrees.
- **Half-lives**: the library's typical non-pregnant terminal half-lives are 48 h (R) and 33 h (S)
  (`R/drugs_methadone.R:28-29`), so pregnant ~28 h and ~19 h (derived), longer than Bogen's 20.0 and 13.8 h; but
  Bogen's are from a 28-h window at steady state, not a terminal phase, so they are not directly comparable.
