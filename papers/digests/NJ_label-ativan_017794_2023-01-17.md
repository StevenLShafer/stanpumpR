# Ativan tablets label 2023: oral lorazepam

## Study
Not a study: US prescribing information for oral lorazepam tablets (anxiety). The Clinical Pharmacology
section gives summary PK with no study design, n or assay, except the elderly IV comparison below.
This is the tablet label; the intravenous and intramuscular values stanpumpR cites are in the separate
injection label (NDA 018140, digested as NJ_label-ativan_018140_2023-01-17).

## Model and parameters
No model. Clinical Pharmacology (label p. 1-2):

```yaml
  - {name: t_half_glucuronide, value: 18, unit: h, source: "Clinical Pharmacology", note: about}
  - {name: protein_binding, value: 85, unit: "%", source: "Clinical Pharmacology", note: "approximately, clinically relevant concentrations"}
```

Other statements: plasma levels proportional to dose; no accumulation up to 6 months. Lorazepam is
conjugated at the 3-hydroxy group to an inactive (in animals) glucuronide excreted in urine. Age: no
significant effect overall, but in one study (single IV 1.5-3 mg) mean total clearance was 20% lower in
15 elderly (60-84 y) than in 15 younger (19-38 y) subjects.

Dosing (Dosage and Administration): usual 2-6 mg/day in divided doses, range 1-10 mg/day; anxiety
initial 2-3 mg/day given two or three times daily; insomnia 2-4 mg at bedtime; elderly or debilitated
initial 1-2 mg/day in divided doses.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect data, no ke0 or tPeak, no therapeutic or toxic concentration range. Single high
doses in volunteers described as tranquilizing without appreciable respiratory or cardiovascular effect.

## Covariates
Age only (the 20% elderly clearance reduction). No equations.

## Caveats
- The 90% F, 2 h tmax and ~20 ng/mL Cmax are rounded summary values with no population, n or assay given.
- The half-life (about 12 h) and binding (about 85%) differ from the injection label's 14 ± 5 h and
  91 ± 2%; the two labels draw on different sources.
