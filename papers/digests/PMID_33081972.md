# Proekt 2021: effect-site equilibration models can always collapse hysteresis

## Study
A mathematical and simulation paper (Matlab); no patients, no drug assay, no fitted human data.
- Three-compartment PK (plasma, "effect-site" compartment 2, "rest of body" compartment 3) with mass-conserving effect-site; effect = sigmoid Hill function of effect-site concentration (h = 5, EC50 = 0.5, normalised concentration, arbitrary units).
- Model A: effect follows effect-site concentration only; hysteresis against plasma concentration collapses when plotted against effect-site concentration (Fig 2).
- Model B: effect-site concentration instead reshapes an energy landscape with two wells (awake, anaesthetised), noisy Brownian dynamics; hysteresis persists against the true effect-site concentration (Fig 3). A two-neurone network (Fig 5) shows the same switching.
- Fig 4: swapping which peripheral compartment is called "effect-site" gives the same plasma curve and same effect but makes B's hysteresis collapse, so the two models cannot be told apart from plasma concentration plus effect.
- Fig 6: a target-controlled infusion simulation using the STANPUMP algorithm (zero-volume effect-site, Sheiner convention) shows hysteresis present or absent depending on the assumed effect-site equilibration speed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No measured end point; effect is a simulated "depth of anaesthesia" (au). No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, MEAC or potency ratio is reported for any drug.
- Argument: ke0 is estimated to minimise hysteresis, so it cannot show hysteresis is absent; effect-site concentration cannot be measured, so different ke0 give different conclusions from identical plasma and effect data (Fig 6e).
- Comparator note: hysteresis claims about human studies discussed (Warnaby 2016, Colin 2017, Kuizenga 2016) are other groups' propofol experiments; no numbers from them are reported here.
- Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Purely theoretical; the two-well and two-neurone models are deliberately simple and were not fitted to data.
- Does not show human hysteresis exists, only that effect-site models cannot settle it.
- Supplementary Figs S1 and S2 are not in the PDF.
