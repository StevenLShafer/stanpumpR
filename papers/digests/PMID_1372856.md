# Murdoch 1992: Fosinopril, a review (Drugs)

## Study
Adis drug-evaluation review; no new data. Pharmacokinetics summarised from healthy volunteers (young and
elderly), hypertensive patients, and renal/hepatic impairment (section 2, PDF pp 8-11, printed 130-133).
Assays cited: radioenzymatic/immunoassays, capillary GC, HPLC (PDF p8). Fosinopril is a phosphinic-acid ester
prodrug hydrolysed by gut-mucosal and hepatic esterases to the active diacid fosinoprilat.

## Model and parameters
No model is fitted. Values quoted (fosinoprilat unless stated):

- Section 2.1 (PDF p9, printed 131), oral fosinopril 10 mg, healthy volunteers: absorbed 32-36% (Singhvi 1988);
  Table II footnotes: 32% solution, 36% capsule. Cmax 99-140 ug/L; tmax 2.8-3.1 h; AUC0-24h 1035-1132 ug/L.h;
  fosinoprilat bioavailability 25-29%. Over single doses 10-640 mg, tmax 2.4-4.2 h; Cmax and AUC proportional to
  dose (r > 0.94). Food slows rate, not extent. Accumulation index 1.2-1.5 at 40-80 mg once or twice daily.
- IV radiolabelled fosinoprilat 7.5 mg, 9 healthy volunteers (Singhvi 1988): mean Cmax 1556 ug/L at 5 min;
  mean AUC0-24h 3166 ug/L.h; Vss 9.8-10.6 L; 95% protein bound at 1-3 h (PDF p9-10).
- Protein binding 89.0-99.8% after oral 10 mg (Table II, PDF p9).
- Total body clearance after IV 7.5 mg, healthy volunteers: 1.55-2.35 L/h (= 25.8-39.2 mL/min, derived)
  (Hui 1991; Kelly & O'Malley 1990; Salvetti 1990; Singhvi 1988; PDF p10, printed 132).
- Plasma radioactivity 1-24 h after oral [14C]fosinopril: <1% parent, 70-80% fosinoprilat, remainder acyl
  beta-glucuronide (PDF p10). Excretion after IV: urine 44%, faeces 46% over 96 h. Table III (PDF p11).
- Terminal half-life 11.5-12 h (Duchin 1991); polyphasic, slow terminal phase attributed to ACE binding.
- Renal impairment (Hui 1991, Fig. 4, PDF p10): IV 7.5 mg in 13 patients, CLcr 0.66-4.32 L/h/1.73 m2; total
  clearance stayed 0.80-0.86 L/h as hepatic share rose to 63, 79, 85% in mild (CLcr 3.6 L/h, n=4), moderate
  (1.92 L/h, n=5), severe (0.96 L/h, n=4). AUC +64 to +90% vs normal controls. Dialysis: total CL reduced 50%.
- Hepatic cirrhosis (n=12): AUC about double healthy volunteers (PDF p11).
- Elderly (65-75 y, n=12) vs young (18-35 y, n=12), oral 10 mg (Levinson 1986): AUC0-48h 1385 vs 1243 ug/L.h,
  Cmax 149 vs 133 ug/L, tmax 3.5 vs 3.4 h, half-life 4.7 vs 4.6 h, urine 9.7 vs 7.6% (PDF p11).
- Antacid co-administration reduced bioavailability about 30% (PDF p16).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is plasma ACE inhibition and blood pressure. Single oral 10-640 mg abolishes plasma ACE activity within
1 h; at 24 h ACE activity is 42% and 20% of baseline after 10 and 20 mg (PDF p6). Ex vivo potency relative to
fosinopril: captopril 0.28, enalapril 0.96, lisinopril 1.93, ramipril 4.03 (PDF p5). IC50 on purified rabbit lung
ACE 9 nmol/L vs captopril 23. No ke0, no concentration-effect model, no therapeutic concentration range. Not an
opioid.

## Covariates
Renal function (compensatory hepatic clearance), hepatic cirrhosis, age (no effect), dialysis; no equations.

## Caveats
Secondary source: many values are ranges pooled across studies, and the review does not always say which study
each number came from (see open questions). Some values cited to abstracts or data on file. The elderly study's
4.6-4.7 h half-life is much shorter than the 11.5-12 h terminal value, presumably a shorter sampling window;
the review does not reconcile them.
