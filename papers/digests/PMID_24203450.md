# Greenblatt 2014: sex differences in zolpidem PK/PD after a buffered sublingual tablet

## Study
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled 4-way crossover, single site (Cincinnati), May-July 2005; secondary
analysis of Roth 2008 data. 24 healthy non-smoking adults aged 21-45 (11 F, mean 37 y; 13 M, mean 33 y), BMI 18-30
(means 24.8 F, 25.0 M); women lighter (weights in Supplemental Table S1, not read). Morning doses after an overnight
fast of zolpidem buffered sublingual tablet (Intermezzo) 1.0, 1.75, 3.5 mg or placebo; washout 5-12 days. Plasma to
12 h. PD (DSST, CRT, symbol copying, VAS sedation, word recall) at 0, 20 min, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5 h. NCA;
two-way ANOVA.

Kinetics linear (dose-normalized values independent of dose). Sex significant for Cmax/dose, AUC/dose (P < .001)
and clearance (P < .02); NOT significant for weight-normalized clearance (P = .12), though still 25-30% lower in
women (Discussion); no effect on T1/2. Abstract: AUC 40-50% higher in women; the gender effect was "incompletely
explained by body weight" (Abstract).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Placebo-normalized 5 h effect areas: in men none differed from zero at any dose; in women DSST, CRT response time,
CRT lapses, SCT and VAS differed at 1.75 and/or 3.5 mg (Table 2). Aggregate mean-concentration vs effect r2: women
DSST 0.79 (power function y = Bx^A), CRT response time 0.51, lapses 0.69, VAS 0.40; men <= 0.32; individual-subject
r2 always < 0.2-0.3 (Table 3). No PD effect beyond 3 h; none at 4-5 h. Authors infer greater intrinsic sensitivity
in women. No ke0, EC50 or hysteresis analysis. Not an opioid; no MEAC.

## Covariates
Sex (on clearance and sensitivity); body weight partly accounts for the clearance difference.

## Caveats
Sublingual low-dose product (1-3.5 mg), not oral IR; small n (11 F, 13 M); sponsor-funded (Transcept, Purdue)
with sponsor-paid writing support; weights only in a supplement.
