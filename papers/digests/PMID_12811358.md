# Peck 2003: single clinical trial plus causal evidence

## Study
Regulatory commentary (pp. 481-490). Argues that under section 115a of the FDA Modernization Act of 1997 one
adequate, well-controlled trial plus confirmatory evidence can establish effectiveness. The second trial of the
traditional 2-trial rule is said to serve causal rather than empiric confirmation (ruling out confounding,
interaction, transience); pharmacologic action shown by a randomized, blinded phase IIB dose- or
concentration-response trial, with longitudinal biomarker/concentration monitoring and mechanistic model-based
analysis, can serve instead. No patients, drugs or fitted values.

## Model and parameters
None. One statistical remark (p. 487): two independent trials each at two-sided P<.05 give a type I error favouring a beneficial
treatment effect (both trials in that direction, one-sided 0.025 each) below 0.025^2 = 0.000625; the authors suggest a single trial need only reach P<.025.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency. Concept only: dose/concentration-response relation within and between individuals as
evidence of pharmacologic action; biomarkers on the causal path as confirmatory evidence (appendix Table I,
pp. 489-490, lists disease/drug class/biomarker triplets, none opioid or anesthetic). Opioid audit: none.
