# Lalonde 1987: propranolol Emax on exercise heart rate, total and unbound

## Study
- Nine healthy male volunteers, 23-39 years (mean 27), 66-82 kg (mean 75); nonsmokers, no caffeine, alcohol
  or other drugs (PDF p2, printed 570).
- Randomised crossover of three regimens: regular propranolol 160 mg (two 80 mg Inderal tablets), one
  sustained-release 160 mg capsule (Inderal LA), or no drug (control). Phase I single dose; Phase II the same
  regimens once daily for 7 days (steady state). Oral, fasting, 8 AM (PDF p3, printed 571).
- End point: treadmill exercise (6 mph, 10% grade) before dosing and at 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 32 and 48 h; heart rate
  from 15 R-R intervals on a modified V5 lead. Effect = percentage reduction in exercise HR versus the
  time-matched control-period HR (Eq. 1). Control exercise HR about 157-163 bpm (Table I, PDF p5).
- Assay: SERUM (clotted, no heparin, to avoid binding artefacts), HPLC for propranolol and 4-hydroxypropranolol
  (CV 1.5-10.5%, LLOQ 1 ng/mL); unbound fraction by equilibrium dialysis at 37 °C with 3H-propranolol (98%
  radiochemical purity), volume-shift corrected (PDF p3-4).
- Analysis: linear, Emax and sigmoid Emax models fitted per subject by unweighted nonlinear least squares
  (PCNONLIN); comparison by residuals, parameter precision and AIC. Sigmoid Emax was not identifiable
  (parameter correlations >0.99, CVs >400%); Emax beat linear by AIC in every subject (PDF p8, printed 576).
  IR and SR data, and single-dose and steady-state data, were POOLED within each subject because the
  relationships did not differ (PDF p8).
- No pharmacokinetic parameters are reported here (unbound PK is in Straka 1987, ref. 16).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: % reduction in exercise-induced heart rate (treadmill, ~160 bpm control), not resting HR, not BP.
- No hysteresis seen when concentration-effect points were joined in time (Fig. 1, PDF p5; text PDF p4): the
  authors conclude no apparent delay between serum concentration and effect. Sampling was at 2 h and later after
  oral dosing, so this cannot resolve a delay of minutes. No ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak reported.
- 4-hydroxypropranolol: AUC molar ratio to propranolol 0.074 ± 0.032 (single dose) and 0.037 ± 0.030 (steady
  state) after regular propranolol; metabolite contribution judged negligible (PDF p4, p8).
- Authors' derived blockade levels from mean EC50 (PDF p12, printed 580): 50% of maximal blockade at 18 ng/mL total
  (1.7 unbound), 75% at 55 (5 unbound), ~90% at 160 (15 unbound).
- Unbound EC50 consistent with in-vitro Kd 0.8-3.4 ng/mL (human and guinea-pig cardiac tissue; PDF p11).
- Fig. 4 (PDF p12): simulated Emax time course; effect declines roughly linearly in time between 0.25× and 4× EC50.
- Not an opioid paper; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Unbound fraction tracked alpha-1-acid glycoprotein (r = 0.776); no concentration-dependent binding
(r = 0.088) (PDF p11). Unbound concentrations did not reduce intersubject variability in EC50.

## Caveats
- n = 9 young healthy men; exercise tachycardia only; individual fits, no population model, no SEs of the means.
- Abstract gives EC50 18.2 ± 15.6 ng/mL total and 1.66 ± 1.56 unbound; the sample SDs of the Table II values are
  14.7 and 1.47 (derived), and the table's own CVs (81, 88%) match the latter, so the abstract's ± is not the SD of
  Table II. Not used by stanpumpR.
- Text on PDF p8 says the pooled parameters are in "Table I"; they are in Table II (misprint).
- Unbound fraction by radiolabel overestimates true fu by ~0.009 (authors' estimate, PDF p11).
- Data pool IR tablets and SR capsules (Inderal LA), and single-dose and steady state.
