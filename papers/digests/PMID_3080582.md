# Cloyd 1986: mannitol PK and serum osmolality in dogs and humans

## Study
- Dogs: 5 mongrels (3 F, 2 M), 9-12 kg, pentobarbital anaesthesia, ventilated; 20% mannitol IV bolus 0.5, 1.0,
  1.5 g/kg (injected over ~10, 15, 35 s), same sequence, >= 4 days apart; sampling 0-120 min.
- Humans: n = 4 men. Two healthy (20 and 35 y, in a chemical-dependency programme), two critically ill with
  cerebral oedema (26 y, grade III astrocytoma; 24 y, comatose after subarachnoid haemorrhage); normal renal and
  liver function. 0.5 or 0.7 g/kg (0.71, 0.5, 0.67, 0.5) over 15 min by pump; sampling to 180 min after the end
  of infusion. Ambulatory subjects drank ad lib; comatose patients on D5 1/3 NS.
- Assay: enzymatic (mannitol dehydrogenase, NAD-linked; Blomquist 1981); osmolality by freezing-point depression.
- Analysis: weighted nonlinear least squares (D'Argenio & Schumitzky 1979), two-compartment, elimination from
  central; infusion time included. TBC = dose/AUC.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point serum osmolality (no ICP, no effect site). Dogs: osmolality change > 10 mOsm/kg linear in mannitol,
y = 0.8x + 1.09 (mOsm/kg vs mmol/L, N = 73, r = 0.9; Fig 3); peak changes 42.6, 66.3, 51.6 mOsm/kg; only 1.5 g/kg
held > 10 mOsm/kg beyond 10 min. Humans: peak osmolality rise 17 mOsm/kg in both critically ill patients, 1 min
after end of infusion; authors state the osmolality-change / mannitol ratio was 1.0 (Discussion, p305). (derived
from Fig 4, patient 1: rise ~17 mOsm/kg against peak mannitol ~24 mmol/L, which looks nearer 0.7; figure
reading only.) Authors expect 0.25-1.0 g/kg rapid infusion to raise osmolality 10-20 mOsm/kg.

## Covariates
None modelled.

## Caveats
n = 4, heterogeneous; patient 4 (TBC 22.5 mL/min/kg, Vss 1.21 L/kg) is implausible and drives the means; human
sampling only 3 h, so a slower terminal phase would be missed. Dogs anaesthetised, one animal dropped at 0.5 g/kg.
Fluid not at steady state (diuresis).
