# Vallner 1981: Hydromorphone IV and oral PK and bioavailability

## Study
Pilot randomised two-way crossover, University of Georgia (Knoll-supported). Six healthy non-smoking male volunteers, 25-29 y (mean 27.5), 68-90 kg (mean 76.8); drug-screen negative; fasted 8 h. Hydromorphone HCl **2 mg IV** (2 mg/mL) and **4 mg oral** (one 4 mg tablet), one week apart. Plasma at 0, 15, 30, 45 min, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 h, plus 2, 5, 10 min after IV. Assay: modified morphine radioimmunoassay (morphine-6 antiserum, tritiated dihydromorphine tracer, charcoal separation), standards 2.5-20 ng/mL; 8-12 h samples below the standard curve were estimated with fourfold plasma volume and may read high from nonspecific binding (p. 156). Analysis in SAS; one-compartment model (a two-compartment fit gave an alpha half-life of 2.22 +/- 1.44 min, judged unrealistic and ignored).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic measurement: no MEAC or analgesic concentration, no ke0/t1/2ke0/tPeak, no hysteresis. Side effects only: after oral, 1 of 6 nausea and drowsiness; after IV, 5 of 6 nausea, 3 drowsiness. Introduction states hydromorphone has about **seven times** morphine's analgesic effect by IV injection (citing Keeri-Szanto 1976, ref 1); no oral potency ratio.

## Covariates
None (six men; Vd reported per kg only).

## Caveats
n = 6 young men; RIA of a morphine antiserum (cross-reactivity with hydromorphone metabolites possible); AUCs truncated at 12 h; terminal-phase samples below the calibration range; F from 0-12 h AUC ratios with very high variability (one subject 1.25); authors say more subjects are needed before a definitive bioavailability statement.
