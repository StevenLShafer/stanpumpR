# Daneshmend 1982: food and the oral and IV kinetics of labetalol

## Study
Six healthy volunteers (4 M, 2 F, 20-24 y, non-smokers; weights not reported), randomized four-way crossover at weekly
intervals: 200 mg orally fasting; 0.5 mg/kg IV fasting; 200 mg orally after a standard 1.7 MJ breakfast; 0.5 mg/kg IV
after the breakfast (PDF p1). Supine 6 h. Venous samples at 15-360 min (first at 15 min). Spectrofluorometric assay
(Martin 1976), sensitivity 15 ng/mL (PDF p2). Non-compartmental: terminal half-life by regression; AUC trapezoidal plus
extrapolation; CL = dose/AUC(iv); V = dose x t1/2 / (AUC x 0.693) (an area volume); F = (AUCoral/AUCiv) x (dose
iv/dose oral). Salt or base basis of the doses not stated.

Checks (derived): CL x t1/2 / ln2 = 1923 x 214 / 0.693 = 594 L, near the 567 L mean. Per kg without weights: 0.5 mg/kg /
23986 ng min/mL = 20.8 mL/min/kg (ratio of means). The IV decline was biexponential, but the first sample at 15 min
leaves the distribution phase unsampled; no compartmental parameters. Food raised mean V by 21% and CL by 20% (NS),
lowered AUC(iv) (P = 0.02), raised AUC(oral) 11% and F 38%; half-lives unchanged (PDF p2-3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Supine BP and HR half-hourly to 4 h then hourly: small falls, no significant fed-fasting difference in absolute values;
greater falls in SBP and HR after food when changes were examined (Figs. 3-4, PDF p3-4). No concentration-effect
analysis. Authors note labetalol CL at least twice hepatic plasma flow, suggesting extrahepatic clearance (PDF p4).

## Covariates
Food only.

## Caveats
Six young subjects; weights not given; spectrofluorometric assay; 15-min first sample; SEM not SD.
