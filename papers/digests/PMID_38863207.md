# Robinson 2024: CPIC guideline for CYP2B6 genotype and methadone

PDF pages map to printed pages as PDF p. 1 = printed p. 932 through PDF p. 7 = printed p. 938.

## Study
- Clinical practice guideline (CPIC), built on a systematic literature review summarised in supplementary Table S1 (not in the archive). Last author and corresponding author Kharasch ED. Received 15 March 2024, accepted 22 May 2024 (printed p. 932 / PDF p. 1).
- No new data. Scope: interpreting CYP2B6 genotype results and the evidence on methadone pharmacokinetics, dose, and clinical outcomes. Detailed methadone dosing is outside its scope (p. 932).
- Evidence base as characterised by the authors (p. 935 / PDF p. 4): mostly oral rather than IV, steady state rather than single dose, total rather than unbound concentrations, patients rather than healthy subjects, and mostly CYP2B6*6 or c.516G>T alone. Dose and genotype were studied only in opioid use disorder, never in pain.

## Model and parameters
No model. Genotype-to-phenotype assignment, Table 1 (printed p. 933 / PDF p. 2):

| Predicted phenotype | Allele combination | Example diplotypes |
|---|---|---|
| Ultrarapid (UM) | two increased-function | *4/*4 |
| Rapid (RM) | one normal + one increased | *1/*4 |
| Normal (NM) | two normal | *1/*1, *1/*2, *2/*2 |
| Intermediate (IM) | normal + decreased; normal + no-function; increased + decreased; increased + no-function | *1/*6, *1/*18 |
| Poor (PM) | two decreased; two no-function; decreased + no-function | *6/*6, *18/*18, *6/*18 |
| Indeterminate | one or two uncertain-function alleles | *1/*3, *3/*3 |

- Table 1 footnote b: few clinical data for increased plus decreased/no-function diplotypes. For methadone, limited data suggest *4/*6 may have more activity than normal metabolisers (ref 43, Kharasch 2015).
- Allele function groups (p. 933): normal *1; decreased *6, *9; no function *18; increased *4; uncertain *3. *6 minor allele frequency 15-60% by ancestry. 49 star alleles defined by PharmVar. UM and RM categories were created for the efavirenz guideline; their clinical significance "remains to be established" (p. 933).
- Genotyping limits (p. 934 / PDF p. 3): untested alleles default to *1; c.516G>T alone should be reported *9 but is sometimes reported *6; *6 needs c.785A>G confirmed and other variants excluded.

- Dose and genotype in opioid use disorder (p. 935): *6 carriers more often stable on < 55 mg than > 100 mg (321 Han Chinese, ref 45). Mean maintenance dose 96 / 129 / 150 mg for c.516G>T homozygotes / heterozygotes / noncarriers (Israel, ref 46); 44 / 52 / 68 mg in 100 Taiwanese (ref 47). Most studies fail to replicate.
- Rapid metabolisers (p. 935): only two studies. One found higher R and S oral clearance in *4 carriers after a single dose (ref 43, Kharasch 2015). The other found lower clearance in *4 carriers, but analysed *4 and *9 separately without accounting for the *6 haplotype (ref 44, Bart 2021). No study included *4/*4 homozygotes.
- Clearance effect is smaller for R than for S, and decreased-function alleles reduce S-EDDP formation more than R-EDDP (p. 935, ref 43).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Dosing recommendations, Table 2 (printed p. 936 / PDF p. 5):

| Phenotype | Implication | Recommendation | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ultrarapid | no data | no recommendation | no recommendation |
| Rapid | limited evidence for decreased R and S concentrations | standard dosing, titration and monitoring | moderate |
| Normal | normal metabolism and concentrations | standard dosing, titration and monitoring | strong |
| Intermediate | higher S concentrations, unknown clinical implications; no difference in steady-state R | standard dosing, titration and monitoring | moderate |
| Poor | higher S concentrations, unknown clinical implications; no difference in steady-state R | standard dosing, titration and monitoring | moderate |
| Indeterminate | n/a | no recommendation | no recommendation |

- Conclusion (abstract p. 932; Therapeutic recommendations p. 935): current evidence does not support changing standard methadone prescribing, acute or chronic, on CYP2B6 genotype.
- Reasons given (pp. 935-936): oral methadone is already titrated slowly, with no evidence for slower titration in poor metabolisers; only one study compared QTc across phenotypes; the concentration-QTc relation is weak and small, so a pharmacokinetic effect cannot be extrapolated to QTc risk. ECG monitoring guidelines are unaffected by genotype.
- Treatment response in opioid use disorder (p. 935): no genotype effect (208 patients, ref 41; 105 patients, ref 48). End points were self-reported cessation or a negative urine opioid screen.
- Paediatrics (p. 936): CYP2B6 mRNA reaches adult levels by 1 year; genotype effects on PK similar to adults (refs 50, 51); no change recommended in children.
- CYP2D6 (p. 936): does not appear to affect methadone; the CPIC opioid guideline gives no recommendation (level C, ref 55).
- No MEAC, no ke0 or tPeak, no analgesic concentration-response data. Pain dosing and genotype were not studied (p. 935).

## Covariates
CYP2B6 phenotype from genotype (Table 1). Inducibility (p. 934): phenobarbital, rifampin and chronic methadone itself induce CYP2B6 and can change the genotype-phenotype relation (refs 7, 8). Inhibition and induction are noted again on p. 937.

## Caveats
- Recommendations rest on a supplementary evidence table (Table S1) that is not in the archive, so the per-study evidence cannot be audited from this card.
- Long QTc is defined as > 450 ms (males) and > 460 ms (females) on p. 934, but the Eap 2007 study is described with > 470 ms for females on p. 935. Different definitions in the source, not an extraction error.
- Most evidence is oral, steady-state, total concentrations, in opioid use disorder; pain is unstudied.
- Indeterminate genotypes (uncertain-function alleles such as *3) have no phenotype assignment.
