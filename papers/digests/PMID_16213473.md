# Parsey 2006: acute SERT occupancy by sertraline ([11C]DASB PET) vs plasma concentration

## Study
- 17 healthy volunteers (8 M / 9 F), age 30 +/- 9 (18-61) y; no psychiatric diagnosis or psychotropics.
- Baseline PET, then repeat PET after oral sertraline: 25 mg/day x 4 d (n = 3), 50 mg/day x 4 d (n = 6), or
  50 mg x 2 d then 100 mg x 4 d (n = 8). Scans and plasma sampling ~24 h after the last dose (trough), assumed
  > 90% of steady state from a 26-h half-life.
- [11C]DASB, arterial input function, LEGA distribution volumes; cerebellar grey matter chosen as reference
  region (post-mortem autoradiography plus in vivo occupancy).
- Plasma sertraline by HPLC with fluorescence (dansyl chloride); linear 10-800 ng/mL; minimum quantifiable
  level about 5 ng/mL. Two subjects' levels were too low to measure (Fig 8).
- Supported in part by Pfizer.

## Model and parameters
Occupancy = a x C / (b + C) (Eq 2), C = total plasma sertraline (ng/mL).

Cerebellar maximal occupancies: grey 32.8%, white 36.0%, vermis 37.5%, total 39.0%. Dorsal putamen max
occupancy 78% vs 88% striatum in Meyer 2004 (chronic dosing).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: SERT binding potential occupancy by PET (not a clinical effect). No ke0 or time course;
  steady-state trough occupancy vs trough plasma concentration.
- Occupancy is near-saturated at the lowest doses; dose-plasma correlation weak (R = 0.493, p = 0.062).
- Discussion estimates ~74% DAT and ~15% NET occupancy at ~70 nM plasma (200 mg/day, from Meyer 2004).
- Desmethylsertraline said not to cross the blood-brain barrier (citing Preskorn 1993); not modelled.

## Covariates
None; weight, BMI, age and sex did not correlate with plasma concentration.

## Caveats
- The fitted EC50 (1.9 ng/mL) lies below the assay's ~5 ng/mL quantifiable level and has a large SD (1.5);
  few data at low concentrations.
- Maximal occupancies above 100% are a fitting artefact. Small n; healthy volunteers; acute (4-6 d) dosing.
