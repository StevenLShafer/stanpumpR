# Yano 2001: posterior predictive check for PK/PD models

## Study
Simulation study of the posterior predictive check (PPC) as a PK/PD model-evaluation tool. Setting is deliberately simple: one individual, 10 observations, individual-level (not population) models. No drug and no patients. Analysis software NONMEM; S-plus 3.2 for the density and interpolation steps (p. 176-179).

- Analysis models: PK1 mono-exponential with proportional error; PK2 biexponential with proportional error; PD1 Emax model with additive error on the logit scale.
- Alternative simulation models: PK2 (against PK1), PK2ε biexponential with additive error (against PK2), PD2 sigmoid Emax (against PD1).
- PK design: bolus 10 units, 10 samples at 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6 time units. PD design: 10 concentrations from 0.125 to 16.
- 100 simulated data sets per model pair, 100 posterior draws each; three approximations to the parameter posterior: f1 (point estimate), f2 (parametric bootstrap), f3 (asymptotic normal from the NONMEM covariance matrix).
- Statistics tested: Table I (PK: AUC, AUMC, MRT, VRT, Css, Cmax, Cmin, individual concentrations, MPE/MAE/RMSE-type residual summaries), Table II (PD analogues).

## Model and parameters
No clinical PK or PD parameters. Simulation settings only (p. 177): PK2 parameters (A, a, σ) fixed at (10, 3, 0.15), ln(b) drawn from a normal centred on ln(0.693); PK2ε uses σ = 1.5; PD2 has (Emax, C50, σ) = (1, 1, 0.15) with ln(γ) centred on ln(4).

## Findings
- The PPC is conservative under the null: for many statistics P(pPPC <= α) < α, so adequate models are rarely rejected (abstract, p. 171; Fig. 3).
- Power is modest. In Appendix Table AI, MRT detects PK2-vs-PK1 in 83% of runs at p<=0.2 and AUC in 0%; error-model misspecification (PK2ε vs PK2) was the hardest to detect (p. 184).
- Statistics that are also functions of the parameters (residual-type measures) perform worst.
- A simple "sufficiency" screen (maximum correlation R²max between statistic and parameters) is only a weak guide (Fig. 4, p. 186-187).
- No advantage for the costly f2 over f1, the point-estimate approximation (p. 184-185, 188).
- Failure of the PPC to reject a model is little evidence the model is right (p. 189).

## Caveats
Single subject, 10 points, two-compartment at most; the authors say generalisation to population models is untested (p. 188).
