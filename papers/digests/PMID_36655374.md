# Mahdy 2023: PBPK of risperidone and paliperidone in pregnancy and a neonate

## Study
- **Design:** PBPK modelling (Simcyp v21) of risperidone (RIS) and its active metabolite paliperidone (PAL,
  9-hydroxyrisperidone), with a case report.
- **Model building:**
  - adult model built from literature in vitro, IV and oral data;
  - verified against White and Japanese single 1 mg oral RIS studies (Figure 1, nine datasets);
  - extrapolated to children and, through Simcyp's pregnancy population (Abduljalil gestational equations), to
    pregnancy;
  - pregnancy model checked against Westin's four pregnant patients on repeated 5 mg oral RIS (Figure 3, p6).
- **Case:** one 38-year-old Japanese woman, 55.1 kg, 152 cm. She took RIS 0.5 mg twice daily for 12 days in the
  third trimester, then 1 mg once daily postpartum, and delivered at 40 weeks 5 days. Her son weighed 2852 g;
  his serum creatinine was 0.88 mg/dL at birth and 0.41 mg/dL on day 6, and he was not breastfed (p4).
- **Assay:** serum LC-MS/MS. RIS was below 0.5 ng/mL in every maternal and neonatal sample.
- **Pregnancy inputs at term (p4):** CYP2D6 activity ×3 and CYP3A4 ×1.24 against the pre-pregnant state. Plasma
  volume +50%, RBC volume +28% and cardiac output +30%. HSA −31%, haematocrit −38% and GFR −27%; the GFR figure
  is printed as a decrease.
- A fixed-parameter, one-parameter-per-simulation analysis held each physiological change at its non-pregnant
  value in turn.

**Fixed-parameter results (Table 2, p9; RIS CLt at 40 weeks 43.8 L/h is the reference):**
- Holding CYP2D6 at its non-pregnant value lowered RIS CLt to 31.8 L/h (0.73) and raised PAL Cmax to 5.51 ng/mL.
- Serum albumin gave 40.9 L/h (0.93) and haematocrit 41.9 L/h (0.96).
- The other parameters changed RIS CLt by 1% or less.
- With everything fixed, CLt was 27.5 L/h and PAL Cmax 7.08 ng/mL, matching the 0-week model.

**Adult non-pregnant verification:**
- The Results (p5) give predicted "CLt/F and CLt values for RIS and PAL" as 26.5 and 65.1 L/h. The Discussion
  (p10) calls the same two numbers RIS CLt and CLt/F, against reported 23.6 and 65.4 L/h. The labelling differs
  between the two places.
- The Discussion gives predicted PAL CLt 5.19 L/h, against 5.19 L/h in the literature. Table 1's 0-week PAL CLt
  is 6.14 L/h; that is the pregnant-population model at 0 weeks with an i.v. PAL probe, so the two are not the
  same quantity.
- Mass balance after a 1 mg oral dose (p5): F 59%, first-pass 40%, urinary excretion 4% for RIS and 44% for PAL.

**Observed case values:**
- Maternal PAL 2.05-3.80 ng/mL before delivery and 3.80-9.90 ng/mL postpartum, all within the 90% prediction
  interval.
- Cord PAL 1.10 ng/mL (umbilical artery) and 1.05 ng/mL (vein). Median maternal-to-cord ratio 2.83 (range
  1.98-3.61).
- Neonatal PAL 0.99, 1.03, 0.83 and 0.82 ng/mL on days 0-3, then undetectable.
- Flanders metadata eGFR was the most accurate of 10 neonatal renal-function methods: ME 22.3% ± 6.0%, RMSE
  23.8%.

**Westin cohort (as cited here, p10):** active-moiety concentration fell from 24.4 ng/mL at baseline to
18.4 ng/mL in the third trimester (derived −25%).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No concentration-effect, therapeutic range or receptor occupancy model.

## Covariates
Gestational age, through Simcyp's pregnancy equations. The paper gives no explicit parameter-covariate equations.

## Caveats
- PBPK prediction, not measured pregnancy clearance. The pregnant verification set is Westin's four patients,
  and the case is n = 1 with RIS undetectable, so only PAL was compared.
- Only three neonatal PAL values entered the eGFR comparison. Day-1 creatinine was used throughout, because Simcyp
  cannot follow its fall in the first days (p11).
- Fetal exposure was not predicted.
- The adult CLt/CLt/F labelling is inconsistent between the Results and the Discussion (see above).
