# Cortegiani 2018: precision and bias of propofol TCI in prolonged neurosurgical use

## Study
Retrospective chart review, one centre (Turin, Italy), 3 months. 15 adult ASA I patients (8 M, 7 F), age 54.9 +/- 13 y, BMI 24 +/- 3.2 (Table 1: weights 53-84 kg, heights 154-183 cm), elective brain-tumour removal then ICU sedation. Propofol 1% by Diprifusor TCI (Marsh model) and remifentanil by TCI (Minto model). Induction target 4 ug/mL propofol, maintenance 3-5 ug/mL (BIS 35-50); postoperative sedation target initially 2-3 ug/mL aiming RASS -4/-3, BIS 40-60. Remifentanil effect-site 3-6 ng/mL at induction, mean 4.1 +/- 0.8 ng/mL intraoperatively, 2.4 +/- 0.4 ng/mL postoperatively. Drug administration lasted 31 +/- 6 h. Arterial blood propofol (Cmeas) by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (assay CV 2-4%, QC 94-98% accurate) at 40 min, 2 h, 4 h, 8 h, 16 h, then every 8 h and at emergence (RASS -2). 94 paired Cmeas/Cpred. All patients were on at least one antiepileptic drug (discussion).

## Model and parameters
No model was fitted. The Marsh model was evaluated (Cpred is the TCI's prediction). Results (abstract PDF p. 1; Results PDF p. 4-5):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points were RASS and BIS, not concentration-effect fits. No ke0, tPeak, EC50, MEAC or opioid potency ratio is reported. Cited from Barr 2001 (discussion, not measured here): plasma propofol 0.25, 0.6, 1.0, 2.0 ug/mL for Ramsay 2, 3, 4, 5. Remifentanil (Minto model) was co-medication only; no remifentanil concentration-effect result.

## Covariates
None modelled. Authors speculate on higher clearance (cardiac output, fat distribution, enzyme-inducing antiepileptics, brain-tumour clearance about 40% higher per their ref 35) to explain overprediction.

## Caveats
Retrospective, n = 15, ASA I only, remifentanil co-administration, no clearance or cardiac output measured. The discussion's phrase "positive bias (overestimation of the pump)" runs opposite to the PE formula, so read direction from the formula and the -34.7% value. Typos: "Cmean", "MADPE".
