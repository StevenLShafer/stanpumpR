# Clark 2013: IV vs oral topiramate, absolute bioavailability, healthy volunteers

## Study
Open-label two-way crossover, 12 healthy adults (6 F, 6 M; 11 white, 1 African American), age 19-55 (mean 35) y,
mean weight 78.2 kg (58.3-112.3), CrCl > 70 mL/min, no medications. Two subjects got 50 mg IV then 50 mg oral;
10 got 100 mg IV and 100 mg oral in random order, 2-week washout. IV: 10 mg/mL topiramate in 10% Captisol
(sulfobutylether beta-cyclodextrin), 15-min syringe-pump infusion. Oral: Topamax 50 mg tablets, fasted 2 h before
and 4 h after. Plasma sampled pre-dose, 5, 15, 30 min, 1, 2, 4, 6, 10, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120 h. LC-MS (ESI),
linear 0.1-20 ug/mL, LOQ 0.04 ug/mL. Non-compartmental analysis, WinNonlin 5.2; parameters dose-normalised.

Bioequivalence (Table 2, oral/IV % LS-mean ratio, 90% CI): Cmax/D 92.26 (84.17-101.13), AUClast/D 109.24
(103.20-115.62), AUC0-inf/D 108.44 (102.85-114.34). (derived: Vd 1.06 L/kg x 78.2 kg = about 83 L.)
The Discussion writes clearance as "1.2-1.3 L/kg", evidently a unit slip for L/h (Table 1).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK-PD modelling. Mild CNS adverse effects (cognitive impairment, ataxia, lightheadedness, nystagmus,
intoxication, memory impairment) within 2 h of dosing by either route, during or within 15 min of the IV
infusion, taken by the authors as rapid brain entry (Table 3). No ke0. No vital-sign, ECG or infusion-site changes.
A 1 mg/kg IV dose predicted to give Cmax about 1 mg/L (0.75-1.25 in two-thirds). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Age, height, weight and sex had no significant effect on any PK parameter (univariate ANOVA, n = 12).

## Caveats
Small n, healthy volunteers, single dose, 50 and 100 mg pooled after dose-normalisation; NCA only. The 42-h
half-life is longer than the commonly cited 21 h; authors note Lambrecht 2011 found a similarly long value.
Cmax after the 15-min IV infusion is affected by sampling times (first sample 5 min post-start).
