# Igarashi 2021: population PK of vincristine in adults with lymphoma

## Study
- 30 adults with newly diagnosed malignant lymphoma, University of Fukui Hospital, Japan, 2009-2014, on CHOP-like
  regimens (R-CHOP 22, R-THP-COP 6, R-CVP 1, CHOP 1) (Table 1, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 504).
- Table 1 (page image), median (range): 17 women, 13 men; age 60 (25-82) y; height 159 (138-182) cm; weight 59
  (42-97) kg; BSA 1.60 (1.29-2.10) m²; vincristine dose 1.8 (0.40-2.0) mg; albumin 3.6 g/dL; bilirubin 0.5 mg/dL;
  ALP 210 IU/L. CYP3A4 inhibitors in 9, inducers in 3. Ethnicity is not stated.
- Dosing: 10-min IV infusion; plasma at 10 and 45 min, 3, 7 and 19 h after the start (PDF p. 2 / printed p. 502).
  164 samples, 3 (1.8%) below the limit of quantification and excluded; mean 1.1 cycles, 5.5 samples per patient.
- Assay: LC-MS (APCI), vinblastine internal standard, linear 0.3-50 ng/mL, intra-day CV < 10% (p. 3 / 503).
  Samples were assayed a median 218 days (3-1148) after collection; re-assays at 244 and 683 days gave 101.8% and
  97.8% of the first value.
- Analysis: NONMEM 7.3, FOCE; exponential IIV and residual error; stepwise covariate search (ΔOBJ > 3.84 forward,
  < -6.63 backward); bootstrap of 1000, 659 of which returned results.
- Not stated: the dose basis (vincristine sulfate or base) and the BSA formula.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No effect-site model, ke0 or therapeutic range. Exposure-response only (Table 3, PDF p. 8 / printed p. 508, and
p. 5 / 505), CTCAE v4.0 grades split at the median:
- Anaemia: lower CL with higher grade (P = 0.008; 0.040 after Bonferroni).
- Peripheral neuropathy: first-course Cmax lower in the higher-grade group (P = 0.002; 0.024 Bonferroni); no relation
  to mean or cumulative exposure. (The Discussion reads this as Cmax being related to neuropathy; the direction in
  the Results is the opposite of a simple "higher peak, worse neuropathy".)
- Neutropenia: AUC trend (P = 0.035, 0.175 after correction). No relation for thrombocytopenia, nausea, constipation.
- DLBCL (n = 16): AUC ≥ 60.5 ng·h/mL (ROC AUC 0.794) and dose ≥ 1.22 mg/m² (ROC AUC 0.746) went with longer PFS
  (log-rank P = 0.023 and 0.013); not OS (Fig. 3).
- Table 3 medians: Cmax 48.9-64.9 ng/mL, AUC 44.9-65.0 by group. Table 3 prints AUC units as "ng h/L"; the text and
  Fig. 3 use ng·h/mL, so "ng h/L" is a misprint.

## Covariates
CL only: BSA (power 1.74, reference 1.60 m²) and age (power -0.485, reference 60 y). Height and weight were also
significant univariately (ΔOBJ 33.34 and 24.52; BSA 32.18); BSA chosen because it is used for dosing. Removing BSA
raised OBJ by 22.85. Not significant: sex, albumin, bilirubin, AST, ALT, ALP, GGT, CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers.
Authors' check: BSA 1.4→1.9 m² gives 1.7-fold CL, age 53→82 gives 0.8-fold (p. 5 / 505).

## Caveats
- n = 30, one Japanese centre, mostly a single cycle; inter-occasion variability not estimated.
- First sample at 10 min, so the 3-min phase and V1 are poorly identified (bootstrap CI 1.24-18.5 L).
- Q2 and V2 fixed at an unstated "average value"; 341 of 1000 bootstraps failed.
- Median peaks (49-65 ng/mL) lie at or above the 50 ng/mL top of the calibrated range; no dilution is described.
- Last sample at 19 h, roughly one terminal half-life (17.6 h, derived), so V3 and Q3 rest on short tails.
- Long frozen storage before assay (median 218 days), though re-assay stability was shown in 3 samples.
- Exposure-response tests are small and multiple; co-administered cytotoxics not accounted for.
