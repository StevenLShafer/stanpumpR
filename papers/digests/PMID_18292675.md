# Peyton 2008: second gas effect on arterial sevoflurane

## Study
Randomised two-group study (Austin Hospital, Melbourne), 14 adults ASA I-III having general surgery, BMI < 30, no lung disease (PDF p 1-2). Control n = 7: fresh gas 2% sevoflurane in oxygen. N2O group n = 7: 2% sevoflurane in 33% O2 / 67% N2O. Fresh gas flow 9 L/min to hold inspired partial pressure constant. Anaesthesia: midazolam 1.5-2.0 mg, propofol 1.5-2.0 mg/kg, an opioid and a non-depolarising relaxant; volume-controlled ventilation (7-10 mL/kg, 9-12 breaths/min) to end-tidal CO2 30-33 mmHg; propofol infusion started at 5.0 mg/kg/h and titrated to BIS 40 during the measurement period. Baseline arterial gas after 5 min of stable state, then the allocated mixture started. Arterial samples at 2, 5, 10 and 30 min; simultaneous end-tidal and inspired sevoflurane (mean of 5 breaths) by a Capnomac Ultima infrared analyser with a linear correction for N2O. Arterial sevoflurane partial pressure by a double headspace equilibration method (validated against tonometered blood; the paper gives the equation for Pa(sevo) from the two equilibrations, PDF p 3). Statistics: unpaired t test per time with Bonferroni, two-way ANOVA, paired t test comparing the group difference in PA/PI vs Pa/PI.

Baseline (Table 1, PDF p 3, mean (SD), control vs N2O): age 64.3 (17.2) vs 63.3 (10.5) yr; height 163 (14) vs 166 (11) cm; weight 80.8 (18.6) vs 78.3 (6.5) kg; PaO2 433 (112) vs 393 (104) mmHg; PaCO2 38.8 (3.9) vs 38.2 (3.8) mmHg; Hb 11.9 (2.0) vs 14.2 (2.4) g/dL (P = 0.08). Four women, three men per group.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model is fitted. The paper presents a qualitative two-compartment illustration (Figures 2 and 3, PDF p 5-6): with half the inspired N2O taken up, an inspired 1% "second gas" is concentrated to 1.7% in a single compartment; with blood flow split 3/4 : 1/4 the arterial second gas becomes 2.2%. These are illustrative numbers, not fitted.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.
- End point: sevoflurane partial pressure as a fraction of inspired, end-expired (PA/PI) and arterial (Pa/PI).
- PA/PI(sevo) higher with N2O by 9.8% at 2 min (P = 0.011) and also at 5 min (P = 0.003, PDF p 3); not significant at 10 and 30 min after Bonferroni (text, PDF p 3-4).
- Pa/PI(sevo) higher with N2O by 23.6% at 2 min, declining to 12.5% at 30 min (P < 0.01 at 2, 5 and 30 min; text PDF p 4). Overall both ratios were higher with N2O (two-way ANOVA P < 0.001, no group-time interaction: P = 0.63 and 0.93); the group difference was larger for Pa/PI than PA/PI (P < 0.05).
- The authors state the arterial effect was two to three times that on end-expired partial pressure (abstract and Discussion).
- BIS at 5 min: 40.7 (6.6) control vs 25.3 (8.5) N2O, P = 0.004 (Table 2, PDF p 3). The abstract and text print 25.4 for the N2O group; the Table prints 25.3 (a rounding difference in the paper itself).
- Table 2 (PDF p 3), end-tidal CO2 higher with N2O: 30.0/29.5/29.6/29.8 control vs 31.1/32.2/32.7/33.5 mmHg at 2/5/10/30 min (P = 0.26, 0.03, 0.008, 0.003); minute ventilation 6.2/6.1/6.2/6.2 vs 5.8/5.5/5.5/5.5 L/min (not significant).
- Mechanism offered: N2O uptake is perfusion-driven and concentrated in low V/Q compartments that carry most of the blood flow, so the arterial blood is enriched more than mixed expired gas. Not a fitted model.

## Covariates
None analysed beyond group (N2O vs none). The N2O group was relatively under-ventilated (higher PE'CO2).

## Caveats
n = 7 per group; the N2O group had higher Hb (P = 0.08) and ended with a higher end-tidal CO2; propofol titrated to BIS 40 in both groups, so the BIS difference is not a clean pharmacodynamic comparison; sevoflurane measured by infrared with a calibration for N2O; the authors note Pa/PI results rest on a validated but non-chromatographic headspace method. Sevoflurane only; one inspired concentration (2% fresh gas).
