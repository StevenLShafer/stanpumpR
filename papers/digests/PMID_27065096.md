# Beck-Schimmer 2016: editorial on volatile vs intravenous anesthesia and outcome

## Study
Two-page editorial on the Uhlig et al. meta-analysis (68 RCTs, 7104 patients) of volatile anesthetics versus total intravenous anesthesia. It reports Uhlig's findings: lower mortality and complications with volatiles in cardiac surgery, no difference in other surgery. No new data, no pharmacokinetics or pharmacodynamics, no dosing, no concentrations.

## Caveats
Opinion piece. The authors note that an RCT shows assignment matters, not why; possible confounders include experience, anesthetic depth, co-administered drugs (remifentanil is more common with propofol), local anesthetics and statins; publication bias; inconsistent definitions of pulmonary infection; volatiles pooled although sevoflurane and desflurane looked better than isoflurane in cardiac surgery; surrogate biomarker end points dominate. The two authors disagree on whether another definitive trial is warranted.
