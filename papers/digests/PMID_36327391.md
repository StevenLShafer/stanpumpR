# Dowell 2022: CDC opioid prescribing guideline (MME table)

## Study
Clinical practice guideline (12 recommendations) for outpatient opioid prescribing for acute, subacute and chronic
pain in adults; based on systematic reviews. Not a PK or PD study. The only quantitative pharmacology is the MME
conversion table (MMWR p. 30, "TABLE") and the dosage benchmarks in Recommendation 4.

## Model and parameters
MME conversion factors (Table, p. 30): multiply the daily dose by the factor; all doses mg/day except transdermal
fentanyl (mcg/h). Routes are not stated in the table (oral chronic prescribing implied). Sources: adapted from
Von Korff 2008 and Nielsen 2016 (dose-based, not concentration-based).

  - {name: hydrocodone,    value: 1.0,  unit: MME per mg, source: "Table p.30"}

  - {name: morphine,       value: 1.0,  unit: MME per mg, source: "Table p.30"}
  - {name: oxycodone,      value: 1.5,  unit: MME per mg, source: "Table p.30"}

Table cautions (paraphrased): factors are estimates; not to be used to convert one opioid to another (new opioid
dosed substantially lower for incomplete cross-tolerance); methadone and transdermal fentanyl need particular
caution; buprenorphine excluded; not for opioid use disorder dosing.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No MEAC, no ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak, no concentrations.
- Equianalgesic/potency ratios: only the MME factors above (p. 30).
- Dosage benchmarks (Recommendation 4, pp. 30-31): opioid-naive starting dose often ~5-10 MME single dose or
  20-30 MME/day; pause and carefully reassess before increasing to >=50 MME/day, and add precautions (naloxone,
  follow-up) at >=50 MME/day. 90 MME/day appears only in evidence descriptions (few trials at >=90; a taper study),
  not as a recommended threshold. Observational overdose risk: 50 to <100 MME/day 1.9-4.6x and >=100 MME/day
  2.0-8.9x vs 1 to <20 MME/day (chronic); acute pain 4.73x and 6.64x (p. 30).

## Covariates
None quantitative; caution for age >=65 and renal/hepatic insufficiency.

## Caveats
MME factors are dose-based conventions adapted from two epidemiological sources, explicitly not conversion rules;
the guideline notes there is no single standard MME formula.
