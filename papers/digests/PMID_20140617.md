# Owen 2010: bendamustine population PK and exposure-response in indolent NHL

## Study
- Phase 3, multicentre, open-label, single-agent study (6 cycles) in rituximab-refractory indolent B-cell NHL (p. 2 / 1040).
- Inclusion: adults 18 years or older, WHO performance 0-2, creatinine clearance (Cockcroft-Gault) above 30 mL/min, adequate liver function.
- Dose: bendamustine 120 mg/m2 by 60-min IV infusion on days 1 and 2 of six 21-day cycles; dose reduction allowed for toxicity. The paper writes "bendamustine HCl" in the abstract but never says whether amounts were modelled as the salt or the base.
- Sampling: sparse (up to 4 samples per patient, days 1 or 2 of cycles 1 or 2) in 88 patients; full profiles to 24 h in a subset of 12 (cycle 1). EDTA plasma, on ice, separated within 30 min, frozen at -20 °C.
- Assay: HPLC-MS/MS; LLOQ 0.10 ng/mL (bendamustine, M4), 0.11 ng/mL (M3); inter-day CV at most 10.9, 12.3 and 9.2%.
- Dataset (p. 4 / 1042): 347 bendamustine concentrations from 78 patients (M3: 302 from 77; M4: 254 from 74); 22 patients removed because their samples were assayed outside the validated stability period.
- Table 1 (PDF p. 5 / 1043; rendered), bendamustine column, median (min, max): age 58.5 (31, 84) y; weight 85.0 (44, 151) kg; BSA 2.0 (1.33, 2.72) m2; creatinine clearance 93.3 (40.7, 150) mL/min; albumin 42 (24, 49) g/L; bilirubin 8.55 (1.71, 29.1) µmol/L; ALT 24 (9, 115) U/L; AST 22 (13, 135) U/L. 50 men (64.1%), 28 women; 69 Caucasian, 5 Black, 1 Hispanic, 2 other. (The M3 column prints a median ALT of 240, likely a misprint for 24; it is not used.)
- Software: NONMEM VI level 1.0, FOCE for PK; Laplacian with LIKELIHOOD for the logistic PK/PD models. Index/test/total dataset approach; final parameters re-estimated on the total set.

## Model and parameters
Bendamustine: three compartments, zero-order input, first-order elimination; IIV (exponential) on CL, V1, V2, V3; proportional residual error. No covariate retained (sex, age, race, weight, BSA, CrCL by Peck's IBW-modified Cockcroft-Gault, ALT, AST, bilirubin, albumin tested; forward alpha 0.05, backward 0.001). Fig. 1 pairs Q1 with peripheral 1 (V2 in Table 2) and Q2 with peripheral 2 (V3).

Other results (pp. 6-7 / 1044-1045):
- Predicted concentration 12 h after the first dose was 0.27 ng/mL; mean C12:Cmax ratio was 1:25,000, so no accumulation is expected.
- Median bendamustine AUC was 13,635 ng x h/mL and median Cmax 5,839 ng/mL. These are post hoc individual estimates; the AUC window is not defined, see open question.
- Prediction error: %IPE 6.5% and |%IPE| 18.2%.
- M3: t½α 0.41 h and t½β 2.80 h. M4: t½ 0.62 h. Median AUC was 1,252 ng h/mL for M3 and 115 for M4, about 1/10 and 1/100 of the parent's.
- Exposure did not differ notably by sex, age or race. Across BSA quartiles, median AUC and Cmax differed by up to about 30%. Exposure was similar with normal (N = 52) and mild (N = 26) hepatic dysfunction, and with normal (47), mild (23) and moderate (8) renal impairment.
- Comparators:
  - The paper calls its CL similar to earlier non-compartmental values (about 27 L/h/1.73 m2) in CLL and solid tumours (refs 21, 22).
  - It calls its overall Vd (about 40 L) larger than those studies' (about 20 L), and attributes the gap to their shorter post-dose sampling.
  - Both comparisons are against those earlier studies, not against a label.

Derived (this card, from Table 2 micro-constants): half-lives 0.290, 0.681, 110.4 h. Vss 40.22 L. For 120 mg/m2 at 2.0 m2 over 1 h, the base basis (× 0.9076) gives Cmax 6044 ng/mL and single-dose AUC 6872 ng·h/mL. The hydrochloride basis gives Cmax 6660 ng/mL and AUC 7571 ng·h/mL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, effect site or concentration-effect model; not an opioid (no MEAC/potency ratio).
- Relative cytotoxic potency is taken as 1 for M3 and 1/5 for M4, relative to bendamustine (ref. 8, Teichert 2007; p. 2 / 1040). This was used to weight the composite exposures.
- Efficacy: 68 of 80 patients (85%) responded. No exposure measure predicted responder status, duration of response or PFS (Kaplan-Meier on cycle-1 AUC above or below the median, P = 0.3025).
- Nausea, logistic model (Fig. 6, PDF p. 9 / 1047; rendered):
  - Cmax was the only significant predictor (P = 0.013). P(nausea) = e^[1.1329 + 0.042·((CMAX − 5878.2)/100)] / (1 + e^[...]), with CMAX in ng/mL.
  - Odds ratio 1.00043 per ng/mL (95% CI 1.00000-1.00088).
  - Predicted probability 0.68 at a Cmax of 5,001.20 ng/mL (25th percentile) and 0.81 at 6,567.20 (75th percentile).
  - Hosmer-Lemeshow P = 0.1115; ROC area 0.63.
  - 64 of 80 patients received antiemetics, so their influence could not be tested.
- Vomiting: P = e^(−1.2435 + 1.4304·SEX − 0.4025·(prior treatments − 3)) / (1 + e^(...)), with SEX = 1 for female. No exposure measure was significant.
- Neutropenia and fatigue showed no exposure relationship. Mean (SD) AUC was 13,547 (5,261) ng x h/mL with grade 3/4 neutropenia and 12,459 (3,654) without.
- Ex vivo reference (ref. 13, cited in Discussion): the median lethal concentration in CLL cells was 4.3-7.4 µg/mL. The paper says 120 mg/m2 gave a Cmax of about 6 µg/mL. This is not a therapeutic range.

## Covariates
None retained in the PK model (list above). Sex and number of prior treatments enter only the vomiting logistic model.

## Caveats
- Patients with indolent NHL, sparse sampling (12 full profiles); 22 of 100 sampled patients excluded for sample stability.
- The salt or base basis of the dose is not stated.
- The narrow exposure range (one BSA-based dose level) limits the exposure-response analysis, as the authors note.
- No moderate or severe hepatic, or severe renal, impairment in the PK data.
- The terminal γ phase (110 h) rests on concentrations near the LLOQ (Fig. 2 plateau about 0.2 ng/mL).
- The author affiliations in the PDF include Cephalon, which funded the analysis.
