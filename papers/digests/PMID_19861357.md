# Masui 2010: propofol PK models compared on four validation datasets

## Study
Simulation/validation study. Three compartmental propofol models (Marsh, Schnider, Schüttler) and one physiologically based recirculatory model (Upton and Ludbrook 2005, modified for weight and age) were used to predict measured arterial propofol plasma concentrations from four published datasets, none used to build any of the models (p.2-3, printed 370).

Datasets (Table 2, PDF p.4 / printed 371):
- Bolus, n=10 (Struys 2007): 2.5 mg/kg within 10 s, arterial samples 0.5-5 min.
- Short infusion, n=50 (Masui 2009): 1.2 mg/kg at 10, 20, 40, 80 or 160 mg/kg/h (27-432 s), 5-s sampling for the first minute then to 9 min.
- TCI, n=30 (Coetzee 1995): Marsh, Dyck or Tackley TCI up to 206 min, or Schnider-model TCI up to 245 min (see Table 2 footnotes); no samples in the first 10 min or for 2 min after a target change.
- Long infusion, n=18 (Doufas 2004): slow-rate infusion up to 245 min, with frequent samples after changes.
All: forearm vein infusion, radial artery sampling. Raw data at openTCI.org. Simulations were individual (each subject's dose and demographics), NONMEM VI for the compartmental models, MATLAB/SIMULINK for Upton. Predicted central-compartment (or Cart) concentration compared with measured. Statistics: MDPE, MDAPE (Varvel), median log(measured/predicted) as bias, median |log| as accuracy; Steel-Dwass tests.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamics. No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, EC50 or hysteresis reported; the end point is plasma (arterial) concentration only. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Marsh: weight only. Schnider: age (V2, Cl2), weight, height and LBM (Cl1). Schüttler: age, weight, sex (per authors' description), plus bolus/infusion and venous/arterial sample indicators in the equations. Upton (as modified here): weight and age through cardiac output and a size factor Wt/70.

## Caveats
- Authors: the TCI dataset has no samples in the first 10 min or in the 2 min after target changes, so its good MDPE/MDAPE may overstate performance; conclusions should rest more on the other sets (printed p.377).
- Upton model: lag time was fitted on central administration but data are from a forearm vein; initial predicted Cp of 0 produced a division by zero (set to a very high number); bolus n=10 only.
- Schüttler's bolus/infusion switches limit clinical use (authors).
- Healthy or surgical adults; datasets from the same group's colleagues. Bias is "measured minus predicted" so positive MDPE = underprediction by the model.
- Authors conclude Schnider is the recommended model for TCI and advisory displays, "although still not perfect".
