# de la Pena 2014: PK/PD model of high-dose U-500 regular insulin

## Study
Eli Lilly population PK/PD modelling and simulation of concentrated human regular
U-500 insulin (U-500R; Humulin R U-500) and human regular U-100 (U-100R). Model
built from 3 single-dose crossover euglycaemic clamp studies (Methods, PDF
p2/printed 822): (1) healthy obese without diabetes — 50 U (0.4-0.6 U/kg, N=22)
and 100 U (0.8-1.3 U/kg, N=24); (2) healthy normal-weight without diabetes —
0.05-0.4 U/kg, N=18 (data on file); (3) normal-weight type 1 diabetes — 12 U
(0.1-0.2 U/kg, N=30, data on file). Intensive PK sampling; serum insulin by
validated radioimmunoassay; glucose infusion rate (GIR) at clamp as the PD effect.
Sequential model (PK fixed, then PD), built from Landersdorfer & Jusko 2010,
NONMEM VII, FOCE-with-interaction, VPC (200 replicates). PD data averaged every
15 min. Combined PK appeared linear over 0.05-1.3 U/kg; PD was nonlinear (Emax).

## Model and parameters
PK (Figure 1, PDF p2): 1-compartment, first-order absorption and elimination,
with **separate absorption rate constants for U-500R (Ka2) and U-100R (Ka1)** and
elimination K20; proportional error. PD (Figure 1): effect-compartment model,
GIR = E0 + Emax·Ce/(EC50 + Ce), parameterized by Ce, baseline GIR E0, Emax, and
EC50 (concentration for 50% of Emax); the effect compartment (K23/K32) accounts for
the PK-to-GIR delay. **No numeric parameter table is reported** — V/F, CL/F, Ka1,
Ka2, Emax, EC50 and their variances are not printed anywhere in the paper; only
goodness-of-fit (Fig 2) and simulation profiles (Figs 3-6) are shown.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is glucose infusion rate (GIR) at euglycaemic clamp — an insulin-action
measure, not analgesia. Not an opioid; no MEAC, no analgesic concentration, no
EEG/pupil/ventilation end point. Hysteresis handled by an effect compartment
(K23/K32). No numeric ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, Emax or EC50 value is given. Reported
qualitatively: onset of action within 11-16 min, similar for both formulations
(from the earlier single-dose study, ref 7); U-500R has longer time-to-peak
concentration and time-to-peak effect and longer duration than U-100R; for single
doses, median Cmax rose ~5-fold across 165-750 U while the maximum effect (max GIR,
Rmax) rose only ~1.5-fold (Emax nonlinearity, p3/printed 823). Simulated
steady-state reached ~24 h after the first dose; for QD dosing, peak concentration
~5 h and peak effect ~7 h after a 7 am dose. No equianalgesic/potency ratio
(not applicable to insulin).

## Covariates
Dose: significant covariate on absorption rate constant (Ka) and apparent clearance
(CL/F). Weight: significant on apparent volume (Vd/F). BMI: significant on Emax.
Age and sex were tested and not retained (Figure 1 legend / Results, PDF p2-3).

## Caveats
Pooled healthy-obese, healthy normal-weight, and type 1 diabetes data stand in for
the insulin-resistant high-dose target population; simulations extrapolate to doses
up to 7.5-fold above the 100 U data, assuming PK stays linear beyond 1.3 U/kg and
PD stays Emax. Single-dose data only (no steady-state observations); no
interoccasion variability or insulin-receptor saturation; GIR-to-blood-glucose link
not established; EC50 assumed possibly higher in type 2 diabetes. Lilly-funded;
several authors Lilly employees. No parameter estimates published.

## Verification against stanpumpR
Not applicable as a cited source; treated as an independent check of the library's
U-500 model (Tham 2017):
- `R/drugs_insulinRegularU500.R` / `R/insulinTham.R`: library U-500R is a
  1-compartment, first-order absorption/elimination model with V/F 178 L, CL/F 127
  L/h, ka 0.185/h (U-500) vs 0.67/h (U-100), weight on V/F ((BW/80)^1.62) and dose
  on CL/F ((Dose/10)^-0.282) (confirmed in the R source and
  `inst/extdata/antidiabeticParameters.csv` lines 25-29). **Structurally consistent**
  with de la Pena: both are 1-compartment, first-order, with a slower absorption
  rate constant for U-500R than U-100R (de la Pena's Ka2<Ka1; Tham 0.185<0.67) and
  both make dose a covariate on clearance and on absorption. **Direction matches**:
  de la Pena reports U-500R has a longer time-to-peak than U-100R, which the
  library's smaller ka reproduces. **Cannot verify numerically**: de la Pena
  publishes no parameter values, so no value-for-value comparison of V/F, CL/F, ka,
  Emax or EC50 is possible. de la Pena models the PD (GIR via an Emax effect
  compartment); the library deliberately plots concentration only (no GIR/glucose),
  so there is no PD element to compare.
