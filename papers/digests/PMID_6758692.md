# Jacobs 1982: beta-lactam MICs and MBCs for group B streptococci

## Study
In-vitro susceptibility note (printed pp 897-900, PDF pp 1-4). 100 clinical group B streptococcus (S. agalactiae)
isolates: 64 from blood or CSF of paediatric patients (Buffalo, Cleveland, Pittsburgh) and 36 from adult carriers
(Cleveland); identity confirmed by Lancefield grouping (coagglutination). 16 beta-lactams.
- MIC: agar incorporation, Mueller-Hinton agar + 5% sheep blood, doubling dilutions 0.008-256 ug/mL; inoculum
  about 10^4 organisms per spot (Steers replicator); 35 C overnight, room air. MIC = lowest concentration
  completely inhibiting growth (p 897).
- MBC: broth macrodilution, 1-mL Mueller-Hinton broth; MBC = lowest concentration killing at least 99.9% of the
  inoculum after overnight incubation. Penicillin G MBC in 20 strains; all other agents in 5 strains (Table 1 footnote).
- Penicillin-tolerance screen of all 100 strains with a single penicillin G concentration (0.2 ug/mL nominal,
  assayed 0.21 ug/mL) (pp 897-898). Tolerance was screened for **penicillin only**, not for the cephalosporins.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points are in-vitro inhibition (MIC, agar, ~10^4 CFU/spot) and 99.9% kill (MBC, broth). Not an opioid paper:
no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio. Authors note (p 899) that CSF bacterial counts can reach far higher inocula, at which
MICs rise.

## Covariates
None. Isolate source (patient vs carrier) made no difference; all MICs within 1-2 doubling dilutions (p 898).

## Caveats
MBC in only 5 strains per agent (20 for penicillin); reported as a value or range, not as MBC50/MBC90. Standard
inoculum only. Tolerance screened for penicillin, not cephalothin. 1982 isolates, US only.
