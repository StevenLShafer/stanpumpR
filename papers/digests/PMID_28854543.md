# Colin 2017: dexmedetomidine haemodynamic PKPD (MAP and heart rate)

## Study
18 healthy volunteers (stratified 18-34, 35-54, 55-72 y; 3 M and 3 F per stratum), each studied on two occasions. Dexmedetomidine i.v. by target-controlled infusion using the Dyck model: a bolus phase, a 10 min recovery, then stepwise-increasing targets (Fig 1 shows 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 ng/mL steps); infusion rate limited to 6 or 10 mcg/kg/h (p. 219). Continuous arterial MAP (arterial cannula) and ECG-derived HR, 1 Hz, median-filtered (60 s span) and thinned to about 1 per min: median 458 MAP and 394 HR points per subject per session. PK was NOT re-estimated: the individual Bayesian PK parameters (V1, V2, V3, CL, Q2, Q3) from Hannivoort 2015 (the companion PK model, ref 9) were fixed per subject and session. NONMEM 7.3 FOCE-I; exponential IIV and IOV. Companion paper (ref 10, PMID 28854538) covers BIS/MOAA/S.

## Model and parameters
MAP: double-sigmoid Emax with two effect compartments (Eq 2): hypotensive term Emax fixed to 1 and Ce/(EC50,Hypo+Ce); hypertensive term Emax,Hyper with EC50,Hyper = EC50,Hypo + DEC50 (so the hypertensive EC50 is always the larger). Age on baseline: BaseMAP,i = BaseMAP x exp(theta_age x (Age-20)) (Eq 3). HR: Schoemaker-type nonlinear model (Eq 1 form) with Emax = BaseHR,i - HRmin (Eq 5), one effect compartment, additive error whose SD falls exponentially with the fall in HR (Eq 4).

Simulated end points (Table 2, printed p. 216, text layer; 27-yr-old, 77 kg): after 2 mcg/kg over 5 min, Cmax 8.5 ng/mL, HR nadir -27%, MAP max +5.7% at 7 min (text: +7%), MAP min -27% at 102 min, MAP back within 5% of baseline at 10.4 h. After 4 mcg/kg/5 min: Cmax 17 ng/mL, HR -38%, MAP +19%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Effect-site models on MAP and HR (the end points are haemodynamic, not sedation). ke0 and t1/2ke0: MAP hypotensive 0.0529/min (t1/2 13 min); MAP hypertensive 0.0902/min (t1/2 7.7 min); HR 0.396/min (t1/2 1.75 min), all Table 1. No tPeak is reported.
- EC50: hypotensive 0.364 ng/mL; hypertensive about 1.56 ng/mL (derived). Plasma concentration at which MAP returns to baseline: about 2.4 ng/mL (Ce, typical 20-yr-old; p. 213). Max MAP fall -27% (hypotension plateaus at 25-27% below baseline in simulations); max rise +43%. HR minimum 22.5 beats/min (population, below the observed volunteer minimum of about 40).
- Plasma concentrations are those predicted by the Hannivoort 2015 model (total dexmedetomidine, plasma); assay details are in that paper, not here.
- Sedation link (Fig 4, p. 217): a 10 to 20% HR fall corresponds to BIS 60 to 40 and at least 80% probability of loss of MOAA/S 3 (typical subject); MAP normalisation after the hypotensive phase is a similar marker.
- No MEAC, analgesic concentration or opioid-equivalence ratio (not an opioid paper).
- Comparison the authors make: Potts 2010 (children after cardiac surgery) ke0 for the sympatholytic effect 0.072/min (t1/2 9.65 min), EC50 0.10 (hypo) and 1.1 (hyper) ng/mL.

## Covariates
Age on baseline MAP only (Eq 3, delta OFV -9.6). Weight, height, BMI, sex, session: none significant on EC50 or DEC50 (MAP) or on S0 (HR). No covariates on baseline HR.

## Caveats
Healthy volunteers, awake; the authors note that sympatholysis is attenuated under general anaesthesia (Talke 2003), so applicability to anaesthetised patients is unknown. PK fixed from a separate model. Simulations beyond infusion rates of 6 or 10 mcg/kg/h are not validated; the early hypotension before hypertension at high rates may be a model artefact. The model under-predicts the hypertension after 2 mcg/kg doses in Bloor 1992 and Dyck 1993 (7 and 8% predicted against 22 and 24% observed) and over-predicts it at steady state against Snapir and Ebert (15 and 23% against 10 and 12%). Volunteers mostly did not return to baseline in 5 h of recovery; the 10.4 h recovery is extrapolated. RUV in MAP was larger in recovery (unrestrained volunteers).
