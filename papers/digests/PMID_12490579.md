# Visser 2003: EEG effects of nine GABA-A modulators in rats, with in vitro-in vivo correlation

A mechanism-based PK/PD model separating **receptor activation** from the **stimulus-response**
relationship, applied to nine non-steroidal GABA-A modulators in rats, and used to ask whether in
vivo potency and efficacy can be predicted from in vitro receptor measurements.

stanpumpR cites it once, in prose, for a methodological argument about **unbound** potency ratios —
not for any parameter. See *Used by stanpumpR*.

entries having been filed normally.

## Study
Rats, with bipolar EEG leads on the left hemisphere (Fl-Cl), given one of **nine prototypical
GABA-A receptor modulators** while plasma concentrations were measured alongside the EEG. The nine
are **six benzodiazepines** (bretazenil, clobazam, diazepam, flunitrazepam, midazolam, oxazepam),
**one imidazopyridine** (zolpidem), **one cyclopyrrolone** (zopiclone) and **one beta-carboline**
(DMCM, methyl 6,7-dimethoxy-4-ethyl-beta-carboline-3-carboxylate).

Population PK/PD modelling gave monophasic concentration-EEG relationships with large differences
in potency (EC50) and in intrinsic activity between compounds. The data were then analysed with the
mechanism-based model previously developed for neuroactive steroids, which splits the in vivo
response into an apparent in vivo receptor affinity (K_PD) and an in vivo intrinsic efficacy
(e_PD), so that the two can be compared separately against in vitro measurements.

## Model and parameters
The structural idea: the concentration-EEG relationship is decomposed into receptor activation,
characterised by an in vivo affinity `K_PD`, and a stimulus-response step, characterised by an in
vivo intrinsic efficacy `e_PD`. `e_PD` is an **absolute** intrinsic efficacy rather than one
relative to a reference agonist, which the authors note as a feature of the approach.

The two correlations are the paper's result: in vivo affinity tracks in vitro affinity, and in vivo
intrinsic efficacy tracks the in vitro GABA shift. Both are computed on **unbound** plasma
concentrations; the paper notes that for DMCM no free fraction could be determined.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
The end point is the **EEG** — amplitude in a defined frequency band, the standard
pharmacodynamic measure in this group's rat work. There is no behavioural score, no sedation scale
and no clinical end point of any kind, and nothing here is measured in humans. Potency is expressed
as EC50 and as the derived K_PD; efficacy as intrinsic activity and the derived e_PD. Flumazenil is
discussed as having no intrinsic efficacy of its own, which is what makes it useful for estimating
the others'.

## Covariates
None. One species, one strain, one route per compound; no body-size, age or sex covariate is
fitted, and the paper does not set out to find one.

## Caveats
- **Rats, and the EEG.** Nothing in this paper is a human measurement, and its end point is an EEG
  signal rather than sedation, amnesia or any clinical effect. It cannot supply a human therapeutic
  range, and stanpumpR does not use it for one.
- **No pharmacokinetic parameter for any library drug.** Diazepam and midazolam appear among the
  nine, but their rat K_PD and e_PD are properties of this model in this species; the library's
  diazepam and midazolam models come from elsewhere.
- **DMCM has no free fraction**, so one of the nine is missing the unbound correction that the rest
  of the affinity correlation rests on.
- **Correlations across nine compounds.** r = 0.93 and r = 0.95 are strong, but over nine points,
  and the paper's claim is about the class rather than about any one drug's prediction.
- Tables and figures were read from the text layer. No table value is pinned in this card beyond
  what the abstract and Results prose also state.
