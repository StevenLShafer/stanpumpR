# Wulkersdorfer 2021: clindamycin protein binding by intravascular microdialysis

## Study
- Six healthy male volunteers, age 33.3 ± 10.9 y, BMI 25.5 ± 1.4 kg/m2 (weight not given).
- Clindamycin phosphate (Dalacin C) 900 mg single IV infusion over 30 min.
- Total plasma: venous samples at 0, 0.5 (end of infusion), 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 h. Unbound: in vitro ultrafiltration (UF, Vivafree 30 kD, 37 °C, pH 7.4 buffered) of the same plasma, and in vivo intravascular microdialysis (MD), two forearm-vein probes per subject, 30 mm, 20 kDa cut-off, 2 µL/min, intervals 0-0.5, 0.5-1, then hourly to 8 h; recovery by retrodialysis (mean 36.9 ± 8.3%).
- Assay: HPLC-UV; LOQ 0.1 mg/L plasma, 0.03 mg/L saline/dialysate. fu of QCs 16.0 ± 2.7% (8 mg/L) and 8.77 ± 0.81% (1 mg/L).
- NCA (WinNonlin 8) and NONMEM 7.4.3 / PsN; uncertainty by LLP-SIR. 178 samples (36 plasma, 35 UF, 107 MD).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment linear PK (better than one-compartment, dOFV -29.8) with a saturable binding model in molar terms (Eq. 1): Ctotal = Cu + NP·CP·Cu/(KD + Cu); Eq. 2 solves for Cu. Constant-fu and linear fu models were inferior (dOFV -85.2, -7.2). CP (AAG concentration) is **not reported**; KD is reported in mg/L. NCA (Table 1, mean ± SD): Cmax total 14.95 ± 2.46, UF 3.39 ± 0.75, MD 2.32 ± 0.85 mg/L; AUC0-8 41.78 ± 11.22, 5.80 ± 1.54, 6.14 ± 2.82 mg·h/L; total CL 17.28 ± 5.66 L/h, Vd 72.18 ± 10.18 L (column alignment garbled in the text layer). AUCunbound/AUCtotal 13.9 ± 1.8% (UF) and 14.7 ± 3.1% (MD) (p 2109). Maximum fu about 16% in vitro, 26% in vivo; fu rose linearly with total concentration (Figure 2). Model-predicted unbound at 0.5 h: 4.31 (MD) vs 3.27 mg/L (UF); at 6 h 0.30 vs 0.30 mg/L.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD. Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0 or potency. The paper frames binding as relevant because only unbound drug is active; no MIC target analysis.

## Covariates
None explored (n = 6). AAG concentration not measured/reported.

## Caveats
n = 6 healthy young men; single dose; probe-to-probe variability in distribution phase; MD interval averaging blunts early peaks; Bmax (NP·CP) cannot be computed without CP, which is not given; AAG is an acute-phase protein, so patients (surgery, infection) will bind more; binding parameters in healthy volunteers only.
