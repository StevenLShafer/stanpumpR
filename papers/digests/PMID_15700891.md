# Kolterman 2005: single-dose exenatide PK/PD in type 2 diabetes

## Study
Two randomized, single-blind, placebo-controlled single-dose studies, sponsor-run (Amylin; all authors employees).
Oral antidiabetic agents stopped 14 days before. Subcutaneous injection (anterolateral thigh).
- **Study A**: 8 men, 35-65 y, HbA1c 8-10%; rising doses placebo, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4 µg/kg (or placebo) on days
  1, 3, 5, 7, 9, given with a 7 kcal/kg liquid meal and acetaminophen 20 mg/kg (Table 1). Exenatide sampled to 15 h.
  7 of 8 completed (one withdrew after a seizure judged probably unrelated).
- **Study B**: part 1, 6 patients, 0.01 and 0.1 µg/kg or placebo on consecutive days, glucose only to 5 h; part 2,
  8 patients, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.1 µg/kg or placebo, 15 min before meal + acetaminophen; exenatide sampled to 5 h.
- Demographics (Table 2, PDF p. 4, confirmed on the rendered page): weight 88.5 ± 9.4 kg (A), 86.3 ± 15.4 (B1),
  88.8 ± 12.1 (B2); age 53 ± 10, 56 ± 5, 52 ± 8 y; BMI 29.5, 32.5, 28.6 kg/m²; HbA1c 9.5, 8.4, 7.6%.
- Assay: plasma exenatide by Amylin immunoenzymetric assay (ref. 23); acetaminophen by HPLC.
- Analysis: noncompartmental AUC0-t (log-transformed), ANOVA / mixed-effect models in SAS. No compartmental model.

mean tmax 2.1-2.2 h, neither dose dependent. The authors describe PK as dose proportional. Detectable 10-15 min
post dose (the earliest samples). Derived: at B2's mean 88.8 kg, the three doses are about 1.8, 4.4 and 8.9 µg, and
Cmax per µg is about 25, 25 and 21 pg/mL; AUC0-5h is 156, 387 and 690 pg·h/mL (derived: /60). These use the group
mean weight, not individual doses.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid; no ke0 or concentration-effect model. End points: postprandial plasma glucose (study B2 baseline-adjusted
AUC0-5h 1072, 918, 828 mg·h/dL at 0.02, 0.05, 0.1 µg/kg vs 1150 placebo; Cmax 285, 245, 230 vs 300 mg/dL), glucagon
(study A AUC0-3h 218, 186, 209, 163 ng·h/L at 0.1-0.4 µg/kg vs 331 placebo), insulin (Fig. 3A), and gastric emptying
by acetaminophen (AUC0-120min 907 and 645 µg·min/mL at 0.05 and 0.1 µg/kg vs 1288 placebo; Cmax 13.8, 11.4, 7.7 vs
15.6 µg/mL) (PDF pp. 4-5). 0.01 µg/kg did not differ from placebo; 0.02 µg/kg is called the minimally effective dose
for postprandial glucose. Nausea rose with dose above 0.2 µg/kg (60% at 0.4 µg/kg).

## Covariates
None examined.

## Caveats
Very small n (8, 6, 8), sponsor authors, weight-based doses unlike the fixed 5/10 µg clinical doses, sampling
truncated at 5 h in study B (AUC0-5h is not AUC0-inf, so CL/F cannot be derived), geometric means without
dispersion in the text, study A Cmax only in a figure. PD numbers above are from the text layer only (not used by
stanpumpR).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `R/drugs_exenatide.R:70` "citing Kolterman 2005": **discrepancy (attribution).** Kolterman 2005 has no 2.5 or 5 µg
  arm; its doses were 0.01-0.4 µg/kg, and the four numbers 56, 85, 159, 340 appear nowhere in it. Its nearest arms
  (0.02 µg/kg ≈ 1.8 µg and 0.05 µg/kg ≈ 4.4 µg at 88.8 kg, derived) gave Cmax 45 and 109 pg/mL and AUC0-5h 156 and
  387 pg·h/mL (derived), which are not those values. The digest of Knop 2017 (`digests/PMID_28085521.md`) already
  found Knop cites McCormack 2014, not Kolterman, for them. The "peak at about 2 h" does agree with Kolterman's
  2.1-2.2 h (study B) and 2-3 h (study A). See open question.
- `inst/help/drugs/exenatide.md:25` "citing an early study": the values cannot be traced to this paper; if "early
  study" means Kolterman 2005, the same attribution problem applies. **cannot verify** from this paper.
- Independent check against the library's Cirincione 2017 model (`R/drugs_exenatide.R:59-67`, which does not cite
  Kolterman): the model's 10 µg peak at 75 kg is 263 pg/mL (26.3 pg/mL per µg) and 22% lower at 100 kg (repo header);
  Kolterman's 0.1 µg/kg arm (~8.9 µg at 88.8 kg) gave a geometric mean 187 pg/mL (~21 pg/mL per µg, derived), which
  lies near the model's figure for that weight band. Kolterman's tmax 2.1-2.2 h is later than the model's 1.35 h
  peak, which the repo header already notes ("observed peaks were at about 2 h"). Kolterman's exposure is thus closer
  to the model than to the 56/85 pg/mL figures the header attributes to it, which weakens the header's claim that
  the model gives "about twice the exposure" of this early study; nothing in the model itself depends on it.
