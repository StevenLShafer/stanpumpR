# Ming 2014: the 100% oxygen anatomic shunt test, audit of 80 patients

## Study
Retrospective audit, Royal Brompton Hospital, London, April 1996 to November 2012: 189 patients had the test, 81 sets of notes available, 80 analysed (51% men, mean age 50.2 y, 24% inpatients; one excluded for a known shunt). Indication: disproportionate hypoxia (61%), breathlessness (21%). Reference ("anatomic shunt") was positive contrast echocardiography (grade I or above) and/or a catheter study showing a cardiac or pulmonary shunt; 15 patients (18.8%) had one. Thirteen low-probability patients had no further tests and were left out of the shunt analysis (all had calculated shunt below 8.3%). ROC analysis n=67.

Test: room-air radial arterial blood gas for PaCO2, then 20 min or longer breathing 100% O2 from a 200 L Douglas bag, seated with nose clip, end-tidal O2 by mass spectrometer to confirm washout, then a second arterial gas. Shunt (% of cardiac output) = (ideal PaO2 - actual PaO2) / 2.66, with ideal PaO2 computed from barometric pressure, FiO2 and PaCO2 (equation garbled in text layer; PaO2 in kPa). Maximum measurable shunt is about 30% because the relation is non-linear below PaO2 13.33 kPa (p. 1).

Normal shunt fraction is stated as 5% of cardiac output, and a shunt above 5% is said to indicate a high likelihood of an anatomical shunt (p. 1, introduction, citing Berggren 1942); this is background, not a finding of the audit. No shunt difference with or without underlying lung disease (p=0.66).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not applicable (no drug). End point: contrast echo / catheter-defined anatomical shunt. After transcatheter embolisation the O2 test normalised in 73% while echo stayed positive in 90% (p. 2).

## Covariates
Age, sex and resting air PaO2 did not differ between shunt-positive and shunt-negative patients (p. 2).

## Caveats
Retrospective, single tertiary centre, referral population mostly with unexplained hypoxia, no gold standard, assumed cardiac output, Hb and VO2 (pp. 2-3); inhaling 100% O2 may cause alveolar collapse. Awake spontaneously breathing, not anaesthetised.
