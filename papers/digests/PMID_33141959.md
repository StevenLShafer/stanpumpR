# Foo 2021: intravenous lidocaine consensus statement (efficacy and safety)

## Study
Consensus statement / narrative review by a UK-led multidisciplinary working party, prompted by a patient death
after postoperative i.v. lidocaine in an English hospital (p. 239). Not a PK study: no new patients, samples or
model. It reviews the meta-analyses of efficacy (Weibel 2018 Cochrane, 68 trials, 4525 patients; earlier reviews),
the literature on plasma concentration and toxicity, and off-label prescribing, and gives 15 recommendations.
Endorsed with caveats by RA-UK and the Faculty of Pain Medicine (p. 247). Supporting Information (Appendices
S1-S3: pain score analysis, an example SOP, nurse education material) is not in this file.

Efficacy (pp. 240-241, Weibel 2018 [17]): pain at 0-4 h SMD -0.50 (95%CI -0.72 to -0.28; 29 studies, 1656
patients), about 0.37-2.48 cm on a 10-cm VAS; no clinically relevant effect at 24 or 48 h; opioid consumption
-4.52 (-6.25 to -2.79) mg morphine equivalents (40 studies, 2201 patients); ileus RR 0.37 (0.15-0.87); time to
first bowel movement reduced by 7.92 h; postoperative nausea RR 0.78 (0.67-0.91). Evidence quality mostly very low.

Dosing recommendations (pp. 238-239, 245-246):
- Loading dose no more than 1.5 mg/kg (ideal body weight) as an infusion over 10 min.
- Then no more than 1.5 mg/kg/h, for no longer than 24 h; if extended beyond 24 h, reduce the rate to 50%.
- Absolute cap 120 mg/h for any patient; not for patients under 40 kg.
- Ideal body weight = height (cm) - 100 for men, height (cm) - 105 for women (p. 245, rec. 5).
- Not within 4 h of a nerve/fascial plane block or port-site infiltration; no block until 4 h after stopping;
  wound/epidural catheter infusions (no boluses) may start 30 min after stopping; remove 5% lidocaine plasters.
- 20% lipid emulsion available; on an adverse event, take blood in EDTA and lithium heparin tubes for levels.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, tPeak, EC50 or MEAC. No analgesic concentration is proposed; efficacy is judged on pain scores and
  recovery outcomes, not concentrations.
- Toxicity: Figure 2 (p. 245, reproduced from Lin et al. [97]) is a textbook diagram of plasma concentration vs
  effects: perioral tingling, light-headedness, tinnitus and slurred speech below about 5-8 mcg/mL; muscle
  twitching, loss of consciousness and convulsions around 10; coma and myocardial depression about 15;
  respiratory arrest and arrhythmias about 20; ventricular arrest about 25 (read from the figure; approximate).
  The authors say the concentration-symptom relationship is not reliable and the figure is a general guide only.
- Neurological signs come first; cardiovascular/ECG signs are late (p. 246, rec. 13).
- Not an opioid paper; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Qualitative only (p. 242): volume of distribution and clearance reduced in heart failure; clearance tracks cardiac
index (hepatic blood flow); reduced in liver failure, similar in renal failure (metabolite accumulation possible);
MEGX has pro-convulsant properties similar to lidocaine; acidaemia, hypoxaemia and hypoalbuminaemia raise risk;
beta-blockers and amiodarone reduce metabolism/clearance; low body weight and high BMI (actual-weight dosing) both
raise risk. No equations.

## Caveats
- A consensus/narrative review; most toxicity data are old and small.
- Printed p. 241 says modern regimens are "typically 12 mg.kg-1.h-1"; the page image shows the same. Given the
  sentence calls 30 mg/kg/h 10-20 times higher, this is presumably a lost en dash (1-2 mg/kg/h); not used by the
  repo.
- Koppert's SD (3.1) exceeds its mean (1.9) in Table 1, as printed.
- Matrix (arterial/venous) and weight basis of the Table 1 studies are not given.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `therapeuticRanges.csv:210`, "Foo 2021 ... consensus abstract gives no concentration": the abstract (p. 238)
  indeed gives doses only (1.5 mg/kg over 10 min, 1.5 mg/kg/h, 24 h, 4 h block interval). **matches**. The full
  text, read here, does give concentrations (Table 1; > 5 mcg/mL toxic definition; < 5 at the recommended
  regimen), so the `verification` column's "abstract only" can be upgraded and the note revisited.
- The band 0.5-1.5 mcg/mL (typical 1; also `R/drugs_lidocaine.R:17-19`) is not attributed to Foo, and Foo does
  not support it: its Table 1 means at the recommended 1.5 mg/kg/h are 1.1-2.5 mcg/mL (n-weighted 1.94, derived).
  **cannot verify** against this paper; see open question.
- Independent model check (Foo is not the model source): Schnider 1996 set C at the 70 kg reference gives
  1.28 / 1.18 / 1.16 mcg/mL at 1 / 2 / 4 h on Foo's regimen (derived; Css 1.16 = 1.75 mg/min / 1.505 L/min), lower
  than Table 1's perioperative means, and a 22 min terminal half-life against the ~100 min Foo quotes from
  Rowland. The comparator for Table 1 is surgical patients in 11 trials (weight basis unstated), not the
  library's volunteer-derived model. The header's own note (lines 69-70) that clearance 1.5 L/min and Vss 39 L
  cannot reproduce the long tail predicts this direction.
- Toxic thresholds: Foo's > 5 mcg/mL (McCarthy's definition) and ~9-10 (Gianelly) are consistent with the label
  threshold the repo uses (CNS reactions above 6.0 mcg/mL).
- `R/drugs_lidocaine.R` uses total body weight scaled by fat-free mass; Foo recommends ideal-body-weight dosing.
  That is a dosing rule, not a PK claim, so no discrepancy.
