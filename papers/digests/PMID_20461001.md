# Olofsen 2010: remifentanil non-steady-state respiratory model

## Study
Ten healthy men, 18-30 yr, BMI < 28 kg/m2 (PDF p3). Each had three remifentanil TCI runs 120 min apart: two awake, one on a background propofol infusion targeting BIS 80 (average target plasma propofol about 1 mcg/mL). Remifentanil by custom TCI with Minto/Remifusor kinetics, staircase plasma targets (steps 1 ng/mL in seven subjects, one-minute steps of 6 and 9 ng/mL in two) with step durations 0.5-6 min; estimated plasma rates of rise 0.17-9.0 ng/mL/min, maximum targets 4-9 ng/mL (Table 1, p4). Arterial line; breath-by-breath inspired minute ventilation (pneumotachograph) and end-tidal PCO2 with 100% oxygen inhaled (hyperoxia); mask artificial ventilation given during apnea or desaturation below 90%. Remifentanil in arterial BLOOD (3-6 samples per subject in two of three runs) by LC-MS/MS with citric acid to stop esterases. NONMEM VII with MCMC Bayesian estimation; PK step then PD step with individual Bayesian PK.

## Model and parameters
PK: remifentanil three-compartment Minto 1997 structure with typical values and IIV fixed, individualized by age and lean body mass; only V2 differed significantly, a factor of 0.522 +/- 0.125 (95% CI 0.323-0.808) of Minto's value (p 5, Fig 4). Effect site by first-order equilibration t1/2ke0.

PD (Eq. 2-6, pp 4-6): ventilation V' = G x (PE - B), tau dV'/dt form with tau fixed at 2.5 min; remifentanil shifts the apneic threshold B linearly, B(C) = B0 x (1 + C/C100), leaving gain G unchanged (C100 = 6.14 +/- 0.77 ng/mL from the authors' earlier steady-state surface, Fig 1B, p 2); propofol reduces G. Combined: V'(C) = V'0 x (1 - C/(2 x C50)) + G x (PE - PE_0). Two-compartment CO2 kinetics (alveolar volume fixed at 3 L). Apnea occurs at a finite concentration, which is 2 x C50 for the drug-only term (stated as 3.2 ng/mL in the text, p 12, awake).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit (remifentanil):
- **ke0 / t1/2ke0**: t1/2ke0 0.53 min (SE 0.02, 95% CI 0.49-0.58), Table 2 (PDF p8); end point = inspired minute ventilation in a closed-loop (CO2 feedback intact), hyperoxic, non-steady-state model; propofol had no effect on it. Derived ke0 = ln2/0.53 = 1.31 min^-1. tPeak not reported. The authors compare it with 2 min (Babenco, isohypercapnic ventilation) and 1.6 min (EEG end points), and cite slower opioids (fentanyl about 5 min, buprenorphine about 1 h, morphine hours; from other work).
- **C50**: 1.6 ng/mL for 50% depression of ventilation, effect-site, defined at constant PCO2 (term 1 of Eq. 6); 1.3 ng/mL under propofol (Table 2). Population: 10 healthy young men, arterial-blood remifentanil assay (whole blood, LC-MS/MS). Compared by the authors with 1.4 ng/mL (Babenco) and 0.7 ng/mL (their earlier surface study, ventilation at fixed PCO2 55 Torr) (p 11-12). Apnea at about 2 x C50 (3.2 ng/mL cited, awake). This is a respiratory-depression C50 only.
- **MEAC / analgesic concentration**: not reported. **Equianalgesic or potency ratios**: none (opioid comparisons only qualitative on ke0).
- Other: propofol (about 1 mcg/mL) lowered G by about 54% (factor 0.46) and C50 by 16%; apnea occurred only in the propofol runs (8 of 10; mean 4.4 min, range 1-7 min); none awake. Simulation (Figs 7-8): slowing the rate of rise from 5 to 1 ng/mL/min deepens the ventilation nadir; apnea duration rose from 2 min at 2.5 to 9 min at 0.6 ng/mL/min and apnea disappeared at 0.31 ng/mL/min or less (16-min infusion) (p 9-12).

## Covariates
None in PD. PK individualized by age and lean body mass as in Minto; V2 scale factor 0.522. No sex effect studied (all men); the authors note possible sex dependence of the CO2 slope.

## Caveats
Men only, young, healthy; one propofol level; hyperoxia (G smaller than their earlier normoxic 1.6 L/min/Torr); the study was on the flat part of the CO2 response; tau and alveolar volume were fixed, not estimated; fast bolus-type input in only two subjects; end-tidal PCO2 unreliable near apnea (handled with an asymmetric residual error); the 100% O2 may have prolonged apnea and G may rise with hypoxia; simulations should not be extrapolated to other scenarios (p 12).
