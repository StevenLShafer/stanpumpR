# Reeves 2001: ketamine added to morphine PCA after abdominal surgery

## Study
Double-blind randomized trial, 76 patients recruited, 71 analysed (PDF p.1, printed 116): Group MK n = 36 (morphine 1 mg/mL plus ketamine 1 mg/mL in the PCA) and Group M n = 35 (morphine 1 mg/mL). 71 had surgery and received study drug per protocol (Results); the Results also state 7 withdrawals (4 for inadequate analgesia, 2 per group; 1 protocol violation) and 2 ICU admissions for respiratory depression (one per group). Elective major abdominal surgery with midline incision, stratified by upper/lower incision, 48 h study. No other analgesics or regional blocks allowed. PCA settings (bolus, lockout, background) left to the anaesthetist; not reported. Demographics (Table 1, p.2 printed 117, mean (SD)): age 54 (13) MK vs 47 (14) M; weight 84 (20) vs 76 (18) kg; sex M/F 20/16 vs 16/19; ASA I/II/III 7/24/4 vs 11/20/4. No concentrations measured.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Dose facts: ketamine 1 mg/mL equal to morphine 1 mg/mL in the PCA solution, so ketamine to morphine 1:1 by mass; authors give average ketamine use 3.2 mg/h over the first 24 h (p.4 printed 119; consistent with PCA use of 77 mg/24 h in Group MK, Table 2: 77/24 = 3.2, derived). PCA use, mg/24 h, mean (SD) (Table 2, printed 118): 0-24 h 77 (31) MK vs 71 (38) M; 24-48 h 50 (24) vs 56 (34); P = 0.82.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit (morphine arm):
- MEAC / analgesic concentration for morphine: none measured or reported.
- ke0 / t1/2 ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported for either drug.
- Equianalgesic ratio: none stated.
- Ketamine: the introduction cites an effective plasma concentration of 100 ng/mL producing analgesia in an ischemic forearm model (single-dose study, reference 4, Clements and Nimmo 1981; side effects not studied); this is a literature statement, not measured here (p.1 printed 116).

Primary end points, no difference between groups: subjective efficacy (1 excellent to 5 very poor) median (10th-90th percentile) 2.0 (1.0-3.1) MK vs 2.0 (1.0-4.0) M at 24 h, 2.0 (1.0-3.0) vs 2.0 (1.0-3.7) at 48 h, P = 0.80; VRS pain at rest P = 0.89 and on movement P = 0.92 (Figure 1, printed 118). Secondary (Table 2): trail-making B at 48 h 109 s (SD 7.9) MK vs 86 s (6.8) M, P = 0.037 (n = 18 vs 24); trail-making A 42 vs 39 s, P = 0.53; quality of recovery at 48 h 14.6 vs 13.5, P = 0.20; sedation, nausea and antiemetic use not different. Table 3 (PDF p.4, printed 119; MK vs M, risk ratio, P): vivid dreams at 24 h 21% vs 14%, 1.3 (0.62-2.6), P = 0.71; at 48 h 29% vs 13%, 1.8 (0.78-4.3), P = 0.13 (the abstract's relative risk of 1.8); hallucinations at 24 h 13% vs 18%, 0.82 (0.46-1.5), P = 0.73; at 48 h 33% vs 23%, 1.3 (0.73-2.4), P = 0.43. None significant.

## Covariates
Outcomes adjusted for age, gender, incision site and duration of surgery (and incision length for pain). No PK covariates.

## Caveats
Pain scores and subjective efficacy only; trail-making tests available in only 22 vs 25 (A) and 18 vs 24 (B) patients; missing scores filled by last observation carried forward; PCA settings not standardised. Authors conclude small-dose ketamine adds nothing to morphine PCA here, and note a prior study with about 1.2 mg/h ketamine did show benefit.
