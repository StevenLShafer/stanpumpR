# Okada 2008: CYP2C19, CYP3A5 and co-medication effects on zonisamide CL/F

## Study
- Short communication. 99 Japanese patients with epilepsy (62 M / 37 F), age 14.3 ± 8.0 y (range 1.36-39.24), total body weight 40.7 ± 20.2 kg (10-117), zonisamide 251.2 ± 152.2 mg/day (40-800) (Table 1, PDF p2 / printed p541). Mostly children and adolescents.
- 282 steady-state routine TDM serum concentrations, drawn 4.3 ± 2.8 h after a dose; mean 24.4 ± 16.1 µg/mL (2-89) (Table 1).
- Normal renal and hepatic function required. Assay: HPLC (BML Inc.), LOQ 0.1 µg/mL, linear range given as 0-800 µg/mL (p541).
- Genotyping: CYP2C19*2, *3 and CYP3A5*3 by PCR-RFLP. CYP2C19 homozygous EM 37, heterozygous EM 43, PM 19; CYP3A5 *1/*1 4, *1/*3 44, *3/*3 51. Allele frequencies 29.8% (*2), 11.1% (*3), 73.7% (CYP3A5*3) (Table 1, Results).
- Co-medication counts (Table 1): ZNS monotherapy 24, carbamazepine 98 (sic, see open questions), valproic acid 60, phenytoin 29, phenobarbital 20, clonazepam 24, clobazam 13.
- Model: one compartment, first-order absorption and elimination; WinNonMix 2.0.1 (Pharsight) per Methods (the key words say NONMEM). Forward inclusion / backward elimination. Exponential IIV, proportional residual error. No validation of the parameters (authors' statement, p543).

## Model and parameters
Final model (p541 equation; Table 3, PDF p3 / printed p542):

CL/F = θ0 × (BW/44)^θ1 × DOSE^θ2 × θ3^(CYP2C19 hetEM) × θ4^(CYP2C19 PM) × θ5^CBZ × θ6^PHT × θ7^PB × e^ηCL

BW in kg (44 = the normalising "mean", though Table 1 gives 40.7), DOSE = zonisamide mg/day; all genotype and co-medication terms are 0/1 indicators. The printed Results equation labels the last term "SMID" (a typo for PB; the abstract and Table 3 footnote say PB). **No unit is given for CL/F** anywhere in the paper; V/F and ka are not reported.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No efficacy or toxicity end points; no therapeutic range proposed. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
- Body weight (power 0.77), daily dose (power -0.17: CL/F falls with dose; the authors call it a non-linear effect), CYP2C19 genotype (hetEM 0.84, PM 0.70: 16% and 30% lower than homozygous EM, gene-dose effect), carbamazepine 1.24, phenytoin 1.28, phenobarbital 1.29.
- **Comparator** for the inducer effects: patients in the same cohort without that drug, adjusted for the other terms; effects multiply when combined (the model form).
- Not retained (Table 2, backward elimination): sex (P 0.22), valproic acid (P 0.25), benzodiazepines (P 0.24). CYP3A5 genotype was not significant even at forward inclusion (P 0.09).

## Caveats
- No CL/F unit stated; no V/F or ka reported; no validation.
- Mostly paediatric Japanese TDM cohort; samples at ~4.3 h post-dose, not troughs.
- Inducer doses/concentrations not reported; indicators are presence/absence only.
- Table 1's carbamazepine count (98 of 99) is inconsistent with 24 monotherapy patients.
- Normalising weight 44 kg differs from the reported mean 40.7 kg.
