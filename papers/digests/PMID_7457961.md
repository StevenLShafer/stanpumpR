# Austin 1980: Blood meperidine concentration and analgesic response (MEAC)

## Study
Prospective observational study, Flinders Medical Centre, Adelaide. Nine adult surgical patients (Table 1, p. 461): 5 F / 4 M, age 25-63 y (mean 45, SD 15), weight 65-93 kg (mean 76, SD 10), height mean 172 cm; operations: 5 cholecystectomy, 2 arthrotomy, 1 hysterectomy, 1 patellectomy. Premedication diazepam 10 mg PO; anaesthesia atropine, thiopental, N2O/O2, relaxant, halothane, controlled ventilation; **no analgesic before or during anaesthesia**.

Dosing (p. 461): meperidine HCl 75 mg **IM** into the buttock on arrival in recovery and then on request until early evening; overnight 100 mg IM on request; next day (~20 h after surgery) 75 mg IM on request again; minimum interval 1.5 h. Study ran 32 h. Not PCA: injections were given by staff on the patient's request.

Sampling: subclavian venous **whole blood** (catheter via antecubital fossa) before each injection and every 30 min until the next request; overnight a sample at each request. Assay by the group's earlier method (ref 3, Stapleton 1979), concentrations **expressed as meperidine base**. Pain scored by the patient every 30 min in the daytime with a brief questionnaire: 2 = severe, 1 = moderate, 0 = no pain. Eysenck Personality Inventory (N, E, L) taken the evening before surgery. Statistics with SPSS 7.01 (Kruskal-Wallis, Mann-Whitney, Spearman, multiple regression, partial correlation).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC** (defined p. 462 as the second inflection point, the concentration at which the patient passes from some pain, score 1 or 2, to effective analgesia, score 0): **0.46 ug/mL (SD 0.18, CV 39%), range 0.24-0.76 ug/mL**, 19 complete severe-to-no-pain cycles; adults after major surgery with **severe** postoperative pain; **whole blood** (subclavian venous), total (protein binding and red-cell uptake not measured, p. 465), expressed as base. Patient 7 never reached analgesia. MEAC reached in only 27 of 46 detailed curves (p. 464).
- **MCP** (first inflection: highest concentration still with severe pain): 0.41 ug/mL (SD 0.17), range 0.1-0.98. Authors call MCP a reasonable but slightly lower approximation of MEAC (p. 466).
- Individual curves very steep (transition within ~0.05 ug/mL); interpatient shift about fourfold (Fig. 1); intrapatient MCP CV 20-51%; intrapatient CV of the midpoint 31% (SD 14%, n = 9); individual MEACs for Patient 8 ranged 0.25-0.49 ug/mL (p. 463). No trend over the two days (Fig. 2). No apparent dependence on operation type.
- Fig. 3 overlays the MEAC mean +/- SD (0.46 +/- 0.18; caption says "g/ml", evidently ug/mL) on blood levels after 4-hourly 100 mg IM from the earlier study.
- Authors state the minimum analgesic concentration is the same by IV infusion or IM injection (p. 464, citing refs 3-4).
- No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis analysis; concentrations taken at 30-min intervals and treated as at equilibrium. No potency ratio.

## Covariates
MCP correlated (Spearman, Table 4) with lying girth (r = -0.786, P <= 0.01), standing girth (-0.726), extroversion E (+0.671), body weight (-0.628, P <= 0.07), mean dosing interval (-0.798). Regression equations (Table 5 footnote): Estimate 1 MCP = 1.365 - 0.011 Lygirth; Estimate 2 = 0.948 - 0.008 Lygirth + 0.010 N + 0.011 E; Estimate 3 = -1.612 + 0.002 Lygirth + 0.016 N + 0.024 E + 0.010 Ht - 0.004 BWt; Estimate 4 = -5.027 + 0.023 Lygirth + 0.022 N + 0.045 E + 0.022 Ht - 0.007 BWt + 0.046 L - 0.009 StGirth (girth, height cm; weight kg). MEAC itself not modelled (too few estimates).

## Caveats
n = 9, preliminary report; MEAC from only 19 transitions; ordinal 3-point pain score; IM dosing so concentrations were often too low for a full response range; whole-blood (not plasma) concentrations; diazepam and halothane residuals; regression with 9 patients and up to 8 predictors is overfitted (error SD 0.03 ug/mL for Estimate 4). Table 5's "Mean MCP" column does not match Table 2's per-patient MCPs row for row (e.g. Patient 2: 0.20 vs 0.45), so one table appears misordered in print.
