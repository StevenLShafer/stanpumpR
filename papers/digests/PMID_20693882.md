# Ueyama 2010: EEG response to sevoflurane is the same in term parturients and nonpregnant women

## Study
Prospective two-group comparison (Osaka University Hospital), not randomized. 15 term parturients for elective
caesarean section under general anaesthesia (age 23-38 yr; 31 ± 6 yr; weight 64.5 ± 7.0 kg, pre-pregnancy
59 ± 8 kg; gestation 39 ± 0.4 wk) versus 15 nonpregnant women for open gynaecological surgery (myomectomy 7,
ovarian cystectomy 5, hysterectomy 3; age 21-41 yr; 32 ± 4 yr; 58 ± 8 kg). ASA I-II. Table 1, PDF p3 (printed 579),
mean ± SD; confirmed on the page image. Seven parturients had idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (reasons for GA).

Induction: thiopental 4 mg/kg, fentanyl 2 µg/kg, vecuronium 0.15 mg/kg or suxamethonium 2 mg/kg (rapid sequence
in parturients). Maintenance: sevoflurane in air/oxygen (no nitrous oxide), fentanyl 2 µg/kg after delivery (pregnant)
or before incision (nonpregnant) and 1 µg/kg every 30 min. End-tidal sevoflurane held at 2.0% and 1.5% (during
surgery) and 1.0% and 0.5% (after surgery), 15 min at each step after equilibration (protocol from Gas Man
simulation). End points: BIS A-1050 raw EEG analysed offline for SEF95, amplitude, BIS and bicoherence peaks
(pBIC-low 3-5 Hz, pBIC-high 5-10 Hz). Two-way ANOVA (group × concentration) and unpaired t tests; sample size
powered for a 20% BIS difference (10 units, SD 9).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic or concentration-response model fitted; no MAC measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: EEG (BIS, SEF95, amplitude, bicoherence) at steady end-tidal sevoflurane; not movement, not MAC.
- No EC50, ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis reported. Not an opioid paper (fentanyl was co-administered, not studied):
  no MEAC, no potency ratio.
- BIS > 60 at 1.5%: 2 patients (one per group); at 1.0%: 10 nonpregnant and 8 pregnant (p5 text). No recall in any
  patient. Authors suggest at least 1.5% sevoflurane without EEG monitoring or N2O.
- Secondary (cited, not measured here): MAC of volatiles falls about 30% in first trimester (Gin & Chan 1994);
  postpartum isoflurane MAC 0.775% at 1-12 h, 0.825% over the next 36 h, normal 1.125% by 72 h (Chan & Gin 1995) (p7).

## Covariates
Pregnancy (group) only; no covariate model.

## Caveats
- Comparison is pregnant vs nonpregnant women in the same study; it addresses brain (hypnotic) sensitivity, not MAC
  (immobility). It cannot say whether MAC itself is reduced at term.
- Powered for 20% differences; the authors acknowledge smaller differences may be missed. "No significant
  difference" is not "identical".
- Different operations (caesarean vs gynaecological surgery), different fentanyl timing; heart rate higher in
  pregnant group (Table 3). Thiopental and muscle relaxants given; 1.0 and 0.5% steps recorded after surgery.
- Table 4 "Difference" column has apparent errors: for pBIC-low the 1.5% and 1.0% differences (−0.1, 4.8) look
  swapped against the means (derived: 35.1 − 30.3 = 4.8; 24.5 − 24.6 = −0.1). Means and P values themselves are
  as printed.
- Sevoflurane only; isoflurane, desflurane and nitrous oxide were not studied.
