# Blin 1999: PK/PD of the memory effects of oral lorazepam in volunteers

## Study
Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled two-way crossover; 12 healthy volunteers (age, weight and
sex not reported in this short report). Single 2 mg oral lorazepam; 15-day washout. 12 blood samples
from before to 48 h. GC with electron-capture detection. Tests: choice reaction time (CRT), immediate
and delayed cued recall of word pairs, immediate and delayed free recall and recognition of pictures
(picture tasks only at 0.5, 2, 6, 26 h). PK: linear three-compartment model (apparent, per F). PD:
effect compartment + sigmoid Emax, per subject, Siphar.

## Model and parameters
Mean (SD) PK (Results): Cmax 33.4 (4.8) ng/mL; AUC 405 (95) ng.h/mL; CL/F 5.4 (1) L/h; Vss/F 111.6
(19.4) L; terminal t1/2 16.6 (5.2) h. Individual compartmental parameters not reported.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Counter-clockwise hysteresis, collapsed with an effect compartment. Table 1, mean (SD):

| Test | ke0 (1/h) | t1/2ke0 (h) | EC50 (ng/mL) | gamma |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CRT | 1.27 (0.56) | 0.54 (0.18) | 13.5 (3.66) | 4.87 |
| Cued immediate recall | 1.64 (0.86) | 0.42 (0.15) | 15.1 (6.85) | 3.93 |
| Cued delayed recall | 1.47 (0.82) | 0.47 (1.36) | 13.8 (6.80) | 3.33 |
| Free immediate recall | 1.70 (0.95) | 0.41 (0.22) | 15.3 (4.60) | 5.94 |
| Free delayed recall | 1.39 (0.77) | 0.49 (0.098) | 14.8 (5.40) | 5.55 |
| Picture recognition | 1.37 (0.29) | 0.51 (0.08) | 12.2 (7.15) | 6.53 |

Table 1 is on PDF p. 3 (printed p. 512), checked on the rendered page. The cued delayed recall t1/2 SD of 1.36 h
is as printed, but is implausible beside its own ke0 SD (0.82 on 1.47 /h) and the other tasks' t1/2 SDs
(0.08-0.22 h); likely a typo in the source table, left as printed.

EC50 range 12.2-15.3 ng/mL (effect-site/plasma steady-state equivalent, total plasma lorazepam). The
equilibration half-times, about 25-32 min (derived: 0.41-0.54 h), are much slower than an EEG-based
estimate (for comparison, Greenblatt 2000's 8.8 min). Delayed recall and recognition had lower EC50s
than immediate recall; CRT (sedation) not distinguishable from memory effects.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Short report; n = 12; demographics, IIV and model fit details absent; oral single dose so PK is
apparent; per-subject fits averaged; ke0 from oral data with sparse picture-task sampling. The
authors note their EC50s are far lower than earlier psychomotor studies.
