# Sheiner 1979: The effect-compartment (ke0) model, d-tubocurarine

## Study
UCSF (Clinical Pharmacology; Anesthesia). d-Tubocurarine (dTC).
- Group 1: 7 healthy surgical patients 21-49 y; thiopental 4 mg/kg, succinylcholine 1 mg/kg, N2O 70% + halothane 0.5-0.7% end-tidal; dTC 16.8 ug/kg/min for 10 min then 1.2 ug/kg/min to end of surgery (70-240 min); 20-30 venous samples each.
- Group 2: 21 patients, same protocol, maintenance morphine 0.3 mg/kg + diazepam 0.3 mg/kg + N2O (14) or halothane 1.0-1.2% (7); used for the reduced-data analysis.
- Group 3: reanalysis of Miller 1977: 10 normal and 10 renal-failure patients, bolus 0.3 or 0.5 mg/kg, 7-9 samples each, population (maximum likelihood) analysis.
Effect: thumb adduction force (ulnar nerve, single 0.1-ms stimuli at 0.3 Hz), as fraction paralysis 0-1. Assay: radioimmunoassay, CV 8%, LLQ 0.05 ug/mL. PK: biexponential (2-compartment) per patient, NONLIN, unit weights; then effect fitted with PK fixed.

## Model and parameters
Effect compartment linked to the central compartment by k1e (assumed negligibly small so it does not alter PK) and emptied by ke0; dAe/dt = k1e A1 - ke0 Ae (eq 2). Effect expressed through the equivalent steady-state plasma concentration Cpss = ke0 Z / V1 (eq 7; k1e cancels) in a Hill equation: E = Cpss^gamma / (Cpss^gamma + Cpss(50)^gamma) (eq 8). Closed forms for bolus, infusion and post-infusion (eqs 4-6). If ke0 is very large, effect parallels the central compartment; if ke0 = k_i1, it parallels peripheral compartment i.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: twitch depression (thumb adduction), not EEG. Onset of detectable effect ~5 min after infusion start; peak effect ~5 min after plasma (Fig. 2).
- Table I: ke0 = infinity and gamma = 1 alternatives both fit significantly worse in all 7 patients (F up to 5,068 and 1,018); ke0 also not equal to k21.
- Theoretical ke0 from muscle perfusion 3 mL/100 g/min and muscle:blood partition 0.2 = 0.15/min, close to 0.13 (Discussion).
- Reduced data (first 20-25 min) predicted steady-state effect (r = 0.86) and Cpss(50) (r = 0.97) (Figs 5-6).
- Renal failure: suggestion that ke0 and Cpss(50) differ (Table III, 2dLL 8 and 5, both 16).
- Fig. 7: cumulative dose-response technique biased by disequilibrium (apparent Cp50 1.1, 0.7, 0.55 ug/mL for ke0 40%, 100%, 160% of normal; true 0.6).
- No tPeak reported. No MEAC; not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Renal failure (group 3) only.

## Caveats
Small n; volatile anesthetic background (halothane potentiates dTC); the group 3 SEMs as printed (0.14 for ke0, 0.01 for gamma) look implausible against group 1. Model limits listed by the authors: cannot separate perfusion/diffusion/receptor steps, assumes no tolerance, requires plasma concentrations, may be too simple.
