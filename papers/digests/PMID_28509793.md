# Shafer 2017: Remifentanil dosing at extremes of body weight

## Study
Two-page editorial accompanying Kim 2017 (Anesthesiology 126:1019-32, remifentanil PK in obesity) and Eleveld 2017 (126:1005-18, allometric remifentanil PKPD). No new data; no assay, no population of its own. DOI not confirmed (the text layer garbles the DOI in the copyright line), so it is left blank.

## Model and parameters
No parameter table. Statements about the two models (PDF p1-2, printed 993-994):
- Kim: pooled nine published remifentanil PK data sets; typical prediction accuracy about 20% in normal and obese people. Lean body mass is the "fat-free mass" of Janmahasatian 2005. Minto 1997 used James equations for LBM, which turn downward at high weight.
- Eleveld: also reanalysed published data; scales all parameters to a reference individual (70 kg, 170 cm, 35-yr-old man, fat-free mass 54.5 kg by Al-Sallami 2015 equations); adjusts volumes and clearances for age as well as weight. Al-Sallami was chosen because it predicted best (Eleveld supplement), not for mechanistic reasons.
- For adults of average size the two models give nearly identical predictions (same data sets).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, tPeak, MEAC, EC50 or potency ratio reported. (Opioid audit: none.) Dosing statements are about PK (blood concentration) only.

## Covariates
Dosing implications stated (read from Kim fig 7A and the Eleveld article, not this paper's own data):
- Infusion rate to hold a target concentration in a 250-kg patient is about twice that in a 50-kg patient (absolute µg/min); 5x the dose would give 2.5-fold concentration. A 250-kg patient behaves PK-wise like a 100-kg patient.
- Remifentanil distributes into tissue: over the first 30 min the rate should fall by about 20%; example start 0.12 then 0.1 µg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹.
- Children aged 5-20 yr: nonobese infusion rate in µg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ about 1.5x the adult rate; below 5 yr about 1.75x. Obesity adjustment in children rests on adult observations.

## Caveats
Opinion piece; numbers are approximate readings of other authors' simulations. Obese children were barely represented in the data.
