# Dash 2010: erratum to the haemoglobin O2 and CO2 dissociation-curve model

## Study
A mathematical-modelling paper, not a clinical study; no patients, no drug. It is the republished (corrected) version of Dash and Bassingthwaighte 2004. The History note on PDF p.1 (printed 1683) says the 2004 proofs were not sent to the authors. Corrections were first listed in an appendix to the 2006 paper, and the one remaining is that [2,3-DPG] is 4.65 mM, not molar (Eq. 11). The authors state the equations here match the downloadable physiome.org model code.

## Model and parameters
Equilibrium binding of O2 and CO2 to haemoglobin inside red cells, with Hb as four heme-amino chains. The fractional saturations are Hill-type with exponent 1 and concentration-dependent apparent coefficients: SHbO2 = K_HbO2[O2] / (1 + K_HbO2[O2]) (Eq. 7a), and the same form for CO2 (Eq. 7b). The coefficients depend on PO2, PCO2, pH, 2,3-DPG and temperature through K04 (Eq. 9, a power-law in those variables with exponents n0..n4). Appendix B gives the analytical inversion for PO2 from SHbO2 (Eqs. B.1-B.3), with Hill exponent 1 + n0. Appendix A gives whole-blood O2 and CO2 content.

P50 as a polynomial (Eq. 11) is quadratic in pH_rbc, PCO2 and DPG and cubic in temperature, fitted to the Buerk and Bridges model, not to raw data. Kelman's form (Eq. 12) is given for comparison.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. This is a physiology model, not a drug effect model: no ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio, and no opioid content, so the opioid audit does not apply.

## Covariates
Model inputs, not patient covariates: PO2, PCO2, pH_rbc, [2,3-DPG] and temperature in the exponent terms, plus haematocrit for whole-blood content.

## Caveats
- The authors say the fit used published summaries (P50 values from Buerk-Bridges, Kelman and Winslow), because the original data tables were not preserved.
- Accuracy is poor below about 30% saturation (PO2 about 20 mmHg), where it underestimates saturation by 1 to 2%; the authors suggest Severinghaus or Kelman for that range.
- The authors expect roughly 2-3% accuracy for PCO2 above 20 mmHg.
- No clinical validation.
