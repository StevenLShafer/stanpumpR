# Zhang 2020: CYP- and UGT-mediated buprenorphine metabolism in pregnancy

## Study
- Pooled analysis of two prospective, open-label, nonrandomised longitudinal studies at Magee-Womens Hospital,
  University of Pittsburgh (PDF p2-3).
  - **Study 1**: singleton pregnancies stable on twice-daily sublingual buprenorphine (2-16 mg) for at least 7 days.
    Steady-state PK at the second trimester (14w0d-28w0d), the third trimester (28w1d to term) and postpartum (about
    4 weeks). Ten samples at 0 (trough), 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 8 and 12 h.
  - **Study 2**: a PK/PD study with phases before 20 weeks, from 20 weeks, and at least 4 weeks postpartum. Dosing
    frequency not restricted to twice daily. 10-13 samples over 12 h or the dosing interval.
- Exclusions: CYP3A inducers or inhibitors, and grapefruit juice within 3 days. Urine drug screens were done.
- Assay: UPLC-MS/MS for BUP, NBUP, BUPG and NBUPG. Study 1 linear 0.05-50 ng/mL (NBUP 0.5-50). Study 2 BUP
  0.05-100, NBUP 0.2-100, BUPG and NBUPG 0.2-200 ng/mL. Bias within 15%. Plasma.
- Analysis: concentrations converted to molar units. AUC over the dosing interval at steady state by the trapezoidal
  rule. Metabolic ratios: (AUC_NBUP + AUC_NBUPG)/AUC_BUP is the CYP3A N-dealkylation index, and AUC_BUPG/AUC_BUP
  the UGT index. The log ratios were compared with a univariate linear mixed-effects model (time fixed, subject
  random; Stata 14).
- n = 26 women. 19 completed the combined first/second-trimester study, 18 the third-trimester study and 14 the
  postpartum study (PDF p3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- **MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration: none reported.** Study 2 is described as PK/PD, but no PD
  results are given.
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported.**
- **Equianalgesic or potency ratio: none.**
- The Discussion quotes Cmax values from other studies (Concheiro, Kuhlman, Harris, PDF p5-6). None of them is this
  paper's data.

## Covariates
Pregnancy stage only. Albumin and total protein are in Table 1 but are not analysed. No genotyping was done.

## Caveats
- The ratios are indices of metabolite formation, not clearances. A metabolite/parent AUC ratio also depends on
  how fast the metabolite is eliminated, which the authors acknowledge (PDF p7). In some women, two of them on both
  pathways, the ratios fell in pregnancy.
- The comparator for "higher" is **the same cohort at about 4-6 weeks postpartum** (Table 1), not non-pregnant women
  or a population model.
- The trimester groups mix the two studies, and their boundaries differ (20 weeks in study 2, 28 or 29 weeks
  elsewhere). The SD of the third-trimester UGT ratio (3.62) is larger than its mean.
- The paper does not measure absorption, bioavailability, tmax or salivary pH. It says the higher metabolism could
  partly explain the lower exposure seen earlier (ref 15, Bastian 2017).
- Sublingual dosing only: apparent, not absolute, disposition.
- Small n, and the sample shrinks to 14 postpartum.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Author (R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:251, :296; pregnancyRegistry.csv:36): discrepancy.** The first author is
  **Zhang H**, not "Liu Y". The DOI, journal, volume, pages and title are correct. No author named Liu appears.
- **Mechanism (R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:251-252): partly matches.** The paper does report higher CYP3A
  N-dealkylation and UGT glucuronidation ratios in pregnancy than postpartum (Table 2). "**Not to absorption**"
  goes beyond it: the paper neither measured nor discussed absorption, and says only that metabolism *partially*
  explains the lower exposure. The evidence that absorption was unchanged (tmax and salivary pH) is Bastian 2017's,
  as R/drugs_buprenorphine.R:247 itself says.
- **pregnancyRegistry.csv:36 notes:** these say the paper "attributes the lower exposure to increased CYP3A/UGT
  metabolism (clearance), not reduced absorption, and Tmax/pH were unchanged". **Discrepancy:** this paper reports
  no Tmax or pH, and it does not exclude absorption.
- **Mapping the factor to cl1:** the decision to scale elimination clearance (not F) is a modelling choice that is
  consistent with this paper's metabolic finding. The paper does not quantify it. The 1.83 comes from Zhu 2024/2025,
  not from here. Nothing in this paper is transcribed into a parameter. The CYP ratio, third trimester over
  postpartum, is 1.38 (derived), but it is a formation index and **must not** be read as a clearance ratio.
- **Comparator:** "higher during pregnancy" is against the same women at about 4-6 weeks postpartum. Zhu's 1.83 has
  the same comparator, and the library applies it against Bjornsson's non-pregnant population model. That baseline
  shift belongs to Zhu's card, not this one.
- No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency in the paper, so nothing to check against `BUPRENORPHINE_TPEAK` or the band.
