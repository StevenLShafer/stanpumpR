# Calleja 2025: pharmacogenetics of a single 300 mg pregabalin dose

## Study
Pharmacogenetic substudy of a 2-period crossover bioequivalence trial (EudraCT 2022-501160-18-00). 24 healthy volunteers (14 F, 10 M), age 33.8 +/- 9.95 y, weight 67.79 +/- 13.32 kg, height 1.64 m, BMI 24.89 (Table 1); women 59.05 kg, men 80.02 kg. 21 of mixed (mostly Latin American) ancestry, 3 European. Single 300 mg oral dose fasted, test and reference formulations averaged per subject. 20 samples to 48 h; LC-MS/MS, LLOQ 30 ng/mL. Noncompartmental (WinNonlin 8.0).

Only the sex differences in exposure disappear after dose/weight correction: AUC/DW and Cmax/DW did not differ by sex (Results 2.1, PDF/printed p. 3; Table 2, p. 4). Men still had a higher weight-normalised Vd/F (704.43 vs 572.88 mL/kg; univariate p = 0.001; multivariate beta 0.180, p = 0.003, R2 0.36) and a longer t1/2 (6.53 vs 5.21 h; multivariate beta 0.186, p < 0.001, R2 0.598); both are underlined in Table 2 as significant after multivariate analysis. Cl/Fw did not differ by sex. Genetic findings: NAT2 slow acetylators ~25% longer t1/2, among others (exploratory, small n). Intro cites (ref 9, Tjandrawinata 2015) 150 mg AUC 27.9 ug.h/mL and Cmax 3.85 ug/mL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Adverse reactions in 19/24; ADR subjects had higher AUC and Cmax (8.20 vs 6.26 ug/mL). No PK/PD model, no MEAC/ke0 (not an opioid).

## Covariates
Sex: the exposure (AUC, Cmax) difference is explained by dose/weight, but sex remained associated with Vd/Fw and t1/2 after multivariate analysis (Results 2.1, p. 3; Table 2, p. 4). No covariate equations. Several pharmacogenes (exploratory; NAT2 slow acetylators also had longer t1/2 and lower Cl/Fw, Table 3, p. 4).

## Caveats
n = 24, single dose, multiple genetic comparisons; observed mean tmax 1.67 h is later than the 0.7-1.3 h of Bockbrader 2010.
