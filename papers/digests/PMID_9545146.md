# Graves 1998: population PK of maintenance carbamazepine in 829 adults with epilepsy

## Study
- Retrospective chart review, routine clinic TDM, 1991-1995, three centres (MINCEP, Mayo, Marshfield).
- Index set: 829 patients, 1834 observations; validation set 50 patients, 312 observations (Table 2).
  Index: 51% women; age 35 y (17-89); weight 75 kg (28-159); CBZ dose 964 mg/day (100-3200); concentration
  7.2 mg/L (1-17.4). Comedication: phenytoin 26%, valproate 19%, phenobarbital 15%, felbamate 6%; smokers 28%;
  alcohol 22%; age >= 70 only 1.7%. Patients < 17 y excluded.
- Dosing times assumed when not documented (40% of observations). All observations assumed post-induction steady
  state. Formulation not recorded.
- Assay: serum total carbamazepine by fluorescence polarization immunoassay (MINCEP, Marshfield) or EMIT (Mayo).
- NONMEM V, ADVAN2 TRANS2 (one compartment, first-order absorption and elimination); exponential IIV, additive
  residual error; forward inclusion p < 0.01, backward elimination p < 0.001; mixture model for low-clearance
  subgroup.

## Model and parameters
Final model (Results, Table 4; 95% CI):
CL/F (L/h) = (0.0134 x TBW + 3.58) x 1.42 (phenytoin only) x 1.17 (phenobarbital or felbamate only) x 1.62
(phenytoin and phenobarbital or felbamate, interaction term) x 0.749 (age >= 70 y); V/F (L) = 1.97 x TBW;
ka = 0.441 /h.

Mixture model: 20% (7.4-32%) of patients in a low-clearance subgroup with CL/F 64% (59-70%) of the rest (not
used in the final equations). Predictive performance (Table 5): regression MPE 0.6 mg/L, MAE 2.4, RMSE 2.9;
Bayesian MPE -0.06, MAE 0.92, RMSE 1.2. Table 6: known vs estimated dosing times overlap (V/F and ka poorly
estimated with estimated times).
Authors' worked examples (Discussion): 40-y, 70-kg patient, no other drugs, needs 864 mg/day for Css 8 mg/L;
with phenytoin + phenobarbital 1400 mg/day; 70-y, 79-kg patient 652 mg/day (1056 with PHT + PHB).
Derived at 70 kg, no comedication, age < 70: CL/F = 4.518 L/h (0.0645 L/h/kg), V/F 137.9 L, t1/2 21.2 h; Css
for 864 mg/day = 864/24/4.518 = 7.97 mg/L (consistent with the authors' example).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None modelled. Commonly accepted therapeutic range 4-12 mg/L quoted (Introduction). Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0
or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Total body weight (linear, on CL/F with intercept; proportional on V/F); comedication (phenytoin, phenobarbital,
felbamate); age >= 70. Not significant: valproate, gender, smoking, alcohol, age >= 60; no covariate on V/F.

## Caveats
Retrospective, sparse, mostly trough TDM data; 40% of dose times assumed; immunoassays at different sites; ka and
V/F weakly informed (V/F IIV 82%); formulation not recorded; elderly effect from 1.7% of patients; assumes full
autoinduction; epoxide and unbound drug not measured.

- **Steady-state check: discrepancy.** carbamazepine.R:47-48 says 200 mg twice daily averages about 6.6 mg/L,
  inside 4-12 mg/L. With the model's own CL/F at 70 kg, 400 mg/day gives 400/24/4.518 = 3.7 mg/L (derived), below
  the 4 mg/L floor; 6.6 mg/L needs about 715 mg/day. The authors' own example (864 mg/day -> 8 mg/L) confirms the
  3.7 figure. Comment only; the code is unaffected.
- Formulations (carbamazepine.R:40-45): Graves did not record formulation; "suspension, IR and chewable share
  these parameters" is the repo's assumption. **cannot verify** from this paper.
- Body size: repo evaluates the weight terms at pkWeight with the switch on; the paper used total body weight
  (repo convention, docs/weight-adjustment.md). With the switch off, **matches**.
- Band 4-12 mg/L (carbamazepine.R:54): paper quotes the same commonly accepted range (cited in the repo to
  Patsalos 2008). Consistent.
