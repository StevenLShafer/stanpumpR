# Henderson 2002: modified patient-maintained propofol sedation in volunteers

## Study
Twenty healthy adults (age 32 [24-46], 14 M : 6 F, weight 69 kg [58-104]; Table 1, median [range]) fasted 6 h and tried to render themselves unconscious using a patient-maintained propofol TCI. System: Graseby 3400 pump on RS232 to a microprocessor running the Marsh adult propofol PK set (ref 6; Marsh 1991 children's paper as cited) and an effect-site estimate from White 1999 (ref 7). Changes from the earlier 2000 system (Murdoch, PMID_10992841): lockout 4 min (was 2), start target 0.5 ug/mL (was 1.0), increment 0.1 ug/mL per double press within 1 s (was 0.2); maximum target 3 ug/mL. Handset activated after about 5 min equilibration. A keyword was given every 15 min; recall tested after the subject could walk. Monitoring: NIAP, ECG, SpO2, respiratory rate, modified Steward score every 5 min. Oxygen if SpO2 below 94% or rate below 8. Logistic regression gave the Cp50 for loss of memory.

No subject lost verbal contact. One had brief apnoea with SpO2 85% at an estimated blood concentration of 1.0 ug/mL (recovered when told to breathe). No fall over 10% in HR or mean pressure.
Figure 1 (p389, PDF p3): simulations of blood and effect-site concentration for a subject who presses continually; with the old settings the effect-site overshoot above an assumed threshold of 1.0 ug/mL was 0.4 ug/mL, with the new settings 0.1 ug/mL (discussion, p390).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Cp50 for loss of memory for words: 1.26 ug/mL, end point = failure to recall the keyword (binary), predicted blood concentration, healthy volunteers, no assay (predicted).
- No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak is reported; the effect-site curves come from the White 1999 model (ref 7), whose ke0 is not stated here.
- Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
Predicted concentrations only. Healthy volunteers without stimulation; maximal-sedation time 65 min is too slow for practice (authors). Ceiling of 3 ug/mL was never reached (max 2.5). The Forum article is brief and gives no regression detail.
