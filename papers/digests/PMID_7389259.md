# Tsuei 1980: dexamethasone disposition in parturient women

## Study
- 12 women at term on an antenatal dexamethasone regimen for prevention of neonatal respiratory distress, 11 of them
  awaiting caesarean section (Table I, p. 89). Age 23-40 yr, gestation 30-41 wk, weight 53-112 kg (Table I).
- Dose: 8 mg dexamethasone phosphate (Decadron), stated equivalent to 6.66 mg dexamethasone (base), either IV over
  about 5 s (n = 5, plus patient D. D., who was in premature labour, got salbutamol and delivered 47 min after the dose:
  n = 6 IV in the abstract) or IM into the buttock (n = 6), about noon the day before surgery (p. 89).
- Excluded from the means: D. D. (IV; delivery 47 min after dosing, prior doses 61 h earlier) and K. B. (IM;
  epileptic on phenytoin 300 mg/day, an inducer). So means are n = 5 IV and n = 5 IM; combined n = 10.
- Sampling: antecubital venous plasma at about 5, 15, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 240, 300, 360, 420 and 480 min.
- Assay: HPLC (their ref. 34), total plasma dexamethasone.
- Placental transfer: 14 other mothers with paired maternal and umbilical vein samples at delivery (Table IV).
- Binding: equilibrium dialysis at 37 °C, pH 7.4; blood/plasma ratio (λ) in vitro with spiked blood.
- Analysis: individual bi- or triexponential fits to IV data (equations 1 and 2); IM data not fitted, NCA only.
  t½ = 0.693/λ1 (λ1 is the TERMINAL, smallest exponent in this paper's notation), AUC = Σ(Ci/λi) for IV,
  trapezoidal + extrapolation for IM; Clp = F·D/AUC with D = dexamethasone base dose and F assumed 1.

Triexponential for 4 of the 5 IV women (M. Cl., J. M., M. Ch., M. Ca.), biexponential for M. L. and D. D.
(p. 91). Table II (IV, PDF p. 3, printed p. 90), mean (SD) of n = 5 excluding D. D.:

t½ 142 min (harmonic mean), AUC 13.1 (3.9) µg·min/mL. K. B. (phenytoin): t½ 120 min, AUC 4.7.

Clearance (p. 94, PDF p. 7): mean total plasma clearance 559 mL/min in the pregnant women against 243 mL/min in
nonpregnant women (their earlier study, ref. 35, women only), p < 0.01. IV and IM groups were pooled because AUC and
t½ did not differ between routes (p. 92).
(derived: averaging the ten individual 6660 µg / AUC values from Tables II and III gives 558.5 mL/min, so 559 is the
mean of individual clearances over 5 IV + 5 IM women, IM assuming F = 1, dose as base; IV alone 573 mL/min;
559/243 = 2.30; 559 mL/min = 33.5 L/h.)

No volume of distribution is reported anywhere in the paper.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD end point, no ke0, no effective concentration. Not an opioid. Clinical comments (p. 96): after 8 mg IM the
maternal plasma level was about 5 ng/mL at 8 h (about 1/15 of the mean peak); after 8 mg IV about 3.5 ng/mL at 8 h.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (term) against nonpregnant women of another study: total plasma clearance 2.30x, AUC smaller (p < 0.01),
terminal t½ not significantly different (CV 47% in pregnant vs 7% in nonpregnant), protein binding not different,
blood/plasma ratio higher (attributed to lower haematocrit). Phenytoin (one patient) lowered AUC. Weight not analysed.

## Caveats
- Small n (5 + 5); nonpregnant comparator from a separate study whose details are not in this paper.
- Clp assumes instant, complete hydrolysis of the phosphate and F = 1; the authors say the clearances are useful
  comparatively rather than absolutely (p. 94). The 2.30 ratio is the robust number, not 559 mL/min itself.
- IM clearances in the pooled mean assume IM F = 1.
- The pregnant IV data needed a third exponential (slowly equilibrating compartment, possibly the fetus;
  apparent distribution equilibrium about 4 h after IV), which the nonpregnant data did not (p. 95).
- The authors attribute the extra clearance mostly to placental metabolism (estimated placental blood clearance
  220-300 mL/min against an increase in blood clearance Clp/λ of 310 mL/min, p. 96).
- No volume reported: "volume doubled" is an inference from unchanged t½ with higher CL.
