# Sorooshian 1996: cisatracurium PK/PD in young and elderly adults

## Study
64 ASA 1-3 patients, Newcastle and Liverpool (Table 1, PDF p4): 31 young (18-50 y; 36 +/- 9 y, 73 +/- 16 kg, 168 +/- 9 cm,
M:F 9:22) and 33 elderly (> 65 y; 74 +/- 6 y, 70 +/- 12 kg, 166 +/- 10 cm, 19:14). Thiopental, fentanyl, 60% N2O and
about 1% end-tidal isoflurane for > 20 min before the relaxant. Cisatracurium 0.1 mg/kg over 5 s, then 0.025 mg/kg
boluses after >= 25% T1 recovery, or an infusion from 3 mcg/kg/min (8 patients) (PDF p2). At least five venous samples
per patient from 2 to 120 min (three more in infusion patients); acidified plasma, HPLC with fluorescence, sensitivity
10 ng/mL, CV 15% at 15, 12.5% at 500 and 9% at 1500 ng/mL. Adductor pollicis T1 (TOF 2 Hz every 15 s) by force
transducer. NONMEM, first-order method; PK fitted first, then PD on post hoc PK (PDF p2-3).

## Model and parameters

age as a dichotomous covariate (PDF p3). Table 2 (PDF p4, population means with 95% CI; CV column is residual
unexplained variability):

No distribution clearance (Q) or V2 printed. The only PK age effect: a 37% larger peripheral volume in the elderly
(text, PDF p4 and p7). Vss underestimated by a factor 1.55 because of peripheral (Hofmann) elimination, wholly in the
peripheral compartment (underestimated by 1.94), from a naive-pooled fit with peripheral elimination fixed at the in vitro
0.0237 /min (PDF p3-4, Table 2 footnote).
(derived: young V2 = 9.7 - 4.76 = 4.94 L; Q = 0.297 L/min reproduces t1/2 4.20 and 28.4 min; with V2 x 1.37 = 6.77 L and
the same Q the half-lives are 4.49 and 36.3 min, matching the elderly column; Table 2's elderly Vss 13.3 L (V2 8.54 L)
would give about 4.7 and 44 min. 13.3/9.7 = 1.37, so the table's elderly Vss looks like 37% applied to Vss rather than
V2: an internal inconsistency between Table 2 and the text plus half-lives; which is the typo is not stated in the paper.)

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: adductor pollicis T1 twitch depression (mechanomyography), isoflurane anaesthesia. Sigmoid Emax on an
  effect compartment; ke0 young 0.071 /min, elderly 0.060 /min (16% lower), the main age difference (PDF p4, p7).
- C50 rises with weight, 10% of the value per 10 kg (Table 2 footnote; Fig 2). Age effects on C50 and gamma accepted;
  sex had no PD effect.
- Observed (PDF p4): peak block median 99% (96-100) young vs 98% (91-100) elderly; onset 3.0 (1.75-11.4) vs 4.0 (2.4-6.5)
  min; 5% recovery 39.5 vs 39.4 min, 25% recovery 50.0 vs 51.4 min. Table 3 (PDF p6) predicted time course for 70 kg
  typical patients, e.g. 6 mg: 99% block at 6 (young) / 8.75 (old) min, 25% recovery 56 / 55.5, 75% recovery 77 / 80.5.
- Median residual error 9.1% for Cp, 2.7% of control for twitch (PDF p5).

## Covariates
Age (dichotomous) on V2, ke0, C50 and gamma; weight on C50 only. No weight term on PK.

## Caveats
Venous, sparse sampling; isoflurane-specific PD; the authors say the PD model is tied to this PK model and should not be
used with PK models from other methods; Vss not physiologically interpretable (central-elimination model). Ages 51-65
not studied. C50-weight relation possibly an artefact of weight-based dosing (authors' discussion, PDF p8).
