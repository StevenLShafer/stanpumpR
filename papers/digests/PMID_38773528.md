# Chotsiri 2024: primaquine, carboxy-primaquine and PQ carbamoyl-glucuronide popPK in Malian males

## Study
- Part of an open-label, nonrandomised dose-adjustment trial of single low-dose primaquine (SLD-PQ) in G6PD-deficient and G6PD-normal males in Mali without microscopic malaria (NCT02535767). Only the 28 adult males (18-50 y) who gave PK samples are analysed. Pages 2-3.
- Single oral dose, 0.40 mg/kg (n=7), 0.45 mg/kg (n=7) or 0.50 mg/kg (n=14), from a 15 mg tablet (Sanofi) crushed in 15 mL water, taken after a fatty snack. Doses 17.6-41.5 mg; weight 44.0-83.0 kg (group medians 57.9-64.1); Hb 12.0-17.5 g/dL (Table 1, p. 4). 18 were G6PD-deficient (Figs 4-5); CYP2D6 phenotypes recorded.
- Sampling: venous plasma at pre-dose, 1, 4, 8, 24 h; capillary plasma at 2, 4, 6 h; 196 samples, all above LLOQ. Haemoglobin and methaemoglobin to day 28.
- Assay: LC-MS (Avula method), plasma, parent plus carboxy-primaquine (CPQ) and primaquine carbamoyl-glucuronide (PQCG); LLOQ 5 ng/mL (PQ, PQCG), 1 ng/mL (CPQ). Racemic mixture, enantiomers not resolved.
- NONMEM 7.4, FOCE-I, log-transformed data; each compound modelled separately; metabolite dose taken as the molar PQ dose with a 1:1 conversion.

## Model and parameters
One-compartment disposition with a transit-compartment absorption (5 transits for PQ and PQCG, 2 for CPQ); a peripheral compartment did not help. Body weight allometry fixed (0.75 on clearances, 1.0 on volumes, centred at 62.5 kg). A proportional capillary-to-venous conversion factor (CF) is estimated; separate exponential residual errors for venous and capillary samples. Table 2, PDF p. 7 (typical individual at 62.5 kg); all clearances and volumes are apparent (F fixed to 1) and absolute (L, L/h), not per kg.

Derived: the primaquine half-life from the typical CL/F and V/F is ln2 x 163 / 15.4 = 7.3 h, consistent with 7.42 h. For CPQ, ln2 x 93.3 / 0.129 = 501 h (about 21 d), consistent with the 19.5 d quoted. The text (p. 5) says the CPQ half-life is much longer than 15.6 h reported earlier in healthy volunteers, which the authors ascribe to 24 h sampling in that earlier work.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect model, no ke0 / tPeak, no MEAC or therapeutic range, and no potency ratio (not an opioid paper; opioid audit: none). The only end points are haemoglobin and methaemoglobin change to day 28, related to individual Cmax and AUC24 by simple linear regression in G6PD-deficient men (n=18); no correlation differed significantly from zero (Figs 4-5, pp. 9-10). No significant haemoglobin drop after the single dose (Fig 3, p. 8).

## Covariates
Weight only, as fixed allometry. Age, malaria PCR status, G6PD genotype (A- vs wild type), G6PD semi-quantitative phenotype and CYP2D6 phenotype were tested (add p<0.05, remove p<0.001) and none was significant (p. 4).

## Caveats
- Samples only to 24 h, so the CPQ terminal phase (half-life 469 h) is extrapolated; authors say it is poorly captured.
- 28 men only; the doses are 0.40-0.50 mg/kg, above the WHO 0.25 mg/kg SLD.
- Capillary-to-venous factors (32.9%, 69.1%, 40.1%) disagree with the roughly 1:1 expected from the literature, per authors (p. 6); they should be investigated, not used as a conversion.
- Racemate only; PQ was given with food (fatty biscuits); the active quinone metabolites were not measured.
- The Abstract gives the dose as 0.4-0.5 mg/kg and the Conclusion says 0.50 mg/kg; Table 1 age for group 3 prints "39 (25.47)". See open_questions for Table 2 and 3 printing oddities.
