# Anderson 2017: phenylephrine concentration-response for blood pressure after nasal delivery in children

## Study
- Pooled, previously published data (p.1051), analysed with NONMEM 7.3 (FOCE-I, ADVAN4 TRAN4):
  - Nasal: Christensen 2017. 52 children, 2-12 y, 10-40 kg, 0.1 mL/kg of 0.25% or 0.5% phenylephrine under anaesthesia. Venous samples at 0, 2, 5, 10, 20 min; non-invasive MAP before and at 2, 5, 10 min.
  - Ophthalmic: Kumar 1985. 20 adults, 23-81 y, two 32 uL drops of 2.5% (n = 10) or 10% (n = 10). Concentration and BP at 0, 10, 20, 60 min (a viscous solution).
  - Oral: 28 healthy volunteers given 10 mg (Atkinson), sampled to 6 h.
  - IV: Hengstmann 1982. Pooled data of 4 healthy volunteers given 1 mg, entered as one profile (n = 1), 17 samples over 4 h.
- Salt or base not stated for any dose. Ethics approval not required (secondary data).

## Model and parameters
Two compartments with first-order absorption, a lag time and a bioavailability per formulation relative to IV (p.1051-1052). Allometric standardisation to 70 kg: exponent 0.75 for clearances, 1 for volumes, 0.25 for half-times (p.1052). BSV exponential; residual error additive plus proportional. Two compartments beat one (dOBJ 607.623). Table 1 (PDF p.4, printed 1053), from the rendered page:

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: mean arterial pressure (non-invasive in the children). Hyperbolic Emax with no Hill coefficient: MAP = E0 + EMAX x Cp/(EC50 + Cp).
- EC50 10.3 ug/L (ng/mL), the same for adults and children. EMAX 51 mmHg in adults and x 0.498 (about 25 mmHg) in children. E0 85.3 mmHg, x 0.714 in children (Table 1).
- **ke0: not estimable.** An effect compartment was tried and dropped; concentration was linked directly to MAP (p.1052). No t1/2keo or tPeak reported.
- Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Weight (allometry, as above). Age group: FE0 and FEMX scale E0 and EMAX in children. Formulation sets Tabs, Tlag and F.

## Caveats
- Sparse paediatric sampling (to 20 min) and high shrinkage (up to 100%). BSV of nasal F is 354%.
- The IV information is a single pooled mean profile from 4 adults, so CL, V1, Q and V2 rest on extravascular data and F estimated jointly. The 7-h terminal phase carries very little area (derived above).
- Children were anaesthetised (inhalational agents may blunt the response); the adults had eye surgery with a viscous solution.
- The abstract's ophthalmic F values are swapped relative to Table 1, and Table 3 repeats Table 2's numbers (see open_questions).
- Salt or base not stated.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No stanpumpR model uses this paper's parameters. The implemented model is Hengstmann 1982 (V1 41.1 L, V2 300 L, CL 2.09 L/min, Q 3.74 L/min, `R/drugs_phenylephrine.R:37`).
- **7-hour terminal half-life** (`R/drugs_phenylephrine.R:47`; registry): derived 7.15 h from Table 1. **Matches.** The registry's CL 160 L/h, V1 13.3 L, Q 25.3 L/h and V2 225 L at 70 kg also **match** Table 1.
- **"Built mostly from children" / "a few adult intravenous subjects"** (`R/drugs_phenylephrine.R:48`; help :11; registry population "pooled children and 4 adult volunteers given IV"): **discrepancy (description).** The pool was 52 children plus 20 adult ophthalmic, 28 adult oral and one pooled IV profile from 4 adults. Children are about half of the subjects, and the oral and ophthalmic adults are not mentioned.
- **"The early, clinically important part of the curve is similar"** (help :11): **discrepancy.** For the same 1 mg IV bolus at 70 kg (derived), Anderson's V1 is 13.3 L against Hengstmann's 41.1 L:
  - At 0-2 min: 75 vs 24 ng/mL (Anderson about 3 times higher).
  - At 10 min: 7.4 vs 6.4 ng/mL (they cross).
  - At 20 min: 0.8 vs 2.4 ng/mL (Anderson about 3 times lower).
  - At 60 min: 0.08 vs 1.07 ng/mL.

  The early curve differs most. The "longer terminal half-life" is true but has almost no amplitude.
- **Registry "prints no allometric exponents or dose basis"**: the full text gives the exponents (0.75 for clearances, 1 for volumes, 0.25 for half-times; p.1052). The dose basis (salt) is indeed not stated. `evidence_read` "abstract" and readiness "needs_full_text" are now out of date.
- **IV source**: Anderson describes Hengstmann's IV dose as 1 mg with 17 samples over 4 h. The library's header gives 0.84 +/- 0.17 mg sampled to 8 h. That is a difference in how Anderson summarised the source; the library's model does not depend on it.
- **tPeak**: the library's 0.7 min (Schwinn 1989) is consistent with this paper, which found the effect immediate and ke0 not estimable. No value here to compare.
- **No pressure drawn** (`R/drugs_phenylephrine.R:73`, MEAC 0). This paper does give a MAP Emax model (EC50 10.3 ng/mL), which the library does not use. That is a scope note, not a discrepancy.
