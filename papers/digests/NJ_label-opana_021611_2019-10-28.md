# OPANA tablet label 2019: oral immediate-release oxymorphone

## Study
Prescribing information. PK from unnamed healthy-volunteer studies; n and assay not given.

Dosing (2.2): opioid-naive start 10 to 20 mg every 4 to 6 h as needed; do not start above 20 mg. Conversion from parenteral oxymorphone: 10 times the total daily parenteral dose, in 4 or 6 divided doses ("Given OPANA's absolute oral bioavailability of approximately 10%"); example, 4 mg/day IM is about 10 mg orally four times daily. Mild hepatic or CLcr < 50 mL/min: start at the lowest dose (e.g. 5 mg). Contraindicated in moderate or severe hepatic impairment. Take at least 1 h before or 2 h after eating.

Multiple-dose 5 mg: Cmax 1.73 +/- 0.62, AUC 4.63 +/- 1.49; 20 mg: 7.33 +/- 2.93, 21.10 +/- 7.59. Steady state after 3 days; dose proportional 5-20 mg. **No Tmax reported.** Food (40 mg, high-fat meal): Cmax and AUC up about 38%. Metabolites as in the injection label (3-glucuronide AUC about 90-fold parent; 6-OH-oxymorphone AUC about 70% of parent single-dose).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- MEAC: no value; 12.2 says the minimum effective analgesic concentration varies widely and may increase with tolerance.
- No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis.
- Potency ratio: oral = 10 x parenteral (2.2), derived from F about 10%. No cross-drug equianalgesic ratio (prescribers referred to published tables).

## Covariates
Oral ER studies: hepatic bioavailability 1.6-fold (mild), 3.7-fold (moderate), 12.2-fold (one severe); renal +26%, +57%, +65% (mild, moderate, severe; n = 8 each); elderly about 40% higher levels; sex effect gone after weight adjustment.

## Caveats
Table 3 is laid out in the text layer with the half-life column separated from its rows; values assigned by order (three single-dose t1/2 values, then NA for multiple dose). Single-dose t1/2 of 7-9 h reflects the long terminal phase seen orally, which is far longer than the 1.3 h IV half-life the repo carries from the PDR. No Tmax.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- F 0.10 (drugs_oxymorphone.R:37, :173; oxymorphone.md:7): **matches** (12.3 and 2.2; "approximately 10%"). The code calls it a round scenario figure, which is fair.
- Cmax 1.93 ng/mL and AUC 9.10 ng.h/mL after 10 mg (drugs_oxymorphone.R:28, :41, attributed to Adams and Ahdieh): **match** this label's Table 3 exactly, so the label is an equal source for the ka calibration. Tmax: label gives none, consistent with the code's "no time to peak was available" (line 44).
- **Discrepancy (model behaviour)**: the label's single-dose oral half-life is 7.25-9.43 h (Table 3) and 9-11 h (12.3 text). The one-compartment model's terminal half-life is 1.25 h (derived: ln2 x 215.6 L / 2.0 L/min = 75 min), and ka (0.936/h) cannot create a slow tail. So the model drops oral oxymorphone concentrations much faster than the label reports after the peak, and underpredicts multiple-dose accumulation (label 10 mg q6h steady-state Cmax 3.51 versus 1.93 single-dose, about 1.8-fold; a 1.25 h half-life with 6 h dosing gives almost none). Not mentioned in R/drugs_oxymorphone.R or the help page.
