# Ausems 1988: computer-assisted alfentanil infusion vs intermittent boluses with N2O

## Study
Randomized comparison, University of Leiden (with Hug from Emory and Stanski from Stanford / Palo Alto VA on
sabbatical; supported in part by Janssen). 20 women, ASA I-II, lower abdominal gynaecological surgery, 10 per group
(PDF p2, printed p852). Table 1, mean +/- SD (no significant differences): infusion group age 41 +/- 11 yr, weight
64 +/- 6 kg, anaesthesia 220 +/- 79 min, total alfentanil 30 +/- 8 mg; bolus group 36 +/- 7 yr, 67 +/- 10 kg,
204 +/- 72 min, 35 +/- 12 mg. Premedication diazepam 10 mg PO, atropine 0.5 mg IM; pancuronium 20 ug/kg
pretreatment; 66% N2O throughout; succinylcholine 1 mg/kg for intubation; ventilation to end-tidal CO2 4-5%.

- **Bolus group:** alfentanil 10 mg over exactly 2 min (extra 4 mg if not unconscious at 1 min), then 1 mg (or
  2 mg for an extreme or multiple response) for each sign of inadequate anaesthesia.
- **Infusion group:** TIAC (Titration of Intravenous Agents by Computer, Janssen Scientific Instruments, on a
  Hewlett-Packard 86) running a BET scheme from two-compartment alfentanil data (analytic solutions per Schwilden).
  Targets from the authors' earlier Cp50 study: 475 ng/mL for intubation (600 if still conscious at 1 min), 280
  for skin incision, 310 before opening the peritoneum, then +50 or +100 ng/mL per response, -50 ng/mL after 15
  min without response, below 200 ng/mL near the end.
- Response criteria: SBP more than 15 mmHg above "normal", HR above 90, somatic responses, other autonomic signs.
  Arterial plasma alfentanil by gas chromatography. Naloxone 0.04 mg every 2 min if adequate spontaneous
  ventilation was not regained within 10 min after N2O. Cp50s by logistic regression (ELSFIT).

## Model and parameters
No new PK model; the infusion used published two-compartment data (refs 7, 12; values not printed). Prediction
error (measured minus predicted) of TIAC: whole-group mean -64 +/- 40 ng/mL (overprediction), individual means from
4 +/- 44 to -112 +/- 37 ng/mL, significant in 8 of 10 patients (PDF p7, printed p857); range of concentrations
150-600 ng/mL (abstract).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Opioid audit: no MEAC, no ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak, no potency ratio. The concentration-effect measures are the
  plasma Cp50s above (50% probability of suppressing a response to each stimulus, with 66% N2O, except
  spontaneous ventilation without N2O), arterial plasma, total alfentanil by GC. Individual intra-abdominal
  curves were steep (Cp10 to Cp90 usually under 100 ng/mL) but positioned with a CV of about 40% between patients.
- Outcomes. Induction (Table 2): rigidity 3/10 vs 8/10 (P = 0.1), hypotension 1/10 vs 3/10, bradycardia 0/10 vs
  1/10. Responses (Table 3, median per patient): total 8 (5-14) vs 15 (10-24), SBP 5 vs 13, HR 1 vs 5 (all P < 0.05);
  muscular 3 vs 4, other autonomic 0 vs 0 (not significant). Time with SBP above normal + 15 (Table 4) 6.1 +/- 2.1% vs
  11.8 +/- 2.8%; HR above 90, 2.2 +/- 2.6% vs 5.6 +/- 4.4% (P < 0.05). Each hemodynamic episode was shorter with
  boluses (Table 5: SBP 1.8 vs 2.8 min). Emergence (Table 6): alfentanil at skin closure 178 +/- 33 vs 245 +/- 89 and
  10 min after N2O 163 +/- 35 vs 229 +/- 74 ng/mL; naloxone needed 0/10 vs 5/10 (P < 0.05); PaCO2 at 1 h 40 vs 40
  mmHg. No recall. Total dose 30 vs 35 mg, not significant.

## Covariates
None modelled.

## Caveats
- Small groups; investigators not blinded (strict response criteria used instead); women only, gynaecological
  surgery.
- TIAC overpredicted concentrations in 8 of 10 patients; the authors suggest newer population kinetics might do
  better.
- Side-effect differences mostly not significant; the authors say larger groups would be needed.
