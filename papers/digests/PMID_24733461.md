# Elkomy 2014: cefazolin in parturients at caesarean delivery

## Study
- Prospective open-label, Stanford (Lucile Packard). 20 healthy women having elective caesarean delivery under epidural, and 19 of their neonates (PDF p. 2-3).
- Table 1 (rendered): age 32.5 (SD 5.3; 23-43) y; weight at delivery 79.3 (8.26; 62-93) kg; height 162 (6.14) cm; gestation 39.1 (0.855; 36.4-40.4) weeks; dose-to-delivery 1.10 (0.462; 0.20-2.28) h; 6 Hispanic / 14 not; neonates 3.47 (0.5) kg, 10 male / 10 female.
- Cefazolin 1 g IV within 1 h before incision. Maternal samples before, 10 and 25 min after the dose, at delivery, and 2 h and 6 h after delivery; one umbilical venous sample at delivery; one neonatal dried blood spot at 24 h. 98 maternal, 19 cord, 19 neonatal concentrations; 3 below LLOQ excluded.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS, total cefazolin; plasma range 82.3-20,000 ng/mL, DBS 27.4-20,000 ng/mL; intraday CV 3.8-5.5%, interday 7.8-14.8%. No free concentrations or albumin measured.
- NONMEM VII, FOCE-I, ADVAN6; nonparametric bootstrap (1000, stratified by delivery time); VPC.

## Model and parameters
One-compartment maternal model with clearance switched at delivery (CLpreg before, CLpostpreg after); cord as an effect compartment linked by Keq; after delivery the effect compartment becomes the neonate with its own elimination KN0. A two-compartment maternal model had a slightly lower AIC (612 vs 615) but %SE > 70 for some parameters. A pregnancy-specific V worsened the AIC by 3; a pregnancy-specific CL improved it by 12 (PDF p. 4). Table 2 (rendered):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured. Simulation target: free cefazolin >= 8 mg/L (the CLSI 2011 susceptible breakpoint for Gram-positive bacteria) throughout an assumed 1-h operation, in maternal and cord blood, with binding fixed at 75% (pregnant) and 49% (neonate) from the literature (p. 3). Joint maternal-and-cord attainment was highest, 42%, for 2 g given 60 min before surgery; maximum cord attainment 13%, 37% and 58% for 1, 1.5 and 2 g (p. 5). Neonatal free concentration stayed above 8 mg/L for 6-10 h; neonatal AUC0-36 197-436 mg.h/L.

## Covariates
Delivery (the clearance switch) only. No weight or renal term.

## Caveats
- The "after pregnancy" clearance is from samples 2 and 6 h after a caesarean, not a non-pregnant baseline. The authors attribute the fall partly to the fall in renal blood flow immediately after delivery and partly to surgery (blood loss, pain, anaesthesia); they exclude albumin as the cause (Discussion, p. 6-7).
- Volume did not differ before and after delivery; the authors state they could not establish a pregnancy-related change in V, as Philipson found (p. 7).
- One cord and one neonatal sample per pair; binding assumed, not measured; no GFR, renal blood flow or blood loss measured (limitations, p. 8).
- Abstract unit typo: V is "9.44 liters/h" in the abstract and 9.44 L in Table 2 and the Discussion (9.4 L, larger than plasma volume, smaller than extracellular fluid).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- docs/pregnancy.md:369-371 and registry :49: 20 parturients, one-compartment, V 9.44 L, CL 7.18 vs 4.12 L/h, ratio 1.74; registry's caveat that the comparator is hours postpartum: **matches** Table 2 and the sampling schedule.
- drugs_cefazolin.R:68-72: the repo quotes Ansari, not Elkomy, so the quotation itself **cannot verify** here. On the substance, Elkomy supports the clearance half (higher clearance before delivery, renal mechanism discussed) but not the volume half: a pregnancy-specific V worsened the fit, and the authors report no pregnancy change in V. Elkomy's dosing conclusion is 2 g within 1 h of incision, redosed every 2 h in a longer operation in their simulation, not a 3- to 6-hour interval. So the header's "so the simulated peak in a parturient is too high" rests on a volume increase this source did not find (Philipson 1987 also found V unchanged; Allegaert 2009 reported 12.04 L against a non-pregnant literature 6.94 L). Flagged for the repo's attention; it does not change any number the code applies.
- Not used by the repo: the CL 4.12 L/h hours after caesarean is close to the healthy-volunteer CL of the cefazolin label (4.03 L/h).
