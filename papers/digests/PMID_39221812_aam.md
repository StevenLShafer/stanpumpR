# Caritis 2025: buprenorphine PK and PD in pregnancy and postpartum

## Study
Prospective single-centre cohort (Magee Womens Hospital, Pittsburgh; NCT02863601; May 2017 to July 2021), PDF p1-4.
- 22 pregnant people with opioid use disorder on stable sublingual buprenorphine (at least 1 week), 2-8 mg every 6 h (QID) or
  every 8 h (TID), frequency chosen by the patient. 33 studies (1-3 per person); 5 excluded for non-adherence (3 had
  pre-dose levels suggesting a dose just before the study, 2 no rise suggesting the tablet was swallowed or removed),
  leaving 28 analysed (PDF p3).
- Epochs: first half of pregnancy, studied at 15.6 +/- 2.9 weeks (14 studies; 10 TID, 4 QID); second half, 30.1 +/- 6.4
  weeks (7 studies; 3 TID, 4 QID); postpartum, 5.6 +/- 1.0 weeks after delivery (Table 1 lists 12 studies, the text says
  12 participants, all QID; Figure 1 analyses n = 7). PDF p3, p11, p14.
- Table 1 (PDF p14, rendered): age 29 +/- 5.2 y, BMI 27.2 +/- 5.3; 21 non-Hispanic white, 1 bi-racial; all 22 smoked.
  Average daily dose 13.2 +/- 4.5 mg (first half), 17.7 +/- 4.5 mg (second half), 16 +/- 0 mg (postpartum). Weight not given.
- Duration of buprenorphine before the study: 37 (SD 14.7), 88 (53.9) and 142 (59.3) days for the three epochs (PDF p3).
- Morning dose withheld; baseline sample, then the usual dose sublingually under supervision; 8-10 blood samples over one
  dosing interval (6 or 8 h). Pupil diameter (mean of three readings, low light), COWS (NIDA) and a 0-10 craving
  Likert score repeated through the interval (PDF p3-4).
- Assay: plasma buprenorphine (total) by UHPLC-MS/MS, LLOQ 0.05 ng/mL, inter- and intraday CV under 10% (PDF p4).
- Analysis: noncompartmental. Concentrations dose-normalised by dividing by the dose taken at the start of the study;
  individual AUCs compared by one-way ANOVA. Planned 40 studies (to detect a 50% lower AUC); enrolment fell short
  because three other centres withdrew (PDF p4). No compartmental model.

## Model and parameters
No PK model is fitted. Dose-normalised AUC values (mean +/- SD), PDF p1 abstract and p5 Results (rendered page 5):

| Group | n (studies) | Dose-normalised AUC | p vs postpartum |
|---|---|---|---|
| All pregnancy | 21 | 155 +/- 52 | 0.036 |
| First half | 14 | not given numerically | 0.187 |
| Second half | 7 | 119 +/- 34 | 0.014 |
| Postpartum | 7 | 218 +/- 113 | - |

Units: the abstract prints ng*min/ml and the Results ng*ml/min (PDF p5). Since Figure 1 plots concentration/dose over
minutes, the unit is ng.min/mL per mg dose; "ng*ml/min" is a typographical slip (derived: postpartum Figure 1a mean of
about 0.6 x 360 min = 216).

Derived ratios: 218/155 = 1.41; 218/119 = 1.83; the paper states pregnant concentrations about 29% lower
(1 - 155/218 = 0.29) and second-half AUC about 45% lower (1 - 119/218 = 0.45) (PDF p6).

Other observations: raw (not dose-normalised) troughs 1.1 +/- 0.7 ng/mL at the start and 1.2 +/- 0.8 ng/mL at the end of
a dosing interval, no difference by TID or QID; raw peaks 2.5-3.2 ng/mL (PDF p1, p5). Total daily dose 16.0 +/- 0 mg on
QID and 13.8 +/- 6.2 mg on TID (PDF p6). No Cmax, tmax, half-life or clearance tabulated. Figure 1 (rendered, PDF p11):
dose-normalised peak at about 60 min in all groups (about 1.0 postpartum, 0.6 pregnant overall).

Derived apparent clearance (my computation, assuming the AUC spans the full dosing interval at steady state and that
"dose" is in mg): CL/F = 1 mg / AUC = 1e6 / 218 = 4590 mL/min (275 L/h) postpartum and 1e6 / 119 = 8400 mL/min (504 L/h)
in the second half of pregnancy.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC / minimum effective concentration:** no analgesic MEAC. The paper uses about 1 ng/mL total plasma buprenorphine
  (UHPLC-MS/MS) as the concentration at which pupil diameter, COWS and craving returned to baseline in people with OUD
  on maintenance, i.e. a withdrawal/craving-suppression threshold, not analgesia (PDF p1, p5-7). It is not fitted: it is
  the observed trough (1.1-1.2 ng/mL) at which mild withdrawal reappeared; the paper attributes the 1 ng/mL minimal
  therapeutic concentration in non-pregnant people to Greenwald 2014 (its ref 18; PDF p2, p6).
- **ke0 / t1/2ke0 / tPeak:** none estimated. Hysteresis observed: the pupil-diameter nadir lagged the plasma peak by
  30-60 min after a sublingual dose; COWS and craving changed with a similar 30-60 min delay (PDF p5, p7). PD time-courses
  were fitted with third-order polynomials (R2 0.70-0.91), not a PK-PD model (PDF p4-5).
- **End points:** pupil diameter (objective), COWS, craving (0-10). Figure 2 subsets exclude studies with a baseline
  score of 0 (n = 13, 7, 7; PDF p12).
- **Potency / equianalgesic ratio:** none.

## Covariates
Pregnancy epoch only (first half, second half, postpartum), as group comparisons. Dosing frequency (TID vs QID) did not
change troughs or PD return. No weight, age or genotype analysis.

## Caveats
- Small n (7 studies in each of the second-half and postpartum groups) and large SD (postpartum CV about 52%); the 1.83
  ratio is a ratio of group means with wide uncertainty.
- Groups compared by unpaired one-way ANOVA; the paper does not say which studies were in the same women.
- Comparator is postpartum at 5.6 weeks, not non-pregnant women; postpartum physiology may not have fully returned.
- Doses at the start ranged 2-8 mg; sublingual F is dose-dependent, so dose normalisation is not exact. F was not measured;
  the AUC difference is in apparent clearance (CL/F).
- AUC interval not stated (Figure 1 stops at 360 min while TID intervals were 480 min).
- AUC unit printed two ways (ng*min/ml and ng*ml/min).
- Table 1 lists 12 postpartum studies but the analyses use n = 7; the reduction is not explained beyond the 5 exclusions.
- All participants smoked; nearly all non-Hispanic white.
- Author manuscript: numbers may change at proof.
