# Sato 2013: brain H1 receptor occupancy by mirtazapine (PET)

## Study
- 5 healthy Japanese men, 22.3 (2.3) y, 61.7 (0.7) kg. Double-blind randomized crossover, >= 7 days washout: placebo, fluvoxamine 25 mg, mirtazapine 15 mg (single oral doses, lowest Japanese prescribed doses).
- [11C]doxepin PET ~80 min after the second oral administration, timed to known Tmax (mirtazapine 1.1 h); static 70-85/90 min scan; cerebellum as reference; H1RO = (BPR placebo - BPR drug)/BPR placebo x 100%.
- Plasma mirtazapine by LC/MS/MS at 0, 60, 120, 180 min. Subjective sleepiness by line analogue rating scale.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Mean plasma mirtazapine (Table 2, as aligned): 13.23 ng/mL at 60 min, 21.09 at 120 min, 24.82 at 180 min; AUC0-180 46.73 (unit not legible in the text layer).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: brain histamine H1 receptor occupancy by PET. Mirtazapine 15 mg: mean H1RO > 80% in DLPFC, OFC, PC, LTC, OC, ACG and PCG (text); Table 1 regional means 67.9-94.4% in cortex/insula (e.g., inferior DLPFC 90.3%, LTC 88.5%, OC 94.4%, medial temporal 70.5%), striatum 24.7%, thalamus 23.3%. Abstract: 80-90% in neocortex. Fluvoxamine 25 mg: about 0%.
- H1RO correlated with mirtazapine AUC0-180 (r = 0.94, P = 0.0184) and with sleepiness at 120 min (r = 0.87) and 180 min (r = 0.85).
- **No EC50, Emax, ke0 or concentration-occupancy model is reported.** Authors note 5-HT2A/2C antagonism may also promote sleep. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n = 5, single low dose, one time point per scan, healthy young men; occupancy assumes unchanged receptor density.
