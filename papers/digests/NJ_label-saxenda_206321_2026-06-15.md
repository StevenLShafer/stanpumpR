Other facts: exposure increases proportionally from 0.6 to 3 mg; exposure similar at upper arm, abdomen and thigh. Metabolism like large proteins, no specific organ; after [3H]-liraglutide no intact drug in urine or feces, with 6% (urine) and 5% (feces) of radioactivity as metabolites, mostly in the first 6-8 days. Dosing (section 2.2): start 0.6 mg daily for a week, then 1.2, 1.8, 2.4 mg at weekly steps, maintenance 3 mg daily; 2.4 mg allowed in children who cannot tolerate 3 mg; re-start at 0.6 mg if more than 3 days missed. Dose forms: 6 mg/mL, 3 mL pen. Molecular weight 3751.2 Da (section 11).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Mechanism: GLP-1 receptor agonist; lowers body weight through reduced calorie intake, glucose-dependent insulin rise and glucagon fall, delayed gastric emptying (12.2, PDF p22). No QTc prolongation at steady-state doses up to 1.8 mg; Cmax at 3 mg in obese subjects similar to that in the QTc study (12.2). Heart rate rises 2-3 bpm (4-9 bpm on 24 h monitoring) vs placebo (5.5). No ke0, effect-site, EC50, MEAC or therapeutic concentration range is given. Endogenous GLP-1 half-life is 1.5-2 min versus 13 h for liraglutide (12.1). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Section 12.3 (PDF p23):
- Sex: females have 24% lower weight-adjusted clearance than males (population PK).
- Body weight: significant; exposure falls as weight rises; population studied 60-234 kg. No equation or exponent given.
- Age: no effect (healthy elderly 65-83 y; population analyses 18-82 y).
- Race and ethnicity: no effect (White, Black or African American, Asian, Hispanic and non-Hispanic).
- Pediatric 12-17 y (n=134): exposure similar to adults with obesity.
- Renal impairment (single dose; mild CrCl 50-80 mL/min to severe <30, and ESRD on dialysis): AUC lower by 35% (mild), 19% (moderate), 29% (severe), 30% (ESRD) than in healthy subjects.
- Hepatic impairment (single dose, Child-Pugh 5-6 to >9): AUC lower by 11% (mild), 14% (moderate), 42% (severe) than in healthy subjects.
- Drug interactions (steady state 1.8 mg): the Cmax of co-administered oral drugs fell (ethinylestradiol 12%, levonorgestrel 13%, digoxin 31%, lisinopril 27%, atorvastatin 38%, acetaminophen 31%) with Tmax delays, from delayed gastric emptying; griseofulvin Cmax rose 37%.

## Caveats
Label gives means without variability except the 11% intra-subject CV; no population, dose or sampling design for tmax, half-life or clearance; no sex or weight equations. Hepatic and renal numbers are single-dose studies in other populations and are not comparable with the population-model covariate effects.
