# Cortínez 2005: remifentanil vs fentanyl TCI EC50 for analgesia during ESWL

## Study
- Randomised, 44 adults (ASA I-II, age 18-60, BMI <=30), shock-wave lithotripsy, 22 per group (Table 2: age 36±8 vs 40±9 y; weight 72±11 vs 75±11 kg; 16 M/6 F each; ESWL 50±20 vs 58±20 min; mean ± SD).
- Group R remifentanil, Group F fentanyl, by effect-site TCI in STANPUMP (Fresenius Pilot Anaesthesia 2 pump). Minto 1997 PK for remifentanil, McClain & Hug 1980 PK for fentanyl (p. 57). Drug 50 ug/mL, pump cap 50 mL/h.
- Start effect-site targets 1.5 (R) and 2 (F) ng/mL; Dixon up-and-down, steps of 0.5 ng/mL. Positive response = verbal analogue scale (0-10) <3 at the highest lithotriptor energy (20 kV). No drug assay.
- Mean infusion rate over the procedure 0.16±0.07 ug/kg/min (R), 0.15±0.05 (F) (p. 58).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **EC50, effect-site, analgesia** (VAS <3 at maximal lithotripsy stimulus; Dixon up-and-down; computed effect-site concentration from the TCI model, not measured): remifentanil 2.8 ng/mL (95% CI 1.8-3.7); fentanyl 2.9 ng/mL (95% CI 1.7-4.1). Not significantly different (p. 58, Fig 1). This is a 50% response concentration for a procedural stimulus, NOT a MEAC (no post-surgical minimum effective/analgesic concentration reported). No assay, so total/free and blood/plasma are not applicable.
- **Respiration**: logistic regression of RR <10 on effect-site concentration (RR from nasal CO2, 1 min before ESWL; Fig 2). At the analgesic EC50 the predicted probability of RR <10 was 4% (95% CI 0-57%) remifentanil, 56% (13-92%) fentanyl (p. 58).
- **t½ke0**: remifentanil 1.16 min, fentanyl 4.71 min (p. 59). Used to explain observed time to reach target: ~2 min (remifentanil) vs ~6 min (fentanyl). No ke0 or tPeak was estimated in this study; hysteresis end point not measured.
- Potency: authors call the two drugs similarly potent on this end point (EC50 ratio fentanyl/remifentanil = 2.9/2.8 = 1.04, derived). Not a general equianalgesic ratio.
- Complications (p. 58-59): during ESWL hypoxaemia 6 (27%) R vs 13 (59%) F; nausea 0 vs 6 (27%)*; vomiting 0 vs 3 (14%); sedation score 0-1-2: 11-9-2 vs 1-9-12*; sevoflurane rescue 0 vs 4 (Table 3). First recovery hour: hypoxaemia 1 (4.5%) vs 8 (36%)*; nausea 3 (14%) vs 9 (41%); vomiting 0 vs 9 (41%)*; pain 7 (32%) vs 2 (9%); sedation 0-1 21-1 vs 13-9* (Table 4). *P<0.05 vs R.

## Caveats
- High between-patient variability; wide CIs; only 44 patients. Authors cite simulation work showing up-and-down errors of 25% or more per individual, mitigated by at least six crossovers.
- Fentanyl EC50 may be underestimated by initial overshoot of McClain & Hug TCI (authors, p. 59); no concentrations measured.
- Mixed sevoflurane in 4 fentanyl patients. Two orders of text columns interleave in the text layer; table numbers above were read from the page image.
