# Weatherley 2009: maraviroc IV disposition, NAUC dose non-linearity and mass-balance first-pass model

## Study
Three linked analyses of Pfizer phase 1 data (Table 1, PDF p4 / printed p358; studies, n and demographics confirmed on the rendered page):
1. **IV disposition** (study A4001009): 20 healthy males, 22–42 y, 65–93 kg, fasted; maraviroc 3, 10 or 30 mg IV over 1 h
   (the same study also gave 100 mg oral tablet vs 30 mg IV for absolute bioavailability). 473 plasma observations
   (p5 / printed 357). NONMEM V level 1.1, FOCE-INTERACTION, ADVAN7; log-transformed data; exponential IIV; 2-, 3- and
   4-compartment models compared; 100-dataset predictive check and 1000-run bootstrap.
2. **Non-compartmental NAUC analysis**: individual dose-normalised AUC (AUC/dose) from 134 healthy young males and females
   across five single-centre studies (A4001001, -1003, -1004, -1002, -1019; oral solution and tablet, 1–1200 mg, fed and
   fasted, single and multiple QD/BID dosing). Sigmoid Emax model of NAUC vs dose (Equation 1, p5) with IIV on NAUCmax only.
3. **Mass balance** (A4001010): 3 males, 45–53 y, 82–85 kg, 300 mg oral [14C]-maraviroc solution; algebraic model in S-PLUS
   apportioning absorption, first-pass extraction, renal, metabolic and secreted fractions (Appendix B1/B2).

Assay: solid-phase extraction then LC-MS/MS, LLOQ 0.5 ng/mL, in all studies (p5 / printed 357). Plasma concentrations.
Sponsor: Pfizer (authors Pfizer consultant/employee).

**NAUC model** (Equation 1): NAUC = NAUC0 + (NAUCmax − NAUC0)·Dose^γ/(ED50^γ + Dose^γ); reference = single dose, fasted
solution. No parameter table for this model is printed; the discussion (p9 / printed 363) gives the food effect as
an ED50 rise from 59.7 mg to 365 mg (≈5-fold). Predicted absorption (assuming Emax = 100% absorbed): 83.8% for 300 mg
fasted solution, 57.45% for 100 mg fasted tablet (75.4% and 51.7% if maximum absorption is only 90%) (p8 / printed 362).

**Mass balance** (Figure 3 p6, Figure 8 and Table 4 p10, Appendix B1–B2): CL 48 L/h (from the IV model), CLR assumed

extraction E 60.2% of absorbed, bioavailability B 33.3%; rR 0.25, rM 0.65 (0.649), rS 0.10 (0.101). Hepatic blood flow
QH,B = CLH/(rB·E) = 36/(0.59 × 0.602) = 101 L/h (Equation 3, p6). Predicted 100 mg tablet bioavailability 22.9% vs
observed 23.1% (95% CI 19.2–27.8) in A4001009. For the later population model, hepatic plasma flow QH fixed to 59.59 L/h
(= 101 × 0.59), CLR fixed 12 L/h, F = FABS × FHEP (Equations 4–6, p11 / printed 365).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No concentration–effect data, no ke0, no target concentration. Not an opioid paper. (Exposure–efficacy work is
cited as conference abstracts, refs 14–16.)

## Covariates
IV analysis: dose tested as a categorical covariate on CL; no effect. NAUC analysis: food (main effect, on ED50),
formulation (tablet constrained ≤ solution), single vs QD/BID multiple dosing; constraints that tablet NAUC ≤ solution
and fed ≤ fasted. Sex, weight and age not modelled. CYP3A4/Pgp interactions are discussed as acting on ED50 of FABS
(Pgp) and on EH (CYP3A4) in the later model, not estimated here.

## Caveats
- IV data from 20 healthy young males only; three-subject mass-balance study; healthy volunteers throughout.
- Mass-balance results rest on an assumed CLR/CL of 0.25 and on NAUCmax = 100% absorption; rS is very sensitive to the
  absorption assumption (10.1% → 2.0%), as the authors state.
- All metabolism is assigned to the liver; gut-wall CYP3A4 is not separated (authors' stated limitation).
- The NAUC model is over-parameterised (authors: high standard errors); its estimates are not tabulated here.
- For oral data the later model collapses disposition to two compartments with the 12 h terminal phase.
