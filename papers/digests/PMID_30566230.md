# Wang 2019: CPX-351 population PK and exposure-response

## Study
Pooled population PK of CPX-351 (Vyxeos), a liposome holding cytarabine and daunorubicin at a fixed 5:1 molar ratio, from three trials: Study 101 (phase 1 dose escalation, n=38), Study 206 (phase 2, n=26), Study 301 (phase 3, n=131); N=195 patients, 2176 samples (PDF p5, Table 1; p6). Median age 67 y (24-81), weight 79.8 kg (38.9-156.5), BSA 1.94 m2 (1.26-2.80); 61.0% male. Renal function: 83 normal, 83 mild, 28 moderate, 1 severe impairment; 179 had bilirubin <1.2 mg/dL, 16 had 1.2-3 mg/dL. 157 (80.5%) received 100-101 units/m2 (daunorubicin 44 mg/m2 + cytarabine 100 mg/m2) on days 1, 3, 5 as 90-min infusions. Assay: HPLC-MS/MS after liposome rupture, so it measures TOTAL drug (encapsulated plus free) in plasma; range 1000-100 000 ng/mL stated in Methods, LLOQ 5 ng/mL stated in Results. NONMEM 7.3 (SAEM/IMP). The exposure-response work used model AUC in Study 301 (n=130 on CPX-351 vs 151 on 7+3) and the pooled safety set (n=156).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, linear elimination, separate models for total cytarabine and total daunorubicin; BSA covariate with estimated exponents; log-normal BSV; log-additive error. Typical patient BSA 1.95 m2, bilirubin 0.60 mg/dL (cytarabine), lyophilised formulation (daunorubicin). Units: L and L/h, absolute (not per kg), time in hours. Source: Table 2, PDF p6 (printed 753).

Population AUCtau (day 5, 48 h, linear trapezoid) by renal and bilirubin group is in Table 3 (PDF p9); the text says mean AUC for moderate renal impairment was 20% (cytarabine) and 18% (daunorubicin) above normal renal function, and 19% and 15% higher for bilirubin <1.2 than 1.2-3 mg/dL, all within the CV of 33-55%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK-PD effect-site model: no ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, MEAC or potency ratio is reported (this is not an opioid paper). Exposure-response used AUC quartiles of cytarabine (nearly identical for daunorubicin). Multivariate hazard ratios vs 7+3 for overall survival (Q1-Q4) 0.482, 0.710, 0.550, 0.329; median survival 181 d on 7+3 vs 284, 204, 323, 564 d (text, PDF p10); complete remission 26.5% on 7+3 vs 30.3, 37.5, 30.3, 59.4%. Grade 3-5 adverse events 90.7% (7+3) vs 87.2, 92.3, 94.9, 97.4%. No concentration range for therapeutic or toxic effect is given.

## Covariates
BSA (power, in every parameter), bilirubin on CL (small exponents 0.197 and 0.0829), formulation (frozen vs lyophilised) on daunorubicin only. Dose, age, sex, race, weight, creatinine clearance, AST/ALT and WBC were tested and not retained or not clinically significant. Authors conclude no dose adjustment for mild or moderate renal impairment or bilirubin up to 3 mg/dL; severe renal impairment (n=1) and bilirubin >3 were not assessable.

## Caveats
Total (mostly liposome-bound, >99% encapsulated per the authors' unpublished data) drug, so CL and volumes describe the liposome, not free drug. Small Vc (about 4-5 L) means the liposomes stay largely intravascular. BSV on Q and Vp not estimated. Exposure-response is observational (ECOG, platelets, hemoglobin unbalanced across quartiles; higher exposure may track fitter patients). The authors mention a 1-compartment model from an earlier Study 101 analysis.
