# Kim 2019: metoprolol ER formulation variability, PBPK + popPK/PD on exercise heart rate

## Study
Simulation study funded by FDA (grant 1U1FD005210). Three linked steps (Fig. 1, PDF p3, printed 1268):
1. In vitro dissolution of Toprol XL 50 and 200 mg simulated in DDDPlus, varying HPMC content (−5, −10, −15%) or
   the Korsmeyer-Peppas release rate constant K (+20, +40, +60%); similarity by f2.
2. In vivo absorption and PK in a virtual population of 200 subjects with a previously published GastroPlus 9.5
   physiologically based absorption model (Basu et al., ref 5), whose disposition is 2-compartment; single-dose
   bioequivalence on Cmax and AUC0-48 (90% CI within 80-125%).
3. A new population PK/PD model fitted in NONMEM 7.3 to digitised literature data (Table S1, refs 24-34), driven by
   the simulated profiles of 50 and 200 mg once daily for 5 days (500 replicates).
No new subjects were dosed; no assay. Concentrations are plasma metoprolol in nmol/L (Figs 3-4).

## Model and parameters
Final popPK/PD: 2-compartment, first-order absorption, Emax model on percentage reduction in exercise-induced
heart rate (ΔEIHR) (PDF p4, printed 1269). **No parameter values are given in the main text**; they are in
Supplemental Material 1 (Figures S1.2-S1.7, Table S1-2), not in the archived file.

Dissolution (Table 1, PDF p3, confirmed on page image): reference K 0.006 h⁻¹ (50 mg, HPMC 200 mg) and 0.018 h⁻¹
(200 mg, HPMC 50 mg); 24-h dissolution 90% and 92%. f2 for +20% K: 57 (50 mg) and 52 (200 mg), similar; +40% and
+60% K gave f2 < 50 (44, 37 and 40, 33). HPMC reductions of 5-15% all stayed similar (f2 56-84).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: percentage reduction in exercise-induced heart rate from baseline; EIHR = EIHR0·(1 − ΔEIHR/100) with
  EIHR0 150 beats/min (p4). Therapeutic equivalence was declared if EIHR stayed within 50-85% of an average maximum
  190 beats/min for 30-year-olds, i.e. 95-161.5 beats/min (p4).
- Emax model chosen; Emax, EC50, gamma and any effect-compartment rate constant are not in the main text. No ke0,
  t½ke0 or tPeak reported.
- Introductory statement (p2, printed 1267): an 8-23% fall in exercise heart rate corresponds to about 30-80% of
  maximal β1 blockade (cited, not derived here).
- Table 2 (PDF p6, printed 1271, confirmed on page image), steady state on day 5, ΔEIHRmax,ss median (summarised by
  profile): 50 mg reference 10.49% (5th-95th 7.38-13.37), +60% K 12.54%; 200 mg reference 20.69% (16.64-23.67),
  +60% K 23.15%. ΔEIHR AUEτ,ss median by profile: 50 mg reference 153.88, 200 mg reference 361.69 (units not
  stated; %·h presumed but not printed).
- β1 selectivity threshold 300 nmol/L (0.08 µg/mL as printed; derived: 300 nmol/L × 267.4 g/mol = 80 ng/mL,
  consistent); median 200 mg steady-state peaks exceed it with faster release (Fig. 4c).
- No MEAC, no potency ratio. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None reported in the main text.

## Caveats
Entirely simulation; the PK input comes from a PBPK model whose between-subject variability reflects only
formulation and gut physiology, so the authors say the CIs are narrower than real bioequivalence studies. The PD
model is fitted to literature summary data. Table 2 prints a 5th-95th range of "5.54-2.69" for 50 mg Test 1
ΔEIHRmax by time, an obvious misprint in the source (probably 12.69). The authors conclude ΔEIHR may be too
insensitive to show formulation differences. Exercise heart rate is not resting heart rate or blood pressure.
