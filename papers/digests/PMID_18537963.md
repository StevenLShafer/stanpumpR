# Link 2008: midazolam IV and oral, plasma and saliva, before and after rifampicin

## Study
- Open-label randomised two-way crossover; 10 healthy non-smoking men enrolled, 8 completed and analysed
  (two dropouts excluded). Age 27.7 ± 9.6 y (21–46), weight 70 ± 6 kg (60–78) (mean ± SD, Results p. 476).
- Midazolam 2 mg IV (infusion; duration not stated) or 7.5 mg by mouth (Dormicum, with 200 mL water, fasting),
  each under basal conditions and again one day after 6 days of rifampicin 600 mg daily (CYP3A induction);
  ≥5-week washout between phases.
- Plasma (heparinised) sampled to 1440 min (IV: extra 5-min sample); unstimulated saliva to 480 min.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS (ion trap) of unconjugated midazolam, 1′-OH- and 4-OH-midazolam; LOQ 0.05 ng/mL in both
  matrices; 1′-OH-midazolam glucuronide by β-glucuronidase hydrolysis. Plasma = total (bound + free) drug; saliva
  taken by the authors to reflect free drug (midazolam 96% protein bound).
- Non-compartmental analysis (WinNonlin 5.01); results as median (range).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model is reported; NCA only. All values median (range), n = 8.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. Most subjects had light sedation (Results p. 476), not scored. No ke0, tPeak, EC50 or
concentration–effect data. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None studied. Rifampicin (CYP3A induction) is the only factor: IV AUC fell 34.5%, oral AUC 98.5%
(Discussion p. 479).

## Caveats
- n = 8 young healthy men; medians with ranges.
- Table 1's plasma Vd (3.53 L; 0.05 L/kg) cannot follow from its own equation 4 (Vd = Dose/(AUC·λz)) with the
  tabulated AUC and t½: 2 mg / 125.8 ng·h/mL × 2.9 h / ln2 ≈ 66.5 L, about 0.95 L/kg (derived: Dose/AUC = 15.9 L/h
  × 2.9 h / 0.693). The printed Vd is probably wrong by a factor of about 19; do not use it.
- IV infusion duration not stated; the first plasma sample was at 5 min, so the IV Cmax is a sampled value.
- Table 3 saliva AUC carries a "§" mark with no matching footnote.
- The text calls bioavailability "in the range of 20%" (p. 478); the table gives 0.19.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **docs/therapeutic-ranges.md:272: discrepancy.** The paper gave **7.5 mg** by mouth, not 10 mg (abstract,
  Methods, Table 3). Concentrations after that dose: median Cmax 63.1 ng/mL (25.9–80.2) at 20.6 min in healthy
  young men, the oral result the list wants. For comparison, the AGNP oral-premedication range of 50–150 ng/mL
  (`R/drugs_midazolam.R:171-175`, recorded in therapeuticRanges.csv, not drawn) brackets the median peak, though the
  individual range runs down to 25.9. The paper itself gives no therapeutic range.
- **Uncited comparison with the library's IV model** (`R/drugs_midazolam.R:135-141`, Bührer/Zomorodi set):
  CL1 0.535 L/min = 32.1 L/h at the 70-kg reference, about 7.6 mL/min/kg. Link's median IV clearance 15.9 L/h
  in 70-kg men is 0.265 L/min, about 3.8 mL/min/kg (derived) — half the model's. The model's Vss is
  117.6 L (3.3 + 17.56 + 96.76); Link's NCA volume derived from CL and t½ is ≈ 66 L. Link's IV t½ 2.9 h is within
  the usual range. Direction: Link's healthy volunteers clear midazolam more slowly than the library's model, with
  a range (7.4–23.9 L/h) that does not reach the model's 32 L/h. Nothing in the model's header predicts this; it is
  recorded as an independent check, not an error in the model (different population, a 2 mg dose sampled from
  5 min, NCA).
- The library's midazolam model is intravenous only; Link's oral F 0.19 and tmax are not used anywhere.
