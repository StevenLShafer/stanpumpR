# Ludbrook 2002: propofol brain concentrations vs BIS and cerebral blood flow velocity

## Study
Seven adults (5 men, 2 women; 18-50 y, 55-80 kg, elective orthopaedic surgery, Harborview, Seattle; p.1364) with radial arterial and retrograde right jugular-bulb catheters. Sedated with midazolam first (2-15 mg, individual doses listed p.1365). Propofol 1% by infusion pump: 110 mg/min for 5 min, then 10 mg/min for 20 min (p.1364; Appendix 1 implements it as pulses of 500 mg over 5 min plus 250 mg over 25 min, which agrees). Arterial and jugular samples to 35 min (plasma, gas chromatography, assay range 0.2-50 mg/l, sensitivity 0.2 mg/l). BIS (Aspect 2000, V2.21) and suppression ratio, middle cerebral artery velocity (Vmca) by transcranial Doppler. One patient's EEG was discarded for artifact.

Brain concentration was not measured: it was calculated by mass balance from the arterio-jugular concentration difference times cerebral blood flow, with baseline CBF assumed 55 mL/100 g/min for all subjects, scaled by relative Vmca, and a brain volume of 1,400 mL (p.1364-1365). Fits done on mean (pooled) concentrations, not per subject, in Scientist (MicroMath).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment model fitted to mean arterial concentrations (best fit, MSC 4.03; three-compartment MSC 3.7, third compartment poorly defined). Effect (brain) compartment attached to the central compartment with keo and a blood:brain partition coefficient R. Values are from Results, p.1366.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Equilibration (keo 0.108, t1/2keo 6.45 min) was measured on calculated whole-brain propofol concentration, not on EEG. The paper contrasts it with literature EEG-based t1/2keo of about 2-3 min (introduction and Discussion p.1363, 1368). Time to peak brain concentration was "hardly different" from effect-compartment models (p.1368; no number given).
- BIS fell from about 80 to minimum means of 3.8-4.9 at 6.5-7.5 min; suppression ratio peaked at 90.2-92%; at 35 min BIS and Vmca were still 60-65% of baseline (p.1366). Mean brain concentration versus mean BIS was close to linear with minimal hysteresis (Fig. 4A), with large between-subject spread.
- Burst suppression appeared only above mean brain concentrations of 5.5 mg/l and near-maximal suppression needed about 15 mg/l (Fig. 4B, p.1366). Near-maximal Vmca depression (minimum 41.8 +/- 2.3% of baseline at 6 min) came at about half that, BIS 40-50, maximal at BIS about 10-20 (p.1366-1367). Cerebral oxygen extraction did not change (P = 0.49).
- Simulation with the Marsh set (children's set per the reference) and two literature ke0 values (1.21 and 0.20 /min) gave effect-site time courses that diverged from brain concentration in the early maintenance phase (Fig. 3, p.1366, 1368). BIS tracked brain concentration best.
- No EC50, gamma, MEAC or potency ratio. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled. Baseline CBF assumed equal in all subjects (authors flag this as a possible source of the inter-subject spread).

## Caveats
n = 7; fit to pooled means; midazolam premedication (authors say it may have slowed uptake and elution); brain concentration is an indirect calculation resting on assumed CBF, Vmca as flow surrogate, and a fixed brain volume; propofol loading was a 5-minute infusion, not a bolus; healthy adults, mean arterial pressure and PaCO2 fell slightly during the study. The authors note that a different administration rate may change an effect-compartment ke0.
