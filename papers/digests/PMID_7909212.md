# Heier 1994: Mild hypothermia does not change vecuronium pharmacodynamics

## Study
10 unpremedicated ASA I-II patients, 21-46 yr, 54-104 kg, elective non-abdominal, non-thoracic surgery lasting over
4 h. Anaesthesia: thiopental 2-5 mg/kg, **isoflurane 0.9-1.2% and N2O 60-70% end-tidal, fentanyl as required**;
succinylcholine 1.0 mg/kg for intubation. For 90-120 min after induction five were actively cooled (COLD, oesophageal
< 35.0 C) and five kept normothermic (NORM, > 36.0 C; forced air). COLD 38.4 +/- 10.9 yr, 85.2 +/- 14.2 kg; NORM
42.0 +/- 14.8 yr, 87.6 +/- 21.0 kg (p.974). No peripheral vasoconstriction. Vecuronium **3 ug/kg/min IV for 10 min**.
Venous samples at 2-60 min (12 times); capillary GC with nitrogen-sensitive detection (sensitivity 5 ng/mL,
CV 4-15%, specific for parent and 3-desacetyl metabolite). A two-compartment model was fitted to each patient's plasma
data (BMDP) only to drive the Sheiner effect-compartment PD fit; Mann-Whitney U tests.

## Model and parameters
No PK parameters reported: 60 min of sampling after a small dose was too short to define the elimination phase,
"thereby precluding pharmacokinetic modeling" (p.975). Individual PD, Table 1 (PDF p.3, image-confirmed):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: adductor pollicis T1 twitch tension (TOF, 2 Hz every 12 s), T1 at the start of the infusion as control.
No significant difference, nor any trend, in Css50, ke0 or gamma between 34.4 and 36.8 C, so the authors conclude
pharmacodynamic changes do not explain the roughly doubled duration of vecuronium seen in mild hypothermia (their
earlier 1991 study). They suggest larger temperature drops may be needed for PD changes. No tPeak. Not an opioid; no
MEAC.

## Covariates
Central temperature, two-group comparison only.

## Caveats
n = 5 per group; wide individual scatter (normothermic Css50 41-112 ng/mL, gamma 2.4-7.2). Isoflurane potentiation.
ke0 depends on the unreported two-compartment fit to 60 min of data. Muscle temperature not measured (assumed within
1 C of core).
