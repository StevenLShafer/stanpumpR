# Abdul-Aziz 2020: antimicrobial TDM in critically ill adults (ESICM/ESCMID/IATDMCT/ISAC position paper)

## Study
Consensus position paper. Panel nominated by ESICM, ESCMID PK/PD and Critically Ill Patient Study Groups,
IATDMCT and ISAC. PubMed review of English-language studies before 1 July 2019; for efficacy and toxicity only
studies in critically ill adults were used (PDF p2-3, printed 1128-1129). Recommendations scored on a 7-point
Likert scale; consensus when 1+2 or 6+7 reached ≥ 75% (PDF p3). No new patients or PK modelling.

Routine TDM recommended ("YES") for aminoglycosides, beta-lactams, linezolid, teicoplanin, vancomycin and
voriconazole; "neither recommend nor discourage" for the others (Table 4, PDF p8-9).

Total concentrations are what most laboratories report; free concentrations are preferred where feasible, and
some targets (e.g. vancomycin) are defined only as total exposure (PDF p5, printed 1131).

Vancomycin dosing in the critically ill without renal impairment: loading 25-30 mg/kg, then 15-20 mg/kg every
8-12 h (PDF p12, printed 1138, text layer). The AUC0-24/MIC ≥ 400 was derived with broth microdilution MIC;
the E-test equivalent may be only 226 (same page).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points are microbiological/clinical (PK/PD index relative to the pathogen MIC) and organ toxicity
(nephro-, oto-, neuro-, haematological). Beta-lactams: fT>MIC; no toxicity thresholds "well established",
though Table 1 lists drug-specific Cmin thresholds (cefepime, meropenem, piperacillin). Aminoglycosides: Cmax
≥ 8-10 × MIC, with AUC0-24/MIC possibly better for extended-interval dosing (PDF p6). Vancomycin: Cmin (15-20
intermittent) and Css (20-25 continuous) widely used as surrogates, but Cmin is a poor surrogate for AUC0-24;
Bayesian AUC-based monitoring preferred (PDF p12). No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio (not an opioid paper).

Not addressed anywhere in the text layer (including references): clindamycin, metronidazole, cefazolin,
cefalexin. Ceftriaxone is named only as a highly protein-bound beta-lactam whose free fraction rises with
hypoalbuminaemia (PDF p3, p10).

## Covariates
Qualitative: capillary leak and fluid loading (Vd up for hydrophilic drugs), hypoalbuminaemia (free fraction up),
augmented renal clearance (CLcr > 130 mL/min/1.73 m²), RRT, ECMO, obesity (PDF p3-4). No equations.

## Caveats
- Consensus document; many targets come from small or non-ICU studies, and individual MICs vary by 1-2 dilutions
  (PDF p4).
- Targets for most drugs are relative to the MIC, so they are not absolute concentration ranges.
- Supplementary Tables 1-5 (primary data, empirical dosing) are not in the archived PDF.
