# Pandit 2014: NAP5 accidental awareness, main findings

## Study
UK and Ireland national audit, prospective reporting 1 Jun 2012 - 31 May 2013, with a parallel activity survey as denominator (about 2,800,000 general anaesthetics per year; 2,766,600 used in Table 2). Reports of accidental awareness during general anaesthesia (AAGA) were classed by a panel; 95% Poisson CIs (PDF p1-3).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentrations, ke0 or potency ratios (not an opioid paper). Relevant findings, all from the text:
- 300 reports; 110 certain/probable, 31 possible (Table 1, p2). Incidence of certain/probable + possible AAGA 1:19,600 (95% CI 1:16,700-23,450); with NMB 1:8,200, without NMB 1:135,900; Caesarean section 1:670 (Table 2, p5; column alignment in the text layer is scrambled, so the row-to-value pairing for the other subgroups should be taken from the page image).
- Thiopental was 3% of inductions but 23% of AAGA reports. TIVA 18% of AAGA cases vs 8% overall (p3). TIVA plus NMB carried the largest risk ratio, 3.73 (Table 4, p7). Most common cause in TIVA cases: inappropriately low fixed-rate propofol infusion with NMB, or failed delivery (e.g. cannula problem) (p7-8). Volatile converted to TIVA for transfer was most over-represented (Table 6, p8).
- Processed-EEG monitor use: 4.3% of AAGA cases vs 2.8% of anaesthetics overall; the crude ratio was below 1 only in TIVA with NMB (0.47) (Table 5, p7).
- ICU cases: low-dose propofol infusions in sick, hypotensive patients with NMB (p8).

## Covariates
Risk factors listed: female sex, younger adults, obesity, junior anaesthetist, previous awareness, out-of-hours, emergency surgery, obstetric/cardiac/thoracic surgery, NMB use. Not associated: ASA status (P = 0.23), race (P = 0.42), nitrous oxide (P = 0.26) (p3).

## Caveats
Reports rely on patient self-report and an activity-survey denominator; the authors note crude, unadjusted comparisons (Table 5 does not adjust for obesity, sex, age); small cell counts for some subgroups.
