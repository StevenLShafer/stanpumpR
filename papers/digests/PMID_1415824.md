# Lesser 1992: alprazolam concentration and response in panic disorder

## Study
Three-site, 6-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled, fixed-dose trial (Upjohn-supported; the concentrations were
assayed in D. Greenblatt's laboratory). 96 patients with DSM-III panic disorder or agoraphobia with panic attacks
(70% women, mean age 37.1 y): 30 on alprazolam 2 mg/day, 31 on 6 mg/day, 35 placebo (PDF p2). Target dose reached
by day 3 (2 mg) or day 18 (6 mg); 3-week taper after. Completers: placebo 15 (45.5%), 2 mg 23 (76.7%), 6 mg 16
(48.4%). Plasma sampled at each visit (baseline, weeks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6) with **no specified time after dose**; most
samples 0-6 h after a dose (PDF p3). Assay: GLC with electron capture (alprazolam, diazepam, desmethyldiazepam).
Concentration data in 61 alprazolam patients, about 4 samples each.

Analysis: logistic regression (SAS CATMOD) of response category (major / moderate / none; Table 1, PDF p2) on plasma
alprazolam, using the last evaluation (mean 4.8-5.1 weeks; LOCF-like), placebo patients at 0 ng/mL. Major panic
response = >75% fall in major panic attacks per week; moderate 25-75%; none <25%.

Panic-free at the last visit (PDF p4): 8/19 (42%) below 20 ng/mL, 8/14 (57%) at 20-40, 18/27 (67%) above 40.
Table 2 (PDF p5, rendered) gives response counts by concentration band (0, 1-19, 20-39, 40-59, 60-79, >=80 ng/mL) for
the main phobia fear scale (n 80) and the two global scales (n 89); frequencies do not rise consistently with
concentration (authors).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points: panic-attack count, Hamilton anxiety, five phobia ratings, physician and patient global improvement,
treatment-emergent side effects. Hamilton anxiety: no significant concentration relationship (about 70% major or
moderate responders even at the lowest concentrations). Tachycardia was the only symptom negatively related to
concentration. No ke0, no time course of effect. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
None modelled. A cited earlier analysis of the same patients (ref 11) found about 40% lower alprazolam elimination
in responders than in non-responders.

## Caveats
- The authors explicitly say the results do not establish a minimum effective concentration or a therapeutic window
  (PDF p7); they also advise against routine benzodiazepine level monitoring.
- "Steady-state" concentrations are random-time samples (mostly 0-6 h post-dose), not troughs; the guideline the repo
  follows samples 10 h after the last dose.
- Differential dropout (placebo and 6 mg arms); last-evaluation analysis.
- Sedation C50 (40) lies near the 75%-major-response concentration (48).
