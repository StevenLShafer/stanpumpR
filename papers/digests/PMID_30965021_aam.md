# Bergh 2019, accepted manuscript: the same numbers as the published edition

The author manuscript of the only in-vivo carfentanil pharmacokinetic study in rats. It exists in
the archive beside the published edition because, by the archive's standing rule, an accepted
manuscript can differ from the published text and so is never treated as interchangeable with it.
**On everything stanpumpR relies on, these two editions agree.**

The substance of the paper — its design, parameters, caveats and what the library does and does not
take from it — is in `papers/digests/PMID_30965021.md`, which is the published edition's card and
the one to read. This card records only the comparison.

**Why this card was written by hand.** `digest_run.py` records this file as refused by the model's
safeguards even when batched alone, as it does the published edition. The refusal is at
card-writing only; the PDF, its text and its context file were filed normally.

## Study
Identical to the published edition: male Sprague-Dawley rats with jugular catheters and implanted
temperature transponders, a single **subcutaneous** dose of carfentanil 1, 3 or 10 ug/kg or saline,
blood at 15, 30, 60, 120, 240 and 480 min, with core temperature and catalepsy scored at the same
times, and UHPLC-MS/MS for carfentanil and norcarfentanil. Group sizes N = 6, 6 and 5.

**The accepted manuscript states the significance markers more explicitly than the typeset table
does**, and this is the one place it is the better copy to read: its footnote defines `*` as a
significant difference against the 1 ug/kg dose by Tukey at p < 0.05, with `#` marking the further
comparison against 3 ug/kg. The published card's note that the 10 ug/kg Cmax and AUC differ from
both lower doses is confirmed by those two markers appearing together on exactly those cells.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Identical: core temperature and a catalepsy score built from three scored behaviours, correlated
with carfentanil AUC (r = -0.751 for temperature, r = 0.784 for catalepsy, both p < 0.001). No
EC50, no effect-site model, no ke0 in either edition.

## Covariates
None, in either edition. One species, one sex, one route.

## Caveats
- **This is not a second study.** It is the same work, so it is not independent evidence for
  anything; it is a check that the published numbers were not introduced in production.
- **The route is subcutaneous in both editions**, so neither yields an absolute volume or clearance.
- **Its Table 4 is interleaved in the text layer**, like the published edition's. It is decipherable
  by reading down the columns, but anyone pinning a value should still render the page, and this
  card's values rest on the comparison with the rendered published table rather than on the text
  layer alone.
- **The Discussion was not compared line by line** between editions — see *open_questions*. That is
  where an accepted manuscript most often differs.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **The two editions agree on every value stanpumpR's cards pin.** All 21 Table 4 entries match, as
  do the abstract's limits of detection and quantification, the Tmax values, and both correlation
  coefficients. So the published edition's card is not at risk from an editorial change between
  acceptance and print. **Matches.**
- **The discrepancy recorded on the published edition's card stands unchanged**, and this edition
  does nothing to soften it: `R/drugs_carfentanil.R:23`'s claims that the only in-vivo carfentanil
  pharmacokinetics are in rats, and that every human study uses [11C]carfentanil as a PET
  radiotracer, are contradicted by Uddayasankar 2018 (PMID 29679387, a human case report with a
  fitted elimination half-life of 5.7 h), by Mutlow 2004 (PMID 15732589, goats) and by Cole 2006
  (PMID 17319131, common eland). See `papers/digests/PMID_30965021.md`.
- **No parameter of the library's carfentanil model comes from either edition**; the model uses
  sufentanil's disposition on Shafer's judgement. **Consistent.**
- Nothing in this edition contradicts a value stanpumpR holds.
