# Kudriakova 1992: dose-dependent carbamazepine autoinduction at steady state

## Study
- 77 patients with affective illness on prophylactic carbamazepine (Finlepsin), 10 men and 67 women, aged 19-60 y; weight not reported (PDF p2).
- Doses 100-1200 mg/day orally, given twice or three times daily; titrated from 100 mg/day up by 100-200 mg every 5-7 days.
- Carbamazepine alone or with neuroleptics, antidepressants and tranquilizers; no known hepatic inducers.
- Group 1: 21 patients followed from the start of therapy. Group 2: 56 patients on carbamazepine for at least 1 year.
- Trough plasma samples were taken at 09.00 before the morning dose, before each dose change in month 1 and then monthly for 2 years. Values for one patient at one dose were averaged.
- Assay: HPLC for CBZ, CBZ-10,11-epoxide and CBZ-10,11-diol. Sensitivity was 0.13, 0.08 and 0.11 umol/L; interassay CV <4%.
- Analysis: apparent steady-state clearance CLss = (D/T)/Css (Eq 1), with D the daily dose in mg, T 1440 min and Css the trough. This is a population-mean analysis by dose level, not a compartmental model.

## Model and parameters
- Table 1 (PDF p2, printed 612): apparent CLss (mL/min, mean ± s.e.m.), group 1 against group 2. No dose shows a significant difference.

  | Dose (mg/day) | Group 1 | n | Group 2 | n |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 200 | 30.0 ± 2.4 | 14 | 34.5 ± 4.7 | 7 |
  | 400 | 51.8 ± 9.8 | 11 | 48.0 ± 3.7 | 10 |
  | 500 | 54.6 ± 2.5 | 7 | 57.1 ± 7.6 | 8 |
  | 600 | 71.1 ± 7.7 | 16 | 75.9 ± 5.3 | 19 |
- Eq 2 (PDF p3, printed 613): CLss = 25.2 + 0.07D, with CLss in mL/min and D in mg/day. The fit is over 77 patients and 100-1200 mg/day (r = 0.95, P < 0.01). Observed CLss ranged from 31 to 110 mL/min (Fig 2).
- Eq 3: Css = 42.1D/(360 + D), in umol/L (Fig 3). (derived check: 1000/(1440 × 0.07) = 9.92 mg/L, which is 42.0 umol/L at MW 236.27; 25.2/0.07 = 360.)
- The intercept of 25.2 mL/min is read by the authors as the uninduced baseline. They compare it with 24.4 mL/min from single-dose kinetics in Tomson 1983 (a different study), PDF p4.
- Epoxide/CBZ ratio: 0.09 ± 0.02 at 100 mg/day and 0.21 ± 0.06 at 1200 mg/day (mean ± s.e.m., Fig 5). The ratio is nearly constant above 500 mg/day.
- CBZ-10,11-diol rises in proportion to dose (r = 0.993 over 11 dose levels, intercept near zero, Fig 6). The authors infer that absorption is not dose-dependent.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD model, no concentration-effect analysis and no therapeutic range.
- The authors note that no convincing level-effect relation exists. The epoxide is active and the diol is inactive.

## Covariates
- Daily dose only (Eq 2). Duration of therapy (group 1 against group 2) had no significant effect (Table 1). Weight, age and sex were not analysed.

## Caveats
- Trough-based Eq 1 overestimates clearance, as the authors acknowledge (PDF p4).
- Data are population means per dose level, not individual fits. Regression on means inflates r.
- Most patients were women with affective disorders, and many took psychotropic co-medication.
- Table 1 n per cell is 7-19, so "no difference" at 5-7 days is a non-significant result, not shown equivalence.
- The intercept is extrapolated below the lowest dose.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Qualitative claim (all three locations): matches.** The induced part of clearance, 0.07D, is proportional to daily dose (Eq 2), and the authors conclude that autoinduction is dose-dependent. "Proportional" applies to the increment above the 25.2 mL/min intercept, not to total clearance.
- **Mechanism, cannot verify from this paper.** The library drives induction by concentration through a saturating Emax (`antiseizure-interactions.md:20-23`) with an EC50 and calibration that do not come from Kudriakova. Re-expressing Eq 2 and Eq 3 in concentration gives fold = 42.1/(42.1 - C) with C in umol/L. That curve rises steeply rather than saturating (derived), so the paper does not support the Emax shape. See open_questions.
- **Magnitude, independent check.** The paper implies 3.2-fold baseline clearance at 800 mg/day (derived: (25.2 + 56)/25.2), compared with the library's 2-fold (Bertilsson 1986). The comparator for Kudriakova's baseline is its own extrapolated intercept, matched to Tomson 1983's single-dose volunteers. It is not the library's "half of Graves". Trough-based clearance biases the fold upward. See open_questions.
- **Time course, independent check.** The paper says induction completes within 1 week (Table 1). The library's 86.1 h half-life (`antiseizure-interactions.md:31`; the model header `R/drugs_carbamazepine.R:24` says about 70 h, Magnusson) predicts 74-81% by day 7 (derived). The direction is that the library is slower than Kudriakova's reading. See open_questions.
- **Uncited PK comparison with the library's model (Graves 1998, `R/drugs_carbamazepine.R:15-19`).**
  - At 800 mg/day, Kudriakova's CLss is 81.2 mL/min = 4.87 L/h (derived). Graves gives 4.52 L/h at 70 kg. Kudriakova's weight is not reported, and its value is trough-based so biased high. The two agree within about 8%.
  - At 800 mg/day, Kudriakova's Eq 3 trough is 29.0 umol/L = 6.9 mg/L (derived). The scenario's plain model settles near 7.4 mg/L (`carbamazepine-autoinduction.md:5`, an average rather than a trough). The two are consistent.
- **Unit checks.** CLss is in mL/min and Css in umol/L (Eq 3), while the library uses mg/L. The conversions above use MW 236.27. Eq 1 prints Css in "mg ml-1", which is consistent with CLss in mL/min.
- **Opioid audit:** not applicable (not an opioid paper).
