# Sauberan 2011: hydrocodone and hydromorphone in breast milk, postpartum

## Study
Prospective observational milk-transfer study, UCSD Medical Center, Nov 2006 to Oct 2009 (PDF p2, printed 612).
32 consented, 2 withdrew; n = 30 analysed. Inclusion: women 18-50 y, at least 48 h postpartum, breastfeeding,
taking oral hydrocodone-acetaminophen for pain as inpatients. Table 1 (PDF p3, printed 613): age median 30.5 y
(mean 30.2, SD 5.6, range 18-39); weight median 75.3 kg (mean 81.8, SD 22.8, range 56-151.5); postnatal age
median 3.0 d (range 3-11); infant birth weight median 3.58 kg (range 0.42-4.89). 7 preterm births (<36 0/7 wk);
28/30 (93%) caesarean. Study period mean 55.3 h (range 16.9-84.1).

Dosing was clinical (no protocol dose); doses and times taken from the electronic record. Milk: 2-5 mL aliquots
from each breast, either after full pumping (19 women) or by manual expression before a feed (11 women); 125
samples. Assay: isotope-dilution LC-MS (d3 internal standards), LLOQ 1 ng/mL for both analytes, range 1-1,000
ng/mL, accuracy >90%, precision <8% (PDF p2). **No maternal plasma was sampled**: there is no plasma PK, no
milk/plasma ratio and no CYP2D6 genotyping.

Analysis (PDF p3): AUC0-t of milk concentration by a linear rectangular method; average milk concentration = AUC /
study hours; infant dose = average concentration x 150 mL/kg/day. Maternal dose = bitartrate dose per 24 h per kg
x 0.605 (bitartrate hemipentahydrate to anhydrous base). Relative infant dose = infant / maternal dose x 100. One
outlier (715 µg/L, 3 h after 6,050 µg base, 54 times the same woman's next value) was excluded (PDF p4).

than the outlier: 126.5 µg/L, 3 h after a 6,050 µg base dose.

213 µg/kg/d): hydrocodone median 0.6% (range 0.07-4.1); hydromorphone median 0.13% (0.1-6.1); total opiate median
0.7% (0.1-9.9).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC / minimum analgesic concentration: none reported.** No plasma concentrations and no analgesic end point
  were measured; infant outcomes were not followed (PDF p5).
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: none reported.**
- **Potency ratios stated (both in the Discussion, PDF p5, printed 615; neither measured here):** hydromorphone 2.5
  times as potent as hydrocodone (no citation given for the 2.5); hydrocodone 60%-100% as potent as oral morphine
  (ref 14, Stanos and Tyburski, a textbook chapter). Both are dose-potency statements for oral use.
- Minimum infant doses used as comparators (PDF p3): hydrocodone bitartrate 600 µg/kg/d (363 base) and
  hydromorphone HCl 240 µg/kg/d (x 0.887 = 213 base), from the Lortab syrup label via ref 13. Neonatal oral morphine
  200-500 µg/kg/d (ref 15).
- Authors' recommendation: limit hydrocodone bitartrate to 30 mg/day while breastfeeding; observe the infant at
  35-40 mg; avoid >40 mg/day (PDF p6).

## Covariates
None modelled. Pumped vs expressed milk: median hydrocodone concentration 12.3 vs 30.2 µg/L (P = .53); maternal
dose 141 vs 175 µg/kg/d (P = .48), PDF p6. Hydromorphone/hydrocodone infant-dose ratio >1 in 2 women (2.8 and 3.1),
suggested by the authors as possible CYP2D6 ultrarapid metabolisers (not genotyped).

## Caveats
Postpartum (days 3-11), not pregnant; mostly post-caesarean. No maternal plasma, so milk data cannot inform a
maternal PK model. Sparse, irregular sampling; rectangular AUC. Mixed pumped/expressed sampling (hind-milk effect).
One outlier excluded. Table footnotes say "Data are n (95% CI)" although the cells are statistics with CIs.
The Table 3 SD row (3.9) for infant hydromorphone dose equals the Table 2 hydrocodone SD; it is what the page
prints.
