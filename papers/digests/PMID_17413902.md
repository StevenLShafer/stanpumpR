# Takita 2007: exhaled propofol by PTR-MS

## Study
Two-part study in ASA I-II adults having elective surgery with epidural ropivacaine analgesia (PDF p2).
- Study 1 (n = 11): induction with propofol 2 mg/kg, vecuronium 0.1 mg/kg, fentanyl 2 ug/kg; then propofol infusion 3, 6, 9 mg/kg/h, 60 min at each rate (steps up if BIS > 70 at the lower rate; ended when surgery ended). Arterial blood sampled 5 times at 1-min intervals at the end of each step; plasma propofol by HPLC with fluorescence detection (detection limit 10 ng/mL, linear 50 ng/mL-10 ug/mL). Exhaled propofol by proton transfer reaction mass spectrometry (PTR-MS, m/z 179), 750-ms response, 70 C heated sampling line; end-tidal values chosen from breath temperature and averaged over 50 breaths. 24 paired points in total.
- Study 2 (n = 8): induction with midazolam 0.1 mg/kg, vecuronium, fentanyl; then propofol 2 mg/kg bolus (time 0), exhaled propofol recorded 30 min.
- PTR-MS calibration against propofol standard gas 0.40-400 ppb; stated uncertainty about +/-30% (appendix, PDF p5-6).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model fitted. The authors only used the Marsh pediatric-derived propofol model (their ref 7) to check that plasma concentration was at steady state during sampling (PDF p2, p4). No parameters reported.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD end point other than BIS > 70 as a "too light" trigger. Not an opioid paper.
- Exhaled-vs-plasma agreement (study 1, 24 points, values normalised to percent of maximum because units differ, ug/mL vs ppb): modified Bland-Altman bias (mean ratio) 5.2, precision (SD of ratio) 10.4, 95% limits of agreement -15.1 and 25.6 (text p4, Fig. 4). Linear regression is in Fig. 3 (no equation in the text layer).
- Study 2 (bolus 2 mg/kg): exhaled propofol first detected 41.8 +/- 8.0 s after injection; peak at 333.8 +/- 70.0 s (about 5.6 min) (PDF p3-4, Fig. 5). Plasma peak is presumably earlier (under about 1 min, not measured), so the exhaled signal lags plasma; authors attribute it speculatively to pulmonary uptake and release. This is an exhaled-gas lag, not a ke0 or effect-site delay; no ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak for an effect, MEAC or potency ratio is reported.
- No absolute exhaled concentrations (ppb) are given in the text.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
Small n; only healthy adults; breath/plasma relation is shown only at steady state; exhaled signal lags plasma after a bolus, so it cannot track rapid change; calibration uncertainty about +/-30%; inspired-phase baseline did not return to zero (residual propofol in the circuit); the agreement statistic is on normalised, unit-free data (the "bias" is a ratio, not a concentration difference).
