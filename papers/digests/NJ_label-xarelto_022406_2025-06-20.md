# Xarelto (rivaroxaban) US label, revised 06/2025

## Study
Not a study: the FDA-approved prescribing information for XARELTO (Janssen), tablets 2.5, 10, 15 and 20 mg and an
oral suspension 1 mg/mL after reconstitution (PDF p 1, section 3). Initial US approval 2011. 92 PDF pages; printed page
numbers equal PDF page numbers. Rivaroxaban is a direct, selective factor Xa inhibitor (12.1), a pure (S)-enantiomer,
MW 435.89, C19H18ClN3O5S (section 11, p 36).

Adult dosing (Table 1, p 6, confirmed on the rendered page; CrCl by Cockcroft-Gault on **actual** weight, footnote *):
- Nonvalvular AF: 20 mg once daily if CrCl >50 mL/min, 15 mg once daily if CrCl ≤50 mL/min; **take with the evening meal**.
- Treatment of DVT/PE (CrCl ≥15): 15 mg twice daily for 21 days, then 20 mg once daily; **take with food**, same time each day.
- Reduction of recurrent DVT/PE (CrCl ≥15): 10 mg once daily after ≥6 months of standard treatment; with or without food.
- DVT prophylaxis after hip (35 days) or knee (12 days) replacement: 10 mg once daily, starting 6-10 h after surgery
  once hemostasis is established; with or without food.
- VTE prophylaxis in acutely ill medical patients: 10 mg once daily for 31-39 days; with or without food.
- CAD or PAD: 2.5 mg twice daily plus aspirin 75-100 mg once daily; with or without food; no CrCl adjustment.
- CrCl <15 mL/min: avoid (all VTE indications). Footnote ‡: CrCl <30 not studied, but exposure expected to resemble
  CrCl 30 to <50.

Pediatric dosing (Tables 2 and 3, p 7-8): body-weight bands from 2.6 kg (VTE treatment; 0.8 mg three times daily at
2.6-2.9 kg up to 20 mg once daily at ≥50 kg) and from 7 kg (post-Fontan prophylaxis; 1.1 mg twice daily up to 10 mg
once daily). VTE-treatment doses are taken with feeding or food (exposures matched to adult 20 mg daily); Fontan doses
with or without food (matched to adult 10 mg daily). Pediatric renal rules use the Schwartz eGFR (k 0.413 bedside;
0.55 / 0.70 for the original formula) and Table 4 creatinine 97.5th percentiles for infants (p 9).

Perioperative (2.4, p 10): stop at least 24 h before a procedure; restart once hemostasis is adequate.
Neuraxial (5.3, p 14-15): an epidural or intrathecal catheter should not be removed until at least 2 half-lives after
the last dose, given as 18 h (age 20-45 y) and 26 h (age 60-76 y); next dose no earlier than 6 h after removal; delay
24 h after a traumatic puncture. Missed doses (2.5, p 11); crushed-tablet and NG administration (2.6, p 11-12).

Other absorption facts (p 39): release in the proximal small intestine lowers AUC by 29% and Cmax by 56% versus the
tablet; avoid delivery distal to the stomach. Crushed 20 mg in applesauce: AUC and Cmax comparable to whole tablet
(n=44); via NG tube with a liquid meal, AUC comparable and Cmax 18% lower. Gastric pH drugs (ranitidine, antacid,
omeprazole) had no effect. Overdose (10, p 36): exposure not further increased at single doses >50 mg (limited
absorption); not dialyzable.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Dose-dependent FXa inhibition; PT, aPTT and HepTest prolonged dose-dependently (12.2, p 37-38). In children,
  anti-FXa vs plasma concentration is linear with slope close to 1 (p 38).
- **No therapeutic, target or toxic concentration range is stated.** The label says monitoring of the anticoagulant
  effect by PT, INR, aPTT or anti-FXa activity is not recommended (5.2, p 14; 12.2, p 38; both read on the page image).
  No concentration in ng/mL appears anywhere in the text layer.
- No effect-site model, ke0 or EC50.
- Table 18 (p 38, page image): percentage increase vs healthy subjects by CrCl 50-79 / 30-49 / 15-29 / ESRD on
  dialysis / ESRD post-dialysis: AUC 44 / 52 / 64 / 47 / 56; FXa-inhibition AUEC 50 / 86 / 100 / 49 / 33; PT AUEC
  33 / 116 / 144 / 112 / 158.
- Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio applies.

## Covariates
- Renal: Table 18 and Figure 2 (p 41, forest plot of fold change and 90% CI; no numbers printed, so not transcribed).
  Figure 2 footnotes on the page image: severe 15-29, moderate 30-49, mild 50-79 mL/min (the text layer misreads the
  last as 50-76). Renal-impairment PK study: single 10 mg (p 42).
- Hepatic (8.7, p 36): AUC +127% in Child-Pugh B; Child-Pugh C not studied; avoid B and C.
- Age: elderly half-life 11-13 h; Figure 2 compares 75-83 y with 18-43 y.
- Weight: Figure 2 compares ≤50 kg and >120 kg with 70-80 kg (graphical only).
- Sex: no influence on PK or PD (p 42). Race: Japanese exposures 20-40% higher, reduced after weight correction.
- Interactions (7.1-7.3, 12.3 Figure 3 p 44, graphical): avoid combined P-gp and strong CYP3A inhibitors
  (ketoconazole, ritonavir) or inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampin, St John's wort). With erythromycin in mild
  / moderate renal impairment: AUCinf +76% / +99%, Cmax +56% / +64% (p 45, text layer).

## Caveats
- Half-lives come from healthy-volunteer studies (ages 20-45 and 60-76), not from patients; the label gives no
  patient population PK model.
- Absolute bioavailability is dose-dependent and food-dependent; the label states it only for 2.5/10 mg and fasted
  20 mg (the fed 20 mg figure is relative: +39% AUC).
- Figures 2 and 3 are graphical; their fold changes are not tabulated in the label.
- Table 18 and the ESRD statements come from separate single-dose clinical pharmacology studies.
