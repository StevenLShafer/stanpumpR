# Gieschke 2003: Population PK and concentration–effect of capecitabine metabolites

## Study
Population PK and concentration–effect analysis in advanced/metastatic
colorectal cancer. 481 patients, age 27–86 y, from two
open-label phase III trials dosed capecitabine 1250 mg/m² twice daily
(intermittent, 2 weeks on/1 off), plus 24 patients from a bioequivalence study
(single 2000 mg oral dose) used for model building (pp. 252–253). **The abstract's
sex split is a misprint**: it gives "248 males, 193 females", which totals 441, not
481. Table 1 (PDF p3) gives the two phase III studies as n = 248 (148 M / 100 F) and
n = 233 (140 M / 93 F), so the cohort is **288 men and 193 women** (derived by summing
Table 1); 248 is the size of the first phase III study, not the number of men. The
bioequivalence study adds 24 (15 M / 9 F). Plasma
5′-DFUR, 5-FU and FBAL (capecitabine's downstream metabolites) measured by
LC–MS/MS; LOQ 0.05 / 0.002 / 0.02 µg/mL respectively (p. 253). Phase III sparse
sampling in windows 0.5–1.5, 1.5–3.0, 3.0–5.0 h on day 22 (cycle 2) and day 64
(cycle 4). NONMEM V, First Order. Concentration–effect done by logistic
regression (safety, tumour response) and Cox regression (time to progression,
survival). Capecitabine's cascade: dose → 5′-DFCR → 5′-DFUR → 5-FU (active) →
FUH2/FUPA → FBAL (renally excreted) (pp. 252–253).

Reference covariate values (Table 4 equations): BSA 1.8 m², CLCR 80 mL/min, ALP
140 U/L. Predicted systemic exposure at a single 1250 mg/m² dose (Table 6,
p. 259; medians, ug·h/mL for AUC, ug/mL for Cmax): 5′-DFUR AUC 20.13, Cmax 8.89;
5-FU AUC 0.707, Cmax 0.299; FBAL AUC 24.69, Cmax 4.69.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0/tPeak/EC50/MEAC. Concentration–effect was weak: of 42 relationships, only
5 reached significance, with inconsistency across the two studies (Abstract;
Tables 5/7/8/9). Covariate effects on exposure (Table 5, p. 257): ALP×2 → 5-FU
CL 11% lower, AUC 12% higher; CLCR×0.5 → FBAL CL 35% lower, AUC 53% higher; and
V 24% lower, Cmax 41% higher; BSA×1.3 → FBAL V 24% higher, Cmax 19% lower.
Conclusion: plasma exposure poorly predicts safety/efficacy; no case for TDM
(pp. 252, 259). (Not an opioid; no opioid audit applies.)

## Covariates
Significant: ALP on CL of 5-FU; CLCR on CL and V of FBAL; BSA on V of FBAL
(Tables 4–5). No covariate on 5′-DFUR's own disposition.

## Caveats
Sparse phase III sampling (3 windows, 2 occasions); absorption highly variable
(some lag-times >4 h, TLAG IIV 49,498% — authors call the lag "rather small");
only patients on the capecitabine arm analysed; the exposure–response signals
are weak and study-inconsistent. Volumes/clearances are apparent (V/F, CL/F);
absolute F is not identified here.
