# Collins-Smith 2026: pregnancy PBPK model for aspirin (salicylic acid)

## Study
Modelling only, no subjects (p. 3, section 2.1). GastroPlus 9.9 PBPK with ADMET Predictor 11 inputs and the software's age-related physiology (p. 3). A nonpregnant model was built first, then adapted to pregnancy (plasma volume, cardiac output, renal clearance, GI motility, hepatic CYP2C9/3A4/2E1, UGT and esterase changes, fetal and amniotic compartments; p. 2). Observed profiles were digitised from the literature (refs 22, 23, 25) and the model was "refined" by parameter optimisation against them (p. 3), so the pregnant data were used to fit, not only to check. Population simulations: 100 virtual subjects per group (nonpregnant, 1st, 2nd, 3rd trimester); 100 mg non-enteric-coated aspirin in the pregnant groups and 81 mg in the nonpregnant (p. 3). The simulated analyte throughout is salicylic acid (SA), the metabolite (p. 5; Figure 1E-F axes "SA Concentration (ng/mL)"), although Tables 2-3 are headed "aspirin".

## Model and parameters
Input parameters (Figure 1C, p. 4; aspirin / SA): MW 180.16 / 138.12 g/mol; logP 3.22 / 2.26; pKa 3.47 / 2.97; blood:plasma ratio 0.81 / 0.62; fu,p 0.12 / 0.1; CYP2C9 Vmax 6.4 / 3.2 "mg/s" and Km 929.789 / 450 "mg/mL" (ADMET Predictor estimates); UGT1A6/1A9/2B7 CLint for SA 220, 150, 300 uL/min/mg protein; renal clearance 3.5 / 1.8 L/h; hepatic clearance 6 / 5.5 L/h. Tissue Kp values 0.06-0.31, essentially identical nonpregnant vs pregnant (Figure 1D).

Validation (Table 2, p. 5): nonpregnant, observed (ref 25, 81 mg) vs predicted: Cmax 3150 vs 3161.4, Tmax 2.68 vs 1.936 h, AUCinf 2.07e4 vs 2.01e4, AUCt 2.06e4 vs 1.615e4, CL 3.922 vs 3.5 L/h; fold errors 1.01, 0.72, 0.97, 0.78, 0.89. Pregnant (100 mg): Cmax 2417 vs 2501.7, Tmax 2 vs 1.8879 h, AUCt 12187.3 vs 12,050, CL 6 vs 5.25 L/h; fold errors 1.04, 0.94, 0.99, 0.88.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model; despite the abstract's closing sentence, nothing pharmacodynamic is fitted or simulated. The Discussion only cites others' thromboxane data (Patrick: 50 mg extended-release vs 30 mg immediate-release to keep 50% of maximal serum TXB2 inhibition; Sibai: 60 and 80 mg at term; p. 8). No therapeutic SA range; the authors say one has not been established (p. 8).

## Covariates
Gestational age (through GastroPlus pregnancy physiology); the authors say BMI, race and fetal sex can also be set (p. 9). No covariate equations are printed; the supplement holds further inputs.

## Caveats
- **Internal inconsistencies, all on the rendered pages:**
  - Clearance: Results text (p. 5) gives 5.7 L/h at 10 weeks rising to 5.9 L/h at 40 weeks, but Table 3 gives 4.791 and 5.907. The text (p. 7) calls the 10-to-40-week CL change "minimal at 3%", but Table 3 gives +23% (derived 5.907/4.791). The Discussion (p. 8) says CL is 14%, 14%, 15% and 17% above nonpregnant at 10-40 weeks, but Table 3 against Table 2's nonpregnant prediction (3.5 L/h) gives +37% to +69% (derived).
  - Exposure: the text, abstract and Discussion say SA AUC falls about 20%, 25%, 29% and 30% at 10, 20, 30 and 40 weeks (pregnant/nonpregnant AUC ratio 0.72-0.79). I could not reproduce this from the tables. Table 3 AUCinf over Table 2's predicted nonpregnant AUCinf (2.01e4, at 81 mg) is 0.64-0.56 (derived), and 0.52-0.45 after normalising to dose (derived). The comparator the authors used is not stated.
  - Abstract vs body: the abstract gives a "decrease of 10% in systemic exposure" (the body's 10% is the change from 10 to 40 weeks; Table 3 gives -13.5%, derived) and "a 20% increase in fetal exposure". Table 4 fetal AUCt rises from 1210.9 to 2300.4 (+90%, derived), which is the Conclusion's "approximately 90%" (p. 9). The abstract's "fold errors <= 1.04" means predicted/observed never exceeded 1.04; the lowest was 0.72 (Tmax, Table 2).
  - Table 2 labelling: the pregnant "Observed" column cites ref [8] (Angiolillo 2022, a phospholipid-aspirin study), but its values (Cmax 2417, AUCt 12187.3, Tmax 2 h, CL 6) are those of Shanmugalingam 2019 (ref 23) Table 2, 100 mg non-enteric-coated, pregnant (see PMID_31051121). Figure 1's caption names "Shanmugan et al.", so the [8] is a citation error. Table 2's footnote letters are also misassigned: footnote a describes the pregnant column but sits on the nonpregnant header.
- The pregnant "validation" uses the same three-woman-per-arm data the model was optimised against (p. 3); fetal predictions have no validating data (p. 9).
- The authors note overfitting risk and unknown exposure-response for SA in pregnancy (p. 9).

The paper states CL 14-17% above nonpregnant (Discussion p. 8). Its Table 3 gives a 10-to-40-week rise of 1.23-fold, and 1.37- to 1.69-fold over its nonpregnant prediction. The authors themselves attribute part of the exposure change to volume (p. 7). No factor is applied (applied blank, cl_factor NA), so the model is not affected; the text should not say the PBPK model predicts x 1.43.
