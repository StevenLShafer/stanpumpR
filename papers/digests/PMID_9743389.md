# Bouillon 1998: "Does size matter?" (editorial on remifentanil in the obese)

PDF pages are one-based; PDF p1 = printed p557 ... PDF p4 = printed p560.

## Study
- Editorial View accompanying Egan et al., remifentanil pharmacokinetics in obese versus lean surgical patients
  (Anesthesiology 1998;89:562-573). No new data.
- Context from Egan (PDF p1, printed p557): a remifentanil dose of 10 µg/kg over 1 min, tolerated in earlier
  non-obese studies, caused profound bradycardia with hypotension in two of the first three obese patients and the
  protocol dose was reduced. The package-insert sedation regimen (1 µg/kg bolus then 0.05 µg/kg/min) may overdose a
  spontaneously breathing obese patient.
- Proposes scaling to lean body mass, using the James (1976) equation (ref. 8, *Research on Obesity*, HMSO), weight in
  kg and height in cm (PDF p1, printed p557; confirmed from image):
  - men: LBM = 1.1 × weight − 128 (weight/height)²
  - women: LBM = 1.07 × weight − 148 (weight/height)²
- Notes LBM scaling has been recommended for thiopental, methohexital, muscle relaxants and propofol.
- **The odd property** (PDF p1-2, printed pp557-558): for every height there is a weight at which LBM peaks, beyond
  which further weight lowers LBM. The authors call this probably an artifact of the equation and **truncated the
  curves beyond the peak** in Figure 1, so the falling limb is not drawn.
- Ideal body weight (PDF p2, printed p558; ref. 15): men 49.9 + 0.89 (height − 152.4) kg; women 45.4 + 0.89
  (height − 152.4) kg. Fig. 1 legend: the LBM formulas do not apply to highly trained athletes with about 10 % body fat
  or less.
- Figure 2 (PDF p2): "weight to use clinically" with doses labelled per kg of total weight, made by multiplying Fig. 1
  by IBW / LBM(at IBW) for each height (footnote), assuming the labelled per-kg doses are right at ideal body weight.
- Worked example (PDF p3, printed p559): a 170 cm woman of 90 kg should be dosed as 72 kg, e.g. 72 µg/min rather than
  90 µg/min for a 1 µg/kg/min label infusion.
- Conclusions (PDF p3, printed p559): scaling to total weight is not correct a priori; scaling to LBM is a testable
  hypothesis; Egan found no significant obese-lean difference in unnormalised parameters and a small but significant
  LBM effect in NONMEM, and either LBM or no scaling beat total-weight scaling; Gepts found weight did not affect
  sufentanil kinetics. Practical rule: below IBW scale to total weight; above it scale to IBW or IBW plus a fraction
  of the excess; rarely to more than 80 kg in a woman or 100 kg in a man. FDA criticised for approving per-kg adult
  doses without evidence of weight-proportional kinetics.

## Caveats
- Editorial opinion; the scaling rule in Figure 2 rests on the stated assumption that per-kg labels are correct at
  ideal body weight.
- The peak and fall of James LBM are stated in words; the paper does not give the peak weight or BMI, and Figure 1
  stops each curve at its peak rather than drawing the decline.
