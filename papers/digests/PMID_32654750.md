# Eleveld 2020: remifentanil TCI targets consistent with the label

## Study
Simulation study, no patients dosed. Demographics (age, weight, height, sex) of 1033 individuals from the Eleveld 2018 propofol database were used as a test population: age 27 weeks postmenstrual to 88 y, weight 0.68-160 kg (Results, PDF p3). Simulations ran in NONMEM 7.4 and were analysed in R. Three remifentanil PK models were run for both plasma and effect-site targeting: Minto 1997, Eleveld 2017 and Kim 2017. For each person, TCI induction dose and starting maintenance infusion rate were calculated at targets of 4 ng/mL (the 2, 6 and 8 ng/mL results were extrapolated, since remifentanil models are linear in dose). Outputs were compared with the FDA label and EMA SmPC dosing for each age group.

Definitions (Methods, PDF p2): TCI induction dose = loading dose at time 0 plus cumulative infusion over the first 45 s. TCI starting maintenance rate = mean non-zero infusion rate from 45 s to 3 min. Induction dose of at least 50% of the label's lower bound was accepted as adequate; maintenance rate within 30% of the label's value was accepted. Severely obese patients (more than 30% over ideal weight) were dosed on ideal body weight, IBW = 22 * height(m)^2. The Minto model was also run with a maximum body weight, as some pumps do. The Kim model has no ke0, so Minto's ke0 was used for it.

## Model and parameters
No new model. It uses published Minto, Eleveld and Kim models without restating their parameters.

Label doses (Table 1, PDF p3, ug/kg and ug/kg/min): induction 1 (neonates, children), 0.5-1 (adults 12-65 y), 50% reduced from adults (>65 y), adult dose scaled to IBW (severely obese); starting maintenance 0.4 +/-30% (neonates), 0.325 +/-30% (children, interpolated), 0.25 +/-30% (adults), 25% reduced (>65 y), adult scaled to IBW (obese); individualised infusion 0.05-1.3 (children) and 0.05-2 (adults).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- **MEAC / analgesic concentration:** none measured. The authors say the target concentrations are dose-consistent and not shown to give clinical effect (abstract).
- **Plasma targets consistent with the label** (population-typical, plasma concentration, not whole-blood vs plasma discussed): about 4 ng/mL for induction and starting maintenance in adults, older and obese patients for all three models; 3.1-5.3 ng/mL once about 30% variability in requirement is allowed (Discussion, PDF p8). Titrated maintenance: 2-13 ng/mL (Minto, Kim) or 2-11 ng/mL (Eleveld), derived as 4 x 3.25 and 4 x 2.9 from the label's upper limit of 1.3 ug/kg/min (PDF p3-4; derived by the authors).
- **Effect-site targeting:** about 1.8-2 ng/mL for induction and about 4 ng/mL for starting maintenance in older patients (abstract and PDF p4, p8).
- **Other target recommendations quoted:** 2-6 ng/mL (ref 18); some sources advise 5-8 ng/mL under 50 y and 3-6 ng/mL over 50 y (Discussion, PDF p8).
- **ke0 / tPeak:** no ke0 value reported. The paper states remifentanil has a time to peak effect of 1-2 min (Methods, PDF p3, ref 14: Egan 1995), and uses Minto's ke0 for the Kim model.
- **Potency/equianalgesic ratios:** none.
- Dose-effect form: induction dose with effect-site targeting is always larger than with plasma targeting at the same target; starting infusion rates barely change (PDF p8).

## Covariates
Age, weight, height and sex as in the tested models. Minto and Kim have no allometric scaling, which the authors give as the reason for steep age-dosing slopes in children, supporting the 12-year lower age limit on Minto in pumps. Eleveld exceptions at 4 ng/mL plasma: starting rate about 45% too high under 2 y, and about 40% too high in severely obese under 30 y (PDF p8).

## Caveats
Authors' limits: labels are not always informative, there is no objective measure of agreement with the label, and the approach shows dose, not clinical effect. Simulation population is the propofol database, not remifentanil patients. The Minto and Kim models were run outside their development ranges (children). Figs 2-5 not read for this card; only text statements are recorded.
