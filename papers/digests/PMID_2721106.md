# Ridd 1989: diazepam disposition and placental transfer at elective caesarean section

## Study
Five healthy multiparous women at 38-40 weeks, not in labour, elective caesarean under epidural bupivacaine (0.5%,
24 mL) and nitrous oxide; age 20-38 y (mean 30.5), weight 64-83 kg (mean 72.3) in four, plus one obese woman (E.S.,
112 kg). Diazepam 5 mg IV over 2 min, about 1.5-3 h before delivery (PDF p2). Samples 0.08-72 h plus a baseline the
day before; umbilical venous blood at delivery. Plasma total, blood and plasma free diazepam (HPLC, linear 1-800 ng;
free fraction by equilibrium dialysis with 14C-diazepam at 37 C, independent of concentration and free of in vitro NEFA
artefact); albumin, total protein and NEFA measured. Free concentration fitted biexponentially (FUNFIT, 1/C2 weights);
total and blood t1/2 from the terminal phase (20 h on); CL and Varea by conventional methods (PDF p3). No parameters
for E.S. (concentrations below the limit within 10 h).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD. Table II (PDF p5), five pairs at delivery (1.63-3.08 h after dosing): percent free maternal 1.79-4.30 (mean 2.88
± 1.05, abstract) and umbilical 1.23-2.15 (1.52 ± 0.39); free concentrations equal across the placenta, so the higher
umbilical total reflects tighter fetal binding. No correlation of cord concentration with Apgar (all 9-10).

## Covariates
Free fraction rose at the start of surgery and at or within 1 h of delivery (mean 72.1% above the day before), tracking
plasma NEFA (300-1000 µmol/L over surgery; per-patient r 0.626-0.888, all p < 0.02); total and blood concentrations rose
about 52% and 37% at delivery while free concentrations declined smoothly (PDF p4, p5). Albumin fell 28% and total
protein 23% on the first day (fluid loading).

## Caveats
n = 4 with parameters; low 5 mg premedicant dose (no oxazepam or desmethyldiazepam detected); historical comparator;
no labour; fasting surgical patients, whose NEFA changes drive the binding.
