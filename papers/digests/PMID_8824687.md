# Desmeules 1996: yohimbine and naloxone antagonism of oral tramadol analgesia

## Study
- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover, five sessions at least 72 h apart (p. 8).
- 10 healthy male volunteers (medical staff/students), age 25.6 (SD 4.5) y, weight 70.9 (SD 11) kg, height
  177 (SD 9.7) cm; all CYP2D6 extensive metabolizers (dextromethorphan/dextrorphan metabolic ratio). One withdrew in
  the fifth session (nausea, drowsiness).
- Treatments: tramadol 100 mg orally (two 50 mg Tramal capsules) or placebo; 3 h later placebo, yohimbine
  0.1 mg/kg IV + placebo, or yohimbine + naloxone 0.8 mg IV.
- End points: subjective pain threshold (pain intensity numerical scale, PINS) and objective threshold (RIII
  nociceptive flexion reflex, sural nerve stimulation, biceps femoris EMG), in mA, each the current giving 50 %
  positive responses to 40 stimuli (Hill fit). Measured at 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 3.25, 3.5, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 h.
- No drug concentrations measured.
- Analysis: one-factor repeated-measures ANOVA with Scheffé comparisons.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Tramadol raised PINS threshold by about 50 % and RIII by about 40 %; analgesia lasted about 6 h.
- Yohimbine reversed tramadol's effect for about 2.75-2.8 h (maximum decrease RIII 97 %, PINS 67 %); adding naloxone
  abolished it for the rest of the study (RIII 90 %, PINS 79 %; P < 0.05), not significantly different from yohimbine
  alone.
- Yohimbine alone did not significantly lower thresholds.
- Discussion cites Collart 1993 (abstract): naloxone 0.8 mg reversed tramadol analgesia by less than 30 % and for less
  than 1 h; that is not a result of this study.

## Caveats
- n = 10 healthy young men, single oral dose, experimental pain.
- Yohimbine selectivity assumed at 0.1 mg/kg; no human data on yohimbine with morphine (authors' second caveat).
- Yohimbine's larger effect on RIII than PINS may be a ventral-horn (motoneuron) action rather than antinociception.
- Antagonists given at 3 h only; partly supported by Grünenthal.
