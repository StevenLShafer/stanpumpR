# Naropin label (NDA 020533, 04/2022): ropivacaine prescribing information

**This file replaces an earlier archived one.** `NJ_label-naropin_020533_2022-09-21.pdf` (supplement s044) was bag
and carton labeling only and carried no prescribing information, so it could not support the statement
`inst/extdata/therapeuticRanges.csv:168` attributes to the label. It was recycled on 2026-10-28 after an unattended
digest found the gap, and this supplement (s038) was archived in its place. Its card was retired with it.

## Study
Not a study: the FDA-approved prescribing information. Ropivacaine hydrochloride injection for epidural, perineural
or infiltration use; initial U.S. approval 1996. The numbers below are the label's summaries of the sponsor's
clinical pharmacology data, not a single trial.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
The label reports no concentration-effect model and no MEAC-equivalent. The one concentration-anchored endpoint, and
the only figure stanpumpR takes from this document, is a **tolerance** threshold in volunteers (12.2, PDF p16):

> maximum tolerated total and free arterial plasma concentrations of 4.3 (3.4 to 5.3) and 0.6 (0.3 to 0.9) mcg/mL,
> mean (min-max), "at which time moderate CNS symptoms (muscle twitching) were noted"

Three things about that number matter for anyone using it.

1. **The endpoint is moderate CNS symptoms (muscle twitching), not seizure.** It is the concentration at which
   volunteers stopped tolerating the infusion, not a seizure threshold.
2. **It is arterial, not venous**, and total and free are reported separately because ropivacaine is 94% bound.
3. **Beware the second 4.3 on the same page.** The preceding sentence gives mean seizure doses after intravenous
   infusion in animals (dog 4.9, nonpregnant sheep 6.1, pregnant sheep 5.9 mg/kg) "associated with peak arterial
   total plasma concentrations of 11.4, **4.3** and 5 mcg/mL, respectively". The nonpregnant sheep's seizure
   concentration and the human volunteers' tolerance concentration are both 4.3 mcg/mL by coincidence and are
   different quantities in different species at different endpoints. Do not conflate them.

Related, from 12.3 (PDF p19): after a 300 mg brachial plexus block "free plasma concentrations of ropivacaine may
approach the threshold for CNS toxicity", and above 300 mg by local infiltration the terminal half-life may exceed
30 hours.

## Covariates
- **Age**: pharmacodynamic differences with age (upper level of analgesia, MAP fall, motor block), but "no
  pharmacokinetic differences were observed between elderly and younger patients" (12.2, PDF p18).
- **CYP1A2 inhibition**: fluvoxamine 25 mg bid for 2 days reduced plasma clearance by 70% (PDF p13). stanpumpR has
  no ropivacaine model, so nothing applies it.
- **Alpha-1-acid glycoprotein**: total concentrations rise during continuous epidural infusion with the
  postoperative rise in AAG, while unbound concentrations vary less (12.3 Distribution). This is why a total
  concentration is a poor marker of effect for this drug and why the label gives both.

## Caveats
- A label, not a primary source: it summarises studies it does not identify, and gives means with SDs but no
  individual data, no model and no covariate equations.
- Table 7's `t1/2` is the true terminal half-life only for the IV row; for every other route it is flip-flop,
  governed by absorption, which the footnote states.
- The copy archived here carries the FDA banner "This label may not be the latest approved by FDA" on every page.
  It is supplement s038, revised 04/2022; a later supplement may exist.

## Used by stanpumpR
One location, from the retired file's context: `inst/extdata/therapeuticRanges.csv:168`, "registry source". No
other file in the repository cites this label.

The library DOES have a ropivacaine model, `R/drugs_ropivacaine.R` (three compartments from Simon 2006's
intravenous deuterium-labelled tracer in subjects 61 and over, as completed for 70 kg in the Hartog thesis, with a
provisional `ka_RA` tissue depot). It does not draw on this label and is not cited by it, but the label's
healthy-volunteer figures are an independent check on it; see below.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **`inst/extdata/therapeuticRanges.csv:168`** records for ropivacaine: band columns 0/0/0, `band_status`
  `none_established`, `verification` "label read", and the note "toxic thresholds only, no therapeutic range;
  Naropin label (2022): maximum tolerated arterial concentration in volunteers 4.3 mcg/mL total, 0.6 free".
  **Matches.** Both figures are the label's, at 12.2 on PDF p16, and the characterisation is right: these are
  tolerance thresholds, not a therapeutic range, and nothing is drawn.
- The row could carry two things this document gives and it does not: the **min-max ranges** (3.4 to 5.3 total,
  0.3 to 0.9 free) and the **endpoint**, moderate CNS symptoms. Neither changes the row's status; both would make
  the figure harder to misread. Not a discrepancy, and not fixed here.
- The provenance is the point worth recording. Until 2026-10-28 this row's "label read" pointed at an archived file
  that was bag and carton labeling and contained no such sentence. The claim was correct all along -- whoever wrote
  it read a real prescribing information -- but the archive could not show it. It can now.
- **`R/drugs_ropivacaine.R`, as a cross-check the model does not cite.** The implemented set is an OLDER-ADULT one
  (Simon 2006, 61 years and over) and its header says so, adding that healthy volunteers have "CL about 0.40 L/min,
  Vss 40 L and a terminal half-life of 1.7 h (Emanuelsson 1997)" which "do not identify a compartmental model, so
  they are not used". This label is a second, independent statement of those healthy-volunteer numbers, and it
  agrees with Emanuelsson: Vss 41 +/- 7 L against 40, CL 21.2 +/- 7 L/h (0.353 L/min) against 0.40, terminal
  half-life 1.9 +/- 0.5 h against 1.7. Against the implemented model at a 70 kg, 65-year-old man with the
  fat-free-mass switch off (Vss 48.3 L, CL 18.0 L/h, terminal half-life 2.46 h), the label's volunteers have a
  **18% smaller Vss, 15% higher clearance and a shorter terminal half-life** -- the direction the header predicts
  when it says "in a younger adult this model overpredicts exposure". **Consistent**, and nothing to change: the
  gap is the population difference the model already documents, now confirmed by a source it was not built from.
