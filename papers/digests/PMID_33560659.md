# Napoleone 2021: low-dose ketamine and closed-loop propofol-remifentanil

## Study
Double-blind, randomized, saline-placebo-controlled, 2-group equivalence trial (NCT03009409), single site (Eagle Ridge Hospital, BC). ASA I-II adults aged 19-54, BMI 15-45, elective orthopedic surgery >60 min. 60 enrolled; 56 analyzed (ketamine n=29, control n=27). Median [IQR] age 29 [23, 38] y, weight 82 [71, 93] kg (abstract); Table 1 gives weight 81.5 [74.5, 93.0] (ketamine) and 81.0 [71.0, 92.0] kg (control), 41 male overall.
- Ketamine group (racemic, 2 mg/mL): 0.25 mg/kg IV over 60 s immediately before induction, then 5 µg/kg/min for up to 45 min (cap 100 mg cumulative). Median total given 35.7 [32.5, 42.4] mg = 470 [466, 477] µg/kg (p.5). Control: equal volume of saline.
- Propofol and remifentanil by closed-loop controller iControl-RP driven by the NeuroSENSE WAVCNS index; initial setpoint 50, remifentanil baseline 0.1 µg/kg/min. Controller's internal PK/PD model is not described in this paper (refs 4, 14).
- Primary outcome: % time within ±10 WAVCNS units of setpoint in maintenance; equivalence margin ±8.99; Hodges-Lehmann CI.
- No drug concentrations were measured.

Dose data (medians [Q1, Q3], p.5 text): propofol maintenance mean dose 128.5 [106.5, 153.1] µg/kg/min (ketamine) vs 106.7 [83.6, 122.2] (control); median difference 24.9 µg/kg/min, 95% CI 6.5-43.0 (abstract prints 43.1), P=.005. Remifentanil 0.13 [0.13, 0.15] vs 0.13 [0.12, 0.14] µg/kg/min, MD 0.01, P=.076. Intraoperative long-acting opioid morphine-equivalent dose (MED) 5.0 [2.7, 7.5] mg vs 7.5 [6.7, 10.0] mg, MD -3.3 mg, P<.001. Primary outcome 86.6% [79.7, 90.2] vs 86.4% [76.5, 89.8], MD 1.0%, 95% CI -3.6 to 5.0.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is the WAVCNS processed-EEG index (not ketamine effect-site concentration). No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, EC50, or MEAC/analgesic concentration reported for any drug. Ketamine was said to be in the analgesic dose range per consensus guidelines (ref 12); no concentration is attached. Propofol consumption was 23% higher with ketamine (24.9 µg/kg/min, p.8 text); the authors suggest ketamine raises WAVCNS, so more propofol is needed for the same index; this is a hypothesis, not tested. Return of consciousness (time to answering question) 12.7 vs 7.8 min, MD 4.8 (2.0-7.5); eye opening 12.2 vs 6.4 min, MD 5.7 (2.9-8.3) (text p.6-7). Potency/equianalgesic ratios: none stated; opioids converted to MED by online calculators (ClinCalc for sufentanil, Johns Hopkins for others), ratios not tabulated.

## Covariates
Sex, age, weight, height are entered into the controller; no covariate analysis of PK.

## Caveats
Not powered for secondary outcomes (propofol dose, opioid, PONV); setpoint could be changed by clinicians and differed between groups; no EMG adjustment; single dosing regimen; text says "9 of 27" ketamine cases had setpoint <50 although n=29 (internal inconsistency); abstract and text give CI upper limit 43.1 vs 43.0.
