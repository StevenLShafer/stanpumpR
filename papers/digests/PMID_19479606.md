# Murphy 2009: syringe-pump flow error from venous pressure changes

## Study
Engineering paper, no patients. A first-order model of a syringe infusion pump plus line (Flow In = Flow Out + Flow Internal, where Flow Internal = C dP2/dt, Flow Out = A (P2 - P1), A = pi R^4 / (8 eta L) by Poiseuille). Implemented in MATLAB Simulink and checked against bench measurements (water, weighed on a balance accurate to 0.0001 g) of the flow disturbance when pump height changed. Setup: 50 mL disposable syringe, 1.5 m extension line (internal bore 1.5 mm), 20 G cannula (35 mm long, 0.36 mm diameter); set rate 2 mL/h in the bench test.

## Model and parameters
Compliance C measured by occluding the line to 500 mmHg and releasing: bolus 0.5 mL, linear over 0-500 mmHg (PDF p. 3). Typical syringe-pump systems give 0.5-1 mL bolus at this occlusion pressure. A derived from the line and cannula dimensions.

```yaml
parameters:
  - {name: compliance C, value: 0.8e-11, unit: "m3/Pa", source: "p. 471 (printed)", note: "0.5 mL per 500 mmHg, converted by the authors"}
  - {name: restriction coefficient A, value: 0.1e-11, unit: "m3/(Pa s)", source: "p. 472-473 (printed)", note: "as printed in the text layer, 0.1e-11; derived from line plus cannula dimensions"}
  - {name: time constant, value: 8, unit: s, source: "p. 473 (printed)", note: "C/A; independent of set rate"}
  - {name: flow change per venous pressure change, value: 0.5, unit: "mL/h per mmHg", source: "p. 473 (printed)", note: "independent of set rate"}
```

Results (p. 473, printed): at 1 mL/h, +1 mmHg step drops flow at once to 0.5 mL/h, recovering with tau 8 s; -1 mmHg raises it to 1.5 mL/h. A 10 mmHg step gives -4 and +6 mL/h. A +/-5 mmHg sinusoid of period about 6 s gave peak-to-peak error of about +/-2.38 mL/h at 1 mL/h; period about 60 s gave about +/-1.5 mL/h; period about 10 min still gave about +/-20% of the set rate. Bench vs simulated height-change steps (at 106, 226, 369, 474 s) agreed closely in step size and time constant (Figs 2 and 3, not read as images).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None; no drug PK/PD. (Audit: no MEAC, ke0 or potency data.) The authors name dopamine, remifentanil and propofol as short half-life drugs where such fluctuation matters, and suggest manufacturers publish the compliance factor.

## Covariates
None. Error depends on set rate only in percentage terms, not in absolute mL/h.

## Caveats
One syringe type and line set; water at 20 C; simulated (not measured) venous pressure changes; model is linear with constant compliance; only the pump-height disturbance was validated on the bench.
