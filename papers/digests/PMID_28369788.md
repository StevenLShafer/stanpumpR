# Gomeni 2017: double-Weibull convolution model of methylphenidate ER products

## Study
Model-based meta-analysis of **digitised mean** concentration curves (Plot Digitizer), no individual data.
- PK development: Concerta 18, 36, 54 mg mean curves from 8 publications (refs 24-31; adults).
- Qualification: Ritalin LA 40 mg, Metadate CD 20/40/60 mg, Aptensio XR 80 mg, Quillivant XR 60 mg,
  Focalin XR 20 mg, from 5 labels (refs 32-36).
- PK/PD: mean MPH concentrations and SKAMP scores for placebo and Concerta 18/36/54 mg from Kimko 2012 (children).
- NONMEM 7.3 (mixed effects on mean curves; IIV log-normal; combined error); R 3.2.5 + NLOPTR; PopED for design.
FDA-funded (1U01FD005240-02).

## Model and parameters
Disposition: one compartment, first-order elimination, kel and V = Vd/F (F assumed 1). Three absorption models
compared (dual first-order with lags; lag + transit compartments; convolution with prescribed input); the
convolution model with a double Weibull r(t) was retained. ff = fraction in first process, td/td1 = time to
release 63.2% in first/second process, ss/ss1 = sigmoidicity.

**kel and V are not printed in the article; they are in Supplementary Table 3** (S3), as the reference values for
the design optimisation: kel 0.18 /h, V 1520 L (V = Vd/F, F assumed 1) (derived: half-life 3.85 h, CL/F 274 L/h).
The same table gives Concerta's release parameters (ss 3.18, ss1 6.33, td 0.76 h, td1 3.4 h, ff 0.189) and the SKAMP
Emax 0.643 and EC50 0.725 (units not printed), each with its RSE under the 13-sample reference and 7-sample
optimised designs (kel 5.36/6.05%, V 4.96/5.93%, EC50 20.94/19.79%). No IIV values printed.

Internal inconsistency: the Results text (p955) gives Concerta's initial td1 as 3.40 h and its ss/ss1 as 3.18 and
6.33, i.e. the ss1 and td1 columns of Table 1 swapped, and Supplementary Table 3 has the same swap (ss1 6.33, td1
3.4). (derived: td1 * (ln 100)^(1/ss1) with td1 = 6.33, ss1 = 3.40 gives 9.9 h, close to the printed td1_99%
9.79 h; the swapped order gives 4.3 h.) **Supplementary Figure 2 (S5, the in vivo release curves) settles it in
favour of Table 1**: Concerta's curve reads about 0.65 released at 6 h and 0.9 at 8 h (read from the figure), and
0.19 + 0.81 x (1 - exp(-(t/6.33)^3.40)) gives 0.65 and 0.91 (derived), whereas td1 3.4, ss1 6.33 would give
essentially 1.0 by 6 h. So Table 1 is right and the text and Supplementary Table 3 carry the swap. Concerta: ~20%
released in the first process, ~80% in the second; 99% release 1.1 h and 9.8 h.

Optimised release (Concerta 54 mg reference): td 0.31 vs 0.76 h, td1 10.84 vs 3.40 h, ff 0.18 vs 0.19, ss/ss1
1.00/2.15 vs 3.18/6.33; AUEC0-12 39.61 -> 42.59 (+7.5%), AUEC0-24 69.16 -> 88.11 (+27.4%).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point SKAMP composite (classroom rating). Placebo: indirect response with time-varying stimulus
p(t) = AA (e^-P1 t - e^-P2 t). Drug: Emax Cp/(EC50(t) + Cp), with acute tolerance as EC50 rising over time
(EC50b, t50, gamma). The article prints no PD values; Supplementary Table 3 gives Emax 0.643 and EC50 0.725, units
not stated, and the tolerance (t50, gamma) and placebo parameters are printed nowhere. No ke0. Optimal design: 20 subjects, 7 PK and 8 SKAMP
samples (Fig 6, Suppl Table 3).

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Mean digitised data; random effects not estimable (fixed at CV 15% for design); disposition and two PD parameters
only in the supplement; Table 1 / text / Supplementary Table 3 inconsistency for Concerta's second-process
parameters (resolved by Supplementary Figure 2). Analyte mixed: Supplementary Table 1 shows several model-building
sources reporting d-MPH (refs 26, 29-31) beside racemic MPH, and all are adults, so kel and V describe "MPH" in a
mixture of analytes, not d-MPH.
