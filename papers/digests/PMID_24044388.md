# O'Brien 2013: reaction-time-monitored patient-maintained propofol sedation

## Study
Open pilot, 20 ASA 1-2 oral surgery patients (3 men, 17 women), age 35.0 (14.7) y, weight 72.6 (14.7) kg (printed p762). Graseby 3500 effect-site TCI, Marsh PK model with t1/2 ke0 2.6 min (printed p761, ref 8). Start Ce 1 ug/mL; the patient double-presses a handset within 1 s for a 0.2 ug/mL increase, maximum 3 ug/mL. Two lockouts: effect-site (plasma within 10% of target Ce; plasma may be driven up to 100% above target Ce) and reaction-time. Handset vibrates every 1 min; baseline = mean of last two of at least three pre-sedation reaction times; a time under 30% of baseline is rechecked after 20 s. Surgery under local anaesthetic (lidocaine 2% with adrenaline 1:80 000). End points: OAAS 2 or less, SpO2 < 90%, airway intervention, apnoea.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0 or EC50 estimated; no MEAC (not an opioid study). End points: OAAS, SpO2, reaction time. Lowest OAAS 4 in 9 patients and 5 in 11; none lost verbal contact. Lowest SpO2 mean 98.0 (2.1)%, minimum 91%, on room air. Mean sedation time including surgery 25 (11) min. Comparison with the earlier 40-patient study (ref 2): max Ce 1.5 (0.4) ug/mL there. Satisfaction: all happy, 19 of 20 would repeat, surgeon rated cooperation very good in 17 and good in 3; 14 of 20 recalled surgery.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
Pilot, n = 20, uncontrolled, healthy ASA 1-2, mostly women; calculated Ce only (Marsh model, t1/2 ke0 2.6 min); the effect-site lockout is device behaviour, not a finding; the authors call for larger studies before use without an anaesthetist.
