# Solanki 2010: BIS-controlled closed-loop vs manual propofol sedation after cardiac surgery

## Study
Randomised pilot trial, 41 ASA 2-3 adults (18-65 y) after elective open-heart surgery; 21 CLADS, 20 manual; one CLADS patient excluded (re-exploration), so 20 vs 20 analysed (Fig 1, PDF p4). Sedation in PACU for 4-8 h, target BIS 70, propofol only. CLADS = BIS-driven closed-loop controller (Fresenius Pilot C pump, Datex AS5/GE monitor); its algorithm uses propofol PK (distribution, clearance), delays in BIS sensing and in concentration change, and the "peak effect" of propofol, but no values are given (PDF p1-2; cites ref 9). Manual group: anaesthesiologist titrated the rate to BIS 70. Morphine 0.05 mg/kg for analgesia. Performance by Varvel 1992 metrics.
Table 2 (mean ± SD, CLADS vs manual): age 38.3 ± 14.7 vs 38.1 ± 13.5 y; BMI 21.3 ± 4.1 vs 22.6 ± 4.0; CPB 116.6 ± 45.2 vs 107.7 ± 36.2 min; PACU arrival temperature 36.1 vs 36.4 °C.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, EC50, MEAC or potency ratio. End point is BIS (target 70, sedation). The authors mention that the controller's built-in delay accounts for hysteresis between delivery and effect (PDF p5), with no value. Valid CLADS time 88.0 ± 14.7% of sedation time vs 84.6 ± 21.9% valid BIS in the manual group (Table 3). Manual group needed a median 19 (IQR 10-24) rate changes; CLADS 0 (Table 4).

## Covariates
CLADS scales infusion by body weight (stated, PDF p2); no covariate equations. Propofol PK after cardiopulmonary bypass/hypothermia is noted as unstudied in the return-to-normal timeframe.

## Caveats
Pilot, unblinded; the authors note a Hawthorne effect in the manual arm and investigator bias. Table 3 and Table 4 units/labels look inconsistent (see open_questions). EMG artefact in non-paralysed patients.
