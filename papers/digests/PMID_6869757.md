# Schwilden 1983: BET infusion scheme, theoretical considerations

## Study
Two-page conference paper (Bonn). Conceptual chain dose -> pharmacokinetics -> receptor kinetics -> pharmacodynamics (Fig. 1); argues that for etomidate, alfentanil, nitroprusside and newer benzodiazepines the kinetic time constants are slow relative to receptor and dynamic ones, so effects can be controlled from kinetic data. Linear compartment models and superposition allow calculation of the administration scheme for a desired concentration time course.

## Model and parameters
**BET scheme**: Bolus to fill the central volume, an infusion compensating Elimination, and an exponentially decreasing infusion compensating Transfer to the peripheral compartment until its saturation. Combining BET schemes at different levels builds step functions. Fig. 2 example, etomidate target 0.5 ug/mL:

Fig. 3: 200 simulated patients with kinetics drawn randomly within 2 SD of the means of 12 patients. Fig. 4: one mean patient under various published schemes vs the BET variability range.

## Caveats
Simulation only; conclusion that interindividual variability is small relative to differences between dosing schemes rests on 12 patients' etomidate kinetics. Byline spells the third author "Stoekel".
