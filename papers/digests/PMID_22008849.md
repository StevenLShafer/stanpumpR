# Musk 2012: placental transfer of medetomidine and ketamine in pregnant ewes

## Study
- Animal study (sheep). Date-mated merino-cross ewes, 5-6 years old, gestation 128-130 days (term 150):
  singleton pregnant (n=8), twin pregnant (n=8), non-pregnant (n=8) (PDF p 2, printed 47).
- Weights (Table 1, mean (SD), unit not printed, presumably kg): non-pregnant 61.7 (3.2), singleton 60.6 (2.5),
  twin 70.7 (4.3) (twin vs singleton P = 0.039).
- Single IV bolus of medetomidine 20 µg/kg plus ketamine 10 mg/kg (racemic, Ketamil). Pregnant ewes then had
  subarachnoid lidocaine and caesarean delivery; non-pregnant ewes lay recumbent 10 min; all euthanised.
- Maternal venous samples at 1, 5 and 10 min; one umbilical venous sample per fetus at delivery (singleton 9.3
  (1.5) min; twins 8.1 (1.0) and 14.1 (1.2) min after induction; Table 1).
- Assay: SPE-LC-MS/MS of plasma (ion trap; ketamine m/z 238→207). LOQ, precision not reported here (method in ref 21).
- No PK model; no statistical test named for the concentration comparisons.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Lambs at delivery tolerated oral intubation without further drugs (assessed by gross movement, toe-pinch
withdrawal, tongue withdrawal); time to supplemental anaesthesia 3-77 min, mean (SD) 42 (6) min. No
concentration-effect relation, ke0, or EC50.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (vs non-pregnant) and singleton vs twin: no difference in maternal plasma concentration reported at
1, 5 or 10 min (p 2; Figs 1-2).

## Caveats
- Sheep, not humans; a single combined bolus with medetomidine; only 10 min of maternal sampling, so clearance and
  terminal disposition in pregnancy cannot be assessed.
- n=8 per group; no named statistical test or power; error-bar type not stated.
- Concentrations available only as figures.
