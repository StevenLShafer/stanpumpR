# Trocóniz 2007: population PK of dabigatran after total hip replacement (BISTRO I)

## Study
BISTRO I: randomised, open-label, multicentre (Sweden 11 sites, Norway 7) phase IIa dose escalation (p. 2). 289 patients undergoing primary elective total hip replacement received dabigatran etexilate tablets: 12.5, 25, 50, 100, 150, 200 or 300 mg twice daily, or 150 or 300 mg once daily, for 6-10 days. Two patients had no PK data, so 287 were analysed with 4604 concentrations (p. 2, p. 6). First dose 4-8 h after the end of surgery on day 0; next dose about 08:00 on day 1 (12-16 h interval; p. 2). The experimental tablet had no excipient to keep it soluble at raised gastric pH (p. 2). H2-blockers and PPIs were exclusions. Sparse sampling on days 0-10, plus a 7-sample day-4 profile at one centre (p. 3).

Demographics (Table I, p. 3), mean (SD) and range:
- Time to first dose 6.448 (4.43) h, range 1.42-21.83.
- Age 66.97 (9.7), 35-88 y. Weight 78.21 (14.9), 49-130 kg. Height 170.5 (9), 150-203 cm. BMI 26.78 (3.9).
- SCr 0.964 (0.2), 0.66-1.81 mg/dL. CRCL (Cockcroft-Gault) 76.16 (24.3), 29.35-161.1 mL/min.
- **Serum gastrin 34.58 (55), 10-501 pmol/L.**
- 47% male. Opioids in 57%, paracetamol in 70%.

Assay: total BIBR 953 ZW (dabigatran plus its active acylglucuronides, after alkaline cleavage) by LC-MS/MS, LOQ 1 ng/mL (pp. 4-5). NONMEM V, FOCE with interaction (p. 5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. The paper is PK only; Figure 1 labels PD samples, but no PD is reported. No therapeutic range.

## Covariates
- Gastrin on both CL/F (higher gastrin, lower exposure, read as lower bioavailability at raised gastric pH; p. 9).
- CRCL linear on CL/F from day 2: 11% more exposure per 10 mL/min decrease (p. 9).
- SCr and age lower ka(>24 h). The authors read this as flip-flop kinetics: these covariates act on what is really elimination (p. 9).
- No effect of weight, sex, smoking or alcohol (p. 9).
- COM3 (GI-passage accelerating drugs), COM12, low dose and once-daily dosing reached significance but were not retained (p. 7).

## Caveats
- ka(<24 h) of 0.022 /h (t1/2 31.5 h) is a flip-flop rate (p. 8).
- No IV data, so a bioavailability change cannot be separated from a clearance change. A relative-F parameterisation fitted similarly (p. 8).
- MPE for CL/F(<24 h) was +20.5% (p. 7). Population predictions underpredict high concentrations (p. 7).
- Only 9 patients had severe renal impairment (p. 9). The renal groups of Figure 6 put moderate-to-severe at CRCL <= 40 mL/min.
- The linear (1 - θ x SCR/0.964) factor reaches zero at SCr 2.66 mg/dL (derived), outside the fitted 0.66-1.81.
- Residual-error placement is inconsistent within the paper. The text (p. 7), Table II and Table III put the combined error on days 0-1; Table IV's equations put it on days 2-10.
- The breakpoint is labelled "<24 h / >24 h" but defined as "days 0 and 1" vs "days 2 to 10" (Tables II-III footnotes, abstract, Discussion). The paper does not say how a dose or an observation was assigned at the boundary.
- The tablet formulation is not the marketed capsule (tartaric acid core). With pantoprazole, AUC fell 30% for the capsule and 80% for the tablet (p. 9).
