# Karunajeewa 2009: sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine PK in pregnant Papua New Guinean women

## Study
- Alexishafen Health Centre, Madang, Papua New Guinea, Feb-Jul 2006 (PDF p. 1). 30 pregnant women (23 second
  trimester, 14-27 wk; 7 third trimester, 28-31 wk; gestational age median 22 wk [20-28]) and 30 age-matched
  nonpregnant women from the same villages (PDF p. 3).
- Table 1 (PDF p. 3): weight 54.0 +/- 6.4 kg pregnant vs 51.8 +/- 5.5 kg nonpregnant; height 151 vs 149 cm;
  hemoglobin 80 vs 107 g/L; P. falciparum parasitemia 13 (43%) vs 7 (23%); age 26.0 +/- 50.9 [sic] vs 25.5 +/- 8.9 y.
- Single oral dose of Fansidar: sulfadoxine (SDOX) 1,500 mg + pyrimethamine (PYR) 75 mg, plus chloroquine 450 mg
  base daily for 3 days; supervised, not fasted (PDF p. 2).
- Venous samples at 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 48, 72 h and days 7, 10, 14, 28, 42.
- Assay: HPLC-UV (270 nm), plasma. Standard curves 0.1-250 mg/L SDOX, 0.2-10 mg/L N-acetylsulfadoxine (NASDOX),
  2.5-1,000 ug/L PYR. Stated LOQs 0.1, 0.02 and 2.5 ug/L (units as printed; the SDOX/NASDOX limits of detection are
  given in mg/L, so the SDOX/NASDOX LOQ units look misprinted). Values below LOQ (< 0.5%) set to LOQ/2.
- NONMEM 6.2.0, FOCE with interaction; PsN bootstrap (1000) and VPC; covariates by stepwise forward (dOFV 6.63)
  and backward (10.82) selection.
- Allometric scaling on all terms: CL * (WT/70)^0.75, V * (WT/70)^1. All CL and V are apparent (/F).

## Model and parameters
SDOX: two-compartment parent with first-order absorption (no lag), metabolic conversion (CLM/F) to a
one-compartment NASDOX; CL/F of NASDOX fixed at 10 x CL/F of SDOX (Fig. 2, PDF p. 3). PYR: two-compartment,
first-order absorption, no lag. Pregnancy enters additively (typical value + pregnancy increment); the stated
fractions of total (SDOX CL 32%; PYR CL/VC/VP 33.5/34/56%) reproduce from the table values (derived).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect model, MIC or target concentration (authors note an in vivo PYR MIC is hard to define).
Clinical outcome only: of 13 pregnant and 7 nonpregnant women with P. falciparum at entry, 5 and 2 redeveloped
parasitemia by day 28 (PDF p. 3). Simulated dosing (PDF p. 8): 4 tablets stat or 1.5 tablets daily x3 in pregnancy
raise SDOX/PYR AUC toward the nonpregnant 3-tablet value. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Pregnancy only (additive): SDOX CL/F; PYR CL/F, VC/F, VP/F. Weight via fixed allometry (exponents 0.75 / 1).
Age, gestational age, hemoglobin, parasitemia and glucose tested, not retained.

**Comparators**: every "higher/lower in pregnancy" claim (SDOX AUC 33% lower, CL/F 67% higher; PYR AUC 32% lower,
CL/F 47% higher, Vss/F 117% higher) is pregnant PNG women vs the study's own age-matched nonpregnant PNG women,
median post hoc values. The longer PYR t1/2beta (9.9 and 18.8 d vs 2.9-5.1 d in adults) is against earlier
literature with shorter sampling, mostly one-compartment fits.

## Caveats
- Coadministered chloroquine (450 mg base daily x3) in every subject; not fasted.
- Small, lean (about 52-54 kg) Melanesian population; half parasitemic.
- PYR Table 4 bootstrap BSV medians (CL 7.3, VC 5.8, VP 16.3, Q 29.6, ka 112) do not resemble the final estimates
  (27.6, 24.6, 42.5, 57.5, 10.7); the column looks misaligned or in a different metric. Bootstrap CI for pregnancy on
  VP/F printed as 53.2-1205 (likely 120.5). Not resolvable from the PDF.
- Discussion (PDF p. 7) quotes nonpregnant PYR CL/F as 0.71 L/h/70 kg, but 0.707 is the absolute post hoc median in
  Table 5; the typical value per 70 kg is 0.87 (Table 4).
- SDOX Q/F has RSE 44.5% and BSV ka 99.6%; SDOX VP/F (1.11 L) is small next to VC/F (15.8 L).
