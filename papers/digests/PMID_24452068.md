# Laib 2014: serum 4-hydroxybupropion and response to bupropion (TDM threshold)

## Study
Retrospective, naturalistic analysis of routine therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) requests from 10 psychiatric
hospitals (Mainz TDM service). n = 52 patients on bupropion for at least 8 days (26 men, 26 women; age 47 ± 14 y,
Table 1, PDF p3 / printed p475; Table 1 prints "52/52 (50/50)" for men/women, a typo for 26/26 per Table 2).
Diagnoses mainly depressive episodes, recurrent depression, bipolar disorder. Doses 75-450 mg/d (75 mg: n = 1;
150 mg: 21; 300 mg: 24; 450 mg: 6). Formulation (IR/SR/XL) and dosing schedule not stated; body weight not
recorded (the authors say so, p6). 45 of 52 had comedication documented; 40 (88.9%) took other psychotropics,
26 (57.8%) another antidepressant, 12 (26.7%) quetiapine.

Sampling: steady state, morning trough before the first daily dose (p2 / printed p474). Matrix: **serum**.
Assay: new HPLC-UV with on-line column-switching cleanup; 4-hydroxybupropion read at 214 nm. LOQ 20 ng/mL
for 4-hydroxybupropion (5 ng/mL bupropion), detection limit 7.8 ng/mL; imprecision < 13%; recovery 97.4%
(p4 / printed p476). Bupropion itself was not analysed for the study because of its instability.

End point: Clinical Global Impression (CGI) improvement, rated by the treating psychiatrist on the TDM request
form; responder = "markedly improved" (the ROC figure and Discussion call it "very much improved").

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- By CGI improvement (p4 / printed p476, Fig. 2): markedly 1113 ± 576, moderately 825 ± 398, minimally
  475 ± 331 ng/mL (P = 0.042); mean doses 275 ± 113, 260 ± 91 and 194 ± 81 mg/d.
- About 50% improved moderately, 12% markedly, 23% minimally (p4). (derived: 12% of 52 ≈ 6 responders.)
- **ROC threshold 858 ng/mL** for "markedly improved", specificity 76%, sensitivity 67%, ROC P = 0.002
  (p4, Fig. 3; abstract). Rounded to **about 860 ng/mL** as the proposed lower limit (p6 / printed p478).
- **Upper limit 1500 ng/mL is not derived from these data**: no concentration-related side-effect threshold
  was found (patients without side effects had higher levels, 889 ± 544 vs 750 ± 386 ng/mL with mild effects,
  p6); 1500 ng/mL was taken over from the 2011 AGNP guideline (their ref 16) on seizure/insomnia grounds.
  Proposed range 860-1500 ng/mL (p6).
- The 2011 AGNP lower limit of 225 ng/mL (cited by the authors as derived from PK, not PD) is contrasted.
- Background, not measured here: 4-hydroxybupropion said to have about half bupropion's activity and about
  20-fold higher steady-state plasma concentrations (Introduction, citing others).
- No ke0, EC50, or potency ratio. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Sex: women had higher concentrations at similar doses and a better CGI effect (P = 0.018); weight unavailable,
so the authors could not separate sex from weight-adjusted dose. No covariate model.

## Caveats
Retrospective, naturalistic; about 6 responders behind the ROC cut-off (derived); raters differed across 10
hospitals; heavy confounding by co-prescribed antidepressants and quetiapine (norquetiapine is also
noradrenergic); time since start of therapy varied; no weights; formulation and dose interval unknown, so
"trough" is not a fixed time after dose. HPLC-UV, serum.
