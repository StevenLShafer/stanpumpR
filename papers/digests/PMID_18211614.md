# Ueshima 2008: total against unbound valproate in epileptic children

## Study
Retrospective population analysis of routine therapeutic-drug-monitoring records, Department of Child
Neurology, Okayama University Hospital, 1 April 2003 to 31 January 2006. **342 paired total/unbound valproate
records from 108 patients** aged 1-18 years.

Exclusions (printed p 32): severe or mild renal or hepatic dysfunction, hypoalbuminaemia (albumin
< 3.0 g/dL), and any non-antiepileptic medication reported to affect valproate metabolism or binding.
All patients took valproate tablets, syrup or powder (Depakene) two to three times daily for at least five
days, 4.8-87.9 mg/kg/day (median 33.3); samples were drawn about **3 hours after the morning dose**.

Assay: total valproate by FPIA (AxSYM, Abbott), CV < 10%; the unbound fraction separated by Amicon YM-30
ultrafiltration and measured the same way against free-valproate calibrators.

Data sets (Table 1, printed p 33): monotherapy (n = 88 records), combination with **one** additional
antiepileptic (n = 147; subsets: zonisamide 43, ethosuximide 43, phenytoin 19, clonazepam 19,
carbamazepine 12, clobazam 11) and polytherapy with at least two additional drugs (n = 107). Phenobarbital
and primidone were excluded as too infrequent. Median total valproate 99.6 ug/mL (monotherapy; range
19.6-179.8), well above the quoted therapeutic 50-100 ug/mL - high-dose therapy is the point of the study.

## Model and parameters
A **Langmuir (one-site saturable binding) model** fitted in NONMEM V, with interindividual variability on
both parameters:

`Cb = Bm x Cf / (Kd + Cf)`, `Ct = (Cb + Cf) x (1 + eps)`

where Ct, Cf and Cb are total, unbound and bound serum valproate (ug/mL), **Bm** the apparent maximum
binding-site concentration and **Kd** the apparent dissociation constant. Four nested models were compared
(printed p 34): Model 0 (no comedication effect), Model 1 (comedication on Bm), Model 2 (on Kd), Model 3
(on both), with a Boolean AED indicator; significance by the change in the objective function (chi-square).

**Table 2** (printed p 34, PDF p 4; read from the rendered page) - change in objective function from Model 0:

| Data set | n (patients) | Model 0 | Model 1 | Model 2 | Model 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| + zonisamide | 131 (52) | 741.96 | -0.92 | -1.30 | -1.43 |
| + ethosuximide | 131 (45) | 759.88 | -2.42 | **-4.37 (P<0.05)** | -5.02 |
| + phenytoin | 107 (44) | 610.42 | -1.70 | -0.58 | -3.37 |
| + clonazepam | 107 (49) | 628.98 | -0.03 | -0.16 | -0.38 |
| + carbamazepine | 100 (45) | 567.09 | -0.05 | -0.26 | -1.34 |
| + clobazam | 99 (41) | 563.10 | -0.11 | -0.34 | -0.82 |
| polytherapy | 195 (79) | 1092.83 | -0.06 | -0.59 | **-6.11 (P<0.05)** |

So **only ethosuximide (on Kd) and polytherapy (on both) significantly altered valproate protein binding**;
zonisamide, phenytoin, carbamazepine, clonazepam and clobazam did not.

**Table 3** (printed p 35, PDF p 5; read from the rendered page) - final Model 3 fit to all 342 records:

| Case | Bm (ug/mL) | Kd (ug/mL) |
|---|---|---|
| A (neither B nor C) | **130.0 +/- 4.5** | **7.8 +/- 0.7** |
| B (valproate + ethosuximide) | 130.0 +/- 4.5 (theta3 fixed at 1) | **6.8 +/- 0.6** |
| C (polytherapy) | **105.8 +/- 3.6** | **4.2 +/- 0.4** |

Mean +/- SE. The text states the corresponding changes: Kd fell **12.8%** with ethosuximide; Bm fell
**18.6%** and Kd **46.2%** on polytherapy (printed p 35).

**Directions** (printed p 35, Figure 1): the ethosuximide curve shifts **rightward only slightly** (at a
given unbound concentration the total is higher - i.e. slightly *less* free drug at a given total), while
the polytherapy curve shifts **markedly leftward** (more free drug at a given total). At a total of
150 ug/mL the unbound concentration on polytherapy is **27.5% higher** (52.1 vs 40.8 ug/mL).

Population comparison the authors make (printed p 36): Bm 130.0 ug/mL = **0.90 mM** and Kd 7.8 ug/mL =
**54 uM**, against literature values of 0.75-1.24 mM and 36-91 uM.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. The paper's clinical argument is that unbound valproate relates to adverse effects
(hyperammonaemia, pancytopenia) and efficacy, citing the literature; it measures no outcome and fits no
concentration-effect model.

## Covariates
Comedication only, as a Boolean on Bm and/or Kd. Age and weight were compared between data sets and found
not to differ significantly, and were not covariates in the model. Albumin is an **exclusion** criterion
(< 3.0 g/dL), not a covariate - notable, since albumin is the binding protein. No sex, renal or hepatic
covariate.

## Caveats
- **Retrospective TDM records**, with comedication assigned by clinical practice rather than design, and
  very unequal subset sizes (11-43 records).
- Samples were drawn **3 h after the morning dose**, not at trough; the binding relation is modelled as
  instantaneous, so this matters only if the free fraction varies within the interval - which it does, since
  binding is saturable and the total concentration swings.
- The ethosuximide finding is statistically modest (dObj -4.37 on one parameter, P < 0.05) and its
  **direction is counter-intuitive**: ethosuximide is < 5% protein bound, so a direct displacement is
  implausible; the authors say so and offer an indirect metabolic/distributional explanation by analogy with
  carbapenems (printed p 36-37). The ethosuximide effect **lowers** the free concentration slightly, while
  polytherapy **raises** it.
- The polytherapy mechanism proposed - enzyme induction raising 2-en-valproate, which binds albumin more
  strongly and displaces valproate - is a hypothesis supported by citation, not measured here.
- **Japanese children, 1-18 years**, with high-dose valproate (median 99.6 ug/mL total in monotherapy);
  extrapolation to adults or to the usual 50-100 ug/mL band is the authors' own caveat (the polytherapy
  effect is "not obvious" at 50-100 ug/mL and only grows above it).
- A single-site Langmuir fit to total/unbound pairs identifies Bm and Kd only as **apparent** constants of
  that fit; they are not a measured albumin concentration or affinity.
