# Glass 1993: preliminary PK/PD of remifentanil (GI87084B) versus alfentanil

## Study
Double-blind, randomised, parallel-group dose escalation in healthy male volunteers, ASA I, 18-40 yr, <100 kg, within 15% of ideal weight (PDF p.2). Each volunteer studied once, 1-min IV infusion (20 mL, 60 s). Remifentanil starting at 0.0625 mcg/kg, doubled per group (0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, with an added 1.5 and 2 mcg/kg group); alfentanil 8, 16, 32 mcg/kg; placebo pooled (n=7). Four volunteers per active dose group, one placebo per group. Escalation stopped when half of a group needed prompting to keep SpO2 above 85% (reached at 2 mcg/kg). Text says 48 completed (29 remifentanil, 12 alfentanil, 7 placebo); Table 1 (PDF p.4) lists 30 remifentanil (age 24.6 yr, 181.2 cm, 77.6 kg), 12 alfentanil (23.1 yr, 180.1 cm, 75.2 kg), 7 placebo (24.7 yr, 177.7 cm, 70.4 kg); the 29/30 difference is unexplained (one volunteer with a pump error was excluded and replaced).
Sampling: arterial blood at 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 20, 30, 40, 60, 90, 120 min after the end of infusion; urine to 24 h. Assay: whole-blood remifentanil by GC/high-resolution MS (trideuterated internal standard; LLOQ lowered to 0.25 then 0.1 ng/mL); whole-blood alfentanil by GC-MS (LOD 1 ng/mL). Both are WHOLE BLOOD, not plasma.
Effect measures: pain tolerance to a spring-loaded rod on tibia and sternum (maximum pressure capped at 10 kg per 4.43 mm2), PaCO2, PaO2, SpO2, haemodynamics. PK by noncompartmental (SAS) and compartmental (PCNONLIN, weights 1/C^2) analysis; PD by sigmoid Emax (SAS), with PK constrained to each subject's fit. PK/PD modelling was limited to the 2 mcg/kg remifentanil group (n=4), the only group with adequate PK data.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit:
- **ke0 / t1/2 ke0, remifentanil, end point = tibial pressure analgesia (spring-loaded rod):** ke0 0.528 +/- 0.42 /min (text p.6); t1/2 ke0 1.31 min harmonic mean (jackknife SD 1.5; Table 3 prints 1.3 (1.5)) (p.4, p.8). Derived: ln2/0.528 = 1.31 min, consistent. n=4, 2 mcg/kg group only. Peak analgesic effect at the 1- or 3-min assessment (p.4).
- **EC50 (remifentanil, analgesia):** 4.66 +/- 3.3 ng/mL, whole blood (text p.6); gamma 1.76 +/- 0.44; baseline tolerance 2.94 +/- 0.52 kg per 4.43 mm2 (p.6). This is the concentration for half-maximal effect on the rod end point with Emax fixed at the 10 kg cap, NOT a MEAC. No MEAC / minimum effective concentration is defined.
- **t1/2 ke0, EEG spectral edge:** 1.33 min for remifentanil and 1.1 min for alfentanil, quoted from Egan et al. (abstract, ref 8), not measured here (p.8).
- **Potency ratios (remifentanil : alfentanil), on dose (mg:mg, single bolus), not concentration:** analgesia on tibia 22 (95% CI 13-48), on sternum 47 (95% CI -13 to 104) (p.4); respiratory depression 23 (95% CI 14.8-41.2) from maximal PaCO2 rise, 20.7 (14.76-31.38) from lowest PaO2 (p.4); summarised as 20 to 30 times (abstract). The authors state the ratio will differ on an infusion-rate basis or on a plasma-concentration (ng/mL) basis because of the different PK (p.8). Alfentanil 32 mcg/kg and remifentanil 1.5-2 mcg/kg gave similar peak effect.
- Respiratory depression: peak on PaCO2/PaO2 at 5 min; return to within 10% of baseline 30 min (alfentanil 32 mcg/kg) vs 20 min (remifentanil 2 mcg/kg); SpO2 <85% for 1-2 min in 3/4 at 2 mcg/kg remifentanil and 2/4 at 32 mcg/kg alfentanil (p.4).
- Haemodynamics: transient rise in SBP 5-20 mm Hg and HR 10-25/min above remifentanil 0.25 and alfentanil 8 mcg/kg, back to baseline by 10 min; no histamine rise (range 0-13 ng/mL).

## Covariates
None modelled. All male, 18-35 yr; no age, weight or sex effects analysed. Parameters are per kg, from volunteers of about 75-78 kg.

## Caveats
n=4 for the compartmental PK and PD fits; "preliminary". Whole-blood assay (remifentanil is hydrolysed by blood esterases, and the authors preserved samples with acetonitrile). Sternal analgesia was more variable than tibial, so tibial values were used for modelling. The PD fit assumed the biexponential PK per subject. Ratios are bolus dose ratios. The paper uses the pharmacodynamic parameters of a single 1-min dose group only.
