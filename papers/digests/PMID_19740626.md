# Ohman 2009: topiramate dose/concentration ratio during pregnancy

## Study
Prospective observational therapeutic-drug-monitoring series (Sweden, Denmark, Austria). 15 women with epilepsy,
19-41 years, 17 pregnancies (PDF p2, printed p125). Weight, height and gestational weeks of sampling not reported.
Topiramate monotherapy in 9 pregnancies, with other antiseizure drugs in 8 (Table 1, PDF p3, printed p126:
lamotrigine x4, one also with valproate; oxcarbazepine 1800 mg/day; clobazam; phenytoin 300 + clobazam;
carbamazepine 1200 mg/day). Baseline topiramate doses 100-400 mg/day; dose raised in 8 pregnancies, lowered in one.
Steady-state trough samples (before the morning dose), once per trimester and at a baseline outside pregnancy:
before conception (n = 11) or at least 1 month after delivery (n = 6). Complete sets (all three trimesters +
baseline) in 10 pregnancies of 9 women; 7 pregnancies (6 women) enrolled late, third trimester + baseline only.
Assay: plasma, fluorescence polarization immunoassay (Innofluor), quantification range 2.8-94 umol/L, limit of
detection 0.9 umol/L, CV < 10% (PDF p2). Total concentration (no free-drug measurement).
Measure: D/C ratio = daily dose (mg) / steady-state plasma concentration, reported in L/day. The printed formula
gives the concentration unit as "mg/mL" (PDF p2); with L/day as the result unit this must mean mg/L (misprint,
no effect on the ratios). ANOVA across stages; paired t-test third trimester vs baseline.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Mean (SD) D/C ratios, PDF p1 abstract and p2 Results:

| Group | Baseline | 1st trim | 2nd trim | 3rd trim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 complete pregnancies | 37.3 (15.9) | 49.4 (29.4), n.s. | 67.5 (23.4), p < 0.01 | 65.1 (30.4), p < 0.01 |
| all 17 pregnancies | 38.8 (18.0) | - | - | 67.4 (27.5), p < 0.0001 |

Reported increases: 81.1% (2nd) and 74.7% (3rd) in the complete set; 71.8% in all 17. Individual third-trimester
increases 19%-164%, higher than baseline in all 17 (Table 1). Mean increase 77.9% with monotherapy, 85.5% with
polytherapy (derived check from Table 1: 701/9 = 77.9 and 684/8 = 85.5, so these are means of the individual %
increases). Ratios of means (derived): 67.4/38.8 = 1.737 (73.7%); 65.1/37.3 = 1.745; 67.5/37.3 = 1.810;
49.4/37.3 = 1.324. The paper's 71.8% does not equal the ratio of the 17-pregnancy means (73.7%) nor the mean of
the Table 1 individual increases (derived 1385/17 = 81.5%); its computation is not stated.
Discussion: an 80% D/C increase corresponds to roughly 40% lower concentrations at unchanged dose (PDF p4).
Background (cited, not measured here): V about 0.7 L/kg, 15% protein bound, mainly renal elimination (PDF p1-2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis, no therapeutic range proposed, no ke0. Seizures occurred in 9 pregnancies,
increased vs the prior year in 8; no clear correlation between D/C increase and seizure change (Table 1, PDF p4).
Not an opioid paper; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Pregnancy stage only. No apparent difference between mono- and polytherapy. Inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin,
oxcarbazepine) were present in some polytherapy pregnancies.

## Caveats
Small series; trough-only sampling (D/C from troughs approximates CL/F ratio only if the trough/average ratio is
unchanged); doses changed during pregnancy at physician discretion; 6 baselines were postpartum (>= 1 month);
first-trimester change not significant; immunoassay; no weights, so no per-kg clearance; mixed co-medication.
