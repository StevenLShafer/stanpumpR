# Bastian 2017: sublingual buprenorphine exposure lower in pregnancy than postpartum

## Study
Observational within-subject PK study (Pittsburgh, Caritis group), recruited June-November 2014 (PDF p. 3).
17 consented; 14 pregnant women with opioid use disorder on twice-daily sublingual buprenorphine
**tablets** (mono-product; two postpartum studies on Suboxone film were excluded) at steady state (stable dose
>= 7 days) provided data; 6 of the 14 were studied at all three visits (PDF p. 5). Visits: PK-2 at 18-25 wk
(n=7 valid), PK-3 at 31-37 wk (n=11), PK-P 4-18 wk postpartum (n=10); 35 studies done, 7 excluded (4 for C0
much higher than C12, i.e. non-adherence to 12 h dosing; 2 Suboxone film; 1 once-daily dosing) -> 28 analysed
(PDF p. 5). All but one woman studied postpartum was also studied at least once in pregnancy (PDF p. 6), so not
every woman contributes both a pregnancy and a postpartum study.

Demographics (Revised Table 1, PDF p. 12, mean +/- SD, rendered page): age 27.3 / 27.9 / 28.6 y; bodyweight
73.7 +/- 7.6 / 74.1 +/- 8.3 / 66.5 +/- 9.4 kg; BMI 28.5 / 28.6 / 25.3; albumin 3.4 / 3.2 / 4.0 g/dL; total protein
5.9 / 6.0 / 6.6 g/dL; twice-daily dose 8.0 +/- 3.1 mg (range 2-12) / 10.0 +/- 3.7 mg (range 2-16) / 8.0 +/- 0.0 mg
(PK-2 / PK-3 / PK-P). Gestational age 22.0 +/- 2.5 and 34.0 +/- 2.2 wk; postpartum 7.1 +/- 4.2 wk.

Dose observed after an >= 8 h fast; plasma at 0 (pre-dose), 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 8, 10, 12 h (Methods,
PDF p. 4; the abstract omits 10 h). Assay: LC-MS/MS of buprenorphine (BUP), norbuprenorphine and glucuronides,
LLOQ 0.05 ng/mL for BUP, linear 0.05-50 ng/mL, CV < 15%, bias < 5% (PDF p. 4). **Total** plasma
buprenorphine. Non-compartmental analysis in Excel (trapezoidal AUC0-12; Cmax, C0, C12, Tmax observed); terminal
half-life not estimable over a 12 h interval. Statistics: linear mixed models on log-transformed parameters
(SAS 9.4), PK-2 vs PK-3 and mean of PK-2/PK-3 vs PK-P, Sidak-adjusted (PDF p. 5). Saliva pH also measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: **no MEAC** or minimum effective/analgesic concentration; **no ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis**;
**no equianalgesic or potency ratio**. No pharmacodynamic end point was measured (no withdrawal scores, pain,
pupil or ventilation). Population is opioid-use-disorder maintenance, not analgesia. The only effect-related
statements are clinical (higher doses may be needed in pregnancy; reassess dose after delivery; PDF pp. 7-8).

## Covariates
Pregnancy state (PK-2, PK-3 vs postpartum) only. No significant PK-2 vs PK-3 difference in any parameter.
Saliva pH (6.2 +/- 0.44 / 6.4 +/- 0.67 / 6.5 +/- 0.71) did not correlate with dissolution time, AUC, Cmax or Tmax
(PDF p. 6). Weight, albumin and protein differ by visit (Table 1) but were not tested as covariates.

## Caveats
- Small n (7/11/10), unbalanced; only 6 women at all three visits; 7 of 35 studies excluded post hoc.
- **Comparator is the same women postpartum (4-18 wk; mean ~7 wk), not non-pregnant volunteers or a model.**
  Postpartum women had a mono-product dose fixed at 8.0 mg; pregnant doses varied (2-16 mg) and dose
  normalisation assumes linearity, although sublingual F is dose-dependent in Bjornsson 2023 (lower F at higher
  doses would lower DN AUC at the larger PK-3 doses).
- NCA on sublingual dosing gives apparent CL/F; the paper itself predicts, but does not test, metabolism as the
  mechanism (PDF p. 7). Many women broke tablets; not controlled (PDF p. 7).
- Accepted manuscript: "Revised" tables and some internal inconsistencies (see open_questions); cite PDF pages
  only.
