# Berenbaum 1989: What is synergy?

## Study
Narrative review (no data of its own beyond illustrative examples from the literature) of how to define and measure synergy, additivity and antagonism between two or more biologically active agents. Examples are drawn from antibiotics, cytotoxics, anticonvulsants, narcotics, environmental mixtures; the figures show isoboles (e.g. trimethadione with albutoin in mice, Figs 2-3; ADP with ADP-ribose on alcohol dehydrogenase, Fig 1).

## Model and parameters
No PK/PD parameters. Key constructs:
- Zero interaction is defined empirically from the single-agent dose-response curves, not from mechanism (pp.95-96).
- Isobole / interaction index (eq 1-3), Berenbaum's criterion: da/Da + db/Db = 1 for zero interaction (straight isobole); <1 synergy (concave up/below the line); >1 antagonism. Da, Db are the single-agent doses giving the same effect as the combination (da, db). Worked example p.97: 74.5/298 + 8.25/33 = 0.5 (synergy); 760/1520 + 175/210 = 1.33 (antagonism).
- Eight approaches listed (pp.97-99): (a) isoboles; (b) summation of effects; (c) multiplication of surviving fractions; (d) envelope of limiting isoboles; (e) shift of one agent's dose-response by a fixed dose of the other; (f) mass-action calculation; (g) combination effect exceeds each constituent (Gaddum); (h) no stated criterion. Only (a) and (g) are called mechanism-independent; (g) is rejected (two people felling 10 trees each cannot be "synergistic" for 15).
- Methods can disagree: two agents each giving 50% inhibition and 90% together are antagonistic by summation (100% expected) and synergistic by multiplication (75% expected) (p.98).
- Later sections (not fully read): median effect principle, independence (Bliss) vs similar action (Loewe), many-agent interactions, direct-search (simplex / "partition") optimisation of combinations (Figs 23-26, pp.128-130).
