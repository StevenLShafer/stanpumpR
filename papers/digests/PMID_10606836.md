# Jorga 2000: Population PK of tolcapone in Parkinson's patients

## Study
Pooled population PK analysis of three multicentre, parallel, double-blind,
placebo-controlled phase II dose-finding trials of tolcapone (a reversible COMT
inhibitor) added to levodopa/decarboxylase-inhibitor therapy. 412 patients
enrolled (262 men, 150 women; p. 40); tolcapone PK evaluable in 275 (215
fluctuators, 60 nonfluctuators), 1414 concentrations. Doses 50, 200 or 400 mg
three times daily. Sparse steady-state sampling (5–8 samples/patient on 2–5
occasions; a trough, one near Cmax at 0.5–2 h, one at 3–4 h). Plasma by
reversed-phase HPLC with UV detection, LOQ ~0.1 µg/mL (p. 41). NONMEM version
IV, double precision, FO then FOCE. Fluctuators and nonfluctuators were fitted
separately; the two final models were similar (Results, p. 43). Demographics
(Table 1, p. 41): fluctuators median age 65 y (34–82), weight 71 kg (36–153),
LBW 55 kg (25–83), protein 72 g/L (56–87), albumin 44 g/L (26–55), CLcr
68 mL/min (22–148).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment open model with first-order absorption; tlag excluded for
fluctuators (ΔOF only 2), retained for nonfluctuators. Absolute bioavailability
fasted (F1) fixed at 0.6 from the IV study (ref 22, Jorga 1998), so CL and the
volumes are absolute, not apparent (p. 41 text; Table 3, p. 44). Fluctuator
covariate equations (Methods, p. 42–43): CL = TV·(LBW/55)^θ·(protein/72)^θ;
Vc = TV·(LBW/55)^θ·dose factor; Vp = TV·(albumin/44)^θ·dose factor;
F1 = 0.6·(1+IFood·(θFood−1)). Values from Table 3 (p. 44), fluctuator column
(confirmed on the page image):

Nonfluctuator final model (Table 3): CL 4.5 L/h, Vc 3.5 L, Vp 24 L, Q 7.7 L/h,
ka 0.7 /h, tlag 0.4 h, with CLcr on CL (θ 1.19) and protein on Vc (θ −7.34).
Secondary parameters (Table 4, p. 47; means±s.d., µg·h/mL and h): 50 mg AUC
6.7±1.6, t½ 2.9±2.2 (fluctuators only); 200 mg AUC 26.5±7.8 / 28.0±10.8, t½
5.5±4.1 / 8.0±4.2; 400 mg AUC 55.8±16.7 / 54.3±19.8, t½ 7.4±3.3 / 7.7±3.2
(fluctuators / nonfluctuators).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. This is a PK-only population analysis; no ke0/tPeak/EC50/MEAC and no
effect-site or concentration–effect modelling are reported. (Not an opioid; no
opioid audit applies.)

## Covariates
Fluctuators: LBW on CL (greatest effect; ±15 kg about 55 kg gives CL 80%/119%
of typical, p. 47) and on Vc; serum protein on CL; serum albumin on Vp; dose
on both volumes. Nonfluctuators: CLcr on CL, serum protein on Vc. No effect of
age, sex, dopamine agonists or treatment duration (pp. 45, 47). CLcr effect in
nonfluctuators judged likely artefactual (weight is in the CLcr formula; p. 47).
LBW by James's formula (men 1.10·BW−128·(BW/H)², women 1.07·BW−148·(BW/H)²;
Methods p. 40, citing James 1976 and Hallynck 1981).

## Caveats
Sparse sampling; absorption poorly characterised so tlag/food-on-rate not
resolvable. V estimated with poor precision and high IIV (Vc CV 72%, Vp CV 57%
in fluctuators). The authors themselves judge the covariate effects on V to have
been estimated with poor precision, and allow that a handful of unusually large V
estimates may have driven them (p. 45, paraphrased). The Discussion meanwhile
reports that "no relationship was detected" between protein or albumin and
distribution (p. 46), which sits oddly beside Table 3's albumin-on-Vp term. F1 fixed, not estimated. Dose-dependence of
V seen only in fluctuators (total V 15 L at 50 mg, 39 L at 400 mg tid), not
confirmed in nonfluctuators.
