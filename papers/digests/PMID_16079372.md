# Osterberg 2005: Adherence to medication (NEJM Drug Therapy review)

## Study
Narrative review (NEJM "Drug Therapy" series), two authors (VA Palo Alto General Medicine; Stanford Clinical
Pharmacology). No original data, no drug administered, no pharmacokinetic sampling or modelling. Topics: definitions of
adherence/compliance/persistence, methods of measuring adherence (Table 1, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 489), patterns of
medication-taking from electronic monitors, predictors of poor adherence (Table 2, p. 5 / 491), barriers (Fig. 2,
p. 6 / 492), interventions, and disease examples (HIV, hypertension, psychiatric illness, paediatrics), with a list of
strategies (Table 3, p. 7 / 493).

Numbers stated (all quoted from other studies, text layer):
- Clinical-trial average adherence in chronic conditions 43 to 78% (p. 1 / 487).
- Adequacy thresholds used by trials: >80% or, e.g. in HIV, >95% (p. 1 / 487; p. 6 / 492).
- 33 to 69% of medication-related US hospital admissions attributed to poor adherence; about $100 billion a year
  (p. 2 / 488).
- About half of statin patients stop within six months (p. 1 / 487).
- Electronic-monitor patterns: six groups of roughly one sixth each, from near-perfect to few or no doses (p. 4 / 490).
- Claxton et al. review of 76 trials: adherence falls with dosing frequency; four-times-daily about 50% (range 31 to
  71%) (Fig. 1, p. 4 / 490).
- Reasons given by patients: forgetfulness 30%, other priorities 16%, decision to omit 11%, lack of information 9%,
  emotional factors 7%, no reason 27% (p. 4 / 490).
- Cramer and Rosenheck: mean adherence 76% (physical disorders), 58% (psychoses), 65% (depression) (p. 7 / 493);
  schizophrenia 50 to 60%, bipolar as low as 35%; children with chronic disease about 50% (p. 8 / 494).
- "White-coat adherence": better dosing in the 5 days before and after a visit than 30 days after (p. 3 / 489).
- "Forgiving" drugs (long half-life, depot, transdermal) recommended where adherence is doubtful (Table 3, p. 7 / 493).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No drug concentrations, ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratios. Not an opioid paper. The only pharmacological point
is qualitative: drugs with long half-lives or whose effect is not tied to plasma concentration (thiazides, transdermal
clonidine) tolerate missed or late doses better (p. 7 / 493).

## Covariates
Not applicable. Predictors of poor adherence are listed (Table 2): psychological problems (especially depression),
cognitive impairment, asymptomatic disease, inadequate follow-up, side effects, lack of belief in benefit or insight,
poor provider relationship, barriers to care, missed appointments, complex treatment, cost. Race, sex and
socioeconomic status not consistently associated (p. 4 / 490).

## Caveats
Narrative review; figures are secondary citations, not pooled by the authors. One author (Blaschke) declares
consulting fees and shareholdings (p. 8 / 494).
