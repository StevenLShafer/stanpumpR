# Brodie 2017: closed-loop TIVA during major blood loss (case report)

## Study
- Single case report from the same closed-loop study as West 2018 (NCT01771263; see [[PMID_29210791]]). Man, 79 y, ASA 3, 173 cm, 106 kg, scheduled for sigmoid tumour resection, ileostomy and right radical nephrectomy with IVC tumour embolectomy. Hypertension and obesity (p.239).
- iControl system: propofol and remifentanil via Alaris TIVA pumps, controlled on NeuroSENSE WAVCNS. Default WAVCNS target 50, default remifentanil baseline 0.1 ug/kg/min (p.239).
- Thoracic epidural and arterial line before induction; epidural not used until end of surgery. Rocuronium 0.5 mg/kg; remifentanil bolus 0.5 ug/kg (53 ug) before incision; for hypertension the baseline was raised to 0.14 ug/kg/min, WAVCNS target lowered to 45, labetalol 5 mg and hydralazine 5 mg given (p.239).
- Rapid blood loss during IVC manipulation: the anaesthesiologist reset the WAVCNS target to 50 and remifentanil baseline to 0.1, then 0.06 ug/kg/min after burst suppression appeared about 20 min after onset. Mean arterial pressure 50 mm Hg or less for 24.6 min, lowest 16 mm Hg (radial). No vasopressors during the event (p.240).
- WAVCNS stayed within +/-10 of target for 91% of maintenance (p.241). Postoperative right MCA watershed infarct, largely resolved at 6 months and 2 years.

## Model and parameters
No PK model fitted or tabulated. Effect-site concentrations were calculated (Figure 1) but the paper names no model in this case report. The legend (Figure 1) warns that the Ce values during and after the massive blood loss and replacement are likely unreliable and may significantly overestimate true concentrations. The discussion says actual plasma concentrations were undoubtedly lower than calculated because blood loss is not in the PK/PD models (p.242).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: WAVCNS (0 isoelectric, 90-100 awake, 40-60 anaesthesia range) and burst suppression ratio (BSR, fraction of the past 60 s isoelectric).
- Opioid audit (remifentanil): no MEAC, ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak or potency ratio reported. Only infusion setpoints (0.1, 0.14, 0.06 ug/kg/min) and a 53 ug bolus are given.
- Qualitative: hypotension from haemorrhage caused falling WAVCNS and rising BSR in both hemispheres (slight right predominance); the authors cite published work (Johnson 2001, 2003, refs 8-9) that haemorrhagic shock lowers remifentanil and propofol requirements (no numbers here).

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
Single case; calculated Ce unreliable during haemorrhage by the authors' own statement; radial pressure may not reflect central pressure; the causal link between EEG change and ischaemia is a hypothesis.
