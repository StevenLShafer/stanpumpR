# Liem-Moolenaar 2011: PK-PD of scopolamine, CNS effects in healthy men

## Study
- Two double-blind, placebo-controlled, four-period crossover ascending-dose studies (glycinergic compounds in schizophrenia; scopolamine used as a transient cognitive-impairment model). Only the visits with scopolamine or placebo alone are analysed (PDF p.2).
- 90 healthy men, 18-55 y, BMI 18-28.5 kg/m2; 85 completed (PDF p.2-3). Weight is not reported beyond the BMI range.
- Dose: scopolamine 0.5 mg i.v. as a 15-min infusion starting at t = 0 (PDF p.3); salt form not stated.
- PK samples (4 mL) at 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 2.5 and 6.5 h after dosing; LC-MS/MS, LLOQ 10 pg/mL; inter-run CV 1.9-5.6%, bias -1.0 to 4.3% (PDF p.3). Plasma (the Methods do not state whether the sample was plasma or serum beyond "plasma concentrations" in Results).
- PD battery (11 blocks, pre-dose x2 and about 0.75-8.5 h): body sway, saccadic eye movements, smooth pursuit, pharmaco-EEG, pupil:iris ratio, adaptive tracking, finger tapping, Stroop, VVLT memory, Bond & Lader and Bowdle VAS, LH/FSH/prolactin, heart rate and blood pressure.
- NONMEM, FOCE, additive error on log scale; PK and PK-PD fitted sequentially with individual empirical-Bayes PK estimates (PDF p.4, p.7).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. No MEAC or therapeutic concentration is given. Effect-compartment model with a linear concentration-effect relation (intercept + slope x Ce); no Emax could be estimated for any end point with the single 0.5 mg dose (PDF p.7). Equilibration half-life t1/2ke0 (min), Table 3 (PDF p.9, printed p.894, confirmed on the image), value (SEM - IICV as printed):

| End point | t1/2ke0, min | Authors' group |
|---|---|---|
| Heart rate | 16.8 (2.73 - 16.25) | <0.5 h |
| Saccadic peak velocity | 65.1 (NA - 0.0%) | 1-1.5 h |
| Adaptive tracking | 86.6 (4.14 - 28.4%) | 1-1.5 h |
| VAS external perception | 161 (25.5 - 86.5%) | 2.5-3.5 h |
| Body sway | 181 (12 - 30.6%) | 2.5-3.5 h |
| VAS alertness | 199 (NA - 53.8%) | 2.5-3.5 h |
| VAS internal perception | 200 (NA - 1.6%) | 2.5-3.5 h |
| Smooth pursuit | 221 (31.5 - 97.8%) | 2.5-3.5 h |
| VAS feeling high | 483 (NA - 207%) | >8 h |
| Finger tapping | 649 (472 - 62.9%) | >8 h |

- NA = covariance step aborted. Groups are the authors' arbitrary categories (PDF p.8-10). t1/2ke0 is defined as the time for the effect-compartment concentration to reach half the central-compartment concentration (PDF p.7).
- No ke0 in 1/min and no tPeak are reported (derived: ke0 = ln2 / t1/2ke0). Slopes and intercepts are in the outcome's own units per pg/mL (Table 3 footnote).
- No simple PK-PD model fitted for blood pressure, LH/FSH/prolactin, EEG, pupil:iris ratio (effect outlasted sampling), Stroop, VVLT, VAS calmness, mood, colour (PDF p.7, p.10).
- Mean-effect findings versus placebo (Table 1, PDF p.5): heart rate fell about 23% (66.8 to 51.2 beats/min), body sway up 57.4%, saccadic peak velocity down 22.7 deg/s, adaptive tracking down 9.6%, VAS alertness down 9.0 mm.
- Design suggestion (Figures 3D, 4D, PDF p.8-9): 0.45 mg over 15 min at t = 0, then 0.15 mg over 15 min at 150, 250, 350 min holds a 50 deg/s saccadic velocity reduction and a 10 mm alertness reduction for about 7 h.

## Covariates
None reported (healthy young men only; the authors say applicability to older or demented subjects is unknown).

## Caveats
- Single dose level, so no Emax; linear PD slopes with several unstable covariance steps (NA entries in Table 3).
- Sparse PK sampling (5 samples) after a 15-min infusion; no sample during the infusion.
- Healthy men only; salt form of the 0.5 mg not stated.
- The effect-compartment half-lives are descriptive, not mechanistic; the authors suggest CNS retention and secondary mechanisms.
