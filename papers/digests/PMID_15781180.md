# Johnson 2005: Buprenorphine: considerations for pain management

## Study
Narrative review (not a primary PK/PD study) of buprenorphine's receptor pharmacology, formulations (parenteral,
sublingual, buccal, intranasal, transdermal, oral), pharmacokinetics by route, and clinical-trial outcomes in acute,
cancer and noncancer chronic pain, drawn from the authors' survey of >180 prior references. No new modeling or
original data are presented; all PK/PD numbers are quoted from the studies cited.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model is fit in this paper. Narrative/figure values given for context (not used by stanpumpR except
where noted below):
- Octanol:water partition coefficient 1217; molecular weight 468; "25 to 50 times more potent as an analgesic, per
  mg, than morphine" (p.303, PDF p.7, confirmed on rendered image).
- IV clearance 70-80 L/h in the analgesic dose range (p.302, PDF p.6, citing refs 63, 79).
- Terminal half-life: ~5 h after 0.3 mg IV (ref 77); ~6 h after 1 mg IV infused over 30 min (ref 78); 3.2 h after
  1.2 mg IV bolus (Kuhlman, ref 79); 28 h sublingual, 19 h buccal (ref 79) (p.302, PDF p.6).
- Bioavailability by route (Fig. 3 bar chart, p.303, PDF p.7, confirmed on rendered image): IV 100%, IM ~70%,
  intranasal ~51%, sublingual solution ~51%, buccal strip ~29%, sublingual tablet ~25%, oral ~11% (read from bar
  heights, approximate, no table of exact values given).
- Transdermal patch, European 7-day product: 35/52.5/70 µg/h release → 0.84/1.26/1.68 mg/24h; effective levels
  within 12-24 h, constant for 72 h (p.303, PDF p.7).
- Transdermal patch, US 7-day product: 5/10/20 µg/h release → up to 0.48 mg/day; mean (±SEM) Cmax 176±34, 191±19,
  471±77 pg/mL respectively (Reidenberg 2001, two conference abstracts, refs 90-91; p.303, PDF p.7, confirmed on
  rendered image); time to steady state 24-48 h; 15% of total 7-day dose delivered; after removal, concentration
  falls to about half in 12 h, terminal half-life ~26 h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, or formal MEAC/EC50/Emax is reported or modeled anywhere in this paper; it is a narrative
clinical review, not a PK/PD modeling study. The only quantitative items stanpumpR draws from it (both confirmed on
rendered page images):
- **Patch Cmax** (end point: plasma buprenorphine concentration, not an analgesic end point): Reidenberg 2001
  (abstract) transdermal-patch peak levels of 0.176, 0.191 and 0.471 ng/mL (mean ± SEM, total plasma, assay not
  stated) at 5, 10 and 20 µg/h release rates respectively, as quoted in this review (p.303, PDF p.7).
- **Correlation finding, not a MEAC**: of a study (ref 151, Adriaensen 1985, sublingual buprenorphine 0.4-3.2 mg/day
  in patients with malignant and non-malignant chronic pain) this review states: "No correlation between
  buprenorphine plasma levels and analgesic response was found" (p.307, PDF p.11).
- **Potency ratio**: "25 to 50 times more potent as an analgesic, per mg, than morphine" (p.303, PDF p.7) — a
  general statement with no assay, route or population attached.
- Elsewhere the review also notes (not quoting a number, and not used by stanpumpR) that the Oxford group found no
  correlation between peak plasma concentration and duration of analgesia (citing Bullingham 1980 and McQuay 1980,
  p.307-308).

## Caveats
- Review article, not a controlled PK/PD study: most of the cited chronic-pain trials are small, open-label or
  uncontrolled, a limitation the authors themselves note for the cancer-pain literature (p.308, PDF p.12).
- The two quantitative items stanpumpR draws from this review are themselves second-hand here: the patch Cmax
  values come from two conference abstracts (Reidenberg 2001, refs 90-91), and the "no correlation" finding comes
  from a single trial (Adriaensen 1985, ref 151) that this review summarizes in one sentence, with no assay, matrix
  or statistical detail given.
- No explicit MEAC, ke0, or assay-qualified potency ratio is given anywhere in the paper.
