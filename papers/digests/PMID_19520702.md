# Absalom 2009: Pharmacokinetic models for propofol (Marsh vs Schnider in open TCI)

PDF page = printed page - 25 (printed 26-37 = PDF 1-12).

## Study
Narrative review, no new data. Compares the Marsh and Schnider adult propofol models as implemented in the two
open-TCI pumps of 2009 (Alaris Asena PK, Fresenius Base Primea): plasma vs effect-site targeting, how ke0 is
set, the LBM (James) problem in morbid obesity, and the size of the initial and maintenance doses. Dose
comparisons are simulations (Figs 5, 9, 10), not measurements. The context file records no stanpumpR citation
of this paper (`locations` empty); the digest compares it with the library's own propofol models.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No opioid. ke0 and time to peak effect (TTPE) are the paper's subject (not an opioid, so no MEAC audit):
- Definition: TTPE is the delay from a bolus to peak clinical effect, independent of bolus size (PDF p. 4).
  ke0 can be estimated from TTPE, or by choosing the ke0 that collapses the hysteresis loop (Figs 3-4, PDF p. 4;
  hypothetical example: ke0 0.38 /min gives a peak effect-site concentration at 100 s that matches peak effect;
  the other curves use 0.27 and 0.47).
- Marsh: ke0 0.26 /min (TTPE 4.5 min, Table 1); Struys 2000 showed ke0 1.2 /min with BIS tracked better, TTPE
  about 1.6 min (PDF p. 5).
- Schnider: ke0 0.456 /min; the end point was a canonical univariate EEG parameter; median TTPE 1.69 min by that
  parameter, 1.0 to 2.4 min (median 1.6) by visual EEG; with TTPE fixed at 1.6 min the median individual ke0 was
  0.456 /min (PDF p. 6). Figure 8 example, both 70 kg, TTPE fixed at 1.6 min: age 18, 160 cm gives ke0 0.357;
  age 80, 180 cm gives ke0 0.525 (legend of Fig 8, PDF p. 7; text layer only).
- Two implementations for the Schnider model (PDF pp. 6-7): Asena PK, Rugloop and Stanpump hold TTPE fixed and
  solve a ke0 per patient; Base Primea holds ke0 at 0.456 /min so TTPE varies. For ordinary patients the fixed-TTPE
  ke0 is near 0.456; for some severely obese patients it is larger or smaller and the induction dose differs.
- Plasma overshoot at an effect-site target increase: Schnider about 300% of target; Marsh with ke0 0.26 at
  least 150%; Marsh with ke0 1.2 about 50% (PDF p. 8). First dose for a 4 mcg/mL effect-site target in a 70 kg
  patient: Marsh ke0 0.26 172 mg, Schnider 77 mg, Marsh ke0 1.2 98 mg (40 y, 170 cm).
- No EC50, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Marsh: weight only (volumes proportional; rate constants fixed). Schnider: V2/k12/k21 by age; k10 by total weight,
height and LBM (James); V1, V3, k13, k31 fixed. Servin's input weight for the Marsh model in the obese:
IBM + 0.4 x (TBW - IBM) (PDF p. 11).

## Caveats
Review, so no new estimates. The Schnider volunteers (24: 11 F, 13 M; 44-123 kg; 25-81 y; 155-196 cm) did not
include severely obese patients; neither model has validated predictive performance in the morbidly obese.
Marsh ke0 0.26 was never published. Schnider's predictive performance beyond its derivation study was thinly
documented in 2009.
