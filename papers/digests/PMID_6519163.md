# O'Hare 1984: propranolol pharmacokinetics in pregnancy

## Study
- Design: randomised crossover (oral vs IV order randomised, washout at least 1 week), each subject studied at 32-36 weeks gestation and again at least 6 weeks postpartum, after lactation had stopped and with no hormonal contraception (PDF p1-2). Registered as a randomised trial in PubMed.
- Population: 6 healthy pregnant volunteers, normal renal function, no hepatic history. Mean body weight 66.9 +/- 3.4 kg antenatal vs 58.7 +/- 3.3 kg postnatal (p<0.001) (PDF p2). Age, height, parity not given.
- Oral: propranolol (Inderal) 120 mg after overnight fast at 09.00 h; heparinised blood at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12 h.
- IV: 10 mg in 10 ml infused over 10 min; blood before, at end of infusion, then 15 and 30 min, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 h. Only 5 of 6 women are in the IV analysis (one antenatal assay failed technically).
- Assay: plasma, GLC with electron-capture detection, metoprolol internal standard; limit 1 ng/ml; CV 8.2% at 40 ng/ml. Total (racemic) plasma concentration; salt form of the doses not stated (Inderal injection/tablets, mg as labelled).
- Analysis: non-compartmental. Terminal rate constant (lambda) by least-squares regression of the terminal log-linear phase (from 4 h onward in 6 of 10 IV curves, shorter periods in the rest); t1/2 = 0.693/lambda; AUC0-inf by trapezoidal rule; CL = dose per kg / AUC; V = CL/lambda (so a terminal-phase volume); bioavailability = AUCoral/AUCiv dose-adjusted. Paired t-test and Wilcoxon. Results are mean +/- SEM.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect measure (heart rate, beta-blockade), no ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, EC50 or MEAC/analgesic concentration is reported, and no potency ratio. The only time-to-peak is the observed oral Cmax time (1.5 h antenatal, 1.7 h postnatal), an absorption quantity, not an effect-site one. Not an opioid paper; the opioid audit does not apply.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (32-36 weeks vs postpartum) is the only comparison; no covariate equations. Body weight differed by 8.2 kg between occasions and CL and V were divided by body weight before comparing. Obstetric explanation offered: hepatic blood flow, on which propranolol (extraction about 90%, cited from Shand 1971) depends, may not rise in pregnancy (PDF p4).

## Caveats
- n=6 (5 for IV), huge between-subject variation (oral AUC SEM about 35-50% of mean, bioavailability SEM 30-43 points); the study could miss a moderate change. Authors' conclusion is "not significantly altered", not equivalence.
- Per-kg values hide the weight change (see open question): in absolute terms the antenatal V is smaller and CL somewhat larger (derived).
- One woman had unusually high oral concentrations on both occasions, inflating mean F; excluding her, the F range is 7-77% antenatal and 9-47% postnatal (PDF p4).
- Sparse early sampling after the 10-min infusion (end of infusion, then 15 min); the model-free V = CL/lambda is a terminal volume, not Vss. Half-lives from short terminal segments in some curves.
- Table half-lives do not equal 0.693/mean lambda (open question 3).
- Single-dose, third-trimester only; the authors cite Smith 1983b for changes in metabolite kinetics (naphthoxylactic acid) with unchanged parent drug.
- Comparators: the authors compare their postnatal CL (10.6 ml/min/kg) with 14.0 ml/min/kg in normal subjects (Evans 1973) and V with 150 L and 216 L (Shand 1970; Evans 1973). Their own comparator for "pregnancy" is the same women postpartum.

Per kg, 10.6 ml/min/kg is below Evans's 14.0 (cited by the paper) and Fagan's roughly 14.6 (derived: 1.11 L/min / 76 kg). Women, different lab and sampling; the paper does not state a sex effect. No code change implied.
