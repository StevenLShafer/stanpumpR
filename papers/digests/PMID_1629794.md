# Shafer 1992: effect-site targeting algorithm for computer-controlled infusion pumps

## Study
Methods paper, simulation only. Argues that targeting the plasma (Schwilden 1981 BET; Bailey & Shafer 1991) gives
only a slurred rise and fall at the effect site, and gives two algorithms to reach a target effect-site concentration
as fast as possible without overshoot, then hold it. Example: fentanyl during a simulated anaesthetic (Figs. 2, 3, 10).
Clinical motivation (Discussion): plasma-targeted fentanyl left patients awake 10-15 min; effect-site targeting gives
a large dose over the first 20-30 s and sleep within 2-3 min.

## Model and parameters
Three-compartment mammillary model plus an effect compartment, reformulated as a 4-compartment model with the effect
site returning drug to V1: V4 = V1/10,000, k41 = ke0, k14 = ke0/10,000 (pp. 154-155). Fentanyl example, Table I
(p. 151; from Shafer, Varvel, Aziz, Scott 1990; ke0 from Scott & Stanski 1987):

Algorithm (Appendix A, pp. 159-163):
- Euler difference equations with Δt = 1 s; pump rate updated every 10 s (10-20 s typical).
- tpeak: time of peak effect-site amount after a 10-s unit infusion with no drug in the body (array E[]), computed once.
- Each update: B4[j] = effect-site amount j s ahead with the pump off, j = 1..tpeak. By superposition A4 = B4 + I·E;
  I = (target A4 − B4[jpeak]) / E[jpeak], iterated from jpeak,0 = tpeak (after a target increase) or the previous
  jpeak until jpeak is stable — the rate whose trajectory just touches the target.
- If B4[10] > target the rate is 0 until it falls below.
- Once Ce is within 5% of target, hold the plasma at the target instead (Bailey & Shafer), to stop rate oscillation
  from discrete time (p. 163).
- Euler error < 5% in the first 1-2 min, < 0.5% after several minutes; runs on a 4.77 MHz 8088.
Appendix B: Laplace-transform analytical form (Eqs. B1-B8); A4(t) = B4(t) + I·E(t); finding jpeak still needs a
numerical search, so Appendix A is preferred.
Key properties: with no drug on board tpeak is dose-independent; with drug on board the time to peak after a bolus
is always less than tpeak (superposition argument, Figs. 7-8); targets reached before tpeak must overshoot (Fig. 6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit: fentanyl ke0 0.147/min (Table I, from Scott & Stanski 1987, EEG spectral edge end point); no tPeak value
is printed for this parameter set; no MEAC; no potency ratio. Authors advise erring towards larger ke0 to limit
overshoot (p. 159).

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Deterministic controller (no parameter error); Euler approximation; toxicity at a faster-equilibrating site is
discussed as a possible fifth compartment; no clinical validation data in this paper.
