# Avram 2003: front-end kinetics and target-controlled infusions (thiopental, dogs)

## Study
Laboratory simulation study using data from an earlier study (Avram 2002, ref 17) in five male dogs, 32-42.3 kg (36.7 +/- 4.6), anesthetised with ketamine 5 mg/kg then halothane 1.5%. Thiopental 100 mg with indocyanine green 5 mg flushed into the right atrium in 4 s; 30 arterial samples every 0.05 min for the first minute, every 0.1 min for the next, then 30 more to 10 h. ICG by HPLC (Grasela method), thiopental by HPLC (plasma, within 24 h). Recirculatory model fitted with SAAM II; first-pass data with two Erlang functions in TableCurve. Four three-compartment mammillary models were fitted per dog (SAAM II): model 1 to samples from 1, 2, 3 min onward; model 2 from the recirculation peak onward; model 3 as 2 but VC fixed to the recirculatory VC + VND; model 4 to simulated 2-min, 30 mg/min infusion data (32 points, 5% random error, from each dog's recirculatory model). BET infusions targeting 20 ug/mL in VC for 60 min were designed from each model and simulated with the recirculatory model; error = area between concentration and target (infusion delta-AUC).

Findings: models 1 and 2 overestimate VC (more than 2.5x), VF (more than 10%) and ClF (about 60% and 40% larger); VSS about 8% larger; infusions overshoot the target (delta-AUC more than 13% and 10% over 60 min; 32% and 24% by 10 min). Models 3 and 4 (small VC) behave like the recirculatory model, delta-AUC not different from it. Paper gives no recirculatory chain shape (delay elements 2-30 compartments); not printed.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None: no effect-site, ke0, EC50, MEAC. Not an opioid paper; opioid audit: none (alfentanil is mentioned only through Raemer and Barvais infusion-performance comparisons).

## Covariates
None (five dogs, mean values; cardiac output = sum Cl is the physiological anchor).

## Caveats
Simulation: infusions evaluated with the recirculatory model, assuming it is a close approximation of reality; authors say results should be confirmed by a real infusion study. Five dogs under halothane, which lowers cardiac output; dog, not human.
