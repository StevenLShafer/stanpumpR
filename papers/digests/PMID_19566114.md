# Punyawudho 2009: carbamazepine de-induction by an enzyme turnover model

## Study
- Design: multicentre (Minneapolis, Miami, Atlanta) population PK study of carbamazepine (CBZ) **de-induction** (PDF p2).
- Population: 15 adults with epilepsy whose oral CBZ was being stopped; 6 women, 9 men; mean weight 78 kg, mean age 39 y (PDF p4). Comedications known to interact with CBZ excluded. 14 completed all three occasions, 1 completed two; all 15 analysed.
- Drug/route: stable-labelled CBZ (SL-CBZ), 100 mg intravenously over 10 min, on three occasions: (1) the morning after the last oral dose (last oral dose given as mean 140 mg ± 50.7 mg; see open question), (2) 6-8 days after, (3) 6-8 weeks after the last oral dose (PDF p2).
- Sampling: 14 samples, pre-dose and 0.083, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 10, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96 h after the end of infusion; 524 concentrations in all (PDF p2, p4).
- Assay: plasma SL-CBZ by LC-MS (Agilent 1100 LCMSD, electrospray), CBZ-d10 internal standard (PDF p2-3). Total plasma concentrations.
- Analysis: NONMEM VI, ADVAN6, FOCE-I; exponential IIV, proportional residual error; 500-sample nonparametric bootstrap (493 minimised) and visual predictive check (PDF p3-5).

## Model and parameters
One-compartment CBZ model linked to a hypothetical enzyme compartment (Figure 1, PDF p9; equations PDF p3):

- dA1/dt = k0 − CL · Cp · A2 (A1 amount of CBZ, mg; CL L/h; A2 enzyme amount relative to t = 0)
- dA2/dt = kenz,in · (1 − FACTOR) − kenz,out · A2, with A2(0) = 1 at steady state, so kenz,in = kenz,out.

De-induction is modelled as an **all-or-none** fractional cut in enzyme production at t = 0 (first SL-CBZ dose), not as a function of CBZ concentration. The new steady state is A2 = 1 − FACTOR. Half-life of de-induction = ln 2 / kenz,out.

Stated derived values: de-induction half-life 86.1 h = 3.6 days (abstract, PDF p1; "86 hours", p5); CBZ elimination rate constant in the induced state 0.048 /h, half-life 15 h (Discussion, PDF p6). Induction reduced by half at 3.6 days and by three-quarters at 7.2 days; de-induction complete within 2 weeks (PDF p6). Sensitivity analysis: start of de-induction moved from 0 to 48 h gave kenz,out 0.008-0.0087 /h (PDF p5).

Derived (mine): CL/V = 3.05/64.3 = 0.0474 /h (t½ 14.6 h), consistent with the stated 0.048 /h; fully de-induced clearance = 3.05 × (1 − 0.494) = 1.54 L/h, i.e. induced/de-induced clearance ratio 1/(1 − 0.494) = 1.98; bootstrap CI of kenz,out corresponds to a half-life of 10.5-139 h; NONMEM CI 54-231 h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. This is a pharmacokinetic study; the "end point" is plasma SL-CBZ concentration. No therapeutic range, ke0, EC50 or effect measure is reported. (Not an opioid paper.)

## Covariates
Only weight was tested (small n), on CL, Vd and kenz,out, linearly, centred at 80 kg. Only weight on Vd was retained (ΔOFV −19.7; PDF p5). Equation form and units of the coefficient not given (open question).

## Caveats
- n = 15; kenz,out and FACTOR have no IIV, and the bootstrap CI for kenz,out (0.005-0.066 /h) is far wider than the asymptotic one (PDF p5).
- The 86.1 h is a hybrid over all enzymes CBZ induces (CYP3A4 and others), not one isoenzyme (PDF p5). The authors set it beside Magnusson's probe-substrate estimates, 70 h for CYP3A4 and 105 h for CYP1A2 (PDF p6; cited as Magnusson, Clin Pharmacol Ther 2007, no volume).
- De-induction start assumed at the first SL-CBZ dose; the two early tracer doses might slightly delay de-induction (authors judge this minimal; PDF p6).
- Concentration-dependent (linear and Emax) autoinduction models could not be fitted (PDF p4-5); the final model is all-or-none. A model with increased degradation fitted equally well but was rejected as less plausible.
- The authors' statement that induction and de-induction share the same rate constant rests on reference 19 (Abramson 1986) and on the inducer's half-life being much shorter than the enzymes'; induction itself was not measured.
- The "initial" (occasion-1) state is whatever induction these patients had on their pre-study oral doses, which are reported only as a mean last dose of 140 mg (open question).
- Accepted manuscript: tables may differ from the published version.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **86.1 h / kdeg 0.00805 /h** (`R/antiseizureInteractions.R:55-56`, help `antiseizure-interactions.md:31`, `data-raw/antiseizureInteractions.R:48`): paper abstract (PDF p1) and Table I (PDF p15), confirmed on rendered pages. ln 2/0.00805 = 86.1 h. The code converts correctly: `kdeg <- log(2) / (row$turnover_half_life_h * 60)` (`R/antiseizureInteractions.R:257`) gives per-minute, matching the engine's minutes. **Matches.** The value is pinned by an implemented model and read from the accepted manuscript (see open question).
- **"intravenous stable-label carbamazepine, read in full"; "a hybrid of every enzyme carbamazepine induces"**: matches PDF p1-2 and p5. **Matches.**
- **Magnusson's 70 h (CYP3A4) and 105 h (CYP1A2) bracket it**: the paper says the same (PDF p6). **Matches.**
- **Induction half-developed in 3.6 days, over in about two weeks, de-induction as long**: the paper states 3.6 days, 7.2 days for three-quarters, and completion within 2 weeks for de-induction (PDF p6); the induction half is the paper's own inference via Abramson (PDF p5-6), not a measurement. With eq. (2) of the repo, constant target, half-time is ln2/kdeg = 86.1 h. **Matches (induction by the paper's inference).**
- **Model form** (`R/antiseizureInteractions.R:48-54`, dE/dt = kdeg (fold(C) − E)): the paper's equation (2) with E normalised to 1 is the same turnover structure, and when C falls to ~0 after stopping the repo's target becomes a fixed lower level, as in the paper's all-or-none FACTOR. The concentration dependence of the target, fold(C), does **not** come from this paper: its linear and Emax concentration-driven models failed to fit (PDF p4-5). The repo attributes the dependence to its EC50 assumption, not to Punyawudho, so this is not a misattribution. Applying the CBZ hybrid half-life to phenytoin, phenobarbital and other inducers is the repo's stated extrapolation (`:57-58`), not supported or tested by the paper.
- **Independent check of the carbamazepine autoinduction fold** (CSV row 9: fold 2 at Cref 8 mcg/mL, EC50 4; help `:35` cites Bertilsson 1986): FACTOR 0.494 implies induced clearance 1.98 × the fully de-induced clearance (derived), comparator = the same patients 6-8 weeks off CBZ. Agrees with the library's ×2.
- **Independent check against the library's carbamazepine model** (`R/drugs_carbamazepine.R`, Graves 1998, not citing this paper): Graves CL/F at 78 kg = 0.0134 × 78 + 3.58 = 4.63 L/h (apparent oral); with the header's F ≈ 0.8 that is ~3.7 L/h, about 1.2× Punyawudho's IV 3.05 L/h. Graves V/F 1.97 L/kg (≈ 154 L at 78 kg, ≈ 123 L if F 0.8) is about 2× Punyawudho's IV 64.3 L. Hence Graves's half-life of 21 h (header line 19) against Punyawudho's 15 h. The library model's longer half-life comes from its larger apparent volume; the header does not discuss this. Not a discrepancy for the cited use, but a direction worth knowing: the library's carbamazepine falls more slowly than this IV study's.
- **Scenario line 11** (`carbamazepine-lamotrigine-deinduction.md:11`, "half-life ~15 hours in the induced state"): this is Punyawudho's 0.048 /h (PDF p6), but the plotted carbamazepine uses Graves, whose half-life is ~21 h at 70 kg. The text and the curve disagree by ~40% in half-life (the "near zero within about four days" still roughly holds: 4 days ≈ 4.6 Graves half-lives). Minor help-text inconsistency, outside the cited line 16.
- **Scenario line 16** (86 hours, Punyawudho 2009, IV stable-labelled CBZ): **matches.**
- Lamotrigine is listed in the context's drugs; the paper reports nothing about lamotrigine. The CBZ-on-lamotrigine factor is from Rivas 2008, as the scenario says.
