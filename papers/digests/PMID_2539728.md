# Möser 1989: turnover of adenosine in plasma of human and dog blood

## Study
In-vitro physiology, not a pharmacokinetic study of the drug. Heparinised venous blood from healthy volunteers
(n per experiment 2-14; age, sex and weight not reported) and arterial blood from anaesthetised mongrel dogs,
2-mL aliquots preincubated 15-20 min at 37 °C, then mixed with tracer [3H]adenosine (or [14C]adenosine to raise
plasma adenosine to 1 or 5 µM) and stopped at 2-6 s with a dipyridamole (1 mM) + EHNA (100 µM) solution.
Plasma adenosine by HPLC (UV 254 nm), specific activity by enzymatic peak shift to inosine and scintillation
counting; recovery 92 ± 8% (n = 9). Also: washed red cells, AOPCP (5'-nucleotidase inhibitor), anoxia, SAH
hydrolase activity, and an in-vivo forearm-ischaemia study (3 min tourniquet, cubital venous samples by a
dual-chamber stopping syringe, adenosine by radioimmunoassay) with and without 75 mg oral dipyridamole.
Means ± SE throughout. No compartmental model; half-lives are from the initial log-linear fall of tracer or of
total adenosine.

## Model and parameters
No model; the transferable numbers are in-vitro half-lives in human whole blood at 37 °C (all means, read on the
page images, PDF pp. 3-4):

- Basal plasma adenosine 82 ± 14 nM (n = 9) after 15 min preincubation; range 20-200 nM; 100 ± 22 nM after 1 h
  (printed C801).
- Tracer at basal level (total unchanged): half-life 0.6 s (Fig. 2B); tracer left 10.3, 5.8, 3.5, 2.1% at 2, 3,
  4, 5 s. Turnover 1.5 µmol/L blood/min (= 1.5 nmol/mL/min) at haematocrit 46%.
- Washed red cells: 0.65 and 0.7 s (two experiments). With AOPCP (basal 22 ± 6 nM, n = 3): 0.9 s (Fig. 4);
  turnover 0.5 nmol/mL/min (Discussion, C804).
- Raised to 1 µM (1,047 ± 84 nM, n = 14): total adenosine 387, 288, 166 nM at 2, 4, 6 s; total half-life
  1.4 s (Fig. 5A, Results), tracer half-life 1.2 s (Fig. 5B). At 5 µM: 1.4 and 1.5 s (two experiments).
- Dipyridamole IC50 for uptake 3 µM at 5 µM adenosine; 2.3, 2.0, 2.2 µM at 0.1, 0.3, 1 µM adenosine.
- Dog blood: half-life about 3 min (Fig. 7B), about 300 times slower than human.
- Forearm (in vivo, venous plasma): basal 72 ± 8 nM, peak 190 ± 33 nM at 60 s after release, 120 ± 12 nM at
  7 min; after dipyridamole (plasma 3.0 ± 0.6 µM) basal 111 ± 13 nM.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Forearm blood flow rose about 10-fold after ischaemia and returned within 2 min while venous adenosine stayed
raised for at least 7 min; the authors conclude flow and venous adenosine do not track each other. No
concentration-effect relationship, ke0 or tPeak. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None. Species (human vs dog) is the only large difference, which the authors attribute to dog red cells lacking
the adenosine carrier.

## Caveats
- In vitro whole blood: there is no endothelium in the vial, so the half-lives reflect red-cell (and other blood
  cell) uptake only. Endothelial uptake is discussed (C805) from literature kinetics, not measured.
- The 1 µM half-life is 1.5 s in the abstract but 1.4 s in the Results and Fig. 5A; the abstract's "11% within
  5 s" fall in specific activity differs from the Results' "about 20% in 6 s".
- Small n (two experiments for washed cells and for 5 µM); subjects not described.
- Concentrations far below therapeutic bolus levels (6-12 mg IV) are not studied; 5 µM is the highest.
