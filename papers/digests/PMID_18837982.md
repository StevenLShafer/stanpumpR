# Hammer 2008: fenoldopam PK/PD in anaesthetised children

## Study
- Design (p2): randomised, multicentre, placebo-controlled, double-blind, dose-ranging, two-phase trial designed
  with FDA consultation. Phase 1: 30-min blinded infusion of placebo or fenoldopam 0.05, 0.2, 0.8 or 3.2
  mcg/kg/min after anaesthesia was stable. Phase 2: open-label titration starting at 0.1 mcg/kg/min, in steps of
  0.3-0.5 mcg/kg/min no more often than every 20 min, to a target MAP (floor 50 mmHg, 40 mmHg in neonates), until
  deliberate hypotension was no longer needed.
- Population (p1, p3): 77 children enrolled, up to 12 years and Tanner stage 1-2, having surgery with planned
  deliberate hypotension of at least 2 h (spinal fusion, orthopaedic, craniofacial, neurosurgery). Exclusions:
  weight <2 kg, sulfite allergy, contraindication to dopaminergics. One child got no drug (pump failure); 76
  completed. Mean age 51.9 months (range 3 weeks to 12.75 years). 56% male, 65% Caucasian.
- Anaesthesia (p2) was not standardised: propofol, sevoflurane or halothane induction, vecuronium or
  cisatracurium, and fentanyl. It was held constant during the blinded period.
- Table 1 (p4, rendered) gives group means: weight 14.1-19.1 kg, height 84.7-95.1 cm, age 45.6-60.6 months.
- Sampling (p2-3): one sample at 20 min into the blinded infusion (C20) in all children. Children of 2 years or
  more gave a second at 5, 10 or 15 min (sparse design). All gave samples just before and 5 min after the end of
  the open-label infusion. Total blood drawn was capped at 1% of estimated blood volume.
- Assay: validated HPLC-MS/MS, plasma fenoldopam (p3).
- Analysis (p3): non-compartmental (kel from the end-of-infusion and post-infusion samples, CL = rate/C20) and
  population compartmental modelling in NONMEM V. MAP and HR were each modelled against plasma concentration in
  NONMEM.
- Infusion exposure (p5): mean 3.21 h (range 0.50-6.75 h). Mean average dose 1.00 mcg/kg/min (<0.05 to 3.2); mean
  maximum dose 1.90 mcg/kg/min (<0.05 to 4.10).

## Model and parameters
A one-compartment model fitted the plasma data (p6, rendered). The paper has no parameter table: every estimate
is in the Results prose (p6) and repeated in the Discussion (p7).

  - {name: kel (non-compartmental), value: 12.4, unit: 1/h, source: "Results p6 (printed 'L/h'); Discussion p7 (h-1)", note: "mean ± 0.10 SE; unit typo on p6"}

  - {name: t1/2, value: 3.15, unit: min, source: "Discussion p7", note: "from compartmental kel; 3.35 min from the non-compartmental kel"}
  - {name: V, value: 0.232, unit: L/kg, source: "derived: CL/kel = 3.06/13.2", note: "NOT reported by the paper; NCA pair gives 0.244 L/kg"}
  - {name: "adult kel (quoted)", value: 9.05, unit: 1/h, source: "Discussion p7", note: "± 1.21; comparator, source not cited at this point"}
  - {name: "adult CL (quoted)", value: 1.66, unit: L/h/kg, source: "Discussion p7", note: "± 0.42; comparator"}

- C20 was linear in dose (p6). Age had no significant effect on kel in either analysis (p6).
- The paper reports no volume, no interindividual variability and no residual error.
- Arithmetic checks (derived): ln 2/13.2 h is 3.15 min and ln 2/12.4 h is 3.35 min, both as printed. At steady
  state, 0.5 mcg/kg/min ÷ 3.06 L/h/kg = 9.8 ng/mL, which matches the Discussion's "approximately 0.5 mcg/kg/min"
  for 10 ng/mL (p7). Paediatric/adult CL = 3.06/1.66 = 1.84. The paper nevertheless calls the paediatric values
  close to the adult ones relative to their variances.
- Dose basis: the paper speaks of fenoldopam mesylate doses and measures plasma fenoldopam. It never says
  whether the mcg are salt or base.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MAP (p6, rendered):** the Emax model fitted better than a linear one. The population estimates, with
  approximate SEs:
  - Emax -27 ± 5.4 mmHg (a fall).
  - EC50 9.1 ± 4.23 ng/mL.
  - Age and baseline MAP were tested as covariates on Emax. Baseline MAP significantly improved the fit, but the
    paper prints no functional form or coefficient for it. Age was not significant.
  - The paper does not say whether the effect was direct or delayed through an effect compartment, and reports
    no ke0.
- **HR (p6):** linear in concentration, with intercept 39 ± 6 bpm and slope 0.24 ± 0.05 bpm per ng/mL. Baseline HR
  was a significant covariate; age was not.
  - The Discussion (p7) instead gives the paediatric slope as 0.261 ± 0.10 bpm per ng/mL, against an adult
    0.501 ± 0.11. That is an internal inconsistency with the Results' 0.24 ± 0.05; the source does not explain it.
- **Blinded phase (Figure 1, p4; text p3-4):**
  - 0.2 mcg/kg/min was the lowest rate that lowered MAP. The maximum fall was at 0.8 mcg/kg/min, at 25 min
    (Figure 1: about -11 mmHg).
  - At 3.2 mcg/kg/min the changes were similar, with significant falls at 5, 15, 20 and 25 min.
  - At 3.2 mcg/kg/min, HR rose 9-17 bpm (Figure 2, p5).
- **Open-label phase (p4):** rates up to 1.2 mcg/kg/min lowered MAP; above 1.2, little more fall but more
  tachycardia.
- **Onset and offset (p6):**
  - Observed onset was about 5 min, the first measurement significantly different from placebo, at 0.8.
  - Observed offset: median 19 min to a rise of the lesser of 10 mmHg or 10% on two consecutive readings, possibly
    an overestimate given 5-min sampling.
  - Model-predicted onset and offset: 4.0 and 15.8 min.
- **Effective range: the paper is internally inconsistent.**
  - The abstract (p1) gives 0.8-1.2 mcg/kg/min, the open-label titration.
  - The Conclusion (p8) gives 0.2 to 0.8 mcg/kg/min "when given with anesthesia", from the blinded phase.
  - The adult package-insert range is quoted as 0.05-0.3 mcg/kg/min (abstract p1; Discussion p6).
- **Not reported:** no MEAC, no ke0, no tPeak. This is not an opioid.
- **Adverse events (Table 2, p6):** drug-related AEs in 1/15 at 0.05 and 3/15 at 3.2 (blinded), 13/74 (17%)
  open-label (hypotension 9) and 1/77 at follow-up (oliguria). Hypokalaemia occurred in 7/76, 1 judged possibly
  drug-related.

## Covariates
- Weight: CL is reported per kg, with no allometric exponent tested or reported.
- Age: not significant for kel, CL, MAP or HR.
- Baseline MAP on Emax and baseline HR on the HR relation: significant, with no equations given.

## Caveats
- **Group sizes are internally inconsistent.** The text (p3) gives 15, 15, 16, 15, 15 for placebo, 0.05, 0.2, 0.8
  and 3.2, summing to 76. Table 1 headers (p4, rendered) give N = 16, 16, 15, 15, 15, summing to 77. Table 1's own
  gender counts imply 16, 15, 16, 15, 15. The Corlopam label's Table 6 (NJ_label-corlopam_019922_2020-09-04,
  p10) has that last set.
- The 12.4 "L/h" for the non-compartmental kel (p6, rendered) is a unit typo; the Discussion (p7) prints h-1.
- PK sampling was sparse (two samples per child of 2 years or more, fewer under 2) and short-infusion. kel rests
  largely on end-of-infusion samples.
- The children were anaesthetised with non-standardised regimens during deliberate hypotension. Excluded:
  neonates under the weight floor, adolescents beyond Tanner II, chronic hypertension, hepatic or renal
  dysfunction (p8 limitations).
- Population conflict: the paper describes all 77 as surgical patients having deliberate hypotension (p2). The
  Corlopam label's account of the paediatric trial (label p9-10) says 58 were enrolled with surgery and 19 in an
  ICU. The cause is unresolved.
