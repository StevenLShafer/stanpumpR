# Ericsson 1999 (EJCP): clevidipine 1-h infusion with mass balance in healthy men

## Study
- Open single-dose study (Methods, p62) in 8 healthy male Caucasians: age 27.6 (3.8) y, range 23-33. Weight is
  given as 78.8 kg (no SD printed), range 69-86.
- Dosing: a fixed 1030 nmol/min for 60 min, as 30 mL of 20% lipid emulsion containing 0.94 mg/mL clevidipine plus
  a 0.12 MBq/mL tritium tracer.
  - (Derived: 1030 nmol/min ≈ 13 nmol/min/kg at 78.8 kg. 30 mL × 0.94 mg/mL ÷ 456.3 g/mol ÷ 60 min ≈ 1030
    nmol/min.)
- Sampling: venous blood from the forearm opposite the infusion, from 0.5 min to 32 h after the start, densely
  sampled around the end of infusion (60.5-70 min). Urine and faeces were collected for 7 days.
- Assays: GC-MS for clevidipine, LOQ 1 nmol/L (CV 1.3-8.2%). LC for H 152/81, LOQ 50 nmol/L.
- Analysis: individual one- and two-compartment models with zero-order input in WinNonlin 1.1, weighted
  1/ypred², chosen by AIC.

Other results:
- **Blood metabolism:** about 7% of total clearance (Results, p63). This assumes 5.5 L of blood per 70 kg and the
  in-vitro hydrolysis rate.
- **Mass balance:** 83 (3)% of the radioactivity was recovered, 68 (5)% in urine and 15 (5)% in faeces. No parent
  drug was found in the excreta.
- **Lag:** venous concentrations started to fall about 1 min after the infusion stopped. The authors attribute
  this to an arterial-venous reservoir.

**Arithmetic check (derived):** the two-compartment identity with CL 0.142, Vss 0.58 and half-lives 1.6 and
15.5 min returns V1 0.356 L/kg, k10 0.399/min, C1 99.0% and an AUC share of 91.1%. These are consistent with
Table 1. Note that the means of individual derived values need not satisfy the identity exactly.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- MAP fell from 87 (8) to 77 (6) mmHg and HR rose from 59 (6) to 82 (9) bpm during the infusion (Results, p64-65).
- The effects reached steady state by the first reading, 15 min after the start, and were back to baseline at the
  first reading 15 min after the stop.
- **No concentration-effect model** was fitted, and no ke0, EC50 or hysteresis analysis was done. The authors
  infer that blood-biophase equilibrium is rapid and that the metabolite is haemodynamically inactive.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- n = 8 healthy young men at one dose rate.
- Venous sampling: the companion Anesthesiology 2000 paper shows that venous concentrations at steady state are
  about half the arterial ones.
- The authors attribute the longer terminal half-life (15.5 vs 9.5 min in the BJCP study) to the lower LOQ, which
  let them follow concentrations for longer (Discussion, p65).
- The authors do not exclude elimination from the peripheral compartment.
