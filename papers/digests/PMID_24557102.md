# Ting 2014: response surface predictions of wake-up time, scoliosis surgery

## Study
Retrospective record review, Taipei Veterans General Hospital, 2005-2011. n = 23 ASA I-II scoliosis patients (8 M / 15 F), age 15.9 +/- 2.9 y (range 12-24, median 15), weight 51.2 +/- 8.6 kg, height 162.0 +/- 6.6 cm (Table 3, PDF p3 / printed 548). Excluded: missing demographics, neurological disability, impaired hearing, epilepsy, ongoing opioid use, antiepileptic/sedative/stimulant drugs. Propofol 2 mg/kg plus fentanyl 5 ug/kg induction, maintenance with fentanyl and desflurane, cisatracurium stopped 30 min before the wake-up test. Fentanyl infusion stopped 14.5 +/- 9.2 min before the test; vaporizer turned off at the test start, fresh gas flow raised 2 to 4 L/min. Wake-up time = vaporizer off to first finger/toe movement: 10.3 +/- 4.7 min (mean +/- SD).

The authors built three sevoflurane-remifentanil response-surface models from previously published volunteer data (16 volunteers, 263 observations, OAA/S scale), then adapted them to desflurane-fentanyl by potency ratios. Fit by -2LL with Matlab fminsearch, naive pooled.

## Model and parameters
Modified Greco surface (Eq 1, PDF p2): Effect = Emax * U^n / (U^n + 1), with U = Cer/C50r + Ces/C50s - alpha*(Cer/C50r)*(Ces/C50s); Emax fixed at 1 (probability of no response).

Fit to volunteers (Table 2): 88% / 87% / 90% of predictions matched observations; Spearman rho 0.76 / 0.73 / 0.75 (all P < 0.001) for OAA/S<2 / <3 / <4.

PK used (not tabulated): published fentanyl PK (Shafer 1990) with Ce from Scott 1991 (refs 17, 18); a modified multicompartment desflurane brain-tissue model (Lerou, refs 22-23) driven by recorded end-tidal desflurane. Ce predicted every 10 s from vaporizer off to 10 min after response.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: OAA/S (Observer's Assessment of Alertness/Sedation) probability of non-response; OAA/S<2 = no response to moderate prodding/shaking, <3 = responds to prodding but not to name called loudly, <4 = responds to name called loudly. In patients the end point was movement of fingers and toes on command.
- C50r is a remifentanil Ce in ng/mL (volunteer-derived), not a MEAC; no MEAC, analgesic concentration, ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak of its own is reported. The fentanyl-to-remifentanil conversion carries the opioid information (potency 1:1.2, above). Assay (total vs free, blood vs plasma): not applicable, effect-site model concentrations only.
- Results (PDF p4-5, printed 550-551): mean time difference, observed response minus time model probability reached 50%: -2.6 +/- 3.6 min (OAA/S<2), 2.8 +/- 5.6 min (OAA/S<3), 52.6 +/- 32.3 min (OAA/S<4). ANOVA P<0.001; OAA/S<2 and <3 better than <4 (P<0.001), not different from each other (P=0.946). The sign convention in the Discussion is unsigned (2.6 +/- 3.6).

## Covariates
None in the model. Volunteers were 24.1 +/- 3.5 y; patients 12-24 y, so the adult model was applied to adolescents without adjustment (the authors' stated limitation).

## Caveats
- Retrospective, n = 23, three surgeons, 11 anesthesiologists; no consent. Model-predicted, not measured, concentrations.
- Surface fit on volunteers without pain or an endotracheal tube; adaptation to desflurane/fentanyl rests on assumed potency ratios (the authors note a different 3.3 would change results).
- Residual propofol/midazolam ignored. Authors state they lacked data to evaluate analgesia predictions.
- The authors declare equity interest in Applied Medical Visualizations (drug-display company).
