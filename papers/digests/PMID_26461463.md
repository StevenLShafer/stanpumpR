# Ryu 2016: metoprolol pharmacokinetics in pregnancy and lactation

## Study
- Multicentre (Obstetric-Fetal Pharmacology Research Unit Network) open study of steady-state oral metoprolol given for clinical reasons.
  Twenty-two pregnant women aged 21–40 years; 15 white, 4 black, 3 other or mixed. Mean age 30 ± 5 years and
  height 167 ± 8 cm on study day 1 (PDF p3 / printed 583).
- Indications: hypertension 10, arrhythmia 4, cardiac hypertrophy 2, tachycardia 2, dilated aorta 2, migraine 1,
  heart disease/MI 1.
- Study windows: early pregnancy (10–14 wk, n = 4), mid (22–26 wk, n = 14–15), late (34–38 wk, n = 15–17), and
  ≥3 months postpartum (n = 9; 4 lactating, 5 not). The abstract and Table 1 give slightly different n.
- Doses 25–750 mg/day in divided doses, immediate-release (Caraco) or extended-release (Toprol-XL), with the same
  formulation on every study day. The study supplied the drug for the 3 days before each PK visit.
- Table 1 (PDF p3): body weight 73 ± 8 / 89 ± 25 / 98 ± 26 / 81 ± 27 kg and median dose 87.5 / 75 / 100 / 75 mg/day
  (early / mid / late / postpartum).
- Sampling: plasma at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 24 h, truncated to the dosing interval. Urine was
  collected over one interval; breast milk every 2–3 h; cord blood at delivery.
- Assay: HPLC-MS/MS, plasma and milk LOQ 1.0 ng/mL (LOD 0.5 ng/mL). Total plasma concentrations.
- Analysis: noncompartmental. CL/F = dose/AUCτ,ss (linear trapezoid), with actual body weight for weight-adjusted
  values.
- CYP2D6 genotypes: 14 EM, 6 IM, 1 PM (*4/*4). One subject could not be fully genotyped. No duplications were found.
- The PM and one subject on fluoxetine were excluded from the PK comparisons. Subjects on sertraline (3) and
  hydroxychloroquine (1) were kept.
- ER and IR results were pooled: CL/F, CLrenal and the metabolic ratios did not differ between formulations.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No pharmacodynamics were measured: no heart rate, blood pressure or effect concentration, and no ke0 or tPeak.
- The Discussion notes that α-hydroxymetoprolol has about one-tenth of metoprolol's β-blocking potency (cited from
  Regårdh 1979, a dog study; PDF p8).
- Not an opioid, so there is no MEAC or equianalgesic ratio.

## Covariates
- Pregnancy stage. CYP2D6 genotype: EM > IM > PM, with no pregnancy induction in the PM.
- Formulation (ER and IR were pooled; half-life differed).
- CLrenal correlated weakly with creatinine clearance (r = 0.38) and urine pH (r = −0.39).
- No covariate equations.

## Caveats
- Small paired groups (n = 5 mid, n = 8 late), with large SDs (CV about 50–60%).
- Doses varied between and within subjects. Bioavailability is dose-dependent, which the authors raise themselves
  (PDF p7).
- ER and IR were pooled.
- Oral only, so CL and F cannot be separated. The comparator is the same women ≥3 months postpartum, some of them
  lactating, not non-pregnant volunteers.
- Weight rose in pregnancy, so the absolute and per-kg ratios differ (2.96 against 2.62).
- The PM is a single subject. Some text values differ slightly from Table 2.

- The registry note (csv:194) that the ratio is unadjusted for dose nonlinearity reflects the authors' own caveat.

The derivation:
- **matches** (arithmetic): well-stirred with E0 = 0.5 means fu·CLint = QH. A 2.96-fold CLint gives
  E = 2.96/3.96 = 0.747, so CL × 1.495 and F × (1 − 0.747)/0.5 = 0.505, and CL/F × 2.96 (derived).
- The constants 1.5 and 0.5 are rounded correctly.
- The split assumes complete absorption and unchanged hepatic blood flow. Ryu's Discussion argues that absorption is
  not impaired and that the change is CYP2D6 first-pass metabolism, which supports the split qualitatively.
  Ryu itself does not measure CL and F separately, which the header says (lines 99–103).

Applying the ratio to Ryu's IM, normal and ultrarapid phenotypes but not to the PM is consistent with Ryu:
- The ratio pooled EM and IM.
- The PM showed no α-hydroxylation.
- Ultrarapid metabolisers were not studied (no duplications found), so applying the ratio to them is an
  extrapolation.

Comparator:
- Ryu's ratio is late pregnancy against the same women ≥3 months postpartum.
- stanpumpR applies it to Lammers 2017's healthy young men (CL/F 65.8/0.45 = 146 L/h at the published size;
  derived).
- Ryu's postpartum women had 192 L/h (1.31 × Lammers) and late-pregnant women 568 L/h. The model's pregnant typical
  value at the published size is 146 × 2.96 ≈ 439 L/h (derived), before size scaling.
- Applying a within-subject ratio to a non-pregnant baseline is the usual construction. The header says plainly that
  the factor is relative to the published values (line 144).

Citation year:
- The header, roxygen and test call it "Ryu 2015" / "(2015)" (lines 66, 145, 165; test :72). That is the epub year.
- The reference list, reference string, docs and registry give 2016;56:581-589, which is correct.
