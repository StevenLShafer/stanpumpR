# Wadelius 1997: CYP2D6 induction in pregnancy (dextromethorphan probe)

## Study
140 healthy pregnant Swedish women aged 19-41 y were genotyped for CYP2D6 (*1, *3, *4; PCR/restriction digestion). 6 were poor metabolizers (4.3%; five *4/*4, one *3/*3), 96 extensive (68.6%), 38 heterozygous extensive (27.1%). 17 completed phenotyping: 4 PM, 7 HEM, 6 EM; age 23-34 y (mean 28), 13 primiparous. Probe: 30 mg oral dextromethorphan, one plasma sample 2 h after dose, in week 36 of pregnancy and again 7-11 weeks postpartum (same women as controls). Mean weight 71 kg (63-80) in week 36, 62 kg (55-76) postpartum. Plasma assay by the method of Mortimer 1989 (not detailed). Wilcoxon matched-pairs test. No codeine was given: it is a probe study, not a codeine study.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamic end point, ke0, tPeak, MEAC or potency ratio (not an opioid study; dextromethorphan is a CYP2D6 probe, with the sample timed at 2 h as an approximation of the peak). The related metoprolol study (refs 9-10, Hogstedt) is quoted in the Discussion: metoprolol clearance 4-5-fold higher in pregnancy and CYP2D6 metoprolol ratios reduced 64-65%.

## Covariates
CYP2D6 genotype (EM, HEM, PM) and pregnancy state. No gestational-age dose-response: single time point at week 36.

## Caveats
Small n (17; 13 EM/HEM, 4 PM). One sample per phenotyping at 2 h, so a changed absorption rate or blood flow could shift the ratio (authors argue against this using the CYP3A ratio). The PM ratio rose 63% (opposite direction), not tested statistically. The change is a ratio of a probe metabolic ratio, not a clearance, and is not codeine-specific. Postpartum control at 7-11 weeks; weights differ by 9 kg.
