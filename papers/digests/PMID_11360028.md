# Battino 2001: single-dose lamotrigine kinetics in children

## Study
Open single-dose study at the Carlo Besta Neurological Institute, Milan. **19 patients with epilepsy aged
8 months to 30 years** (8 were <= 6 y, 7 were 7-14 y, 4 were older than 14 y), all hospitalised, all
studied after their **initial** lamotrigine dose, none with a disease affecting pharmacokinetics.

Comedication, unchanged for at least a month before the study (printed p 218, PDF p 2):
- **enzyme-inducer group, n = 10**: carbamazepine, phenytoin or phenobarbital, with or without drugs taken
  not to affect microsomal enzymes (vigabatrin, ethosuximide);
- **valproate group, n = 9**.

There is **no monotherapy arm**: every patient was on comedication, so the study measures the
inducer-against-valproate contrast only.

Dose: a single oral lamotrigine dose 0.6-2.2 mg/kg after an overnight fast (12.5 or 25 mg capsules); the
lower doses were used once reduced starting doses were recommended to lower rash risk. Blood at 0, 0.5, 1,
2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 36 h, and up to 96 h in 10 subjects. HPLC assay, day-to-day precision 3.8%, LOQ
0.1 mg/L, external QC (Heathcontrol, Cardiff); comedication by FPIA.

Analysis: **non-compartmental**. Half-life from the log-linear terminal slope, AUC by the trapezoidal rule
with extrapolation by Ct/lambda, Cl/F = dose/AUC, Vd/F = Cl/(F x lambda); Siphar/Win. Cmax and AUC were
dose-normalised to 2 mg/kg. Geometric means; Mann-Whitney between groups; linear regression and Spearman
for age.

## Model and parameters
**Table 1** (printed p 218, PDF p 2; read from the rendered page - the text layer scrambles this table's
columns badly). Geometric means with ranges:

| Parameter | Inducer group (n = 10) | Valproate group (n = 9) |
|---|---|---|
| Tmax (h) | 1.7 (0.5-3.0) | 2.3 (0.5-6.0) |
| Cmax (ug/mL, normalised to 2 mg/kg) | 1.9 (1.0-4.1) | 2.1 (0.3-6.2) |
| AUC (ug/mL per hour, normalised) | 16.5 (8.9-60.7) | 60.4 (4.3-303.9), p < 0.01 |
| Vd/F (L/kg) | 1.20 (0.47-1.95) | 1.11 (0.61-5.93) |
| T1/2 (h) | **8.1** (3.8-29.7) | **41.7** (19.1-102.1), p < 0.001 |
| Cl/F (mL/h per kg) | **110** (30-207) | **38** (7-158), p < 0.001 |

Demographics: inducer group mean age 12 y (range 2-30), weight 39 kg (12-80); valproate group mean age 8 y
(0.8-19), weight 27 kg (9-68); groups comparable for age and sex, with a tendency for the valproate
patients to be younger.

The abstract quotes the same contrast in L/h per kg: "Cl/F 0.11 vs 0.04 L/h per kg" and "T1/2 8.1 vs
41.7 hours", inducers vs valproate. 110 mL/h/kg = 0.110 L/h/kg and 38 mL/h/kg = 0.038 L/h/kg, so the
abstract rounds the valproate clearance from 0.038 to 0.04. **Abstract and Table 1 agree.**

Derived ratio (mine): Cl/F 110/38 = **2.9** between the inducer and valproate groups; half-life ratio
41.7/8.1 = 5.1. The paper states AUC and T1/2 were "approximately 4-fold and 5-fold" different respectively
(printed p 219).

Age: within each comedication group, Tmax, Vd/F and T1/2 showed **no** correlation with age; Cmax and AUC
tended to rise with age (significant by linear regression for both in the valproate group and for Cmax in
the inducer group; by Spearman only Cmax in the valproate group). Cl/F fell with age but not significantly.
By the Figure 1 regression lines, AUC rose 2- to 4-fold between ages 1 and 16 (printed p 220).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Single-dose kinetics only; no effect measure, no ke0, no target range. The Discussion notes that no
clear-cut lamotrigine concentration-response relationship has been established.

## Covariates
Comedication class (inducer vs valproate) and age, analysed within comedication group. Dose is normalised
per kg and Cmax/AUC are dose-normalised. No sex, renal or hepatic covariate. The authors' notable negative:
the age effect on Cmax and AUC is **not** explained by half-life (which showed no age relation), so it is
not a pure metabolic-rate effect; they point to a possible volume-of-distribution/absorption contribution.

## Caveats
- **Small (19 patients, 10 vs 9) and with no monotherapy control**, so the study gives a ratio between two
  comedication regimens and cannot attribute it between induction and inhibition.
- Apparent parameters only (Cl/F, Vd/F): no intravenous reference, so bioavailability is not separable - the
  authors say so.
- Dose range 0.6-2.2 mg/kg with dose-normalised Cmax and AUC; linearity is assumed, not shown.
- One "child" is 30 years old and another 22 (Table 1), so the title's "children" is loose; the valproate
  group skews younger than the inducer group, confounding the comedication contrast with age in a study that
  separately finds an age effect on AUC.
- The terminal half-life in the valproate group (up to 102 h) rests on sampling to 36-96 h; the two groups
  were not sampled for the same duration in every subject, which is exactly the artefact that inflates a
  long half-life relative to a short one.
- The text layer of Table 1 is garbled (column values interleaved); any future use of these numbers should
  read the rendered page, as was done here.
