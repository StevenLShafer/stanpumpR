# Dyck 1993: computer-controlled infusion of IV dexmedetomidine in adult volunteers

## Study
Two data sets. (1) Zero-order data: the 10 healthy men of the preceding paper (Dyck 1993, Anesthesiology 78:813-820),
2 ug/kg IV over 5 min; Discussion gives this group's range as 29-44 y, 60-98 kg. (2) Prospective CCIP test: 6 healthy
men, age 31.5 y (27-40), weight 82 kg (71-98), ASA 1-2, STANPUMP targeting 0.49, 0.65, 0.81, 0.97 ng/mL for 30 min
each (60-120% of the peak after 2 ug/kg IM); radial arterial samples 5 and 25 min after each step and 10-120 min after
stopping. GC-MS assay (Farmos). Three-compartment mammillary model fitted with MKMODEL (extended least squares,
weights 1/predicted^2), two-stage and pooled (naive pooled) approaches; covariates (age, weight, height, LBM by James,
BSA by DuBois) entered linearly; log-likelihood difference 4 required.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, no ke0. Notes concentration-dependent nonlinearity (smaller apparent V1 at higher concentrations,
attributed to alpha2 vasoconstriction) and haemodynamic effects of rapid injection.

## Covariates
Age, weight, LBM, BSA: no improvement. Height on Cl1 (linear) significant. Weight adjustment not supported within
60-98 kg.

## Caveats
Small n (10 + 6), healthy young men only; naive pooled fitting without IIV; nonlinear PK across concentrations; CCIP
model unreliable before 5 min and after 60 min (Fig 7). The authors state the pooled zero-order parameters gave good
prospective performance.

- **discrepancy (documentation)**, R/drugs_dexmedetomidine.R:9-14 and inst/help/drugs/dexmedetomidine.md:3: they say
  the fixed parameters are "close to the pooled estimates (V1 8.05, V2 12.4, V3 175; Cl2 2.05, Cl3 2.0)" and that 0.445
  L/min is the pooled height equation at about 176 cm. Those are the *with-covariates* column; the code's values are
  exactly the separate no-covariate pooled column, in which Cl1 = 0.445 L/min is a fitted constant. The "height term not
  applied" framing is therefore misleading: the code implements a published model (the one the authors tested
  prospectively), not a truncated one. Coincidentally 0.0101 x 176 - 1.33 = 0.448 L/min.
