# Onwuchuruba 2014: maternal and cord vancomycin levels at delivery

## Study
Prospective, single centre (University of Tennessee, Knoxville), a little over 2 years (p1-2, printed
352.e1-e2). Laboring women with GBS-positive culture (or preterm, GBS unknown) and high-risk penicillin allergy
with clindamycin resistance or unknown sensitivity. 56 consented, 55 analysed (one emergency delivery 25 min
into the first infusion excluded: maternal 27.8 µg/mL, cord undetectable). Three sequential dosing phases:
- Phase I (13 months, n = 31, one twin pair, 32 neonates): 1 g IV every 12 h.
- Phase II (6 months, n = 12): 15 mg/kg IV every 12 h.
- Phase III (7 months, n = 12): 20 mg/kg IV every 8 h, maximum single dose 2 g.

Weight-based doses rounded to the nearest 250 mg bag; 1.25-1.5 g infused over 90 min, 1.75-2 g over 2 h
(institution rate 1 g/h otherwise). Maternal serum and cord blood total vancomycin at delivery. Assay not
described. Cockcroft-Gault creatinine clearance in phase III only. Maternal weights, ages and BMI values not
tabulated. Statistics: chi-square / Fisher exact.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
"Therapeutic" defined as 10-40 µg/mL total serum, for adults and neonates (p2, citing Rybak 2006 and de Hoog
2000; the text layer drops the µ). Proportion therapeutic at delivery: phase I 32% maternal / 9% cord;
phase II 50% / 33%; phase III 83% / 83% (p1, p3). GBS MIC breakpoint 1 µg/mL exceeded in all 56 cord samples
(p3). Cord level exceeded maternal in 19 of 56 (35%), at 3.5-11.5 h after the last dose, 15 of them beyond 6 h.
Phases I-II: 88% of mothers with therapeutic levels delivered within 6 h of the last dose vs 22% of those
without (P < .001). Authors' inference: transfer is slow in both directions, with fetal levels reaching
maternal. Protein binding 10-50% (cited, not measured). Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. BMI > 30 kg/m2 and number of doses examined descriptively, not significant.

## Caveats
Single samples per mother at an uncontrolled time; maternal weight and gestational age not reported per
patient; assay not described; renal function measured only in phase III; small phases (n = 12). No volume,
clearance or half-life estimated, so the paper cannot by itself quantify pregnancy changes.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/drugs_vancomycin.R:78-92.** The repo says Ansari's Table 7 gives "increased volume of distribution and
  clearance" for vancomycin and that the review prints no factor; it applies `PREGNANCY_GFR_FACTOR` = 1.5
  (R/constants.R:810) to the Cockcroft-Gault term when no creatinine is entered, and does not apply a volume
  change. This paper gives neither a volume nor a clearance and no pregnancy-vs-non-pregnant comparison. Its only
  related statements are qualitative: renal filtration increases substantially in pregnancy (citing its ref 14)
  and vancomycin clearance is proportional to creatinine clearance. So the paper is consistent with the
  direction the repo applies but supports no number; the 1.5 comes from Ansari's physiological statement, as the
  header says. The repo makes no quantitative claim from this paper, so nothing is mis-transcribed.
  **Cannot verify** the volume/clearance claim from this source; see `open_questions`.
- **Independent rough check against the library's Thomson 2009 model (derived, not a verification).** Simulating
  the phase III doses with the table's timing, weight assumed = dose / 20 mg/kg (true weight not reported;
  rounding to 250 mg bags makes this ±8-10%), at a creatinine clearance of 150 mL/min (mid of 122-198), predicted
  maternal levels were 6.3-30.0 µg/mL against observed 6.6-35.5, median observed/predicted about 1.1 (range about
  0.8-2.0); at 100 mL/min the median ratio was about 0.8. The library, with the creatinine left blank, multiplies
  an assumed normal clearance by 1.5, which for a young woman falls in that range. No evidence here of a gross
  error in the pregnant-patient curve, but single uncontrolled samples cannot test the unapplied volume change.
- **Units**: paper reports µg/mL total serum; library concentrations are mcg/mL total serum (R/drugs_vancomycin.R:5-6).
  Consistent. The library's threshold (1.4 mg/L total = MIC 1 / fu 0.70) is a different construct from the paper's
  10-40 µg/mL "therapeutic" range; the paper's MIC breakpoint of 1 µg/mL for GBS matches the MIC the library uses.
