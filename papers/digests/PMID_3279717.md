# Kanto 1988: Pharmacokinetics of atropine, scopolamine and glycopyrrolate (review)

## Study
Narrative review (no new data) of anticholinergic assays and pharmacokinetics, mostly atropine; much of it from the authors' own Turku group. Covers assays (bioassay, RIA, radioreceptor assay RRA, GC-MS), IV/IM/oral/rectal/oropharyngeal/intratracheal/inhaled atropine, placental and lumbar-CSF transfer, age and sex, metabolism; short sections on scopolamine and glycopyrrolate.

Assay point that matters for every number below (PDF p2, printed p70): atropine is racemic and only l-hyoscyamine is active. RIA measures d+l; RRA measures active antimuscarinics. In Aaltonen (ref 21) RIA concentrations were 2-3x the RRA ones, while RRA gave larger V (3.9 ± 1.5 vs 1.7 ± 0.7 L/kg) and CL (15.4 ± 1.3 vs 5.9 ± 3.6 mL/min/kg); half-lives were similar (RRA 3.7 ± 2.3 h, RIA 4.3 ± 1.7 h). RRA showed distribution half-lives of 2.4 and 37.2 min. RIA/RRA sensitivity 0.5-2 ng/mL; chromatographic/RRA 0.1-0.9 ng/mL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t½ke0, EC50 or effect-compartment fit is reported. After IV atropine, plasma concentration and heart-rate rise did not correlate (refs 24, 27); heart rate related to the amount in the peripheral compartment (Adams, ref 24) and heart rate and antisialogogue effect to the log of the peripheral amount (Hinderling Part II, ref 28). The authors conclude the effect site differs from the sampling site (PDF p3).
- Dose-dependent HR: 1.43 µg/kg IV lowered and 4.29 µg/kg IV raised heart rate (ref 26).
- IM/oral: salivary secretion, heart rate, sweating and drowsiness follow absorption (max 1-2 h); pupil and near-point vision peak at 6 h (ref 31). Oral peripheral effects peak at 2-4 h (ref 33).
- Intratracheal 0.02-0.04 mg/kg: peak serum within 2-6 min, maximal heart-rate effect after 6-10 min (unpublished).
- 5.2 ng/mL is cited as a concentration that has caused systemic effects as premedication (ref 44, PDF p5).
- Fetal vagal blockade lags clinically significant umbilical levels (5-15 vs 2-3 min).
- Lumbar CSF: 29.7-50% of serum after 0.015 mg/kg IM, none detected after 0.01 mg/kg IV; no equilibrium with free serum drug by 105 min (ref 27).
- Scopolamine: 0.005 mg/kg IV gave only slight sedation and no amnesia, unlike IM 0.01 or oropharyngeal 0.035 mg/kg (ref 57). Glycopyrrolate antisecretory action about 2-3 h.
- Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
- Age (Table 6, Fig. 4; ref 27): t½ longer under 2 y (larger Vβ) and over 65 y (lower CL, 2.9 vs 6.8 mL/min/kg in adults); Aaltonen also found longer t½ over 55 y from lower CL.
- Sex: no effect in children (ref 25) or the elderly (ref 52).
- Pregnancy: placental transfer rapid (umbilical vein 12% and 93% of maternal at 1 and 5 min, ref 13; maternal-umbilical equilibrium at 6-8 min after 0.01 mg/kg IV, ref 44); after 0.01 mg/kg IM umbilical vein about half of maternal at 40 and 60 min. The review states atropine PK does not differ between parturients and non-pregnant adults (refs 27, 44; PDF p6).
- Renal: renal plasma clearance near renal plasma flow (tubular secretion) and urine-flow dependent (reabsorption); hepatic and renal insufficiency expected to matter (ref 18). Organophosphates appear to inhibit atropine metabolism (ref 56).
- Induced hypotension (nitroprusside + trimetaphan) did not change IM absorption (Fig. 3, ref 32).

## Caveats
- A secondary source: every number is transcribed from the primary papers, several from the authors' own group, often with tiny n (Hinderling n=3, Table 8 n=4).
- Assays differ by 2-3x in concentration and in derived CL and V (RIA vs RRA vs GC-MS), so rows of Table 1 are not interchangeable.
- Table 1's first row (ref 44) is described only as "adult subjects" under anaesthesia; that they were parturients is inferred from the reference title and the p74 text, not stated in the table.
- Scopolamine and glycopyrrolate kinetics are called poorly understood; glycopyrrolate has no PK parameters here (> 90% of radioactivity gone from serum in 5 min, > 80% of biliary and urinary radioactivity unchanged drug).
