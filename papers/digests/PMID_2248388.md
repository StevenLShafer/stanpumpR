# Shafer 1990: Pharmacokinetics of fentanyl by computer-controlled infusion pump

## Study
- 21 patients, ASA 1-4, various surgery under GA (5 open heart, studied to bypass); 7 men; age 58 +/- 11 yr; weight 69 +/- 17 kg. Stanford / Palo Alto VA.
- Fentanyl by CCIP (STANPUMP; Euler approximation, rate updated every 15 s): first 11 patients programmed with McClain and Hug parameters, next 10 with Scott and Stanski. Targets chosen clinically; infusion stopped so predicted concentration fell to 1.5 ng/mL for emergence.
- Arterial samples, 0.5-1 min intervals for 10 min then sparser, mean 372 min (80-1097); 603 samples (594 used in fitting). Serum RIA (Michiels), LLQ 0.25 ng/mL, CV < 5%.
- Part 2: naive pooled fit of all 21 patients with modified MK-MODEL (extended least squares, variance proportional to Cp^2); unscaled, weight-proportional and linear (constant + scalar x weight) models. Part 3: 11 parameter sets tested against data from McClain, Scott, Hudson and Varvel by RMS error and MDAPE.

Log likelihoods: unscaled -1177, weight-scaled -1169, linear -1112 (linear best, improvement small). Unscaled chosen as simpler, because weight scaling did not consistently help in Part 3. Recommended only for 40-90 kg.

Table 2 also gives each prior study re-expressed unscaled (V1/V2/V3 L; CL1/CL2/CL3 L/min): Hudson 22.4/58.9/341, 0.769/4.23/1.62; McClain 26.6/45.8/196, 0.883/4.59/3.48; Scott 12.7/50.7/274, 0.574/4.01/1.95; Varvel 8.71/32.7/178, 0.715/3.43/1.27.

Performance: McClain parameters MDAPE 61% (MDPE +61%), Scott 33% (MDPE +19%); current-study parameters MDAPE 21% (MDPE -13%) on own data. Table 3: current parameters best or near best on all external data sets except Hudson's own.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None: no ke0, tPeak, MEAC or potency. Targets clinical; 1.5 ng/mL used as emergence target. One patient needed naloxone 40 ug for RR 4/min.

## Covariates
Weight examined (proportional and linear); unscaled presented. Height not recorded, so lean body mass could not be tested. No age term.

## Caveats
Naive pooled analysis (no inter-individual variability). Data mostly from older surgical patients; extrapolation before 1 min or beyond 10 h not advised. Small V1 reflects rapid arterial sampling.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- R/drugs_fentanyl.R:22-27 values 12.1/35.7/224 L and 0.632/2.8/1.55 L/min do **not appear in this paper**. Its pooled model is 6.09/28.1/228 L and 0.504/2.87/1.37 L/min (unscaled); the weight-scaled model at 70 kg gives 7.35/31.2/236 L and 0.587/3.32/1.39 L/min (derived); the linear model at 70 kg gives 6.83/31.6/256 L and 0.565/3.22/1.54 L/min (derived). None matches. The code's set may be a later pooled re-analysis of these five data sets (e.g. the STANPUMP default), but this paper does not report it - **discrepancy** for references.md:155 ("The pooled fentanyl parameters") and fentanyl.md:3, which present these as the 1990 paper's model.
- Weight scaling: the paper chose not to scale by weight and recommends use within 40-90 kg; stanpumpR scales volumes by FFM and clearances by FFM^0.75. That is a stanpumpR design decision, not supported by this paper - note, not an error.
- R/drugs_fentanyl.R:33 tPeak 3.694 min "from Shafer/Varvel" - not in this paper (it is Shafer and Varvel 1991) - **cannot verify** here.
- Citation details **match**.
