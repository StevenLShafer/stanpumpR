# Fox 1982: whole body VO2 against perfusion flow at 20 C on bypass

## Study
Prospective clinical physiology study, Birmingham, Alabama, September-October 1979. 17 adults having coronary
artery bypass grafting (2 women, 15 men; age 57 +/- 9.7 y; BSA 2.0 +/- 0.11 m2; mean +/- SD, PDF p1).
Anaesthesia: diazepam 0.3-0.5 mg/kg, N2O/O2, halothane 0.5-1.5%, pancuronium 0.1 mg/kg. Nonpulsatile bypass,
perfusate cooled to 15 C then held at 20 C (nasopharyngeal 21.3 +/- 0.47 C); alpha-stat-style constant
acid-base (100% O2, no CO2, no bicarbonate). Venous Hb 7.1 +/- 1.53 g/100 mL, PCV 21.7 +/- 4.52%.
Five flows (target 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 L/min/m2) in random order, 10 min each, median four flows per patient;
baseline at 2.2 L/min/m2. VO2 by Fick (arteriovenous O2 content difference x calibrated pump flow); jugular
venous samples in 10 patients. Hyperbolic model fitted by nonlinear regression (SAS) to all points treated as independent.

## Model and parameters
Rectangular hyperbola VO2 = a * Q / (Q + b), VO2 in mL O2/min/m2 (body surface area), Q in L/min/m2.
Fig. 1 / eq. 3 (PDF p3): a = 35 +/- 2.03, b = 0.42 +/- 0.055 (SE); asymptote printed as 35.3; r2 = 65%.
Table I (PDF p2; mean +/- SD, flow measured, n not given per row):

|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 +/- 0.084 | 14 +/- 5.4 | 45 +/- 9.6 | 5.6 +/- 1.30 |
| 0.54 +/- 0.101 | 20 +/- 4.1 | 27 +/- 5.3 | 3.7 +/- 0.87 |
| 1.02 +/- 0.107 | 25 +/- 5.7 | 17 +/- 3.4 | 2.5 +/- 0.53 |
| 1.56 +/- 0.129 | 28 +/- 5.8 | 13 +/- 3.5 | 1.8 +/- 0.38 |
| 2.08 +/- 0.180 | 33 +/- 8.2 | 11 +/- 3.3 | 1.6 +/- 0.41 |

Other fits: % O2 extraction = 100 exp(-1.68 * Q^0.47) (eq. 5, r2 89%); ln(MAP) = 3.31 + 0.089 ln(Q) (eq. 4, r2 70%).
Composite normothermic (37 C) animal fit from three older studies (Fig. 6, eq. 6): VO2 = 160 (+/-13.4) * Q/(Q + 0.70 +/- 0.120),
r2 69%. The authors compare the two asymptotes: Q10 of about 2.4 (Discussion, journal p. 246; text layer only); the slope term b differs by 0.29 +/- 0.124 (p = 0.02).
At 20 C, 83% of the asymptote is reached at Q = 2.0, and about 85% needs 2.4 L/min/m2 (text).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not a drug paper. No drug concentrations, ke0, EC50, MEAC or potency ratios. The end points are physiological:
whole-body VO2, % O2 extraction, mixed venous and internal jugular PO2/saturation. Mixed venous saturation
(Table II) 29% at Q 0.25, 54% at 0.54, 78% at 1.02, 94% at 1.56, 99% at 2.08. Jugular saturations are lower
at every flow (25, 41, 58, 69, 82%). The authors read the data as brain flow preserved relative to the body at low Q.
Their practical conclusion: Q of about 1.2 L/min/m2 is probably adequate at 20 C.

## Covariates
Multivariate analysis: Q explained 65% of VO2 variability; adding arterial O2 content and haematocrit (p < 0.0001
each) gave 78%. Patient-to-patient intercept/slope variability accounted for 22% of the remaining variability. No age,
weight or sex effect analysed.

## Caveats
Mean 21.7% PCV (haemodilution), anaesthetised with halothane/N2O/pancuronium, so the VO2 is not that of an awake
or normothermic patient. Only one temperature (20 C) was studied in the patients; the temperature scaling to 37 C rests on
a pooled animal curve. Repeated measures within patients were analysed as independent points. VO2 is per m2 of BSA,
not per kg.

## Verification against stanpumpR
Nothing cites this paper, so there is nothing to verify. For context only: R/gasProperties.R:304 sets
VO2 = 0.0035 * weight (L/min), i.e. 245 mL/min at 70 kg, at all temperatures. This paper's 20 C asymptote of
35 mL/min/m2 at BSA 2.0 is about 70 mL/min (derived: 35 x 2.0), roughly 0.29 of 245 (derived). Its own normothermic
animal asymptote is 160 mL/min/m2 (about 320 mL/min at 2.0 m2; derived). The ratio 35/160 gives the
authors' Q10 of about 2.4 (derived: (160/35)^(10/17) = 2.44, agreeing). Different baseline from the library, and
anaesthetised, haemodiluted patients; see the open question.
