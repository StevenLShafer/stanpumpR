# Youngs 1994: which volumes and clearances shorten decrement times

PDF pages are one-based; PDF p1 = printed p833 ... PDF p10 = printed p842.

## Study
- Simulation study, no patients. Three-compartment mammillary models of fentanyl, alfentanil and sufentanil
  (chosen for their different kinetics), plasma concentration only, deliberately not effect site, to limit the
  number of variables (PDF p2, printed p834).
- Simulations: DOS PC, programs in C written by S.L.S. (Shafer); Euler integration of the compartment equations with
  a 1 s step; Set III target found by Gauss-Newton minimisation (PDF p2 and p4, printed pp834, 836). A footnote offers
  the software on request or on the VA Palo Alto server. The program is not named STANPUMP.
- Infusions: "bolus-elimination-transfer" (BET) regimen, a bolus then an exponentially declining rate that reaches and
  holds a steady plasma concentration, i.e. what a computer-controlled pump delivers (PDF p3, printed p835);
  durations 0-600 min.
- **Set I:** bolus to an initial 100 ng/mL; each of the six parameters raised (and separately lowered) by 5 %;
  percent change in concentration over 0-600 min (Eq. 1).
- **Set II:** 20 %, 50 % and 80 % **decrement times** (time for that percentage fall in plasma concentration after the
  infusion stops) after BET infusions of 0-600 min, each parameter raised 5 % (Eq. 2).
- **Set III:** the change in each parameter alone that would shorten the decrement time by 30 % (taken as about the
  smallest clinically discernible change) (Eq. 3).
- Term defined (PDF p2, printed p834): the 50 % decrement time is identical to the context-sensitive half-time;
  "decrement" was chosen because it refers only to the fall in plasma concentration, whereas "recovery" implies a
  clinical effect, of which plasma concentration is only one determinant.

Results (fentanyl shown in figures; alfentanil and sufentanil described as similar; images checked):
- Set I (PDF p4, printed p836; Fig. 1-2): larger V1 → higher concentration at all times; larger Cl1 → lower at all
  times. Larger V2 lower at first, higher after 50 min; larger V3 lower at all times for fentanyl (alfentanil higher
  after 210 min). Larger Cl2 lower at first, higher after 6 min; larger Cl3 higher after 100 min. 5 % decreases were
  near mirror images.
- Set II (PDF pp4-5, printed pp836-837; Fig. 3-4): larger V1 always lengthened and larger Cl1 always shortened the
  decrement times. Fentanyl 80 % decrement time: larger V2 shortened it after brief infusions but lengthened it after
  infusions > 8 min; V3 the same with the crossover at 90 min; Cl2 at 2 min; Cl3 at 12 min. Order of transition with
  infusion length: Cl2, then V2, Cl3, V3; transitions come earlier for larger decreases (PDF p8, printed p840).
- Set III (PDF p6, printed p838; Fig. 5, fentanyl 50 % decrement time): for many durations no single-parameter change
  achieves a 30 % shortening. Smaller V1 and larger Cl1 always helped; larger V2 helped below 25 min, larger V3 below
  270 min, larger Cl2 below 2 min (smaller Cl2 at 11-70 min); larger Cl3 for short and smaller Cl3 for long infusions.
- Tables 2-4 (PDF p7-8, printed pp839-840): the qualitative summary. Table 4 (parameter change giving a faster
  decrement): V1 decrease in all four conditions; Cl1 increase in all four; V2, V3, Cl2, Cl3 increase for short
  infusions and small decreases, decrease for long infusions and large decreases.
- Discussion examples (PDF p9, printed p841): alfentanil washes out faster than fentanyl after long infusions because
  five of six parameters (V1, V2, V3, Cl2, Cl3 all smaller) favour it, only Cl1 being less favourable; sufentanil beats
  fentanyl after medium infusions through larger V3, larger Cl1 and smaller Cl3, making it a rational choice for cases
  of about 3 h. Remifentanil's early data are called promising because it is rapidly cleared (larger Cl1).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No pharmacodynamic model, no effect site: all results are plasma decrement times.
- Opioid audit:
  - MEAC / minimum analgesic concentration: **none**.
  - ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: **none** estimated or used; alfentanil's rapid plasma-effect-site
    equilibration is mentioned qualitatively, citing Shafer and Varvel 1991 (PDF p9, printed p841).
  - Potency / equianalgesic ratio: **none**.

## Covariates
None; fixed typical-adult parameter sets.

## Caveats
- One parameter changed at a time; the authors argue the conclusions still hold across the three drugs.
- Plasma only; the effect site ignored, which matters after boluses and very short infusions.
- Compartments are mathematical, not anatomical.
- A fast decline is assumed always desirable; the authors note that is not true for every clinical goal.
- Internal inconsistencies in the paper: the Results say that for alfentanil a larger Cl3 led to **higher**
  concentrations after 360 min (PDF p4, printed p836), the Discussion says **lower** after 360 min (PDF p7, printed
  p839). Table 3's footnote defines both arrows as "longer decrement time" (PDF p8, printed p840); the down arrow must
  mean shorter.
- The text says the other drugs' curves were "equally complex" and not shown; only fentanyl is plotted for Sets II-III.
