# Claahsen-van der Grinten 2005: tramadol and M1 in labouring mothers and their neonates

## Study
- Open, single-group study, Nijmegen (two hospitals), sponsored by Grünenthal. 49 labouring women given IM tramadol
  hydrochloride; 27 excluded (birth outside 1.5–6.0 h after last dose n=13, caesarean n=4, other analgesics n=6,
  failed sampling n=4). 22 valid mother–neonate pairs (PDF p.3, printed 525): age 31±5 y, height 167±7 cm, weight at
  delivery 82±15 kg, BMI 29±5 kg/m² (mean±SD); neonates ≥37 weeks; 20 of 22 Caucasian.
- Dose: 100 mg IM, optionally a second 50 or 100 mg and a third dose, maximum 250 mg; valid cases received
  141±45 mg (range 100–250): 9 one dose, 10 a second 50 mg, 1 a second 100 mg, 2 three doses. First dose
  243±130 min and last dose 172±71 min before birth (PDF p.3).
- Sampling: maternal vein, umbilical vein and artery at birth; then one paired maternal and neonatal venous sample
  per pair at a randomly assigned 1, 2, 3, 6 or 12 h post partum (4–5 pairs per time).
- Assay: capillary GC with nitrogen-phosphorus detection, serum; tramadol hydrochloride and M1 (O-desmethyltramadol,
  non-stereoselective); CV < 6%, accuracy 100±5%, detection limits 2 ng/mL (tramadol), 1 ng/mL (M1) (PDF p.2).
  Total concentrations; whether reported as salt or base is not stated.
- Analysis: pooled ("naive-pooled" population) data, WinNonlin 3.1, two-compartment model; placental transfer as
  umbilical vein/maternal vein ratio.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No MEAC, minimum effective or analgesic concentration measured or stated; no pain scores reported.
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis.
- No equianalgesic or potency ratio.
- Neonatal end points: NACS 37.8±1.5 (all ≥35, normal) at 69±27 min post partum; 1-min Apgar 8.0±1.7, 5-min
  9.1±1.2 (n=47); no significant relation between UV tramadol (R=0.193, P=0.402) or M1 (R=0.019, P=0.933) and 1-min
  Apgar, after excluding neonate 11 (PDF p.4, Fig. 3). Neonate 11 (250 mg group; UV tramadol 557.4 ng/mL, M1 111.9
  ng/mL, mother 519.1/97.6 ng/mL) had respiratory depression reversed by naloxone, the one AE judged probably
  drug-related (PDF p.5).

None modelled.

## Caveats
- One post-partum sample per pair, pooled across subjects (naive pooling), sampling only to 12 h after IM doses given
  1.5–6 h before birth; the neonatal M1 half-life (85 h) is acknowledged as not estimable precisely.
- Post-partum maternal half-lives describe a woman just delivered, not pregnancy itself; no non-pregnant comparator.
  The authors call maternal tramadol (about 7 h) and M1 (5.5 h) half-lives compatible with literature values in adults
  (refs 4, 5, 7, 11), not a measured comparison. "15-times longer" M1 half-life is neonate vs their own mothers.
- Concentrations measured as "tramadol hydrochloride"; salt vs base basis of the ng/mL values not stated.
- Industry-sponsored; 27 of 49 enrolled excluded.
