# Hogan 2020: diazepam nasal spray vs rectal gel vs oral tablet

## Study
- Phase 1, open-label, randomised, single-dose, three-period, six-sequence crossover; ≥ 28 days between periods. Sponsor Neurelis.
- 48 enrolled, 46 dosed, 44 completed; 18-55 y; 25 men, 23 women (enrolled); 79% Black.
- Weight bands: 51-75 kg ("low") received nasal spray (NRL-1, Valtoco) 15 mg and rectal gel (Diastat) 15 mg; 76-111 kg ("high") received 20 mg of each. All received diazepam tablets (Valium) 10 mg.
- Fasted ≥ 10 h before and 4 h after dosing; Fleet enemas before rectal gel.
- Samples 10 min to 240 h. LC-MS/MS, LLQ 1.0 ng/mL diazepam, 0.10 ng/mL nordiazepam. Noncompartmental (WinNonlin).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Sedation score (0-5) and somnolence AEs only (somnolence 56.5% nasal, 89.1% rectal, 82.6% oral). No concentration-effect analysis. Discussion notes no fixed diazepam plasma level associated with CNS depression.

## Covariates
Weight band only (dose-stratified).

## Caveats
- Not an IV-referenced study; oral used as comparator. Fasted, ideal conditions.
- Mostly Black volunteers; healthy adults.
