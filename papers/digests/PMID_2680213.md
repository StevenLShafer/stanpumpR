# Chiou 1989 (Part I): drug concentration depends on the blood sampling site

## Study
Review (University of Illinois at Chicago) of arteriovenous concentration differences across many drugs and species,
and their consequences for clearance, volumes and PK/PD. Nitroglycerin (glyceryl trinitrate, GTN) is one example;
its data are all quoted from other papers.

For Armstrong 1982's infusion Chiou says only that a degree of pulmonary metabolism "similar" to Hill's 16.4% was
reported; it does not quote the pulmonary arterial concentration (29.8) or the 17.4% lung extraction. Chiou attributes
GTN's steady-state arteriovenous difference to metabolism in the sampling tissue (rat vessels metabolise GTN, Fung 1983)
(PDF p15).
