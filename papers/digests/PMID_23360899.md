# Vereecke 2013: nitrous oxide, sevoflurane and fentanyl response surface

## Study
Reanalysis of the raw data of Katoh 1999 (Hamamatsu, Japan): ASA I adults aged 20-50, elective abdominal, extremity or body-surface surgery, randomised to 66 vol.% N2O (n=86) or none (n=96). After exclusions (2 hypotension and ephedrine, 3 awake before incision) 177 patients analysed: 86 with N2O, 91 without (p897). Fentanyl TCI (Shafer 1990 PK) at 0-8 ng/mL targets (measured plasma before incision 0-10 ng/mL), end-tidal sevoflurane 0-4.5 vol.% targeted (measured 0-4), 20 min equilibration, then abdominal incision. Responses within 60 s: movement, or hemodynamic (heart rate or systolic pressure up more than 15% over baseline). Fentanyl venous samples 5 min before and after incision; excluded if differing by more than 35%. Analysis in NONMEM VI (FOCE Laplace likelihood), bootstrap 500 sets of 177, log-likelihood profiles.

## Model and parameters
Hierarchical response surface (equations 1-6): P(tolerance) = U^g/(1+U^g); U = U_SEVO*(1 + U_O^gO); U_O = (C_O + A)/(C50_O*B); U_SEVO = (C_SEVO + X)/(C50_SEVO*Y). X is vol.% sevoflurane equivalent of 66% N2O, A the ng/mL fentanyl equivalent, B and Y multiplicative potentiation of the C50s. X, Y, A identical for movement and hemodynamic response. Final choice: sevoflurane/opioid-additivity model XA (X and A).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Fentanyl "MEAC" is not reported. The fentanyl potency measure is C50_O: the fentanyl concentration that halves the sevoflurane C50 (equations 3-4): 2.07 ng/mL for movement (control and XA) and 0.43-0.47 ng/mL for hemodynamic response (Table 1). End point: response to surgical incision (movement or hemodynamic). Population: Japanese ASA I adults under sevoflurane; concentrations measured in venous plasma (assay type, free vs total, not stated in this paper; original in Katoh 1999).
- No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak: a 20-min delay was imposed so observations are at pseudo steady state, and venous plasma fentanyl was checked within 35% before and after incision.
- Potency equivalence: 66 vol.% N2O = 0.54 vol.% sevoflurane plus 0.27 ng/mL fentanyl (XA); MAC reduction by N2O alone 46% (movement) and 49% (hemodynamic) (Table 2). The paper itself reports the original 54% and 57% N2O MAC reduction in text (p901), and the Katoh C50 of fentanyl as 1.8 ng/mL there against 2.07 here.
- Other literature quoted: isoflurane MAC reduced 68% by 70% N2O (Stevens), sevoflurane 50% by 65% (Fragen), desflurane 50% by 60% (Rampil) (p901).
- No opioid equianalgesic ratio, only a remark that other opioids may fit if equipotent doses are used.

## Covariates
Ethnicity (all Asian) noted as a limit; no covariate modelled except the N2O factor itself. Interindividual variability tested, not retained (not significant).

## Caveats
Single N2O concentration (66 vol.%), single population (Asian), historical data, no prospective validation, applies only to noxious-stimulus responses (not hypnotic end points, text p901-902); estimate of A is uncertain (SE 43%); XY fits as well (OFV difference not significant) but is rejected on pharmacological grounds; sevoflurane-alone fit gamma 6.4-7.1 means a very steep concentration-response. At 6 ng/mL fentanyl with N2O the model gives MAC 0 (opioid alone suffices in more than 50%).
