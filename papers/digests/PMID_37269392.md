# Wanar 2023: Accelerated fentanyl metabolism in pregnancy (norfentanyl/fentanyl ratio)

## Study
Retrospective chart review (described as case-control) at one urban safety-net clinic (Boston), March 2018 to December 2020, of 420 records. Included: pregnant, normal AST/ALT/creatinine within a month of the screen, no fentanyl prescription, urine GC/MS positive for both fentanyl and norfentanyl (after a qualitative immunoassay with 1 ng/mL cutoff; GC/MS reference range 0.5 ng/mL; Quest). 112 ratios from 76 patients (each positive screen used separately). Cohort (Table 1, PDF p3): age 30.69 y (SD 4.67), gestational age 161.28 d (SD 76.01), BMI 29.81, AST 37.25, ALT 35.30, creatinine 0.69 mg/dL; 95% on methadone or buprenorphine, 55% positive for another substance. Comparator: published non-pregnant reference-lab ratios (n = 4366, ARUP, McCarthy 2020), subject counts unknown. Welch t-test.
Matrix is urine, not plasma; fentanyl came from illicit use at unknown dose and time.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Metabolic ratio (MR) here is norfentanyl/fentanyl (reversed from the usual parent/metabolite) (PDF p3-4).

Welch t(112) = 4.55, p = .0001, d = 0.86. Ratio range 0.01-460 (pregnant), 0.01-232 (non-pregnant).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not a PD study. No MEAC, ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, hysteresis or potency ratio reported. Norfentanyl is described as clinically inactive. Audit: none.

## Covariates
Pregnancy vs non-pregnant only. The authors say a separate analysis (Wanar 2022) found maternal age and gestational age the only physiologic predictors; no regression is reported in this paper.

## Caveats
Urine spot samples; unknown dose, route and timing; the MR depends on time since use and on norfentanyl's slower clearance (the authors note prolonged norfentanyl clearance in pregnancy from prior work), so a higher ratio cannot by itself prove faster parent clearance. Non-pregnant comparator is a different population, laboratory and method set, with undefined n. Repeated screens per patient are treated as independent. 95% on methadone/buprenorphine. SD exceeds the mean in both groups, so a t-test on means is weak. No parent-drug clearance, AUC or half-life is reported.
