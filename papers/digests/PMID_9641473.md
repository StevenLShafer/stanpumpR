# Ritchie 1998: myocardial milrinone uptake and the timing of its effects

## Study
- 12 men having diagnostic catheterisation for chest pain, Queen Elizabeth Hospital, Adelaide (PDF p. 2). Excluded: severe or unstable angina, left main stenosis, conduction abnormalities, significant valve disease, recent transmural MI, LVEF < 30%, significant renal or hepatic disease. Beta-blockers and calcium antagonists stopped >= 5 half-lives before.
- Table 1 (rendered, mean +/- SEM): age 58 +/- 3 y (43-72), CI 2.8 +/- 0.2 L/min/m2, HR 69 +/- 3, LVEF 65 +/- 2%, PCWP 8 +/- 1 mmHg; fixed CAD in 8 of 12. Weight not reported, so the dose per kg is unknown.
- 1 mg milrinone IV bolus into a running line. Whole blood from femoral artery and coronary sinus every 30 s for 3 min, then 4, 5, 7.5, 10 and 15 min; myocardial uptake by mass balance with thermodilution coronary sinus flow. Constant atrial pacing (cycle 775 +/- 31 ms) so dP/dt is at fixed HR; spontaneous HR, PR and QT recorded during ~5-s pacing pauses.
- Measurement times: LVP, LV dP/dt max, MAP and electrophysiology at 2, 5, 10 and 15 min; CO and CSBF at 3, 8 and 12 min (PDF p. 3).
- New whole-blood HPLC assay (C18, UV 326 nm, amrinone internal standard): LOD 0.6 ng/mL, linear 1-1,000 ng/mL, interassay CV <= 3.5%, intraassay <= 7%; recovery 82% (PDF p. 6).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model is fitted; the myocardial content is a mass-balance calculation, not a compartment.

```yaml
parameters: []  # no PK model; myocardial-uptake and effect-timing observations only (Tables 2-3, Results)
```

Myocardial milrinone content (MMC, relative to resting CSBF; PDF p. 14-15): coronary sinus concentration exceeded femoral arterial within 1 min (net efflux); peak MMC at 0.56 +/- 0.06 min (0.36-0.75), 182 +/- 32 ng/mL/min (46-414), equal to 1.89 +/- 0.30% (0.34-3.60%) of the dose; residual at 12.5 min 123 +/- 22 ng/mL/min, 69.1 +/- 5.7% of peak. Not related to CAD extent, LVEF or resting CI. Coronary sinus plasma cAMP did not change.

- Haemodynamic effects were significant from 2 min, electrophysiological ones from >= 5 min; the Discussion places the maxima at 7-10 min after injection. The mean LV dP/dt curve (Fig. 2A, rendered) is highest at the 5-min point (about 112.5% of baseline) and declines slightly at 10 and 15 min; MAP (Fig. 2B) reaches its plateau at 5 min. The mean of individual maxima (7.3 min) is therefore later than the peak of the mean curve.
- Hysteresis: peak effects lagged peak myocardial content (0.56 min) markedly; effect/MMC ratios rose over the first 6 min (Fig. 5). Peak MMC did not correlate significantly with maximal HR rise (P = 0.07) or dP/dt rise (P = 0.17).
- No ke0, EC50 or concentration-response fitted; CI did not rise significantly in these patients with normal LV function, unlike heart-failure studies.

## Caveats
- Mostly normal LV function; whole blood, not plasma; global (not regional) myocardial content; fixed paced rate; 15-min window; no weight.
- Coarse effect grid (2, 5, 10, 15 min; CO at 3, 8, 12) limits the resolution of the times of maximal effect.
- The PDF is a web printout; table images are low-resolution but legible.
