# Fuenmayor 1992: oral verapamil SR vs IR in Hispanic hypertensive patients

## Study
- Randomised, open-label, two-way crossover (6-day outpatient + 2-day inpatient period per arm, 2-week washout),
  Perez Carreno Hospital, Caracas (PDF p2).
- 30 Hispanic patients with untreated mild-to-moderate essential hypertension; 18 on 240 mg/day (age 45 ± 7 y,
  weight 68.8 ± 12 kg) and 12 on 480 mg/day (age 45.8 ± 7 y, weight 72 ± 15 kg) (p3). All 30 received SR, 24 also IR.
  Sex not given.
- Oral only. IR (Isoptin) 80 or 160 mg every 8 h (0800, 1600, 2400); SR (Isoptin-SR) 240 mg once daily (0800) or
  240 mg every 12 h (0800, 2000). Day 7 dose after a standardised meal; day 8 after ≥8 h fast (p2).
- Steady-state sampling over 24 h on inpatient days; BP by sphygmomanometry (sitting), PR from lead II.
- Assay: HPLC with fluorometric detection (modified Harapat & Kates 1980), verapamil and norverapamil, plasma (p3).
- Analysis: ANOVA of non-compartmental quantities (AUC, Cmax, Cmin, tmax). No compartmental model.

## Model and parameters
No PK model; non-compartmental steady-state oral values only. No intravenous arm, no clearance or volume.

Other values (text layer, PDF p6): trough Cmin 98 ± 12 µg/L (IR 80 mg tid) and 81 ± 12 µg/L (SR 240 mg od); SR
240 mg bid Cmin 247 ± 13 µg/L daytime vs 321 ± 16 µg/L morning predose (p < 0.01). Peak:trough ratio 1.3 (SR bid),
2.3 (SR od), 2.3 (IR 80 mg tid average) (Fig. 4). Doubling the dose raised AUC 2.2-fold (IR) and 2.4-fold (SR).
IR relative bioavailability 10 to 15% greater than SR. Verapamil:norverapamil AUC0-24 ratio (Table III, PDF p8,
text layer): 1.00/1.09 (IR 240 fed/fasted), 1.02/1.07 (SR 240), 1.28/1.29 (IR 480), 1.24/1.26 (SR 480), 480 vs 240
p < 0.05. Plasma half-life 2.5 to 7.5 h is quoted from the literature (p2), not measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid paper. Table I (PDF p4, rendered), mean ± SE presumably (not stated): time of maximum change after
dose (tmax of effect), SBP/DBP/PR: IR 80 mg tid 4 ± 1 / 4 ± 0.4 / 3 ± 0.5 h; SR 240 mg od 7 ± 1 / 7 ± 1 / 7 ± 0.4 h;
IR 160 mg tid 3.4 ± 0.5 / 3.2 ± 0.5 / 2.6 ± 1.3 h; SR 240 mg bid 6.7 ± 0.5 / 5.6 ± 0.6 / 5.2 ± 0.5 h. Maximum PR
change 51, 45, 45, 46 ms respectively; PR effect not dose dependent (Fig. 2). Text: peak PR 2.5 to 2.8 h after IR,
5 to 7 h after SR (p4). These are oral absorption-dominated times, not effect-site equilibration. No ke0, EC50 or
concentration-effect model. The discussion cites Abernethy 1986 for a PR plateau at 60 to 100 µg/L (p9; not this
study's data). One 46 kg patient had Wenckebach block at 233 µg/L. No potency ratio; authors conclude SR may replace
IR mg for mg.

## Covariates
None modelled. Dose (240 vs 480 mg/day) and food (fed vs fasted) compared by ANOVA.

## Caveats
Oral steady-state only; small n; table values lack dispersion (Table II); Table I dispersion type not stated in the
table (summary data elsewhere unspecified); funded by Knoll; Hispanic population only; no IV dosing.
