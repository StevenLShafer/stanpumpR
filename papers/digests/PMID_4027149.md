# Graham 1985: metabolism and pharmacokinetics of nicardipine in man

## Study
A set of studies in healthy male volunteers (Syntex, Edinburgh/Palo Alto). (1) Metabolism: 30 mg 14C-nicardipine
oral solution in 4 men, collections to 168 h; 60% of excreted 14C in urine; no unchanged drug in urine; major urinary
products glucuronides of alcohol metabolites (p3). (2) IV infusion 5 mg/h for 3 h (rate chosen from a bolus pilot
to give about 100 ng/mL at steady state) in 5 subjects (Table 1), fitted to a two-compartment infusion model with
ELSFIT (p4). (3) Oral 20 mg tid for 28 days (8 volunteers); (4) 10-40 mg every 8 h with an IV 14C tracer (0.89 mg)
in 6 men for bioavailability; (5) food effect. Assays: GC after oxidation to the pyridine metabolite M5, and HPLC
for nicardipine and M5 (p2); which assay was used for the IV study is not stated. Body weights are not reported.

Derived: CL 8.23 mL/min/kg = 0.494 L/h/kg. ln2 / mean beta = 3.47 h, shorter than the mean of individual half-lives
(4:45), as expected for a mean of reciprocals. At an assumed 70 kg (weights not given): CL 34.6 L/h, k10 1.68 /h,
k21 0.41 /h, V2 about 77.6 L, Vss about 98 L (1.40 L/kg); steady state at 5 mg/h about 145 ng/mL.
Oral: bioavailability dose-dependent (non-linear) over 10-40 mg tid; food before or with the dose reduces it; AUC
rises over the first 3 days of tid dosing, then declines (p4-p6). No dose dependency of elimination found (p6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n = 5 for the IV parameters; weights unreported, so per-kg clearance and absolute Vc cannot be combined exactly;
subject 3 clearance (13.0) is an outlier; the terminal half-life varies 1:46-9:45 and the authors suggest the
assay was not sensitive enough to see late phases after low or short dosing (p6). Supplement article, sponsor
authors.
