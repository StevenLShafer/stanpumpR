# Rook 2006: population PK of IV and inhaled ("chasing the dragon") heroin and metabolites

## Study
- 106 chronic heroin-dependent patients in the Dutch heroin-assisted treatment trial, all also on methadone
  (median 70 mg/day). 74 inhalers ("chasing the dragon": caffeine:heroin base 1:3 heated on aluminium foil),
  32 IV auto-injectors (heroin HCl). No crossover between routes.
- Study 1 (rich sampling, n = 20, men only, clinical research ward, controlled drug/alcohol use): samples 0, 2,
  5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60, 115, 180, 240, 480 min on days 2-5; morning doses varied -33% / +50%; inhalation split
  40%/60%, max 30 min. Study 2 (sparse, outpatients, n = 86 of 100 after excluding 14 with acetylcodeine/codeine):
  baseline and within 30 min of dosing on 1-3 occasions.
- Table I (median, range): age 41 (28-54) y; 84% male; weight 71 (39-132) kg; height 178 (156-202) cm; CrCl
  (Cockcroft-Gault, capped 150) 116 (60-150) mL/min; IV dose 300 (105-450) mg; inhaled dose 300 (100-450) mg;
  cocaine users 50.9%; benzodiazepine users 22.6%.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS of heroin, 6-AM, morphine, M3G, M6G in fluoride/oxalate plasma; LLOQ 5 ng/mL for all.
- NONMEM V, ADVAN5, FO, log-transformed data, additive error on log scale.

## Model and parameters
Structure (Fig. 4): heroin 2-compartment -> 6-AM 2-compartment -> morphine 2-compartment, with conversion at
equal rates from central and peripheral compartments (k13 = k24; k35 = k46); heroin and 6-AM central volumes set
equal; back-distribution rates fixed at 5-10% of the forward rate (not estimated); heroin assumed fully converted
to 6-AM; no renal excretion of 6-AM. M3G and M6G as 1-compartment models fitted on POSTHOC Bayesian estimates;
morphine fate fixed from literature: 10% renal, 75% to M3G, 15% to M6G. IV dosing as a 2-min infusion (study 2);
inhalation as zero-order input of estimated duration.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD measured. No MEAC or analgesic concentration, no ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis, no potency ratio.
- Discussion only: heroin is a prodrug with little receptor affinity; 6-AM is credited with the immediate
  euphoria; morphine and M6G with longer-lasting effects; M3G inactive at opioid receptors.

## Covariates
Only significant covariate: cocaine-positive patients had a 13% shorter 6-AM terminal half-life (ΔOFV 4.6,
P < 0.05). The abstract says "lower"; the discussion (p.415) says "prolonged by 13%" — internally inconsistent.
Sex, weight, BMI, alcohol, benzodiazepines, CrCl (> 60 mL/min) not significant.

## Caveats
- Heroin back-distribution and 6-AM elimination rates fixed, not estimated; morphine fate fractions fixed from
  literature; no mass balance.
- Morphine V1 of 1.6 L is far below the 12-20 L of IV-morphine models (authors attribute to formation kinetics).
- Heroin t½ 7.6 min longer than literature 2-4 min (authors: earlier assays missed a second phase).
- Study 2 sparse, injection duration assumed 2 min; inhalation input is zero-order of estimated duration.
- All patients on methadone, many on cocaine; tolerant chronic users; inhaled F may be underestimated
  (biliary excretion of metabolites).
- Concentrations reported in µmol/L in figures.
