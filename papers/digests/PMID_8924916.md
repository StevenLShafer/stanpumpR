# Odani 1996: phenytoin population PK, Michaelis-Menten (DDCL) model, Japanese epilepsy patients

## Study
- Retrospective routine TDM data, Kyoto University Hospital, Nov 1989 to Sep 1994 (p. 445).
- 116 patients (112 outpatients, 43 female), age 15.6 +/- 6.6 y (range 1-37), weight 42.4 +/- 16.5 kg (Table 1, mean +/- SD). No severe hepatic/renal failure.
- Oral phenytoin, conventional tablet (Aleviatin, Dainippon) and/or a preformulated ground tablet, dosed > 4 weeks (steady state). Mean daily dose 200 +/- 64 mg; 12-h interval in 87 patients; 59 patients on a single dose level, 33 on > 3 levels.
- Co-medication: phenytoin alone 21; zonisamide 39, carbamazepine 43, valproate 50, phenobarbital 15, others 22 (Table 1).
- 531 serum concentrations: 263 near-peak samples (2.5-4 h post dose) and 54 troughs (8-24 h); the discussion says most were taken 2-6 h after dosing (pp. 445-446).
- Assay: fluorescence polarization immunoassay (TDx, Dainabot) - **total serum phenytoin**.
- NONMEM IV level 2.1, FOCE with eta-epsilon interaction. Structural model: one compartment with dose-dependent clearance (DDCL), CL_ij = (Vmax_i - D/tau)/Km_i (Eq. 1); rapid absorption, F = 100% assumed (Eq. 2); no absorption phase identifiable.

## Model and parameters
Size model (Eqs. 3-5): SIZE_i = 42 x (WT_i/42)^thetaWT; Vmax_i = Vmax x SIZE_i x (1 + eta); V_i = V x SIZE_i x (1 + eta). Km_i = Km x thetaZNS^ZNS x (1 + eta) (Eq. 6). Vmax in mg/day/kg and V in L/kg are values for a typical 42 kg patient. A separate weight exponent for V vs Vmax did not improve the fit (l.l.d. about 0, p. 447).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No therapeutic range, effect site or concentration-effect data. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
- Weight via SIZE (above), the same exponent for Vmax and V; explains the lower concentrations per mg/kg in children (< 15 y) (Figs. 2-3).
- Zonisamide: Km x 1.16 (p < 0.01, l.l.d. 15.8); authors translate this to apparent clearance about 14% lower and concentration 16% higher.
- Carbamazepine, valproate, phenobarbital: no significant effect detected.

## Caveats
- Japanese patients, mostly children and young adults (1-37 y, mean 42 kg); the 70 kg adult is an extrapolation.
- V is poorly determined (95% CI 0.32-2.15 L/kg) because few troughs and little half-life information (p. 446).
- F fixed at 1; absorption not estimable; total (not unbound) concentration.
- Formulation given as phenytoin tablets (Aleviatin); the paper does not state salt form.
