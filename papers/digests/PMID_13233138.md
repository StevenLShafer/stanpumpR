# Radford 1955: ventilation standards for artificial respiration

## Study
A derivation, not a trial: a nomogram of the tidal volume (and so minute ventilation) needed to hold alveolar PCO2 at 40 mmHg, from body weight, sex and ventilatory frequency, for patients in prolonged artificial respiration (poliomyelitis, anaesthesia). Basal CO2 production comes from Benedict and Talbot / Harris and Benedict metabolism data (Fig. 1, PDF p. 2); respiratory dead space comes from a literature-and-own-data plot of mean dead space against mean body weight for several groups (Fig. 2, PDF p. 4: 15 newborn infants, 10 normal adult males, 6 female and 7 male poliomyelitis patients, 4 normal adult females, 45 normal adult males (Fowler), 10 normal adult males, 11 "old men", 5 normal adults, 10 normal adult males (Carpenter), 8 normal boys (Robinson)). Validation (Fig. 5, PDF p. 7): 13 poliomyelitis patients (age 8-37), 13 normal adult males (22-36) and 7 infants.

## Model and parameters
Equations (PDF p. 1-4, read on rendered pages 2 and 4):
- Eq. 3: standard alveolar ventilation = 20 x VCO2 (STPD), PACO2 40 mmHg, gas at 24 C saturated.
- Eq. 5: standard VT = VD + 20 x VCO2 / f (f breaths/min).
- Empirical: VCO2 = theta(W) read from Fig. 1 (separate curves, male and female); respiratory dead space in mL (BTPS) = W, body weight in lb (the 8% correction to 24 C saturated neglected; "not justifiable above 200 lb").
- Eq. 6: standard VT = W + 20 theta(W) / f, drawn as a nomogram (Fig. 3, PDF p. 6; its values were not read).
- Corrections (text, p. 5-6 and Fig. 3 legend): daytime activity, add 10%; fever, add 5% per F above 99 F rectal; altitude, add 5% per 2000 ft; metabolic acidosis under anaesthesia, add 20%; tracheotomy or tracheal intubation, subtract a volume of half the body weight in lb (the oro-nasal and upper-tracheal share, about 50% of anatomical dead space); apparatus or mask dead space, add its volume.
- Prediction error against measured ideal VT: mean deviation +5%, CV 10% (Fig. 5); estimated CV of dead space about 15% for normals, of basal metabolism about 6%, of the overall adult prediction about 7%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None; not an opioid paper (opioid audit: none). The end point is alveolar PCO2 of 40 mmHg; the authors expect 68% of adults ventilated by the standard to fall at PACO2 34-41 and 95% at 31-46 mmHg (p. 458, 460).

## Covariates
Body weight (lb) and sex (basal VCO2); frequency; the corrections above. Age is deliberately neglected. Height is not used.

## Caveats
The dead space fit is rough (the author says so); newborn and boys' points are described as unreliable; the nomogram assumes basal metabolism, normal dead space (no emphysema), no muscle activity, no severe acid-base disturbance. The dead-space figure is a pooled physiological (Bohr, CO2) value in groups in various postures; the author's population is not an anaesthetised one, which is why intubation is handled by subtracting half. It is a clinical teaching aid, not a PK or PD study.
