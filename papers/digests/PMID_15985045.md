# Arafat 2005: enalapril and enalaprilat by specific enzyme immunoassays

## Study
- Two studies in healthy Jordanian **male** volunteers (Methods, p. 320 / PDF p. 2):
  - 10 mg study: n = 24, aged 18-43 y (mean 28.3 ± 6.1), weight 60-90 kg (76.7 ± 9.0). Two-way crossover of 10 mg enalapril maleate tablets, Enalapril® (test) against Renitec® (reference), with a 1-week washout.
  - 20 mg study: a separate 24 men, aged 19-43 y (30.0 ± 7.4), weight 76.1 ± 8.9 kg, given 20 mg Renitec®.
- Dosing and sampling: single oral dose after a 10-h fast, with 240 mL water. 17 blood samples from 0 to 24 h; the last two were at 12 and 24 h. Heparinized **plasma**.
- Assays: competitive enzyme immunoassays specific for each moiety.
  - Enalapril: rabbit anti-enalapril antiserum, validated over 1-1000 ng/mL.
  - Enalaprilat: anti-lisinopril antiserum, which also detects lisinopril and does not cross-react with enalapril. Validated over 1-500 ng/mL.
  - Intra-assay CV was under 10% for both.
- Analysis: non-compartmental (AUC by the linear trapezoidal rule, extrapolated with the terminal slope).
- Pharmacodynamics: linear regression of the % change in BP against plasma enalaprilat.
- No population model.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: % fall in systolic and diastolic BP in normotensive men.
  - Maximum fall after 10 mg: systolic 6-7.7% and 7.6-8.4%, diastolic 11.3-12.5% and 12.3-13.6%, both at 4-6 h. This coincides with enalaprilat Tmax.
  - After 20 mg: systolic 11-12%, diastolic 17.2-18.5%.
- Linear model %DBP = %DBP_predose + S·C, fitted to mean BP against mean enalaprilat at each time (Fig. 4, p. 326):
  - Systolic: y = −0.2218x − 0.8248, R² = 0.8193.
  - Diastolic: y = −0.3157x − 2.6501, R² = 0.7977.
  - In both, x is ng/mL and y is %.
- Enalaprilat correlated with the fall in BP (pooled 10 + 20 mg: r = 0.946 systolic, 0.954 diastolic). Enalapril did not.
- Not reported: ke0, effect compartment, hysteresis, EC50, a therapeutic or minimum effective concentration, and any potency ratio.

## Covariates
None analysed. Healthy young men only.

## Caveats
- Sampling stopped at 24 h, so the terminal half-lives (3.5-4.5 h for enalaprilat) describe the early elimination phase. Enalaprilat's long terminal phase cannot be seen in this design, and AUC0-∞ extrapolated from it is an underestimate of the full area (reviewer's observation, not the authors').
- Enalapril Cmax is about 10× the ACE-inhibition-assay literature, by the authors' own account. This EIA reads the parent far higher than other methods.
- Cmax is the mean of individual maxima. The distributions are skewed: Table 2 rows run from 23.5 to 144 ng/mL.
- Interpretation in the text:
  - The authors say their enalaprilat at 12-24 h was lower than the literature measured by ACE-inhibition assay but similar to LC/MS/MS (p. 327).
  - They attribute part of the difference to faster clearance in young healthy subjects.
- Typos: the Methods spell the product "Renetic" once and "Lisinorpil" once.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Transcription matches.**
  - Enalaprilat Cmax 54.8 ± 29.5 and 57.2 ± 29.0, AUC 266.9 and 255.9, and parent Cmax 313.5/310.1 were confirmed on page images. Table 2's means were recomputed from its rows (1373.4/24 = 57.2; 1315.7/24 = 54.8).
  - "Within 3% of each" is right: 55.9/54.8 = 1.020 and 55.9/57.2 = 0.977.
  - Brand 1 = Enalapril® = 266.9, as the test says. Both studies used 10 mg enalapril **maleate** tablets, as the repo says.
  - The quoted phrases are in the abstract: enalaprilat at 12-24 h "lower than those reported in the literature", and parent Cmax "about 10 times".
- **Dose basis: discrepancy (over-claim).**
  - The repo compares a typical-individual model peak with the arithmetic mean of individual Cmax. Neither the mean curve (Fig. 2, about 36-39 ng/mL) nor the median Cmax (48.2 and 54.25, derived) is the 54.8-57.2 the code treats as the measured range.
  - "Measured range" here is two brand means 2.4 ng/mL apart, not an uncertainty interval. The SEMs are about 6 ng/mL (derived).
  - The labelled-strength basis is consistent with Arafat. The alternative, dividing by 1.308 to give 42.7, is less consistent, but Arafat does not exclude it as decisively as "settles" claims (see open_questions).
  - The paper reports no bioavailability.
- **Terminal phase: discrepancy (framing).**
  - The repo says the AUC disagreement is Arafat's terminal phase departing from the published picture (help :43, docs :1904).
  - Arafat's "lower than the literature" was against the ACE-inhibition-assay literature, and they add that LC/MS/MS gave similar values. The comparator is not Faisal or the general literature.
  - Their 24-h sampling and about 4-h NCA half-life also structurally truncate an 18-h terminal phase. That is a design reason for the lower AUC that the repo does not mention.
  - "Recorded, not reconciled" is a reasonable stance. The attribution is not supported as written.
- **Tmax, not discussed in the repo.** Arafat's enalaprilat Tmax is 4.3-4.6 h; the help gives the model's as about 3.3 h. The model peaks earlier, and the repo is silent on this.
- **Weight.** Arafat's subjects averaged 76.7 kg against Faisal's 69.76. In the model only the parent's volume scales with weight and the rate constants are fixed, so the metabolite peak does not depend on weight. The comparison is unaffected.
- **Context location.** `docs/antihypertensives.md:1847` does not mention Arafat; the actual lines are 1881-1912.
