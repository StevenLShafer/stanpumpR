# Grond 1999: serum tramadol and M1 enantiomers before PCA demands

## Study
Short report from a randomised, double-blind trial (clinical results published in Grond 1995, *Pain* 62:313). It
enrolled 99 women after vaginal hysterectomy or gynaecological laparotomy under **opioid-free anaesthesia**; 98
received drug (one in the (−) group did not, because of respiratory depression). The arms were (+)-tramadol,
racemic (±)-tramadol or (−)-tramadol. Dosing was an IV loading bolus (50 mg, repeated up to a maximum of 200 mg)
until pain relief was acceptable, then PCA with 20 mg demands, a 5 min lockout, for 24 h. The paper does not say
whether doses are salt or base; its introduction names the drug as tramadol hydrochloride. PCA lasted 20, 21 and
12 h, and total consumption was 289, 316 and 356 mg in the (+), (±) and (−) groups. Responders (satisfied with
relief) were 27, 25 and 13 (p 2).

Sampling: 3 mL of venous blood was drawn from a separate cannula **immediately before each PCA demand**, up to
50 mL per patient. Samples were allowed to clot and spun for **serum**, stored at −20 °C. On average 6±3, 7±3 and
7±4 samples per patient. Patients with no demands or no pre-demand sample were excluded: one in the (+) group and
five in the (±) group (p 2).

Assays: tramadol enantiomers by chiral GC-MS (CP-cyclodextrin column, deuterated internal standard). M1
enantiomers by chiral GC with a nitrogen-selective detector after diazoethane derivatisation. LLOQ 5 ng/mL;
precision (CV) 7.1 to 11.4%. The assays measure **total** (bound + unbound) drug; free fraction was not measured.
Statistics: ANOVA with Scheffé, and paired and unpaired t tests.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model. The results are mean ± SD across patients of each patient's pre-demand serum
concentrations ("therapeutic serum concentrations"), from Tables 1 and 2 on PDF page 3 (printed 256), confirmed
on the rendered image.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **End point:** the concentration immediately before a PCA demand, when the patient was beginning to lose
  analgesia. Population: women after gynaecological surgery under opioid-free anaesthesia. Matrix: venous
  **serum**, **total** drug, enantioselective assay.
- **MEAC / MEC.** The racemate group's pre-demand means are total M1 **84 ± 34 ng/mL**, (+)-M1 33 ± 15 and
  (−)-M1 51 ± 21 (n=22), with total tramadol **590 ± 410 ng/mL** (n=28), from Tables 1 and 2. The authors
  **decline to call these minimum effective concentrations** (Discussion, p 3). Their reasons: the share of each
  of the four species in the analgesia is unknown, and inter- and intra-individual variability is large. They use
  the term "therapeutic concentrations" instead.
- **(+)-tramadol group:** tramadol 470 ± 323 and M1 57 ± 18 ng/mL, with only the (+) enantiomers present.
  **(−) group:** 771 ± 451 and 96 ± 41.
- **Potency:** the authors read the lower concentrations as showing that (+)-T is more potent than (−)-T. They
  state no numeric potency ratio.
- **For comparison:** the authors cite an earlier racemate PCA study (Lehmann 1990) with a mean of 298 ng/mL
  tramadol, in patients given fentanyl intraoperatively and consuming 258 mg. They also cite Hackl 1986, a median
  of 916 ng/mL with a background infusion.
- Not reported: ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, EC50, and free-fraction data.

## Covariates
None analysed. All patients were female, and age and weight are not given in this report.

## Caveats
- The values are means of samples taken at demand times, so they mix parent and metabolite effects. Whether the
  dose is salt or base, and the assay calibration's salt form, are not stated.
- Each patient contributed a different number of samples. The racemate M1 subset is small (n=22).
- The PCA lasted 24 h after major surgery, so the concentrations were not at steady state.
