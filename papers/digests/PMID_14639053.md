# Carmichael 2003: population PK of hydroxychloroquine in rheumatoid arthritis

## Study
- Pooled data from 4 earlier studies (refs 14, 16, 17 and the HCQ + methotrexate cohort), all coordinated from St Vincent's Hospital, Sydney. 123 adults with rheumatoid arthritis (35 male). Table 1 (PDF p3, printed p673), mean ± SD (range):
  - Bioavailability study: 2 M / 7 F, age 50.2 ± 16.1 (20–72) y, weight 72.6 ± 13.3 (56–89) kg, 590 samples, HCQ 264 ± 263 (1.1–2700) µg/L.
  - Single-agent HCQ: 23 M / 51 F (74 patients, including the 9 above), age 53.9 ± 14.8 (20–81) y, weight 69.2 ± 12.4 (44–89) kg (weight known for 33 only), 461 samples, 329 ± 326 (1.1–1745) µg/L.
  - HCQ + MTX: 12 M / 37 F, age 56.8 ± 12.6 (25–75) y, weight 71.0 ± 16.3 (58–81) kg (weight known for 48), 319 samples, 640 ± 479 (0–2000) µg/L.
- Drug: Plaquenil 200 mg HCQ sulfate = 155 mg HCQ base per tablet. **Doses are stated as base.** Bioavailability study (n = 9): randomized crossover, single oral 155 mg and 30-min IV infusion of 155 mg. Other 65 single-agent patients: 155 mg (n = 44) or 310 mg (n = 21) every 24 h orally; MTX patients: 155 mg (n = 25) or 310 mg (n = 24) every 24 h (p672).
- Sampling (p673): bioavailability study every 15 min for 8 h, plus 24 and 32 h. Single-agent multi-dose: 22 patients had 1–6 troughs (~24 h post dose) between day 22 and day 192; 43 had one trough at day 180. MTX group: one trough at each of 7 visits (2 weeks, then monthly).
- Assay: **whole-blood** HCQ by HPLC with fluorescence (ref 20). Concentrations in µg/L.
- Analysis: NONMEM 5.1, FOCE with interaction; exponential IIV; combined proportional + additive residual error; covariate significance at P = 0.01 (ΔOFV 6.6).

## Model and parameters
One-compartment, first-order absorption with lag time. A 2-compartment model lowered OFV (4388.55 → 4372.59) but AIC favoured 1 compartment (3876.29 vs 3878.61) (p675). F was estimated only from the 9-patient IV/oral data and fixed at 0.746 in the population models. Final model (d) puts MTX on V as an additive shift: Vi = (V + VMTX·MTX)·exp(ηV) (eq. 3, p674).

Derived checks: half-life from (b) = 0.693 × 612 / 9.8 = 43.3 h, the value the text gives (p679); from (d) 42.4 h. Average steady state from (d) = 0.746 × 155 000 µg / (9.89 L/h × 24 h) = 487 µg/L, matching the text's 487 µg/L for 155 mg/day and 974 µg/L for 310 mg/day (p679); this confirms the dose is base and Cl is systemic whole-blood clearance (not CL/F). The paper itself notes the 43 h half-life matches the middle phase of earlier triexponential models; HCQ's terminal half-life exceeds 40 days (p671).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, no effect-site parameters. The paper mentions earlier conflicting concentration–effect data (refs 16, 17, 22) and no established target range. It cites one recommendation (ref 23, children, corneal deposits) to keep serum below 370–470 µg/L, which it equates to about 740–940 µg/L whole blood (p679). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Methotrexate (indicator) on V only: + 1070 L (175% higher V with MTX, p679). Clearance unchanged by MTX. Weight, age and sex were not tested (weight missing for many patients).

## Caveats
- Sparse trough-only data for 114 of 123 patients; the authors attribute the 1-compartment fit and the lower V without MTX to the clustering of samples at the end of the dosing interval (abstract; p679). The "MTX effect" on V may be a sampling artefact: the MTX cohort had a different sampling design (p679).
- Fit (a) on rich 0–32 h data gave a very different Cl (39.3 L/h) and V (153 L) from the population fits; the 1-compartment model captures only an intermediate phase of a multi-exponential drug, so it cannot describe the weeks-long terminal phase.
- F (0.746) comes from 9 patients; ka/tlag in (c) were fixed from (b).
- IIV CVs stated in the text do not match the tabled ω² (see open question).
- Whole-blood concentrations; not interchangeable with plasma or serum (whole blood about 2× serum by the paper's own conversion).
- Table 2 row (d) is misprinted "All 23 patients" for 123.
