# Levy 1995: CYP isoforms and antiepileptic drug interactions

## Study
Narrative mechanistic review (single author, symposium supplement), no new clinical data. Argues that inhibition is isoform-specific and substrate-independent, and applies this to phenytoin (PHT) and carbamazepine (CBZ). Draws on in vitro work from the author's laboratory (human liver microsomes, chemical inhibitors, antibodies, cDNA-expressed enzymes).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model or parameters. Quantities stated:
- PHT para-hydroxylation to p-HPPH accounts for 70-90% of total clearance, catalysed mainly by CYP2C9, secondarily CYP2C19 (PDF p3, printed S10; Fig. 1).
- Sulfaphenazole IC50 0.4 µM for PHT 4-hydroxylation and 0.6 µM for tolbutamide methylhydroxylation; PHT and tolbutamide hydroxylation correlated r² 0.82 across 18 liver preparations (PDF p3, citing ref 6).
- CYP2C19 poor metabolisers about 5% of whites, 20% of Asians; CYP2D6 poor metabolisers 5-10% of whites, 1% of Asians (PDF p3).
- Of 17 drugs that raise PHT levels, 11 explained by CYP2C9 inhibition; 6 (felbamate, omeprazole, cimetidine, fluoxetine, imipramine, diazepam) attributed to CYP2C19 (PDF p3-4).
- Felbamate: among seven isoforms tested, only CYP2C19 was inhibited at therapeutic felbamate concentrations; CYP2C9 unaffected (PDF p4, printed S11). No IC50/Ki or concentration given.
- CBZ-10,11-epoxide formation: troleandomycin reduced it by 70% (confirmed on image), anti-CYP3A4 antibody maximum 60% inhibition, correlation with CYP3A4 content r² 0.57 (PDF p4).
- Major interactions linked to CYP2C9 inhibition, minor ones to CYP2C19 (PDF p4).

## Covariates
CYP2C19 poor-metaboliser status: the author predicts lower PHT dose needs and no felbamate interaction in such patients (PDF p5, printed S12); a prediction, not measured.

## Caveats
Review; the felbamate-CYP2C19 finding is from the author's own in vitro study and an abstract (ref 10), with no magnitude. Comparative claims ("major" vs "minor" interactions) are against each other, not quantified.
