# Hiemke 2018 (AGNP TDM consensus, update 2017): therapeutic reference ranges

## Study
Consensus guideline of the AGNP TDM task group: structured literature review giving, for 154 neuropsychiatric drugs,
therapeutic reference ranges, laboratory alert levels, half-lives, levels of recommendation for TDM (1 strongly
recommended ... 4 potentially useful), conversion factors and dose-related concentration (DRC) factors.

## Model and parameters
Definitions (p. 22, boxed "Definition"): the therapeutic reference range is a population-based, orienting range of
drug concentrations in blood with a lower limit below which response is relatively unlikely and an upper limit above
which tolerability falls or further improvement is unlikely. Ranges refer to **trough concentrations at steady state**
(constant dose for at least 4-6 half-lives; sampled immediately before the next dose) (p. 17). "Drug concentration in
blood" covers plasma or serum. Only 17 drugs' ranges rest on randomised trials; most come from studies at effective
doses. The laboratory alert level is generally twice the upper limit.

Table 4 comments (paraphrased):
- **Bupropion**: hydroxybupropion is the major active compound (about 50% of bupropion's activity; other metabolites
  at most 20%). Because bupropion is unstable, the range **refers to hydroxybupropion only**, despite the row label.
- Citalopram / escitalopram: N-desmethyl metabolites may contribute weakly.
- Duloxetine: no active metabolites; renal disease raises concentrations.
- Fluoxetine: long half-life, long-lasting CYP2D6 inhibition.
- Mirtazapine: N-desmethyl metabolite does not contribute.
- Sertraline: N-desmethylsertraline has twice the half-life but 1/20 the activity.
- Venlafaxine: ODV predominates; active moiety above 222 ng/mL predicted response; below 100 ng/mL acts mainly as an SSRI.
No "typical" value inside the range is given for any drug.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Ranges are clinical-outcome (response/tolerability) anchored trough concentrations, not effect-site quantities. No
ke0, EC50 or tPeak. No opioids in the rows read; no MEAC or potency ratios.

## Covariates
Table 5 gives CL/F, F, t½ and DRC factors for computing expected troughs (not used by stanpumpR; not transcribed).

## Caveats
- Population, orienting ranges; trough-based. Comparing a model's peak or average to them overstates agreement for
  short-half-life drugs.
- Level-3/4 ranges (fluoxetine, paroxetine, desvenlafaxine) have weak evidence.
- The pdftotext -layout file misplaces Table 4's first-page values (e.g. 850-1,500 appears beside amitriptyline and
  30-120 beside desvenlafaxine); the -raw extraction resolves them as tabulated above.
