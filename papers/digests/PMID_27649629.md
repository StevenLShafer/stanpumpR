# Liu 2017: Allometry is a reasonable choice in pediatric drug development

## Study
Retrospective label analysis (printed pp. 469-473). Drugs with pediatric label updates from 1998 to 2015 were taken from the FDA New Pediatric Labeling Information Database. Only small-molecule drugs given orally or IV were kept. Of 558 database records (134 drugs), 46 drugs had PK in both adults and children: 12 IV, 34 oral; 36 eliminated by the hepatic pathway and 10 by the renal pathway. This gave 91 pediatric predictions (34 in adolescents for 32 drugs, 57 in children for 37 drugs); the per-drug table is supplementary Table S1.

Adult weight was assumed to be 70 kg. Pediatric weight was the reported mean or median, else the midpoint of the reported range, else read from the CDC growth chart by age. Observed clearance came from reported CL, or weight-adjusted CL times weight, or dose/AUC, or BSA-adjusted CL times BSA (1.73 for adults; Boyd formula for children). In some cases it was imputed from dose ratios when the label said exposure was similar.

## Model and parameters
Predicted pediatric CL = adult CL x (WT_ped / WT_adult)^0.75. The Clark rule (linear per-kg) was predicted CL = adult CL x WT_ped / WT_adult. Metric: prediction-observation ratio = predicted CL / observed CL.

Other reported findings: no weight- or age-dependent trend in the ratio (Fig. 3, not seen); the Clark rule gave negligible difference above 40 kg and under-predicted clearance below 40 kg (p. 473). Volume of distribution was not evaluated, because reported volumes (often Vz) are ambiguous (p. 474).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Not an opioid paper. No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio. The authors note that if a concentration at a time point (Cmax, Cmin) must be matched, volume extrapolation also matters (p. 474).

## Covariates
Body weight only, exponent 0.75 on clearance. Applies to children aged 2 years and older; the authors argue enzyme maturation is near adult level by 2 years (p. 474). Neonates and infants need a maturation term (the authors cite morphine and clonidine examples, p. 470).

## Caveats
Weights are partly derived from CDC charts and adult weight is assumed at 70 kg, so some predicted-versus-observed differences may be artefacts (p. 475). Several observed clearances were imputed from dose or AUC assumptions. The 2-fold criterion is meant to guide pediatric PK study design, not to set doses (abstract, p. 470). Apparent CL (CL/F) for the oral drugs mixes clearance with bioavailability. The analysis uses label means only, not individual patients. The Acknowledgment thanks Steven Shafer for discussions.
