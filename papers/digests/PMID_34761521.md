# Stillemans 2022: atorvastatin PopPK, real-life ambulatory cohort

## Study
Sparse, opportunistic sampling in ambulatory patients (Cliniques Universitaires Saint-Luc, Brussels, 2017-2019): 70 patients, 132 PK samples (post-dose delay 2.2 to 40 h, median 14.6 h; last-dose time self-reported), oral atorvastatin 5 to 80 mg q24h (Table 1). Age median 53.8 y (IQR 21.6), 35 female, BMI median 26.0, 94% white. Two rich support datasets pooled for structural model development: Lemahieu 2005 (control phase only, healthy/transplant crossover) and Hermann 2006 (13 myopathy patients, 15 controls). Assay: LC-MS/MS of atorvastatin acid in plasma (total, not free). NONMEM 7.4.3; first-order absorption. Outcomes: CK, myalgia (6 of 70, 8.6%), % change in total and LDL cholesterol at the last visit.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment disposition (dOFV -167 vs one compartment), first-order absorption with ka fixed at 2.5 /h (sensitivity 0.5 to 4.5 had little effect), exponential residual error. CL had separate fixed and random effects for the investigation cohort and the support cohorts. All parameters are apparent (CL/F, Q/F, Vc/F, Vp/F), absolute (not per kg). Covariate form (Table 3 note): CL/F = thetaCL x (1 + thetaSLCO1B1), 0 for 521TT, in the investigation cohort. Residual and omega scale: the table labels omega as SD. F is not estimated (apparent parameters only). Population predictions were biased above 10 ng/mL (mostly Lemahieu data); individual predictions correlated well (Spearman 0.84 population, 0.96 individual, p. 672).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No effect-site model, ke0 or hysteresis. Exposure-response (individual Bayesian CL/F, investigation cohort): CL/F vs mean CK r = -0.26, p = 0.042 (CK falls 0.20 units per 1 L/h increase); myalgia OR 0.68 (95% CI 0.46 to 0.99) per 50 L/h increase, p = 0.031; ROC AUC 0.77, best cutoff 414.67 L/h (sensitivity 83.3%, specificity 73.0%), OR 13.43 (CI 2.47 to 124.31) for CL/F below the cutoff vs above; cholesterol: CL/F vs % decrease in total and LDL cholesterol r = -0.34 (p = 0.005) and -0.36 (p = 0.014), -0.44 and -0.49 adjusted for dose (p. 673). Higher CL/F associated with larger cholesterol reduction (the authors' interpretation: more hepatic extraction). No MEAC or therapeutic concentration range; lactone concentration 17.19 nM +/- 3.00 in low-CL/F patients vs 6.82 +/- 2.05 (p. 677; plasma, atorvastatin lactone). Not an opioid; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Tested on CL/F only: race, sex, dose, genotypes (CYP3A4*22, CYP3A5*3, ABCB1, ABCC1, SLCO1B1 388 and 521, SLCO1B3, SLCO2B1), DDIs (CYP3A, ABCB1, OATP inhibitors and inducers), age, BMI. Forward search: OATP2B1 inhibitor (dOFV -4.3), sex (-4), SLCO1B1 521T>C (-20.7); only SLCO1B1 521T>C kept after backward elimination. Carriers of 521C had lower CL/F; the lowering was larger in heterozygotes (-0.402) than CC (-0.041, very imprecise). CL/F IIV fell from 0.127 to 0.0677 with the covariate (p. 672). No weight, age or renal covariate.

## Caveats
Sparse data; structural model borrowed from rich datasets with different assays and populations; high shrinkage for Q, Vc, Vp (39 to 52%) so covariates tested on CL/F only; time-varying covariates summarised over the whole observation period; linearity across doses untested; metabolite (active 2-OH) and lactone PK not modelled; only 70 patients (5 CC, 16 TC), CC effect not estimable. Estimated mean CL/F 535 L/h is comparable with 652, 504 and 596 L/h (70 kg) in other PopPK studies the authors cite (p. 674).
