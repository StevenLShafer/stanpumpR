# Strathe 2026: semaglutide 1.7 mg in adolescents (MIDD)

## Study
Model-informed extrapolation of the semaglutide 1.7 mg maintenance dose (never tested in an adolescent RCT) from 2.4 mg data. Data: STEP TEENS (N = 201 adolescents, mean age 15.4 y, 125 female, weight 107.5 +/- 24.5 kg, BMI 37 +/- 6.4; randomised 2:1 to 2.4 mg or placebo) and adult STEP studies (STEP 1 N = 1961, age 46.5, 1453 female, 105.3 +/- 21.9 kg, BMI 37.9; STEP 2, STEP 6, phase 2; ~3100 adults in all). Population PK models from earlier adult work (Overgaard 2019, Diabetes Ther 10:649; Strathe 2023) were updated with STEP TEENS data; a joint adult-adolescent BMI/weight exposure-response model was built, then 1000 simulated studies of the 201 STEP TEENS subjects (parameter and sampling uncertainty). Nonmem 7.5.1, PsN 5.3.1, R 4.2. Simulations assume adherence (trial-product estimand). Assay not described in the PDF.

## Model and parameters
No parameter table in the PDF (all in Supporting Information Tables S2, S4, S5). Facts stated in the text:
- 1.7 mg exposure was obtained from 2.4 mg by dose proportionality, ratio 1.7/2.4 ~ 0.71 (Sect. 3.4); average exposure 74.0 nmol/L at 2.4 mg and 52.4 nmol/L at 1.7 mg (derived ratio 0.708).
- Cmax at about 24-72 h after a dose (weekly, steady state, Fig. 1D).
- Relative exposure vs the reference adult (Sect. 3.3, PDF p. 3): 0.71 [0.70; 0.72] at 147 kg (95th percentile), 1.28 [1.26; 1.30] at 76 kg (5th percentile); age 12 to <15 y 0.89 [0.85; 0.93] and 15 to <18 y 0.96 [0.91; 1.01] (90% CI), bioequivalent to adults. Sex, race, ethnicity and glycaemic status of no or minor relevance.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. No ke0, tPeak, MEAC or analgesic concentration. The end point is % change in BMI (and body weight) at week 68, with exposure-response on model-derived Cavg. Simulated mean BMI change in adolescents: 1.7 mg -15.2% (95% CI -11.4 to -18.8), 2.4 mg -17.4% (-13.4 to -21.4), placebo ~0 (-4.01 to 3.79). Proportions with >=5/>=10/>=15% BMI reduction: 83.7/71.1/56.2% (2.4 mg), 79.6/65.2/49.0% (1.7 mg), 27.2/11.3/3.64% (placebo). Simulated weight loss 17.5% (adolescents) and 16.3% (adults) versus trial-product estimands 17.9% and 16.9%. Vomiting about 35% in STEP TEENS semaglutide vs 0-25% in adult studies (also higher with placebo); nausea similar (Fig. 5).

## Covariates
Body weight is the main covariate: higher weight lowers exposure (above). Age group (12-<18 y) and sex, race, ethnicity, glycaemic status do not matter. Adolescent placebo response slightly lower and drug effect slightly higher than adults (model adjusts placebo and Emax).

## Caveats
Simulation, not a trial; uncertainty larger in adolescents (N = 201). Dose proportionality assumed for 1.7 mg versus 2.4 mg, with no PK data at 1.7 mg in adolescents. No PK tables in the PDF. Industry authors.

## Used by stanpumpR
The context file lists no locations and the repo does not cite this paper. The library models semaglutide (R/drugs_semaglutide.R, Carlsson Petri 2018, type 2 diabetes, 0.5-1.0 mg weekly) and tirzepatide; this paper is an independent PK comparison in adolescents and adults with obesity at 2.4 mg.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Dose proportionality: R/drugs_semaglutide.R is a linear one-compartment model (bioavailability fixed, concentration scales with dose), consistent with the paper's linear scaling of 2.4 to 1.7 mg (ratio 0.708): **consistent**.
- Size effect: direction and approximate size match. The code's CL/F exponent 0.774 on weight/85 kg (R/drugs_semaglutide.R:80,123) gives 1.28 at 76 kg if the paper's reference is ~105 kg (derived), but the paper's 0.71 at 147 kg implies a steeper effect (~1.0, derived). Reference weight and the exact covariate form are unstated: **cannot verify** (open questions).
- Magnitude: code's reference subject, 1.0 mg weekly, gives Cavg 30.3 nmol/L (header :66-68); dose-proportional 2.4 mg gives ~73 nmol/L at 85 kg (derived: 30.3 x 2.4), near the paper's 74.0 nmol/L, but the paper's population mean weight is higher (STEP 1 105 kg; adolescents 107.5 kg) so the paper's averaging basis is unclear: **cannot verify**.
- Age: paper finds no adolescent age effect (0.89 and 0.96 vs adults, within limits); the code applies age factors only from 65 y: **no conflict**.
- Range: the code's model is fitted to 0.5-1.0 mg in type 2 diabetes; use at 1.7-2.4 mg, in obesity or in adolescents, rests on dose proportionality only. The paper supports proportionality up to 2.4 mg for the Novo Nordisk model; no discrepancy found, but the code's source is a different model (Carlsson Petri 2018).
