# Zlotnik 2014: LCIG infusion through pregnancy and delivery (case report)

## Study
Letter to the Editor / single case report (PDF p.1-2, printed p.1317-1318). No PK
sampling, no assay, no model. A professional singer with young-onset Parkinson
disease (diagnosed age 29) was on LCIG monotherapy from age 37; at a stated
1100 mg/day of levodopa she became pregnant and was carried through pregnancy and
a normal vaginal delivery at 39 weeks (infant 2350 g, Apgar 9/10), then breastfed
3 months (PDF p.1). During the first hours of labour, severe dyskinesia led to the
infusion rate being reduced from 50 mg/h to 20 mg/h, which produced an "OFF" state
and slowed labour; returning to the higher rate sped labour and increased
dyskinesia (PDF p.1).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic parameters are reported. The one quantitative drug-product fact
is the formulation: LCIG is levodopa 20 mg/mL and carbidopa 5 mg/mL in a gel
infused to the duodenum (PDF p.1, opening paragraph).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0, tPeak, hysteresis or potency ratio. Effect described
only clinically (dyskinesia, motor fluctuations, "OFF" state) against infusion
rate changes.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n=1 case report; levodopa is pregnancy category C. Doses are clinical totals, not
PK observations. The 1100 mg/day figure is the daily levodopa amount; the 50→20
mg/h figures are continuous infusion rates during labour.
