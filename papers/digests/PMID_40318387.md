# Jimenez 2025: Cenobamate and pregnancy, single-centre series

## Study
Retrospective chart review, Yale Comprehensive Epilepsy Center, Jan 2020-Dec 2024. 215 adult women received cenobamate (CNB); 147 were of childbearing age (median 35, range 18-49). 33 documented contraception. Four unintended pregnancies (2.7% of 147): one live early-term birth (induced at 37w6d for worsening seizures; no malformation or delay to 5 months), two elective terminations (6w and 7w1d), one spontaneous miscarriage (7w3d). Pharmacokinetic measure: concentration-to-dose ratio (CDR) = steady-state trough serum CNB concentration divided by total daily dose in mg (Methods, PDF p. 2). No assay method, timing relative to dose, or total vs free stated; blood-draw timing was inconsistent (authors' limitation).

Patient 1 (CNB 100 mg pre-pregnancy, 150 mg to 28 weeks, 200 mg at 33-36 weeks, 250 mg one month post-partum; on carbamazepine 600 mg, brivaracetam 200 mg, lamotrigine 600 then 800 mg from 9-12 weeks):
- >6 months pre-pregnancy: CDR 0.038, level 3.8 ug/mL.
- 9-12 wk: 0.053 (8 ug/mL); 13-16: 0.064 (9.6); 17-20: 0.061 (9.1); 21-24: 0.057 (8.5); 25-28: 0.066 (9.9); 29-32: no CDR (dose 150 mg); 33-36: 0.048 (9.6; dose 200 mg); 1 month post-partum: 0.067 (16.8; 250 mg).
- Text (PDF p. 4): +39.4% CDR pre-conception to 9-12 wk; -27% at 33-36 wk; +39.6% post-partum vs 33-36 wk. (Check: 0.053/0.038 = 1.39; 0.048/0.066 = 0.73; 0.067/0.048 = 1.40. The -27% is vs the 25-28 wk value, not vs baseline; 0.048 is still above the 0.038 baseline.)
Patient 2 (CNB 200 mg): CDR 0.075 at 5-8 wk (14.9 ug/mL) vs 0.082 post-termination (16.4 ug/mL); text says a 0.007 (9.33%) difference. Pregnancy value was lower than post.
Patient 4: CDR 0.039/0.029/0.03 (CNB 300-400 mg, 11.8/11.5/11.8 ug/mL) >6 months pre; 0.073 one month pre (22.1 ug/mL, 300 mg); 0.072 at 0-4 wk (21.6 ug/mL); text +120.3% vs the mean of the earlier values. Patient 3 stopped CNB at confirmation; no levels.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No efficacy concentration, no MEAC, no ke0; seizure frequency only mentioned qualitatively (patient 1 worsening). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
None analysed. Concomitant antiseizure drugs and CNB dose changes vary between time points; patient 1's CNB dose rose 100 to 150 to 200 mg during pregnancy, and her carbamazepine and lamotrigine (enzyme interactions) also changed.

## Caveats
Four pregnancies, one with serial levels; retrospective; inconsistent draw timing; polytherapy with enzyme-active drugs; CDR is not corrected for dose-dependent induction; authors call the findings anecdotal and speculative. They attribute the late dip to pregnancy changes in distribution, renal clearance and hepatic metabolism without data. The abstract says CDR trended downward in pregnancy; the body shows a rise first (patient 1), with a dip at 33-36 weeks.
