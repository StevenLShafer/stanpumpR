# Würthwein 2017: time-varying clearance of PEG-asparaginase (Oncaspar) in children with ALL

## Study
Population PK of therapeutic-drug-monitoring data from the AIEOP-BFM ALL 2009 trial (German and Czech
centres; samples 16.06.2010-31.12.2013; NCT01117441). Oncaspar (PEG-asparaginase, E. coli enzyme with
succinimidyl-succinate PEG linker) 2500 IU/m2 as a 2-h IV infusion, maximum 3750 IU; induction doses day 12
and day 26 (median interval 14 d, range 11-46), reinduction day 8 (median 150 d after, range 117-220; treated
as a new, independent exposure). Monitoring on days 7 and 14 after each dose (p. 2, Table 1 p. 3).
- 1342 patients (786 male, 556 female), 3064 administrations, 6107 samples (152 < LLOQ) (p. 4).
- Table 3 (p. 5, rendered): age median 5.2 y (1.0-17.9), height 113 cm (71-198), weight 19.6 kg (8.5-132),
  BMI 16.1 (11.4-38.0), BSA 0.79 m2 (0.41-2.65; Mosteller), dose 1950 IU (710-5300), 2493 IU/m2 (1002-4882).
- Excluded: samples after allergic reaction, switch to Erwinase, silent inactivation, and implausible rises;
  353 observations excluded (p. 4). So the model describes non-inactivated ("standard") kinetics only.
- Assay: serum asparaginase activity by AHA hydrolysis / indooxine colorimetry at 710 nm, central lab
  (Münster); LLOQ 5 U/L (p. 3). BLQ data (2.5%) omitted for estimation (M3 unstable); FOCE-INTER, NONMEM 7.3.
- 42.7% of samples at 7 +/- 1 d and 38.2% at 14 +/- 1 d after dose; only 0.9% at 1-5 d (p. 4).

## Model and parameters
Compared: 1- and 2-compartment linear, zero-order and Michaelis-Menten models (inadequate); one-compartment
models with CL rising with time after dose (TAD) or with time after first dose (Eqs 1-6, Table 2: linear,
exponential, concave, sigmoid Emax, Weibull); TAD versions were better by BIC 206-213 (p. 5). Best overall:
**transit model 3** (BIC 72156 vs 72428 for sigmoid-Emax-on-TAD): a chain of **n = 14** compartments joined by
Qtr; compartments 1 to n-1 each eliminate with CL_initial; the last eliminates with CL_initial + CL_induced and
has no onward transfer (Fig. 1, p. 5, rendered). Dose enters compartment 1; measured activity is the SUM of all
compartments; one volume V (the ODEs divide every A(k) by the same V). Compartments were added only if OFV fell
> 6.62 (2-20 tested). BSA enters as a linear scaling of V and all CL terms: F_i = F(1 + F_BSA (BSA - 0.79))
(Table 4 footnote). IIV on CL_initial and Qtr (exponential). Residual: combined additive + proportional.

Comparators quoted (not this study's data): Asselin 1993 NCA terminal half-life 6.86 +/- 3.08 d (days 4-14) and
2.99 +/- 1.57 d (days 15-26) (p. 2); native E. coli asparaginase CL 1.72 +/- 0.21 L/day/m2 (range 0.95-3.59;
Borghorst 2012), which the authors call the theoretical maximum not reached here (p. 7). V 1.27 L/m2 is said to
lie within the reported plasma volume of children.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. End point is serum asparaginase activity (U/L); no asparagine depletion, activity threshold, ke0 or
exposure-response analysis. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
BSA only, inside the structural model (linear, centred on 0.79 m2, Table 4). No age, sex, phase or dose effect
was tested or reported in this paper. Weight and height recorded per administration.

## Caveats
- Empirical model; the de-PEGylation mechanism (hydrolysis of the ester linker) is a hypothesis, unmeasured.
- Sampling is sparse before day 6 (0.9% of samples 1-5 d), so the distribution phase and Cmax are barely informed.
- Inactivated patients and BLQ samples excluded; the VPC under-predicts the BLQ fraction after TAD 18 d (p. 6),
  probably under-predicting the late rise in CL.
- Activity units, not mass; product- and linker-specific (does not transfer to calaspargase pegol).
- The per-m2 values are the 0.79 m2 estimates divided by 0.79, while BSA enters as 1 + 1.52(BSA - 0.79), so the
  model is not proportional to BSA: at BSA 1.79 m2 the factor is 2.52 vs 2.27 for straight proportionality
  (derived).
