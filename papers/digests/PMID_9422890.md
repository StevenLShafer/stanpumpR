# McCrae 1997: ropivacaine vs bupivacaine extradural in labour

## Study
Randomized, double-blind, 22 ASA I women in established labour (>=36 weeks, singleton vertex), extradural at L2-3: ropivacaine n=10 (weight 74.9 kg SD 6.1, height 163.5 cm, age 26.9) or bupivacaine n=12 (74.5 kg SD 10.5, 161.9 cm, 27.8) (Table 1). Both 0.5%: 20 mg test dose, then 30 mg 5 min later (50 mg main dose), then top-up 25 mg of 0.25% on request (median 111 min ropivacaine, 126 min bupivacaine). PK data in 20 patients (2 bupivacaine patients replaced for sampling failures). Venous plasma sampled at 0 (end of main dose), 10, 20, 30 min, 1 h (2 h if no top-up yet) and before top-up; at 20 min extra samples for free drug and alpha1-acid glycoprotein (AAG). Total drug by capillary GC (LOQ 0.010 mg/L); free drug by ultrafiltration plus coupled-column LC (LOQ 0.003 mg/L); AAG by radial immunodiffusion. Non-parametric (Wilcoxon).

## Model and parameters
No model. AUC and t1/2 could not be estimated (too few samples, top-ups, variable delivery timing; PDF p4). Table 2 (page image confirmed), main dose, venous plasma, mg/L:

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Clinical only: sensory level to pinprick (T6-11 ropivacaine, T4-11 bupivacaine after main dose), modified Bromage (Table 3), analgesia good in 9/10 and 10/12 at 30 min. No ke0, MEAC/analgesic concentration or potency ratio. Authors note toxicity relates to free concentration; no adverse maternal or fetal effects.

## Covariates
None modelled; labour, parity (5/5 vs 8/4 primi/multiparae). Free fraction vs AAG shown (Figure 3).

## Caveats
Venous sampling, sparse, no AUC/half-life/clearance. Sponsor (Astra) funded; assay at Astra. Small n.
