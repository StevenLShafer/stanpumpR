# Campanero 2008: terazosin PK/PD of blood-pressure effect in healthy volunteers

## Study
Single-dose PK/PD study in 24 healthy volunteers (12 M, 12 F), age 22-26 y, 50-80 kg, all
within 20% of ideal weight, non-smokers, normotensive (excluded if BP >140/90 or <110/60
mmHg) [PDF p3/printed 141]. One oral 2 mg terazosin tablet (Deflox, Abbott) with 200 mL
water after a 10-h fast. Terazosin plasma assayed by HPLC-fluorescence (prazosin internal
standard; LLOQ 0.25 ng/mL) at 20, 40, 60, 80, 100, 120, 150 min and 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13
h. Blood pressure by non-invasive oscillometry (HP M1205A) at baseline, through 13 h, and
at 72 h. PK and PD modelled **subject by subject** in WinNonlin 1.5: a one-compartment
oral model with first-order absorption and monoexponential elimination (Eq. 1), then its
fixed PK parameters carried into an inhibitory-Emax **effect-compartment** PD model
(Eq. 3) relating effect-site concentration to systolic (SBP) and diastolic (DBP) blood
pressure.

## Model and parameters
**PK — Table I (PDF p5/printed 143), mean ± SD across 24 subjects:** AUC∞ 457.1 ± 80.12
μg·h/L; t½,ka 0.27 ± 0.08 h; t½,ke 6.19 ± 2.12 h; CL/F 4.4 ± 8.1 L/h; V/F 39.1 ± 4.1 L;
tmax 1.27 ± 0.11 h; Cmax 44.4 ± 5.2 μg/L. (The CL/F SD of 8.1 exceeds its mean — printed
as-is.) No covariate model; absorption/clearance/volume are the only PK summary given.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **End point:** cuff (oscillometric) systolic and diastolic arterial blood pressure, not
  EEG/pupil/analgesia. Observed mean falls after 2 mg: SBP −9.3 ± 4.3 mmHg (9.78%), DBP
  −12.0 ± 7.3 mmHg (19.51%) [PDF p5/printed 143]; DBP was the more sensitive response.
- **Structure:** inhibitory-Emax on an effect compartment (Eq. 3), fitted per subject.
- **Emax:** 29.9 mmHg (SBP), 39.69 mmHg (DBP) — fitted asymptotes from a single 2 mg dose,
  not achievable clinical targets.
- **EC50:** 29.9 μg/L (SBP), 28.7 μg/L (DBP).
- **ke0 / hysteresis:** the paper names the effect-compartment rate constant **ke0** (the
  column header in Tables II/III is "ke0 (h⁻¹)", footnoted "ke0 = first-order rate
  constant") and uses it to resolve a plasma-vs-BP hysteresis loop (PDF p6/printed 144).
  ke0 = 0.401 /h (SBP), 0.472 /h (DBP). **t½ke0 = ln2/ke0** is given as ~1.2-1.8 h, so the
  maximum BP decrease occurs about 2-3 h after dosing (PDF p6). The discussion states
  ke0 ≈ 0.4 /h corresponds to a distribution half-life ≈ 1.2 h (PDF p7/printed 145).
- **tPeak / MEAC / potency ratio:** not applicable (antihypertensive, not an opioid; no
  MEAC, no analgesic concentration, no equianalgesic ratio).

## Covariates
None. Modelling was per-subject; no structural covariates on PK or PD. Oral
bioavailability stated only as "approximately 90%" in the introduction (background, not
fitted; PDF p2/printed 140).

## Caveats
- **n = 24 healthy normotensives, single 2 mg dose.** Emax values (especially DBP 39.7
  mmHg) are fitted asymptotes, not clinically achievable drops; the authors say so.
- **No dose-to-concentration chain for the PD model.** Table I reports CL/F and V/F, but
  the effect-site records in the registry are tagged as structure-only; the library does
  not implement a terazosin PK model (registry row is an effect relation plus a descriptive
  label record), so there is no simulated plasma curve to drive EC50.
- **Uncertainty type unstated.** The paper never says whether "±" is SD or SE. ke0's
  ± 0.006 /h (1.5% CV) is far smaller than the 25.3% CVinter in the same table and smaller
  than the ± on Emax (35%) and EC50 (14%) fitted in the same 24 subjects — not credible as
  a between-subject SD; likely an SE or a decimal error in the source (stanpumpR's docs
  flag this; the full text does not resolve which).
- Single-dose **t½,ke 6.19 h** here is shorter than the ~12 h terminal half-life quoted on
  the product label for chronic dosing.
