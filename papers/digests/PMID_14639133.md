# Sani 2003: MAC attack? (plus two other editorials)

PDF page = printed page - 1248 (1249 = p1 ... 1254 = p6). Three editorials, no PK or PD tables. The DOI is not printed in the PDF text and is left blank.

## Study
Narrative commentary; no subjects, no assay, no model.
- **MAC attack? (PDF p1-2).** Comments on Lu, Ramsay, Bailey 2003 (mixed-effects logistic regression of binary data). With few observations per individual, population analysis gives a hugely biased Hill coefficient; the editorial says at least 10 observations per subject are needed. MAC is defined as the steady-state partial pressure with 50% probability of no movement to incision; between-individual variation is stated as no more than 10-15%. Naive pooled ("giant rat") analysis underestimates steepness; each individual's curve is steeper than the population curve. Group differences in MAC may be artifacts.
- **Sample surveys (PDF p3-4).** Survey design (sampling frame, sample size, nonresponse bias). Not pharmacology.
- **Ropivacaine cardiac arrest (PDF p5-6).** Two case reports discussed: a 66-yr-old woman given ropivacaine 6.67 mg/kg for lower-extremity blocks, and a 66-yr-old 100-kg man given 1.88 mg/kg for a lumbar plexus block. Both were resuscitated, both received 0.75% ropivacaine and hydroxyzine premedication. No plasma concentrations are given in this editorial.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Ropivacaine in vitro potency stated as about 25% less than bupivacaine (PDF p5, printed 1253; no source given for the number).
- Minimum local analgesic concentration (MLAC) of ropivacaine in labouring women given epidural analgesia: "almost twice" that of bupivacaine (PDF p5; cites Polley 1999 and Capogna 1999; no concentration value and no end-point definition in this editorial).
- Inhaled anaesthetic MAC: no numeric values.
- Not an opioid paper.
