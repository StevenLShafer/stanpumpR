# Holbrook 2012: ACCP 9th edition, evidence-based management of anticoagulant therapy

## Study
Clinical practice guideline (ACCP AT9), GRADE methodology. Panel limited recommendations to questions with at least one
comparative study of >= 50 patients per group. 23 questions; only two strong recommendations. Covers VKAs, UFH, LMWH and
fondaparinux; excludes pregnancy and children (PDF p3, printed e154S). Not a pharmacokinetic study.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic parameters are reported. Dosing recommendations (summary, PDF p1-3, printed e152S-e154S):
- 2.1: outpatients, warfarin 10 mg daily for the first 2 days, then INR-based dosing (Grade 2C).
- 2.2: against routine pharmacogenetic dosing of VKA (Grade 1B).
- 6.1: IV UFH weight-adjusted, bolus 80 units/kg then 18 units/kg/h (VTE) or 70 units/kg then 15 units/kg/h
  (cardiac/stroke), or fixed 5,000 units then 1,000 units/h (Grade 2C).
- 6.2: SC UFH for outpatient VTE, 333 units/kg then 250 units/kg, unmonitored (Grade 2C).
- 7.1: therapeutic LMWH, reduce dose when CrCl < 30 mL/min (Grade 2C).
- 8.1: fondaparinux for VTE above 100 kg, 10 mg rather than 7.5 mg daily SC (Grade 2C).
- 9.1: INR 4.5-10 without bleeding, no routine vitamin K (2B); INR > 10, oral vitamin K (2C). 9.3: major bleeding,
  4-factor PCC rather than plasma, plus vitamin K 5-10 mg slow IV (2C).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is the INR. Section 4.1 (PDF p14, printed e165S; text layer): systematic review of 19 studies (> 80,000 patients); the lowest
composite of major haemorrhage and symptomatic thromboembolism at INR 2.0-3.0. Against INR 2.0-3.0, RR 2.4 (1.9-3.1)
for INR < 2, 1.8 (1.2-2.6) for INR 3.0-5.0, 11.9 (6.0-23.4) for INR > 5. Table 10 (page image): INR < 2 vs 2-3,
thromboembolism RR 3.5 (2.8-4.4), major haemorrhage RR 1.1 (0.7-1.7). Higher-risk valve patients (mechanical mitral,
mechanical aortic with risk factors, caged-ball/caged-disk) are traditionally, without evidence, treated at INR 2.5-3.5,
deferred to Whitlock et al in the same supplement (PDF p14). No drug concentrations, ke0 or EC50. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None modelled. Weight (UFH, fondaparinux) and renal function (LMWH) enter only as dosing adjustments.

## Caveats
- Mostly low-quality evidence; INR-range evidence is largely observational and warfarin-dominated.
- The text's RRs for INR 3-5 (1.8) differ from Table 9's major-haemorrhage RR 2.7 because they are different outcomes
  (composite vs bleeding).
- Valve-specific targets are not this chapter's recommendation.
