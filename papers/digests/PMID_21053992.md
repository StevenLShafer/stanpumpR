# Retlich 2010: intravenous linagliptin, target-mediated disposition and absolute bioavailability

## Study
Single rising-dose, randomized, placebo-controlled, single-blind (within dose group) phase 1 study (p1-2,
printed 829-830). 36 healthy men, 18-50 years (mean 38.2), BMI 18.9-29.4 kg/m2 (mean 25.06); 35 White, 1 Black
(p4, printed 832). Four sequential groups: 0.5, 2.5 and 10 mg IV (6 active, 2 placebo each); group 3 a two-way
crossover of 5 mg IV and a 10 mg oral tablet (10 active, 2 placebo per treatment; washout at least 25 days).
IV infusions in 40 mL 0.9% NaCl over 90 min after a 10 h fast. Plasma sampling to day 9; urine to 120 h.
Assay: HPLC-MS/MS, [13C3]linagliptin internal standard; LLOQ 0.1 nmol/L plasma, 1.0 nmol/L urine (p3).
Total (bound + unbound) plasma concentration measured. DPP-4 activity by fluorescence enzyme assay.
NCA in WinNonlin 4.1; population model in NONMEM V, FOCE-I, log-transformed data (p3).

## Model and parameters
**Noncompartmental (Table I, PDF p6, printed 834; geometric mean [geometric %CV], tmax median [range]).**
Total-concentration parameters are dose dependent because of target binding:

Renal clearance 0-4 h up to 132 mL/min (10 mg), 0-120 h 2.00-77.7 mL/min (Table I). NCA bioavailability was
dose dependent: 12.0%, 30.7%, 40.3% and 68.3% for the tablet against the 0.5, 2.5, 5 and 10 mg infusions (p5,
printed 833), which the authors reject as invalid for nonlinear PK.

**Population model (Fig. 3, PDF p7, printed 835; Table II, PDF p8, printed 836).** Three compartments
(central V2, peripheral V3 and V4); unbound drug distributes and is cleared first-order; quasi-equilibrium
saturable binding to DPP-4 in the central compartment (Bmax, concentration) and in peripheral compartment 1
(Amax2, amount), same KD in both. Total plasma Ctot = A2/V2 + Bmax·(A2/V2)/(KD + A2/V2). Oral: first-order
absorption with lag. No IIV on disposition (not identifiable); IIV only on F, ALAG, ka. Parameters are
absolute (IV data included), not apparent /F.

Unbound drug terminal t½ 17 h and accumulation t½ 10 h; total-drug terminal t½ 116-136 h (p8, printed 836).
Free fraction rises from about 1% to 30% as concentration rises from low nanomolar to about 30 nmol/L (p7).
The authors say their Vss and CL are about 2-fold lower than the earlier model-based analysis (their ref 23,
Retlich J Clin Pharmacol 2010), from an unexplained 21-41% inter-study exposure difference (p8).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: plasma DPP-4 activity inhibition (% of baseline), direct sigmoid Emax on total plasma concentration,
same after IV and oral (Fig. 6; p6-7, printed 834-835): Emax 94.2%, Hill coefficient 2.51, EC50 2.82 nmol/L,
EC80 5.61 nmol/L. No hysteresis, ke0 or tPeak reported. Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None tested (healthy men only, single study; no IIV on disposition).

## Caveats
Healthy men only; small groups (n = 6 or 10). Disposition parameters have no IIV. VPC overpredicts slightly at
48-120 h in the 10 mg group. Only total concentration was measured, so linear plasma-protein binding cannot be
separated from unbound drug. Industry funded (Boehringer Ingelheim).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/drugs_linagliptin.R:30-31** ("QP, VP, Amax,P and Kd were fixed to the earlier model's estimates (Retlich
  et al. 2010)"). The library's values (lines 24-26, 102-132) are QP/F 412 L/h, VP/F 1650 L, Amax,P/F 1650 nmol,
  Kd 0.0652 nmol/L, in a two-compartment model. Retlich 2015 (archive PMID_25637172, Table 4 footnote d) says
  these were fixed to the previous model, its ref 15: Retlich, J Clin Pharmacol 2010;50:873-85, a two-compartment
  model from the phase 1 patient studies. This paper is a different, three-compartment, healthy-volunteer IV model
  whose values do not match: KD 0.0835 (not 0.0652) nmol/L, Amax2 548 nmol absolute, Q3 24.3 L/h, V3 293 L.
  None of the library's fixed values can be found in this paper. **Discrepancy (citation)**: the repo sentence is
  true of the J Clin Pharmacol paper, but it names no journal, and the archive links it to this PDF. The model's
  numbers are unaffected. See `open_questions`.
- **Independent comparison with the library model (Retlich 2015, type 2 diabetes, oral, apparent /F).** Unbound
  clearance: this paper 26.9 L/h absolute; library CL/F 258 L/h × F 0.295 = 76 L/h (derived: 258 × 0.295), about
  2.8-fold higher. Central binding: Bmax 3.44 nmol/L here (healthy men) vs 4.97 nmol/L (library, male with T2DM).
  Kd 0.0835 vs 0.0652 nmol/L. Peripheral binding amount: 548 nmol here vs 1650 × 0.295 = 487 nmol (derived).
  Unbound distribution volume 402 L here vs (715 + 1650) × 0.295 = 698 L (derived). The paper's own Discussion
  already reports that its CL and Vss are about 2-fold below the earlier patient model. Because the library
  works on apparent oral values, the F of this paper is not used. Not a defect of the library; recorded as an
  independent source.
- **Units**: library converts nmol to mcg with MW 472.54 (line 75); the paper reports all concentrations in
  nmol/L, so 1 nmol/L = 0.4725 ng/mL holds for any comparison. The library plots total concentration, as this
  paper measured.
- **Default (inst/extdata/drugDefaults_global.csv:147)**: no MEAC/threshold (0); consistent with this paper,
  which gives only a DPP-4 inhibition EC50 (2.82 nmol/L), not used by the library (lines 7-9 say DPP-4
  inhibition is not plotted).
