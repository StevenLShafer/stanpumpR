# Kelz 2008: orexin neurons and emergence from isoflurane/sevoflurane (mice)

## Study
Animal (mouse) study of inhaled-anaesthetic induction vs emergence.
- c-Fos immunohistochemistry: adult male C57BL/6J, 2 h of 1.25% isoflurane or 2.14% sevoflurane in oxygen (or oxygen control) from lights-out. Isoflurane cut c-Fos-positive orexin neurons by 30% (P < 0.05), sevoflurane by 50% (P < 0.001); adjacent MCH neurons were unchanged (PDF p.1-2).
- Genetic ablation: orexin/ataxin-3 narcoleptic mice (26 transgenic, 66 ± 5 d; 13 wild-type siblings, 61 ± 9 d) and the orexin-1 receptor antagonist SB-334867-A (5 or 20 mg/kg i.p.) vs vehicle (24 C57BL/6J mice).
- Induction: stepwise anaesthetic increases every 15 min (8-12 steps, each +6 ± 3%; start 0.44% isoflurane or 0.96% sevoflurane, peak 1.19% and 1.52%); loss of righting reflex (LORR) = failure to turn prone within 2 min. Emergence: time from stopping 2 h of 1.25% isoflurane to return of righting. EEG/EMG on 6 + 6 mice with 1.30% isoflurane for 2 h. Brain isoflurane measured by HPLC (PDF p.2-4).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model. The sigmoid dose-response fit gives MAC_LORR (ED50, the concentration at which half the mice lose the righting reflex) and a Hill slope (Table 1, PDF p.2, printed p.1310; concentrations are % in oxygen, i.e. vapour %):

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: LORR (induction), return of righting reflex (emergence), EEG/EMG wakefulness, c-Fos in orexin neurons.
- Emergence times: orexin/ataxin-3 mice took about 50% longer than wild-type for both agents (F1,72 = 18.17, P < 0.001, Fig. 3A). Isoflurane after 2 h at 1.30%: EEG/EMG wakefulness returned at 9.8 ± 2.2 min (wild-type) vs 19.2 ± 2.9 min (orexin/ataxin-3) (P = 0.015). SB-334867-A 20 mg/kg delayed emergence from 1.25% isoflurane (F2,45 = 8.80, P < 0.001); 5 mg/kg gave a smaller, dose-dependent trend (Fig. 3B).
- Induction was unchanged: equal MAC_LORR and Hill slopes with ablation or antagonist, and equal latency to LORR; brain isoflurane at 4 min of 1.25% was the same in vehicle and 20 mg/kg groups (P = 0.76). Equal brain isoflurane at delayed emergence argues against a kinetic cause.
- No ke0, effect-site equilibration time, MEAC or potency ratio is reported; mouse data. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None (genotype and antagonist dose only; both sexes in the behavioural assays, males in the pharmacology).

## Caveats
- Mouse, righting reflex end point; MAC_LORR values are mouse ED50s and are not human MACs.
- i.p. antagonist at 20 ml/kg; the authors say the delay is not cataplexy (none seen on EEG/EMG).
- Repeat exposures of the same mice 24 h apart for dose-response means.
