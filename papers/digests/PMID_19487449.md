# Nannini 2009: cefazolin MICs and the inoculum effect in MSSA

## Study
- 98 MSSA clinical isolates: 86 randomly selected from cSSTI (ATLAS), hospital-acquired pneumonia (ATTAIN) and endocarditis (ICE) repositories, plus 12 from hemodialysis bacteremia patients treated with vancomycin; a further 12 cefazolin-treated bacteremia isolates analysed separately (24 hemodialysis isolates in all).
- Broth microdilution, cation-adjusted Mueller-Hinton II (CLSI), cefazolin MICs at low (~5x10^4), standard (~5x10^5 CFU/mL, SI), intermediate (~5x10^6) and high (~5x10^7 CFU/mL, HI) inocula, read at 24 h. blaZ typed by sequencing (A, B, C, D).
- Pronounced inoculum effect defined as cefazolin MIC >= 16 ug/mL at HI.

## Model and parameters
No PK. MIC results (ug/mL = mg/L):

- Bla types: A 25.5%, B 15.3%, C 45.9%, D 0%, blaZ-negative 13.2%.
- Type A producers at SI: MIC50 1, MIC90 4; at HI MIC50 8, MIC90 64 (Table 1).
- Hemodialysis bacteremia substudy: 3 of 6 cefazolin failures vs 0 of 6 cefazolin cures had a pronounced inoculum effect (P = 0.09).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD target. Discussion cites serum cefazolin about 17 ug/mL 4 h after 1 g IV and bone 6.9 ug/mL at 60 min (other sources), and notes time above MIC as the driver.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- MIC distribution from multinational trial isolates (endocarditis, HAP, cSSTI) and hemodialysis bacteremia; not surgical-prophylaxis isolates.
- Clinical association (inoculum effect vs failure) not statistically significant, n = 12.
- 2 mg/L is the standard-inoculum MIC90; at high inoculum MICs rise to 16-64 mg/L in about a fifth of strains.
