# Wang 2011: oral metronidazole across the stages of pregnancy

## Study
University of Texas Medical Branch (OPRU network). 20 pregnant women (singletons, age 18 or over) with bacterial
vaginosis whose own providers prescribed metronidazole, in windows of 10-14 weeks (early, n = 2), 22-26 weeks
(middle, n = 9) or 34-38 weeks (late, n = 9) (Table I, PDF p10; group sizes also in Results). Exclusions:
haematocrit below 28%, clinically significant hepatic, renal or gastrointestinal disease. Table I: age 23.3 +/- 3.3
yr; weight 80.8 +/- 28.6 kg overall (early 60.2 and 67.8 kg; middle 80.7 +/- 36.1; late 84.7 +/- 23.5); 1 smoker,
8 on co-medication, 16 on prenatal vitamins; Caucasian 5, Hispanic 9, African-American 6.

Dosing: Flagyl 500 mg (2 x 250 mg tablets) orally twice daily for 3 days; PK after the morning dose on day 4
(steady state). Venous plasma before and at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 h; urine in intervals to 12 h.
HPLC-UV, plasma linear 0.2-25 ug/mL, precision 1.40-4.30%, accuracy -4.37 to 0.42%, recovery above 95%. Model-
independent analysis (Kinetica 5.0); CL/F and Vdss/F from AUCss; one-way ANOVA; SPSS 12.0. No nonpregnant
control group.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. MIC50 values quoted for bacterial-vaginosis organisms (Gardnerella 8, Mobiluncus 16, Bacteroides and
Prevotella 1-2 ug/mL; Discussion, cited); the authors judge 500 mg twice daily adequate from the peak and trough.

## Covariates
Stage of pregnancy: no significant difference in any weight-normalized parameter; absolute AUC differed (P = 0.005),
driven by the two early-pregnancy women who received more drug per kg (7.8 +/- 0.7 vs 7.0 +/- 2.2 and 6.3 +/- 1.9
mg/kg). Race with stage explained about 40% of the variability in CL/F and half-life, neither significant on its
own. Caffeine intake: no effect.

## Caveats
- Only 2 women in early pregnancy; no nonpregnant control (historical comparison only).
- Abstract vs Discussion: the abstract says CL/F and volume were in the range of nonpregnant values; the
  Discussion says exposure slightly lower, volume slightly greater and half-life shorter than nonpregnant values
  (AUC 105-159 mg h/L, Vd 610-690, t1/2 7.0-8.8 h, refs 22-24). The study's half-lives (5.2-6.2 h by group) lie
  below that nonpregnant range. The Discussion prints the nonpregnant Vd as "L/kg"; against Table II's mL/kg values
  of the same order this unit looks wrong, but the paper does not correct it.
- Apparent (oral) parameters only; F not estimated. Plasma truncated before 12 h in some women (extrapolated C12).
