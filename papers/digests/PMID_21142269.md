# Grönlund 2011: CYP2D6/3A4 inhibition and IV oxycodone pharmacokinetics

## Study
Randomized, three-phase, crossover, placebo-controlled drug-interaction study in 12 healthy
subjects (9 men, 3 women; age 19–30 y, weight 52–83 kg). Each phase: 5 days of oral pre-treatment
with placebo, paroxetine 20 mg/day (CYP2D6 inhibitor), or paroxetine 20 mg/day + itraconazole
200 mg/day (CYP2D6 + CYP3A4 inhibition); 4-week washout. On day 4, 1 h after pre-treatment, a
single IV dose of **oxycodone 0.1 mg/kg** was given over 2 min (Oxynorm 10 mg/mL). Plasma sampled
to 48 h (14 timepoints); oxycodone, noroxycodone, oxymorphone, noroxymorphone measured by
LC-MS/MS (LLQ 0.1 ng/mL oxycodone). CYP2D6 genotyped. **Noncompartmental** analysis (statistical
moment theory) in WinNonlin 4.1; this is a PK/interaction study, not a compartmental model fit.
Pharmacodynamics: VAS, pupillometry (Cogan), Maddox wing, DSST, and the **cold pain test** (ice-water
immersion) for analgesia.

Key results: paroxetine alone had negligible effect on parent oxycodone (CL fell ~10%, p=0.016; CL
8.74 ± 1.02 mL/min/kg; other parameters unchanged) but cut oxymorphone AUC48 by 66%. Combined
paroxetine + itraconazole **doubled oxycodone exposure** (AUC 18.6 vs 9.4 µg·min/mL, p<0.001),
prolonged t½ from 3.8 to 6.6 h, and cut CL by 48%. Oral comparison (reference [5]): combined
inhibition raised oral oxycodone exposure 2.9-fold vs 2-fold here, consistent with bypass of
presystemic metabolism (oral bioavailability cited as 60–87%).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
**No MEAC / minimum effective or analgesic concentration** is reported; no concentration–effect
(EC50/Emax) model, **no ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis**, and **no equianalgesic or potency
ratio** are given. This is a pharmacokinetic/interaction study with descriptive PD only. Analgesia
was assessed by the cold pain test (cold pain threshold CPT; cold pain intensity at 30 s and 60 s,
NRS 0–100) summarised as AUEC to 6 and 12 h (Table II, PDF p.8). **No MEAC can be derived.** The
raised oxycodone concentrations were **not** reflected in stronger opioid effects: CPT and cold-pain
intensity did not differ between phases (Table II); AUEC12 for Maddox, DSST, VAS drug effect and
pleasantness were unchanged; paroxetine itself caused mydriasis, confounding pupil size as an opioid
surrogate. Authors note the study was powered for PK, not PD. Oxycodone caused miosis in all phases.

## Covariates
CYP2D6 genotype (10 extensive, 1 ultrarapid, 1 poor metaboliser); the small n did not allow
conclusions on genotype effect, and reanalysis of the 10 extensive metabolisers was unchanged.
No weight/age covariate model (noncompartmental study).

## Caveats
Small healthy-volunteer sample (n=12); single IV dose; noncompartmental analysis (no micro-rate
model, no effect site); no CYP3A4-only phase; paroxetine's own mydriatic and subjective effects
confound the PD read-outs; oxymorphone/noroxymorphone often below LLQ after inhibitor pre-treatment.
