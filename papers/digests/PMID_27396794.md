# Wiesen 2016: rivaroxaban in breast milk, one postpartum woman

## Study
Single case report (University Hospital of Cologne). A 40-year-old white woman, **84 kg**, with hypertension
and hypothyroidism, emergency caesarean for placental abruption at 30.5 weeks; on postpartum day 5 transferred
with submassive bilateral pulmonary embolism, then diagnosed with peripartum cardiomyopathy (EF 41%) and advised
to stop breastfeeding. Enoxaparin 80 mg bid for 2 days, then **rivaroxaban 15 mg bid** orally (PDF p1-3).
Comedication: metoprolol, hydrochlorothiazide, ramipril; bromocriptine 2.5 mg bid started after sampling.
**Sampling on day 3 of rivaroxaban**, before bromocriptine: whole output of each breast pumped before and 3, 6
and 10 h after the morning dose, venous citrated blood drawn after each milk collection (p3). Serum creatinine
and creatinine clearance not reported; whether the dose was taken with food is unknown (p4).
Assay: validated LC-MS/MS (ref 4) with [13C6]-rivaroxaban internal standard; milk calibrators in pooled milk
from three healthy women in the first postpartum week; linear range 1-500 µg/L plasma, 1-350 µg/L milk
(Figure 2 caption, p2; the text layer renders µg as "mg", the rendered page shows µg/L). Matrix effects
checked by post-column infusion (Figure 3). NCA, linear trapezoidal rule (Table 2 footnote).

## Caveats
- n = 1; the mother was not breastfeeding (milk pumped for the study), infant exposure is estimated, not
  measured; infant bioavailability not considered (authors).
- Authors note steady state may not have been reached by day 3 and food intake is unknown.
- Postpartum (roughly day 10 after delivery, derived: transfer on day 5, 2 days enoxaparin, day 3 of
  rivaroxaban; exact day not stated), not antepartum. Renal function not reported. Plasma sampled only to
  10 h of a 12-h interval.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Registry row (pregnancyRegistry.csv:149).** Calls Wiesen 2016 a breast-milk case report that gives no
  maternal antepartum PK. Paper: a postpartum case measuring milk and plasma concentrations; no antepartum
  data, no clearance estimate. Citation (authors, Chest 2016;150:e1-4, PMID) correct. No factor is derived from
  it. **matches.**
- **Against the library model (`R/drugs_rivaroxaban.R`, Willmann 2018), uncited independent check.** The paper
  gives no CL/F, so only a rough comparison is possible (all derived): model F at 15 mg = 0.590 + 0.66 x
  exp(-ln2 x 15/14.4) = 0.911; CL/F at 84 kg with creatinine clearance at the reference = 6.58 x (84/81)^-0.278 =
  6.51 L/h (VTE indication, factor 1) or 5.53 L/h with the library's fixed atrial-fibrillation factor 0.849;
  predicted steady-state AUC over 12 h = 0.911 x 15000 / CL/F = about 2100 µg·h/L (VTE) or 2470 µg·h/L (as the
  library computes). Observed AUC0-10 is 949.2 µg·h/L; adding about 2 h at the ~44 µg/L trough gives roughly
  1040 µg·h/L over 12 h, about 0.5x (VTE) or 0.4x (library as coded) the model's typical exposure. Direction:
  this postpartum woman's exposure ran lower than the non-pregnant model, consistent in direction with the
  registry's Rudd 2015 postpartum-failure note, but renal function, food and steady state are unknown and n = 1,
  so it cannot support a factor, as the registry already concludes. The model's header does not address
  postpartum patients.
