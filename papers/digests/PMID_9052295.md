# Downing 1997: epidural lidocaine and bupivacaine at cesarean section

## Study
Non-compartmental PK in 20 healthy term parturients (age 17-38 yr) having elective cesarean section under epidural block; 10 per drug (lidocaine group A-J, bupivacaine group K-S, with patient labels K,L,M,N,O,P,Q,U,R,S). Lidocaine: 2% with epinephrine 1:200,000. Bupivacaine: plain 0.5%. A 3 mL test dose of 1.5% lidocaine with epinephrine was given first, then 5 mL aliquots every 5 min until a sensory level was reached (the paper does not say whether the test dose is counted in the mg/kg dose, see open questions). Epidural fentanyl 1 mcg/kg was also given 10-15 min before incision. Maternal radial ARTERIAL blood sampled at 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180 min after the epidural test dose, plus maternal arterial and umbilical venous blood at delivery. Lidocaine assayed by GC (thermionic detector), bupivacaine by HPLC (UV 210 nm); both total plasma concentration, linear 0.1-10 mcg/mL. Protein binding by equilibrium dialysis of the 30-min arterial sample. Terminal slope by log-linear regression, AUC by trapezoidal rule, Cl = dose/AUC (apparent, extravascular dose), Vd and t1/2 non-compartmental.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD end point measured. Not an opioid paper. The authors state two lidocaine patients (A, peak 10.4; E, 9.8 mcg/mL) exceeded concentrations they cite as toxic in animals, with no clinical toxicity; bupivacaine peaks (max 1.10 mcg/mL) stayed well below toxic levels. No numeric toxic threshold is given in the text I read. No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Peak arterial concentration did not correlate with mg/kg dose for either drug (r = 0.156 bupivacaine, NS, Figure 2; lidocaine NS, significant only after dropping patient E, r = 0.9) nor with height or weight. No covariate equations.

## Caveats
Single-arm, no non-pregnant comparator. Small n, wide interpatient variability. Arterial rather than venous sampling (a strength). Lidocaine given with epinephrine (slows absorption). Groups differed in age (P<=0.05). Cl and Vd are apparent. Concurrent epidural fentanyl, antacid, ranitidine, metoclopramide. Total (not free) concentration; binding is a single 30-min dialysis point.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- pregnancyRegistry.csv:15: 6.1 mL/kg/min, n=10, epidural with epinephrine, "apparent" clearance: **matches** Table 1 (page image confirmed). Unit is per kg and per min as written. The registry's n=53 total equals 10+10+10+23, consistent.
- Cross-check against the library model (not cited there): `R/drugs_lidocaine.R:14` uses cl1 = 0.0215 L/min/kg x 70 (reference 70 kg male), i.e. 21.5 mL/kg/min for the central clearance. Downing's apparent Cl/F of 6.1 mL/kg/min is about 3.5 times lower; because dose/AUC after an extravascular dose is >= true clearance, this runs opposite to what a larger model clearance would predict. I did not read the model's header beyond the grep lines, so I cannot say whether the model's clearance is fitted to IV data or what its stated aim is; the registry correctly gives no factor, so there is no effect on plotted curves.
