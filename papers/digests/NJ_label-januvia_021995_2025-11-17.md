# Januvia label (NDA 021995): sitagliptin

## Study
Not a study: the US prescribing information. Indication: adjunct to diet and exercise for glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes; not for type 1 (section 1). Tablets 100, 50 and 25 mg of free base (section 3, section 11: 32.13, 64.25 and 128.5 mg of the phosphate monohydrate equal 25, 50 and 100 mg).

## Dosing (section 2)
100 mg once daily, with or without food. eGFR 45 to under 90 mL/min/1.73 m2: no change. eGFR 30 to under 45: 50 mg once daily. eGFR under 30 or ESRD on dialysis: 25 mg once daily, with no regard to dialysis timing.

## Model and parameters
No model. Section 12.3 values, healthy adults, single oral 100 mg dose unless stated:

- Mean plasma AUC 8.52 uM.h, Cmax 950 nM, apparent terminal half-life 12.4 h (derived with free-base MW 407.3, which the label does not print: Cmax about 387 ng/mL, AUC about 3470 ng.h/mL).
- Dose-proportional AUC; steady-state AUC about 14% above the first dose; intra- and inter-subject CV of AUC 5.8% and 15.1%.
- Median Tmax 1-4 h; absolute bioavailability about 87%; a high-fat meal has no effect.
- Vss about 198 L after 100 mg IV; protein binding 38%.
- About 79% excreted unchanged in urine; renal clearance about 350 mL/min (derived: 21 L/h); metabolism minor (about 16% of radioactivity as metabolites, CYP3A4 with CYP2C8); 13% faecal and 87% urinary recovery of radiolabel in a week. Substrate of hOAT-3 and P-gp; active tubular secretion.
- Pharmacokinetics similar in healthy subjects and type 2 diabetes.
- About 13.5% of the dose removed in a 3-4 h haemodialysis session (section 10).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
DPP-4 enzyme activity inhibited over 24 h at the recommended dose; 2- to 3-fold rise in active GLP-1 and GIP after a glucose load or meal (12.2). No ke0, EC50, IC50 or therapeutic concentration range is given. QTc: no effect at 100 mg; at 800 mg (peak about 11 times that of 100 mg) the placebo-corrected maximum QTc rise was 8.0 ms at 3 h (12.2). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Renal function is the one that matters. Section 12.3: AUC about 2-fold in moderate impairment (eGFR 30 to under 45) and about 4-fold in severe impairment including ESRD on haemodialysis, against healthy controls. Moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 7-9): AUC +21%, Cmax +13%, not clinically meaningful; no data for Child-Pugh above 9. Elderly (65-80 y): about 19% higher concentrations than younger subjects, largely renal; age, BMI, sex, race otherwise not clinically meaningful (population PK). Not studied in children (8.4: efficacy not shown in 10-17 year olds). Cyclosporine raises sitagliptin AUC 1.29 and Cmax 1.68 (Table 5); sitagliptin changed the exposure of digoxin, glyburide, simvastatin, rosiglitazone, warfarin, oral contraceptive and metformin little (AUC ratios 0.85-1.12, Table 4).

## Caveats
A label reports means without ranges or fitted parameters; there is no compartment model or per-kg basis. Pregnancy (8.1): no human data; no adverse developmental effects in rats and rabbits at 30 and 20 times clinical AUC; placental transfer seen in animals. Lactation (8.2): human milk data absent; rat milk-to-plasma ratio 4:1.

Nothing cites the label, so nothing to verify against it. As an independent check of the registry note (data-raw/antidiabeticRegistry.R:279): the registry says terminal t1/2 about 10 h and fasted Cmax 492 ng/mL from Sangle 2018 (100 mg, healthy Indian men). The label's means are t1/2 12.4 h and Cmax 950 nM (about 387 ng/mL, derived); the t1/2 is near, the Cmax is lower and from a different population, with no contradiction since both are single-study means. The registry's renal AUC ratios from Scheen 2015 (x2.26 moderate, x3.77 severe, x4.50 dialysis) agree with the label's about 2-fold and about 4-fold. The label's 87% bioavailability, Vss 198 L, 38% binding and renal clearance 350 mL/min are not in the registry note and could be added if a model is ever built. Verdict: not-applicable.
