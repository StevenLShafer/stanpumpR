# Lötsch 2005: PK/PD modeling of opioids (review)

## Study
A narrative review (symposium proceedings, single author) with no new data. It sets out effect-compartment
PK/PD modelling (equations 1-10, PDF pp3-4: unit-impulse disposition, convolution, first-order transfer
fT(t) = ke0·e^(-ke0·t) with t½,ke0 = ln2/ke0, sigmoid Emax, inhibitory Emax, two-agonist additive model, power
model). It then summarises the published opioid PK/PD studies in **Table 1** (PDF p6, printed S95) and the
text (PDF pp6-11). The opening case (PDF pp2-3, Angst 2000) is a 22-year-old man in renal failure given 110 mg
IV morphine in all, who became unconscious 31 h after surgery from M6G and stayed so for 34 h after
dialysis had cleared M6G from plasma.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
**ke0 / t½,ke0 (all from Table 1, PDF p6, unless stated).** Abstract summary: alfentanil and remifentanil about
1 min, fentanyl and sufentanil about 6 min, methadone about 8 min, morphine 2-3 h, M6G about 7 h (all
t½,ke0, plasma-to-effect-site). **Morphine** t½,ke0 across Table 1 spans **1.6 h to 4.8 h**: pupil 2.8, 3.9,
2.8 h (healthy volunteers); transcutaneous electrical pain threshold 1.7 h men / 3.9 h women and pain tolerance
1.6 h men / 4.8 h women (Sarton 2000, 20 healthy volunteers, arterial sampling, 100 µg/kg bolus then
30 µg/kg/h for 1 h; PDF p10); Skarke 2003 pain tolerance 2.8 h (Table 1) but 2.6 h in the text (PDF p10). The
text also reports Murthy 2002 (heat pain, 8 volunteers, 10 mg IV) at **0.16 h**, a morphine/M6G hybrid,
which is not in Table 1 (PDF p11, printed S100). Dershwitz 2000 (pupil) is 3.85 h in the text, 3.9 h in the table.
No tPeak values are given. Hysteresis: remifentanil gave counter-clockwise loops on arterial and clockwise
on venous concentrations (Hermann 1999; PDF pp8-9).

**EC50 (no MEAC is reported anywhere in the review).** Units in Table 1 are nmol/L; the text gives some in
ng/mL. Morphine: pupil 4.8 ng/mL (Dershwitz; Table 1 17 nM; derived: 17 nM × 285.3 g/mol = 4.85 ng/mL),
36.2 nM (Lötsch 2001; Table 1 says 34), 26.4 nM (Skarke 2003; Table 1 says 27); pain tolerance 76 ng/mL men vs
33 ng/mL women (Sarton; Table 1 268/115 nM; derived 76.5/32.8 ng/mL). Assay basis (total plasma vs whole
blood, arterial vs venous) is not stated for most studies; Sarton used arterial blood. M6G: 25% increase in pain
tolerance at 275 nM effect-site (Romberg 2004, 10 Hz) vs 500 nM (Skarke, 5 Hz). Methadone pain relief EC50
0.29 ± 0.38 µg/mL (Inturrisi 1987) and 0.359 ± 0.158 µg/mL (Inturrisi 1990); R-methadone pupil 2.3 ng/mL.
Remifentanil experimental pain EC50 4.66 ng/mL (Glass 1993); remifentanil EEG EC50 by age 13.1 − 0.148·(age − 40)
ng/mL and ke0 0.595 − 0.007·(age − 40) /min (Minto 1997, PDF p9). Alfentanil PaCO2 EC50 60.3 µg/L (Bouillon 1999,
no ke0). Piritramide 12.1 ng/mL, γ 1.9 (PDF p11).

**Potency ratios (all from the text; end point in brackets).** Fentanyl 75× alfentanil [EEG spectral edge; EC50
6.9 vs 520 ng/mL] (PDF p7); sufentanil 12× fentanyl [EEG] (PDF p7); remifentanil 19× alfentanil [EEG] (PDF p8);
M6G 22× less potent than morphine [pupil; 740.5 vs 36.2 nM] and needing 2.8× the dose (PDF p10); morphine
twice as potent in women as in men [pain tolerance] (PDF p10). No equianalgesic dose table.

## Covariates
Only as reported from source studies: age on fentanyl/alfentanil dose requirement (50% lower from age 20 to 89,
via brain sensitivity, Scott 1987); age on remifentanil EC50 and ke0 (Minto 1997, equations above; age and lean
body mass on PK); sex on morphine potency and t½,ke0 (Sarton 2000).

## Caveats
A narrative review: values are second-hand, and the text and Table 1 disagree in several places (morphine pain
tolerance 2.6 vs 2.8 h, Dershwitz 3.85 vs 3.9 h, Lötsch 2001 EC50 36.2 vs 34 nM and M6G 740.5 vs 743 nM,
methadone 7.7 ± 3.6 min vs a harmonic mean of 3.6 min). The morphine range's extremes (1.6, 4.8 h) are
sex-specific values from one small experimental-pain study in healthy volunteers. Morphine values are mostly
pupil and experimental pain, not clinical analgesia. No MEAC, no tPeak.
