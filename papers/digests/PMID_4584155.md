# Reidenberg 1973: Hydralazine elimination in man

## Study
Four experiments. Exp 1 in vitro stability of hydralazine in blood/plasma (PDF p4). Exp 2 in vivo: obese, otherwise healthy hospitalised volunteers (Table I weights 76-150 kg), 4 given hydralazine IV (20 or 25 mg) and 9 given oral hydralazine (75 or 100 mg, 25 mg tablets); each also given 100 mg oral dapsone on another day (3 h plasma MADDS/DDS ratio) to phenotype acetylator status (Table I; one subject, SI, confirmed rapid by isoniazid half-life 1.4 h). Multiple heparinised blood samples, plasma hydralazine by a modified Schulert colorimetric method (4 ml plasma, protein precipitation with sulfosalicylic acid, p-hydroxybenzaldehyde derivative, chloroform extraction, absorbance 365 nm, duplicate). Half-life graphical from a semilog plot; Co is the line extrapolated back to t = 0. Exp 3 authentic 3-methyl-s-triazolo[3,4-a]phthalazine (MTP) in rabbits/rat (low recovery). Exp 4 plasma hydralazine in 30 hypertensive outpatients on 2-4 tablets of 25 mg daily (Fig 2, vs BUN and time since dose). Exp 5 13 subjects, MADDS/DDS vs isoniazid half-life (Fig 3). No compartmental model, no software.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Table I (PDF p4, printed 973) gives per-subject graphical values; there are no population means except the text's mean Vd of 46% of body weight.

IV subjects (Table I): Go 135 kg, MADDS/DDS 0.63 (R), 20 mg, T/2 2.2 h, Co 0.31 ug/mL, Vd 64 L; Ka 118 kg, 1.4 (R), 20 mg, 3.1 h, 0.58, 34 L; Re 102 kg, 0.19 (S), 25 mg, 5.8 h, 0.36, 69 L; Ko 95 kg, 0.11 (S), 25 mg, 3.3 h, 0.66, 38 L. Check (derived: dose/Co) gives 64.5, 34.5, 69.4, 37.9 L, so Vd = dose/Co. Text (Discussion, PDF p7, printed 976) gives Vd as 29%, 40%, 47%, 68% of body weight, mean 46%, so about 0.46 L/kg (derived: 34/118, 38/95, 64/135, 69/102 agree).

Oral subjects (Table I), T/2 h, Co ug/mL (back-extrapolated), MTP in 24-h urine mg: Go 100 mg 2.5, 0.38, <1.0; Ke 100 mg 2.5, 0.44, 5.7 (kg 88); Pa 100 mg 3.3, 0.49; SI 100 mg 7.8, 0.24, 1.3 (kg 150, rapid by INH); Us 100 mg 2.4, 0.91, 3.6; Ho 100 mg 3.5, 0.57, 2.9; Cz 75 mg 3.8, 0.32; McG 75 mg 2.0, 0.64; McI 100 mg 2.6, 1.40, <1.6. Table II gives the measured plasma concentrations (0.05-0.64 ug/mL at 3-9 h after oral doses; IV 0.27 and 0.44 ug/mL at 1 h in Go and Ka).

Derived apparent clearance, Vd x ln2 / T/2 (derived; Vd here is dose/Co, not a central volume, so only an order of magnitude): Go 20 L/h (0.15 L/h/kg), Ka 7.6 L/h (0.064), Re 8.2 L/h (0.081), Ko 8.0 L/h (0.084).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect relation. Text (PDF p7): flushing and headache similar after IV 20-25 mg and oral 75-100 mg, which the authors say matches the custom of treating them as equieffective doses. No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio (not an opioid).

## Covariates
- Acetylator phenotype (MADDS/DDS from dapsone): IV half-life and concentrations similar in rapid and slow; oral concentrations lower in rapid acetylators (first-pass acetylation). Using Vd mean 46% of weight, fraction of oral dose reaching plasma as hydralazine (derived by authors: Vd x Wt x Co / dose) 22% in rapid, 38% in slow acetylators.
- Renal function: higher plasma concentrations in azotemic hypertensives (Fig 2); one uremic patient (BUN 108) had half-life about 14 h from two samples (3 h 0.44, 5.5 h 0.40 ug/mL).
- Half-life ranges stated in the abstract: 2.2-7.8 h rapid, 2.0-5.8 h slow acetylators.

## Caveats
- Assay is a colorimetric derivative method; the paper does not address whether hydralazine pyruvic-acid hydrazone or other hydrazones also react (later work attributed the long half-life and low Vd to this).
- Subjects were obese (76-150 kg), so per-kg values are low for lean body size; n = 4 IV subjects.
- Vd = dose / Co from a terminal-phase back-extrapolation, not a central compartment; half-lives graphical, no distribution phase fitted. In vitro degradation in plasma (0.07 ug/mL/h) means sample handling affects results.
- Two outlier values flagged in Table II (Ke, 7 h, 0.16 ug/mL: duplicates 0.11 and 0.22, probably contamination).
