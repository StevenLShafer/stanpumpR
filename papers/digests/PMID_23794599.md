# Rincón 2013: cefazolin inoculum effect in South American MSSA

## Study
Microbiological surveillance, no patients dosed and no PK.
- 364 MSSA clinical isolates from two prospective multicentre studies: 2001-02 (15 Colombian centres) and
  2006-08 (32 hospitals: Colombia 22, Ecuador 5, Peru 3, Venezuela 2). Bloodstream n = 296, osteomyelitis n = 68
  (bone biopsy cultures). Species and mecA confirmed by multiplex PCR.
- Cefazolin MIC by CLSI broth microdilution (cation-adjusted Mueller-Hinton II) at standard inoculum (~10^5 CFU/mL)
  and high inoculum (~10^7 CFU/mL), read at 24 h by two researchers; inocula checked by colony count.
- **Inoculum effect (InE) definition**: MIC ≥16 mg/L at high inoculum with ≤8 mg/L (susceptible) at standard
  inoculum (Methods, PDF p2). Controls: TX0117 (type A high producer), ATCC 29213, ATCC 25923.
- PFGE on all InE isolates; MLST and partial blaZ sequencing (residues 128-216) on selected isolates.

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Results (rendered pages confirmed):
- Overall InE 131/364 = 36% (abstract; Results PDF p3): 97/296 bloodstream (33%), 34/68 osteomyelitis (50%).
- All isolates susceptible at standard inoculum, highest MIC 2 mg/L.
- 28 isolates had high-inoculum MIC ≥128 mg/L (18 blood, 10 bone).

Table 1 (PDF p3, printed p2775), the 131 InE isolates, MIC in mg/L, SI / HI:

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: cefazolin MIC at 10^7 vs 10^5 CFU/mL in vitro. No clinical outcomes, no MBCs, no concentration targets.
The discussion states cefazolin's half-life as 1.8 h (uncited aside, PDF p4) and cites prior prevalence of 19%
(Nannini 2009, 19/98) and 27% (Livorsi 2012, which used a ≥4-fold-rise definition).

## Covariates
Not applicable. Clinical source (bone > blood) and country differed in prevalence.

## Caveats
- Regional sample; authors say the findings cannot be generalised to all hospitals in the region.
- InE definitions differ between studies (this paper ≥16 mg/L at HI; Livorsi ≥4-fold rise), so prevalences are
  not strictly comparable.
- MIC only (no MBC, no time-kill); clinical relevance untested, as the authors note.
