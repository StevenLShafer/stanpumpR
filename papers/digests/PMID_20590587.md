# Samer 2010 (PK paper): CYP2D6 genotype and CYP2D6/3A inhibition vs oxycodone PK

## Study
Randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, placebo-controlled 5-arm crossover (Latin square, washout ≥ 1 week).
10 healthy non-smoking men, mean age 26 y, 76.4 ± 8.1 kg, 181.2 ± 5.4 cm. CYP2D6 by AmpliChip and
dextromethorphan/dextrorphan ratio: 6 EM, 2 UM (*1/*2xN, *41/*41xN), and 2 "deficient" analysed together as "PM":
one *4/*4 PM and one *5/*41 IM. Pretreatment quinidine 100 mg, ketoconazole 400 mg, both, or placebo; 1 h later
midazolam 75 µg + dextromethorphan 2.5 mg microdoses; 1 h later oral oxycodone HCl 0.2 mg/kg drops (mean 15.3 mg,
13-18 mg) or placebo. Naloxone 0.8 mg IV given 1.5 h after oxycodone in every session. Plasma at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 6,
24 h. LC-MS/MS, LLOQ 100 pg/mL oxycodone and oxymorphone, 50 pg/mL noroxycodone and noroxymorphone. Noncompartmental
(WinNonlin 4.01).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured in this paper (companion paper PMID 20590588). Introduction/Discussion cite oxymorphone 14× more potent
than oxycodone (Chen 1991), µ affinity 40× oxycodone and 3× morphine; noroxymorphone 3× oxycodone. No MEAC, ke0 or tPeak.

## Covariates
CYP2D6 phenotype (genotype + dextromethorphan MR); CYP3A activity (midazolam MR) and inhibitors.

## Caveats
n = 10, only 2 UM and 2 "PM" (one of whom is genotypically IM); sparse sampling (7 points in 24 h); naloxone at 90 min
in every session (the authors suggest it may distort curve shapes); weight-based dosing; quinidine 100 mg did not fully
block CYP2D6; men only.
