# Kim 1996: rifampin induction and de-induction of haloperidol in schizophrenic patients

## Study
- 22 Korean inpatients with chronic schizophrenia (DSM-III-R), stable on haloperidol 10-30 mg/day (once daily at night) for at least 3 weeks; BPRS stable for 10 months or more.
- Group I: 17 enrolled, 12 completed (7 men, 5 women; 37.7 +/- 9.0 y; 56.7 +/- 8.8 kg); rifampin 600 mg/day (morning) added for 4 weeks. Two refused on days 1-2; three withdrawn on days 6, 10, 14 for re-emerging psychotic symptoms.
- Group II: 5 patients (3 men, 2 women; 31.6 +/- 8.4 y; 57.6 +/- 7.5 kg) on antituberculous drugs for more than 9 months, switched to rifampin 600 mg/day alone, then rifampin stopped.
- Mean haloperidol doses 12.1 mg/day (group I) and 20 mg/day (group II) (Fig. 1 legend).
- Troughs (before the nightly dose) on days -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 10, 14, 21, 28. HPLC-UV (Miller and DeVane method), plasma, detection limit 1.0 ng/mL, CV 7.6%.
- Clinical: BPRS total and psychiatric subscale, PSS, NSS at weeks -1/0 to 4; responder = 30% or greater change.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Group I: BPRS total rose, not significant (P < 0.20); responders (30% or more change) at day 28: BPRS total 6/12 (50%), BPRS subscale 3/12 (25%), PSS 2/12, NSS 1/12 (Table 2). Group II: no responders on any scale.
- Cited: haloperidol above 9-15 ng/mL unlikely to add symptom reduction (Ferry, ref 15).
- No concentration-effect model, no ke0.

## Covariates
Not analysed.

## Caveats
- Small groups (12 and 5), troughs only, wide haloperidol dose range, no EPS rating, possible CYP2D6/3A mechanism not resolved.
- Rifampin may change both clearance and first-pass; the paper cannot separate them.
- Abstract's day-7 value (41.3%) disagrees with Table 1 and the text (37.4%).
