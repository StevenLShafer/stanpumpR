# Dutta 2004: divalproex ER vs delayed-release relative bioavailability (meta-analysis)

## Study
Meta-analysis (Abbott) of five multiple-dose crossover studies of divalproex extended-release (Depakote ER, once
daily) vs divalproex delayed-release (Depakote, enteric; Q6H, Q8H or Q12H). Healthy volunteers: Studies 1 (n = 14,
1000 mg/day), 3 (n = 33, 1000 mg/day) and 4 (n = 35, ER 1000 or 1500 vs DR 875 or 1250 mg/day); epilepsy patients
on enzyme-inducing co-medication: Studies 2 (n = 22, 1000-3000 mg/day) and 5 (n = 64, ER 1000-5000 vs DR 875-4250
mg/day). Fasting and non-fasting (300-660 kcal) conditions (Table 1). Steady-state 24-h AUC, Cmax and Cmin of
plasma total valproic acid; log-AUC differences weighted by inverse variance, random effects (REML), SAS PROC
MIXED; Studies 4-5 dose-normalised; Cmin by bivariate meta-analysis with Fieller CI.

Per-study AUC ratios ranged 0.77-0.97 (equal-dose comparisons); Table 5 by DR frequency: Q6H 0.944, Q8H 0.873,
Q12H 0.891 / 0.905 (no consistent pattern). The 0.89 meta-estimate underpins the Depakote ER label's conversion
advice (ref 2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None modelled. Cites the usually accepted therapeutic window for plasma total valproate, 50-100 mg/L (Chadwick
1985). Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Food and DR dosing frequency had no material effect on relative bioavailability. Enzyme-induced patients (Studies
2 and 5) not analysed separately for the meta-estimate.

## Caveats
Sponsor analysis of sponsor studies; mixed populations (healthy, induced epilepsy patients); Study 3 "nonfasting"
was dosing just before a light breakfast. Table 3 Cmin upper CI (1.20) conflicts with the abstract (1.02); the
latter fits the Table 5 values. Relative, not absolute, bioavailability; no ER absorption rate given.
