# Biesenbach 2018: serum and urine magnesium after a 20 mmol bolus after cardiac surgery

## Study
Prospective, uncontrolled; 20 consecutive adults in the Austin Hospital ICU (Melbourne) after elective cardiac
surgery, August 2016, given IV magnesium at the clinician's decision; pre-existing or acute renal failure and
baseline Mg >= 1.5 mmol/L excluded (PDF p2). Table 1 (PDF p3, rendered): age 70 (IQR 65-76), 4 (20%) female, weight
79 (73-96) kg, creatinine 71 (62-83) umol/L, baseline Mg 1.04 (0.94-1.23) mmol/L, urine output 90 (45-165) mL/h;
19 for arrhythmia prevention, 1 for AF. Intervention: 20 mmol magnesium sulfate (Hospira) through a central line
over 1 hour. Serum and spot urine before, at 60 min, 6 h and 12 h after the start. Six had intraoperative
magnesium; cardioplegia contained 16 mmol/L Mg (median 4,236 mL). Non-compartmental calculations; SPSS.

Derived here, not in the paper: a 3.0-h half-life in 26.6 L implies a total clearance of about 6.1 L/h (102 mL/min),
far above the measured urinary clearance of 10-20 mL/min; the paper does not reconcile the two (distribution out of
the sampled space after 1 h is the obvious contributor). The Discussion's implied dosing: about 25 mmol to raise
serum Mg by 1 mmol/L, about 3 mmol/h to hold a rise (PDF p4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. 5 patients had AF in ICU (3 with prior AF); Mg at baseline and 60 min did not differ by AF; onset 22
(7-24) h after the dose (PDF p3). No target concentration defined.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
n = 20, no control group, four sampling times, normal renal function only, variable time from ICU admission (Table 1
prints 4.9 (5-14.1) h, a median outside its own IQR). Table 2 prints dose per kg in mg/kg where mmol/kg is meant.
"Additional urinary excretion of 7 mmol/L over baseline" (PDF p3) has an odd unit. Published 2018 (e-pub 2017).
