# Hiemke 2011: AGNP consensus guidelines for TDM in psychiatry, update 2011

## Study
Consensus guideline from the TDM group of the AGNP (German-speaking working group for neuropsychopharmacology and
pharmacopsychiatry), 27 authors, 41 PDF pages (printed 195-235), updating the 2004 consensus. Literature review by
structured process; for each neuropsychiatric drug it gives a therapeutic reference range, half-life, laboratory
alert level, a level of recommendation for TDM (1-4), a mass-to-molar conversion factor, and dose-related
concentration factors. Not a primary PK study; no patients of its own.

Definitions used for the quetiapine row (text layer: the reference-range, alert-level, recommendation-level and
blood-sample-collection sections):
- **Therapeutic reference range**: lower limit below which response is relatively unlikely, upper limit above which
  tolerability falls or further gain is unlikely; population-based and "orienting", for the primary indication.
- **Laboratory alert level**: concentration above the range at which the laboratory tells the prescriber at once;
  based on intolerance/intoxication reports but in most cases set arbitrarily at twice the range's upper limit.
- **Level 2, "Recommended"**: ranges from plasma concentrations at therapeutically effective doses, related to
  clinical effects, with reports of decreased tolerability or intoxication at supratherapeutic concentrations.
- **Sampling**: trough at steady state, usually one week after stable dosing and just before the morning dose,
  12-16 h after the last dose (24 h if dosed once daily in the morning). For extended-release forms such as
  quetiapine, the time of intake must be taken into account (blood sample collection section; text layer).
- Matrix: plasma or serum (no consensus which); whole blood abandoned for psychiatric TDM.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point of the range: clinical response and tolerability in the primary indication (population-based), not a
concentration-effect model. The text cites PET: optimal antipsychotic response at 70-80% D2 occupancy, 80% as the
threshold for extrapyramidal effects (printed p. 204). No quetiapine-specific occupancy value, EC50, ke0 or potency
ratio in the parts read. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
None quantified for quetiapine. P-gp (ABCB1) genotype is said to influence quetiapine concentrations and
effectiveness in schizophrenia (ref. 470, printed p. 201). Ranges apply to adults without relevant comorbidity.

## Caveats
- A consensus table: the quetiapine range is level 2, from effective-dose concentrations rather than controlled
  concentration-response trials.
- Table 4's quetiapine C/D factors do not follow from its own formula c = De/Clt with the listed clearance (derived:
  1 mg/day ÷ (1146 mL/min × 1440 min) = 0.61 ng/mL per mg; ÷ 2421 mL/min gives 0.29), 2-3 times the printed
  0.13-0.21. Not used by stanpumpR; noted only as an internal inconsistency of the source.
- Only the parent drug; norquetiapine is not in the range.
