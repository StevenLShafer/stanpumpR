# Müller 2021: oral THC and the MAC of sevoflurane in rats

## Study
Randomised, observer-blinded animal study (Vienna, 2019). 38 adult Wistar rats (19 male 396-511 g, 19 female 235-284 g; 81-95 days old), 19 per group (A: 10 F + 9 M; B: 9 F + 10 M). Group A: oral dronabinol (2.5% formulation) 10 mg/kg by gastric gavage under brief sevoflurane (3.2 vol%, 5 min); group B: sunflower oil placebo. MAC measured 2 h later in an airtight chamber (O2 3 L/min) with Dixon up-and-down design, first rat 2.4 vol%, step 0.2 vol%, 5 min + 10 min equilibration, tail clamp (third lock position, max 1 min); positive = gross movement (p. 59-60). MAC by isotonic regression, 95% CI by bootstrap. Serum THC from cardiac puncture, GC-MS/MS, validated 1-100 ng/mL (p. 60). Sample size from simulation (placebo MAC 2.4%, SD 0.4%, 20% reduction, ~80% power at 19/group).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: movement to tail clamp (MAC, rat). 26% MAC reduction as reported (2.8 to 2.1 vol%; derived from the rounded values 0.7/2.8 = 25%, so the 26% presumably comes from unrounded estimates). No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis reported; the 2 h wait is assumed from rat literature (ref 22) to match peak serum/brain THC. No EC50 or MEAC; serum THC varied widely (4.3-77.5 ng/mL) and no concentration-effect analysis is reported (Fig. 2 shows per-animal concentration with response). Not an opioid; no potency ratios.

## Covariates
Both sexes pooled; sex not analysed separately.

## Caveats
Rat data; 10 mg/kg far above human doses (1-20 mg total); serum levels about twice those of humans on 40 mg TID dronabinol (authors, p. 62). Body temperature not measured; THC-induced hypothermia could lower MAC (authors). Large variability in absorption (gavage after short fast, sedated). Single dose, THC-naive animals; chronic exposure unstudied. Funded by Medtronic educational grants.
