# Zuo 2020: population PK/PD of IV zanamivir

## Study
- Pooled 8 GSK studies: six phase I healthy-volunteer studies, phase II NAI113678 and phase III NAI114373 in hospitalised patients with suspected or confirmed influenza (Table 1, p.160; Table S2 not in PDF).
- 658 subjects (125 healthy adults, 533 hospitalised adults and children; 58 paediatric/adolescent), 5273 serum samples. IV infusion single doses 100-1200 mg and 300-600 mg b.i.d. repeat dosing; renal-impairment doses adjusted to CrCL for 5-10 days. Weight median 72 kg (range 7.1-188); age down to 0.6 y (Table 1).
- Assay: serum zanamivir by protein precipitation and LC-MS/MS; LLOQ 0.02-10 ng/mL depending on study; BLQ imputed at LLOQ (0.01 ug/mL).
- NONMEM 7.2, FOCE-I; full-covariate-model approach (effect ranges 0.8-1.2 categorical, -0.2 to 0.2 continuous); pcVPC, 1500 bootstraps (1187 successful). CrCL by Cockcroft-Gault (adults) or Schwartz (<13 y).
- PD: time-to-event Cox models (536 patients) for clinical response and virologic end points, and viral-load change plots, against post hoc Cmax, AUC0-tau and Ctau.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, IV infusion, first-order elimination. CL driven by CrCL as a stepwise linear function: 1 above 97 mL/min, 1 + (CrCL - 97) x slope below 97 (p.162). Table 2 (printed p.162, rendered page confirmed). Reference individual: healthy, 70 kg, CrCL above 97 mL/min.

Derived/reported quantities: typical Vss = V1 + V2 = 18.8 L at 70 kg (text p.167); CL 5.16 L/h (85.9 mL/min) for an infected 70-kg subject; t1/2 3.2 h normal renal function, 8.93 h in end-stage renal disease (p.163). Zanamivir protein binding below 10%. RRT and ECMO effects on CL were dropped (Figure 1).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No effect-site model, ke0 or hysteresis, and no MEAC. Not an opioid.
- In vitro IC90 (virus neuraminidase inhibition): A/H1N1 0.00173 ug/mL (Methods) or 0.00176 (Table 3 footnote), A/H3N2 0.00171, influenza B 0.00783 ug/mL; max values 5.31 nM (A/H1N1) and 23.55 nM (B) as used for the comparison (p.163). Serum concentrations were scaled for 10% protein binding.
- Table 3 (p.164): percent of infected subjects with steady-state trough above IC90 (A / B): 300 mg q.d. 79.4 / 74.7; 300 mg b.i.d. 96.4 / 95.5; 600 mg q.d. 82.9 / 78.8; 600 mg b.i.d. 97.7 / 97.0.
- Exposure-response: no exposure measure (Cmax, AUC0-tau, Ctau) significantly affected time to clinical response or virologic end points; ICU/ventilation was the dominant factor (about 4-fold lower response rate). Epithelial lining fluid reached about 65% of serum (cited, p.167).

## Covariates
CrCL on CL (above); influenza infection on CL (x0.756) and on IIV(CL); weight on V1, V2 and Q; study NAI114346 on V1 and V2. RRT and ECMO dropped. Age and sex not retained.

## Caveats
- Few patients under 2 y and with severe or end-stage renal impairment (authors); only 11% paediatric.
- The 24% lower CL in influenza is against healthy volunteers in the same model (reference individual), not against another population.
- Time-to-event models are retrospective, exposure range narrow (troughs mostly above IC90), so no concentration-effect relation was identifiable.
- Weight effect on V1/V2 appears as a 0.711 estimate whose functional form (power exponent on WT/70) is not spelled out in the text layer.
