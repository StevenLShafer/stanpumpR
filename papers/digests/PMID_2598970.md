# Guerret 1989: intravenous and oral nicardipine with a stable isotope

## Study
Six healthy male volunteers, 24-27 y, mean weight 72 (SD 5) kg, non-smokers (p2, printed 382). Latin-square
crossover, sessions 15 days apart, fasting: (a) 2 mg unlabelled (D0) nicardipine hydrochloride IV; (b) 20 mg D0
nicardipine hydrochloride orally, then 1.25 h later 2 mg deuterium-labelled (D3) nicardipine hydrochloride IV.
IV doses diluted in 20 mL isotonic glucose and given over 5 min by syringe pump. Venous plasma at 0.5, 10, 15, 20,
30, 45 min and 1-12 h (plus 0.5 and 1 h after the oral dose). GC-MS (negative chemical ionisation), D7 internal
standard, limit 0.05 ng/mL; reproducibility 15.4/12.5% at 0.25 ng/mL and 6.5/6.0% at 5 ng/mL (D0/D3). IV data fitted
to a three-compartment model (extended least squares, PHARM program, zero-order input); oral data to two
compartments with zero-order absorption. CL = dose/AUC; Vss by moments; F = AUCpo Div / (AUCiv Dpo).

## Model and parameters
Individual three-exponential fits; the paper prints only the exponents (M, alpha, beta), terminal half-life, AUC,
CL and Vss, with no coefficients or compartmental volumes, so no compartment model can be rebuilt from it.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured: no haemodynamic end point, no ke0 or tPeak, no concentration-effect relation.

## Covariates
None (six homogeneous young men).

## Caveats
n = 6 healthy young men; sampling to 12 h only; per-subject exponents vary widely (subject 1's beta 0.06/h in
Table 2). Dose is the hydrochloride. Table 2's Vss is misprinted. The hepatic extraction of about 0.90 (p4) is
inferred from F and complete absorption reported by another study, not measured.
