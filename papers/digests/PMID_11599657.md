# Erdlenbruch 2001: cisplatin PK in children and nephrotoxicity

## Study
Prospective PK/nephrotoxicity study, University of Goettingen. 12 children and adolescents with solid tumours (5 male, 7 female), age 5-19 y (mean 11), weight 18.2-67.9 kg, BSA 0.78-1.80 m2 (Table 1, PDF p. 2). Normal renal and hepatic function before entry. 25 courses (1-4 per patient) analysed. Cisplatin was given with other cytostatics (ifosfamide, methotrexate, etoposide, etc.) by one of: 72-h continuous infusion (120 mg/m2, osteosarcoma, patients 2-5), single 6-h infusion (75 mg/m2, patient 1), repeated 1-h infusions (3 x 40 mg/m2 over 3 days in medulloblastoma, patients 6-11; 5 x 20 mg/m2 over 5 days in dysgerminoma, patient 12; first course 5 x 15 mg/m2). Hydration 3 L/m2/day from 12 h before to 48 h after, plus mannitol.
Sampling: heparinised plasma pre-dose, at end of infusion, several times over 8 h and at 24 h from start (plus during the 72-h infusion); times not exact. Urine collected as often as possible. Free Pt: plasma deproteinised by perchloric acid (recovery about 90% of ultrafiltrate values in vitro, CV < 6%); total Pt also measured but only free Pt reported. Pt by flameless atomic absorption (265.9 nm), lower limit of quantitation 0.03 ug/mL, CV < 5% urine, < 10% undiluted plasma. Two-compartment model fitted to plasma and urinary renal elimination with TopFit. Nephrotoxicity from creatinine clearance and urinary marker proteins; mean follow-up 8.3 +/- 5.9 months.

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, free (non-protein-bound) platinum. Table 2 (PDF p. 4, printed p. 396, confirmed on the rotated page image) gives per-patient values for one representative course each; means below. Mean (SEM) n = 12 representative courses; n = 25 all courses in brackets.
- t1/2a 0.36 +/- 0.16 h [0.31 +/- 0.17] (text: 21.6 +/- 9.6 min)
- t1/2b 25.87 +/- 16.21 h [26.72 +/- 17.99]
- Total clearance Clt 135 +/- 45.3 (mL/min)/m2 [135 +/- 58.4]
- Renal clearance Clr 56.8 +/- 22.8 (mL/min)/m2 [54.0 +/- 23.4]; renal Pt clearance about 0.6 of same-cycle creatinine clearance (text)
- Vss 126 +/- 94.2 L/m2 [122 +/- 77.8]
- AUC (dose-normalised) 13.5 +/- 4.97 [15.5 +/- 9.1] h.ug/mL per 100 mg/m2
- Ae (cumulative urinary Pt) 45.4 +/- 5.7 mg/m2 = 41.7 +/- 6.6 % of dose [44.9 +/- 6.9 mg/m2; 40.9 +/- 5.7 %]
- Cmax 1.52 +/- 1.34 ug/mL [1.52 +/- 1.44]; per-patient range 0.06-4.17 ug/mL, highest with the fastest infusions
- Total clearance about 2.4 times renal clearance (text).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No effect site, ke0 or tPeak. Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio. Toxicity end points: creatinine clearance fell to a mean nadir of 70% of baseline (Table 3: GFR 111 to 77.9 +/- 20.7 mL/min/1.73 m2 during course, n = 25, P < 0.001). Urinary albumin, alpha1-microglobulin, beta-NAG, total protein rose in all courses; serum creatinine and BUN stayed normal. Peak free Pt in plasma and in urine correlated positively with GFR nadir reduction and with peak urinary albumin and beta-NAG (Table 4, Fig. 3; r about 0.4-0.6, P < 0.05); cumulative urinary Pt (Ae) correlated negatively. AUC did not correlate with any marker (Table 4). Higher delivery rate (mg/m2/h) gave higher peak free Pt (r 0.79 plasma, P < 0.001) and lower Ae (r -0.54).

## Covariates
Doses normalised to BSA; no covariate model. AUC per 100 mg/m2 varied up to 2.8-fold at identical doses and schedules. Clearance was not related to infusion schedule. No age or weight analysis of PK.

## Caveats
Small n (12 children, unequal courses), mixed regimens and co-medication (ifosfamide, methotrexate), irregular sampling, maximum urinary Pt probably underestimated for 1-h infusions. Free Pt by perchloric acid precipitation, not ultrafiltration (about 90% of ultrafiltrate values). Cisplatin vs Pt mass basis not stated. No population (NONMEM) analysis; individual fits per course.
