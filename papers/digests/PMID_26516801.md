# Schnider 2016: the safety of target-controlled infusions

## Study
Review of TCI safety after about 20 years of use. Sources: PubMed search (infusion pumps/adverse effects, 1995-2015, plus TCI adverse-event terms), survey of TCI manufacturers (Arcomed, Bionet, Braun, CareFusion, Fresenius, Terumo), German/Swiss/Australian regulator websites, anecdote from the first author's institution (>100,000 TCI patients in 10 years).

## Model and parameters
No new model. Drug-swap simulations (Figs. 1-2) used Schnider propofol with **ke0 0.456/min** and Minto remifentanil, a 40-year-old 70 kg 1.7 m man, propofol 1% and remifentanil 50 ug/mL, effect-site targets 3 ug/mL propofol and 3 ng/mL remifentanil.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Remifentanil model on propofol syringe: initial bolus 1 mL, propofol about 0.6 ug/mL vs target 3 (Fig. 1).
- Propofol model on remifentanil syringe: remifentanil peak about 16 ng/mL vs target 3 (about 8 ng/mL at 25 ug/mL dilution) (Fig. 2).
- Illustrative: propofol Ce 1 ug/mL sedated but conscious; 4 ug/mL likely deeply unconscious (p. 80).
No MEAC, no potency ratio. Remifentanil appears only in the drug-swap simulation.

## Key findings
- 7 publications 1995-Dec 2014 on TCI safety; 4 describe events not specific to TCI (pump drive nut, Diprifusor mechanics); 4 describe prefilled-syringe tag problems (overlapping citations); the only TCI-specific report (Spanish safety system): propofol model chosen for a remifentanil pump.
- Regulators: 5 notifications without recall, including the James LBM formula failing at high BMI for Schnider propofol and Minto remifentanil, so pumps limited to **BMI < 35 kg/m2 in women and 42 kg/m2 in men**; and two implementations of the Schnider model delivering different propofol during induction. Manufacturers: 2 software recalls, no harm.
- TCI drug swap identified as the one TCI-specific hazard, analogous to syringe swap.
- Regulatory proposal: judge a TCI device on whether it delivers the infusion profile defined by target, model and covariates (bench-verifiable), not on measured patient concentrations.
- TCI launched commercially at the **1996 World Congress in Sydney** (p. 83).
- No adverse event attributed to the TCI algorithm; with an assumed 20,000 systems x 200 uses/yr x 5 yr = 20 million uses and the rule of 3, upper 95% bound **< 1:7 million** (p. 84).

## Caveats
Not every manufacturer replied; search may be incomplete. The denominator is a rough assumption. Arithmetic: 3/20,000,000 = 1 in 6.7 million (derived), so the paper's "< 1:7 million" is slightly rounded in the favourable direction. Authors are consultants to Codan / RUGLOOP co-owner (disclosed).
