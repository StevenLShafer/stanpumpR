# Slepchenko 2003: target-controlled sufentanil in morbidly obese patients

## Study
Prospective open-label study, Nice, France. 12 consented, 1 excluded (pump/computer disconnection), **11 analysed** (PDF p.3). Adults 18-60 yr, BMI >35, ASA I-II, laparoscopic gastroplasty. Table 1 (PDF p.3, printed p.67), mean ± SD (range): age 38.7 ± 11.1 (24-55) yr; weight 125.4 ± 23.3 (82-155) kg; BMI 45.0 ± 6.5 (35.0-52.6) kg/m2; sufentanil infusion 216 ± 104 (94-456) min; propofol infusion 253 ± 105 (125-486) min; total sufentanil dose 1.2 ± 0.4 (0.7-2.1) ug/kg; total propofol 18.5 ± 9.1 (8-38) mg/kg. The paper does not say whether the ug/kg dose is per actual or corrected weight. Sex split not tabulated in the text read.

Anesthesia: propofol TCI (Diprifusor, plasma target 6 ug/ml induction, 3 ug/ml maintenance, using corrected weight = IBW + 0.4 x excess weight) plus sufentanil by STANPUMP, **no weight correction for sufentanil**. Sufentanil used the Gepts 1995 PK set (intercompartmental constants and Vc) and the Scott 1991 ke0 (EEG); the ke0 value is not printed. Effect-site target 0.4 ng/ml initially, changed a mean of 4 times (range 2-10) in 0.05 ng/ml steps for MAP/HR change >15%; applied range 0.3-0.65 ng/ml. Reaching 0.4 ng/ml took 6 min.

Sampling: arterial, pre-dose, 2, 5, 15, 30, 45, 60 min then every 15 min to end of infusion, then 5, 10, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90 min, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24 h after (post-infusion sampling mean 711 min, range 240-1,440). Assay: plasma radioimmunoassay after solvent extraction, LOQ 0.02 ng/ml, CV <10% for 0.05-10 ng/ml. Predicted concentrations from STANPUMP simulation (to 2 h after stop). PK analysis: NONMEM, 2- and 3-compartment, zero-order input.

Predictive performance of the Gepts set (STANPUMP) in these patients, Tables 3-4 (PDF p.4, printed p.68), median performance error / absolute error, all patients: during infusion MDPE median -6.8% (range -43.6 to 28.2), MDAPE median 26.3% (7.6-43.6); after infusion MDPE -11.4%, MDAPE 16.9%; whole period (Gepts) MDPE -12.8%, MDAPE 19.8%. Divergence during infusion -3.4 %/h (95% CI -10.2 to 3.1). Wobble (Gepts, whole period) median 15.1%. Note: the table prints the same row labels for "Present Study" (the new NONMEM fit): MDPE median 8.4%, MDAPE 30.8%, wobble 19.8%. The abstract's median PE of -13% (range -42 to 36%) matches the Gepts whole-period column (-12.8%, -42.0 to 35.6), and its MDAPE of 26% during and 17% after infusion match the infusion and post-infusion columns.

Bias vs BMI: MDPE = -2.4 x BMI + 102.4, r2 = 0.52, P<0.05 (Fig. 4, PDF p.6): measured below predicted above about BMI 40. Cl vs BMI (Fig. 6A): Cl = 0.024 x BMI + 0.121, r2 = 0.42; Vc vs BMI (Fig. 6B) r2 = 0.01. No covariate (age, sex, height, weight, BMI) lowered the NONMEM objective function enough to be retained.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t1/2 ke0 or tPeak is reported. The Scott 1991 ke0 was used inside STANPUMP; its value is not given. 6 min to reach a 0.4 ng/ml effect-site target is a TCI ramp time, not a tPeak.
- **No MEAC or analgesic concentration** is reported. Measured plasma concentrations: mean 0.39 ng/ml (range 0.18-0.94) between incision and closure (PDF p.4); 0.13 ± 0.03 ng/ml (range 0.07-0.18) at the start of spontaneous ventilation (plasma, RIA, in propofol 3 ug/ml co-anesthesia). The authors compare this with 0.25 ng/ml from Shafer & Varvel 1991 (their ref 21, an EEG/ventilation-recovery figure), and cite Brunner 1994 for 0.2-0.5 ng/ml reducing isoflurane MAC by at least 60% (PDF p.7). These are cited, not measured here.
- No equianalgesic or potency ratio.
- Clinical events (Table 2, PDF p.3): loss of consciousness 2.6 ± 0.7 min and intubation 6.3 ± 2.4 min after starting; after stopping, spontaneous ventilation 49.6 ± 17.5, eyes open 52.4 ± 18.9, extubation 57.9 ± 19.3 min.

## Covariates
Tested on Cl, Vc, Q, Vp: sex, age, height, body weight, BMI (objective-function criterion 6.61 for P<0.01 then 10.82 in backward elimination). None retained. Cl tended to rise with BMI (Fig. 6A, not significant in the model).

## Caveats
n = 11, all morbidly obese (BMI 35-52.6), no non-obese control group; sufentanil co-infused with propofol and atracurium; post-infusion sampling truncated in some patients (authors' own caveat) which affects the terminal half-life and Vp; very wide 95% CIs (Cl 1.07-3.76, Vc 24.0-109.8) so Table 5 is poorly defined.

## Verification against stanpumpR
Not cited, so nothing to verify as transcription. Independent comparison with `R/drugs_sufentanil.R:46-53` (Gepts two-stage means, unscaled: V1 14.3, V2 63.1, V3 261.6 L; CL1 0.92, CL2 1.55, CL3 0.33 L/min; scaled by FFM-based size factors, lines 28-29):
- The paper's Cl 1.27 L/min is 1.38x the unscaled library CL1 (0.92) (derived), same direction the authors predict (higher Cl with obesity). The library's FFM scaling (clearance ~ FFM ratio^0.75) also raises Cl for a heavy patient, but I did not compute the library's value for these patients.
- The paper's Vc 37.1 L is 2.6x the library's V1 (14.3) (derived). Vc is not size-related in the paper (Fig. 6B). The two-compartment Vc is not comparable with a three-compartment V1 and the authors attribute differences from Gepts to sampling length; do not read it as a correction.
- Paper's overall conclusion: the Gepts set, used unscaled, is adequate to within MDPE -6.8% to -12.8% in these patients, with overprediction growing above BMI 40. The library's FFM scaling is a different baseline from the unscaled Gepts set tested here; the paper does not test it (see open question).
- The library's tPeak 5.6 min (line 58) and MEAC 0.056 ng/ml (line 59) have no counterpart in this paper.
