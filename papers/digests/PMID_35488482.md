# Matsuda 2022: serum midazolam after oral premedication in children

## Study
Single-centre prospective study (Kitasato University, May 2017-Dec 2020; UMIN R000052504). ASA 1-2 children aged
6 months to 8 years for surgery with slow sevoflurane/N2O induction. Midazolam 0.5 mg/kg (max 10 mg) by mouth as a
1 mg/mL syrup made from the injection, 30 min before entering the OR. Sedation scored at mask application on a
modified Wilton 5-point scale (>= 3 = effective). One venous serum sample after induction; HPLC-UV, limit of
detection 10 ng/mL, CV <10%. 104 enrolled; 98 analysed (2 incomplete forms, 4 dosed >60 min before OR entry).
Table 2: 48 M / 50 F; age median 41 months (IQR 18-64); weight 14.3 kg (10.2-18.0); sCr 0.31 mg/dL.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: acceptance of the induction mask (modified Wilton score). No concentration-response relation: sedated and
unsedated children had the same serum concentrations. The Discussion states the serum concentration required for
sedation as 50-150 ng/mL, citing a textbook (ref 11: Coté, Lerman, Anderson, A Practice of Anesthesia for Infants
and Children, Elsevier Saunders 2013, p. 141), and remarks that the measured values were slightly below it while
about 70% were well sedated. Brosius 2003 quoted: 43.4 +/- 19.6 ng/mL 45 min after 0.5 mg/kg of the commercial syrup.
No ke0. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Age and being an only child predicted ineffective sedation; weight and kindergarten excluded as collinear with age.

## Caveats
- Three descriptions of the same comparison: abstract 48.0 vs 49.1 ng/mL; Results text calls 47.6 vs 49.8 "mean";
  Table 3 gives 47.6 vs 49.8 as medians (IQR). The table is taken as the source of record.
- Single post-induction sample; time from dose to sampling not reported precisely (roughly 30-60 min); 1'-hydroxy
  metabolite not measured; HPLC-UV with a 10 ng/mL detection limit; subjective score.
- The 50-150 ng/mL range is quoted, not measured or tested.
