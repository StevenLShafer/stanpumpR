# Ansari 2025: intravenous calcium chloride vs gluconate at cesarean delivery

Page key: printed journal page = PDF page + 120 (printed p. 121 = PDF p. 1).

## Study
- **Design.** Pooled analysis of three Stanford (Lucile Packard) trials in which parturients received IV calcium after
  umbilical cord clamping and had serial venous ionized calcium measured (Methods, printed p. 122 / PDF p. 2):
  1. NCT05973747, IRB 70603: randomized bioequivalence (dose-ratio) trial, 34 parturients, registered 2023-08-03.
  2. NCT03867383, IRB 43076: placebo-controlled pilot trial of 1 g calcium chloride in 40 parturients (PI Carvalho).
  3. NCT05027048, IRB 62206: placebo-controlled trial of 1 g IV calcium chloride in 120 parturients at intrapartum
     cesarean delivery.
- **Bioequivalence trial** (printed p. 122, 124-125 / PDF p. 2, 4-5; Fig. 1 CONSORT read from the page image, PDF p. 4):
  age 18-45 yr, cesarean at >= 34 weeks. Exclusions: severe hypertension (> 160 systolic or > 110 diastolic mmHg) within
  48 h, serum creatinine > 1.0 mg/dL, congenital or acquired cardiac disease, digoxin or calcium channel blocker,
  maternal weight < 55 or > 100 kg, magnesium infusion within 24 h. These exclusions are stated for this trial only.
  123 screened, 34 randomized (Aug 9 to Dec 15, 2023). First phase 1:1: calcium gluconate 2 g (n = 10) vs calcium
  chloride 0.5 g (n = 10), i.e. a 4:1 ratio chosen from anhydrous weights, (430.4 g/mol)/(111.0 g/mol) = 3.9 (printed
  p. 125). Interim analysis after 20 showed gluconate raising ionized calcium more, so the protocol's second phase gave
  calcium gluconate 1.5 g to 14 (13 analyzed; 1 excluded because the clinical team gave extra calcium chloride).
  All doses by 10-min infusion. Samples: baseline, then 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 min after infusion start (Methods; the
  Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 legends say "after a 10-min infusion"), 181 measurements.
- **Pilot trial** (printed p. 122-123): 18-45 yr, >= 2 atony risk factors, 1 g calcium chloride or saline over > 10 min
  from cord clamping; 4 samples per consenting patient (baseline + 3 at arbitrary times over 90 min).
- **Single-center trial** (printed p. 123): laboring patients >= 34 weeks, 18-55 yr, on oxytocin, then cesarean; 1 g
  calcium chloride or saline over > 10 min starting 1 min after cord clamping; 4 samples (baseline + 3 over 90 min).
- **Assay** (all trials): **venous serum ionized calcium**, mmol/L, and pH, from a saphenous vein cannula, Abbott iSTAT
  with EG7+ cartridges. Total calcium was not measured or modelled.
- **Population** (Table 1, printed p. 127 / PDF p. 7, n = 70 including 15 placebo): age 34.2 +/- 6.1 yr; weight
  79.8 +/- 12.0 kg; BMI 30.6 +/- 4.4 kg/m2 (height not reported); gestational age median 39 [38-39] weeks; blood loss
  estimated (n = 23) 800 [600-975] mL, quantitative (n = 47) 796 [614-1,019] mL; second-line uterotonic 18 (26%);
  race: Asian/Pacific Islander 26%, Hispanic/Latina 30%, non-Hispanic Black 6%, non-Hispanic white 34%, other 4%.
  Doses: calcium chloride 1 g 21, 0.5 g 10; calcium gluconate 1.5 g 13, 2 g 10, 3 g 1 (the 3 g patient is not
  explained in the text); saline placebo 15. **Baseline ionized calcium 1.15 +/- 0.05 mmol/L.** All women, all
  pregnant at delivery.
- **Model and software** (printed p. 123 / PDF p. 3): NONMEM 7.5 with PLT Tools, FOCE with interaction; one- and
  two-compartment models with first-order elimination considered; exponential IIV; additive and proportional residual
  error evaluated; covariates by likelihood ratio (3.84 for P < 0.05). Confidence by -2LL profiling and 2,000-sample
  bootstrap (95% CI = 2.5th-97.5th ranks). R 4.2.2. Data, control stream and R code are supplementary files
  (printed p. 130; not archived here).

## Model and parameters
**Observed variable.** The change in venous serum ionized calcium (mmol/L) = each post-dose value minus that patient's
own pre-dose baseline (Statistical Analysis, printed p. 123). The baseline is subtracted, not modelled.

**Structure.** Two-compartment, first-order elimination, zero-order 10-min infusion input; the gluconate:chloride
dose ratio (BE) estimated as a model parameter. Final model omitted the 15 placebo patients (Results, printed p. 125 /
PDF p. 5), so it was fitted to 55 calcium recipients; the 311 measurements from 70 parturients are the **initial**
model's data set (the abstract words this as the data set for the population PK). The number of observations in the
final fit is not given.

**Units of the model (Table 2 footnote, printed p. 127 / PDF p. 7).** Dose in **grams of calcium chloride dihydrate**;
predicted concentration in g (CaCl2.2H2O) per L. A calcium gluconate dose is divided by 3.11 to give the equivalent
calcium chloride dose; predicted g/L are multiplied by 1,000 mmol/147 g to give mmol/L.

Table 2, printed p. 127 / PDF p. 7 (confirmed from the page image). NONMEM estimates with bootstrap 95% CI. **No SE,
%RSE, IIV (omega) or residual-error magnitudes are reported** in the paper; the selected residual-error form is not stated.

Other numbers:
- **Practical kinetics** (Table 3, printed p. 129 / PDF p. 9, confirmed from the page image; values rounded to one
  significant digit): gluconate dose equivalent to 1 g calcium chloride 3 g (2.8-3.5); peak change in ionized calcium
  0.4 mmol/L (0.38-0.42); time to half the peak after the start of a 10-min infusion 30 min (23-40). The abstract gives
  the unrounded peak 0.39 (0.38 to 0.42) mM and half-time 29 (23 to 40) min.
- **Fig. 5** (printed p. 129): predicted change after 1 g calcium chloride over 10 min (2,000 bootstrap models), peak
  just under 0.4 mmol/L at 10 min, about 0.16 mmol/L at 60 min (read from the figure).
- **Slower half-life 109 min**, computed by the authors from Table 2 (Discussion, printed p. 128-129). Derived check:
  lambda1 = 0.2009 /min (t1/2 3.45 min), lambda2 = 0.006331 /min (t1/2 109.5 min) (derived: micro-constants from
  Table 2). The authors state sampling for at least five half-lives would be needed and longer sampling would almost
  certainly give a slower half-life and smaller clearance.
- Gluconate: the visual 3:1 ratio, and 1.5 g gluconate gave nearly identical curves to 0.5 g chloride (printed p. 125).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. The end point is the ionized calcium itself; no ke0, tPeak, EC50 or effect on uterine tone, blood
pressure or ECG was modelled. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC or potency ratio. The "potency" analogue is the dose
ratio 3.11 g gluconate per g chloride (Table 2). Discussion (printed p. 126): calcium chloride insert's maximal rate
100 mg/min, calcium gluconate insert's 200 mg/min; by the 3:1 ratio, 300 mg/min of gluconate equals 100 mg/min of
chloride. Deaths from substituting 1 g gluconate for 1 g chloride in hyperkalemia are cited (ref. 8, MHRA).

## Covariates
Maternal age, weight (kg), blood loss (mL) and venous pH were tested; none improved the fit (printed p. 123, 125).
No size covariate; no covariate equations.

## Caveats
- Only parturients at delivery; the authors list males, the elderly and renal insufficiency as unstudied (printed
  p. 127); magnesium-infusion patients excluded in all their obstetric studies. Children are not studied (all >= 18 yr)
  but are not in the authors' list.
- Only 10-min infusions; the authors note a rapid bolus would give a higher peak, and rapid citrated transfusion would
  reduce the effect by chelation (printed p. 127-128).
- Sampling to 60 min (bioequivalence) or 90 min (clinical trials); elimination not characterized (printed p. 128-129).
- No IIV, SE or residual error reported; uncertainty given only as bootstrap CIs and -2LL profiles (Fig. 4).
- Gluconate products' manufacturer/composition not stated, so the fitted 3.11 applies to whatever products were used.
- Patients were actively bleeding during sampling (median ~800 mL).
- Author note: Shafer is senior author of the source and of stanpumpR.
