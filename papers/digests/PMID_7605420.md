# Olkkola 1994: IV oxycodone kinetics and ventilation in children

## Study
n = 18 healthy ASA 1 children, 2-10 y (Table 1: age 5.4 ± 2.1 y, weight 21.1 ± 7.0 kg), scheduled for extraocular
muscle surgery. Premedication oral liquid flunitrazepam 0.1 mg/kg (max 2 mg); thiopentone 5 mg/kg, pancuronium
0.1 mg/kg, mechanical ventilation, N2O in 30% oxygen with halothane 1-2 vol%; neostigmine and glycopyrrolate at the
end. **No opioid was given as premedication or during anaesthesia** (Discussion, PDF p5) - which is what makes the
ventilatory measurement interpretable.

Thirty minutes after discontinuing anaesthesia and 10 min after stopping supplementary oxygen, a single IV bolus of
**0.1 mg/kg oxycodone hydrochloride** was given into a foot vein for prophylactic analgesia (PDF p2, printed 72).
Samples from a hand vein at 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60, 120, 180, 240 min and 6-8 h; total 15-16.5 mL of blood. Plasma
oxycodone by gas chromatography, lower limit of determination 3 ng/mL, intra-day CV 7% at 52 ng/mL (n = 10).

Monitoring (Cardiocap and Capnomac, Datex): blood pressure every 5 min; ventilatory rate, end-tidal CO2, end-tidal
halothane and SpO2 as the mean of measurements every 10 s, continued until the child no longer tolerated the nasal
sampling tube, usually about 3 h.

**Patient 9 received 10 mg in error, 0.5 mg/kg, five times the intended dose.** She is in Table 1 (marked) and in
the means; the authors state her parameters did not differ from the others'. At 45 s she stopped breathing and was
mask-ventilated for 15 min. Three further patients needed manual ventilation on the correct dose.

## Model and parameters
Individual plasma curves fitted by a multiexponential function, iteratively reweighted non-linear least squares with
reciprocal squared prediction weighting, best fit by Akaike's criterion. **A biexponential function gave the best fit
in every patient**; r between measured and predicted 0.970-0.999. t½1, t½z, V1, Vss and CL by standard methods.
Parameters were computed for all 18; the PK-PD model for only 12, because of a central-monitoring-computer failure
and manual ventilation in four.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
The effect measured is **ventilation, not analgesia**. ETCO2 was modelled as E = E0 + k1 Ce(t) + k2 CET(t), with CET
the end-tidal halothane - a linear model in two drug concentrations, needed because the children were emerging from
halothane anaesthesia. ETCO2 was best described with a delay between plasma oxycodone and effect, giving
**t½ke0 5.8 ± 3.1 min** (Table 2, n = 12); r 0.838-0.979. A sigmoid Emax form was also tried and **its parameters
could not be identified** because of noise in the ventilatory measurements.

Time course of the effect (Figure 3, n = 12): maximum mean ETCO2 and minimum mean ventilatory rate both at **8 min**,
minimum mean SpO2 at **4 min**. Mean of individual maximum ETCO2 8.2 ± 1.0% (range 6.7-10%). The authors' own
conclusion is that 0.1 mg/kg oxycodone "appears to cause greater ventilatory depression than comparable analgesic
doses of other opioids" (synopsis, PDF p1).

### What the 12 ng/mL is, exactly
This is the number other papers cite from here, so it is worth being precise about. Results (PDF p4): "The
theoretical concentration of oxycodone at the time of administering the rescue analgesic, based on the fitted
function describing the pharmacokinetics of oxycodone in individual patients, was 12 ± 8 ng ml-1 (median 10 ng ml-1,
range 3-32 ng ml-1)."

- It is a **plasma** concentration, read off each patient's fitted PK curve at the moment rescue was given. It is not
  an effect-site concentration and not a measured sample.
- The **Discussion nevertheless calls the same number "the mean theoretical concentration of oxycodone at the site of
  action"** (PDF p5). The paper uses one value for both, and the distinction matters here because this paper is also
  the source of a t½ke0 - on a different end point (ETCO2), in a subset of 12.
- The threshold is **onset of pain, not failure of analgesia**: rescue IV diclofenac 1 mg/kg was given at a pain
  score **>= 2**, assessed by a trained observer, in children who were "mostly asleep". Pain scores are not reported
  at all, for that reason.
- It is basis-independent in a way the clearances are not: it is an assay concentration of oxycodone, so the salt
  question below does not touch it.

## Covariates
None identified. The paper's own comparison is with adults: clearance in these children was "almost 50% higher" and
Vss "18% lower" than adult values (Discussion, PDF p5, citing refs 5-7). **The comparator is adult literature, not a
single matched study**, and the adult sources carry their own dose-basis problem (see below), so the 50% is not a
clean size effect. The authors add that halothane anaesthesia lowers hepatic metabolic clearance, so the true
child-adult difference "may be even greater" - which is an argument about direction, not a measurement.

## Caveats
- Children emerging from halothane anaesthesia: ETCO2 and ventilatory rate before oxycodone were already abnormal,
  which the authors attribute to residual halothane and handle by carrying CET in the PD model.
- n = 18 for PK, 12 for PD; one of the 18 received five times the dose and is kept in the means.
- Assay limit 3 ng/mL; concentrations fell below it at 416 ± 94 min, so t½z may be underestimated - the authors say
  so themselves.
- Only 12 of 18 needed rescue, so the 12 ng/mL is from 12 children.
- **Dose basis not stated.** The paper says "oxycodone hydrochloride" and names no product. The same Helsinki group,
  with Seppälä as a coauthor, used Oxanest (the hydrochloride TRIHYDRATE) in Pöyhiä 1991 and Kirvelä 1996, so the
  product was plausibly the same, but this paper does not say so and has no dose column to settle it the way
  Pöyhiä's does. If the 0.1 mg/kg is trihydrate, CL 15.2 mL/min/kg is on the trihydrate and becomes 13.2 on the
  anhydrous-hydrochloride basis stanpumpR doses; if it is the base, 17.0. Nothing in the library depends on this
  today, and **the 12 ng/mL does not depend on it at all**.

## Verification against stanpumpR
**not-applicable** at master c98b281c: no repository location cites this paper yet. Two things it bears on:

- `R/drugs_oxycodone.R` MEAC section sets 12 ng/mL "for consistency with morphine" and, at master, cites no
  measurement for it. This paper reports 12 ± 8 ng/mL at rescue, in children, as a fitted plasma concentration.
  **Decided: cite it** (Shafer, 2026-10-29). The pending commit carries all four caveats into the user-facing text -
  that the children were mostly asleep and in mild pain, that rescue came when an observer scored the pain "at 2 or
  more", that this is "nearer the concentration at which pain returns than a MEAC", and that the value is "fitted
  rather than measured". Checked against `claude/gracious-mirzakhani-432b8e` at `283ed449`: the rescue criterion is
  rendered as two or more, which is what the paper says. **The number is not evidence that 12 ng/mL is right**; it
  is one direct measurement that happens to agree with a value chosen for consistency with morphine, and the help
  page says so, beside Kokki 2012's adult MEC of 21 and MEAC of 45-50 ng/mL.
- `R/drugs_oxycodone.R` takes oxycodone's effect-site equilibration from elsewhere. This paper's t½ke0 of 5.8 min is
  on **end-tidal CO2**, not analgesia, in 12 anaesthetised children, from a linear model that also carries halothane.
  It is a ventilatory ke0 and should not be substituted for an analgesic one without saying so: PK and PD measured on
  different end points, in different populations, do not compose.
