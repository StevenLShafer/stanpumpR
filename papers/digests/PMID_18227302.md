# Johnson 2008: remifentanil-propofol response surfaces tested in surgical patients

## Study
21 adult patients (10 men, 11 women), elective surgery, ASA I-III; age 41.7 yr (sd 17), weight 79 kg (sd 17), height 172 cm (sd 10), BMI 26.6 (sd 5.7) (PDF p4). No chronic opioid users. One patient was on chronic benzodiazepines.
Midazolam 12.5 mcg/kg IV about 10 min before; then remifentanil and propofol infusions, computed to reach target effect-site concentrations (Ce) within 10 min, five randomized groups from high propofol/low remifentanil to the reverse (Table 2, p3). Group infusion rates, mean (sd), mcg/kg/min: propofol 317 (39), 199 (20), 158 (11), 97 (20), 79 (6); remifentanil 0.145 (0.018), 0.337 (0.034), 0.524 (0.038), 0.534 (0.113), 0.691 (0.050) (groups 1-5; overall 0.432 +/- 0.206).
Ce at tracheal intubation (TI), by group: remifentanil 2.5, 6.0, 8.0, 10.8, 14.0 ng/mL (overall 8.0 +/- 4.4); propofol 2.4, 2.3, 1.6, 1.1, 0.9 mcg/mL (overall 1.7 +/- 0.8). Time to LOR 5.6 +/- 2.5 min overall; to TI 9.3 +/- 2.7 min.
Ce were not measured but computed from published PK-PD models: propofol Marsh 1991 (with keo), remifentanil Minto 1997 (I and II), fentanyl Shafer 1990. Fentanyl Ce converted to remifentanil-equivalent Ce with a fentanyl:remifentanil potency ratio of 1:1.2 (ref 17, Lang 1996) (PDF p4).
End points: loss of responsiveness (LOR; OAA/S 2 to 1, shouting and prodding) and its return; heart-rate response (>20% rise) to laryngoscopy and intubation; first rescue fentanyl in the recovery room (25-150 mcg increments, 30 min) compared with the pressure-algometry and electrical-tetany surfaces.

## Model and parameters
Greco response-surface model (Eq. 1, PDF p2): Effect = Emax * U^n / (U^n + 1), U = Cer/C50r + Cep/C50p + alpha * (Cer/C50r)(Cep/C50p). Parameters were fitted to the volunteer data of Kern 2004, except loss of responsiveness, which was refitted here (naive pooled, Matlab; r2 0.82) with the LOR end point OAA/S 2 to 1.

Model performance (Table 3, text layer): LOR induction accuracy 42.3%, bias 42.3%, RMS 31.9%; return of responsiveness 15.7%, 13.6%, 9.3%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- ke0 / tPeak: the propofol model used Marsh PK with keo 0.51 and t-peak 2.7 min (Minto 2003 time-to-peak method) (PDF p7, rendered). Units are not printed beside keo (per min is the usual unit; not stated). No remifentanil ke0 or tPeak is printed (the Minto 1997 model is cited). The authors suspect a misspecified keo of either drug explains the early prediction of LOR during induction.
- No MEAC or minimum analgesic concentration. The end points were on the response surfaces: LOR (OAA/S), laryngoscopy heart-rate response, and surrogate pain (algometry, tetany) versus rescue fentanyl.
- LOR: the model predicted unresponsiveness 2.4 +/- 2.6 min before observed LOR; mean predicted probability of LOR at observed LOR 81% +/- 22%; 10 patients above and 11 below the 95% isobole. Return of responsiveness: predicted 0.6 +/- 5.1 min before observed; mean model probability of response 60% +/- 30%.
- Laryngoscopy: mean predicted probability of no response 89% +/- 5%; observed 81%; 4 patients had a >20% heart-rate rise (23%, 21%, 40%, 33%; Table 4), predicted no-response probabilities 69%, 94%, 78%, 92%.
- Recovery: at first rescue fentanyl, mean probability of no response 23% +/- 35% (algometry surface) and 9% +/- 24% (tetany surface); 4 of 21 needed no fentanyl. Both surfaces judged more stimulating than recovery-room pain.
- Potency ratio: fentanyl:remifentanil 1:1.2 (Ce equivalence, from Lang 1996, an isoflurane-MAC study); the direction, per the paper's wording, is 1 fentanyl Ce to 1.2 remifentanil Ce (the text prints only the ratio 1:1.2). Not derived from this paper's data.
- Midazolam 12.5 mcg/kg: simulated Ce peak 26 +/- 5 ng/mL at 9 min (PDF p8), 9% of the concentration for OAA/S <= 3 and 7% of that for sleep.

## Covariates
None modelled. A patient with BMI 41 on chronic benzodiazepines returned to responsiveness 7 min earlier than predicted; the authors note the propofol set has no size scaling and no models exist for chronic opioid or benzodiazepine use.

## Caveats
n = 21, mixed surgeries, clinician-directed dosing (clustering at emergence); the laryngoscopy surface was built on laryngoscopy alone but tested with intubation; only the upper isoboles (>75%) of the laryngoscopy surface were sampled; midazolam premedication is a confounder; Ce are model-predicted, not measured, so biophase error (keo) enters every comparison.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Propofol-remifentanil interaction** (R/modelInteraction.R:1-18): the repo implements Bouillon 2004 Table 4 for laryngoscopy only, not Kern's surfaces. Johnson's surface is a different parameterisation, so no numbers compare. **Cannot verify.**
- **Propofol tPeak** (R/drugs_propofol.R:276): 1.6 min with Eleveld 2018 PK, from Schnider 1999; this paper used Marsh PK with tPeak 2.7 min. Different kinetic models, so the values are not comparable. **No conflict.**
- **Remifentanil** (R/drugs_remifentanil.R:104-120): tPeak 1.6 min and MEAC 1 ng/mL (typical 1.2, bounds 0.8 and 2.0, matching the drugDefaults_global.csv row) are the library's; the paper gives neither, and its remifentanil Ce at intubation (2.5-14 ng/mL, with propofol) are interaction values, not single-agent MEAC. **Cannot verify.**
