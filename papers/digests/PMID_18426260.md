# Sit 2008: citalopram, escitalopram and sertraline level-to-dose ratios across pregnancy and postpartum

## Study
- Prospective observational case series within the NIMH study Antidepressant Drug Use in Pregnancy
  (NCT00279370), Pittsburgh, 2003-2006 (p1-2). 11 women aged 32-43, with DSM-IV major depression (SCID),
  white except one African American woman on sertraline. Drugs prescribed by their own physicians (not
  allocated): citalopram n = 3 (20-40 mg/d racemic in the text; Table 1 shows 20-50), escitalopram n = 2
  (10-20 mg/d), sertraline n = 6 (50-200 mg/d). Monotherapy or non-interacting co-medication; stable dose
  ≥ 2 weeks before each sample.
- Samples at 20, 30, 36 weeks' gestation, delivery, 2 and 12 weeks postpartum (4-6 weeks postpartum only for
  breast-feeding pairs). Plasma trough levels 8-15 h after the last dose (p2).
- Assay: total (not free) plasma chiral concentrations; citalopram/escitalopram by enantioselective HPLC
  (Cyclobond I 2000 AC; adapted from Rochat 1995) with fluorescence detection; sertraline per Wisner 2006. Limit
  of quantitation 3.0 ng/mL; day-to-day CV 2.6-8.2% (medium/high controls), 5.2-10.0% (low control) (p3).
- Outcome: level-to-dose (L/D) ratio, plasma level (ng/mL) over daily dose (mg/d), unadjusted; adjustment for
  weight and BMI did not change the pattern (p4-5). Mood by SIGH-ADS. No statistics tests reported.
- No PK model, no clearance or half-life estimated in this study (half-lives on p2 are quoted from Sidhu 1997
  and Warrington 1991).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis, no therapeutic range proposed. End point: SIGH-ADS depression score; the
authors report that falls in L/D coincided with worsening depression, and that dose increases restored mood in
2 of 3 citalopram/escitalopram and in 3 sertraline mothers (p4). Desmethylcitalopram activity quoted as 10 times
lower than parent (from Hyttel 1992). Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Weight and BMI adjustment reported not to change L/D trends (numbers not shown).

## Caveats
- n = 3, 2 and 6; many missing cells (NA); delivery and 2-week citalopram values are single women.
- The comparator inside pregnancy is 20 weeks' gestation (already pregnant); the only non-pregnant reference is
  12 weeks postpartum, with 2 citalopram and 1 escitalopram woman (subject 5) contributing.
- Doses were changed during the study by clinicians; L/D assumes linear kinetics (assumed from the literature).
- Trough times 8-15 h vary; one smoker on sertraline (subject 9), one escitalopram woman resumed smoking postpartum.
- Total, not free, concentrations.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Citalopram row** (`pregnancyRegistry.csv:70`; `data-raw/pregnancyRegistry.R:807`): "Sit 2008 (small n)" is
  accurate (3 citalopram women) and no number is attributed to it; the factor comes from Westin. **matches** for the
  citation. The same finding's sentence that the decline is mainly the R-enantiomer while S changes little is
  not supported by Sit within pregnancy: S and R L/D both fall 43% from 20 to 36 weeks (derived from Table 2).
  Against 12 weeks postpartum, S is 0.4 vs 0.5 (−20%) and R 0.8 vs 1.3 (−38%) (derived, different women at the
  two times), and in the escitalopram group S at 36 weeks (2.3) is 0.72 of 12 weeks postpartum (3.2, one woman;
  derived) — so Sit is mixed on the point. The claim is not attributed to Sit; see open question.
  The library's citalopram model plots total (R+S) citalopram, which Sit gives only by enantiomer.
- **Sertraline row** (`data-raw/pregnancyRegistry.R:818-827`, csv:71): no factor applied ("unquantified").
  Sit 2008 is now archived, so the `to_obtain` entry for it can be closed. Its data point the same way as
  Heinonen 2021 and Stika 2022 and against Westin 2017: sertraline L/D 0.5 (20 wk) to 0.3 (36 wk) and 0.2 (delivery),
  0.4 at 12 weeks postpartum; the metabolite L/D rises (0.6 to 0.8). This supports the decision to apply no
  factor. Schoretsanitis's 1.37 ratio for "Sit 2008 (6)" (digests/PMID_32238008_aam.md:49) was not re-derived here.
