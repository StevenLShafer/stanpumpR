# Calvey 1979: neostigmine kinetics and EMG effect during tubocurarine reversal

## Study
- 6 **female** patients, plastic surgery, 22-62 y, 48.5-72.6 kg, no hepatic or renal disease. Premedication nitrazepam 10 mg and diazepam 5-10 mg orally; thiopentone 200-400 mg, tubocurarine 45 mg (patient 3: +15 mg); N2O 60-70% + halothane 0.5%, IPPV.
- Reversal: neostigmine methylsulphate **5.0 mg** with atropine 1.2 mg injected over 15 s (68.9-103.0 ug/kg).
- Venous samples at 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 min. Plasma GLC (nitrogen detector) after iodide-complex extraction (Chan 1976). Red-cell acetylcholinesterase radiometric. EMG: adductor pollicis compound action potential and train-of-four decrement.
- Biexponential fits per patient; two-compartment parameters by standard methods (Gibaldi & Perrier).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- EMG facilitation (compound action potential amplitude, decrement) within 2 min; usually **maximal between 7 and 15 min** (patient 1 the exception; patients 1, 5, 6 maximum potentiation only at 25-30 min). End point is reversal of tubocurarine block, not a fitted ke0.
- Negative correlation of central-compartment amount with CMAP amplitude (patients 1, 4, 5, 6 significant).
- Red-cell AChE: almost completely inhibited by 2-3 min; 10.2% at 10 min, 28.3% at 30, 55.2% at 60 min.
- No ke0, EC50 or MEAC; not an opioid; no potency ratio.

## Covariates
None (per-kg normalization only).

## Caveats
Six patients, first sample 2 min (fast phase extrapolated), halothane may reduce hepatic/renal flow, huge spread in Vc (0.11-10.86 mL/kg) attributed by the authors to uneven early mixing; dose given as methylsulphate. Assay calibration details are in the cited Chan 1976 paper, not here.
