# Ashraf 2024: CYP2D6 activity score and codeine-to-morphine formation (popPK, n = 997)

## Study
- Prospective trial, 1000 ambulatory surgical adults, Jorvi Hospital, Finland (2016-2018); 997 analysed (p. 1551).
- Age 47.8 (SD 12.9) y, weight 79.8 (13.8) kg, BMI 25.9; ASA 1/2/3 = 513/424/60 (Table 1). Sex not given in the main text.
- Oral premedication: codeine 60 mg + paracetamol 1000 mg (combination tablets). The salt (phosphate vs base) is not stated in the main text.
- Sparse sampling: 2 venous samples per patient, at 20-60 min and 180-360 min (p. 1548).
- Assay: LC-MS/MS of codeine, morphine, C6G, M6G (M3G also modelled); LOQ 0.05 ng/mL (M6G 0.1); CV < 11% (p. 1549).
- Genotyping: TaqMan + copy number, or targeted NGS; 13 alleles + CNV; 64 genotypes observed. Phenotype from activity score (AS): PM = 0, IM 0 < AS < 1.25, NM 1.25-2.25, UM > 2.25; *10 scored 0.25 (Table 1 footnotes). Group n: PM 37, IM 268, NM 629, UM 65 (Table 1).
- NONMEM 7.4.3, FOCE-I, ADVAN13, PsN; SIR for uncertainty.

## Model and parameters
One-compartment codeine with first-order absorption and F; codeine elimination split into CYP2D6 (ke x fmor -> morphine) and non-CYP2D6 [ke x (1 - fmor) -> C6G]; one-compartment morphine, C6G and M3G; 60% of morphine elimination fixed to M3G (Fig. 1, p. 1552). CL and V allometrically scaled to the population median weight (exponents in the supplement only). Two-compartment codeine gave no improvement (dOFV +2).

Covariate (p. 1552): fmor = A x exp[B x (ASref - ASi)], ASref = 2, A = reference fmor, B = scaling factor (main-text sign as printed; the exact form, and any odds/logit transform, is in Online Resource 1, not seen). Exponential model retained; power model better by OFV but unstable. Before covariates fmor was 8% with 52% unexplained variability; 45% with phenotype classes, 33% with AS.

Section 3.3 (p. 1552): median (IQR) apparent CYP2D6 activity, as % of total codeine clearance: PM 0.55 (0.34-0.75), IM 6.82 (5.39-8.67), NM 13.8 (10.9-16.7), UM 19.9 (16.8-23.1). AS 3 and 4 vs 1.5 and 2: about 2.1- and 1.69-fold higher activity (p. 1554). *10 heterozygotes showed more activity than *41.

Table 3 (simulated, 60 mg single dose) morphine AUC/F mean (SD), ug.h/L: UM 19 (14), NM 8.7 (7.3), IM 3.7 (3.4), PM 0.98 (1.3); codeine AUC/F about 200-257 mg.h/L across groups (units as printed). Abstract: UM 218% higher morphine exposure than NM.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD model fitted, no ke0, no tPeak.
- Morphine respiratory-depression EC50 9.1 ng/mL (cited from Dahan 2004, ref 31; described as EC50 for analgesia and respiratory depression in 50% of patients) drawn on Fig. 4; Coetzee (ref 30) "theoretical therapeutic window" for morphine 9-14 ng/mL (p. 1557). Both secondary citations; plasma morphine, total.
- No MEAC for codeine or morphine measured; no potency ratio given.
- Only constipation associated with CYP2D6 phenotype among adverse effects.

## Covariates
CYP2D6 AS (continuous, exponential on fmor); weight (allometric on CL and Vc for codeine and morphine). Age, height, ASA, smoking tested, none retained.

## Caveats
- Two samples per patient; absorption phase poorly characterised (ka IIV very large, 3.75).
- One-compartment morphine with CL 357.5 L/h is far above usual IV morphine clearance; formation fraction and morphine clearance are only identifiable as a ratio.
- M3G fraction fixed at 60% (ref 21); C6G fraction assigned as 1 - fmor.
- Simulations: methods say typical 80 kg, Fig. 4 legend says 70 kg.
- Equation sign convention in the main text is ambiguous; supplement not archived.
- Co-administered paracetamol; patients, not volunteers.
