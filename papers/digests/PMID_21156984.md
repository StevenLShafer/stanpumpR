# Moore 2011: reinforcement learning for propofol hypnosis control

## Study
Tutorial ("Special Article") introducing reinforcement learning (RL) for closed-loop depth-of-anesthesia control. It contains no patient data, no PK or PD analysis and no assay. It builds a toy Markov decision process: states S1/S0/S2 = BIS increasing/stable/decreasing (ΔBIS > 0, = 0, < 0); actions = propofol bolus of 0, 10 or 40 mg; reward −1/+1/−1 for S1/S0/S2 (Eq. 2). Watkins' Q-learning with ε-greedy exploration: Q ← Q + α[r + γ·maxQ' − Q] (Eq. 4); suggested ε = 0.1 (reduce over time), α = 0.2 (reduce toward 0), episodes of 1000 steps, stopping when Σ|ΔQ| falls below θ. Learned policy (Table 4): 40 mg in S1, 10 mg in S0, 0 mg in S2.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model. All transition probabilities (Tables 1-2, e.g. S0→S2 0.85 after a 40 mg bolus) and Q values (Table 3) are arbitrary, illustrative numbers chosen by the authors, not estimates from data.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: BIS (as a three-state change variable). No EC50, Emax, gamma or MEAC.
- Only PD-timing statement: an approximately 2.5-minute delay between propofol infusion and peak hypnotic effect is cited as an example of delayed action motivating a large discount factor (PDF p.6, printed 365). No source is given there, no ke0 or t½ke0 is stated, and it is not a measurement in this paper.
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Authors state the example is too coarse for real control; RL is best developed in simulation, since naïve action choice is random.
- No clinical or simulation results are shown.
- The 2.5 min delay is uncited and unspecific (bolus vs infusion, BIS vs other effect).
