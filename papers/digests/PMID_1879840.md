# Bailey 1991: recursive analytical solution and plasma-targeting rate for a CCIP

## Study
Methods (theory) paper; no subjects, no data. Bailey (Emory) and Shafer (Palo Alto VA / Stanford). Received March 1990, revised Sept 1990. Supported by the VA Merit Review program.

Context given by the authors (p. 522-523): computer-controlled infusion pumps (CCIPs) change a zero-order rate every 5-30 s (Delta t); existing CCIPs used Euler difference equations (refs 6, 7, including Shafer's own) or the bilinear Z-transform (Alvis, CACI); exact solutions by Maitre and by Jacobs 1988 existed but were judged too complex for any CCIP.

## Model and parameters
No drug parameters. The disposition function is P(t) = sum of A_i exp(-lambda_i t), the plasma response to a unit bolus (units kg/mL in the paper's notation; Eq. 1). Fig. 1 is the 3-compartment mammillary model (k10, k12, k13, k21, k31; X1-X3 amounts; Eqs. 5-7).

Key equations (p. 523-524):
- Eq. 8: Cp(t) = integral of I(t') P(t - t') dt' (convolution).
- Eq. 9-10: define state variables R_i(t) = integral of I(t') A_i exp(-lambda_i (t - t')) dt'; Cp(t) = sum R_i(t).
- **Eq. 14 (the recursion):** R_i(t + dt) = exp(-lambda_i dt) R_i(t) + A_i I (1 - exp(-lambda_i dt)) / lambda_i, for a constant rate I over the interval.
- Eq. 15: unit-rate response over one interval, Cp(dt)|I=1 = sum A_i (1 - exp(-lambda_i dt)) / lambda_i (from R_i(0) = 0).
- Eq. 16: concentration at t + 2dt with the pump off, Cp(t+2dt)|I=0 = sum exp(-lambda_i dt) R_i(t + dt).
- **Eq. 17 (plasma-targeting rate):** I = (C_T - Cp(t+2dt)|I=0) / Cp(dt)|I=1; if I < 0, the rate is 0 and the plasma falls to target. Described as a modification of Jacobs 1990 (ref 10) and as plain superposition. The rate is computed during interval (t, t+dt) for application over (t+dt, t+2dt).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. The authors state (p. 524) that the equations solve only the central compartment, so effect-site targeting is not possible with them; they cite Sheiner 1979 and Scott & Stanski 1987 for the effect site. No ke0, no MEAC. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Authors' listed disadvantages versus Euler (p. 524): cannot represent concentration-dependent (nonlinear) kinetics; give no peripheral or effect-site concentrations. Advantages claimed: simpler than earlier exact solutions, exact to machine precision, extensible beyond three exponentials, exact infusion rate, variable update interval allowed. Also noted: the recursion lets nonlinear regression fit >1000 consecutive infusions (Shafer et al., fentanyl by CCIP, ref 11).
