# Ogungbenro 2019: simvastatin and metabolites, paediatric population PK

## Study
Secondary analysis of a single-centre, open-label, single-dose, genotype-stratified study (Wagner 2018, ref 15) at Children's Mercy, Kansas City. 32 hyperlipidaemic children/adolescents (study range 8-20 y; 30 of 32 Caucasian); SLCO1B1 c.521TT n=15, TC n=15, CC n=2. Dose: simvastatin 10 mg (8-17 y) or 20 mg (>=18 y), oral. Samples 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 h (venous, plasma). Assay: UHPLC-MS/MS for SV, SVA, HMSV, HMSVA, DHSV; LLOQ 0.5 nM; 40% of SVA values below LLOQ, handled with the M3 method. NONMEM 7.4, FOCEI, log-transformed data; bootstrap n=500; VPC 1000 simulations. Body weights were mostly outside the normal range for age (overweight).

## Model and parameters
Joint model (Fig. 1): SV one-compartment with two parallel absorption processes (lag on the second), each zero-order then first-order; fractions F1 = 1/(1+BA), F2 = BA/(1+BA). Each metabolite one-compartment, with apparent formation and elimination parameters (CL/V ratios, since metabolite volumes are not identifiable). HMSV elimination is saturable (sigmoid Imax). Back-conversion SVA to SV not modelled. Exponential BSV; no correlations. Double-exponential residual error (time-dependent 0-2 h vs 2-8 h for SV, HMSV, DHSV).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured (PK only; no LDL, no CK). Not an opioid; no ke0, MEAC or potency ratio. The authors suggest simulating to match concentrations linked to efficacy in adults but give no target value.

## Covariates
Tested: age, weight, sex, height, LBM, BMI, six SNPs (three SLCO1B1, three CYP3A5). Kept: c.521T>C on CLLA and VSVA (additive; up to 6.3-fold higher simulated SVA exposure in CC versus TT, PDF p6, from the VPC), age on CLHSVAe and CLDHSe (sigmoid decrease). Weight and lean body weight made the model worse (exponents 0.75/1 fixed or estimated). CYP3A5*3 not significant (zero AA, four AG, 28 GG).

## Caveats
Sampling only to 8 h, so one compartment for SV and CLSVAe poorly estimated (the authors say the genotype effect on formation rather than elimination is unexpected). n=32 with only two CC subjects. Age effects on clearance run opposite to maturation (authors attribute to overweight cohort). Parameters are apparent, no volumes identifiable for metabolites; high shrinkage for several BSV terms (up to 60%). Extrapolation outside observed concentration range cautioned.
