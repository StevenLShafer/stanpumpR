# Schnider 1999: influence of age on propofol pharmacodynamics (ke0, tPeak, C50 for LOC)

## Study
- Randomized, double-blind, two-period crossover; Stanford IRB; same volunteers as the companion PK paper (Schnider 1998, PMID 9605675). 25 enrolled, 24 completed (one dropped out for depression and was replaced) (p1503).
- Age-stratified, 8 per group: 18-34, 35-65, >65 y. Table 1: age 25-81 y, weight 44.4-123 kg, height 155-196 cm, 13 M / 11 F (derived count from Table 1).
- Each session: manual bolus over median 18 s (13-24 s) of 2 mg/kg (1 mg/kg if >65 y), then 1 h later a 60-min infusion at 25, 50, 100 or 200 µg/kg/min (2 per age group per rate); propofol with or without EDTA on the two days (p1503).
- Arterial samples at 0-600 min (21 times). EEG (Cz, FP3, FP4, P3, P4; 128 Hz) processed by semilinear canonical correlation into the canonical univariate parameter CUPpropofol (10 frequency-bin weights, Table 2). LOC/ROC by eye-opening to command every 10 s (p1503).
- Biphasic (activation then depression) PD model fitted per study (7 parameters x 48 studies) in Excel Solver (p1505).

## Model and parameters
No PK estimated here (PK from Schnider 1998). ke0 was estimated twice: with model-independent "connect-the-dots" plasma concentrations, and with the covariate-adjusted Schnider 1998 PK (p1505).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: EEG CUPpropofol (biphasic) for ke0 and C50,A/C50,D; loss of consciousness (response to command) at end of infusion for C50 for LOC. These are two separate models: the EEG model (two Emax models, activation then depression, driven by effect-site concentration through ke0; parameters defined on PDF p3, printed p1504, where no explicit equation is printed) gives ke0 and tPeak; the LOC model below has no ke0.
- LOC model ("Loss of Consciousness Pharmacodynamic Analysis", PDF pp4-5, printed pp1505-1506; fitted values PDF p8, printed p1509), as printed:
  - age-independent: P_asleep = C^γ / (C^γ + C50^γ)
  - age-dependent: C50 = a + b · age, so P_asleep = C^γ / (C^γ + (a + b · age)^γ); γ, a and b estimated (the text calls γ "g").
  - formulation test: P_asleep = C^γ / (C^γ + (x · PREP + y · (1 − PREP))^γ), PREP 0 or 1 for EDTA absent/present.
  - likelihood L = P_asleep^R · (1 − P_asleep)^(1−R), R = 0 awake, 1 asleep; objective LL = Σ(i=1..48) R_i · log(P_asleep) + (1 − R_i) · log(1 − P_asleep).
  - C = measured arterial plasma propofol drawn immediately before the 60-min infusion was stopped, assumed equilibrated with the effect site (the PK model predicts <5% rise between 50 and 60 min); P_asleep = probability of being unconscious at that moment; age in years.
  - Fitted (p1509): age-independent C50 1.68 µg/mL (SE 0.196), γ 3.79 (SE 1.58); age-dependent a = 2.9 (SE 0.48), b = −0.022 per year (SE 0.0093), printed as C50 = 2.9 − 0.022 · age with C50 in µg/mL, γ 4.29 (SE 1.77); age improved the fit (likelihood ratio P = 0.027); C50 2.35, 1.8, 1.25 µg/mL at 25, 50, 75 y. Separate C50 per formulation did not improve the likelihood.
- tPeak: visual EEG 1.6 min (1-2.4); predicted 1.69 min with ke0 0.456 /min + Schnider PK; 1.96 min with ke0 0.316 /min + connect-the-dots. Authors: the connect-the-dots ke0 has little use outside the analysis; the 0.456 value is the one to use with their PK (p1509-1510).
- Measured tPeak tended to rise with age but not significantly (Fig 8).
- Discussion (PDF pp11-12, printed pp1512-1513): t1/2ke0 1.5 min is shorter than earlier propofol reports (Billard 2.6-3.3 min by end point; Schuttler 2.9 min; STANPUMP had used ke0 0.25 /min from Dyck's unpublished work), attributed possibly to the biphasic model and the optimised EEG measure. The connect-the-dots ke0 cannot be used with parametric PK; the recommended ke0 is the one estimated with the companion (Schnider 1998) PK. Observed median peak EEG effect 1.6 min agrees with that PK + ke0 0.456 /min (Flaishon's BIS peak 1.8 min cited as background). Sleep-duration prediction median error 1.24 min, bias about 34 s.
- Older subjects more sensitive: C50,A, gammaA, gammaD correlated with age (P = 0.017, 0.035, 0.02); EDTA did not alter potency.
- Sleep duration after a 2 mg/kg bolus increased with age (<=65 y; P < 0.0001), via later ROC, not earlier LOC (Fig 2). Prediction bias −0.72 min (−0.56 with age-adjusted C50).
- No MEAC, no potency ratio (not an analgesic).

## Covariates
Age on C50,A, gammaA, gammaD and C50 for LOC (linear). Weight/height/sex not tested for PD in the pages read.

## Caveats
- 24 healthy volunteers; >65 y given half the bolus, so excluded from sleep-duration analysis; one 27-y subject never lost consciousness after the bolus.
- Low R² in the 25 µg/kg/min group (little EEG effect); median CUP R² 0.48.
- ke0 is specific to the PK used (Schnider 1998); 0.456 vs 0.459 inconsistency between text and Table 4.
- No age effect on C50,D (50% EEG depression) was found, but the authors say this is a design limit, not evidence of none: many slow infusions did not depress the EEG enough to characterise C50,D, so an age effect may exist undetected; faster infusions would have left everyone unconscious at end-infusion and defeated the LOC analysis (PDF p13, printed p1514).
- Conclusions (p1514): CUPpropofol is offered as a research surrogate for propofol PD; the authors do not propose it has clinical utility (contrasted with the bispectral index).
- Mammillary PK misspecifies the first ~30 s; ke0 was estimated two ways partly to address this (p1513).
