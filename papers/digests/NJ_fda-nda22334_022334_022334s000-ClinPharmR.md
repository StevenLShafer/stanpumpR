# FDA 2009: Afinitor (everolimus) NDA 22-334 clinical pharmacology and pharmacometric review

## Study
FDA Office of Clinical Pharmacology review (reviewer Julie M. Bullock; pharmacometrics Christoffer Tornoe) of Novartis's original NDA for everolimus 10 mg once daily in advanced renal cell carcinoma. Pivotal trial C2240: randomized double-blind phase 3, everolimus 10 mg QD + best supportive care (n = 272) vs placebo (n = 138); median PFS 4.01 vs 1.87 months (PDF p.10). Clinical pharmacology rests on phase 1 solid-tumour studies (C2101, C2102, C2107, C1101 Japanese), a healthy-volunteer bioavailability study (C2119, n = 40 in the PK population) and a QT study (C2118, healthy women), plus transplant-programme studies reviewed earlier (ADME W107, DDI, hepatic impairment A2303, food W302). Assay: everolimus in whole blood by LC/MS or LC-MS/MS (LLOQ 0.3-0.378 ng/mL; Tables 23-24, PDF pp.38-39); whole blood is the matrix because red-cell uptake is concentration-dependent above 100 ng/mL (PDF p.17).

## Model and parameters
Source for the library model: Pharmacometric Review, population PK of 398 patients with advanced solid tumours (phase 1/1b studies plus C2240), 1667 whole-blood concentrations, 1-20 per patient (PDF p.53, printed p.9). Two-compartment, first-order absorption, apparent (CL/F, V/F), NONMEM VI, FOCE with interaction. Sponsor model (Table 1, PDF p.54) carried a renal-cancer factor on clearance of 0.682; the reviewer's refit (Table 4, PDF p.58, printed p.14) removes it (objective function 8314 sponsor vs 8361 reviewer; the reviewer calls the 0.68 factor probably a study effect or an artefact of unreliable troughs).

Other PK facts: AUC dose-proportional 5-70 mg (log-log slope 0.97, 95% CI 0.84-1.09), Cmax less than proportional at 20 mg and above (PDF pp.19-20). Accumulation about 1.5-fold after daily dosing; steady state by week 2 (PDF p.14). Table 6 (PDF p.14): 5 mg single (n = 4, week 4 of weekly dosing, truncated at 24 h) Cmax 32.4 +/- 15.3 ng/mL, AUC0-24 173 +/- 26, CL/F 21.4 +/- 2.7 L/h; 5 mg QD Cmax 31.5 +/- 9.4, AUC0-24 255 +/- 46 (n = 3), CL/F 20.0 +/- 3.5; 10 mg single Cmax 69.1 +/- 8.1, AUC0-24 296 +/- 91.7, CL/F 27.3 +/- 13.5. Table 7 (PDF p.15), C2240 10 mg QD: day 1 (n = 13) Cmax 68.1 +/- 29.8, Cmin 7.9 +/- 3.4, AUC0-t 455 +/- 168.5; day 15 (n = 12) Cmax 76.7 +/- 39.3, Cmin 19.8 +/- 12.3, AUC0-t 729.1 +/- 262.7, CL/F 15.4 +/- 5.3 L/h (7.5 +/- 2.3 L/h/m2); the reviewer attributes the differences from the phase 1 AUCs to the lack of samples between 5 and 24 h. Table 8 (PDF p.16): healthy 10 mg (n = 40) Cmax 64.4 +/- 17.8, AUC0-t 510.1 +/- 165.8, CL/F 20.6 +/- 6.8 L/h, t1/2 36.9 +/- 9.5 h; patients (n = 4) Cmax 69.1 +/- 8.1, AUC 573 +/- 258, CL/F 22.1 +/- 14.1, t1/2 39.3 +/- 17.0. Table 21 (PDF p.35, text layer only): healthy single 10 mg AUCinf 537.8 +/- 206.6, Cmax 61.2 +/- 19.1 ng/mL.

Metabolism and elimination (ADME study W107, 3 mg 14C dose in transplant patients on cyclosporine; Table 10, PDF p.19): parent is 39.9% of blood radioactivity AUC0-24; mono-hydroxylated P42 12.6%, P50 12.4%; rapamycin 1.2%; metabolites about 100-fold less active. Recovery 79.5 +/- 6.0% faeces, 5.1 +/- 1.7% urine; no parent in excreta. CYP3A4 and P-gp substrate. Food (high-fat): Cmax down 60%, AUC down 16% (PDF p.36). Protein binding about 73% at 10 ng/mL; red-cell binding about 85% (5-100 ng/mL).

Interactions (single 2 mg doses unless stated; Tables 14-20, PDF pp.28-34): ketoconazole AUC x15.3, Cmax x4.1; erythromycin AUC x4.9, Cmax x2.1; verapamil AUC x3.6, Cmax x2.4; rifampin AUC down 62%, Cmax down 59%; atorvastatin and pravastatin little effect. Hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 7-9, 2 mg, n = 8 vs 8): AUC +115% (245 +/- 91 vs 114 +/- 45 ng*h/mL), CL/F 9.1 +/- 3.1 vs 19.4 +/- 5.8 L/h, t1/2 79 vs 43 h (Table 12, PDF p.24); 50% dose reduction. No effect of renal function. Japanese patients had higher exposure (CL 9-14 L/h vs 17-24 L/h Caucasian, Table 11, PDF p.21), judged of unknown relevance.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid or anaesthetic; no MEAC, ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak in the document. Therapeutic/exposure facts, all in oncology: preliminary PK-PD suggested trough concentrations of 10-35 ng/mL are needed to affect downstream effectors (PDF p.10 and p.54); biomarker p70 S6 kinase 1 inhibition in PBMCs, skin and tumour (daily 5 and 10 mg and weekly 50 mg near-complete). The sponsor's Cmin groups (<10, 10-35, >35 ng/mL; n = 32, 130, 55) gave median PFS 4.47, 4.30 and similar months; the reviewer's quartile analysis of C2240 steady-state troughs (1.4-12.4, 12.5-19, 19.1-30.6, 30.7-135 ng/mL; the text layer reads one bound as "1.1-30.6") found no exposure-efficacy and no exposure-safety trend (PDF pp.11, 47-48, 59). Some of those troughs were not true troughs (sampling-time misreporting), so the exposure-response is not interpretable with confidence (PDF p.59). In phase 1 study C2107, skin and metabolic AEs rose with trough tertiles of 2.3-6, 7-11, 11.7-46.7 ng/mL (PDF p.62). QT: 20 and 50 mg single doses, upper bound of the 90% CI for dQTcF below 10 ms (PDF p.12). No target concentration or band for transplant is stated here.

## Covariates
CL/F: primary cancer site (kidney) factor 0.68 in the sponsor model, dropped by the reviewer; age, weight, sex, creatinine clearance, bilirubin/albumin, simvastatin: none detected. Hepatic impairment halves clearance in the dedicated study. Race: only 11 of 398 were Asian, so power was low.

## Caveats
Patients had cancer and took no calcineurin inhibitor; the transplant setting differs (see library header). Apparent parameters (no F). Troughs in C2240 were unreliable. The sponsor's 0.68 renal-cancer clearance factor is not applied in the reviewer's model, so the reviewer's CL/F 14.8 L/h is a pooled value (sponsor non-renal-cancer 18.8, renal-cancer 18.8 x 0.682 = 12.8 (derived)). Reviewer's model terminal half-life (derived from Table 4: k10 = 0.086, k12 = 0.279, k21 = 0.097 per h; slow eigenvalue about 0.0189/h) is about 36.7 h, consistent with the observed 30.7-39 h. The review's printed page numbers restart in the appended Pharmacometric Review.
