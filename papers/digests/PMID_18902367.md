# Rosenthal 1948: temperature coefficient of blood and plasma pH in vitro

## Study
In-vitro measurement of pH of anaerobically drawn blood (human antecubital vein; dog, cat, rabbit from heart or femoral artery), defibrinated or oxalated (0.1-0.2 %; plus 0.06 % NaF if held 24-48 h). Each sample split and read simultaneously at 38 C and at room temperature (18-24 C) with glass electrodes (accuracy 0.01 pH). Whole blood and plasma/serum. No drug is studied; pure physical chemistry (PDF pages 1-3).

## Model and parameters
pH is linear in temperature; slope is independent of initial pH (7.25-7.45) and of hemoglobin, plasma protein or bicarbonate changed by about 50 %. Warming/cooling 38-3 C was reversible to 0.01 pH with fluoride.

Tables I and II print the coefficients as positive magnitudes (pH rises as temperature falls); the text and Eq 1 carry the negative sign. Eq 1: blood pH38 = pH_t - 0.0147 (38 - t). Eq 2: plasma pH38 = pH_t - 0.0118 (38 - t). Eq 3 (plasma separated at t_c, read at t_p): blood pH38 = plasma pH - 0.0118 (38 - t_c) - 0.0029 (38 - t_p) (text layer, PDF p. 5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None (no drug). Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency data.

## Covariates
Species differences small in whole blood (variance within species exceeded between-species variance by a factor of 2.3); larger in plasma. Initial pH, Hb, protein and bicarbonate did not change the slope (authors' claim; later work cited by Kelman 1966 found a pH and CO2 dependence).

## Caveats
Samples included deliberately altered pH; room-temperature range only 18-24 C. Plasma separated at other than 38 C ("derived plasma") differs from true plasma by about 0.06 pH at 18 C. CO2 content of derived plasma varied 2.5-14.5 %, chloride about 3 %.
