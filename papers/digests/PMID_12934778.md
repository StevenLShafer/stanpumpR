# Appell 2003: transdermal vs extended-release oral oxybutynin in healthy subjects

## Study
- Randomized, open-label, 2-way crossover, Watson-sponsored; one clinic (St Charles, MO), October–November 2001 (p697).
- 15 enrolled, 13 completed (7 men, 6 women); age 35 ± 12 y, weight 71 ± 8 kg, BMI 25 ± 2 kg/m² (mean ± SD, p697–698). Complete PK and paired data in 12. Inclusion: BMI 20–30, weight 50–90 kg, healthy.
- Treatments: transdermal Oxytrol 3.9 mg/day (two systems in sequence on the abdomen, the first for 84 h, the second for 96 h); oral Ditropan XL 10 mg once daily for 6 days. Doses are the products' nominal delivered/labelled amounts; salt form not stated.
- Sampling: venous blood at baseline and "periodically", matched to time after the second patch and the third tablet (exact times not listed; see Figure 1). Saliva every 12 h by 2-min paraffin chewing (weighed).
- Assay: plasma racemic and R/S oxybutynin and N-desethyloxybutynin (DEO) by LC-MS/MS (MDS Pharma). Racemates validated 0.05–50.0 ng/mL; enantiomers 0.05–25.0 (oxybutynin) and 0.25–25.0 ng/mL (DEO); precision ≤ 13% (p697).
- Analysis: noncompartmental (AUC, Cmin, Cmax, fluctuation index = (Cmax − Cmin)/Cavg); 95% CI, paired t, ANOVA, linear regression.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model; no clearance, volume, ka or bioavailability estimated. Table 1 (PDF p4, printed p699), mean ± SD, confirmed on the page image:

Derived averages from Total AUC: oxybutynin Cavg 259/84 = 3.1 ng/mL (patch) and 222/96 = 2.3 ng/mL (oral); DEO 3.8 and 9.5 ng/mL. These agree with Figure 1 (patch oxybutynin 2.2–3.8, oral 1.8–3.0 ng/mL).

Enantiomers (Figure 2 and p698; average concentration = AUC/84 h): patch R-oxybutynin 1.2, S-oxybutynin 1.7, R-DEO 1.6, S-DEO 1.7 ng/mL; oral R-oxybutynin 0.7, S-oxybutynin 1.4, R-DEO 5.0, S-DEO 4.1 ng/mL.

Steady state: patch troughs at 84 h after the first and second systems 2.3 vs 2.2 ng/mL (P = .49); oral 24 h after the second and sixth tablets 2.0 vs 1.9 ng/mL (P = .63) (p698). The discussion cites (not measured here) an apparent half-life of about 10 h for the patch (skin depot) and 2–4 h after oral dosing (refs 6–8, 16; p700).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: stimulated saliva output (weighed, 2-min paraffin chew every 12 h), a surrogate for dry mouth. Total saliva 15.7 ± 9.3 g (patch) vs 12.2 ± 6.8 g (oral), P = .02; paired difference 3.5 ± 4.5 g (range −0.3 to 14.4), greater on patch in 11 of 12 (p698–699).
- Concentration–effect: average DEO vs total saliva r = −0.59 (P = .04); oxybutynin r = −0.40 (P = .18); R-oxybutynin r = −0.27 (P = .39); R-DEO r = −0.53 (P = .07) (p699). No EC50, Emax or effect-site rate constant. No ke0, no potency ratio. Not an opioid paper.
- Adverse events: 6 subjects had 9 events; only one treatment-related (nausea on oral).

## Covariates
None examined.

## Caveats
- n = 12 analysable healthy young adults; open-label; sponsor-run (three authors were Watson employees).
- The "24-hour AUC" row appears to be Total AUC/24 rather than a per-day AUC (open question); the abstract's "comparable 24-hour AUC" therefore rests on mis-scaled numbers, although the Total AUC comparison (P = .22) still holds.
- Dose comparison is nominal 3.9 mg/day delivered (patch) vs 10 mg/day (oral tablet), so no relative bioavailability can be computed from this paper alone.
- Plasma (not whole blood); total (not free) concentrations.
