# Shanmugalingam 2019: salicylic acid PK after aspirin in pregnancy (dose, coating, timing)

## Study
Twelve high-risk pregnant women from South Western Sydney high-risk clinics, three in each of four arms: 100 mg enteric-coated (EC), 100 mg non-EC, 150 mg non-EC at 08:00 and 150 mg non-EC at 20:00. Three nonpregnant women each took all four regimens in crossover, at least 30 days apart (p. 2, Figure 1 p. 3). Pregnant women took their own prescribed aspirin. The 150 mg dose was half of a 300 mg non-EC tablet (p. 2). Aspirin was taken right after food. Renal and liver dysfunction were excluded (p. 2).

Table 1 (p. 4), pregnant arms: mean age 31.4-33.2 y, weight 70.8-73.9 kg, BMI 25.7-26.8, **gestation 24.6 +/- 1, 26.1 +/- 1, 25.2 +/- 1 and 26.3 +/- 1 weeks** (100 mg EC, 100 mg non-EC, 150 mg morning, 150 mg evening). Comorbidities: hypertension, prior preeclampsia, SLE, diabetes. Nonpregnant: 37.4 +/- 5 y, 75.6 +/- 7 kg. Sampling at 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 12 and 24 h (p. 2). Salicylic acid (SA) only, by LC-MS/MS with D4-SA internal standard; standards 0-500 ng/mL (pp. 2-3). Parameters by two-compartment analysis in PKSolver; ANOVA and t tests in SPSS 25 (p. 3). Acetylsalicylic acid itself was not measured.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured: no platelet, thromboxane or clinical end point. The authors call for PD and outcome studies (p. 8).

## Covariates
Pregnancy, dose, preparation and dosing time, as group comparisons only.

## Caveats
- **Headline percentages (stated):**
  - Abstract and Comment: pregnancy lowers AUC0-24 by 40% +/- 4% and Cmax by 29% +/- 3%, and raises CL by 44% +/- 8%, with 100 mg (p. 1, p. 5).
  - Results (p. 3-4): AUC 41% +/- 2% and 34% +/- 4% lower with 100 and 150 mg non-EC, CL +43% +/- 8%, Cmax 25% +/- 2% and 31% +/- 3% lower. Also 43% +/- 2% lower AUC and 44% +/- 1% higher CL across EC and non-EC 100 mg, and 35% +/- 4% lower AUC and 46% +/- 4% higher CL across the 150 mg timing arms.
  - The Figure 2 caption gives AUC 38.6% lower with 100 mg and 42% lower with 150 mg.
- **The tables do not reproduce those percentages (derived from the rendered tables):**
  - AUC ratio pregnant/nonpregnant is 0.58 at 100 mg (-42%) and 0.68 at 150 mg (-32%). These do not match the Figure 2 caption's 38.6% (100 mg) and 42% (150 mg); unresolved.
  - Table CL is 0.006 vs 0.003, a 2.0-fold rise, and dose/AUC gives 8.2 vs 4.7 L/h, 1.73-fold. Neither is +44%.
  - EC vs non-EC Vd is 0.16 vs 0.08, +100%, not the reported +48% +/- 1%.
  - Table Vd (60-160 L, about 0.8-2.2 L/kg) is not the "0.1-0.2 L/kg" the Comment (p. 6) calls consistent with this study.
  - CL x t1/2 / ln 2 is about 22 L, not the 80 L Vd, so "Vd" is not defined consistently with the CL and t1/2 shown. Unresolved; the paper does not define its PKSolver outputs.
  - The same 100 mg comparison carries P < .01 in Table 2 and P = .03 in Table 3.
- The 150 mg pregnant AUC (19993.3) is 5% below the 100 mg nonpregnant AUC (21096.6), and Cmax 2.3% above it (p. 4). This is the basis for "150 mg restores exposure".
- EC vs non-EC (100 mg): Tmax 4 h later, Cmax 47% +/- 2% lower, Vd 48% +/- 1% higher, no AUC or t1/2 difference (p. 4; the abstract says 47% +/- 3%). No morning vs evening difference in any parameter. No four-way interaction (F3,16 = 0.773, P = .5; p. 5).
- n = 3 per arm. The nonpregnant comparator is the same three women in every comparison. All women were in the second trimester (about 25-26 weeks), so there is no first- or third-trimester information. Dosed after food. Renal disease excluded (p. 8).
