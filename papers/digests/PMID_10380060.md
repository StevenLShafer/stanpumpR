# Tanaka 1999: antiepileptic drug-drug interactions (narrative review)

## Study
Single-author narrative review (University of Tsukuba) of pharmacokinetic interactions among antiepileptic
drugs, organised around the cytochrome P450 isoforms. It contains **no primary data**: every quantitative
statement is quoted from a cited primary study. Three "Cases" present worked numerical examples from the
literature.

Sections: metabolism of each drug (Table 1), induction and inhibition (Table 2), the three Cases, a
qualitative interaction matrix (Table 3), conclusion.

## Model and parameters
No model, no fitted parameter. The quantitative content is:

**Case 1 (printed p 89)** - carbamazepine as victim, from refs 46-47 (Brodie 1983; Rambeck 1987): mean serum
carbamazepine is **59.4%** of monotherapy with phenytoin, **58.2%** with primidone, **65.7%** with
phenobarbital and **83.0%** with valproate (monotherapy = 100%); carbamazepine-epoxide is raised by
valproate (144.8%), primidone (118.5%) and the combination (167.4%).

**Case 2 (printed p 89)** - carbamazepine level/dose ratios fall and epoxide/diol ratios rise on phenytoin
or phenobarbital; phenytoin is called a potent inducer of carbamazepine epoxidase, phenobarbital only
moderate (ref 48, Liu & Delgado 1995).

**Case 3 (printed p 90)** - **valproate as victim**, from ref 49 (May & Rambeck 1985, 259 in-patients):
the serum concentration of valproic acid is "clearly lower when the drug is given in combination with
phenytoin (**49.5%**), carbamazepine (**66.2%**) or phenobarbital (**76.3%**) than when given alone
(100%)". The percentages are explicitly **of monotherapy**, i.e. reduced *to* those values, not *by* them.

**Table 3 (printed p 90)** - a qualitative matrix of direction only. The valproic-acid column records
"decreased" for phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone and carbamazepine as perpetrators; the valproic-acid row
records increases in phenobarbital and primidone and "variable" for phenytoin and carbamazepine, and
"increased" for ethosuximide.

Derived clearance factors from Case 3 (mine): 1/0.495 = **2.02** (phenytoin), 1/0.662 = **1.51**
(carbamazepine), 1/0.763 = **1.31** (phenobarbital). The review itself derives no such factors.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. The review is entirely pharmacokinetic; it states that interaction management rests on serum
concentration monitoring and clinical observation, with no concentration-effect model.

## Covariates
None fitted. The review notes qualitatively that the amount of enzyme induction is proportional to the dose
of the inducing agent and is strongest on multiple inducers (printed p 89, citing Perucca 1984 and
Patsalos 1988) - the same dose/concentration dependence the stanpumpR model parameterises with its
saturable `fold(C)`.

## Caveats
- **Secondary source.** Nothing is measured here. Every figure is a quotation, and the primary studies
  (May & Rambeck 1985, PMID 3936236; Brodie 1983; Rambeck 1987; Liu & Delgado 1995) are the real evidence.
- The percentages are **ratios of mean serum concentrations between comedication groups**, not clearances;
  converting to clearance assumes dose normalisation and linear kinetics, neither of which the quoted
  summary states. Valproate's kinetics in particular are concentration-dependent through saturable protein
  binding (see `PMID_18211614`), so the factors are approximate.
- No n, variance, dose range or age distribution is given for Case 1 or Case 2; Case 3 gives only n = 259.
- Table 3 is direction-only and in places conflicts with better evidence (it marks carbamazepine -> phenytoin
  "variable", consistent with Spina 1996's "controversial and probably inconsistent").
- The review predates lacosamide, topiramate dosing data, and every drug added to the library since 1999.
