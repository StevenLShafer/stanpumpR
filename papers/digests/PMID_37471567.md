# Wesner 2023: therapeutic reference range for olanzapine (AGNP systematic review)

## Study
Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42021216182) by the AGNP TDM task force, following the Hart 2021 protocol and
PRISMA. MEDLINE, Web of Science, PsycINFO and Cochrane searched to December 2021; English/German, human data.
2,824 records + 1 from a reference list; 34 studies in qualitative synthesis (23 oral concentration-effect,
4 LAI, 7 neuroimaging: 5 PET, 2 SPECT); 13 oral studies in the quantitative meta-analysis (p 2, Figure 1 p 3).
Random-effects meta-analysis of mean concentrations (R metafor/meta), linear regression of mean concentration on
mean dose. Blood matrix: most studies plasma, some serum (p 9); the authors note olanzapine is stable in EDTA
plasma but generally unstable in whole blood and serum. 41% of studies reported no detection limit (p 9).
Population: patients with schizophrenia / schizophrenia spectrum disorders mainly; some bipolar, mixed diagnoses,
children/adolescents (Fekete 2017), late-life schizophrenia (Graff-Guerrero 2015). No new data were collected.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
**Recommended therapeutic reference range: 20-40 ng/mL**, oral and LAI, schizophrenia and spectrum disorders,
for a sample drawn **12-15 h after once-daily dosing** (abstract p 1; Clinical Points p 2; conclusion p 10). The
authors stress this is not a trough; levels about 1.6-fold lower are expected at 24 h, and no 24-h range is set.
Lower limit: ROC thresholds of 23 ng/mL 12 h post-dose in three moderate-quality studies (Perry 2001 23.2,
Fellows 2003 23-25, Lu 2016 22.8; Table 3 p 7), and 65% D2 occupancy. Upper limit: a therapeutic ceiling, not a
toxicity limit (maximum effect at about 40 ng/mL in Mauri 2005; 80% occupancy at 41-44 ng/mL). Concentrations
above 40 ng/mL are generally tolerated and need not prompt dose reduction with good response (p 10). Weight
gain can occur within the range. Overall evidence for a concentration-effect relationship: Level C ("low").
No alert level is proposed; the 2017 guideline range 20-80 ng/mL with alert 100 ng/mL is cited as the
current recommendation (p 2).

narrows the two adequate PET studies to lower 19-20 and upper 41-44 ng/mL. No ke0, hysteresis or time course
of effect is reported. No potency ratio. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC.

## Covariates
None modelled. Smoking and CYP1A2 co-medication, sex and age noted to influence levels (p 9); women had higher
levels and more anticholinergic effects in Kelly 2006.

## Caveats
- Evidence level C; high heterogeneity (I2 91.7%); flexible dosing in 19 of 23 oral studies; co-medication in 22
  of 34 protocols; 12 of 23 studies sampled at 12-15 h (not trough).
- Internal inconsistency: text p 7 says Perry 1997 and 2001 gave 12-h and 24-h breakpoints of 9 and 23 ng/mL
  "respectively", but Table 3 lists Perry 2001 at 12 h (23.2) and Perry 1997 at 24 h (9.3). Not used by the repo.
- Typo on p 6 ("maximum occOne SPECT study") truncates the Kapur 1999 sentence.
- LAI range rests on pharmacokinetics and one PET study (Mamo 2008), not on a concentration-effect study.
