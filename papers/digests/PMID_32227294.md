# Cook 2020: Neurocritical Care Society guideline on acute treatment of cerebral edema

## Study
Clinical practice guideline (Neurocritical Care Society; endorsed by SCCM, AAN, AANN). Panel formed 2017; 16 PICO
questions; Ovid Medline/EMBASE search to 5 July 2018 plus supplementary searches to February 2019; GRADE
methodology; adult studies only, case series of 5 or more included (PDF p1-2, printed 647-648). Covers hypertonic
sodium solutions (HTS), mannitol, corticosteroids and non-drug measures in SAH, TBI, AIS, ICH, bacterial/TB
meningitis and hepatic encephalopathy. No original pharmacokinetic data.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic model or parameters. Numbers relevant to mannitol and osmotherapy monitoring:

- Serum osmolarity 320 mOsm/kg or osmolar gap 20-55 mOsm/kg are what clinicians commonly use to estimate AKI risk
  with mannitol; both indirect surrogates of serum mannitol concentration (PDF p12, printed 658; image confirmed).
  The same paragraph writes the 320 threshold as mOsm/L twice, so the paper mixes osmolality and osmolarity units.
- Recent studies show an osmolarity threshold above 320 mOsm/L does not affect AKI incidence (ref 88) (PDF p12).
- Osmolar gap of 20 mOsm/kg used in practice without supporting evidence; one study found a gap of 55 mOsm/kg or
  higher best correlated with serum mannitol concentration (PDF p11, printed 657; image confirmed).
- Mannitol-induced AKI case series: mean osmolar gap 74 mOsm/kg (8 patients, ref 86) (PDF p12).
- AKI incidence 6-12% of mannitol-treated patients; risk factors heart failure, diabetes, illness severity,
  pre-existing renal dysfunction (PDF p11).
- Mannitol dose mentioned only for one old AIS RCT: 0.8-0.9 g/kg once daily for 10 days (PDF p7, printed 653; text
  layer only).
- HTS: sodium targets 145-155 mEq/L in cited studies; suggested upper limits sodium 155-160 mEq/L and chloride
  110-115 mEq/L to limit AKI (PDF p12).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points: ICP and cerebral edema (imaging), neurological outcome (GOS/GOSE, mRS, mortality), AKI. Main
mannitol-related recommendations (all conditional, low or very low quality):
- TBI: HTS suggested over mannitol for initial ICP/edema management; mannitol an effective alternative; neither
  improves neurological outcome; mannitol not suggested pre-hospital (PDF p4-5, printed 650-651).
- AIS: either agent; HTS if mannitol response inadequate; against prophylactic scheduled mannitol (PDF p6, printed
  652).
- ICH: HTS over mannitol; panel could not recommend mannitol (PDF p7-8).
- Hepatic encephalopathy: either agent (PDF p10).
- Monitoring: osmolar gap suggested over serum osmolarity thresholds to monitor AKI risk; insufficient evidence for
  any osmolar-gap cutoff; monitor renal function closely (PDF p11).
No therapeutic serum osmolality target is recommended anywhere in the guideline.

Not applicable (no model). AKI risk factors listed above.

## Caveats
Evidence mostly low or very low quality; no direct comparison of osmolar gap versus osmolality threshold; serum
mannitol concentration rarely available. Units for 320 alternate between mOsm/kg and mOsm/L. Supplementary
evidence tables not read (not in the archived PDF).
