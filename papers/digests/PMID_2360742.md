# Raemer 1990: Maitre vs Scott population alfentanil parameters in a computer-controlled infusion pump

PDF pages are one-based in the archived file; PDF p1 = printed p66 ... PDF p7 = printed p72.

## Study
- Prospective evaluation of a computer-controlled infusion pump (CCIP) for alfentanil, two centres (PDF p1-2,
  printed pp66-67).
- **Group 1:** 29 women, day-surgery laparoscopy (< 1 h), Brigham and Women's Hospital; median age 34 y (24-45),
  median weight 59 kg (43-93). Midazolam 1-2 mg; target (C_T) 100 ng/mL for 10 min, thiopental 3-4 mg/kg and
  succinylcholine 3 min into the infusion; N2O 70 %; then C_T 200 ng/mL for 8-12 min to the end of surgery, then off.
  Arterial samples before and 1, 3, 6 min after each change; 12 samples per patient.
- **Group 2:** 22 men, Palo Alto VA, longer procedures; median age 64 y (29-76), median weight 85 kg (69-101).
  C_T 400, 550, 700 ng/mL for 5 min each for induction (N2O 70 %, vecuronium), then titrated to responsiveness;
  isoflurane in about one-third; C_T 200 ng/mL about 45 min before the end, off about 20 min before the end.
  14-24 arterial samples per patient. **Group 2A** = first 11 (Maitre parameters); **group 2B** = next 11 (Scott
  parameters).
- Group 1 and 2A ran on Maitre et al. 1987 (covariate-adjusted for sex, weight, age); after these 40, the Scott et al.
  parameters appeared better, and group 2B was run on them prospectively (PDF p2, printed p67).
- Assay: serum, radioimmunoassay (Michiels, modified by Schüttler and White), validated against GC; LLOQ 40 ng/mL;
  paired-aliquot CV < 5 % (PDF p2, printed p67).
- Device (PDF p2-3, printed pp67-68): software written in Better Basic **by one of the authors (D.B.R., Raemer)**,
  driving a customised C. R. Bard syringe pump over a serial link; model equations integrated by Euler's method once
  per second; maximum rate 50 µg/kg/min; infusion record saved to disk for re-simulation. Pump accuracy within 1 %
  gravimetrically (PDF p6, printed p71). The paper does not name STANPUMP.
- Performance measures (PDF p3, printed p68): PE = [(C_M − C_T)/C_T] × 100, i.e. relative to the target (earlier
  studies divided by the measured value). Bias = MDPE (median PE over all samples); inaccuracy = MDAPE = median |PE|.
  Rationale: the parameters were fitted by iteratively reweighted least squares, and J.R.V. (Varvel) showed MDPE and
  MDAPE track the mean squared error while mean PE and mean |PE| do not; PE is not normally distributed; MDAPE has a
  direct interpretation.

Fig. 6). Re-analysing Maitre's own "prospective" Ausems data with these measures gave MDAPE 27 %, MDPE 16 %
(PDF p6, printed p71).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No pharmacodynamic end point modelled. Targets were clinical choices (100 and 200 ng/mL in group 1; 400, 550,
  700 ng/mL at induction in group 2, then titrated), not effective concentrations.

Opioid audit:
- MEAC / minimum analgesic concentration: **none** reported.
- ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis: **none**; the pump targeted serum (plasma-type) concentration only.
- Potency / equianalgesic ratio: **none**.
- Assay: serum, arterial (total drug), RIA; LLOQ 40 ng/mL.

## Covariates
None estimated here. The Maitre set carries age (CL, k31 above 40 y), sex (V1 15 % larger in women) and weight (V1);
the Scott set has none.

## Caveats
- Explanations examined for Maitre's 50 % bias (PDF p6-7): assay (cross-laboratory and GC checks, serum vs plasma:
  no error found), pump/software (verified), population (two dissimilar groups, same bias), PE definition (partly),
  arterial vs venous sampling (venous MDPE −6 % in six patients, but Ausems's data were arterial), and study design
  (Maitre's sources were sub-30-s boluses with long sampling; Scott's an infusion over 4-6 min). The authors are left
  without a complete explanation.
- Even with Scott's set, overshoot after each target increase and high concentrations late in elimination.
- Small group 2B (n = 11); the Scott set was assayed in the same laboratory.
- Two probable misprints in the paper: Table 1 k21 (0.673) and the Table 3 group label "1 and 2B".
