# Shintaku 2012: predicted fetal NSAID exposure and ductus constriction, built from non-pregnant maternal parameters

A hybrid physiologically based model that predicts the HUMAN FETAL plasma concentration of three
NSAIDs after oral dosing to the mother, and converts it into a predicted constriction of the fetal
ductus arteriosus. For stanpumpR it is a negative result in the most useful sense: it is the kind of
paper a pregnancy search returns for diclofenac, and it contains **no maternal pregnancy
pharmacokinetics at all**, which is exactly what the registry row says.

**Why this card was written by hand.** `digest_run.py` records this paper as refused by the model's
safeguards even when batched alone. The refusal is at card-writing only — the PDF, its extracted
text and its context file were filed normally. See *Caveats*.

## Study
Not a clinical study. It is a modelling paper assembled from four sources, and the provenance of
each is the point:

1. **Maternal pharmacokinetics** — one- or two-compartment models with first-order absorption,
   fitted to plasma profiles after a single oral dose in **healthy women** (Table 1, PDF p. 3):
   diclofenac 50 mg in 7, salicylic acid 300 mg in 6, antipyrine 600 mg in 6, each taken from the
   literature. The women are not described as pregnant anywhere, and the paper calls them "healthy
   females" when it uses the fitted profile as its input function.
2. **Transplacental parameters** — taken from the group's own earlier human placental perfusion
   studies (their refs 6 and 7), as influx clearances per placenta and first-order rate constants.
3. **Concentration-response** — from RAT data: the ratio of the inner diameter of the ductus
   arteriosus to that of the pulmonary artery (DA/PA), 4 h after NSAID administration to pregnant
   rats, taken from the literature and fitted to `E = 1 - D^gamma/(ED50^gamma + D^gamma)`.
4. **Rat fetal exposure**, to convert the rat dose-response into a concentration-response: fetal
   plasma 4 h after maternal intravenous salicylic acid 10 mg/kg or antipyrine 18.8 mg/kg was 28.6
   and 19.0 mcg/mL, with an unbound fraction of 0.413 in rat fetal plasma for salicylic acid.

Simulated regimens (PDF p. 5, Figure 3): diclofenac 50 mg twice daily, salicylic acid 300 mg three
times daily, antipyrine 600 mg three times daily.

**The fetal volume is an assumption, not a measurement**: `V = Vd x 3/60`, a 3 kg fetus against a
60 kg mother, which reproduces all three printed fetal volumes exactly (4666/20 = 233.3,
5845/20 = 292.25, 35000/20 = 1750). So the fetal compartment is the maternal volume scaled by
bodyweight, and nothing in the fetal disposition is fitted.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
The end point is **constriction of the fetal ductus arteriosus**, as the DA/PA inner diameter ratio,
and it is a toxicity end point, not a therapeutic one. The concentration-response is fitted to rat
data and then applied to predicted human fetal concentrations. Diclofenac's EC50 of 0.00137 mcg/mL
is five to six orders of magnitude below the other two, which is what drives the headline: a
predicted mean DA/PA ratio of 39.0% for diclofenac against 5.9% for each of salicylic acid and
antipyrine. The authors note a threefold shift in the concentration-response leaves the ranking
essentially unchanged.

There is no analgesic end point here, no EC50 for pain, and nothing that could become a band or a
threshold in stanpumpR.

## Covariates
None. Pregnancy is not a covariate in this model; it is the setting. No maternal covariate is
fitted, and the fetal compartment's only "covariate" is the assumed 3 kg bodyweight.

## Caveats
- **It contains no maternal pregnancy pharmacokinetics.** The maternal input function for every
  simulation is a fit to healthy, non-pregnant women. A reader looking here for a pregnancy
  clearance or volume for diclofenac will find none, which is the whole of its relevance to the
  registry.
- **The pharmacodynamics are rat.** Both the dose-response for ductus constriction and the fetal
  exposures used to turn it into a concentration-response come from rats; only the pharmacokinetic
  transfer parameters are human.
- **The fetus is assumed to have no metabolic capability.** The authors defend this (fetal CYP and
  UGT expression is much lower than maternal; CYP3A7 predominates over CYP3A4; CYP2C9 and CYP2C19
  activities reach only about 30% of maternal even at delivery) and note it is deliberately
  conservative, since it avoids a false-negative risk assessment. It is worth flagging for
  diclofenac in particular, which is a CYP2C9 substrate.
- **The fetal volume is assumed from bodyweight**, not measured.
- **Single oral doses underlie the maternal fits** (50, 300 and 600 mg), while the simulations are
  of repeated dosing.
- **The paper also covers salicylic acid and antipyrine**, both of which the library knows in other
  contexts (salicylate as aspirin's metabolite; antipyrine in the front-end kinetics models). It
  supplies no parameter to either, and its antipyrine and salicylic acid numbers are likewise from
  non-pregnant women.

## Verification against stanpumpR

- `No human pregnancy or post-cesarean PK study of diclofenac found (search returned efficacy trials
  and placental perfusion/PK-PD predictions using nonpregnant parameters)` -- this paper is exactly
  the second kind, and the "nonpregnant parameters" description is literally right rather than
  approximately right. Table 1 names its diclofenac source as healthy women, n = 7, after a single
  oral 50 mg dose, and that fit (F 0.54, ka 5.08 /h, alpha 3.38 /h, beta 0.775 /h, tlag 2.00 h, Vd
  4666 mL) is the maternal input function for every simulation in the paper. The transplacental
  parameters come from human placental perfusion, and the concentration-response from rats. So the
  paper reports no maternal pregnancy pharmacokinetics of any kind. **Matches.**
- The row's `cl_factor`, `v_factor`, `f_factor` and `gfr_factor` are all `NA`, and this paper could
  not support any of them: there is no pregnant maternal clearance or volume in it to compare with a
  non-pregnant one. **Consistent.**
- The row's `design: review` and `confidence: low` are fair for this source. It is a modelling
  paper, not a measurement in pregnant women.
- The row also cites **Alshabi 2023 (PMID 37060457)** for the in vitro CYP2C9 result. That paper is
  not read here, so the row's `evidence_read` cannot be advanced on this card alone -- see

- The paper supplies **no pharmacokinetic parameter** to stanpumpR, and the repo takes none from it.
  **Consistent.**
- Nothing in the paper contradicts a value stanpumpR holds.
