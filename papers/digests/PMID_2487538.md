# Carosella 1989: verapamil kinetics and ECG effects in elderly patients

PDF page n is printed page 416 + n.

## Study
Rome (Università Cattolica, geriatrics). 22 inpatients in sinus rhythm, 30-86 years, two groups of 11 (6 men, 5
women each). Group 1: mean 45 ± 11.37 years (30-63), hypertension 5, PSVT 2, CAD 4. Group 2: mean 79.67 ± 4.74 years
(73-86), hypertension 4, PSVT 2, CAD 5. Table 1 (PDF p2, rendered): weight 74.2 ± 10.1 vs 67.55 ± 11.3 kg; creatinine
clearance 67 ± 8.4 vs 55 ± 10 mL/min; smokers 3/8 vs 2/9; baseline HR 77.8 vs 75.4 bpm, MAP 104.8 vs 105.9 mmHg, PR
0.15 vs 0.15 s; no significant differences. No hepatic, renal or endocrine disease; no antiarrhythmic,
antihypertensive, antianginal or anticoagulant drugs.

Four steps: (1) 24-h control Holter; (2) verapamil **hydrochloride 0.1 mg/kg IV over 5 min**, plasma at 5, 10, 15,
30, 45 min and 1-24 h, Holter from 30 min before; (3) eight per group: single 120 mg oral HCl, at least 72 h later;
(4) same 16: 120 mg every 8 h for a week. Plasma verapamil and norverapamil by HPLC (Padrini; linear 5-500 ng/mL;
LOD 1-2 ng/mL). PR and RR from Holter as the mean of ten cycles at each sample and hourly. IV data fitted to a
two-compartment open model; parameters from coefficients and exponents; AUC by trapezoids. PD: linear model
E - E0 = S × C, using only points between appearance and disappearance of the peak effect; a sigmoid Emax was tried
and rejected because of counter-clockwise hysteresis.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **PR interval (Holter):** maximum prolongation delayed about 15 min after the peak plasma concentration after IV
  (the plasma peak was at the end of the 5-min infusion) and about 1 h after the oral peak, giving a
  counter-clockwise hysteresis loop (PDF p6, Fig 4). No ke0 or explicit time to peak effect is fitted.
- **PR slope, linear model (Table 3, PDF p5, rendered):** IV group I 0.1297 ± 0.062, group II 0.0692 ± 0.0323 ms per
  ng/mL (r 0.804, 0.9398); oral group I 0.028 ± 0.007, group II 0.1207 ± 0.0324 (p < 0.05 vs group 1). **Source
  conflict:** the text (PDF p6) gives 0.129 ± 0.01 (IV) and 0.121 ± 0.02 (oral) for the **elderly** and 0.069 ± 0.01
  and 0.028 ± 0.01 for the **younger**, and the abstract says the slope was greater in the elderly after both routes.
  Table 3 assigns the IV 0.1297 to group I (younger) and 0.0692 to group II; the oral values agree with the text.
  The table's IV column labels and the text cannot both be right; the SDs also differ (0.062 vs 0.01). Unresolved.
- **Blood pressure:** largest fall within 30 min of IV in both groups; MAP change -10.3 ± 9.1 (group I, NS) and
  -12.4 ± 7.4 mmHg (group II, p < 0.01 vs baseline).
- **Heart rate after IV:** -8.9 ± 4.1 (group I; five rose, six fell) and -10.8 ± 4.2 bpm (group II, p < 0.05). No
  time to the HR nadir reported.
- Long-term: PR significantly longer than at matched control times in the elderly (p < 0.0001); one 71-year-old had
  PR 0.21 s 16 h after IV. No bradyarrhythmias or AV block.

## Covariates
Age group only (no significant PK effect). Weight and creatinine clearance reported, not modelled.

## Caveats
Small groups of mixed cardiac inpatients; oral steps in 8 of 11 per group. Two-compartment fits summarised only as
means of derived parameters. Table 2 internally loose (see checks); Table 3 conflicts with the text on which group
had the steeper IV PR slope. PR slope fitted only on post-peak data, by the authors' choice.
