# David 2000: maternal and umbilical nitroglycerin after an IV bolus at caesarean delivery

## Study
- **The trial:** the same double-blind RCT as David 1998 (PMID 9464734): Virchow and Charite, June 1994 to July
  1996, 97 elective caesareans, and 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg or saline IV at the uterine puncture incision (p2). The 2000
  paper reports the PK and neonatal outcomes and cites the 1998 paper (ref 3) for fetal extraction.
- **PK set (p3):** 62 women, 30 of 32 given 0.25 mg and 32 of 34 given 0.5 mg. Four were excluded for sampling or
  analysis problems.
- **Maternal samples:** 57 women had both the 1- and 5-min samples and 5 had one. Some draws were about 1 min late
  and were still included in Figures 1-2.
- **Sampling (p2):**
  - The dose went into a cannula on the back of the right hand or the right wrist, followed by a 100 mL saline
    flush over 1 min.
  - Venous blood was drawn at 1 and 5 min, "usually" from an antecubital catheter **in the same arm**.
  - Umbilical blood was taken at cord clamping and grouped T1-T4 by the time from dose (half were in T1, 30-90 s).
- **Assay:** validated GC-MS. LLOQ 6 pg/mL for GTN and 25 pg/mL for the dinitrates; accuracy and precision better
  than ±10%.

**Fall from 1 to 5 min (derived):** 80/3.2 = 25-fold and 38/1.2 = 32-fold in 4 min. These give apparent
half-lives of 0.86 and 0.80 min. The paper itself quotes a "biologic half-life" of 2-2.5 min from a German
monograph (ref 11, p5), not from these data.

**Disagreements with other reports of the same trial:**
- Umbilical vein at 0.5 mg: the abstract gives 0.41 ng/mL. The Discussion (p6, rendered) gives 0.49, and David
  1998 Table 4 gives 0.489.
- Umbilical artery at 0.5 mg: 0.03 here, against 0.045 in David 1998.
- The 1998 paper says the 5-min maternal value was about one third of the 1-min value. The medians here fall 25-
  to 32-fold.
- The papers do not explain these differences. The analysed subsets differ (62 against 66).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **Neonates (Table II):** Apgar scores, umbilical pH, BP and pulse at 1, 5 and 10 min did not differ from placebo.
  In the 0.5 mg arm, about 25% had lower 1-min pulse and Apgar scores, which was not significant.
- **Transfers:** 3 of 62 infants were moved to the neonatal unit, none judged drug-related, against 1 of 31 on
  placebo.
- **Comparator (p6, cited):** in adults given 2.3 mg/h (0.5 mcg/kg/min) by continuous infusion, steady-state
  plasma is 1.6-5.2 ng/mL.
- **Concentrations:** the paper gives no maternal concentration-effect relation.

## Covariates
None analysed.

## Caveats
- The samples were drawn from the arm of the injection, after a 1-min flush.
- Medians only, with two maternal sampling times.
- The 1:1 vein/artery ratio at 0.25 mg is hard to reconcile with the "11-12 times" of the 1998 report. The
  authors call the ratios similar "irrespective of the dose", and the cause is not stated.
- There is an abstract-Discussion disagreement on the 0.5 mg umbilical vein value (0.41 against 0.49).
