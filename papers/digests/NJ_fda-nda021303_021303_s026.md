# Adderall XR label (NDA 021303 s026, 2013)

## Study
Prescribing information (PLR format). Section 12.3 summarises PK studies in healthy adults, healthy children 6-12 y, and adolescents (13-17 y) and children with ADHD; n given only for the sex (20 women, 20 men) and race comparisons.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Product: capsule with two types of drug-containing beads giving a "double-pulsed" delivery (section 11). Strengths 5-30 mg, equal masses of the four salts.

Total amphetamine base equivalence (section 11 table): 5 mg 3.1; 10 mg 6.3; 15 mg 9.4; 20 mg 12.5; 25 mg 15.6; 30 mg 18.8 mg.

Other facts (12.3): d:l salts 3:1 in both IR and XR. A single XR 20 mg gave plasma profiles of both isomers comparable to IR 10 mg twice daily 4 h apart (Figure 1: XR 20 mg at 8 am vs IR 10 mg at 8 am and noon, **fed state**, mean d- and l-amphetamine to 24 h, y axis 0-30 ng/mL; curve values not read). Linear PK over 5-30 mg in children 6-12, 10-40 mg in adolescents <=75 kg, 20-60 mg in adults and adolescents >75 kg; no unexpected accumulation in children. Body weight is the primary determinant of PK differences across age; CL/F, Vz/F and t1/2 rise with weight; children have higher mg/kg clearance and, after mg/kg dose normalisation, 30% lower exposure than adults. Exposure 20-30% higher in women, explained by mg/kg dose. Sprinkling on applesauce comparable to intact capsule; equal doses of different strengths bioequivalent. Metabolism and urinary-pH dependence as in the Adderall IR label (pKa 9.9; urinary recovery 1-75%).

Dosing (section 2): switching from divided IR doses to the same total daily XR dose once daily, "based on bioequivalence data". Children 6-12: start 10 mg once daily (or 5 mg), increments 5-10 mg weekly, maximum 30 mg/day. Adolescents 10 mg/day, may go to 20 mg/day after a week. Adults 20 mg/day. With or without food.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect relationship, EC50 or therapeutic range. Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Body weight (qualitative, no equation); urinary pH; CYP2D6 (qualitative); renal and hepatic function (qualitative).

## Caveats
- Only means quoted; no SD, n or study identity for the half-lives.
- Figure 1 is fed state, while McGough 2003 and the food-effect study are separately described; the "comparable" XR vs IR-bid statement is a profile comparison, not a description of the bead release mechanism.
