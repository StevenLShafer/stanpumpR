# Schulz 1984: kinetics of nitroprusside and its cyanide, thiosulphate and thiocyanate products

## Study
Narrative review (single author, Cologne), no new data; it summarises the author's own earlier work (Schulz 1979b,
1982, 1983) and the literature. Covers sodium nitroprusside (SNP) itself, cyanide (prussic acid, HCN), the
thiosulphate cofactor, and the thiocyanate end product, then argues for a fixed SNP + thiosulphate "mixed infusion"
kit (50 mg SNP + 50 mL 1% thiosulphate; p.246).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model is fitted for any species. Numbers reported (all review values, citing other papers):

- **SNP**: formula Na2[Fe(CN)5NO]·2H2O; CN- is 44% w/w and is completely released in the bloodstream (p.239, PDF
  p.1). Biological half-life about 2 min at "therapeutic" concentrations **in rats** (Hobel 1976; p.241). Distribution
  roughly extracellular volume (Kreye and Reske 1982, rat; p.241). Not orally absorbed (p.240). No human plasma
  half-life, clearance or volume measured.
- **Cyanide/HCN**: 98-99% of blood HCN is in erythrocytes (bound to methaemoglobin); plasma fraction 1.5 ± 0.6% in 26
  patients (Vesey 1976; p.242). Relative volume of distribution about 0.075 L/kg, variable with total load and
  methaemoglobin (Schulz 1982; p.243). Detoxification by rhodanese is **zero-order**, rate about 1 µg/kg/min in
  humans, limited by thiosulphate supply; this corresponds to an SNP infusion of about 2 µg/kg/min (Summary; p.243).
  Endogenous thiosulphate pool detoxifies about 50 mg SNP short-term (Ivankovich 1983; p.246). Physiological
  methaemoglobin binds about 10 mg HCN in total (Summary); about 8 mg HCN ≈ 18 mg SNP (p.247-248).
- **Thiosulphate**: not orally absorbed; serum half-life 15-20 min after IV bolus (Summary says about 15 min); 20-50%
  excreted unchanged in urine; volume assumed equal to extracellular space (p.244). Optimal CN:thiosulphate molar
  ratio in vitro about 1:3 (p.244).
- **Thiocyanate** (Schulz 1979b; 7 healthy subjects, 8 renal-insufficiency patients; oral KSCN 900-3000 mg, ~100%
  absorbed): Vd 0.25 L/kg healthy vs 0.36 L/kg renal insufficiency; elimination half-life 2.7 days healthy vs 9 days
  renal insufficiency; almost wholly renal; elimination constants inversely proportional to creatinine clearance
  (p.245, PDF p.7, confirmed on page image; Summary rounds to "about 3 days").

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, ke0 or EC50. Toxic-concentration bands (literature compilation):
- **HCN in erythrocytes/blood** (p.243): healthy < 1 µmol/L (25 subjects); < 150 µmol/L usually asymptomatic;
  150-250 headache, palpitations, hyperventilation; 250-350 metabolic acidosis and coma; 300-3000 µmol/L in fatal
  cases. Assay lower limit about 1 µmol/L (p.242).
- **Serum thiocyanate** (Fig. 3 legend, p.247): physiological 50-250 µmol/L; 1000 slight symptoms possible; 2000
  more serious; 4000 life-threatening. At 2-5 µg/kg/min SNP for days, toxic thiocyanate expected after about 7-14
  days with normal renal function, 3-6 days with severe renal impairment (p.248); Figs 3-4 show predicted
  accumulation curves for 1.25-10 µg/kg/min.
- Clinical: nitroglycerin non-response in 15-20% of patients vs SNP almost always effective (p.245). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Renal function for thiocyanate (Vd and half-life above; elimination constant proportional to creatinine
clearance, no equation given). Thiosulphate co-infusion raises the HCN-accumulation threshold (Fig. 2).

## Caveats
Review; SNP half-life and distribution are rat data; human SNP itself was never measured. HCN numbers depend on
whole-blood/erythrocyte vs plasma sampling. Advocacy for the author's own kit. Thiocyanate kinetics come from oral
KSCN in 15 people.
