# Soliman 2025: omeprazole PBPK/PD, CYP ontogeny and pediatric DDI

## Study
- Whole-body PBPK model of omeprazole and two metabolites (hydroxy-omeprazole, omeprazole sulphone) built in GastroPlus 9.8.2 (ADMET Predictor 9.5), then linked to a published indirect-response PD model for intragastric H+/pH, run in Pumas 2.2.0 (PDF p. 3).
- Development/verification data: 6 published clinical studies, 79 subjects (Table 1, PDF p. 3): healthy adults (IV 40 mg; PO 40 and 90 mg; 20 mg and 40 mg multiple-dose), and pediatric data (Marier 2004: 12 children, mean age 6.1 +/- 4.4 y, 10-20 mg PO multiple doses; Jacqz-Aigrain 1994 IV loading 60-80 mg/1.73 m2 then 40 mg/1.73 m2 q12h; two further IV/PO pediatric studies). Literature profiles were digitised, not patient-level data.
- Simulations: single 10 mg dose at 4, 6, 9 months and 1, 2, 6, 16, 32 y for CYP contributions; 8-day once-daily population simulation (n = 100 per age group) at 20 mg (adults, 37 y) and 0.7 mg/kg (children of 6 y, 2 y, 8 months, 4 months); hypothetical full CYP3A blockade DDI (CYP3A4 in adults; CYP3A4 and CYP3A7 in children).

## Model and parameters
- Physchem inputs (Table 2, PDF p. 8; text layer scrambled): MW 345.4 g/mol, logP 2.23, Fup about 4.99% (text layer shows "4.9852%"; assignment of the stray values not confirmed), reference solubility 0.0823 mg/mL at pH 7.4.
- Metabolism: CYP2C19, CYP2C9, CYP3A4 (liver and gut) with Michaelis-Menten parameters from in vitro data then Vmax re-fitted; CYP3A7 Km set 5.1x and Vmax 75% lower than the CYP3A4 values (p. 5-6). Competitive inhibition and mechanism-based inactivation of CYP2C19/CYP3A4 by omeprazole and its metabolites (Table 3, from Shirasaka 2013 and Zvyaga).
- Accuracy: adult predicted/observed AUC and Cmax within 1.25-fold (Discussion, p. 14); about 75% of pediatric simulations within 2-fold (p. 9, 14). Predicted fluconazole DDI ratio 5.65, said to match observed (p. 8).
- DDI classification by AUC ratio: weak 1.25-2, moderate 2-5, strong > 5 (p. 7).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- PD model: indirect response model of H+/K+-ATPase synthesis and degradation with irreversible (covalent) inhibition (kd), circadian rhythm and food effects; taken from Liu et al. (esomeprazole), assumed to apply to omeprazole and unchanged across ages. End point: intragastric pH, summarised as daily average pH and time with pH > 4 and > 6 on day 8 (p. 6).
- Reported anchors (Discussion p. 15): healthy adults, 20 mg single dose mean 24-h pH 1.8 and 30.4% time with pH > 4; multiple 20 mg doses 3.5 +/- 1.0 and 48.7 +/- 20.5%.
- Simulation result: at 0.7 mg/kg infants under 1 y had pH > 4 for about 100% of the day, above adults; pH > 6 duration was long in 4- and 9-month-olds (p. 15).
- Full CYP3A blockade raised exposure about 4-fold in children <= 2 y and about 2-fold at 6 y, but not significantly in adults; the pH response did not change significantly (p. 11, 16).
- No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, MEAC or potency ratio; not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Age/ontogeny (CYP3A4, CYP3A7, CYP2C19, CYP2C9 abundance) and age-dependent physiology via the GastroPlus PEAR module. CYP contribution to omeprazole metabolism: under 1 y, CYP3A4 and CYP2C19 dominate; by 6 y CYP2C19 about 72% (p. 13). No genotype, disease or liver-function covariates (p. 13).

## Caveats
Authors give: no CYP2C19 genetics or disease modelled; enteric coating not fully represented (tablet for adults, suspension for children); CYP3A7 kinetics assumed from CYP3A4; one pediatric subject excluded for a dosing error; Du Bois equation used to back-calculate weight in the 1994 study. Our additions: only about 75% of pediatric predictions within 2-fold; PD parameters are not re-estimated; fasting vs fed infant data conflict (p. 15).
