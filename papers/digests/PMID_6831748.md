# Mutschler 1983: pharmacokinetics of triamterene and its active sulfate metabolite

## Study
Narrative review of the Frankfurt group's work (several cited works "in press": Gilfrich et al. IV study, Knauf et al. renal failure, Mohrke et al. elderly). Assay: thin-layer chromatography with fluorodensitometry measuring triamterene (TA), p-hydroxytriamterene (OH-TA) and p-hydroxytriamterene sulfuric acid ester (OH-TA-ester) together in plasma/urine, no extraction, detection limit 10 pg per spot (PDF p6, printed 254). Number of subjects in the IV/oral crossover is not given in the text read. Printed pages 249-269 (PDF 2-22; PDF page = printed - 247).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect model. OH-TA-ester is pharmacologically active, about as potent as TA on transepithelial potential difference in the rat submaxillary duct (lumen application; Figs 2-3), and natriuretic/potassium-sparing in rat, dog and man (cited, PDF p4-5). No MEAC, ke0, EC50 or therapeutic range is stated. Not an opioid; audit: none.

## Covariates
Liver disease: in cirrhosis TA t1/2 about 4 times longer than in healthy subjects, ester t1/2 unchanged, ester formation reduced (hydroxylation is the rate-limiting step); in hepatitis biliary excretion of TA is abolished and urinary recovery rises from about 40-50% to over 80% of a dose. Renal disease (32 patients): TA AUC unrelated to creatinine clearance; OH-TA-ester AUC rises hyperbolically as creatinine clearance falls; renal clearance of both falls with it; avoid in creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min. Age: see Table 2.

## Caveats
Narrative review from a scan with a garbled text layer; most primary data are "in press" elsewhere; subject numbers and demographics largely missing for the IV/oral study; V reported per kg without body weight; units mix ng/mL and ug/mL*min; the IV half-life is from urinary excretion rate, not plasma; elderly group is patients (older, ill) versus young volunteers.
