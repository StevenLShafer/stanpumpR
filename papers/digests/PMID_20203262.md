# Limdi 2010 (IWPC): one VKORC1 SNP predicts warfarin dose in all three racial groups

The International Warfarin Pharmacogenetics Consortium's test of whether VKORC1 -1639G>A alone is
enough, against other VKORC1 SNPs and against haplotypes, in Asian, black and white patients. In
stanpumpR it is not a pharmacokinetic source: it supplies the **ancestry distribution of the three
-1639G>A genotypes**, which is what lets `R/constants.R` say how likely the default genotype is in
a given patient.

**Why this card was written by hand.** `digest_run.py` records this paper as refused by the model's
safeguards even when batched alone. The refusal is at card-writing only — the PDF, its text and its
context file were filed normally. See *Caveats*.

## Study
A retrospective cohort of patients on stable chronic warfarin, pooled by the IWPC from sites on
several continents. **4886 participants** entered the analysis cohort (Results, PDF p. 3; Table 1
gives their clinical and demographic characteristics by race). The outcome is the stable
therapeutic warfarin dose, modelled on its square root.

**91 Indian subjects were excluded from every analysis**, because their VKORC1 allele frequencies
differed substantially from the other Asian participants (Results, PDF p. 3). The paper returns to
this in the Discussion, noting South Indians differ from other Asian groups. So the cohort’s Asian
figure does not describe a South Indian patient, and the paper offers no figure that does.

Genotyping covered four VKORC1 SNPs (-1639G>A, 1173C>T, 1542G>C, 2255C>T), with haplotypes inferred
from six SNPs in the subset having complete data (247 Asians, 365 blacks, 556 whites).

## Model and parameters

stanpumpR is the allele frequency by group, from **Table 3 (PDF p. 4, rendered)**.

**The headline result.** Adding further VKORC1 SNPs to -1639G>A did not improve dose prediction in
Asians or blacks (all P > .18); in whites 1173C>T and 1542G>C added 0.3% to 1%, which the authors
call unlikely to be clinically relevant. Haplotypes did no better than the single SNP in any group
(Asians P = .9, blacks P = .96, whites P = .15; Table 4). Hence the title: one SNP suffices.

**Why the variance explained differs by race.** Not a different biological effect — the -1639A
allele lowers dose similarly in all three groups. It is the allele frequency: a SNP explains most
variance when it is common but not fixed, and the paper's simulation puts the maximum at a MAF of
60-70%. So the low R-squared in blacks (4.2%) follows from A being rare there (10.05%), and the
high Asian frequency (91.03%) likewise limits how much variance it can explain within that group.
(The paper's simulated maximum is at a **-1639A allele frequency** of 60-70%, not at a
minor-allele frequency: a minor allele cannot exceed 50%, and the Asian row's 91.03% is the major
allele. The card said MAF here until CodeRabbit caught it on PR #888, 2026-11-25; it is the same
loose labelling the open question below records in the paper's own Table 3.)

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
The end point is the **stable therapeutic warfarin dose** in mg/week, modelled on its square root;
no concentration is measured and no INR time course is modelled. Predicted mean doses, from Table 4
(PDF p. 4): Asians 21.55 +/- 6.56, blacks 40.79 +/- 8.18, whites 33.23 +/- 11.02 mg/week. There is
no PK model, no EC50 and no effect compartment anywhere in the paper.

## Covariates
The multivariable model adjusts for CYP2C9 genotype, age, height, weight, enzyme-inducer status and
amiodarone status (Table 3 footnote), restricted to the 1638 participants with complete data (619
Asian, 354 black, 665 white). Race is the stratifying variable throughout rather than a covariate
in one pooled model. No pharmacokinetic covariate is fitted, because no concentration is measured.

## Caveats
- **Not a pharmacokinetic study.** No warfarin concentrations, no clearance, no INR model. It
  associates genotype with maintenance dose. Nothing here can be used to parameterise a PK model.
- **Retrospective, on stable patients.** Everyone in the cohort had already reached a stable dose,
  so it says nothing about induction, time to stability, or over-anticoagulation on the way.
- **The Asian group excludes South Indians**, by the authors' own decision (n = 91). The 91.03%
  figure must not be applied to a South Indian or other South Asian patient; the paper supplies no
  figure for them, and `R/constants.R` says so.
- **The racial groups are the paper's own labels**, self-identified and coarse, standing in for
  ancestry. The paper's own argument is that what matters is the allele frequency, not race, and
  that the frequency is what differs. A measured genotype always beats inferring one from ancestry,
  which is what `R/constants.R` and the help page both tell the user.
- **MAF is loosely labelled** -- see *open_questions*.
- **Supplemental material is not in the archived PDF**, including the global haplotype survey of
  8751 subjects that supports the claim of consistency within groups across countries.
