# Passot 2005: TCI vs manual propofol vs etomidate/desflurane, hip fracture, elderly

## Study
Prospective randomised, 3 groups, age >80 yr, ASA I-II, >40 kg, hip fracture surgery. Analysed n: ETO/DES 16, TCI 18, MAN 15 (abstract; Results text says 17/18/17 enrolled, 1 and 2 excluded for missing data). Table 1 (image-confirmed): age 89/88/88 yr, weight 57/56/55 kg, height 160/158/159 cm; female 14/16/11 of 16/18/15. All: hydroxyzine 1 mg/kg orally, alfentanil 20 ug/kg bolus then 0.4 ug/kg/min, 60% O2/40% N2O.
- TCI: Graseby 3400 pump with STANPUMP, plasma-targeted, Dyck and Shafer age/weight-adjusted model, tPeak 2.3 min (Methods, printed p1339). Start 1 ug/mL, +0.5 ug/mL every 3 min to loss of eyelash reflex; maintenance changes by 20%.
- MAN: 1 mg/kg over 60 s, then 5 mg/kg/h, adjusted by 50%; extra 10 mg boluses.
- ETO/DES: etomidate 0.4 mg/kg, desflurane from 2.5% end-tidal.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End points were MAP/HR stability and recovery times, not drug effect modelling. No predicted effect-site concentrations are reported in the text read. Opioid audit: alfentanil dose only; no MEAC, ke0, tPeak (except propofol tPeak 2.3 min assumed by the TCI model for propofol; end point not stated), and no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Age and weight in the Dyck and Shafer model (as stated).

## Caveats
Small n, no depth-of-anaesthesia monitor, hemodynamic titration only, non-blinded; MAN induction different design from TCI so differences also reflect induction technique. Authors note effect-site targeting not tested in elderly.
