# Theil 1993: computer-assisted vs manual fentanyl and midazolam infusion for CABG

## Study
Randomized, double-blind comparison, 24 adults for elective CABG (LVEF > 40%), 12 per group: manually controlled infusion (MCI, Bard Alfentanil Infuser) vs computer-assisted continuous infusion (CACI, pharmacokinetic-model-driven pump), the other device running saline. Age 62.3 +/- 8.1 (MCI) and 62.3 +/- 5.0 y (CACI); weight 81.3 +/- 16.0 and 77.3 +/- 15.7 kg; 9/3 and 10/2 men/women; anaesthesia 246.5 +/- 35.0 vs 230.8 +/- 44.1 min (Table 3, PDF p3). Premedication methadone 10-15 mg plus diazepam 5-10 mg or secobarbital 200 mg orally. Midazolam and fentanyl as separate infusions, titrated to keep MAP and HR within 20% of baseline; vecuronium 100 mcg/kg; FiO2 1.0; adjuvants (nitroglycerin, nitroprusside, vasopressors, some isoflurane/enflurane) comparable between groups (Table 4).
Dosing: MCI induction midazolam bolus 100-150 mcg/kg, CACI target 75-150 ng/mL; fentanyl begun 3 min after induction, MCI 2.5 mcg/kg bolus then 0.05-0.10 mcg/kg/min, CACI plasma setpoint 2-7 ng/mL; setpoints raised by 1-2 ng/mL fentanyl or 25-50 ng/mL midazolam (max 150 ng/mL) for hypertension.
Assays: arterial blood into heparinized tubes on ice, plasma at -70 C, at the 7 timepoints of Table 2. Fentanyl by radioimmunoassay (limit 0.2 ng/mL); midazolam by GC-ECD after benzene extraction (linear 5-500 ng/mL). These are total PLASMA concentrations, arterial.
Results: MAP and HR control did not differ between groups. Mean total fentanyl 30.27 +/- 11.14 (MCI) vs 34.61 +/- 5.40 mcg/kg (CACI, not different); midazolam total 267.79 +/- 84.22 vs 191.85 +/- 49.51 mcg/kg (P<0.05) (Table 6, PDF p5). No recall in any patient.

## Model and parameters
No model is fitted. The CACI pump used published sets (page 301, PDF p3). Constants as printed (V1 per kg; rate constants per min):
- fentanyl, from McClain and Hug 1980: V1 0.356 L/kg; k31 0.020, k21 0.103, k10 0.041, k12 0.185, k13 0.141 /min.
- midazolam, from Smith et al 1981: V1 0.581 L/kg; k31 0.00844, k21 0.0084, k10 0.013, k12 0.0366, k13 0.003 /min.
Derived for 70 kg (derived: V1 x weight, CL = k10 x V1, Q = k1x x V1, V = Q / k): 
- fentanyl V1 24.9 L, V2 44.8 L, V3 175.7 L, CL 1.02 L/min, Q2 4.61, Q3 3.51 L/min (Vss 245 L).
- midazolam V1 40.7 L, V2 177 L, V3 14.5 L, CL 0.529 L/min, Q2 1.49, Q3 0.122 L/min (Vss 232 L).
Predictive accuracy of the CACI predictions (CACI group, n = 12; 82 fentanyl and 67 midazolam pairs; p304, PDF p5): fentanyl MDPE -14%, MDAPE 28%; midazolam MDPE +9%, MDAPE 25% (PE = (measured - predicted)/predicted x 100, so a negative fentanyl MDPE means measured below predicted). ## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit. No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, MEAC or minimum effective/analgesic concentration was measured, and no equianalgesic or potency ratio is stated. The only end point was haemodynamic stability (MAP and HR within 20% of pre-induction baseline) plus absence of recall. The plasma targets the authors used, which they say come from earlier studies and experience (discussion, PDF p6): fentanyl 5-8 ng/mL before CPB and 2-5 ng/mL during CPB and after; midazolam 100-200 ng/mL before CPB and 50-100 ng/mL during CPB and after; induction loading fentanyl 4-8 mcg/kg and midazolam 0.08-0.15 mg/kg; maintenance fentanyl 0.08-0.20 mcg/kg/min, midazolam 0.4-0.7 mcg/kg/min. These are clinical setpoints for a combined midazolam + fentanyl technique in CABG patients on methadone premedication, not defined as effective concentrations. The authors note fentanyl levels were below those reported by Wynands for fentanyl-only anaesthesia and attribute adequacy to the interaction with other agents; fentanyl-midazolam additive hypotension is mentioned (negative inotropy, vasodilation). Measured fentanyl in practice: 2.5-6.8 ng/mL means (Table 9; plasma, arterial, RIA).

## Covariates
None analysed. Weight enters only through the per-kg dosing and the per-kg V1 of the pump constants.

## Caveats
Small (n = 12 per arm), older cardiac surgical patients, cardiopulmonary bypass with haemodilution and hypothermia, methadone and benzodiazepine premedication, occasional volatile agents and several vasoactive drugs; concentrations from single arterial samples at 7 events. CACI predictive error is for the pump's own old kinetic sets (fentanyl over-predicted by 14% in median). Midazolam samples missing for 3 patients (contamination: 1 CACI, 2 MCI), so Figure 2 has n = 11 and 10. Abstract and tables disagree on induction concentrations (open question). Two figures (Figs 2-3) show individual points only.

## Verification against stanpumpR
No cited location, so no verdict. Comparisons with the library's own models (derived, as above):
- Fentanyl: library is Bae 2020 (R/drugs_fentanyl.R:76-85): at 70 kg V1 10.1, V2 26.5, V3 206 L, CL 0.704, Q2 2.38, Q3 1.49 L/min (Vss 242.6 L). The McClain-Hug set Theil's pump used gives, at 70 kg, V1 24.9 L (2.5x larger), CL 1.02 L/min (45% higher), Q2 4.61, Q3 3.51, V2 44.8, V3 175.7, Vss 245 L (near Bae's). Theil's median performance error of -14% (measured below predicted) is in the direction expected from a model with a larger V1 and faster clearance than the library's refit; arterial samples from CABG patients under bypass, so this is not a like-for-like check.
- Midazolam: library is the Buhrer set (R/drugs_midazolam.R:150-157; at the 70 kg reference approximately V1 3.0, CL1 0.50 L/min after the size factors; published V1 3.3, V2 17.56, V3 96.76 L, Cl1 0.54, Cl2 2.01, Cl3 0.83 L/min, Vss 117.6 L). Smith's set from Theil's pump gives V1 40.7 L, CL 0.529 L/min (matches the library's CL1 0.535 closely), Vss 232 L (2x the library's), Q2 1.49 L/min. Theil's midazolam MDPE of +9% with this set is within the range the authors call adequate; it does not test the library's set.
- The paper's concentration targets (fentanyl 2-8 ng/mL, midazolam 50-200 ng/mL) can be set against the library's fentanyl MEAC 0.6 ng/mL (R/drugs_fentanyl.R:114) and midazolam typical band 0.101-0.304 mcg/mL (drugs_midazolam.R:191-193): the paper's midazolam range overlaps the library's band; the paper's fentanyl range is well above the library's MEAC because it is a surgical (CPB) anaesthetic target in the presence of midazolam, not an analgesic threshold.
