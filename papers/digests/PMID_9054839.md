# Landry 1997: vasopressin deficiency in vasodilatory septic shock

## Study
Observational and interventional ICU study (Columbia-Presbyterian and Allegheny), adults (>= 18 y, no suspected
pregnancy); no ages, weights or sex reported (PDF p 2).
- **Septic shock** (n = 19): Bone 1992 criteria, SVR < 800 dyne.s/cm5 before catecholamines, on catecholamines
  1-2 days after fluid loading (PCWP >= 12 mm Hg). **Cardiogenic shock** (n = 12): SAP <= 90, PCWP > 15, cardiac
  index <= 2 L/min (unit as printed), also on catecholamines (PDF p 2).
- Plasma vasopressin by **radioimmunoassay** (Sakurai 1985, ref 19); reference range (95%) in hemodynamically normal
  subjects < 2.2 pg/mL at serum osmolality < 285 mOsm/kg; sensitivity 0.3 pg per tube (PDF p 3). Concentrations are
  mass (pg/mL), not units; no U/mg conversion stated.
- Drug: vasopressin injection USP (8-arginine vasopressin), central vein, **0.04 U/min** (10 septic patients) and
  **0.01 U/min** (2 patients for steady-state concentration; 6 patients in the withdrawal/re-start study) (PDF p 3-4).
- Statistics: paired/unpaired t tests.

The paper says 27 and 34 pg/mL are the values expected at this rate in other subjects (ref 22, Mohring 1980), and
concludes clearance is not increased in septic shock; no clearance number is given.
(derived: 0.01 U/min = 10 mU/min; a plateau of about 30 pg/mL total.)

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: systolic/diastolic arterial pressure (radial line), cardiac output (thermodilution), SVR.
- **0.04 U/min, n = 10, septic shock on catecholamines** (Table 2, PDF p 6, image-confirmed; Pre-AVP vs AVP,
  mean +/- SE): SAP 92 +/- 4 -> 146 +/- 4 (P < .001); DAP 52 +/- 5 -> 66 +/- 3 (P < .05); CO 6.6 +/- 1.0 -> 5.8 +/- 0.3
  L/min (P < .01); SVR 664 +/- 55 -> 1187 +/- 8 dyne.s/cm5 (P < .001); HR 111 +/- 9 -> 111 +/- 12 (NS). AVP values are
  the 15-minute interval of peak SAP within the first hour (PDF p 3). Catecholamines reduced when SAP > 130; 6 of 10
  maintained on vasopressin alone.
- **Onset**: the pressure rose within minutes (< 15 min) of starting the infusion (PDF p 3). No bolus, no ke0,
  t1/2ke0 or tPeak, no concentration-effect model.
- **0.01 U/min, n = 6 on vasopressin alone** (Fig 2, PDF p 5; text PDF p 4): stopping gave SAP 126 -> 83 (+/- 3) mm Hg
  within minutes; restarting at 0.01 U/min gave 83 -> 115 mm Hg (P < .01 each). SVR not measured.
- Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Small n; the ~30 pg/mL "steady state" at 0.01 U/min rests on **two patients**, with no stated sampling time.
- Internal inconsistencies, image-confirmed: Abstract SVR baseline 644 vs Table 2 664 dyne.s/cm5; Abstract
  cardiogenic shock SAP 99 +/- 7 and CO 3.5 +/- 0.9 vs Table 1 101 +/- 3 and 3.6 +/- 0.4; septic AVP SE 1.0 (Abstract) vs
  0.4 (Fig 1). Table 1 septic SVR is 837 +/- 78 (on catecholamines), above the < 800 entry criterion measured before them.
- Fig 1 shows one cardiogenic patient near 4 pg/mL and all septic values below about 8 pg/mL.
- RIA immunoreactive mass, not bioassay units; the paper gives no U/mg factor.
