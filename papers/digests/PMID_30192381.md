# Ounissi 2019: vigabatrin S(+)/R(-) enantiomer PK in infants and a minimal effective dose

## Study
- Part of SoluWest (EudraCT 2014-000360-17); 28 children, 0.4-5.7 y, on oral vigabatrin (Kigabeq soluble 100/500 mg tablets), given twice a day. Exclusions: >2 other antiepileptics, gastric-tube dosing, weight <4 kg (PDF p3).
- Protocol 1 (n=13, infantile spasms already stabilised): 4-6 samples each, 144 concentrations including both enantiomers (derived from Table 1 text: 174 total with protocol 2's 30). Used to build the model. Protocol 2 (n=15, new-onset): one pre-dose trough each, used as the validation set. 87 concentrations per enantiomer overall (PDF p7). None below LLOQ 0.2 ug/mL.
- Table 1 (PDF p5, text layer): median age 1.42 y (0.4-5.7), median weight 9.55 kg (6.5-22.8), median dose 84.92 mg/kg/day of racemate (38.8-140.5); 14 boys, 14 girls.
- Assay: LC-MS/MS of the two enantiomers after OPA/NAC derivatisation, plasma, 0.2-50 ug/mL, intra/interday precision and accuracy <15%.
- Fit: Monolix 4.3.2 (SAEM). R and S fitted independently; half the racemic dose entered as the dose of each enantiomer.
- Adult comparison model: built from S(+) concentrations of 6 healthy volunteers published by Haegele and Schechter 1986 (reference 2), then scaled to children by allometry and renal maturation.

## Model and parameters
Pediatric final model (Table 2, PDF p6, confirmed on rendered page): one compartment, zero-order absorption (duration Tk0), no lag, first-order elimination, proportional residual error. Reference weight is the dataset median, 12.1 kg. Parameter_i = TV x (BW/12.1)^theta.

Final model, (S)-vigabatrin (the active enantiomer):
- Tk0 = 0.682 h (RSE 37%); V/F = 17 L (RSE 13%), theta_BW on V/F = 1 (RSE 59%); Cl/F = 2.36 L/h (RSE 8%), theta_BW on Cl/F = 1.04 (RSE 34%).
- IIV (omega): V 0.38 (27%), Cl 0.245 (24%); no IIV for Tk0, interoccasion variability on Tk0 1.02 (31%); shrinkage Tk0 74%, V 28%, Cl 21%; residual error 0.229 (12%).
- Abstract states IIV as 24.5% (Cl) and 38% (V), consistent with omega 0.245 and 0.38.
- Theory-based allometry (0.75 / 1) combined with a maturation function could not be estimated (PDF p7). Weight was the only covariate retained; age, height, creatinine clearance (Schwartz) and sex were not.

Final model, (R)-vigabatrin: Tk0 0.521 h, V/F 9.54 L (theta 1.01), Cl/F 1.9 L/h (theta 0.771), omega V 0.255, omega Cl 0.205, IOV Tk0 1.01, residual 0.325.

Adult model scaled to children (S enantiomer, PDF p6): two compartments, zero-order input with lag. Tlag 0.241 h, Tk0 0.111 h, Cl/F 11.5 L/h, V1/F 54.6 L, Q/F 6.85 L/h, V2/F 41.8 L. Scaling: Fsize = (BW/70)^alpha with alpha 1 (volumes) and 0.75 (clearances); Cl multiplied by the Rhodin maturation PMA^3.40/(47.7^3.40 + PMA^3.40), PMA in weeks (Eqs 7-8). This version predicted the pediatric data with MPE 23.2%, RMSE 5; the pediatric model on the validation set gave MPE <16%, RMSE <6, r 0.81 (R) and 0.85 (S).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD data and no concentration-effect relation: the authors state none exists for infantile spasms (PDF p11). No ke0, EC50, tPeak or hysteresis. Not an opioid, so no MEAC or potency ratio.
- Efficacy target is an exposure surrogate: the S(+) AUC reached by the 36 mg/kg/day racemic dose, which the randomized trial (Elterman, refs 14-15) found suboptimal (18-36 mg/kg/day lower efficacy than 100-148 mg/kg/day). Risk = percentage of the 95% CI of simulated steady-state S AUC at a dose overlapping the 95% CI at 36 mg/kg/day.
- Table 3 (PDF p10, text layer): overlap risk, pediatric model / adult model: 50 mg/kg/day 69.6% / 67.8%; 75: 11.4% / 12.0%; 80: 6.1% / 7.7%; 95: 0% / 0.5%; 100: 0% / 0%. Median S AUC (ug.h/mL): 46.4 at 36; 65.1 at 50; 103.9 at 80; 129.4 at 100. Simulated steady-state racemic trough: median 2.48 ug/mL at 36 mg/kg/day to 7.25 at 100 (95% CI 0.33-6.8 to 1.04-19.5).
- Conclusion: 80 mg/kg/day as a minimal effective dose (<10% overlap); the authors say dosing should continue to follow clinical effect until a concentration-effect relation exists.
- Adult-derived reference range for racemic vigabatrin trough, 0.8-36 ug/mL (refs 25-26): 91-99% of simulated troughs fell in it even at 36 mg/kg/day, so the authors judge it uninformative for infants. Assay: plasma, total (vigabatrin is reported as not protein bound), racemate.
- Weight-normalised values from the pediatric Bayesian estimates (PDF p9, discussion): S clearance 0.19 L/h/kg, t1/2 5.0 +/- 0.8 h, apparent V 1.4 L/kg; R clearance 0.16 L/h/kg, t1/2 3.5 +/- 0.4 h, V 0.8 L/kg.

## Covariates
- Body weight only, empirical allometric power on Cl/F and V/F (Eq 1). Exponent for S Cl/F 1.04, for S V/F 1.0; for R, 0.771 and 1.01.
- Age, height, Schwartz creatinine clearance, sex: tested after weight, none kept. The authors warn the model should not be used outside the ages or weights studied (about 0.4-5.7 y, 6.5-22.8 kg).
- Adult-scaled model: theory allometry plus renal maturation by PMA (Eq 8), no estimated covariates.

## Caveats
- Small pilot (13 profile subjects), narrow age and weight range; the exponent for S Cl/F of 1.04 has RSE 34% and is not the 0.75 theory value, so the apparent weight-independence of dose-normalised AUC rests on it.
- Parameters are apparent (oral, F unknown) and per enantiomer on half the racemic dose; the R and S enantiomers differ (V/F 9.54 vs 17 L).
- The adult model treats glomerular filtration as the only elimination route, which the authors call a simplification.
- Only 28 children, almost all infantile spasms (protocol 2 includes 4 with pharmacoresistant focal epilepsy); the authors report no concentration-response data.
- Several authors report industry ties (Orphelia Pharma patent on the dosage form).
