# Eleveld 2014: general-purpose propofol PK model

## Study
- Pooled re-analysis of 21 previously published propofol datasets (Open TCI site plus datasets supplied by the authors): 660 individuals (433 men, 227 women), 684 occasions, 10,927 plasma concentrations (7024 arterial, 3903 venous). Age 0.25-88 y, weight 5.2-160 kg, BMI 11.2-53. 500 patients and 160 healthy volunteers (PDF p4, journal p1224; abstract, p1).
- Assay concentrations were treated as plasma concentrations. NONMEM 7.2, FOCE with interaction, PLT-Tools 4. Three-compartment, log-normal IIV, proportional plus additive error with per-dataset proportional error and between-individual proportional-error variance (eta8).
- Reference individual: 70 kg, 35 y, 170 cm male patient. Height was missing for 108 individuals (17-88 y); BMI 24.2 was assumed for them.
- Predictive-performance metric (intraoperative): percent of observations with APE <=20% minus percent with APE >60%, using patients only, observations 0.5-8 ug/mL, and t > 2 min; averaged over 5 subgroups with equal weight; 2-fold cross-validation during model building.
- No effect-site / PD data; no ke0.

## Model and parameters
Final model (equations on PDF p7, journal p1227, confirmed on the page image). Units: V in L, CL/Q in L/min, WGT kg, AGE y, PMA = AGE + 40/52 y.

- fsigmoid(x, E50, lambda) = x^lambda / (x^lambda + E50^lambda)
- ADLT = fsigmoid(WGT, 16.6, 2.75); faging(x) = exp(-0.001 * (AGE - 35) * x)
- CLAG = 1 - fsigmoid(PMA, 69.2, 8); PMAL = fsigmoid(PMA, 22.8, 8) for males, 0 for females
- KGEN = exp(0.225 * (CLAG*(1 - PMAL) + (1 - CLAG)*PMAL) * ADLT)
- V1 = {5.74 healthy, 9.77 patient} * ADLT/ADLTref * {1 male, 0.830 female} * faging(3.55) * exp(eta)
- V2 = {11.8 healthy, 29.0 patient} * (WGT/70) * faging(8.91)
- V3 = {222 healthy, 134 patient} * (WGT/70)^0.35 * ADLT/ADLTref
- CL = {1.83 healthy, 1.53 patient} * (WGT/70)^0.75 * KGEN/KGENref * faging(3.55)
- Q2 = {3.10 healthy, 1.42 patient} * (V2/29.0)^0.75 * faging((1 - ADLT)*27.6 + ADLT*6.34)
- Q3 = {1.08 healthy, 0.608 patient} * (V3/134)^0.75 * faging(6.34); no IIV on Q3 in patients
- Compartmental allometry: Q2 and Q3 scale with the individual's own V2 and V3 (normalised to the reference values 29.0 and 134), not with weight.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD. No ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak, EC50 or MEAC reported. Not an opioid paper (opioid audit: none). The authors suggest that patient-versus-volunteer differences (lower CL, Q2, Q3, V3 in patients) may reflect concomitant opioids and other drugs, but concomitant medication was not documented well enough to model.

## Covariates
- Weight (allometric; V1 sigmoidal in weight so that V1 is flat above ~30 kg), age (exponential decline in V1, V2, CL, Q2, Q3; none for V3), sex (V1 0.830 in women; CL sex term via PMAL/KGEN), postmenstrual age (CL maturation, KGEN), and patient versus healthy volunteer (all parameters). The authors recommend the patient parameters in nearly all clinical situations.
- Height and BMI were not covariates in the final equations (BMI was used only to define the high-BMI performance subgroup).
- Sampling site (arterial vs venous) added no benefit for V1 in the final model.

## Caveats
- Front-end kinetics are not captured: the final model over-predicts the first 15 min after administration (Figure 8); late samples (<0.025 ug/mL, >10 h) show bias.
- Ages ~12-20 y are under-represented; young children all come from one study; high-BMI subgroup performed worst (MDPE -5.9%, MDAPE 26.7%, metric 17.4%).
- Final-model performance: patients, intraoperative filter, MDPE/MDAPE (%): young children -1.4/15.5, children -6.9/18.1, adults -3.7/19.3, elderly -3.3/18.6, high BMI -5.9/26.7 (Table 5, text layer; Figure 7 prints the same values for children, adults, elderly, high BMI). Complete dataset: MDPE -1.4%, MDAPE 21.5% (p1229 text).
- Patient/volunteer split is a proxy for unrecorded co-medication; V3 may be underestimated because sampling was short (authors cite 1390-3940 L in the literature).
