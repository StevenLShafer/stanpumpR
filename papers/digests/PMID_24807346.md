# Bapat 2014: dabigatran and its prodrug across the perfused human placenta

**Exclusion notice.** Gideon Koren is an author. Under the repository's rule (AGENTS.md:161, Shafer 2026-11-16) no
paper of his is cited or used, as a source, cross-check or background. This card records the paper's content only so
an audit can see what was dropped; nothing below should be cited by stanpumpR.

## Study
- Ex vivo dual perfusion of a single isolated cotyledon from term placentae after uncomplicated caesarean delivery
  (Toronto, St Michael's Hospital). Six cotyledons from six placentae: 3 perfused with dabigatran, 3 with
  dabigatran etexilate mesylate (the prodrug). Median cotyledon weight 18.90 g (IQR 12.26-24.69) (p. 3, printed 1258).
- Closed circuits: maternal 250 mL, fetal 150 mL perfusate (M199 with dextran; antipyrine 1 mM in the maternal side as a
  flow marker). Flow 14.10 (IQR 13.98-14.38) mL/min maternal, 2.62 (2.09-2.90) mL/min fetal (p. 3).
  Maternal pH 7.4, fetal 7.35. 1 h control phase, then 3 h experimental phase.
- Drug added to the maternal reservoir: dabigatran 35 ng/mL, or the prodrug 3.5 ng/mL (p. 2, printed 1257).
- Assay: UPLC-MS/MS (ABSciex QTRAP5500), dabigatran-d7 internal standard; standards 0-50 ng. Perfusate, not blood or
  plasma; no protein in the perfusate is stated beyond dextran.
- Statistics: medians and IQR, Wilcoxon signed-rank.

## Caveats
- n = 3 per drug; term placentae only, so not extrapolable to earlier gestation (authors). Transporters
  (P-glycoprotein, BCRP) not studied.
- Ratios at 3 h are not at equilibrium (dabigatran's ratio still rising in Figure 2A); no maternal-fetal clearance
  index is given.
- The Discussion gives dabigatran's molecular weight as 628 Da; the mass transitions (472.1 for dabigatran, 628.2 for
  the prodrug, p. 3) show 628 is the prodrug's, so that sentence mixes the two.
- The prodrug's ratio is 0.17 in Results and abstract, 0.16 in the Discussion.
- "Therapeutic" 35 ng/mL is a low trough-range value; the authors assume (not test) the same ratio at 150 ng/mL.
- Placental transfer is not maternal pharmacokinetics; nothing here bears on maternal CL or V in pregnancy.
