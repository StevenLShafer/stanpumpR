# Mandema 1992: building population PK-PD models, covariate effects

## Study
Methods paper proposing a three-step covariate search for NONMEM population models (p. 512-514):
1. Fit a base population model with no covariates; obtain empirical Bayes individual parameter estimates (NONMEM V4 level 1.0; exponential random effects, proportional residual error).
2. Regress the individual estimates on the covariates with a generalized additive model (GAM), built by stepwise addition/deletion on Akaike's criterion (AIC), with covariates left out, linear, or a natural cubic spline with two internal knots at the 33rd and 66th percentiles (4 df). S-plus 3.0.
3. Use the GAM as the starting model for a final NONMEM fit; covariance terms are explored there.

It extends Maitre 1991 (midazolam), which only inspected scatterplots of the Bayes estimates.

Four disguised real data sets illustrate it (p. 515-516): drug A, an NSAID given orally to 93 febrile children (411 samples; weight 5.6-45.5 kg); drug B, an oral antiarrhythmic in 136 men; drug C, an antihypertensive in 64 patients; drug D, an antibiotic in 74 critically ill patients. No drug is named.

## Covariates
Method findings (p. 522-527):
- Across the four drugs 27 covariates were significant in the final NONMEM models; 25 were already in the minimum-AIC GAM and the other 2 in GAMs close to it (p. 527).
- One-at-a-time screening both over-selects (drug D: 13 of 16 covariates, p<0.05) and misses covariates (RACE for drug A, SPB for drug D) (p. 526).
- GAM spline shapes flagged nonlinear effects (drug C: Cl on height above about 160 cm and on age above 60 years; replacing the age term by a straight line raised -2LL by 18.8, height by 7.4, p. 524).
- Weakness: empirical Bayes shrinkage when data are sparse (about 2.7 samples per drug B patient), covariates that change within a person, and no covariate interactions (p. 516-517, 525-527).

## Caveats
The authors state the examples are illustrative, not definitive, and that the final model is "useful" not "true" (p. 515, 527).
