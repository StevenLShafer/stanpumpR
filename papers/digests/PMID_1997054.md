# Hosie and Nimmo 1991: temazepam premedication, capsule vs elixir

## Study
Randomised, blinded study (Sheffield) in 100 ASA I-II elective surgical patients aged 16-70 (PDF p1-2): group 1
temazepam 30 mg capsules + placebo elixir (n 40; 8 M/32 F, age 41 (16-68), weight 68.5 kg (46.5-103)); group 2
temazepam 30 mg elixir + placebo capsules (n 40; 10 M/30 F, 42 (17-64), 71.2 kg (45.5-120)); group 3 placebo both
(n 20; 5 M/15 F, 46 (18-65), 67.9 kg (47-96.5)) (Table I). Premedication planned 1 h before surgery; surgery usually
started about 2 h after. Venous plasma every 15 min for 2 h; HPLC. Three patients not analysed. Many had residual
temazepam at time 0 from a hypnotic dose the previous evening. Sedation self-rated every 15 min (awake, drowsy,
sleepy; observer rated asleep if unresponsive to voice). Anxiety by VAS and Spielberger STAI before and 60 min after.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Sedation (four-point scale): significant in active groups at 30, 45, 60 min; plasma concentration at 60 min
correlated with sedation (Spearman r = 0.339, P < 0.005) but not with change in anxiety. Anxiety fell in all groups
at 60 min, including placebo on the STAI. Discussion: unsedated at mean 211, sedated above 785 ng/mL; authors say a
concentration above 200 ng/mL is needed to ensure sedation and that the study did not identify the concentration
for anxiolysis. They note agreement with Saletu 1986 (deterioration at about 250 ng/mL). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Age (time to peak only); anxiety state vs trait (no overall effect).

## Caveats
- The Summary says concentrations above 200 ng/mL are required "for sedation and anxiolysis"; the Discussion (PDF
  p5) says anxiolysis could not be related to concentration. The Summary overstates.
- The awake mean (211) itself exceeds 200, so ">200" is a loose floor; the sedated means are near 800.
- Table III averages per rating, not per patient; residual drug from the previous night inflates early values.
- Volunteers' peak time quoted as 1.6 h (ref 9) against 30 min here.
