# Johannessen Landmark 2026: cenobamate TDM series, Oslo

## Study
Retrospective observational therapeutic drug monitoring study, Section for Clinical Pharmacology, National Center for
Epilepsy, Oslo University Hospital; data 2022-2025, collected August 2024 to March 2025 (Methods 2.1). 258 patients
with refractory epilepsy on cenobamate (CNB) add-on therapy; 672 CNB measurements, 1-7 per patient (abstract;
Table 1 gives 917 "total number of serum concentration measurements", not reconciled in the paper). Table 1
(PDF p4, rendered): female 145 (57%; abstract 56%), male 113; age median 36 years (range 11-77), mean 39 (SD 14.6);
dose at last measurement median 150 mg/day (12.5-400), mean 164 (SD 88.6). Concomitant ASMs 1-6 (mean 1.91); most
common valproate 93, lamotrigine 81, clobazam 71. Samples drawn drug-fasting in the morning at steady state
(Methods 2.2). Assay: in-house UHPLC-MS/MS, measuring range 5-140 umol/L, validated 5-280 umol/L with dilution,
CV < 10%; serum, total concentration.

Group effects on CNB C/D (abstract, 3.2): strong inducers -9%, inhibitors +14% against neutral (p < .05); consistent
with the Figure 1D means (derived: 0.311/0.339 = 0.92, 0.384/0.339 = 1.13). 85% of measurements lay in the
15-130 umol/L range. Weight-corrected C/D 0.005 (women) and 0.004 (men) umol/L per mg (n=85).

Effect of CNB on other ASMs (Table 2, PDF p5, rendered; mean C/D before -> after; the % column is the range of
individual changes): clobazam n=57 0.03 -> 0.03; desmethylclobazam n=57 0.33 -> 0.70; desmethylclobazam/clobazam
ratio 13.08 -> 33.6; phenobarbital n=15 0.62 -> 0.86; carbamazepine n=7 0.05 -> 0.04; lamotrigine n=57 0.12 -> 0.09
(individual -74% to +7%); valproate n=53 0.34 -> 0.33; others unpredictable. The abstract's group changes
(desmethylclobazam +233%, ratio +344%, phenobarbital +36%, lamotrigine -28%, carbamazepine -26%) are not reproduced
by the ratios of the Table 2 means (derived: +112%, +157%, +39%, -25%, -20%); the paper does not say how its
percentages were computed, so this is unresolved. Clobazam dose was reduced 32% (14.55 -> 9.86 mg/day).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No efficacy or concentration-effect analysis. The reference range is adopted from Steinhoff 2024, and the authors
state it seems relevant in practice. GGT rose a mean 20% (n=68, Table 3). One case of intoxication with CNB 200
mg/day, high free valproate and desmethylclobazam (Figure 3B).

## Covariates
Comedication only (inducers, inhibitors). No sex difference (weight-corrected C/D). Renal and hepatic markers
(Table 3) did not change apart from GGT.

## Caveats
Retrospective, incomplete dose and comedication records, adherence unknown, CYP2C19 genotype unknown (4.4). Group
means of individual C/D ratios from a skewed distribution. Includes patients from age 11. The paper's mg/L conversion
of its own reference range is wrong (15 and 130 umol/L x 267.67 g/mol are 4.0 and 34.8 mg/L, derived).
