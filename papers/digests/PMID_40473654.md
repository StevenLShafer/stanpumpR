# Ayub 2025: PBPK model of nicardipine in renal and hepatic impairment

## Study
Whole-body PBPK model in PK-Sim 11.3 built from literature: 10 studies, 6 IV and 7 oral healthy profiles, 2 CKD and
1 cirrhosis (Child-Pugh A) profile, digitised from published graphs (p2). Table 1 (p2) lists the IV sources: 0.16
mg/kg (n 14, ref 33 Campbell 1985), 5 mg/h over 3 h (n 6, ref 34 Graham 1985), 0.885 mg (n 6, ref 35 Wagner 1987),
1.05 mg (n 1, ref 36 Hirota 1998), 5 mg (n 8, ref 37 Rocha 1990), 210 ug/kg over 30 min (n 8, ref 38 Modi 1993).
Virtual cohorts of 1000; evaluation by visual predictive check, predicted/observed ratios and average fold error
(AFE), 2-fold acceptance (p5-p6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Disease states as system changes: CKD with eGFR 35.01 and 26 mL/min/1.73 m2 (p5); cirrhosis CP-A per Table 3
(functional liver volume 0.81, haematocrit 36.6%, plasma protein factor 0.81, GFR 83.7 mL/min, blood-flow factors).
Recommended oral dose reductions (from 30 mg): CP-A 43%, CP-B 52%, CP-C 75%; moderate CKD 11%, severe CKD 14% (p6,
p10-p11).

## Caveats
- Internal inconsistencies: the abstract puts CP-A exposure about 1.5-fold healthy; the Discussion (p9) says AUC
  increased about 2.7-fold; Table 4 observed AUC 307.21 (LC) vs 83.06 ng.h/mL (healthy, same source ref 39) is
  3.7-fold (derived), and the predicted 173 vs 85.95 is 2.0-fold. Unresolved.
- CKD: observed exposure up 50-70% (p11; Table 4 gives 1.63 and 1.50 vs ref 41 healthy, derived), yet the
  recommended reductions are only 11-14%, because the model under-predicts CKD exposure (predicted/observed AUC
  0.87, 0.68).
- A renal clearance of 4 mL/min/kg is used although no unchanged nicardipine is excreted in urine (Graham 1985;
  Cardene IV label 12.3).
- The printed molecular formula C26H29N2O6 (p3) does not give 479.5 g/mol; nicardipine is C26H29N3O6 (a source typo).
- Table 1 lists the 40 mg oral study as ref 36 while Fig 3 legend labels 40 mg as ref 39 (minor labelling).
- Data digitised from figures. Supplement now read (2026-10-27); its clearances come from liver transplant
  patients (Drici 1993), not healthy volunteers.
