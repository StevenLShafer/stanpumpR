# Sandra 2024: lisinopril population PK in hypertensive children

## Study
- Lisi-ped, an open-label, single-arm dose-escalation pilot study (NCT02184858; Ghent University Hospital). 13 children and adolescents with primary or secondary hypertension: 1 infant, 5 children and 7 adolescents; 9 F and 4 M; 12 Caucasian and 1 Black (Table 2, PDF p6).
- Age 13.5 y (median; range 2.0 to 17.9 in Table 2, 1.9 to 17.9 in the text). Weight 53.2 kg (9.6 to 97.2). Height 163.8 cm. BSA 1.62 m². eGFR (bedside Schwartz 2009) 99.92 mL/min/1.73 m² (range 55.49 to 179.8). Three patients had eGFR < 60 (Table 2; Results, PDF p6).
- Oral lisinopril (Zestril tablets, or magistral capsules for young children), once daily between 07:00 and 09:00. The protocol started at 0.1 mg/kg/day and escalated by 0.1 mg/kg/day a month, up to 0.4. In practice, doses were 0.05 to 0.2 mg/kg (Figure 3, PDF p7). Renally impaired patients followed an adapted regimen.
- Sparse sampling: peak and trough samples at visits 3, 5 and 7, giving 46 concentrations from 13 patients at steady state (PDF p6).
- Assay: UPLC-MS/MS in plasma, with lisinopril-d5 as the internal standard. LLOQ 0.5 ng/mL, LOD 0.02 ng/mL, linear 0.5 to 75 ng/mL (Methods and Table 1, PDF p5).
- Analysis: Monolix 2020R1 (SAEM) and R 3.6.2. Log-normal between-subject variability, proportional residual error, stepwise covariate selection with ΔOFV 3.84.

## Model and parameters
- One compartment, first-order absorption and elimination. Parameters are apparent (/F) because there was no IV arm. The paper cites adult F ≈ 25% but does not use it in the model (PDF p9).
- Allometry on total body weight, referenced to 70 kg, with exponents fixed: 0.75 on CL/F and 1 on V/F (Equation 3).
- eGFR on CL/F, centred on 105 mL/min/1.73 m² (Equation 4). See open_questions about its exponent.

- Absorption half-life: ln2/0.075 = 9.2 h (derived; the paper says 9 h, PDF p9). The authors read ka as about tenfold smaller than ke, so the kinetics are flip-flop (PDF p9).
- OFV in Table 3 (PDF p7): base model 286.41, fixed allometry 274.07, eGFR on CL 269.63.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD model. Blood pressure was measured at trough, but no exposure-response analysis is reported.
- No ke0, no therapeutic concentration and no potency ratio. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
- Body weight, through fixed allometry: CL/F × (WT/70)^0.75 and V/F × (WT/70) (Equation 3).
- eGFR on CL/F: Equation 4 as printed is CL_pop × (eGFR/105) × (WT/70)^0.75.
- Two worked examples (PDF p9):
  - A 10 kg patient has 76.8% lower CL than a 70 kg patient. This is consistent with (10/70)^0.75 = 0.232 (derived).
  - A 53.2 kg patient at eGFR 60 has 25.7% lower CL than at eGFR 105. A linear term would give 42.9% (derived), so the text implies an exponent of about 0.53 (derived). See open_questions.
- Age, height, BMI, BSA, sex and race were tested (Table 2) and not retained.

## Caveats
- Small sample: 13 patients and 46 peak/trough points. Allometric exponents were fixed because the data were too sparse to estimate them (authors, PDF p9).
- Flip-flop identifiability. With only peak and trough samples and no IV data, a one-compartment oral model cannot tell ka from ke by the data alone.
  - The swapped solution would need V/F = 24.2/0.075 ≈ 323 L (derived), which is implausible for a hydrophilic drug. That makes the authors' assignment physically reasonable, but not proven.
- Internal inconsistencies in the paper:
  - V/F is 31.38 in Table 4 and 32.4 L/70 kg in the text (PDF p8).
  - omega_CL: Table 4 gives SD 0.3 (CV 30.8%), the Discussion gives SD 0.24 (CV 104%).
  - The abstract and Equation 4 imply a linear eGFR term, which the 25.7% example contradicts.
  - The Figure 4 goodness-of-fit axes are labelled µg/mL, with values up to about 70. That is inconsistent with the ng/mL assay range (0.5 to 75 ng/mL), so the axis label is probably wrong.
  - Table 2 gives the minimum age as 2.0 y; the text gives 1.9 y.
- Comparative claims:
  - "26% smaller" ka is measured against Thomson 1989 (0.104 /h), in elderly and renal adults.
  - "Slightly lower" CL is the Thomson elderly patients (17.6 L/h) against this study.
  - Trachtman's paediatric CL/F (17.9 and 18.6 L/h/70 kg) is slightly lower than this study (PDF p9).
- Three PK concomitant drugs: methylphenidate (2 patients), occasional aspirin (1) and amlodipine (1).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/drugs_lisinopril.R:33-38.** The code cites "Sandra L et al, Br J Clin Pharmacol 2023;90:504-515", 13 hypertensive children, ka = 0.075 /h. The paper has ka 0.075 /h (Table 4, confirmed on the rendered page) and n = 13. **Matches.**
  - The code's point is that slow absorption is not an artefact of Thomson's fit. The paper supports that: its own ke/ka ratio is about 10, against Thomson's 0.479/0.104 = 4.6 (derived).
  - Minor citation point: volume 90 is the 2024 issue (PDF p1 footer: Br J Clin Pharmacol. 2024;90:504-515). 2023 is the epub year, so "2023;90" mixes the two.
- **inst/help/drugs/lisinopril.md:13.** Same claim: 13 children, 0.075 per hour, "same order of magnitude". **Matches**, with the same 2023/2024 citation-year mix.
- **Uncited comparison with the library model** (Thomson 1989: CL/F = 0.251 L/h/kg × (creat/70)^-0.887 × (age/65)^-0.451, V/F 36.7 L, ka 0.104 /h; R/drugs_lisinopril.R:21-23):
  - Sandra's CL/F at 70 kg is 24.2 L/h at eGFR 105, against 17.6 L/h for Thomson's 65-year-old reference. Children with normal renal function clear more, in the direction Thomson's age term predicts.
  - For Sandra's median 53.2 kg child, Sandra gives 24.23 × (53.2/70)^0.75 = 19.7 L/h (derived).
  - The library gives 0.251 × 53.2 × (18/65)^-0.451 = 23.8 L/h (derived). Its age term is floored at 18, and the creatinine term is set to 1 here; the real value depends on adultEquivalentCreatinine().
  - Sandra's V/F scales with weight (23.8 L at 53.2 kg, derived); the library's V/F is a fixed 36.7 L.
  - The library is documented as an adult model, so these differences are not discrepancies. They show it over-predicts volume, and roughly matches clearance, in a mid-sized child.
- Unit checks: ka is in /h in both the paper and the code (the code converts to /min at R/drugs_lisinopril.R:143). No per-kg confusion: ka is not weight-scaled.
