# Lu 2024: cetagliptin PK/PD model to select phase 3 doses

## Study
Pooled analysis of four phase 1 studies (PDF p4 Table 1, journal p1465): single ascending 12.5-400 mg in healthy volunteers (cetagliptin 44, sitagliptin 10, placebo 12); a food-effect study at 100 mg (n 14); repeated dosing in healthy volunteers, 50 mg daily x10 d, 100 mg x7 d, 200 mg x7 d (cetagliptin 30/20 in the two cohorts as printed); and 50 and 100 mg daily x14 d in type 2 diabetes (T2D) patients (Table 1 text layer is scrambled; counts above are the readable ones). Sitagliptin 100 mg was the positive control. 108 subjects with cetagliptin and 27 with sitagliptin entered the PK/PD analysis (p4). No age, weight, sex or other demographic is printed. Oral doses in mg; plasma assay not described here (earlier papers refs 6-8). Phoenix NLME 8.1 (naive pooled data for typical values), NCA in WinNonlin 8.0.

## Model and parameters
Final cetagliptin PK model: two compartments, first-order absorption, saturable efflux from the central compartment back into the absorption compartment (Fig. 3A, PDF p7), linear elimination, combined additive-proportional error. Sitagliptin: plain two-compartment, first-order absorption (Fig. 3B). Table 2 (PDF p10, journal p1472), apparent parameters, estimate (CV %):

Sitagliptin EC50 7.74 ug/L, Emax 90.43%, Hill 1.21. The CV column is not defined (precision or IIV); the paper says IIV was tested on all parameters, and the text says only typical values were estimated. The authors note central V/F and V2/F came out numerically lower than the NCA values of studies 1, 3, 4 (p10). Exposure is superlinear in dose: Cmax and AUCinf rise faster than dose, CL/F rises with dose, and the authors attribute it to saturated efflux transport (P-gp substrate), not saturable clearance (p5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is plasma DPP-4 activity inhibition (%), a direct (non-hysteretic) sigmoid Emax model: Eq. 2, E = Emax C^g/(EC50^g + C^g). No ke0, t1/2ke0 or tPeak: the authors saw no hysteresis in the effect-concentration plots (p5, Fig. 2). Per-study Emax/EC50 from the Emax model (p5): cetagliptin 92.04%/5.98 ng/mL (study 1), 92.81%/5.29 (study 3), 92.47%/5.37 (study 4); sitagliptin 91.94%/9.60, 92.64%/7.03, 91.68%/6.73. Table 3 (PDF p11): steady-state weighted average inhibition (WAI, %) predicted for cetagliptin 5, 12.5, 25, 50, 100, 200 mg: 49.61, 69.31, 78.77, 84.49, 87.81, 89.6 (dose labels in the printed table are shifted/truncated: the last row reads "20"; read as the six doses in order). Observed WAI: healthy volunteers 50 mg 84.53, 100 mg 87.11, 200 mg 89.91; T2D 50 mg 83.94, 100 mg 86.9. Sitagliptin 100 mg predicted WAI 84.65, observed 82.4 (HV), 83.54 (T2D). >=50 mg cetagliptin daily sustains >80% inhibition (p7). HbA1c is predicted from WAI by the Gibbs 2012 meta-model (Eqs 9-11). Chosen phase 3 doses: 50 and 100 mg once daily.
Opioid audit: not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio. No therapeutic concentration range is stated.

## Covariates
None in the final models (naive pooled; healthy volunteers and T2D patients had similar concentration-time and inhibition profiles, p5).

## Caveats
Phase 1 data only; no demographics printed; 95% CIs missing (authors, p10); naive-pooled typical values; superlinear PK explained by saturable efflux, an assumption not proven by the data; V/F lower than NCA values; sponsor employees as authors.
