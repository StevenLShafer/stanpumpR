# Otero Torres 2023: lacosamide concentrations above 10 mg/L and adverse effects

Page key: printed page = PDF page + 38 (printed p. 39 = PDF p. 1).

## Study
- **Design** (PDF p. 1-2): single-centre, retrospective, observational; adult epilepsy outpatients, Bellvitge University
  Hospital, Barcelona, June 2017 to December 2020.
- **Inclusion**: adults on a stable lacosamide dose for >= 4 weeks with a **drug-fasting** (pre-dose) serum
  concentration > 10 mg/L. Exclusion: post-dose samples only (4 of 48 patients excluded).
- **Sample**: 319 lacosamide levels in the period, 55 (17.2%) above 10 mg/L analysed, from 44 patients (25 women, 57%).
  Table 1 (PDF p. 2, page image; median (Q1-Q3)): age 47 (39-61) yr; eGFR (CKD-EPI) 96 (76-109) mL/min; ALT 0.28
  (0.2-0.37) µkat/L; concentration 13.4 (11.2-17.8) mg/L; daily dose 400.0 (300.0-400.0) mg. 39 (89%) on other
  antiseizure drugs (valproate 30%, levetiracetam 25%, brivaracetam 25%...); 5 (11%) monotherapy; 5 (11%) with
  enzyme inducers. Table 1 lists "LCM-SC, n 57" against 55 in the text.
- **Assay** (PDF p. 2): UHPLC-MS/MS (Acquity UPLC-TQD), serum, deuterated internal standard. Total concentration.
- **Adverse effects**: those recorded in the clinical record and attributed to lacosamide by the treating physician
  (retrospective, not systematically elicited). Statistics: Student t test, Excel.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: physician-attributed adverse effects and dose reduction, against trough (drug-fasting) serum concentration.
Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

- Adverse effects in 18/44 (41%): somnolence 9 (20%), abnormal coordination 8 (18%), dizziness 5 (11%) (Results,
  PDF p. 2); Fig. 1 (PDF p. 3) combines "abnormal coordination/dizziness" as 8. 15 of 18 (83%) needed a dose reduction.
- Concentration with vs without adverse effects: 15.6 (12.7-18.4) vs 12.6 (10.7-16.5) mg/L, P = 0.0495 (PDF p. 3).
- Dose reduced vs not: 16.0 (13.2-18.1) vs 12.2 (10.7-14.2) mg/L, P = 0.0244 (PDF p. 3).
  These are labelled "mean" but given with ranges that look like interquartile ranges.
- By dose: 2/7 (29%) at 200 mg/d vs 8/18 (44%) at >= 400 mg/d had adverse effects (PDF p. 3).
- **Proposal** (Conclusions, PDF p. 4): where trough levels are used, adjust the reference range's upper limit to
  **12 mg/L**, because a mean of about 12 mg/L went with no adverse effects and no dose change (Discussion, PDF p. 4).
  The authors conclude that 10-20 mg/L (Patsalos 2018, drawn from samples 0-3 h after a dose) would increase toxicity.
  No lower limit is proposed; efficacy was not assessed.

## Covariates
Concomitant sodium-channel blockers and enzyme inducers: no significant association with adverse effects (small n).

## Caveats
- Only patients above 10 mg/L were studied, so there is no below-10 comparator for toxicity; 44 patients.
- Retrospective, physician-attributed adverse effects (underreporting likely, per the authors); no seizure outcome.
- Time since dose is "drug-fasting", not a stated number of hours.
- Text/table inconsistency on the number of samples (55 vs 57); "mean" values reported with quartile-like ranges.
