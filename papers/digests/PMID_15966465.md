# Lima 2005: COER-verapamil (S-enantiomer) and 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure

## Study
- Re-analysis of an earlier trial (Nguyen et al. 2000). 19 hypertensive patients, aged 21-70 years, clinic DBP 95-115 mmHg, off antihypertensives for 2 weeks (PDF p2, printed 188). Sex and weight are not given here.
- 24-h ambulatory BP (Accutracker II, auscultatory) off drug, then controlled-onset extended-release verapamil (COER-V) 180 mg at 9 p.m., titrated weekly (180, 240, 360, 480 mg) to resting DBP <= 90 mmHg or 480 mg with a >= 10% fall. The target dose was then continued for >= 30 days before repeat ABP and PK sampling. Individual doses are not tabulated.
- PK sampling after the last dose (9 p.m.) at 0, 2, 4.5, 7, 8.5, 10, 11.5, 13, 16, 20 and 24 h. R- and S-verapamil in **plasma** by chiral HPLC with fluorescence detection; standard curve 5-125 ng/mL per isomer; within-day CV < 5% (PDF p2-3). Only S-verapamil is modelled. Total (not free) concentration.
- Software: WinNonlin Professional 1.5. Two-step, individual (not population) least-squares fits.

## Model and parameters
- **Baseline (Eq. 1, cosinor):** BP(t) = M + A·M·cos[(2π/cycle)(t − t0)], where M is the mesor, A the amplitude as a fraction of the mesor, cycle the period and t0 the acrophase. SBP and DBP were fitted together with shared amplitude, cycle and t0 (5 parameters). The paper calls `cycle` the frequency, but its unit is minutes, so it is the period.
- **PK (Eq. 2):** Cp(t) = Cp*(t) + Css, with Cp*(t) = A·[e^(−k2(t−tLAG)) − e^(−k1(t−tLAG))] for t >= tLAG. k1 is the rise and k2 the decline rate constant, and Css is the mean of the pre-absorption concentrations. tP = tLAG + ln(k1/k2)/(k1 − k2).
- **PD (Eq. 3, inhibitory Emax, no Hill, no effect site):** E_BP(t) = E1 − (E1 − BPmax)·(C1 + Css)/(C50 + C1 + Css), with E1 = the untreated cosinor BP(t) and C1 = Cp*(t), both held fixed. Before tLAG, Css alone is used. **BPmax is the lowest attainable pressure (an asymptotic minimum), not a decrement.** One C50 is shared by SBP and DBP. Fitted in patients with a >= 5% fall in both mesors.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: 24-h ambulatory SBP and DBP at steady state. C50 is 32.9 ng/mL of plasma S-verapamil (total), the concentration giving half the fall from E1 to BPmax. The paper describes it as the average C50, 33 ng/mL.
- **No ke0 and no hysteresis term.** The authors state that the model assumes no hysteresis and no adaptation (PDF p7, printed 193). No Hill coefficient.
- COER-V lowered the mesors by about 7% (SBP) and 8% (DBP). It had no consistent effect on amplitude, cycle or acrophase. 5 of 19 patients were non-responders (< 5% fall), and their PK did not differ.
- Not an opioid: no MEAC or potency ratio applies.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Small n. Each patient's PD rests on one dose level at steady state. The fit is two-step: 9 of 12 parameters are fixed, and the authors note that this underestimates the SEs, and that the full 12-parameter fit did not converge.
- The PK parameters are apparent values for one product (osmotic-pump COER-V). There is no clearance or volume, and doses are not tabulated.
- The S-enantiomer only. Racemic or R-verapamil concentrations are not modelled.
- The cycle varied from 6 to 32 h between patients, so it is not a fixed 24-h rhythm.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **C50 32.9 ng/mL, uncertainty 22.8** (parameters.csv:197): **matches** in value, unit, matrix (plasma) and analyte (S-verapamil). **Discrepancy:** the uncertainty type is recorded as not_reported, and the note says the type is not stated in the abstract. The abstract and PDF p6 both say mean ± SD. That SD is between-patient spread of 12 individual estimates. The population field says 19 hypertensive adults, but the PD was fitted in 12 (see open_questions).
- **Fitted minimum SBP 101 mmHg ± 14; DBP minimum 61** (parameters.csv:198, registry.csv:136): **matches**. The repo's emphasis is correct. In Eq. 3, BPmax is the lowest attainable pressure, so treating it as an Emax decrement would be wrong. The same uncertainty-type and population discrepancy applies.
- **The registry's "cosinor circadian baseline ... common C50"** (registry.csv:136): **matches**. One C50 is shared by SBP and DBP within each patient.
- **"Release function, S-enantiomer PK and exact baseline equation not transcribed"** (registry.csv:136): this is true of the repo. The paper does print all three: Eq. 1, Eq. 2 and the mean A, k1, k2, tLAG and Css above. They could be transcribed. The k1/k2 lag model is fitted to steady-state data above a Css plateau, so it is not a single-dose input function.
- **docs/antihypertensives.md:383-384** names Lima among papers whose ± is unstated: **discrepancy** (stated as SD).
- **docs:33, help:13, test:196-199** (separate keys for the product and the enantiomer): **matches**. The paper models S-verapamil from COER-V only.
- **Library model check:** `R/drugs_verapamil.R` is IV racemic verapamil (Kates 1981, 3 compartments, tPeak 4 min on heart rate). Lima gives no clearance or volume and no IV data, and reports a different analyte, so there is nothing to compare numerically. Lima's oral COER S-verapamil Cmax (46.4 ng/mL) must not be compared with racemic concentrations from that model.
