# Mattson 1980: valproic acid and ethosuximide interaction

## Study
Brief communication (PDF pp1-2, printed 583-584). Five patients with absence seizures not controlled on ethosuximide
(boy and girl aged 5, girl aged 9, women aged 16 and 31). Valproic acid was added to steady-state ethosuximide, alone
(the 5-year-old boy) or with phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone or clonazepam. Valproate started at about 10 mg/kg
and was raised to the maximum tolerated dose, 19-56 mg/kg. Ethosuximide by enzyme immunoassay; valproate by gas
chromatography. No other drug dose changed during observation. Serum concentrations (ug/mL).

- Table 2: valproate dose 19-56 mg/kg (mean 42); pre-dose (about 8 h after previous dose) 42-56 ug/mL (mean 49.4);
  peak 69-138 (mean 90).
- Onset: ethosuximide rose within several weeks (mean 19 days) of adding valproate.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD model. Patients felt sedated at the raised ethosuximide levels; sedation cleared when the dose was cut to
20.4 mg/kg to restore the pre-valproate level. Ethosuximide was later stopped in all five and seizures were
controlled on valproate alone. No therapeutic range given.

## Covariates
None. Children tolerated higher valproate doses (42-56 mg/kg) than the teenager (39) and adult (19).

## Caveats
n = 5, uncontrolled, no washout or time course. The one patient on no other drug (the 5-year-old boy, +7 ug/mL,
46 -> 53) showed no interaction; the other four were on at least one more drug that is also oxidised
(barbiturate or phenytoin); authors speculate that competition for oxidative enzymes shows only when several drugs compete. Mechanism
not shown: the authors postulate reduced ethosuximide hydroxylation. Whether the effect was transient was not
determined because the dose was cut. Levels are total serum (ethosuximide is not protein bound, per the authors).
No clearance or half-life reported.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- The repo uses this paper only for the direction (valproate raises ethosuximide levels): **matches**. The paper
  reports a mean +53% serum concentration (73 -> 112 ug/mL) in four of five patients; none of its numbers enter the
  x0.85 clearance factor, which the repo takes from Pisani 1984, so there is no transcription to check.
- The word "inhibition" is the authors' postulated mechanism, not a measured one, and the effect was seen only in
  patients on additional drugs (the one on ethosuximide plus valproate alone showed none). The repo's own notes already
  flag the effect as variable. A level rise of +53% corresponds to CL falling to about 1/1.53 = 0.65 only if dose
  and bioavailability are unchanged (derived; and dose was fixed during observation); this is a larger effect than
  the repo's 0.85, in a different population (patients with co-medication versus Pisani's healthy volunteers).
