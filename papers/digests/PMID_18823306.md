# Hawwa 2008: Population PK of 6-mercaptopurine active metabolites in children with ALL

## Study
- Prospective, 19 children with ALL on oral 6-MP maintenance (target 75 mg/m2/day) plus weekly methotrexate (p.2). Samples: RBC 6-thioguanine nucleotides (6-TGN) and 6-methylmercaptopurine nucleotides (6-mMPN), one sample per monthly occasion, up to 5 per child, taken at least 12 h after the last dose; 75 samples, 150 concentrations (p.1, p.5).
- Demographics (Table 1, text layer only): age median 10 y (range 3-17); 6 F : 13 M; BSA median 1.14 m2, range 0.59-2.0 (prose, p.6).
- Assay: reversed-phase HPLC on RBC, CV 3.1% intra-day and 4.3% inter-day, LOQ 13 and 95 pmol per 8x10^8 RBC (p.3). Genotyping (TaqMan) of XO (2 SNPs), ITPA (2), TPMT*3A/*3B/*3C.
- NONMEM VI, FOCE with interaction; 15 patients index group, 4 validation (data splitting); additive residual error.

## Model and parameters
One-compartment model with first-order absorption and elimination for the parent drug; ka fixed 1.3 /h, F fixed 22%, k20 (6-MP elimination) fixed 0.53 /h, kother = 0.22 x k20 = 0.1166 /h, all from the literature (p.3). Metabolite amounts in the central compartment form from 6-MP with fraction FM3 (to 6-TGN) and FM4 = 1 - FM3 (to 6-mMPN) and are cleared by CL6-TGN and CL6-mMPN. Only RBC metabolite data were modelled; 6-MP and 6-TU were too low to use (p.5).

Final model (p.6): FM3 = 0.019 x 2.56^TPMT; CL6-TGN (L/h) = 0.00914 x BSA^1.16; CL6-mMPN = 0.0228 L/h; TPMT = 1 if mutant. BSA in m2. A median-BSA child (1.14 m2): FM3 0.019, CL6-TGN 0.011 L/h, CL6-mMPN 0.0288 L/h (text, p.6; the last differs from the final-model constant 0.0228, so it appears to be an error or an individual-estimate figure; unresolved). With a TPMT mutation FM3 is 0.049.

Table 5 (p.10): median individual Bayesian estimates: FM3 0.0191 (no TPMT mutation) and 0.0491 (mutation); CL6-TGN 0.0112 L/h (P5 0.0046, P95 0.0235); CL6-mMPN 0.0226 L/h (0.0153, 0.0310); CL6-TGN per m2 0.0082 L/h/m2 (0.0059, 0.0151).

BSA reduced CL6-TGN IIV from 69.9% to 29.3% in the index-group model and to 33.6% in the full-data final model (p.5-6). TPMT on FM3 gave the largest drop in objective function (-8.69, P<0.005, Table 2/3).

Internal validation (n=4): ME -0.047 and 1.67, RMSE 0.16 and 4.84 mg/L for 6-TGN and 6-mMPN (p.5).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, no effect-site concentration, no ke0. The paper cites a therapeutic range for RBC 6-TGN only as dashed lines in Figure 1 and gives no values in the text. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, no potency ratio.

## Covariates
Tested: age, sex, weight, BSA, TPMT, ITPA, XO. Retained: BSA on CL6-TGN (power 1.16) and TPMT mutation on FM3 (multiplicative 2.56). Weight (allometric 0.75, Table 2) was significant but BSA was better; ITPA and XO not significant. Only a handful of mutants (Table 1: TPMT*3A 1, *3C 2 heterozygotes).

## Caveats
- n = 19, only 4 for validation; the authors say external validation is needed before dosing use (p.9).
- No parent-drug concentrations and no samples in the absorption phase; ka, F and k20 are fixed from textbook values, so clearances are conditional on those fixed values.
- Concentration units are mg/L in the model but pmol/8x10^8 RBC in the assay (see open_questions).
- Wording slips: the text says FM3 "would increase by 256%" for a multiplier of 2.56 (an increase of 156%, derived: 2.56-1), and says CL6-TGN "increased by 116% per 1 m2" for an exponent of 1.16 (a power, not a percentage).
- 6-TGN clearance SE is 56.8%; FM3 SE 64.1%.
