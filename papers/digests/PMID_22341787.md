# Kulo 2012: IV paracetamol loading dose after caesarean delivery

## Study
Single-centre observational PK study, 28 women after elective caesarean delivery (combined spinal-epidural
anaesthesia). Paracetamol 2 g IV over 15 min shortly after delivery, then 1 g every 6 h. Plasma at 1, 2, 4 and
6 h (115 samples), HPLC. Table 1 (PDF p2, printed p126), median [range]: age 31.5 [20-42] y, weight 79 [57-110]
kg, height 165 [151-182] cm, BSA 1.9 [1.5-2.4] m2. Per-patient one-compartment estimates from a log trend line
(K = slope x 2.303, t½ = 0.693/K, CL = K x Vd). No population model.

Median concentrations at 1, 2, 4, 6 h: 22.5, 15.25, 7.9, 3.9 mg/L (Results, p126).

Comparator (Discussion, p126): 14 female volunteers from the sponsor's study (Gregoire 2007; same dose, sampling
times, HPLC and calculation). CL 15.5 vs 20.3 L/h, Vd 43.7 vs 58.3 L, CL/BSA 9.6 vs 10.9 L/h/m2, reported
significantly higher after caesarean; the volunteers were lighter (55.5 vs 79 kg) and younger (22.5 vs 31.5 y).
(derived: ratios 1.31 CL, 1.14 CL/BSA; volunteer Vd 0.79 L/kg vs 0.72, so not higher per kg.)

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD measured: no effective concentration, no ke0, no potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled (CL per BSA and V per kg are normalisations only).

## Caveats
Single dose, sampling 1-6 h only, crude method; immediately post-operative, so surgery and delivery are mixed
with pregnancy; comparator is another study's volunteers, not matched non-pregnant women. One outlier (CL 62.8
L/h) retained (p127). CL/BSA range differs between Table 1 and the text.
