# Lazebnik 1985: gentamicin half-life in pregnant vs non-pregnant women

## Study
Comparative study, Tel Aviv. Group A: 19 pregnant women at 28-34 weeks, singleton, with premature rupture of the
membranes, 20-30 years, 62 +/- 5 kg, given gentamicin to prevent amniotic infection while awaiting fetal lung
maturation after betamethasone. Group B: 17 non-pregnant women with pelvic inflammatory disease, same age range,
55 +/- 5 kg (p.1). Normal renal function at admission; renal or systemic disease excluded. Both groups:
gentamicin 4 mg/kg/day IV, one third every 8 h, with cefazolin 3.0 g/day; Hartmann's solution 2500 mL/24 h.
Blood every 15 min for the first hour, then hourly for 8 h (which dose interval was sampled is not stated). EMIT
immunoassay, total serum gentamicin, range 1.0-16.0 ug/mL. Half-life from the log-linear elimination line drawn
after distribution (graphical). BMDP statistics.

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Results (p.1-2, image-confirmed):
- Gentamicin half-life: group A 93.5 +/- 13.5 min, group B 158.9 +/- 14.1 min (P < 0.0001).
- Mean serum gentamicin at 5 h: 1.9 (A) vs 4.1 ug/mL (B), P < 0.0001; at 8 h only traces in A vs 1.6 ug/mL in B.
- Table I (p.2), mean +/- SD: serum creatinine 56.6 +/- 8.8 (A) vs 74.25 +/- 17.7 umol/L (B); creatinine
  clearance 144.78 +/- 4.0 vs 96.29 +/- 4.1 mL/min. The "normal value" columns (40.7 +/- 53.0 and 76.9 +/- 150.3
  umol/L; 132-164 and 90-130 mL/min) read as ranges printed with plus-minus signs.
- Figure 1 (p.2): half-life falls linearly with creatinine clearance across both groups (pregnant at CrCl about
  136-152 mL/min, non-pregnant about 88-104); no regression equation or r is given.
Derived: CrCl ratio 144.78/96.29 = 1.50; elimination rate ratio 158.9/93.5 = 1.70.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect analysis. The authors note serum levels in both groups stayed above the MICs of common
Gram-negative and staphylococcal pathogens for the first 4 h (values not given) and advise 4 mg/kg every 8 h in
pregnancy (p.3). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Pregnancy; creatinine clearance (linear negative relation to half-life, Figure 1).

## Caveats
- The groups differ in diagnosis (PROM with betamethasone vs PID) as well as pregnancy, and in weight (62 vs
  55 kg) although the summary calls them weight matched.
- Volume and clearance are not reported, only half-life, so the clearance change cannot be separated from a
  volume change.
- The creatinine clearance SDs (4.0, 4.1 mL/min) are small but consistent with the narrow clusters in Figure 1.
- Half-life by graphical extrapolation; dose interval sampled not stated; total (not unbound) assay.
