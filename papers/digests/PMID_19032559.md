# Pesonen 2009: fentanyl and oxycodone concentrations and effects, elderly vs middle-aged, after cardiac surgery

## Study
- Prospective, two age groups (methods pp. 101-102, PDF pp. 1-2; Table 1, PDF p. 4): 30 patients aged 75 or older (9 M/21 F)
  and 20 aged 60 or younger (20 M/0 F), all undergoing elective CABG or valve surgery with CPB. Two elderly
  drop-outs were replaced. Mean age 80 (text: ±3; Table 1 prints ±14) vs 52 ± 6 y; weight 72 ± 16 vs 81 ± 14 kg;
  height 163 ± 29 vs 176 ± 6 cm; BMI 27 ± 4 vs 26 ± 4 (Table 1, mean ± SD). Excluded: LVEF below 35%, creatinine
  above 150 µmol/L, liver or neurological disease.
- Anaesthesia: lorazepam premedication 1-4 mg orally; fentanyl 7.5 µg/kg IV induction, then 0.1 µg/kg/min until skin
  closure (units confirmed on PDF p. 2); propofol or etomidate; isoflurane or sevoflurane to BIS below 50. Total fentanyl
  infused 2.3 ± 0.5 vs 2.6 ± 0.8 mg (Table 1). Propofol 2-4 mg/kg/h in ICU; paracetamol 1 g IV 8-hourly.
- Before extubation, nurses gave oxycodone hydrochloride 0.05 mg/kg IV for signs of pain; cumulative 9.3 ± 4.9 vs
  11.5 ± 6.4 mg (Table 1, NS).
- After extubation, three "oxycodone tests" (Fig. 1, PDF p. 3): when VRS (0-4) reached 2 or more, blood was drawn
  (time 0), then oxycodone hydrochloride 0.05 mg/kg IV; pain (VRS, 50-cm Red Wedge Scale), Ramsay sedation and
  oxycodone plasma concentration at 15 and 45 min.
- Fentanyl: arterial samples at the end of infusion and 2 h later. The sampling site of the oxycodone samples is not
  stated.
- Assay (p. 103): GC-MS, nalorphine internal standard, plasma (total drug). LLOQ fentanyl 1.0 ng/mL, oxycodone
  2.0 ng/mL. At 50 ng/mL repeatability RSD 7% (fentanyl) and 11% (oxycodone); intermediate precision 11% and 22%;
  bias 3% and 8%.
- No pharmacokinetic model; statistics were t-tests, chi-square, Mann-Whitney and repeated-measures ANOVA.

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetic parameters are estimated. Observed concentrations (mean ± SD):

- Fentanyl at end of infusion: 5.7 ± 2.2 (elderly) vs 3.8 ± 1.2 ng/mL (abstract; Fig. 2, PDF p. 5; P = 0.001).
  Fentanyl 2 h later: about 2.0 vs 1.5 ng/mL, read from Fig. 2 (P = 0.042).
- Residual fentanyl at the first post-extubation test: 1.43 ± 0.94 ng/mL in 12/30 elderly, about 12 h after the
  infusion, and 1.36 ± 0.67 ng/mL in 8/20 younger patients, about 10 h after (p. 104).
- Oxycodone at time 0 of test 1, when VRS first reached 2 after extubation: 6.3 ± 6.2 (elderly) vs 5.7 ± 2.3 ng/mL
  (younger), NS (p. 104, text; confirmed on PDF p. 4).
- Fig. 3 (PDF p. 5): bar chart of oxycodone at 0, 15 and 45 min in each test. Approximate means read by eye
  (elderly / younger, ng/mL):

| Test | 0 min | 15 min | 45 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~6 / ~6 | ~18 / ~16.5 | ~11 / ~10.5 |
| 2 | ~7.5 / ~8 | ~19 / ~21.5 | ~14 / ~14 |
| 3 | ~10 / ~7 | ~20 / ~23 | ~14.5 / ~16 |

  The paper reports no age difference at any point.
- Interval between tests 2 and 3: 263 ± 160 (elderly) vs 182 ± 78 min (P = 0.036; Fig. 4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEC / MEAC: not reported as such.** The nearest quantities, which the paper does not name:
  - Onset-of-pain concentration (time 0 of a test, VRS 2 or more): 6.3 ± 6.2 and 5.7 ± 2.3 ng/mL in test 1 (p. 104);
    about 7-10 ng/mL in tests 2-3 (Fig. 3, read by eye).
  - Concentration 15 min after a 0.05 mg/kg hydrochloride bolus: about 16-23 ng/mL (Fig. 3, read by eye). Fifteen
    minutes is not steady state. At 15 and 45 min 50-57% of the elderly and 20-40% of the younger patients had VRS 0
    (text p. 105-106; Table 3, PDF p. 6, read from the text layer only).
  - Population: post-cardiac-surgery ICU patients. Assay: total oxycodone in plasma by GC-MS; whole blood was not
    measured, nor free drug. The oxycodone sampling site is not stated.
- End points: VRS 0-4, Red Wedge Scale (50 cm) and Ramsay score. With similar concentrations, the elderly had more
  patients with VRS 0 at 45 min, lower RWS at 45 min (P = 0.001) and more sedation after test 3 (median Ramsay 2 (2-4)
  vs 2 (1-3) at 15 min, P = 0.022; 2 (2-4) vs 2 (1-4) at 45 min, P = 0.035). The authors read this as greater
  pharmacodynamic sensitivity in the elderly.
- **No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis analysis. No equianalgesic or potency ratio.**

## Covariates
Age group only, compared descriptively. Sex is confounded with age (younger group all male); the authors name this
as a limitation. No CYP2D6 genotyping. Eight elderly patients took CYP3A4/5 inhibitors (calcium antagonists,
roxithromycin), with no difference in oxycodone concentration.

## Caveats
- Small groups; sex completely confounded with age group.
- Concentrations are 15 and 45 min after IV boluses, not at steady state, with residual fentanyl in some patients
  and propofol sedation in the ICU.
- Internal inconsistencies: age SD 3 (text) vs 14 (Table 1); the Discussion (p. 107, text layer) says "0.5 mg/kg",
  but the methods give 0.05 mg/kg, which is evidently a typo for the latter.
- Oxycodone intermediate precision RSD 22%. Fig. 3 values are only readable approximately.
- Doses are oxycodone hydrochloride. The paper does not say whether concentrations are of the base, which GC-MS
  normally measures.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- R/drugs_oxycodone.R:257-258: the code reports what Kokki 2012 says about Pesonen, and the Kokki card
  (`PMID_22136407.md`:63) records the same citation. This paper does not state "MEC" or "MEAC", or the ranges 6-12
  and 15-25 ng/mL. Its data agree with them: onset-of-pain means are 6.3 and 5.7 ng/mL in test 1 and about 7-10 in
  tests 2-3 (Fig. 3); 15-min post-bolus means are about 16-23 ng/mL (Fig. 3). Kokki's ranges are therefore a
  plausible reading of this paper's Fig. 3, but the paper does not define them as MEC or MEAC, and the "MEAC" is a
  15-min post-bolus value rather than a steady-state one. The attribution is **cannot verify** until Kokki's
  reference list settles whether refs 14/15 mean this paper (open question).
- Units: ng/mL, total oxycodone in plasma, matching the library's concentration units (oxycodone is plotted in
  ng/mL). The dose is the hydrochloride, but that does not affect concentrations.
- The library's MEAC of 12 ng/mL (R/drugs_oxycodone.R:463) is between this paper's onset-of-pain concentrations
  (about 6-10) and its 15-min post-bolus values (about 16-23). The code does not rely on Pesonen for that value
  (kept at 12 by Shafer's decision of 2026-10-12, R/drugs_oxycodone.R:263). No discrepancy in what the code claims.
- No PK parameters to compare with the library's oxycodone model. Fentanyl concentrations after 7.5 µg/kg +
  0.1 µg/kg/min are reported but are not used by the library.
