# Shafer 2008: Additivity versus synergy (theoretical analysis)

## Study
Theoretical essay (no subjects, no assay). Uses the law of mass action, then circuits of binding sites (suppressors in series, enhancers in parallel) assembled into "signal processing units", to ask whether additivity implies a single site of action. Mathematica for derivations; an Excel workbook (circuits.xls) and Appendix D derivations are web supplements and are not in this PDF. Printed pp. 507-524.

## Model and parameters
Definitions (pp. 508-509): isobole normalized to each drug's concentration giving the effect (e.g. MAC fraction); additive = sum of fractions 0.9-1.1, synergy < 0.9, infra-additivity > 1.1 (statistically significant); "additivity by default" when data cannot exclude synergy. Synergy and additivity refer to adding concentrations, never effects.
Illustrative values (Appendix B-C): occupancy f = [L]/(kd+[L]); suppressors in series with N = 16, Pun = 1, E = 0.2 give Su50 about 0.25 of kd and Hill gamma 1.13; gamma tends to 1.5 for large N (tested to 10,000), independent of E. Enhancers in parallel, Pun = 0, E = 0.1, N = 256 give En50 = 0.025 (about 40-fold potency gain) and gamma 1.41. Additivity in the two-drug circuits when multiplier M >= 0.45 (Fig. A4 legend).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No measured PD. Conceptual result: additivity among drugs at different sites is expected only when the effect concentration is well below kd (below about 1% of the 50%-occupancy concentration); at or above kd synergy is expected. Worked example (Figs 4-5): with MAC 10 times kd, half-MAC gives 83% occupancy and 6% probability of no response; with MAC 0.1 kd, half-MAC gives 5% occupancy. Opioid audit: not an opioid-data paper; fentanyl, alfentanil and naloxone appear only as receptor-competition examples (p. 510). No MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Authors state kd is known for very few anesthetics and the model was not tested against data (p. 514). The circuits are theoretical, not real neural circuits. Numerical instability at large N with small Pun and E (Appendix C).
