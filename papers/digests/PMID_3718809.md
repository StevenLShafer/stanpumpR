# Watt 1986: plasma amiodarone during a two-step IV infusion

## Study
Short report. 8 patients (5 male), 46-74 y, 45-91 kg (68.9 ± 16.8 kg), with supraventricular (5 refractory PSVT,
2 AF prophylaxis after cardioversion) or ventricular (1 VT) arrhythmia; various structural heart disease, none in
failure; one raised urea, two raised AST. Regimen chosen from Riva 1982 data by Shand's method of separate exponentials:
**175 mg/h for 2 h, then 50 mg/h for 46 h** (manufacturer then recommended 5 mg/kg over 2 h then 50 mg/h), in 5%
dextrose, peripheral long line or central. Then oral 200 mg tid. Plasma amiodarone and desethylamiodarone (DEA) by
HPLC (Hutchings 1985). Exact sampling times not listed (Fig 1 spans 0-48 h).

- From 3 to 16 h, plasma amiodarone was below 1.0 mg/L in at least three of eight patients; mean was below the range
  at 5 and 6 h.
- No significant correlation of weight with peak (r = 0.575) or trough (r = 0.562), n = 8.
- Shand-method prediction for a three-step regimen (175 mg/h 2 h, 100 mg/h 10 h, 50 mg/h 36 h): troughs 1.3 mg/L at
  3 h and 18 h (Discussion).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0 or concentration-effect model. Provisional therapeutic range 1.0-2.5 mg/L (Latini 1984; chronic oral therapy),
with the authors arguing the IV-loading range may be higher. One patient had paroxysmal AF at 7 h (0.69 mg/L at 6 h);
one severe sinus bradycardia at 12 h (1.47 mg/L, recent disopyramide); one mild thrombophlebitis. Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Weight tested only by correlation (not significant).

## Caveats
n = 8, heterogeneous cardiac patients, prior antiarrhythmics; no individual data tabulated; sampling schedule not
stated in text. Funded partly by Labaz Sanofi.
