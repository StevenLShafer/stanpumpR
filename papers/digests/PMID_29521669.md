# McCarthy 2018: serum methadone/EDDP ratios across pregnancy and postpartum

PDF page p = printed page 240 + p (PDF 1 = p. 241).

## Study
- Retrospective clinical/quality-assurance series (IRB judged it not human-subjects research), one methadone
  maintenance programme, University of California Davis; January 2015 to January 2016 (p. 242-243).
- 23 pregnant women with opioid use disorder; 67 prepartum samples (13 first trimester, 30 second, 24 third);
  11 of them gave 22 postpartum samples (all within the first month postpartum; usually first draw 3-7 days
  after delivery, repeated at 2-3 weeks). 16 (70%) conceived on methadone, 7 started during pregnancy.
  Age, weight and race are not reported.
- Oral methadone, doses adjusted clinically by withdrawal symptoms (SOWS adapted for pregnancy); split twice daily
  soon after admission, most on 3-4 times daily by delivery (range 2-6 daily). Dose was raised to relieve
  withdrawal while keeping trough serum methadone at or below 600 ng/mL.
- All samples were troughs drawn just before the morning dose. Serum methadone and EDDP by HPLC-MS/MS (San Diego
  Reference Laboratory). Racemic total methadone; no enantiomers, no free fraction.
- Statistics: permutation test for mixed paired/unpaired data (Einsporn and Habtzghi, 5000 replications), patient
  means per period, confirmed by t tests; R 3.4.1.
- No pharmacokinetic model, no clearance, no half-life measured. The methadone/metabolite ratio (MMR) is used as a
  surrogate of metabolic rate: a lower ratio means faster metabolism.

  dose with MMR r = -0.15 (P = 0.29) (p. 243).

  patient 16: T3 310 mg/d, 854 ng/mL, EDDP 143, ratio 6; PP1 260 mg/d, 1020 ng/mL, EDDP 230, ratio 4.4.
- Comparators: nonpregnant MMRs from OTHER studies, not this cohort: Diong 2014 (32 maintenance patients; peak 11.3,
  trough 12.7) and Alburges 1996 (23 patients; 11.9) (p. 244). The postpartum comparator is within the first
  month after delivery, not a return to a nonpregnant state.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **MEAC**: none measured. The paper uses a trough serum methadone range of 200-600 ng/mL (racemic, total, serum)
  as a clinical guide to effective maintenance treatment in opioid use disorder, citing Dole 1988 and the authors'
  own 2015 paper (p. 242); 600 ng/mL is the upper limit used for dose escalation (p. 242). End point: withdrawal
  control (SOWS), not analgesia. Not a minimum analgesic concentration.
- Clinically stable patients spanned 58-854 ng/mL (p. 245; note Table 1 gives the prepartum low as 78).
- **ke0 / t½ke0 / tPeak / hysteresis**: none.
- **Potency / equianalgesic ratio**: none.
- Background (cited, not measured): half-life shortened in pregnancy from about 24 h to 12 h (Bogen) and at times
  4-6 h (McCarthy 2015); a peak/trough ratio above 2 taken as rapid clearance (Westermeyer 2016) (p. 242).

## Covariates
Pregnancy stage (trimester, postpartum). No body size, genotype, or co-medication analysis.

## Caveats
- MMR is an indirect index; no clearance or half-life can be derived from it (the authors only suggest this as
  future work). Trough-only sampling with dosing 2-6 times daily.
- Internal inconsistencies: abstract gives a pregnancy-average MMR of 6.1 while Table 1 prepartum is 5.9 (the
  discussion attributes 6.1 to all actively pregnant patients, a different averaging); prepartum serum low 78 ng/mL
  in Table 1 vs 58 ng/mL on p. 245; "Four of 13 patients" over 600 ng/mL has no stated denominator population.
- No weights, ages, genotypes or adherence data; doses changed between samples, so trends mix dose and metabolism.
- Nonpregnant baseline is from other laboratories' populations, not these women.
