# Zhou 2013: community-acquired respiratory pathogen susceptibility, Shanghai

## Study
Chinese-language surveillance paper (English abstract + Latin-script tables; body text is Chinese).
Prospective collection of community-acquired respiratory tract infection cases from 30 primary
hospitals in Shanghai, Dec 2007 – Jul 2010; patients who had used antimicrobials within the prior
2 weeks were excluded. 806 qualified cases (abstract, p.1). Throat swab / deep-cough sputum
cultured at Zhongshan Hospital. MICs of penicillin G, amoxicillin, cephradine (头孢拉定),
cefalexin (头孢氨苄), cefadroxil (头孢羟氨苄), sulfamethoxazole/trimethoprim and azithromycin
determined by **agar dilution** per CLSI M07-A9 / M100-S21 (2011) breakpoints (abstract and
Methods, pp.1-2). This is a microbiology/susceptibility survey, not a PK or PD study — no
pharmacokinetic model, no ke0, no concentration-effect data.

## Model and parameters

Abstract (p.1): the MIC90 of cefalexin, cefadroxil and cephradine for β-haemolytic streptococcus
spp was ≤2 mg/L (worst case across groups A/B/G is 2 mg/L, from groups B and G).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No MEAC, ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, EC50 or potency ratio — not an opioid or PK/PD paper.
Only in-vitro MIC. No MBC was measured (agar-dilution MIC only).

## Covariates
None (no PK model).

## Caveats
- English abstract (p.1) names the three cephalosporins as "cephalexin, cefadroxil and
  ceftazidime"; the Chinese abstract and Table 5 give the third as cephradine (头孢拉定, cefradine),
  not ceftazidime, which was not among the tested agents. The English "ceftazidime" is an
  abstract mistranslation; cefalexin itself is listed explicitly in both the abstract and Table 5,
  so the quantity stanpumpR uses is not affected.
- Surveillance survey; susceptibility (S%) for cephalosporins vs streptococci is marked "N"
  (no CLSI interpretive breakpoint), so only raw MIC50/MIC90 are reported, not %susceptible.
- MIC only; no MBC, no kill kinetics, no serum/tissue levels.
