# Zhou 2013: community respiratory pathogens and MICs, Shanghai primary hospitals

## Study
Prospective microbiological surveillance (in Chinese, English abstract). 806 patients with community-acquired
respiratory tract infection from 30 community (primary) hospitals in four Shanghai districts, December 2007 to
July 2010; antibiotic use in the previous 2 weeks excluded. Throat swabs (upper respiratory infection, 688
patients) or deep sputum (lower, 118) couriered to Zhongshan Hospital laboratory within 2 h (PDF p1-2, printed
pp346-347). Streptococcal grouping by Slidex Strepto Plus (groups A, B, C, D, F, G). MICs of seven agents
(penicillin G, amoxicillin, cephradine, cefalexin, cefadroxil, sulfamethoxazole/trimethoprim, azithromycin) by
**agar dilution** per CLSI M07-A9; streptococci on Mueller-Hinton agar with 5% defibrinated sheep blood;
interpretation per CLSI M100-S21 (2011). For S. aureus the cephalosporins were interpreted with cefalotin's
breakpoints (S <= 8, R >= 32 mg/L) (PDF p2, printed p347, Methods 4).

Isolates: 184 from upper-tract swabs (H. influenzae 44, S. aureus 44, group G streptococcus 43 most frequent),
33 from lower-tract samples (abstract).

## Model and parameters
No pharmacokinetics. The numbers are MIC50/MIC90 (mg/L) and % susceptible.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is in-vitro growth inhibition (MIC). No MBC, no time-kill, no PK/PD target. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Small streptococcal samples (group B n=10, group A n=20); MIC90 from 10 isolates is close to the top value.
- Agar dilution, not broth microdilution.
- The English abstract names "ceftazidime" among drugs with MIC90 <= 2 for beta-haemolytic streptococci;
  ceftazidime was not tested (Table 5 has cephradine), so the abstract appears to misname cephradine.
- Cephradine group A MIC90 printed as 0.7, not a twofold dilution value (as printed on PDF p4).
- Outpatients in Shanghai 2007-2010, without recent antibiotic exposure.
