# Geisslinger 1993: ketamine enantiomer disposition in surgical patients

## Study
- Randomised, double-blind, two-group study, 50 adult ASA I-II patients (10 women), minor surgery lasting 1-2 h (PDF p.2, Table I). Age mean (range): S(+) group 33 (18-58) y, racemate group 37 (18-59) y. Broca index 1.16 and 1.14. No weight in kg is given.
- Single IV bolus over 30 s, 4 min after midazolam 0.1 mg/kg IV (and midazolam 5 mg IM premedication about 45 min earlier): S(+)-ketamine 1 mg/kg (n=25) or racemic ketamine 2 mg/kg (n=25). Both given as 0.04 mL/kg. Anaesthesia was maintained with nitrous oxide and enflurane from 10-15 min.
- Plasma (heparinised venous blood) at 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, 30, 45 min and 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 h. Chiral HPLC (alpha1-acid glycoprotein column), UV 215 nm, quantification limit 40 ng/mL for ketamine and norketamine enantiomers. Total plasma concentrations.
- Analysis: non-compartmental (Topfit), terminal slope by unweighted log-linear regression, trapezoidal AUC, MRT = AUMC/AUC, Vss = Cl x MRT. For the racemate, each enantiomer's Cl and Vss were computed taking its dose as 50% of the racemic dose (PDF p.3).
- Exclusions from the PK analysis (unexplained concentration fluctuations): 4 in the S(+) group, 1 in the racemate group, leaving n=21 and n=24.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, EC50 or hysteresis analysis. No MEAC or analgesic/hypnotic concentration. No potency ratio is measured here (the text cites 4:1 for S(+):R(-) from other work, ref 14-15).
- Haemodynamics (Fig. 3, PDF p.4; text p.3): systolic and diastolic pressure and heart rate rose significantly in both groups. S(+) group had greater systolic and diastolic increases than racemate at 1, 3 and 15 min (P<0.003, <0.0001, <0.001) and greater heart rate at 1, 3, 10 min (P<0.05). Maximum rises above baseline: SAP 27%, DAP 29%, HR 28% (S(+)); 26%, 27%, 20% (racemate) (Discussion, PDF p.4-5).
- Mean S(+) plasma concentration 1 min after dose: 2635 (SD 2566) ng/mL after pure S(+), 1817 (1884) ng/mL after racemate (PDF p.4).
- Plasma adrenaline and noradrenaline (Table III, PDF p.5; n=15 and 19) did not change significantly and did not correlate with haemodynamic changes.

## Covariates
None analysed. Parameters are per kg of body weight; no weight, height or sex effect is tested.

## Caveats
- Patients had midazolam premedication and induction; nitrous oxide and enflurane followed. Diazepam/midazolam is known to alter ketamine kinetics (authors cite ref 24), so these are not unmedicated values.
- Venous plasma, total concentration; non-compartmental analysis cannot give the initial distribution volume. First sample was 1 min after a 30 s bolus.
- The quantification limit (40 ng/mL) limits the terminal slope; the mean 12 h value is below the figure range shown (Fig. 1-2 end at about 8 h).
- Abstract dose misprint (see open_questions).
- The finding of lower R(-) clearance after racemate is modest against large between-patient variability (SD 4.8-7.2 mL/min/kg).

- **Clearance**: library 0.79 L/kg/h (header line 31; V1Ref x k10 = 4.081 L x 0.226/min = 0.922 L/min = 0.79 L/kg/h, derived). Geisslinger, per enantiomer: 0.98-1.15 L/kg/h (derived from Table II); averaging the two racemate enantiomers (16.5 and 19.1 mL/min/kg) gives about 1.07 L/kg/h (derived). So the library clearance is about 26% lower than Geisslinger's racemate figure (and 7% below Domino's own 0.848 L/kg/h, which the header documents). The comparator here is an independent surgical-patient study (midazolam-premedicated), not the library model, so this is a between-study difference, not an error.
- **Vss**: library 0.0583 x (1 + 0.657/0.0676 + 0.206/0.00489) = 3.08 L/kg (derived; header states 3.1). Geisslinger 2.84-3.29 L/kg. Agrees.

- `tPeak` 3 min and `typical`/band 0.1-0.16 ug/mL (lines 45-52) are not addressed by this paper (no effect-site or analgesic concentration measured). Verdict: not-applicable for citation; PK comparison consistent for Vss, library clearance lower than this study by about a quarter.
