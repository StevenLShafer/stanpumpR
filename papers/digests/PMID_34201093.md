# Tatipalli 2021: spironolactone and canrenone popPK (CaroSpir suspension), bridged to children

## Study
- Pooled data from three single-dose phase I studies of CaroSpir oral suspension, all fasted for the modelled data (Table 1, p3): 063-15 (pilot, 25 mg, crossover vs Aldactone 25 mg, N = 14), 064-15 (pivotal, 100 mg, vs Aldactone 100 mg, N = 56), 084-15 (food effect, 100 mg, fed vs fasted, N = 23 of 24 completed). Only the suspension arms were modelled (p3).
- Population: final model on **N = 92** healthy adult **men of South Asian (Indian) descent** (p5). Table 2 (p6, rendered) lists N = 14/56/24, total 94; the text explains the gap (one 063-15 subject excluded for a sampling deviation; 23 of 24 completed 084-15; p7). All subjects (Table 2): age 30.3 ± 6.2 y (19-44), height 167.2 ± 6 cm, weight 64.1 ± 6.4 kg (53.1-83.1), BMI 22.9 ± 1.7 kg/m2.
- Analytes: spironolactone (SPIR) and canrenone (CAN) in plasma. The assay is not described in the article.
- NONMEM 7.3, ADVAN13/$DES, FOCEI; bootstrap of 1000 resamples, 540 successful (p7). Simulations in MATLAB R2020b.
- Paediatric extrapolation by fixed allometry, (WT/70)^0.75 for clearances and ^1 for volumes, with CDC 50th-percentile weights (Eq. 1, p4); adult IIV and residual error carried over.

## Model and parameters
Structure (Figure 1 and text, p6, rendered): first-order absorption with a lag into SPIR central (V2) with a peripheral SPIR compartment (V3, Q). SPIR leaves central by two routes: `CL x (1 - Fm)` ("30% of SPIR eliminated by other routes") and `CLM1 x Fm` into CAN central (V4); CAN has a peripheral compartment (V5, Q1) and is eliminated by CLM. Fm is fixed at 0.7. Figure 1 caption: spironolactone total clearance = CL x (1 - Fm) + CLM1 x Fm. All clearances and volumes are apparent (divided by F, which is unidentifiable without IV data; p6-7). Exponential IIV, proportional residual error; IIV on ka, CL, V2, Q, V4, CLM, Q1, V5 and CLM1 with a CL-V2 covariance (p7).

Final estimates, Table 3 (p8, rendered). "Bootstrap" columns as printed: mean and an interval the table heads "95% CI".

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No blood-pressure, potassium or natriuresis end point is modelled. Target exposures for the children are the AUCs seen in fasted healthy adults after 25 and 100 mg (p4-5).

## Covariates
None retained. Age, weight, BMI, body surface, urea, creatinine, sodium, potassium, total protein and albumin were screened visually, with no trend (p7). The authors attribute this to the narrow weight range (64.08 ± 6.43 kg) and say no general statement about a weight effect can be made from these data (p14). The paediatric scaling is fixed allometry, not estimated.

Simulation scenarios, Table 5 (p9, rendered):
- Cirrhosis: CL down 84.44%, CLM1 down 37.76% (tuned to the literature CAN Tmax), CLM down 71.55%.
- Fed state, from study 084-15: bioavailability factor f up 100%, ka down 93.5% (flip-flop), Fm down 60%.
- Cirrhotic and fed: all of the above.

Recommended paediatric single doses: 0.5 and 1.5 mg/kg, matching adult 25 and 100 mg exposure (p9, p15).

## Caveats
- The adults are all healthy young Indian men, a single ethnicity and sex, given single doses fasted. The formulation is the suspension, which had 15-37% higher AUC than Aldactone tablets, more at 100 mg (Introduction, p2).
- F is unidentified, so everything is apparent. The split CL x (1-Fm) + CLM1 x Fm depends on the fixed Fm.
- Estimates differ from the bootstrap means for ka (5.22 vs 4.24). One IIV interval crosses zero (Q1).
- The fed and cirrhosis changes are scenario assumptions, not fitted. The paediatric predictions are untested.
- Funded data from CMP Pharma. Four authors are consultants (p17).
