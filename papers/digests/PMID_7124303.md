# Virtanen 1982: atropine pharmacokinetics and age

## Study
Turku, Finland. 52 patients in all (PDF p1, printed p297).
- **Age study, 31 patients**, 0.02 mg/kg IV bolus at the start of a "combination anaesthesia" (general):
  Group 1, 13 children having major surgery, 0.08-10 years (mean 2.9), 3.6-29 kg (mean 12.1);
  Group 2, 8 healthy adults (ASA 1, 4 M / 4 F), 16-58 years (mean 35.7), 60-94 kg (mean 68.6);
  Group 3, 10 elderly (5 M / 5 F; 5 ASA 2, 5 ASA 3 — three with heart failure on digoxin and/or diuretics, one
  hypertensive on hydrochlorothiazide, one mild diabetic on chlorpropamide), 65-75 years (mean 69.3), 55-100 kg
  (mean 76.3), all having major surgery (p1; Table 1, p2).
- Venous samples at 0, 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60 min and 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 12, 24 h (p1).
- **CSF study, Group 4, 21 patients** under spinal anaesthesia (12 M / 9 F, ASA 1-2, 26-77 y, mean 51; 52-94 kg,
  mean 71): one paired serum + CSF sample each, 8-80 min after 0.010 mg/kg IV or 45-105 min after 0.015 mg/kg IM (p1).
- Assay: the authors' own radioimmunoassay (Virtanen 1980) in serum and CSF; serum-water (free) concentration by
  overnight equilibrium dialysis at 37 °C, pH 7.4 (p1). Whether doses are atropine sulfate or base is not stated.
- Two-compartment open model "as in our earlier study" (Kanto 1981); fitting method/software not given.
  Statistics: unpaired Student's t-test.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, EC50 or therapeutic concentration is reported.
- Fig. 2 (p3), mean ± SEM: systolic and diastolic pressure and heart rate for 15 min after 0.02 mg/kg IV,
  adults 16-41 y (n=6) vs elderly 65-75 y (n=8). In adults heart rate is already raised at the first point
  (2 min) and plateaus near 105-108 /min from about 4 min to 15 min; in the elderly it barely changes from ~78-80
  (read from the figure; significance stars at 2-15 min). The response in children under and over 2 y did not
  differ (p2).
- CSF (Table 3, p2, Group 4): none measurable after 0.010 mg/kg IV; after 0.015 mg/kg IM, serum 11.2-30.1 and CSF
  5.6-9.2 nmol/L at 45-105 min, CSF/serum 29.7-50.0 %; no equilibrium between free serum drug and CSF by 105 min.
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Age, by group comparison only (no equation). Elderly: lower CL, longer t½β. Under 2 y: larger Vβ, longer t½β,
clearance unchanged. No age effect on protein binding or distribution phase.

## Caveats
- Small groups; group (not regression) analysis. The elderly group is ASA 2-3 with heart failure and diuretics,
  so age is confounded with disease; the adult comparator is 8 anaesthetised ASA 1 patients.
- The authors say the RIA's sensitivity limited sampling to 24 h, so in some elderly only one or two half-lives
  were observed (p3): the elderly t½β (and so CL) are imprecise.
- Racemic RIA; whether it reads metabolites or only intact atropine is not discussed here. Salt vs base of the
  dose is not stated.
- Clearance here is far lower than the GC-MS study the library models (below); the authors say their adult values
  agree with their earlier RIA work and with Gundert-Remy's GC-MS abstract (p3).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **R/drugs_atropine.R:48-50** — "Clearance falls and the half-life lengthens with age (Virtanen 1982) ... neither
  is a covariate here." Paper: elderly 65-75 y CL 2.9 vs 6.8 mL/min/kg in adults 16-58 y (P<0.01) and t½β 10.0 vs
  3.0 h (Table 1). **Matches** as a statement about the elderly. Two nuances the one-line summary drops: the
  comparator is the paper's own 8 anaesthetised adults, not the library's Hinderling volunteers; and the effect is
  not monotonic in age — children under 2 also have a longer t½β (6.9 vs 2.5 h), from a larger Vβ, with clearance
  unchanged (Table 2). Since the model applies no age factor, nothing in the code depends on either point. The
  short-form citation in the context notes is confirmed as this paper (authors, journal, year, title).
- **Independent check against the library's model (Hinderling 1985, `R/drugs_atropine.R:85-88`), not cited for
  it:** Hinderling's CL 1175 mL/min (base) in 66-79 kg men is about 16 mL/min/kg (derived: 1175/72.5), and the
  library, entering doses as sulfate, uses about 19.5 mL/min/kg of labelled dose (derived: /0.833). Virtanen's
  adults give 6.8 mL/min/kg (dose salt unstated), roughly 2.4-2.9× lower; Vβ 1.6 L/kg vs Hinderling's
  pseudo-steady-state 234 L ≈ 3.2 L/kg (derived), about half; t½β 3.0 h vs the model's 136 min (2.3 h), longer.
  The direction is consistent across all three: an RIA in anaesthetised patients reports a smaller, slower-cleared
  atropine than GC-MS in awake volunteers. The model's header does not mention this; it does not change the code.
- **tPeak 1.5 min (`R/drugs_atropine.R:59-63, 82`)** is not cited to this paper. Fig. 2 is consistent with a fast
  onset in adults (heart rate already up at 2 min, plateau by ~4 min) but its 2-minute first sample cannot place a
  peak at 1.5 min; the elderly showed almost no heart-rate response at 0.02 mg/kg.
