# White 1985: ketamine isomers in volunteers

## Study
- Five healthy male volunteers (age 36 +/- 3 y, weight 75 +/- 3 kg, mean +/- SD), randomised cross-over, each given all three drugs 7-14 days apart (PDF p.1-2).
- Drugs as IV infusion (hydrochloride salts) after glycopyrrolate 0.2 mg: racemic ketamine 50 mg/min, S(+) 25 mg/min, R(-) 75 mg/min, over 5-7 min (stopped at 5 min or earlier when the EEG showed no further change). Total doses: racemate 275 +/- 25 mg, S(+) 140 +/- 21 mg, R(-) 429 +/- 37 mg (abstract and p.2).
- Venous blood at 1-min intervals during the infusion, then every 5-60 min up to 480 min. Total serum ketamine and norketamine by gas chromatography (not stereoselective: racemate concentrations are the sum of isomers). Two-compartment model fitted per volunteer by extended least squares (Sheiner, unpublished).
- End points: anaesthetic state (loss of eyelid reflex), 4-lead EEG, times to eyelid reflex, command-following and orientation, psychomotor battery (Trieger dots, symbol-digit, visual analogue scales, time distortion) to return within 10% of baseline.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis fitted. EEG: all three drugs reached a plateau of maximal slowing (median frequency analysis, "in preparation" Schuttler et al.); the text states median frequency correlated with serum concentration, but no values are given. R(-) could not produce the EEG slowing of S(+) or racemate even at larger doses (Fig. 2).
- No MEAC or analgesic concentration. Concentrations are at recovery from anaesthesia (total ketamine, venous serum, GC, ug/mL; mean +/- SD, n=5), Table II (PDF p.5, printed 201):
  - Opened eyes: racemate 2.6 +/- 0.7 (11 min); S(+) 1.2 +/- 0.3 (8 min); R(-) 5.2 +/- 0.8 (7 min).
  - Squeezed hand: 1.6 +/- 0.5 (22 min); 1.0 +/- 0.3 (12 min); 4.1 +/- 0.9 (9 min).
  - Oriented to person: 1.2 +/- 0.3 (33 min); 0.9 +/- 0.2 (14 min); 3.9 +/- 0.8 (10 min).
  - Oriented to person, place, time: 1.0 +/- 0.1 (45 min); 0.7 +/- 0.2 (21 min); 2.7 +/- 0.5 (18 min).
- Return of psychomotor tests to within 10% of baseline, Table III (PDF p.5): racemate 118-178 min at about 0.4-0.5 ug/mL; S(+) 74-150 min at 0.2-0.3 ug/mL; R(-) 57-123 min at 0.7-1.2 ug/mL (individual tests in the table).
- Potency ratio S(+):R(-): 4:1 from the awakening/orientation concentrations; 3:1 to 5:1 for psychomotor impairment (abstract, p.3). Anaesthetic state (eyelid reflex loss) lasted 6 +/- 2 min and began 3 +/- 1 min after infusion start for all three drugs.
- Maximal rises in mean arterial pressure 42-59% and heart rate 56-84% (abstract and text, Fig. 3); no differences between drugs and no correlation with concentrations.

## Covariates
None analysed (n=5 healthy men).

## Caveats
- n=5, healthy male volunteers, cross-over with fixed sequences; total (not stereospecific) serum ketamine, so the racemate concentrations do not separate isomers (the authors discuss possible chiral metabolic inhibition).
- Distribution-phase parameters are very variable (CV up to 78%), which matters for the Vc value.
- Heart-rate maximum is 56-84% in the Results but 56-85% in Geisslinger's citation of this work; trivial.
- No compartment rate constants are printed, so the model cannot be reproduced from this paper alone.
