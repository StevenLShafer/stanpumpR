# Verstuyft 2012: CYP2C9 and VKORC1 in a PK/PD model of fluindione and acenocoumarol

PDF page = printed page - 40 throughout (PDF p1 = printed 41).

## Study
- Design: open-label, randomised, two-period crossover (PDF p4, printed 44). Period A was fluindione 20 mg (Previscan);
  period B was acenocoumarol 4 mg (Sintrom). Washout was at least 2 weeks. **Two oral doses per period**: the first
  at 09:00 on day 1, the second on the morning of day 2 after an overnight fast. PK and PD were studied from 24 h to
  72 h (day 2 to day 3). This was not a single-dose study.
- Subjects: 24 healthy White adults, all of whom completed. They were chosen from a genotyped database by CYP2C9
  genotype, with BMI between 20.5 and 25.3 kg/m2. 10 smoked (more than 3 cigarettes a day). Table I (PDF p3):
  - CYP2C9 *1/*1 (n = 11): 10 M / 1 F, age 28.5 ± 8.6 y, weight 71.4 ± 10.9 kg, baseline INR 1.13 ± 0.11.
  - CYP2C9 *3 carriers (n = 13, of whom 2 were *2/*3): 9 M / 4 F, age 26.8 ± 8.0 y, weight 67.8 ± 9.8 kg,
    baseline INR 1.28 ± 0.24.
  - VKORC1 1173C>T (CC/CT/TT): 1/6/4 in the wild type and 4/6/3 in the *3 carriers. Smokers: 5 per group.
  - PAX/CAF ratio: 0.51 ± 0.25 (wild type) and 0.64 ± 0.21 (*3) in smokers; 0.40 ± 0.14 and 0.40 ± 0.12 in
    non-smokers.
  - Whole group (PDF p6): age 27.6 ± 8.1 y, weight 69.4 ± 10.3 kg, BMI 22.9 ± 2.4 kg/m2.
- Sampling (PDF p4):
  - INR: before the second dose and at 0.5, 8, 12, 24 and 48 h after it.
  - Drug concentrations: 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 12, 24 and 48 h after the second dose.
- Assays:
  - Fluindione: HPLC-UV, linear over 0.05-6 mg/L, LLOQ 0.1 mg/L.
  - R- and S-acenocoumarol: enantioselective HPLC after derivatisation.
  - CYP1A2 phenotype: plasma paraxanthine/caffeine ratio 5 h after 140 mg caffeine.
  - INR: Thromborel S.
- Primary end point: INR at 48 h, i.e. 24 h after the second dose.
- Software: Monolix 3.1 (SAEM). Model selection used the LRT, AIC/BIC and goodness-of-fit plots. Evaluation used
  VPCs (1000 replicates). Steady-state simulations used 20 doses.

## Model and parameters
- PK: two-compartment, first-order oral input, linear elimination; all parameters apparent (/F). Fluindione has no
  lag time. S-acenocoumarol has a lag; R-acenocoumarol was modelled separately but the data are not shown (PDF p7).
  The paper does not say whether the S-acenocoumarol model's dose input was the racemic 4 mg or the S half.
- Covariate forms (Eq. 2-4, PDF p5): continuous covariates as (BW/median)^θ, with median weight 69 kg in the tables.
  Each genotype is a two-level category (wild type vs carrier of at least one variant), a multiplicative factor on
  the typical value.
- **Sign convention problem (Tables II and III).** The tables print each genotype factor as [1 - θ] with signed θ.
  Read literally:
  - Fluindione: a *3 carrier has CL × 1.363, and VKORC1 CC/CT has C50 × 0.7.
  - S-acenocoumarol: a *3 carrier has CL × 1.3, and CC/CT has C50 × (1 - 1.29) = -0.29, which is an impossible
    negative C50.
  - The literal reading also contradicts the paper's own Table IV and text: *3 and VKORC1 TT give the higher INR.
  - Reading the factor as (1 + θ) gives the directions the paper reports: *3 has CL × 0.637 (fluindione) and × 0.7
    (S-acenocoumarol); CC/CT has C50 × 1.30 (fluindione) and × 2.29 (S-acenocoumarol).
  - The paper does not explain the convention. The (1 + θ) reading is an inference.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: INR (Thromborel S), modelled as 1/INR. Equation 1 (PDF p5) is an indirect-response model after Mentré
  1998 (PDF p5):
  - d(1/INR)/dt = kin·(1 - C^γ/(C^γ + C50^γ)) - kout·(1/INR), with 1/INR0 = kin/kout.
  - Emax is fixed at 100%. C50 is defined as the concentration that reduces 1/INR by 50%.
- Exposure driver:
  - Acenocoumarol: S-acenocoumarol concentration alone. R alone and R + S additive were tested and not selected.
  - Fluindione: the fluindione concentration.
- VPC coverage within the 90% interval: fluindione 95% (PK) and 94% (PD); acenocoumarol 93% and 91% (PDF p6, p8).
- Simulated steady-state INRmax (Table IV, PDF p11; n/a = row has no acenocoumarol value):

- Not reported: no ke0 or effect site, no therapeutic concentration, and no potency ratio between the drugs.

## Covariates
- Fluindione:
  - CL: body weight (exponent 0.75 fixed), CYP1A2 (PAX/CAF exponent 0.617) and CYP2C9*3.
  - V1: weight (linear, fixed).
  - C50: VKORC1 (CC/CT vs TT).
  - The covariates cut the IIV in CL from 40% to 18% and in V1 from 27% to 20%.
- S-acenocoumarol:
  - CL: CYP2C9*3, which cut the IIV from 42% to 24.7%.
  - V1: weight, 66.7% to 53%.
  - C50: VKORC1, 140% to 108%.
- No CYP1A2 or smoking effect on acenocoumarol.
- Age, sex and BMI were tested and not retained.
- CYP2C9*2 was not a separate category: carriers were classed as wild type vs at least one variant.

## Caveats
- Small sample: 24 young, healthy, normal-BMI White volunteers.
- Two doses only. PK was observed for 48 h after the second dose, so steady state is extrapolated. The authors say
  the young age and the two doses differ from clinical use.
- The VKORC1 groups were not balanced by design. The study started before the gene was identified, and the sample
  holds only 5 CC subjects.
- The genotype factors' sign convention is inconsistent as printed; see Model.
- Table IV has an internally inconsistent row and no stated dose.
- Table and text disagree on the C50 IIV for both drugs.
- Fluindione misfits somewhat in the absorption phase (PDF p6).
