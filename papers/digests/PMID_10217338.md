# Armijo 1999: lamotrigine concentration-to-dose ratio, age and comedication

## Study
Retrospective therapeutic-drug-monitoring study, Santander/Spain. **423 blood samples from 164 patients**
aged 1-72 years (22.4 +/- 16.7 y; 68 children <= 14 y, 96 adults) with partial or generalised epilepsy
resistant to conventional drugs, on twice-daily lamotrigine alone (n = 28) or with other antiepileptics
(n = 136).

Sampling: steady state (> 1 month on treatment), fasting, 08:00-10:00 before the dose, ~12 h after the last
dose; 89% outpatients. One sample in 78 patients; in the other 86, a mean 4.0 +/- 3.0 samples averaged per
patient. Lamotrigine assayed in triplicate by HPLC (Cohen's method as modified); carbamazepine, phenytoin,
phenobarbital and valproate in duplicate by FPIA (Abbott). Intraday CV < 10% for all.

**The measured quantity is the C/D ratio**: trough steady-state lamotrigine concentration (mg/L) divided by
the daily dose in mg/kg. Four treatment groups (printed p 184): LTG alone (including drugs not known to
modify metabolism - clobazam, clonazepam, ethosuximide, gabapentin, tiagabine, topiramate, vigabatrin);
LTG + inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone); LTG + valproate; LTG + valproate +
inducers. Statistics: t test, one-way and two-way ANOVA with Newman-Keuls, linear and multiple regression
(SPSS/PC+ 4.0). Data are mean +/- SD in text and tables.

This is a C/D-ratio study: **no compartmental model, no clearance, volume or half-life is estimated.**

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. The Discussion reviews proposed lamotrigine target ranges from the literature (a tentative
1-4 mg/L from early studies; good tolerance above 10 mg/L; refractory patients sometimes above 15 mg/L) and
states no clear concentration-response relationship has been established - a literature summary, not a
finding of this study.

## Covariates
Treatment group (the four comedication classes, and the four inducer subgroups), patient age (in two and
six bands, and continuously), lamotrigine dose in mg, body weight, and the comedication's own trough
concentration (Table 4). Sex is not examined. Multiple regression in adults on inducers:
`C/D = 2.21 + 0.0059 x dose(mg) - 0.021 x weight(kg) - 0.026 x age(y)` (R = 0.76, p < 0.001), printed p 184.
Note the study's striking finding that C/D **rose** with the phenytoin trough level (Table 4), the opposite
of what induction predicts; the authors call it surprising and unexplained.

## Caveats
- Retrospective, unbalanced groups (n = 15 to 92), observational comedication.
- C/D is an **apparent** clearance surrogate: it cannot separate clearance from bioavailability, and the
  authors themselves note that trough-based ratios may exaggerate a comedication effect relative to
  half-life because induction increases the within-day fluctuation (printed p 188).
- The groups differ in age structure, and age itself influences C/D, so the group contrasts are partly
  confounded; the authors address this with two-way ANOVA but the subgroup cells are small (Table 3 has
  cells of n = 1).
- The valproate effect here (~x0.25) is the **strongest** in the lamotrigine literature - a 10-fold
  inducers-vs-valproate spread that the authors explicitly contrast with the 4-fold spread seen in half-life
  data and the 2-fold difference built into dosing recommendations.
- The source file is an Ovid HTML-to-PDF printout (31 pages, navigation furniture interleaved); every table
  value here was read off a rendered page image, not the text layer.
