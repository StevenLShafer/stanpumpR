# Savage 2008: Sizing up allometric scaling theory

## Study
Theoretical and numerical paper, no drug, no patients. It re-derives the West-Brown-Enquist (WBE) vascular-network model of metabolic scaling (eight assumptions, pp. 3-4) and shows that the 3/4 exponent holds exactly only in the limit of infinite network size (Eq. 13, p. 5). For finite networks the model gives a curved log-log relation (Eq. 10), so a straight-line fit over a size range gives an exponent that depends on the smallest organism's size. The authors also compare the model with a published basal-metabolic-rate dataset of 626 mammals (Savage 2004; their Fig. 10, pp. 12-13).

## Model and parameters
No PK model. Key theoretical results, all derived or simulated by the authors:
```yaml
parameters:
  - {name: asymptotic exponent, value: 0.75, unit: dimensionless, source: "Eq. 13, p. 5", note: "infinite-size limit of metabolic rate vs body mass"}
  - {name: WBE finite-size exponent, value: 0.81, unit: dimensionless, source: "Fig. 6B, pp. 9-10; abstract", note: "derived: canonical WBE model, n=2, transition level 24, 8 orders of magnitude of mass"}
  - {name: area-preserving-only exponent, value: 0.743, unit: dimensionless, source: "Fig. 3A caption, p. 7", note: "one simulated example, 8 orders of magnitude; below 3/4"}
  - {name: mammal mass range, value: 8, unit: orders of magnitude, source: "p. 7 and p. 9", note: "shrew to whale"}
```
Direction of the finite-size effect depends on the branching regime (pp. 6-9): area-preserving only gives fitted exponents below 3/4; area-increasing only gives exponents below 1 that approach 1; the mixed canonical network gives exponents above 3/4, approaching 3/4 from above. The empirical mammal data curve the opposite way (exponent rises with mass, Fig. 10). Their Fig. 9 and Fig. 7 explore how changing the branching ratio or the transition level moves the exponent.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Not an opioid paper. No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None. Body mass is the only size variable. Blood volume is taken as proportional to body mass (Assumption 6, p. 4).

## Caveats
The authors state that the 0.81 prediction is at odds with the empirical exponent of about 3/4 or lower, that the data analysis may need reassessment, and that empirical data for restricted size classes are sparse (p. 13). It concerns across-species metabolic rate; it does not address drug clearance or within-species (human) scaling.
