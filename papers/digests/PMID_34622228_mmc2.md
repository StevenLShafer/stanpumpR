# Overgaard 2021b (article + supplement): semaglutide exposure-response, oral PIONEER vs s.c. SUSTAIN

The same paper as `PMID_34622228.md` (digested earlier from the 14-page article alone), re-digested here from the
28-page file that also carries the supplement (Table S1, Methods S1-S2). The supplement alone is `PMID_34622228_mmc1`.
PDF pages: p. 1 graphical abstract; printed pp. 1-9 are PDF pp. 2-10 (Table 1 on PDF p. 3, Figure 2 on p. 5);
STAR Methods e1-e4 are PDF pp. 11-14; supplement PDF pp. 15-28 (Table S1 on p. 17).

## Study
Exposure-response comparison of oral semaglutide (Rybelsus tablets; PIONEER 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 9; 3, 7, 14 mg once daily,
26 weeks) with s.c. semaglutide (SUSTAIN 1, 2, 3, 6, SUSTAIN Japan OAD; 0.5 and 1.0 mg weekly; week 30). Sponsor
trial data, Novo Nordisk authors. Oral dosing: up to 120 mL water, at least 30 min before first meal or other oral drug.
Population PK data: 10,527 observations, 2,345 subjects, 914 below LLOQ (0.729 nmol/L; assigned LLOQ/2), 154 excluded
(1.5%); single sample per visit at weeks 4, 8, 14, 26 etc. (PIONEER 2 aimed at 25 min post-dose; PIONEER 9 two samples
at week 26); total protein-bound semaglutide by LC-MS (STAR Methods, PDF p. 12). Exposure-response population 3,003
subjects including placebo (Table 1, page image): unmatched PIONEER age 59.4 +/- 10.9 y, 44.8% female, HbA1c
8.1 +/- 0.8%, weight 88.2 +/- 21.9 kg, diabetes duration 9.5 +/- 7.9 y, Asian 25.9%, Black 6.3%, renal normal 60.2%
/ mild 28.8% / moderate 11.0%; propensity-matched PIONEER n = 1,551 (weight 87.2 +/- 22.4 kg) vs SUSTAIN n = 1,551
(86.3 +/- 22.7 kg). Dose groups unmatched: 3 mg 629, 7 mg 620, 14 mg 1,182, placebo 572. NONMEM 7.3, PsN 4.60,
R 3.2.3, MatchIt for propensity matching.

## Model and parameters
Oral PK model prespecified from Overgaard 2019 (Diabetes Ther 10:649, clinical pharmacology data): two compartments,
shared disposition across routes; every parameter fixed except F (STAR Methods, PDF p. 12). Final covariates on F
(oral): sex, race (Asian vs White), body weight, upper GI disease, trial effects (text says PIONEER 3 and 5), log-normal
IIV on F, proportional residual error. S.c.: one-compartment model of Carlsson Petri 2018 (CL/F, V/F), same covariates
plus injection site, without GI disease. Cavg = average steady-state concentration in the dosing interval at the target
dose, one per subject; sensitivity analyses gave Cavg correlation >0.99 with the final.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak or hysteresis: exposure is a steady-state Cavg and responses are week-26 (PIONEER) or week-30
(SUSTAIN) means. Exposure-response models (STAR Methods, PDF pp. 13-14): Emax models for HbA1c change (baseline HbA1c
and trial population on effect; placebo effects of diabetes duration and baseline HbA1c) and for % body weight change
(sex and baseline HbA1c on effect; placebo effects of diabetes duration, race, ethnicity); logit models for HbA1c <7.0%
(ADA) and <=6.5% (AACE) and for nausea and vomiting. No EC50, Emax or gamma values are printed in the article text.
Propensity-matched predictions at 14.6 nM (corresponding to 14 mg), oral vs s.c.: HbA1c -1.58% vs -1.62%; body weight
loss 3.77% vs 3.48%; nausea 11.1% vs 11.8%; vomiting 4.2% vs 4.2% (Results, printed p. 4, PDF p. 5, page image).
Highest baseline HbA1c (>=9.1%) about -3% at high exposure vs about -1% for <7.5% (Results, printed p. 3, text layer).
Of 14 mg patients, 19% had exposure below the 0.5-1.0 mg s.c. range yet still about -1% HbA1c; 3% fell below an
exposure the authors call below the therapeutic range, expected HbA1c reduction <0.5% (Discussion, printed p. 5, text
layer). Switching from 14 mg oral to 0.5 mg s.c.: 88% would have a <2-fold increase in exposure (printed p. 4).
No MEAC. No potency ratio between oral and s.c. mg doses (the relation is by exposure). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Exposure (oral F): male 0.814, Asian 1.1, upper GI disease 1.13, weight exponent 0.374 (Table S1); CL and V scale with
weight by fixed exponents 1.01 and 0.923. Body weight is described as the most important variable for exposure for
both formulations (Summary). Exposure-response: baseline HbA1c and trial population (insulin, moderate renal
impairment lower response); sex for weight loss, nausea and vomiting; route of administration did not matter.

## Caveats
Authors: oral and s.c. data from separate programs, partly corrected by propensity matching; no insulin, glucagon,
gastric emptying or GLP-1R data; **total, not free**, semaglutide measured. Oral variability is larger than s.c.; whether
low exposures reflect absorption or adherence is stated to be uncertain (Discussion, printed p. 5). Not stated: whether
PIONEER dosing was supervised (it was outpatient trial dosing). Fixed disposition parameters are borrowed from
healthy-and-patient clinical pharmacology data, so Table S1 CIs cover F only.
