# Learned-Coughlin 2003: striatal dopamine-transporter occupancy by bupropion SR (PET)

## Study
- Open, single-arm PET study (GlaxoSmithKline protocol OHB10001, Uppsala). 6 healthy white men, mean age 30.7 y
  (23-38), weight 80.0 kg (57.4-96.7), height 180.2 cm (172-187), BMI 24.57 (19.4-28.8) (p. 801).
- Bupropion SR 150 mg once daily days 1-3, then 150 mg every 12 h from day 4 to the morning of day 11 (steady state).
- PET with 11C-βCIT-FE (DAT ligand, ~275 MBq IV bolus, 90-min dynamic scan) at baseline (1-7 days before dosing)
  and 3, 12 and 24 h after the last dose (subject 6: 3, 10 and 22 h). Simplified reference tissue model, striatum
  target, cerebellum reference; occupancy = (BPbase - BPdrug)/BPbase x 100%.
- Plasma bupropion and metabolites by GLP-validated LC/MS/MS, predose days 8-11 and for 25.5 h after the last dose.

## Model and parameters

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: striatal DAT occupancy by PET (bupropion plus metabolites together; not separable).
- Table 1, mean (SD) occupancy: 26.0% (8.3) at 3 h, 26.0% (6.6) at 12 h, 25.2% (9.7) at 24 h; individual values
  12-36%. All different from zero (p < .01). Baseline BP 5.7 (0.6).
- No EC50, Emax or concentration-occupancy relation: the authors state that low between-subject variability in PK and
  PD prevented characterising it, so the PK/PD data were not reported (p. 801).
- No ke0 / tPeak; occupancy flat over 3-24 h after the last dose. Contrast given: cocaine 65-75% at reinforcing doses.
- Not an opioid; no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None examined.

## Caveats
- n = 6 healthy young men; not depressed patients (the authors note DAT binding differs in depression).
- Text says baseline BP "ranged from 5.0-5.6 (mean 5.7)", inconsistent with Table 1 (5.0-6.6); the table is internally
  consistent.
- Sponsor-funded (GSK).
