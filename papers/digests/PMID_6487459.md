# Upton 1984: Naproxen pharmacokinetics in the elderly

## Study
- 10 healthy elderly men, 66-81 y (71 ± 4 y, mean ± SD), 72.4 ± 10.4 kg; 10 healthy young men, 22-39 y (29 ± 6 y),
  73.8 ± 5.4 kg. No medication. (p. 208)
- Naproxen (Naprosyn) 375 mg orally, single dose on day 1; from 48 h later 375 mg every 12 h for 6 days, ending with
  the 14th dose at 08:00 on day 9. Fasted 10 h before / 4 h after doses 1 and 14.
- Blood at 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24, 36, 48 h after doses 1 and 14. HPLC (sensitivity 2 ng/mL); unbound fraction by
  equilibrium dialysis (16 h, 37 °C, pH 7.4) with fluid-shift correction, serial binding largely on days 9-10.
- Non-compartmental: AUC by linear trapezoid to peak, log-trapezoid after; CL/F = dose/AUC(0-inf) (day 1) or
  dose/AUC(0-12h) (day 9); V/F = CL/F ÷ λ; CLu/F from unbound AUC on day 9. Creatinine clearance by Cockcroft-Gault.
- Steady state judged reached by day 9 (no difference in 08:00 levels days 7-9).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No effect, threshold or concentration-response data; the authors advise halving doses in the elderly
pending such data (p. 213). Not an opioid.

## Covariates
Age group only (elderly vs young). Unbound fraction doubled in the elderly at peak and trough; CLu/F about 50% lower;
total CL/F lower only after the single dose. Albumin fall judged too small to explain the binding change.
Concentration-dependent binding: CL/F and V/F rose from day 1 to day 9 in both groups.

## Caveats
- Men only, n = 10 per group, healthy; oral only, so F unknown (authors cite near-complete F in young men).
- Binding is concentration-dependent, so λ, V and Vu are themselves concentration-dependent (p. 212).
- Reversible metabolism via renally excreted acyl glucuronide may confound clearance interpretation.
