# Jacobs 1993: Effect-compartment control algorithm

## Study
Duke (Anesthesiology, Biomedical Engineering); simulation only; supported in part by Abbott. Received June 1991, revised January 1993.

## Model and parameters
Linear mammillary model plus a one-compartment effect site (k1e negligible, ke0; Fig. 1). Effect-site unit disposition function udf_e(t) = (ke0/V1)(M1 e^-pi t + M2 e^-alpha t + M3 e^-beta t + M4 e^-ke0 t) (eq 3; coefficients from Holford and Sheiner 1981). Claims, without proof, that no closed form gives the time to the next effect-site peak when drug is already present, and that this time is shorter than t_max for zero initial conditions (Fig. 2), so control must be iterative.

Algorithm (eq 2; steps 1-7, p. 996-997): each 10 s, find the smallest rate r over the next interval whose effect-site peak equals the setpoint: r = min over t' in 1-s steps up to t_max of (Setpoint - Ce_predicted(t*+t')) / upr(t'), where upr is the effect-site response to a unit 10-s pulse. Negative r -> 0 (no infusion while Ce > setpoint). Hand-off: if Cp within 10% and Ce within 0.5% of setpoint, use the plasma algorithm (Jacobs 1990). Rates rounded down to pump constraints.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Setpoint Ce 4 ng/mL reached in 3.9 min, 10-90% rise time 2.3 min, no overshoot (Fig. 4a), vs 15 min rise time when targeting the plasma (Fig. 4b).
- Cp transiently ~20 ng/mL when targeting Ce 4 ng/mL (Fig. 6).
- Fig. 7: setpoints 4, 3, 5 ng/mL with a 2-min pump stop at 50 min; recovers without Ce overshoot.
- Fentanyl tPeak after a 10-s infusion with these parameters 3.78 min (Fig. 3). No MEAC, no potency ratio. End point for ke0: not stated here (cited Scott and Stanski 1987, EEG).

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Simulation only; no clinical data. Authors compare with Shafer and Gregg 1992 and expect no clinically important difference; their preference is for analytic (Bailey-Shafer) rather than difference-equation simulation. Tolerances 10% / 0.5% chosen empirically.
