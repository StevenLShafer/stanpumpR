# Ryu 2018: prednisone and prednisolone pharmacokinetics in pregnancy and lactation

PDF page p = printed page 1222 + p (PDF 1 = p. 1223).

## Study
- Prospective multicentre (Obstetric-Fetal Pharmacology Research Units: Washington, Pittsburgh, Georgetown,
  UTMB Galveston) steady-state NCA study (p. 1224).
- 19 women, 16-41 years, taking prednisone for clinical reasons (SLE 11, RA 3, asthma, transplant, autoimmune
  hepatitis, myasthenia gravis, Wegener granulomatosis 1 each); race 9 black, 7 white, 2 Hispanic, 1 Asian. Study
  day 1: age 27 +/- 6 y, height 162 +/- 11 cm, weight 87 +/- 21 kg (p. 1226).
- Study days: early (10-14 wk, n = 3), mid (22-26 wk, n = 9), late pregnancy (34-38 wk; n = 14 in Results p. 1226,
  n = 13 in the abstract), and at least 12 weeks postpartum with lactation (n = 2) or without (n = 5); one woman did
  both postpartum days; 6 women contributed both pregnancy and postpartum data (p. 1224, 1226).
- Oral prednisone 4-40 mg/day, once or twice daily, at clinical doses; fasted 5 h before to 1 h after the observed
  dose (p. 1224). One subject on cyclosporine was kept in.
- Plasma sampling predose and 0.5-24 h over one dosing interval; urine over the interval; cord venous (13) and
  arterial (6) plasma at delivery; breast milk from 2 women (p. 1224).
- Assay: HPLC-MS, total plasma range 4-800 ng/mL; unbound fraction by equilibrium dialysis (Pierce RED, 4 h, 37 C)
  on every sample because binding was concentration-dependent, unbound range 0.2-100 ng/mL (p. 1224-1225).
- NCA in Phoenix WinNonlin; prednisolone CL/F referenced to the prednisone dose (no molecular-weight correction);
  linear mixed-effects models (lme4) for dose and pregnancy vs postpartum (p. 1225-1226).

- Percent unbound rose with concentration (prednisone r = 0.57, prednisolone r = 0.75) and was significantly lower

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No concentration-effect data. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Dose (prednisone CL/F, Vbeta/F, unbound CL/F rise with dose); concentration (free fraction rises); pregnancy vs
postpartum (lower free fraction; lower total prednisolone CL/F); gestational age: no effect.
Discussion (PDF p. 7): albumin falls about 15% and CBG rises 2- to 3-fold in normal pregnancy (cites Potter 1987).

## Caveats
- Small n; postpartum comparator has 1 subject at 5 and at 10 mg. Patients with autoimmune disease; one on
  cyclosporine. Dose 4-40 mg/day chosen clinically.
- Prednisolone parameters are referenced to the prednisone dose (no separate prednisolone dosing), so "CL/F" mixes
  conversion and elimination.
- Contradiction inside the paper: Results (PDF p. 5) find prednisolone CL/F LOWER in pregnancy (CI -1.8 to -0.1);
  the Discussion (PDF p. 8) says pregnancy increases it.
- Late-pregnancy n is 13 in the abstract and 14 in Results.
- Table 1 SDs of 3.0 for prednisolone Tmax 0.8, Cmax 431.8 and AUC 2054.9 at 20 mg look like a typesetting
  repeat; prednisone Tmax text says it "increased" while the slope given is negative.
- Supplement (assay tables) not read.
