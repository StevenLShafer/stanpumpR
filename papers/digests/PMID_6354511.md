# Ashwood 1983: Temperature correction of blood-gas and pH measurements

## Study
A narrative review (no new data) of formulas for correcting whole-blood pH, PCO2 and PO2 from the measurement temperature to body temperature, covering the derivations, experimental checks and the clinical case for and against correcting. It tabulates literature coefficients and the formulas used by blood-gas analyzers (Table 1), and ends with recommendations (PDF p7-8).

## Model and parameters
All values are literature coefficients for closed-system (anaerobic) temperature change of whole blood, per C.

Clinical-use PO2 correction (eq 25, PDF p8, rendered and confirmed): log(PO2) = log(PO2m) + [(37 - tm)/2.303] x [0.058/(0.243 (PO2m/100)^3.88 + 1) + 0.013]; this is the Severinghaus 1979 form (cited as ref 48), not the 1966 coefficients. The review recommends it over the 1966 Severinghaus form (ref 20, eq 5 in PMID_5912737), the Kelman-Nunn nomogram (ref 19) and Shastri (ref 49), because it holds at all O2 tensions (PDF p5). Table 1 lists the analyzer formulas: pH slopes -0.0146 + 0.0065(7.4 - pH), -0.015, -0.0147 + 0.0065(7.4 - pH), -0.0146; PCO2 exponent 0.021, 0.019, 0.019 (per C, from the text layer).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not a drug paper: no drug pharmacodynamics, ke0, MEAC or potency ratio, and not an opioid paper. The end points are physical-chemical (pH, PCO2, PO2 and oxygen content vs temperature). Relevant clinical statements: oxygen saturation changes by under 2% from 0 to 42 C at saturations above 98% and less at lower saturation (PDF p6), so saturation is treated as temperature-independent; PO2 measured at 37 C is the better index of oxygen content than PO2 at body temperature (Table 5, PDF p6); the reference body-temperature PO2 is 85 +/- 10 Torr, pH 7.40, PCO2 40 Torr. Alveolar-arterial O2 gradient is artifactually decreased by 4 Torr per degree below 37 C if samples are warmed to 37 C before measurement (PDF p7).

## Covariates
pH, temperature, plasma protein, haemoglobin, haematocrit, erythrocyte diphosphoglycerate and total CO2 are discussed as modifiers of the coefficients; the recommended clinical equations ignore all of them.

## Caveats
Review rather than study; the authors take a side (do not correct pH and PCO2 to body temperature when judging acid-base status; they endorse the alpha-stat/Rahn hypothesis), which is a clinical position, not a measurement. The research polynomial (eq 6) should not be extrapolated outside 25-42 C. The review says experimental verification of the PO2 formulas at less than full saturation exists only in three papers. The Shastri equation is called invalid below 50 or above 300 Torr (their unpublished calculation).
