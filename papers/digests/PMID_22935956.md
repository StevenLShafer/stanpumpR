# Allegaert 2012: IV paracetamol 2 g after caesarean delivery, preterm vs term

PDF pages 1-5 = printed pages 484-488.

## Study
- Single tertiary centre (Leuven), EudraCT 2010-020164-37. 36 women recruited, PK estimated in **34** (one sampling
  interval < 3 h; one excluded for an aberrant peak) (p. 485). 28 of them were reported earlier (Kulo 2012 IJOA).
- Women after (semi)elective caesarean under CSE (hyperbaric bupivacaine 7.5 mg + sufentanil, printed "2.5 mg", presumably micrograms),
  TAP blocks (ropivacaine 0.375% 20 mL per side), ketorolac 30 mg q8h (p. 485).
- Mean age 31 y (SD 5), weight **80 kg** (12.8), BSA 1.94 m2 (0.20). 12 preterm (< 37 weeks), 22 term; 8 twin
  pregnancies; 1 type 1 diabetes, 2 mild-moderate pre-eclampsia (p. 485). Height not reported.
- Drug: IV paracetamol (Perfusalgan) **2 g loading dose over 15 min**, shortly after delivery of the newborn and
  placenta. Plasma at 1, 2, 4, 6 h after start of infusion; HPLC (total plasma; method in ref 11).
- Analysis: individual one-compartment, instantaneous input, first-order elimination; log-linear regression of the
  samples; Vd = Dose/C0 (back-extrapolated), K = slope x 2.303, t1/2 = 0.693/K, CL = K x Vd (p. 485).
  Mann-Whitney / unpaired comparisons (MedCalc).

## Model and parameters
Whole cohort (n=34; p. 485, rendered page 2):
- Mean concentrations 23.8 (5.05), 15.7 (4.3), 7.7 (3), 3.9 (1.7) mg/L at 1, 2, 4, 6 h.
- CL mean **22.4 L/h** (SD 9.3); CL/BSA 11.5 L/h/m2 (4.0).
- Vd median 57.8 L (42.9-156); mean **0.74 L/kg** (0.17). t1/2 mean 117 min (22).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. The Discussion cites a meta-analysis (McNicol 2011) that IV paracetamol 1 g gives about 4 h of
analgesia; no effective concentration, ke0 or potency stated. (Not an opioid paper.)

## Covariates
- Preterm delivery (< 37 weeks): higher CL/BSA and Vd/kg than term (comparator: term deliveries in this cohort);
  explained 19% of CL variability (r = 0.43) (p. 487). No equation given.
- Twin pregnancy and maternal disease: no effect.
- Comparisons with non-pregnant women are from **other studies**: the earlier 28-patient report vs healthy
  non-pregnant female volunteers (median 20.3 vs 15.5 L/h, 10.9 vs 9.6 L/h/m2; p. 484, refs 8-9), "+35%" (p. 487);
  Miners 1986 oral third trimester CL/F 27.1 vs 17.1 L/h (+58%); Beaulac-Baillargeon 1994 first trimester +37%.

## Caveats
- Only four samples over 6 h after one dose, one-compartment fit: back-extrapolated Vd and CL = K x Vd are
  approximations of a two-compartment drug (my observation).
- Immediately post-caesarean: surgery, blood loss, fluid shifts and co-medication (ketorolac, intrathecal opioid)
  confound the "pregnancy" effect, as the authors note (p. 487).
- No non-pregnant control group in this study.
- Unlicensed 2 g dose. Height not reported (derived 169 cm from BSA 1.94 m2 and 80 kg, Mosteller).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Registry row** (`pregnancyRegistry.csv:40`): cites this paper but quotes no number from it; the factors come from
  Beleyn 2014, Miners 1986 and Allegaert 2015 (`R/drugs_acetaminophen.R:86-126`). Nothing to transcribe.
  The "0.69 vs 0.74 L/kg" attributed to Kulo 2014 coincides with this paper's term-delivery and whole-cohort means,
  both in pregnant women (see open question). **Cannot verify** without Kulo 2014.
- **Against the library's own model** (Morse 2022, `R/drugs_acetaminophen.R:191`): for this cohort's mean woman
  (80 kg, 169 cm derived, 31 y, female) the model gives CL 25.8 L/h non-pregnant and **38.7 L/h pregnant**
  (x 1.5, line 160) (derived with the library's ffmAlSallami and NFM formula). This paper measured **22.4 L/h**
  (all) and **19.8 L/h** (term) hours after delivery. The library's pregnant clearance is 1.73x (all) to 1.95x (term)
  the measured value, and even its non-pregnant clearance is above it. The 1.5 is a ratio from studies whose
  non-pregnant reference (healthy female volunteers, 15.5 L/h median, p. 484) is far below Morse's 24 L/h/70 kg;
  the header (`R/drugs_acetaminophen.R:96-106`) discusses comparators but not this baseline difference. The CL = K x Vd
  method here would, if anything, overstate clearance, so the gap is not a method artefact in the library's favour.
  Verdict: **discrepancy** (a Kokki-type comparator issue; see open question).
- **Volume**: library V1 + V2 = 73.4 L per 70 kg pharmacokinetic weight, x 1.14 when pregnant; this paper's one-
  compartment Vd is 0.74 L/kg (57.8 L median). Not the same quantity (back-extrapolated one-compartment volume vs
  Vss); no verdict.
