# Stove 2015: unbound vancomycin by equilibrium dialysis vs ultrafiltration

## Study
Laboratory methods study, Ghent University Hospital. 39 anonymised left-over samples (28 serum, 11 plasma) from
22 patients (ICU, burn unit, haematology ward; 21 on continuous, 1 on intermittent infusion), stored at -20 C up
to 3 months. Unbound vancomycin separated by (a) equilibrium dialysis (ED; Fast Micro-Equilibrium Dialyzer,
25 kDa membrane, 37 C, 24 h, HEPES buffer pH 7.28) - the reference; (b) ultrafiltration (UF; Centrifree 30 kDa,
1885 g, 30 min) at 4 C and at 37 C, no pH adjustment. Vancomycin by Architect iVancomycin CMIA (LOQ 3.0 mg/L).
Total protein, albumin, IgA and AAG measured. Prediction model validated in 31 fresh samples; IgA myeloma mixing
experiment (IgA up to 33.8 g/L).

Validation (31 fresh samples, total 8.9-34.7 mg/L): R2 0.945, mean bias 5.0% +/- 7.6%. Proteins (total protein
32-68 g/L, albumin 17-41 g/L, IgA 0.23-5.08 g/L, AAG 0.89-3.32 g/L) did not correlate significantly with fu;
total protein entered the mixed model (P < 0.05) but did not improve AIC. High IgA (myeloma mixing) lowered fu.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD. Notes that a 50% bound rule of thumb is generally used and that lower fu (as with IgA myeloma) would shorten
time above the MIC for free drug. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Total vancomycin concentration is the main determinant of unbound concentration (linear); no protein added value.

## Caveats
Small sample from 22 critically ill / burn / haematology patients; frozen samples for the model; no pH correction
for UF (pH correction lowered UF 37 C fu by 5-10% in a few samples); no clinical covariates (anonymised); total
protein only up to 68 g/L. The source is internally inconsistent on the validation bias: 5.0% +/- 7.6% in the
abstract (PDF p. 1, printed p. 180) and Results (PDF p. 5, printed p. 184), but 3.6% in the Discussion (PDF p. 7,
printed p. 186); this card keeps the directly reported Results value. Literature fu varies from < 10% to about 100%, largely by method and temperature.
