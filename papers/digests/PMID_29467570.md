# Reimers 2018: Norwegian national reference ranges for antiepileptic drugs

## Study
A consensus/harmonization report, not a clinical study. A working group of the Norwegian Association of
Clinical Pharmacology gathered the reference ranges used by Norwegian laboratories for 23 antiepileptic
drugs (AEDs) and compared them with the ILAE 2008 TDM position paper (Patsalos 2008, ref 5, the main
reference), Patsalos and Berry 2013 (ref 9), a PubMed literature search, ranges used at Filadelfia
(Dianalund, Denmark), Bethel and Kork (Germany), and routine TDM databases of three Norwegian hospitals
(PDF p. 2, printed p. 272). Clinical concentration-effect studies were ranked highest and database-derived
ranges lowest. All ranges apply to **drug-fasting serum samples (12-24 h after the last dose) at steady
state** (p. 272; Table 1 notes). Result: ranges differed between laboratories for 4 AEDs (lamotrigine,
valproate, eslicarbazepine, oxcarbazepine); 4 were newly established (brivaracetam, perampanel, stiripentol,
sulthiame); 13 were harmonized, updated or newly set; 10 unchanged; vigabatrin has no range (abstract).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No concentration-effect model of its own. Ranges are population reference ranges for seizure control in
epilepsy, trough serum. Eslicarbazepine's 3-26 mg/L follows Svendsen 2017's concentration/effect study
(ref 25), said to agree with Falcao 2012's PK/PD modelling (ref 26); the authors also argue that, as
licarbazepine (MHD) is 20% R and 80% S (eslicarbazepine), a range about 20% lower than MHD's would fit
(p. 4). Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. The text names seizure type, syndrome, age, comorbidity and comedication as modifiers (p. 8).

## Caveats
The authors say most ranges are tentative and poorly documented, and few rest on systematic
concentration-effect studies. Several are based on unpublished database data or personal communications.
Ranges are serum, drug-fasting, steady state; a sample drawn at another time is not comparable. Eslicarbazepine
is the S-enantiomer of licarbazepine; the assay (enantioselective or not) is not stated.
