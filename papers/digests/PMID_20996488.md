# Fenn 1946: theoretical alveolar air composition at altitude

## Study
Theoretical (no subjects, no drug). Derives equations for alveolar O2 and CO2 tensions from inspired gas composition, barometric pressure, respiratory quotient (R.Q., written Q) and alveolar ventilation, and presents them on a pCO2-vs-pO2 diagram. Printed pp. 637-653 (PDF pages 1-17). The average alveolar-air line data (pCO2 39, 34.8, 32 mmHg at 30,000, 40,000, 42,000 ft; n = 16, 19, 21) are from the Fig. 1 legend.

## Model and parameters
Key equations (numbering as printed; pressures in mmHg, B = barometric, 47 = water vapour pressure at body temperature):
- Eq 1/8 (pure O2): p'O2 = pO2 + pCO2 (diagonal for each altitude, intercept B - 47).
- Eq 6 (general alveolar air equation) and Eq 7 (CO2-free inspired gas): pO2 = p'O2 + pCO2 f'O2 (1 - Q)/Q - pCO2/Q. Eq 7 is the form the stanpumpR docs call the alveolar gas equation (with R for Q).
- Eq 2/17: alveolar ventilation Va' (L/min BTPS) = 272 / pCO2 when CO2 output is 315 mL/min STPD (0.864 x 315 = 272).
- Eq 13: Va' = 0.864 Xo (Q + f'C(1-Q)) / (pC - p'C), in terms of O2 uptake Xo (STPD mL/min); Eq 15: Va' = 0.864 Xo (1 - f'O(1-Q)) / (p'O - pO); Eq 16 for f'O = 1: Va' = 0.864 Xo Q / (p'O - pO).
- Eq 9: Q is infinite when p'O - pO = f'O pC (reciprocal slope of that diagonal is f'O).
- Eq 19/20: O2 iso-ventilation lines (Xo constant), slope -f'O (0.209 for air). Eq 21/22: CO2 iso-ventilation lines (Yo constant).
Worked numbers: at 20,000 ft breathing air, Xo 315 mL/min, Q 0.8, Va' 5 L/min gives alveolar pCO2 42 mmHg; Va' 10 L/min with Q 1.4 gives pO2 22 mmHg above that point; with air, a 10 mmHg rise in pCO2 lowers pO2 by about 2.1 mmHg (pN2 by 7.9), with pure O2 by 10 mmHg (printed pp. 646-647). Adding CO2 to give inspired pCO2 20 mmHg (6.62% CO2, O2 diluted to 19.5%, B - 47 = 302) lowers the equivalent altitude by 4 to 5 thousand ft (p. 651).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No drug. Physiological landmarks only: arterial saturation under about 65% leads to loss of consciousness (labelled anoxia, p. 637); acapnia threshold about 20 mmHg alveolar pCO2; CO2 narcosis about 50 mmHg; oxygen poisoning at 3 atm in some 2 h; nitrogen narcosis above 5 atm (pp. 651-652). Saturation lines use Henderson's nomogram for one subject's blood. No MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Altitude (barometric pressure), inspired O2 and CO2 fractions, R.Q., O2 uptake or CO2 output, alveolar ventilation (dead-space ventilation added separately: Vt = Va + Vd).

## Caveats
Theoretical; R.Q. is transiently raised during hyperventilation until stores equilibrate (about half an hour, p. 646); the survival-limits diagram (Fig. 10) positions are stated to be uncertain; the 315 mL/min CO2 output is arbitrary.
