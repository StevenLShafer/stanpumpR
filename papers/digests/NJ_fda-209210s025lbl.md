# FDA label 2025: Bydureon BCise (exenatide ER)

## Study
Prescribing information, not a study. Product: exenatide extended-release microspheres (50:50 PLGA, 37.2 mg polymer per dose) in MCT oil, 2 mg per 0.85 mL single-dose autoinjector. Indication: type 2 diabetes in adults and children 10 y and older. Label revised 05/2025; the "recent major change" is 11/2024 (5.11, aspiration).

## Dosage
- 2 mg SC once every 7 days, any time of day, with or without meals (2.1). Missed dose: give if the next is at least 3 days away; if 1-2 days away skip.
- Switching from immediate-release exenatide may give 2-4 weeks of transient glucose elevation.
- Not recommended at eGFR below 45 mL/min/1.73 m2 or in ESRD (8.6, 5.4).

- Elimination: glomerular filtration then proteolytic degradation (nonclinical).
- Renal impairment (BCise PopPK): exposure 28% higher in mild (n=96) and 69% higher in moderate (n=24) than in normal (n=70). Byetta in ESRD on dialysis: 3.4-fold exposure.
- Hepatic impairment, pediatric BCise PK: not studied. Age, sex, race and body weight did not alter PK in the population analysis.
- QT study (12.2): therapeutic 253 pg/mL and supratherapeutic 627 pg/mL IV, no QTc effect (upper bound below 10 ms), n=74.
- Pharmacodynamics: fasting plasma glucose fall of 40.4 mg/dL vs placebo at 12 weeks (microsphere study); mean change in 2 h postprandial glucose -78 mg/dL at Week 16. No ke0, no effect-site delay, no MEAC reported.
- Not an opioid label; audit does not apply.

## Covariates
Renal impairment (above); no effect of age, sex, race or body weight reported (this contrasts with Cirincione's weight term on Vc for immediate-release exenatide; the label gives no comparator beyond "did not alter").

## Caveats
Apparent V and CL are quoted for Byetta (IR) not BCise. Extended-release absorption is rate-limited by microsphere erosion, so apparent CL/F values are not a disposition clearance for the ER product.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- R/drugs_exenatide.R is a Byetta model, not a BCise model, so the label's ER absorption cannot be checked against code. Verified that no BCise model exists.
- Disposition comparison, immediate-release model vs the label's Byetta numbers: label CL/F 9.1 L/h; model CL/F at low concentration 7.8 L/h at eGFR 90 (R/drugs_exenatide.R:97-98 header), 5.1-7.5 L/h total at 210 pg/mL: the model's CL is lower by about 14-45%. Label V/F 28.3 L vs model Vc 7.03 L + Vp 7.04 L at 84.8 kg (volume of two compartments is not comparable with a terminal apparent V/F). Different studies; no verdict beyond the numbers.
- Renal direction: label shows higher exposure with mild and moderate impairment (28%, 69%), ESRD 3.4-fold; model eGFR term on linear clearance goes the same direction (header lines 99-108 note it understates ESRD exposure about 2.7-fold, consistent with the label's 3.4-fold).
- Help text inst/app-help/drugs/exenatide.md:29 and data-raw/antidiabeticRegistry.R:112: "steady state takes 6 to 7 weeks" vs this label's about Week 10 for BCise (peak Week 6-7): **discrepancy or different definition**; see open questions. Release phases (48 h, 2 weeks, 7 weeks) are not stated in the sections read.
- The label's body-weight finding (no effect on PK) is not what the model applies to the IR product (Vc scales as (WT/84.8)^2.67); the label is for the ER product, so no conflict is asserted.
