# Yukawa 1990: a population Michaelis-Menten model for phenytoin in Japanese outpatients

## Study
Retrospective routine therapeutic drug monitoring at the epilepsy outpatient clinic, Kyushu University Hospital.
**334 outpatients** (170 male, 164 female) with two or more reliable steady-state serum phenytoin concentrations
measured at different daily doses, giving **756 concentration-dose pairs** (four pairs in 11 patients, three in 66,
two in 257). Patients whose concurrent therapy changed were excluded. All had normal renal and hepatic function.
An update of the authors' earlier 220-patient analysis (ref 11).

Age 0.6-71.1 y (mean 24.3, SD 14.1); weight 9.0-115.0 kg (mean 49.1, SD 15.5); daily dose mean 225.8 SD 73.1 mg/d;
steady-state concentration mean 9.78 SD 7.77 mcg/mL (Table II, PDF p1, printed 1973). Phenytoin acid as Aleviatin
tablets or powder, two or three times daily; 413 of the 756 observations were on tablets. Concentrations measured
at least 30 d after any dose change, samples drawn about 2-5 h after a dose, immunoassay (EMIT or FPIA), assay CV
under 10%.

**Table I, medications** (PDF p1, confirmed on the rendered page):

"Other drugs" are primidone, clonazepam, sultiame, ethotoin, ethosuximide, acetazolamide and diazepam (Table I
footnote).

## Model and parameters
Michaelis-Menten at steady state, fitted in NONMEM (extended least squares, FACOM M-780):
`R = Vm·Css / (F·(Km + Css))`, R the daily dose in mg/d. Covariates are **indicator variables**, not continuous:
`Vm = Vm·(WGT/60)^θPw · θCO^Vm` and `Km = Km · θAGE · θCO^Km`, where θCO applies if the patient takes **any**
co-anticonvulsant and θAGE if the patient is under 15 years. Three competing forms for the bioavailability of the
powder (tablet F fixed at 1): constant (model 1), exponential decline with dose (model 2), linear decline
(model 3). **Model 2 was selected** - lowest objective function and best precision.

### What the co-medication term does, in both directions (derived)
Co-medication raises Vmax by 8% and Km by 32%. The two pull opposite ways, so the effect changes sign with
concentration. **Two different questions, with opposite signs, so keep them apart.** First, the effect on
*elimination capacity* at a given concentration:

- well below Km, clearance is Vmax/Km, so co-medication **lowers** it: 1.08/1.32 = **0.82**;
- well above Km, elimination approaches Vmax, so co-medication **raises** it: **1.08**.

Second, and this is what the table below shows, the **daily dose needed to hold a fixed steady-state
concentration**, `R = Vmax·C/(Km+C)`. That crosses over at `C = (Vm·Kp − Vp·Km)/(Vp − Vm)` = **7.21 mcg/mL**
(derived). The two run opposite ways by construction: where co-medication lowers clearance it also lowers the dose
needed, and so **raises** the concentration reached on a fixed dose. The crossover concentration is the same for
both, since the dose requirements are equal exactly where the concentrations on a shared dose are.

| Css (mcg/mL) | alone | with co-medication | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 | 165 mg/d | 155 | 0.93 |
| 5 | 219 | 215 | 0.98 |
| 10 | 262 | 266 | 1.02 |
| 15 | 280 | 290 | 1.03 |
| 20 | 290 | 303 | 1.04 |

So inside the adult therapeutic range (10-20 mcg/mL, the paper's own figures) the pooled co-medication effect is a
**1.7% to 4.4% higher dose requirement** - equivalently, a 1.7% to 4.4% *lower* concentration on an unchanged dose
- and below about 7 mcg/mL it reverses. That is the quantitative content of calling this interaction small and
ambiguous.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. No effect measure, no ke0, no tPeak, no exposure-response. The paper cites therapeutic ranges from the
literature only: 5-20 mcg/mL in children, 10-20 mcg/mL in adults (Introduction, refs 1-4), not estimated here.

## Covariates
- **Weight** on Vmax, as a power: 0.737 (model 2), preferred to linear at p<0.005. The authors note the similarity
  to the 0.7 power used to scale maintenance doses and to Grasela 1983.
- **Age** on Km, as a binary split at 15 years: Km 24.8% lower in children.
- **Co-anticonvulsants** on both Vmax and Km, as a **single pooled binary indicator** - phenytoin alone against
  phenytoin with any other anticonvulsant. There is no per-drug term: phenobarbital, carbamazepine, valproate,
  primidone, clonazepam, sultiame, ethotoin, ethosuximide, acetazolamide and diazepam all enter the same 0/1
  covariate.
- **Dosage form** on bioavailability: tablet F = 1, powder F = 1 − exp(−9.92/D). The authors note the powder is
  effectively complete below about 2 mg/kg/d (Fig. 2) and attribute the shortfall to the powder's poor
  wettability.

## Caveats
- **The co-medication term cannot be attributed to any single drug.** Only 29 of 334 patients took phenytoin with
  carbamazepine and nothing else, and 45 with phenobarbital alone; 64 (19%) were in a group that explicitly
  includes seven further drugs.
- Vmax and Km move the same way, so the effect on concentration is concentration-dependent and changes sign
  (above).
- Routine clinical data: steady state assumed from a 30-day interval, samples at a single 2-5 h window, no
  compliance check, immunoassay rather than a specific method.
- One dose-form effect (the powder) is particular to the Japanese formulation and does not transfer.
- The authors report both Vmax and Km lower than Caucasian populations' and conclude their patients would reach
  toxic concentrations at lower doses - a between-study comparison across assays and designs, not a measurement.
- Total phenytoin only; no unbound concentrations, which is where valproate's displacement would show.
