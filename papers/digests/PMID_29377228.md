# Zhao 2018: omeprazole in neonates, CYP2C19 and ABCB1

## Study
Population PK-pharmacogenetic analysis of omeprazole and two metabolites in 51 Caucasian neonates and young infants with gastro-oesophageal reflux (part of a dose-finding trial, NCT01657578; 54 enrolled, 51 with PK and DNA). Gestational age 31.3 weeks (SD 3.9, range 24-41), weight 2108 g (SD 609, range 780-3800); postnatal age (PNA) and sex in Table 1 (24 male, 27 female per the text layer). Omeprazole given once daily in the morning, doses initially 1-3 mg/kg/day by a Bayesian dose-finding design; Table 1 dose 41 mg/d mean (7-87), 3.5 mg/kg/d mean (0.8-7.3). Samples 0.5-4 and 4-12 h after the first dose. 73 plasma omeprazole concentrations in the model data (median 217 ng/mL, range below LOQ (n 6) to 2658); 5-hydroxy-omeprazole median 111 (below LOQ n 7 to 842); sulfone median 92 (below LOQ n 22 to 764); values below LOQ set to half the LOQ. HPLC-UV, plasma (not whole blood); calibration 10-1000 ng/mL omeprazole, 25-1000 ng/mL metabolites; LOQ 10 and 25 ng/mL. Total (not free) concentration; omeprazole is over 95% protein bound (intro). Genotyping: CYP2C19*2 and *17; ABCB1 C3435T, G2677T/A, C1236T. NONMEM 7.2, FOCE-I, exponential IIV, additive residual error; covariates by forward (P<0.05) and backward selection; bootstrap 500, NPDE, external validation on 8 infants (12 samples; median prediction error 4.6%, 2.0%, -2.6% for parent and the two metabolites).

## Model and parameters
One-compartment parent with first-order absorption, plus two one-compartment metabolites (5-hydroxy-omeprazole, M1; omeprazole sulfone, M2), first-order elimination (Figure 1, PDF p.5). Metabolite volumes fixed to 1 L because the metabolites were not given alone, so CL_OMZ-M values are clearance divided by metabolite volume (ratios, not absolute). Clearance of unchanged omeprazole estimated below 0.0001 L/h (negligible). Table 2 (PDF p.7, printed p.1003), all apparent (F not separable), final estimate (RSE %):

Derived summaries in the Discussion (PDF p.5): median weight-normalized CL_OMZ-M1 0.287 L/h/kg (range 0.036-1.711), CL_OMZ-M2 0.142 L/h/kg (0.065-0.316), apparent V1 0.24 L/kg (0.09-0.77), stated as similar to adults.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamics, no ke0, no concentration-effect relationship, no therapeutic range (clinical outcomes were not assessed, Discussion). Not an opioid; opioid audit not applicable.

## Covariates
- Postnatal age (days) on CL_OMZ-M1 only: (PNA/38)^0.472; OFV drop 6.32 (P<0.05) after genotype was in. No age effect on the sulfone (CYP3A4) route.
- CYP2C19 genotype on CL_OMZ-M1: IM 0.449 and PM 0.125 of EM/UM (OFV drop 18.25, P<0.01). The comparator is the EM/UM infant of the same postnatal age in this model. Abstract gives 5-95th percentiles 3-14.9% (PM) and 29.9-72.6% (IM).
- ABCB1 C3435T on Ka only (OFV drop 6.98, P<0.01); amount absorbed not affected.
- No weight covariate in the final model (weight enters only through the per-kg summaries).

## Caveats
Only 3 CYP2C19 poor metabolizers (*2/*2; Table 1 text layer, PDF p.4), so the PM factor is imprecise (RSE 44.5%, bootstrap 0.030-0.149). 73 parent samples from 51 infants (sparse); residual error large (additive 55.6 ng/mL, RSE 50%). Large unexplained IIV (Ka 130%, V1/F 99%). Metabolite volumes fixed at 1 L, so M1/M2 parameters are ratios. Caucasian infants only; CYP3A4 effect not separable. Apparent parameters with route and formulation not stated in the text (see open questions). The Ka value of 0.0497 /h is slow for omeprazole in adults; the paper does not comment.
