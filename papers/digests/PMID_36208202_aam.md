# Gargis 2023: reference agar-dilution MICs and WGS of US C. difficile isolates, 2012-17

## Study
CDC Emerging Infections Program (EIP) surveillance, 10 US states. Of 6964 C. difficile isolates submitted
2012-2017, 100 per year were randomly sampled from that year's 10 most common ribotypes; 7 failed WGS quality
metrics, leaving **593 isolates** of 14 ribotypes (RT027 23.1%, RT106 16.9%, RT002 13.3%, RT020 11.3%, RT014
11.0%; Table 1, PDF p13). MICs by CLSI reference **agar dilution** (CLSI M11) for ceftriaxone, clindamycin,
meropenem, metronidazole, moxifloxacin and vancomycin; QC strains B. fragilis ATCC 25285 and C. difficile ATCC
700057 (PDF p3). Whole-genome sequencing (Illumina MiSeq) for resistance genes and vanR/vanS, gyrA/gyrB mutations
and the pCD-METRO plasmid (PDF p3-4). Kuiper's test compared MIC distributions by period and ribotype. No MBC
or killing data: this is an MIC-only paper. No patient or pharmacokinetic data.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is growth inhibition on agar (MIC) only; no MBC, time-kill or clinical outcome. Breakpoints quoted
(PDF p6): CLSI metronidazole (all anaerobes) S ≤8, I 16, R ≥32 µg/mL; CLSI vancomycin epidemiological cutoff
≤2 wild type, ≥4 non-wild type (no clinical breakpoint); EUCAST C. difficile metronidazole and vancomycin S ≤2,
R >2. The authors cite fecal vancomycin concentrations of 100 to 1000 times the MIC90 (their ref 34) and an
association of metronidazole MIC ≥1 µg/mL with treatment failure (ref 33). Not an opioid paper: no MEAC, ke0
or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Ribotype: RT027 had higher MICs to vancomycin, metronidazole, clindamycin and moxifloxacin than non-RT027
(P < .01 each). VanR T115A mutation (65 of 137 RT027) shifted vancomycin MICs upward (MIC50 2, MIC90 4 vs 0.5
and 2 without it). No vanA/B; pCD-METRO absent.

## Caveats
Convenience sample restricted to the 10 most common ribotypes each year; 10 EIP sites may not represent the US;
single (not repeated) AST; fidaxomicin not tested (authors, PDF p7-8). Accepted manuscript: Table 2's footnote
letters are misassigned in places (the clindamycin "7" carries footnote a, the moxifloxacin "136" carries b,
while footnotes b and c describe them); the numbers themselves are consistent with the text. Recheck against the
published version when it arrives.
