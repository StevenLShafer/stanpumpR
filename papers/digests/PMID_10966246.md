# Greenblatt 2000: lorazepam kinetics and EEG dynamics during a bolus plus 4-h infusion

## Study
9 healthy male volunteers, 22-45 y; Table 1 mean (SEM): age 31.6 (2.2) y, weight 75.6 (1.9) kg, height
179 (1.3) cm, total dose 2.61 (0.02) mg. Randomised two-way non-blind crossover with a no-treatment
EEG trial. Lorazepam 2 mg IV bolus at the start of a 2 ug/kg/h infusion for 4 h. Venous samples at 24
times over 24 h; GC with electron capture (sensitivity 1 ng/mL). EEG: 8 leads, FFT, relative beta
amplitude (12-30 Hz in Methods; 13-30 Hz in the abstract) as % change over baseline.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: EEG beta amplitude. Counter-clockwise hysteresis in 7 of 9; model fitted in 7 (one too noisy,
one with no delay). Table 1, mean (SEM) of individuals:

| Parameter | Individual mean | Aggregate fit |
|---|---|---|
| Emax (% over baseline) | 12.7 (1.7) | 13.7 (as reassembled) |
| Hill A | 6.0 (1.4) | 3.7 |
| EC50 (ng/mL) | 13.1 (1.5) | 12.7 |
| kEO (1/min) | 0.101 (0.03) | 0.114 |
| t1/2kEO (min) | **8.8 (1.8)** | 6.1 |

(The aggregate EC50/Emax pairing is my reading of the scrambled column; t1/2kEO 6.1 min is consistent
with kEO 0.114 /min, derived ln 2/0.114 = 6.08.) The mean of individual t1/2kEO, 8.8 min, is not
ln 2 / mean kEO (derived: 6.9 min), as expected for a mean of ratios. EEG maximal 0.5 h after the
loading dose while plasma peaked immediately; effect roughly constant during infusion and declined in
parallel with plasma after; no statistically significant tolerance. Authors note effect delays depend
on the measure and that ICU patients need much higher doses.

## Covariates
None (9 healthy young men).

## Caveats
n = 9, all young healthy men; low doses (about 20 ng/mL); venous sampling; individual vs aggregate
t1/2kEO differ (8.8 vs 6.1 min); Table 1 hard to read in the extracted text.
