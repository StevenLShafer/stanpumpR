# Lischke 1994: dose-dependent QTc prolongation after IV droperidol

## Study
Prospective, randomized, double-blind; 40 surgical patients for elective extracranial head/neck surgery,
QTc < 440 ms at screening. Droperidol IV over 20-30 s at induction: 0.1 mg/kg (n = 10), 0.175 mg/kg
(n = 10), 0.25 mg/kg (n = 20). Diazepam 10 mg IM premedication. 12-lead ECG, QT measured automatically and
checked manually, Bazett correction; recordings before and 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7.5, 10 min after injection.
No plasma concentrations measured. Medians, quartiles, ranges; Friedman, Kruskal-Wallis.

## Model and parameters
No PK. Median QTc prolongation vs control (Abstract): 37 ms (8.0%) at 0.1 mg/kg, 44 ms (10.6%) at
0.175 mg/kg, 59 ms (14.9%) at 0.25 mg/kg. Prolongation appeared within 60 s and did not increase further
over 10 min. Maximum prolongation rose significantly with dose (Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.003, Fig. 2). Heart
rate rose in all groups; MAP fell modestly (significant in groups 1 and 3) (Tables 1-2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point: Bazett QTc (ECG) over the first 10 min after bolus. Dose-group response only; no concentration,
no EC50, no ke0, no slope. No arrhythmias. (Not an opioid paper.)

## Covariates
None analysed; groups comparable for age, size, weight, Na, K, Ca.

## Caveats
High doses (7-17.5 mg in a 70 kg adult), 10 min observation, during anaesthetic induction, Bazett
correction with rising heart rate (overcorrects), no placebo arm (pilot saline data unpublished).
