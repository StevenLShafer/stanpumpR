# Carlsson Petri 2015: Liraglutide PK in pediatric vs adult type 2 diabetes

## Study
Population PK comparing one pediatric trial with two adult trials, all type 2 diabetes (T2D),
subcutaneous once-daily liraglutide (Victoza). Data sources (Table 1, PDF p. 4 / printed 666):

- Trial 1 (NCT00943501), pediatric T2D, n = 13, 8 (62%) female, age 10-17 y, weight 106 (57-214) kg;
  liraglutide escalated 0.3 -> 0.6 -> (0.9) -> 1.2 -> 1.8 mg/day over 5 weeks; sampled at each dose level;
  4 PK profiles/subject, ~25 samples/subject.
- Trial 2 (NCT00993304), adult T2D, n = 12, 6 (50%) female, age 54-73 y, weight 83 (72-104) kg;
  1.8 mg; serial 0-60 h; 12 samples.
- Trial 3 (NCT00873223), adult T2D, n = 32, 9 (28%) female, age 33-68 y, weight 96 (58-140) kg;
  1.8 mg; serial 0-24 h; 13 samples.

Plasma liraglutide by validated ELISA. NONMEM 7.1.2; S-plus 8.0 and R for processing. Concentrations in
nM (nmol/L).

## Model and parameters
A **one-compartment model with first-order absorption and first-order elimination** (ka, CL/F, Vd/F),
previously developed for adults (Ingwersen 2012, ref 8), was applied to the pooled three-trial dataset
with BSV on CL/F and Vd/F and a proportional residual error (Sect. 2.2). The paper does **not** tabulate
the ka/CL/F/Vd/F theta values (structural estimates "data not shown"); it reports individual/empirical
CL/F and derived exposure.

AUC24 derived as AUC24 = dose(nmol)/(CL/F). Median [range] CL/F at the 1.8 mg dose (text, PDF p. 5 /
printed 667, confirmed on rendered page): Trial 1 (pediatric) **1.55 [0.67-3.83] L/h**;
Trial 2 (adult) **0.91 [0.59-2.35] L/h**; Trial 3 (adult) **1.48 [1.04-3.72] L/h**. Dose proportionality
over 0.3-1.8 mg: model-derived AUC24 slope **1.05 (95% CI 0.96-1.15)** (Fig. 2).

Covariate forest plot (Fig. 3, geometric-mean AUC24 ratios vs a 90 kg adult female reference, 90% CI):
53 kg/90 kg **1.64 [1.45; 1.84]**; 216 kg/90 kg **0.44 [0.37; 0.54]**; Pediatric/Adult **0.90 [0.78; 1.03]**;
Male/Female **0.69 [0.61; 0.79]**. Covariates on exposure were power (weight) / categorical (gender,
age group); including them reduced BSV in CL/F by 50%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid; no analgesic concentration, MEAC, ke0, tPeak or potency ratio. PD not modelled here;
the end point is systemic exposure (AUC24, concentration-time profiles). Exploratory short-term glycemic
improvement is mentioned but not quantified for PK.

## Covariates
Body weight (power model) and gender (female) relevant for exposure, as in adults; age group
(pediatric/adult) not pharmacokinetically relevant (minor 10% AUC24 decrease vs same-weight/gender
adult, 90% CI 0.78-1.03). Reference subject: 90 kg adult female.

## Caveats
Small pediatric n (13), sparse sampling, protocol dose deviations (one subject 1.5 mg; two escalated to
0.9 mg), wide weight range (57-214 kg) with a 214 kg outlier; descriptive model limits extrapolation to
children of similar age (10-17 y) and weight; age analysed categorically because of an age gap between
oldest pediatric (17 y) and youngest adult (33 y). Structural PK thetas not reported (reused from
Ingwersen 2012).

## Verification against stanpumpR
No repo location cites this paper, so the formal verdict is **not-applicable**. Independent comparison
with the two implemented liraglutide models (both linear per-kg CL/F):

- `R/drugs_liraglutide.R` (Roge 2015): CL/F 1.83e-4 L/min/kg = 0.011 L/h/kg (header lines 24, 72).
  Predicted absolute CL/F at the trials' median weights: ~0.91 L/h at 83 kg, ~1.05 L/h at 96 kg,
  ~1.16 L/h at 106 kg (derived). Carlsson Petri medians: 0.91 (Trial 2, 83 kg), 1.48 (Trial 3, 96 kg),
  1.55 (Trial 1, 106 kg). Roge matches the lower-weight adult trial almost exactly and sits inside each
  trial's CL/F range; the heavier trials run above a strictly linear-per-kg prediction, consistent with
  this paper's finding that exposure falls **less** than proportionally with weight (AUC 216/90 kg = 0.44,
  53/90 kg = 1.64, both shallower than linear). **Broadly consistent**; the library's exponent-1 weight
  scaling is a modelling choice this paper's sub-linear weight effect would refine but does not
  contradict at these weights.
- `R/drugs_liraglutideWatson.R` (Watson 2010): CL/F 0.013 L/h/kg (line 78) -> ~1.08/1.25/1.38 L/h at
  83/96/106 kg (derived), i.e. slightly higher than Roge and still within the trials' CL/F ranges.
  **Broadly consistent.**
- Age/pediatrics: the library has no age or pediatric covariate for liraglutide; this paper supports
  applying the adult model to adolescents (pediatric/adult AUC24 ratio 0.90, 90% CI 0.78-1.03). **Consistent.**
- Molar basis: the paper derives AUC24 from dose in nmol; the library headers use MW 3751.2 g/mol
  (1 nmol/L = 3.751 ng/mL). **Consistent.**

Net: an uncited but concordant external check of both liraglutide models; no discrepancy found.
