# Hall 1982: bleomycin pharmacokinetics by radioimmunoassay

## Study
19 cancer patients: 11 IV (Table 1, ages 31-65 y, doses 7-30 U, serum creatinine 0.7-1.3 mg/dL) and 8 SC (Table 2, ages 16-77 y, doses 4-30 U, creatinine 0.7-1.8 mg/dL). IV dose given over 5 min (the dilution volume reads 50 mL on printed p22 and 2 mL on p23, so it is ambiguous); SC dose in 2 mL. Blood and urine for 24 h; plasma heparinised and frozen. Assay: radioimmunoassay (Broughton and Strong 1976). Fit: open two-compartment model by least-squares nonlinear regression (software not named); IV vs SC compared with t-tests. Doses in units (activity, not mass), concentrations in microunits/mL. Most patients were on other chemotherapy.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No pharmacodynamics; not an opioid, so no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio. Peak plasma about 900 microU/mL after 30 U SC vs about 1500 after 15 U IV (about one-third at a comparable dose). Authors suggest SC 4-6 U every 6 h could hold about 100 microU/mL; continuous infusion 30 U/day gave 146 microU/mL steady state (their ref 6).

## Covariates
Clearance fell as serum creatinine rose (SC patients, Fig 3; no equation; see open question). Urinary excretion 60-70% of dose in 24 h; the one patient with renal dysfunction excreted far less. AUC proportional to dose.

## Caveats
Small n, mixed tumours and concurrent chemotherapy; one patient per abnormal-renal subgroup; SC half-life is absorption-affected (flip-flop); one patient on repeated SC doses showed some accumulation. The authors cite other work with t1/2 about 115 min and V about 20 L (Crooke) and about 4 h (Alberts).
