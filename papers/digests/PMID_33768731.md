# Zhou 2021: remimazolam-fentanyl Markov PD model for MOAA/S in procedural sedation

## Study
- Pooled analysis of 10 phase I-III trials (5 phase I healthy-volunteer, 2 phase II and 3 phase III procedural-sedation; list in Table S1, not in PDF). 1071 subjects, 35,356 MOAA/S observations (Results, PDF p7). 53.2% men; 17.5% African American, 7.7% Asian; ASA 1/2/3/4 roughly one-third+/46.4/16.3/1.6%; mean age 53.8 y, 224 subjects >=65 y.
- Dosing: remimazolam bolus 0.01-0.5 mg/kg in three phase I studies; procedural sedation 5-8 mg over 1 min with 1-3 mg top-ups at >=3 min; one 1 mg/kg/h infusion up to 1 h; one 5 mg/min x5 min, 3 mg/min x15 min, 1 mg/min x15 min. Initial doses 0.662-38.6 mg, total 0.662-85.0 mg; fentanyl total 25-225 ug. Fentanyl 50 ug (lower in elderly) after DSMB review.
- End point: MOAA/S (5 alert ... 0 unresponsive), recoded to RCMOAA 4 (MOAA/S 5), 3 (4), 2 (3 or 2), 1 (1 or 0), plus 6 for rescue.
- Exposure: remimazolam Cp from empirical Bayes estimates of a prior PopPK model (Zhou 2021, ref 19) in 319 subjects with Cp data; others simulated from population PK. Fentanyl Cp not measured: population-predicted Cp from Scott and Stanski 1987 3-compartment parameters (CL 0.574 L/min, V1 12.7 L, V2 54.2 L, V3 272 L, Q2 4.93 L/min, Q3 2.53 L/min; PDF p3).
- Software: NONMEM 7.3 (Laplacian), R 3.0.2/Xpose, PsN 4.4.0; bootstrap 169 of 250 runs succeeded.

## Model and parameters
Discrete-time Markov model of 4 sedation states plus rescue, 20 transitions; logit of each transition = baseline (G) + Emax drug effect + eta. Effect-site compartments for remimazolam and fentanyl; fentanyl acts synergistically by lowering remimazolam EC50. One Emax for all downward transitions, one for all upward. ## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Not an opioid-as-subject paper, but fentanyl is a co-drug, so audit values:
  - Fentanyl keo 0.569 /min (Table 1, PDF p6), end point MOAA/S sedation (not analgesia, not EEG), effect modelled on remimazolam EC50 (synergy), driven by population-predicted (not measured) fentanyl Cp.
  - Fentanyl EC50 2.30 ng/mL (same end point; population-predicted Cp, so neither total/free nor blood/plasma is defined by an assay).
  - No MEAC / minimum analgesic concentration, no tPeak, no t1/2 keo for fentanyl, no equianalgesic or potency ratio stated. Fentanyl covariate effects were not evaluated (p3).
- Remimazolam keo 0.619 /min on MOAA/S; the Discussion gives t1/2 keo 1.1 min, shorter than 2.7 and 2.8 min in two healthy-volunteer studies and much shorter than 14 min in a Chinese study (refs 15, 16, 24); authors suggest denser sampling, population or fentanyl as reasons (p10). (derived: ln2/0.619 = 1.12 min.)
- Remimazolam EC50 0.258 ug/mL; recommended regimen remimazolam 5 mg plus fentanyl 50 ug, remimazolam over 1 min given right after 2 min fentanyl.
- Simulations: 5 mg with 50 ug fentanyl better than 4 or 6 mg; 50 ug better than 25 or 75 ug; 4 mg + 75 ug a minor, below-chance advantage (Figures 3-5, not read as images).

## Covariates
BMI >25 kg/m2 raises keo x1.29; procedure type (colonoscopy x1.78, bronchoscopy x1.25 vs healthy volunteers) on keo; age on upward (recovery) transition logits (slope term above). No sex, race, ASA effect retained. Elderly: 4-6% more deep/moderate sedation and 2-3 min longer sedation in simulation.

## Caveats
Model overpredicts deep sedation (<10% in colonoscopy, more in bronchoscopy); only first-dose colonoscopy data used for simulation. Fentanyl Cp is population-predicted, no individual PK. High shrinkage on keo. Bootstrap incomplete (169/250). Discrete Markov assumes equal observation intervals. G13 imprecise.
