# Patel 2013: population PK of enoxaparin during the antenatal period

Page convention: printed pp. 1462-1469 = PDF pp. 1-8 (PDF page = printed page − 1461). The online Data Supplement
(base-model table, BLQ handling) is not in the archived PDF.

## Study
Single centre, King's College Hospital thrombophilia clinic, London; recruitment September 2009 - December 2011.
Pregnant women ≥18 y prescribed enoxaparin for any antenatal indication; excluded: CrCl <60 mL/min, suspected
non-adherence (printed p. 1463 / PDF p. 2). 158 referred, 123 contributed data. Monthly visits with up to 3
anti-Xa samples (trough, 1 h and 3 h after the dose) by D-optimal design; injection times from diaries. 795 anti-Xa
activities, 712 antenatal (the rest postpartum). Assay STA-Rotachrom heparin, LLOQ 0.01 IU/mL, QC CV 3.7-4.6%.
Table 3 (printed p. 1464 / PDF p. 3): White 54%, African Caribbean 30%, Asian 7%; BMI normal 50%, overweight 26%,
obese class I-III 22%; prophylaxis 68%, VTE treatment 7%, APS 7%, warfarin conversion 8%; most common dose 40 mg
daily (66%), range 20 mg daily to 100 mg twice daily. Weight, age and gestational age distributions are not
tabulated. There is no nonpregnant control group; the postpartum samples are the only non-antenatal data.

NONMEM 7.2.1; covariate entry ΔOFV ≥ 6.64 (P < 0.01), backward elimination ΔOFV > 10.82 (P < 0.001); 1000-sample
nonparametric bootstrap; VPC (Figure 3).

## Model and parameters
Base: one-compartment, first-order absorption, IIV on CL and Vd, combined error (2-compartment tested and not
chosen). Table 4 base model (printed p. 1465 / PDF p. 4, rendered): CL 1.66 L/h (1.48-1.84), Vd 11.0 L
(8.53-13.97), Ka 0.505/h, ωCL 46%, ωVd 35%.

Final model (text printed p. 1465, Table 7 printed p. 1466 / PDF p. 5, rendered):
CL = POPCL × GESTCL × (Wt/80)^1.06; Vd = POPVd × GESTV × (LBW/42)^1.25, Wt actual weight, LBW prepregnancy lean
body weight (the "mechanistically derived" equation of ref 41, Han 2007), GEST the gestational week, MGEST the week of delivery.
During pregnancy GESTCL = 1 and GESTV = (GEST/MGEST)^0.357; after delivery GESTCL = (GEST/MGEST)^−4.00 and
GESTV = (GEST/MGEST)^−5.49.

Creatinine clearance entered the full model (univariate CL × (CrCl/110)^0.733, Table 5) but was removed in backward
elimination (ΔOFV 4.035, Table 6). Derived (my calculation, typical 80 kg, LBW 42 kg): t½ = 0.693 × Vd/CL = 5.7 h
at term (GEST = MGEST) and about 3.5 h at a quarter of gestation (Vd 8.0 L). Units: dose mg, activity IU/mL; the
paper does not state the mg-to-IU conversion used for the volumes (Figure 3 is "dose normalised").

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. The authors simulated 3-hour and trough anti-Xa for once-daily (King's weight bands, Table 1) and
twice-daily (RCOG, Table 2) regimens: both reach the RCOG 3-h target 0.5-1.2 IU/mL throughout pregnancy; trough
rises as pregnancy advances because Vd rises (Figure 4, printed pp. 1466-1467). They recommend once-daily dosing
and trough rather than peak monitoring. They cite Lebaudy 2008 as reporting CL +48% and Vd +49% in pregnancy,
and Casele 1999 MRT 287 → 325 min (printed p. 1467 / PDF p. 6).

## Covariates
Actual weight on CL; prepregnancy LBW and gestation fraction on Vd; gestation on CL only postpartum (a fall after
delivery). Creatinine and CrCl dropped. The abstract and discussion say clearance and volume both increase in
pregnancy; in the final model the only within-pregnancy rise in CL is through actual weight, and the
pregnant-versus-nonpregnant contrast comes from the postpartum term. No nonpregnant reference is given.

## Caveats
No nonpregnant control; postpartum data few (83 samples, derived: 795 - 712). Simulations assume constant dose, full adherence and
injection at the same time daily. Exclusion of CrCl <60. Single-centre, ethnically mixed population.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- `R/drugs_enoxaparin.R:72-73` "found clearance and volume both higher in pregnancy": this is the authors' stated
  conclusion (abstract, printed pp. 1462, 1466-1467). The model gives CL no gestation term during pregnancy
  (GESTCL = 1) and has no nonpregnant group, so the clearance part rests on weight and on the postpartum fall.
  **partly matches** (statement matches, model structure only indirectly supports it). The library's factors come
  from Lebaudy, not this paper.
- `pregnancyRegistry.csv:144`: "clearance (GEST/MGEST)^0.357 and volume rising with gestational age": 0.357 is the
  exponent on Vd during pregnancy (FACGESTV); CL has no gestational exponent during pregnancy (Table 7, text
  printed p. 1465). **discrepancy.**
- `pregnancyRegistry.csv:144`: "enoxaparin CL 1.66 L/h antenatal": 1.66 L/h is the base model without covariates
  (Table 4); the final model gives 1.60 L/h at 80 kg (Table 7). **discrepancy** (minor).
- `pregnancyRegistry.csv:144`: "123 antenatal women, 795 anti-Xa values ... half-life prolonged": **matches**.
- Comparator noted: Patel's account of Lebaudy (CL +48%, Vd +49%) agrees with the library's CL factor 1.5; the
  library's central-volume factor 1.4 is from Lebaudy's late-pregnancy 41%, which this paper does not quote.
