# Colin 2018: PK/PD perspective on neural inertia (letter)

## Study
A letter (not a study) commenting on Warnaby 2017 (Anesthesiology 127:645-57), which reported a difference in modelled slow-wave-activity (SWA) C50 between induction and emergence from propofol as evidence of neural inertia in humans. Colin et al. simulated Warnaby's propofol schedule (target effect-site changed every 2 min on induction; infusion simply stopped for emergence) using the Marsh model (ref 7) with the Diprifusor ke0 of 0.260 min-1. A hypothetical Emax-type effect, Effect = Ce^gamma / (Ce^gamma + C50^gamma), with gamma 2 and C50 1.5 ug/mL was generated, then gamma and C50 were refitted separately to induction and emergence while varying the ke0 used to generate the effect. The same was done for the design of their own volunteer study (Kuizenga 2018, Br J Anaesth 120:525-36).
Two further items on the same pages: a letter by Schwartz (EEG burst suppression and neuromuscular blockers, dog isoflurane data) and the start of Warnaby et al.'s reply.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Simulation only; end point is a hypothetical effect (the paper's real one is EEG SWA). Reported results (text, Fig 1 not viewed): if the ke0 used to generate the data differs from the 0.260 min-1 assumed, the induction C50 estimate rises and the emergence C50 falls; with ke0 0.160 min-1 a 30% induction-emergence C50 difference arises in Warnaby's design, none at 0.260; in Colin's own design the difference was 4%. The authors conclude the apparent neural inertia can be an artefact of using the wrong ke0 and trial design. Whether ke0 is expressed as t1/2ke0 or tPeak is not given; 0.260 min-1 is ke0 itself. No MEAC or potency ratios (not opioid).

None.

## Caveats
Simulation using assumed gamma and C50; letter format; the reply is truncated in this PDF; no real data. Marsh kinetics were developed in children (ref 7 title), applied here as Diprifusor does.
