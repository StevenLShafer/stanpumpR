# Chauvin 1987: labetalol loading dose plus infusion after aortobifemoral bypass

## Study
Open, uncontrolled study in 6 patients (mean age 57 y, range 30-73; mean weight 64 kg, SD 9; sex not reported)
15 +/- 2 h after aortobifemoral bypass, rewarmed, extubated and sedated, with MAP > 120 mmHg and cardiac index
> 2 L/min/m2 (PDF p1-2). Four had pre-existing hypertension (two on beta-blockers). Normal urea, creatinine and liver
tests. Labetalol 1.5 mg/kg IV over 5 min into a peripheral vein, then 0.2 mg/kg/h started 30 min later and run for
5.5 h (to 6 h; Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 captions, PDF p3-4). Salt or base basis of the dose not stated. The infusion rate was
chosen to give 170 ng/mL from an assumed clearance of 20 mL/kg/min (McNeil and Louis 1984; PDF p2). Radial-artery
samples before dosing, at the end of the loading dose and at 5, 15, 30, 60 min and 2-6 h hourly; fluorimetric assay
(Martin 1976). Clearance = infusion rate / measured plateau, per patient (PDF p2).

(derived: 0.2 mg/kg/h = 3.33 ug/kg/min; 3.33 / 0.264 = 12.6 mL/kg/min from the mean plateau, consistent with the
13.1 mean of individual ratios; 3.33 / 20 = 167 ng/mL, the stated 170 target.) Within-patient CVs of concentration
2-6 h did not exceed 15% (PDF p4). Fig. 2 shows a mean of roughly 5,000 ng/mL at the end of the 5-min loading dose
(read from the figure, arterial sample), falling to the plateau by 2 h. No half-life or volume is estimated.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Within 5 min of the loading dose: MAP -32 +/- 11%, HR -20 +/- 11%, cardiac index -26 +/- 15% (P < 0.05), sustained
  through the infusion; SVR not uniform, never increased, -22 +/- 16% at 3 h (Table III, PDF p4); LVSWI -40% (PDF p4).
- Peak effect within 5 min of the loading dose (Discussion, PDF p5).
- No correlation between plasma concentration and HR or MAP (PDF p4, p6). No effect-site model, no ke0.
- A concentration of 150-220 ng/mL is quoted from Prys-Roberts 1984 as needed for adequate beta-blockade (PDF p5),
  a citation, not a measurement here.

## Covariates
None analysed (n = 6).

## Caveats
- Six patients, single dose regimen, no comparator; postoperative state (sedation, extradural morphine).
- The authors recommend basing the infusion on a clearance of 13 mL/kg/min (0.13 mg/kg/h for 170 ng/mL; PDF p5-6).
  Their advised starting range is printed as "0.07 and 0.15 mg kg-1 min-1" on PDF p6 (rendered), which conflicts with
  the per-hour rates of the rest of the paper; per hour is presumably meant (my inference).
- Fluorimetric assay, which is less specific than HPLC (my caveat; the paper does not discuss it).
