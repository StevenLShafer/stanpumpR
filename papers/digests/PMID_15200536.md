# McMurray 2004: Diprifusor TCI propofol sedation in adult ICU patients

## Study
Multicentre (6 centres, UK and France), non-comparative, open study. 122 ventilated adult ICU patients: 57 post-cardiac surgery, 18 brain-injured, 47 general ICU (PDF p. 3). Propofol 1% by Diprifusor TCI (Marsh model), target adjusted by the clinician to a desired sedation depth (modified Ramsay score; nurse judgement in paralysed patients). Analgesia: morphine about 1-2 mg/h, or equianalgesic alfentanil or fentanyl. Blood propofol was assayed by HPLC with fluorescence detection (ref 9, Plummer 1987) in a subgroup of 18 patients (8 post-cardiac, 10 general ICU) at scheduled times; predictive performance by Varvel indices (MDPE, MDAPE, divergence); clearance computed from steady-state infusion rate and late measured concentration. Mean age 57.8 (SD 15.5) y, mean weight 66.4 kg overall (Table 1; the text-layer table is scrambled, so only the "all patients" values are taken).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: modified Ramsay sedation score (3 agitated to -3 unrousable; A natural sleep, P paralysed) plus light/desired/excessive rating. No EEG/BIS.
- Time-weighted average target (blood, not effect-site) at the desired sedation level (Fig. 1, PDF p. 3): median 1.34 ug/mL post-cardiac (10th-90th percentile 0.79-1.93), 0.98 brain injury (0.60-2.55), 0.42 general ICU (0.16-1.19). All patients 10th-90th percentile 0.26-1.87 ug/mL (text, PDF p. 3). Authors propose settings of 0.2-2.0 ug/mL with titration.
- Mean propofol rate: 1.8 mg/kg/h overall (range 0.2-4.8); 2.4 post-cardiac, 2.1 brain injury, 1.0 general ICU.
- Time at desired depth: 84.4% overall (Table 2). Time to desired level: mean 9.9 min (SD 21.0). Median times after stopping propofol in patients still asleep: eye opening 8 min, spontaneous ventilation 2 min, extubation 7 min.
- No ke0, t1/2ke0, tPeak, EC50 or MEAC reported. Opioid audit: not an opioid paper; opioids were co-administered (morphine 1-2 mg/h, or equianalgesic alfentanil/fentanyl) with no concentrations or potency ratios given.

## Covariates
None modelled. Subgroup (post-cardiac vs brain injury vs general ICU) differences in target setting and bias are descriptive.

## Caveats
Only 18 patients had blood assays. Desired sedation level was investigator dependent and varied across centres. Sponsor-funded (AstraZeneca). Blood (not plasma) propofol is the assayed quantity, compared against the Marsh model's predicted concentration. Authors attribute the smaller bias in general ICU to higher clearance in that subgroup (31.2 vs 25.4 mL/min/kg).
