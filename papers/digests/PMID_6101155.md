# McNiff 1981: IV nitroglycerin pharmacokinetics in healthy volunteers

## Study
- Eight healthy, nonsmoking adult men (subjects 3-10; subject 5 studied twice, 4 months apart, so 9
  infusions). Weight and age not reported (weights implied by Table II's footnote, derived 58-96 kg).
- Infusion: nitroglycerin about 22 mcg/mL in normal saline from a glass bottle, drawn into a 50-mL glass syringe,
  through short pre-saturated tubing (50.8 cm extension), 0.764 mL/min into a forearm vein for 32 min: about 18
  mcg/min, about 0.6 mg in total. Delivered rate computed from the assayed concentration of pre- and post-infusion
  effluent samples (Table I: no difference, p > 0.35; bench runs within 2.8-4.1% of the calculated concentration).
- Sampling: **peripheral venous** blood from the other arm (Table IV: antecubital vein) at 0, 10, 20, 32, 34, 36,
  38, 42, 47, 52, 62 and 77 min; plasma, GLC assay (caprylene internal standard). Concentrations in pg/mL
  (Figures 2-4: roughly 50-600 pg/mL).
- Analysis: K from log-linear regression of only the falling post-infusion points; AUC by spline during infusion
  plus C32/K; CLs = dose/AUC (Eq. 2); Vd = CLs/K (Eq. 3); CL* = k0/C32 (Eq. 4).

- Abstract summary: clearance 0.3-1 L/min/kg, Vd about 3 L/kg, t½ about 3 min.
- Steady-state subjects (5B, 7, 8): mean clearance 0.45 L/min/kg, close to 28 L/min (0.4 L/min/kg at 70 kg) after
  sublingual dosing (Armstrong 1979). In non-steady-state subjects dose/AUC exceeded k0/C32, which the authors take
  as unaccounted AUC (a slow phase below the assay limit).
- Plasma holds about 1.3% of the body load (plasma 42 mL/kg; Vd about 80 times plasma volume).
- Rebound in concentration about 15 min after the stop in several subjects; subjects 6 and 10 oscillated too much
  to analyse.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, no ke0. At about 18 mcg/min (0.6 mg over 30 min) heart rate and blood pressure did not change
significantly (Table III). No effective concentration or potency ratio reported.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
- Small n, healthy men, a low rate; seven analysable profiles, only three at apparent steady state.
- Apparent clearance exceeds cardiac output; the authors list possible reasons (dose error judged unlikely, blood
  degradation about 1-2% of clearance, missing slow phase, first-pass uptake by the vascular bed).
- Venous sampling. The authors themselves attribute Armstrong 1980's lower clearances partly to its arterial
  sampling (Discussion p4). Their comparison of "high" clearance is against liver plasma flow and cardiac output
  and, in Table IV, against Wei and Reid (14-146 L/min) and Armstrong 1980 (7.0-24.7 and 0.9-6.4 L/min).
