# Jacob 2016: An updated overview on therapeutic drug monitoring of recent antiepileptic drugs

## Study
Narrative review of therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) for 14 newer/newest antiepileptic drugs (rufinamide,
stiripentol, perampanel, retigabine, eslicarbazepine acetate, vigabatrin, lacosamide, pregabalin, zonisamide,
levetiracetam, tiagabine, topiramate, lamotrigine, felbamate, gabapentin), summarizing published PK parameters,
reference/target serum concentration ranges, and concentration-effect/toxicity relationships for each drug, plus
analytical methods used to assay them. No original patient data or modeling; all values are drawn from the studies
cited in-text and in Table 1.

## Model and parameters
No PK model is fit. Table 1 ("Pharmacokinetic parameters of current antiepileptic medications," PDF p.3, printed
p.305) gives, for eslicarbazepine acetate (refs [38, 43]), confirmed on the rendered page image because the text
layer interleaves this landscape table's columns across adjacent drug rows:

  - {name: Vd, value: 2.7, unit: L/kg, source: "Table 1", note: "typical value"}

Active metabolite column: "Yes" (eslicarbazepine acetate is itself the prodrug; its active moiety is
eslicarbazepine/S-licarbazepine). Need-for-TDM column: "Intermediate."

Body text (Section 3.3, p.307, PDF p.5) adds: extensively metabolized to S-licarbazepine (~95%); linear
dose-serum-concentration relationship; induces CYPs, increasing clearance of carbamazepine, lamotrigine and
topiramate by 12-16%; metabolites eliminated primarily renally, dose adjustment needed below 60 mL/min clearance;
hemodialysis efficiently removes ESL and metabolites; moderate hepatic impairment has limited clinical effect
(single-dose 800 mg x1 week study showed no PK change, ref 45); switch ratios proposed from oxcarbazepine 1-1.5:1
and from carbamazepine 1-1.3:1 (refs 39, 40).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- "A stable dose-response relationship was recognized between ESL serum concentrations and reductions in seizure
  frequency that were not altered by other AEDs" (p.307, PDF p.5) — no numeric threshold, EC50 or MEAC is given for
  this relationship; it is stated narratively only.
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or effect-site modeling is reported for eslicarbazepine (or any drug) in this paper; it is
  not a PK/PD modeling study.
- The nearest thing to a therapeutic threshold is Table 1's "Reference concentration range (mg/l)" of 10-35 for
  eslicarbazepine acetate (PDF p.3) — explicitly a TDM reference/benchmark range (serum, trough not specified in
  the table; matrix and total-vs-free assay not stated), not a pharmacodynamically derived MEAC. The paper
  elsewhere (Section 1.2) cautions that such "reference concentration ranges" are not necessarily "therapeutic,"
  "effective" or "target" values.
- No potency/equianalgesic ratio applies (not an opioid; eslicarbazepine has no active comparator ratio stated in
  this paper beyond the switch ratios from oxcarbazepine/carbamazepine noted above, which are dosing, not
  concentration, ratios).
- No opioid audit applies to this paper (antiepileptic, not opioid).

## Covariates
None modeled quantitatively. Table 1 and body text give only qualitative notes (e.g., enzyme-inducing AEDs lower
some AEDs' concentrations; renal/hepatic impairment affects several drugs), with no equations.

## Caveats
- Narrative review, not a systematic review or meta-analysis; reference ranges in Table 1 are compiled from
  heterogeneous, mostly small or retrospective source studies (cited by reference number only, not detailed).
- The authors explicitly state they "do not propose routine TDM" for these drugs and that reference ranges "may
  not always be therapeutic, effective, or target" values (Abstract, Section 1.2).
- Table 1's layout is landscape and was extracted with scrambled rows in this archive's text layer; all numbers
  used here were re-read from a rendered page image (PDF p.3).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Both repo locations attribute the actual 3-35 mcg/mL band to the AGNP 2026 consensus guideline (Hart et al.,
  Pharmacopsychiatry 2026;59:166-295, Table 5), citing Jacob 2016 only as one of the inputs that guideline drew on
  (together with oxcarbazepine's MHD range), not as the direct source of "3-35." This framing is **consistent**
  with what Jacob 2016 itself reports: no claim in the repo says Jacob 2016 states "3-35" verbatim.
- However, Jacob 2016's own Table 1 (PDF p.3, confirmed on rendered image) gives eslicarbazepine acetate's
  reference concentration range as **10-35 mg/L**, not 3-35 mg/L — a different lower bound. Since the AGNP 2026
  source document (Hart et al.) is not in this archive, it cannot be confirmed here whether/how Hart et al. combined
  Jacob's 10-35 mg/L with oxcarbazepine's MHD range to arrive at the wider 3-35 mg/L band. **Partly verified**: the
  repo's citation of Jacob 2016 as a contributing (not sole) source is accurate to how Jacob 2016 is cited, but the
  numeric relationship between Jacob's 10-35 mg/L and the AGNP 2026 band's 3-35 mg/L is unresolved from materials in
  this archive (see open_questions).
- No other number from this paper is used by stanpumpR.
