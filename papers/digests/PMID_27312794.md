# Baron 2016: empagliflozin population PK and exposure-response in type 2 diabetes

## Study
Pooled analysis of 10 studies (2 phase I, 4 phase II, 4 phase III; Table 1, PDF p7-8) in adults with type 2 diabetes,
oral empagliflozin 1-100 mg once daily (1129 patients on 10 mg, 1269 on 25 mg). PK: 12,503 plasma concentrations from
2761 patients on active drug; placebo excluded (PDF p6). Efficacy PK/PD dataset: 4065 patients incl. placebo, 25,361 FPG
and 22,012 HbA1c values; FPG model 4289 patients (PDF p10). NONMEM 7.2; full covariate model (no stepwise testing),
non-parametric bootstrap CIs; covariates "negligible" when the effect at the extreme covariate levels stayed within
0.8-1.25 (PDF p3-4). Sponsor Boehringer Ingelheim; analysis by Metrum.

## Model and parameters
Two compartments, first-order absorption with a lag fixed at 0.5 h; data in the first hour after a dose excluded
(Methods, PDF p3); IIV on CL/F, ka and V3/F; proportional residual error. The Methods call the absorption "lagged
first-order", the Results "first-order" (PDF p6). Typical values for the reference patient (50-year-old non-smoking
non-Asian man, BMI 25 kg/m2, MDRD eGFR 100 mL/min/1.73 m2, total protein 70, ALT and AST 20, AP 70, LDH 160 U/L), with
bootstrap 95% CI (Results, PDF p6):

Units slips in the print: ka "L/h" (a rate constant, so 1/h); total protein "70 g/dL" and Table 2's "g/dL" (median 72;
the plausible unit is g/L). Derived (not in the paper): k10 3.38/h, k12 2.02/h, k21 0.090/h give a terminal half-life
of about 12.4 h at the reference.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- FPG: indirect response, exposure (AUCss) stimulating FPG removal by an Emax term; median AUC50 704 nM h (about 3 mg
  once daily); Gmax 22% FPG reduction, larger with higher baseline FPG, smaller with lower eGFR (PDF p11).
- HbA1c: indirect response driven by FPG; HbA1c half-life about 2.6 weeks (95% CI 1.7-3.9) (PDF p11). 10 and 25 mg
  reach about 80% and 90% of the maximal response (Table S4, not in the PDF).
- 24-week HbA1c change at 25 mg: reference -0.71% (-0.79, -0.65); eGFR 60, -0.59% (-0.65, -0.53) in the Results
  (PDF p12), -0.58% (-0.65, -0.52) in the Discussion (PDF p14), a small internal inconsistency; baseline FPG 9 mM,
  -0.89%.
- Safety (logistic): empagliflozin vs placebo OR genital infection 5.08 (2.77-9.34); UTI 0.941, hypoglycaemia 1.11,
  volume depletion 1.44, all not significant; no exposure dependence (PDF p12-13).

## Covariates
Main text: eGFR dominates CL/F. Typical AUCss 18.5% (13.0-24.8), 49.2% (39.2-60.6) and 88.1% (69.9-107.0) higher at
eGFR 60, 30 and 15 than at 100 (PDF p9); CL/F 46.8% lower at eGFR 15 (PDF p14). BMI, total protein, age, female sex,
current smoking, Asian race and AP were statistically significant on CL/F but within 0.8-1.25 at the extremes; ALT,
AST, LDH and former smoking not significant (Figure 1, PDF p9). The coefficients, and the covariates on V2/F, V3/F and
ka, are only in supplement Table S1, which is not in this PDF. Table 2 (PDF p10), PK/PD dataset median (2.5th-97.5th
percentile): age 58 (36-76) y, eGFR 81.8 (33.4-128), BMI 29.1 (21.0-42.4); elderly subset BMI 21.8-38.3; study-10 renal
subset BMI 20.8-42.3, eGFR 60.1 (22.1-86.8).

## Caveats
Sparse phase III sampling, early data excluded and V2/F almost unidentified (CI to 0.00128 L), so the early curve is
weak. Apparent (/F) parameters. Covariate ranges in Table 2 describe the PK/PD dataset, not the 2761-patient PK
dataset. Supplement not archived.
