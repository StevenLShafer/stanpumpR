# Skarke 2003: morphine and M6G on electrical pain tolerance and pupil size

PDF page = printed page − 106 (PDF 1 = printed 107).

## Study
- Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled 3-way crossover (washout ≥7 days); 14 healthy young volunteers
  enrolled, 2 withdrew; 12 analysed (6 men, 6 women; mean age 26 y, weight 67 kg, height 173 cm; Table I, PDF p2).
  Subject 6 (male, G118/G118) received M6G and placebo only and was used only in the pupil PK-PD model.
- Half the subjects deliberately carried the OPRM1 G118 allele (A118G): 4 A/G, 2 G/G, 6 A/A (Table I).
- IV bolus then infusion targeting plasma 100 ng/mL morphine (stated as 350.4 nmol/L) or 3000 ng/mL M6G; infusion
  stopped once pupil ≤3 mm for 1 h. Morphine doses 57.23-142.6 µmol (mean 97 µmol), infusion 1.83-4.43 h (mean
  3.06 h); M6G 222.17-394.06 µmol (Table I; abstract gives 26-66 mg morphine, 63-112 mg M6G). Morphine given as
  the sulfate.
- Effects every 30 min for up to 16 h: pain threshold and tolerance to 5 Hz sine-wave transcutaneous electrical
  stimulation (Neurometer, 0-9.99 mA, 0.2 mA/s, middle finger; median of 5); pupil diameter by pupillograph
  (<14 lux); side-effect VAS.
- Blood (plasma, K-EDTA) 23-32 samples per subject; morphine, M3G, M6G by LC-MS/MS (linear 0.5-4000 ng/mL;
  intra/interday variability <10%). Total plasma concentrations (no free fraction).
- Semiparametric PK-PD (plasma concentrations as linear splines, no compartmental PK model) with an effect
  compartment (ke0); NONMEM V. Pain tolerance: Laplacian with LIKELIHOOD option, censoring at 10 mA via Weibull
  (Appendix). Pupil: FOCE with interaction, additive error. M3G assumed inactive.
- Metabolism by AUC ratio: 9 ± 0.7 µmol M6G formed from 97 ± 23 µmol morphine (9%); 2.6 ± 0.7 µmol morphine from
  226 ± 64 µmol M6G (1%) (PDF p6).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- **ke0 / t½ke0 (morphine)**: pain tolerance (electrical, 5 Hz) ke0 0.27 h⁻¹, t½ke0 2.6 h; pupil diameter ke0
  0.25 h⁻¹, t½ke0 2.8 h (Table II; abstract; PDF p7, p8). Both estimated against plasma (total, LC-MS/MS)
  concentrations described by splines. The authors call the two values almost identical and report standard
  errors of about 40% (pain) and 10% (pupil) for the main PK-PD parameters (p119).
- **M6G**: t½ke0 8.2 h (pain tolerance), 7.7 h (pupil). Note ln2/0.08 = 8.7 h (derived), not 8.2 h; the
  table's 0.08 is presumably rounded.
- **No tPeak reported** (no compartmental PK, so none can be computed from the paper alone).
- **MEAC: none reported.** The nearest quantities are concentration-effect values in healthy volunteers on an
  experimental end point, not a clinical minimum effective concentration: effect-site morphine 85 nmol/L
  (≈24 ng/mL, derived with MW 285.3) for a 50% rise in pain tolerance (p113, p118); pupil EC50 26.7 nmol/L
  (≈7.6 ng/mL, derived) in A118/A118, and 41 nmol/L for 50% pupil constriction (p118; the paper does not show
  how 41 is obtained from Table II). Pain-tolerance relation is linear (no Emax, no γ).
- **Potency ratios**: morphine:M6G 28:1 from the pupil, 12:1 from pain tolerance (p119), with widely overlapping
  confidence intervals; M6G needed 1114 nmol/L for 50% pain-tolerance rise and 1034 nmol/L for 50% pupil
  constriction (p113, p118). Dose terms: 222-394 µmol M6G vs 57.2-142.6 µmol morphine for similar effects, i.e.
  3-4 times more M6G (p116).
- **OPRM1 G118**: decreased pupil potency of morphine (EC50 ×2.1 heterozygous, ×3.6 homozygous) and M6G (×2.4
  [table 2.5] heterozygous, ×2.6 homozygous); no detectable effect on pain tolerance (p114, p119).
- Side effects: no AUC difference between morphine and M6G; vomiting 6.5 ± 6.4 (morphine) vs 6.2 ± 4.8 (M6G)
  episodes; respiratory depression not assessed.

## Covariates
OPRM1 A118G genotype on pupil EC50 only (multiplicative factors above). Sex and G118 were not significant for
pain tolerance. No weight, age or size covariates.

## Caveats
- 12 healthy young adults, half selected as G118 carriers (above the population's ~12%); experimental pain.
- Plasma concentrations were modelled by splines, so the ke0 values belong to no compartmental PK model;
  carrying t½ke0 to another disposition model (as stanpumpR's context does) is approximate.
- Internal inconsistencies in the paper: morphine pupil EC50 26.4 (abstract) vs 26.7 nmol/L (Table II, results);
  M6G heterozygous factor 2.4 (text) vs 2.5 (Table II); the M6G target is 10,512.5 nmol/L in Methods but
  6473.1 nmol/L in the Fig 1 legend (3000 ng/mL ÷ M6G MW 461.4 ≈ 6500 nmol/L, derived; 10,512.5 corresponds to
  the morphine MW); the Fig 1 legend's left/right labels are the reverse of the panel titles; equations 5 and 6
  are each described as the model of the other end point (p113, p114). None touches the morphine ke0.
- The authors note M6G equilibration may have outlasted the observation period (~40 h for 5 half-lives).
