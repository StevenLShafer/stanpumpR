# Brancazio 1995: subcutaneous heparin in early third trimester

## Study
Cross-sectional PK/PD study, Magee-Womens Hospital Clinical Research Center, Pittsburgh. 8 pregnant and 7 non-pregnant volunteers enrolled; 2 and 1 dropped out (catheter problems), leaving 6 pregnant and 6 non-pregnant analysed. Pregnant: uncomplicated singleton, 24-30 wk gestation, within 10% of ideal body weight before pregnancy, Hb not below 10 g/dL. Controls: healthy, not pregnant within 6 months, within 10% of ideal weight.

Dose: heparin sodium 10,000 units/mL, 143 units/kg subcutaneously, right upper abdominal quadrant (chosen to give 10,000 units in a 70 kg woman). Bed rest, forearm IV catheter. Sampling at baseline and 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 195, 210, 225, 240, 255, 270, 285, 300, 330, 360, 420, 480, 540, 600 min for aPTT and plasma heparin (3.8% citrate, plasma frozen at or below -20 C, assayed at the Cleveland Clinic reference lab). Assay: excess factor Xa method (Du Pont aca), intra-assay CV 1.5-9.2%, inter-assay CV 2.4-10.7%, sensitivity 0.02 units/mL; values below that were set to 0.02. Statistics: two-way ANOVA (time, pregnancy), trapezoidal AUC, one-tailed unpaired t test. No compartmental model was fitted; non-compartmental summaries only.

Baseline characteristics (Table I, PDF p.2, mean +/- SE; pregnant vs non-pregnant): gestational age 26.7 +/- 1.0 wk; weight 66.7 +/- 4.4 vs 59.5 +/- 1.0 kg (p 0.14); heparin dose 9500 +/- 640 vs 8416 +/- 170 units (row is mislabelled "units/ml" in the table; p 0.13); Hb 10.9 vs 12.8 g/dL; Hct 31.4 vs 37.3%; baseline plasma heparin 0.022 vs 0.020 units/mL; baseline aPTT 24.6 +/- 0.25 vs 26.2 +/- 1.0 s (p 0.14).

Time course: plasma heparin rose above baseline by 60 min in both groups; it returned to baseline by 285 min in pregnant subjects and by 480 min in controls; controls were higher than pregnant from 150 to 480 min. The pregnant group's aPTT did not rise significantly from baseline (p = 0.463), versus p = 0.0003 for controls.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
End point is aPTT (s) and, as PK, excess-Xa plasma heparin activity (units/mL). No ke0, EC50, MEAC or hysteresis model; the time to peak aPTT (137 vs 230 min) is a time of observed maximum, not tPeak from a fitted effect-site model. The Comment cites a recommended mid-dosing-interval heparin level of 0.05-0.20 units/mL (from other work, refs 19-20 in the paper) and says the pregnant peak (0.11) falls inside it only briefly; this is not a threshold the study derived. Not an opioid paper.

## Covariates
Pregnancy (early third trimester) is the only comparison. Weight differed non-significantly (66.7 vs 59.5 kg) and the dose was per kg (143 units/kg), so total dose was similar (9500 vs 8416 units). No covariate equations.

## Caveats
Authors: single study point in pregnancy, single dose, healthy women within 10% of ideal weight, one dose level only, so a starting dose cannot be derived. Also: n=6 per group; SE not SD; the pregnant gestational age differs between abstract and Table I; no intravenous arm, so bioavailability and clearance cannot be separated; the AUC and "lower" claims compare pregnant women against the paper's own non-pregnant controls at the same unit-per-kg dose. Heparin is reported in assay units, with no stated relation to IU.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Registry heparin row (inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv, heparin record, physical line 140 per context; the heparinCPB record cites the same paper): "143 U/kg SC to 6 third-trimester women vs 6 non-pregnant controls", "peak plasma heparin 0.11 vs 0.23 U/mL", "shorter time-to-peak", n = 12 all **match** the paper (Table II, abstract). "Half as high" is 0.48 (derived). Comparator is the paper's non-pregnant controls, which is what the registry says.
- The library heparin model (R/drugs_heparin.R) is IV, anti-IIa, from Jia 2015 (header lines 3-11), and heparinCPB plots ACT. The paper is SC with an anti-Xa assay and so cannot calibrate or check either; the registry rightly applies no factor. Whether the paper's units equal the library's IU is not settled (open question).
- The registry's "relative heparin resistance... increased heparin-binding proteins, factor VIII and fibrinogen" rests on Whitfield 1983 and Chunilal 2002; Brancazio only speculates on those mechanisms (PDF p.4). Also the registry note "predominantly pharmacodynamic" is not what Brancazio shows: it found lower plasma heparin (a PK result) as well as a blunted aPTT. Listed as an open question.
- Registry "third trimester": gestation 24-30 wk, mean 26.7 (Table I) or 27.1 (abstract); fine.
Verdict: partly-verified (numbers match; attribution of mechanism and unit equivalence open).
