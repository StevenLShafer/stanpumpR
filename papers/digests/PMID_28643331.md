# Westin 2018: serum antipsychotic concentrations across pregnancy (Norwegian TDM)

## Study
Retrospective analysis of routine therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) from two Norwegian services (St Olav, Trondheim; Diakonhjemmet, Oslo), October 1999 - December 2011, linked to the Medical Birth Registry (PDF p. 2, 6-7).
- 201 serum measurements during 110 pregnancies in 103 women, plus 512 measurements from the same women outside pregnancy (476 baseline used in the model, 36 in the first 12 weeks after delivery shown only) (Table 1, PDF p. 2). Mean pregnancy 274 +/- 19 days; maternal age at delivery 29.8 +/- 6.6 y.
- Exclusions: drug not detected (33), intoxication (2), sample < 8 h or > 30 h after last oral dose (22), IM + oral (1), interacting drug (6), dose unknown (6).
- Serum concentrations by LC-MS/MS (validated, cross-validated over time); trough sampling at steady state recommended but not enforced. Concentrations divided by the woman's daily dose and multiplied by the WHO defined daily dose (DDD).
- Linear mixed model (Stata "mixed"), log concentration linear in gestational week with a random intercept per woman; baseline (not pregnant) set to week 0; trimester values are model estimates at weeks 6, 20 and 34. P-values only for drugs with > 10 pregnancies.

Table 1 (number of pregnancies / women; measurements during pregnancy / baseline): quetiapine 35/33 (66/144), olanzapine 29/28 (47/84), aripiprazole 14/12 (31/44), perphenazine IM 8/8, perphenazine PO 7/5, clozapine 4/4, ziprasidone 3/3, risperidone 4/4 (5/9), haloperidol 2/2 (5/2).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None; clinical outcome not available. No therapeutic range applied (the figures show a female reference population's percentiles). Authors suggest doubling the daily dose may be needed in the third trimester for quetiapine and aripiprazole; for olanzapine dose adjustment might not be necessary (p. 6).

## Covariates
Gestational week only. Mechanisms discussed (p. 5-6): CYP3A4 induction (quetiapine), CYP2D6 induction (aripiprazole; parent fell similarly to the active moiety, data not shown); reduced CYP1A2 in T2-T3 for olanzapine and clozapine, with reduced smoking in pregnancy as an alternative explanation that could not be checked (no smoking data). Protein binding falls 20-30% in T3 but cannot explain the differences.

## Caveats
Authors: no clinical data; adherence unknown; dose information from requisitions; variable dose-to-sample interval (8-30 h, not standardised to 12 h); log-linear time course assumed. Small n for all but three drugs. "Baseline" pools samples before and after pregnancy (beyond 12 weeks postpartum) in the same women; it is a mixed-model comparison, not strictly paired samples. Dose adjustment assumes dose proportionality.
