# Kobayashi 1994: mannitol PK and ICP in six patients

## Study
Six patients (3 men, 3 women, mean age 43 y) with intracranial hypertension; 20% mannitol 150 or 300 mL by IV
drip (DIV); infusion durations shown in Fig 2 for some cases (e.g. 7 and 18 min). ICP by epidural water-filled
balloon. Serum mannitol (mg/mL, gas-liquid chromatography) and serum osmolality (mOsm/kg) sampled at 5, 10, 20,
30, 60, 120 min. Weights not given; dose per kg not given.

## Model and parameters
Concentrations biexponential, best fitted by a two-compartment model; the authors say the parameters were similar
to Cloyd 1986 and Dominguez 1947, but **no parameter values are printed**. No tables.

parameters: []   # two-compartment fits described, but no parameter values printed

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Effective (extrinsic) osmolality = measured minus calculated osmolality; peaked at end of infusion and declined
  in parallel with mannitol; "strong positive correlation" with mannitol concentration (no coefficient given).
- ICP fell slowly after the start of infusion and gradually returned to baseline (Fig 1, per case).
- Integrated (central minus peripheral compartment) mannitol concentration difference correlated with the change
  in ICP (Fig 2, cases 3-6); no regression, no coefficient, no fitted PD model. No ke0.

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Short conference supplement; n = 6; no numerical PK or PD results; correlation claims are qualitative; peripheral
concentrations are model-predicted, not measured.
