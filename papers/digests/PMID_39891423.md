# Silvola 2025: gabapentin popPK and breast-milk transfer after cesarean delivery

## Study
Prospective observational, single centre (Indianapolis), Feb-Aug 2021, NCT05099484. 21 healthy women with singleton
pregnancy, 18 y or older, cesarean delivery, planning to breastfeed, on the ERAS protocol: gabapentin 300 mg PO once
before surgery, then 300 mg q6h for 48 h. Table 1 (PDF p5, rendered; n = 21, median (range)): age 29 (19-41) y,
weight 107.5 (68.5-154) kg, height 1.7 m, BMI 35.8 (26.2-48.2), LBM 55.2 kg, eGFR (2021 CKD-EPI) 151 (61-218)
mL/min/1.73 m2, GA at delivery 39.1 (31.1-41.3) weeks. Sampling over one postpartum dosing interval ("PK dose"):
plasma pre-dose, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 6 h; milk at least every 2 h; one infant heel-stick sample; cord blood and
amniotic fluid in 4. One woman on 600 mg q6h was excluded (poor fit, attributed to nonlinear absorption), leaving 125
plasma samples from 20 women and 35 milk samples from 15. HPLC-MS/MS, linear 3-3000 ng/mL, LLOQ 3 ng/mL. NONMEM 7.5
(ADVAN13), FOCE-I, SIR (10,000/1000).

## Model and parameters
One compartment, first-order absorption and elimination for plasma. The unconstrained fit gave flip-flop kinetics,
judged implausible, so later models constrained ka > ke (PDF p4). BSV on CL/F and V/F, not ka. Milk added two ways
(Fig. 1): Model A, milk = MPR x plasma; Model B, a milk compartment V3 fixed at 30 mL with K23 fixed at 1/h and K32
estimated. Covariates destabilised the model and were not included. Apparent CL and V fell non-significantly over
the days after delivery (p = 0.07, Fig. S3).

Derived: ke = 15.6/284 = 0.055/h (t1/2 12.6 h), well below the estimated ka of 0.43/h, so the ka > ke constraint is
not at its bound in the final estimate. Authors compare with healthy volunteers: CL/F 15.6 vs 14-15 L/h, V/F 284 vs
58 L (Discussion, PDF p7; ref 40, McLean 1995), and cite a 41% higher GFR after cesarean (ref 38).

Simulated exposure (Table 4, PDF p7, 10,000 mothers, 300 mg q6h), median (95% PI), Model A day 1 / day 2: plasma
1480 / 2650 ng/mL; milk 582 / 1060 ng/mL; IDD 0.00233 / 0.0137 mg/kg/day; RID 0.0189 / 0.112%. Model B nearly
identical (day 2 IDD 0.0139 (0.0041-0.0469)). Maternal AUC0-48h (Model A post hoc) 103.7 ± 32.2 mg·h/L. Cord plasma
673-1604 ng/mL and amniotic fluid 51-2890 ng/mL (n = 4); infant plasma 24-48 h below LLOQ in 6 of 9, others 5.0-68.7
ng/mL. AIC 1892.3 (A) vs 1902.8 (B).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD model. Opioid use (MME48h, mean 17.7 mg, median 15) was not associated with gabapentin AUC0-48h (r = 0.36,
p = 0.11; high vs low MME 111.9 vs 94.6 mg·h/L, p = 0.5). No analgesic concentration reported.

## Covariates
None in the final model (instability). Not testable: time after delivery, weight, eGFR.

## Caveats
Sampling over one 6-h interval in the first 48 h postpartum, not pregnancy; mostly obese women; ka imprecise (RSE
67%); moderate shrinkage; milk volumes from literature. Internal inconsistencies in the paper: the abstract gives Model
B's day-2 IDD lower bound as 0.00041 where Table 4 has 0.0041; Fig. 3's caption says dosing every 8 h and the excluded
woman's 600 mg "three times daily", while Methods and Results say q6h; "6 infants (78%)" of 9 is 67%; MDD uses a
median weight of 97.7 kg, not Table 1's 107.5 kg. Which is right is not determinable from the paper.
