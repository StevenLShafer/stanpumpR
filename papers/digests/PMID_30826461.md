# Vourc'h 2019: baclofen PK in ventilated ICU patients

## Study
Prospective single-centre study, surgical ICU, Nantes (Mar-Aug 2015). n = 20 mechanically ventilated adults aged 18-70 with alcohol intake above NIAAA limits; all male; median age 60.1 y, median BMI 23.55, median eGFR (CKD-EPI) 103 mL/min/1.73 m2 (Table 1). 5 had renal impairment (2 mild eGFR 60-89, 1 moderate 30-59, 2 severe <30); 5 cirrhosis. Baclofen was given enterally by feeding tube. Daily dose was 150 mg with eGFR >= 90 (15 patients), otherwise 100 mg (2), 70 mg (1) or 50 mg (2) (Table 2). Day 1 was a single full daily dose; thereafter 3 doses a day (50 mg each at 08:00, 16:00, 24:00 for the 150 mg regimen). Serial samples on day 3: pre-dose, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 6, 8 h after the 08:00 dose. Assay: validated LC-MS/MS on plasma (details in a companion Data in Brief article, not in this PDF). Analyses: non-compartmental (Table 3) and NONMEM 7.3 (FOCE; ADVAN2 one-compartment vs ADVAN4 two-compartment, with/without lag); covariates age, weight, eGFR; exponential IIV and residual error.

## Model and parameters
Final model (p. 453-454): one-compartment, first-order absorption with lag time, apparent parameters (oral dose, bioavailability not separable). CL/F = theta1 x (eGFR/103)^theta2, eGFR in mL/min/1.73 m2 (CKD-EPI), centred on the population median 103. The text on printed p. 453 prints the clearance equation with a "+" in place of the multiplication; Table 4 gives the product form, which is the one used here. No weight or age effect was retained.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, ke0 or effect-site concentration. Not an opioid; MEAC/potency items: none. The only concentration threshold is a toxic plasma level of 1.1 mg/L, taken from Schulz 2012 (a compilation, cited p. 453); the authors state all observed concentrations were below it (Fig. 1: highest curves peak at about 1.1 mg/L, so close to the threshold). Four patients (20%) had clinical signs compatible with toxicity (eyes closed 48 h after sedation stop, seizure, etc.); AUC0-t (p = .34) and Cmax (p = .26) did not differ between toxic and non-toxic groups (Mann-Whitney). Concentration is total plasma (assay type not stated as free).

## Covariates
eGFR (CKD-EPI) on CL/F only, power model above; selected by objective-function drop > 6.6 (p < .01). Age and weight tested, not retained. Derived dosing rule (p. 453): dose reduction % = 100 x (CL/F(eGFR) x targetAUC / 50 mg - 1), targetAUC = 50/13.5 = 3.7 mg.h/L; suggested reductions 40% (eGFR 60), 60% (30), 70% (20). (Derived: with CL = 13.5 x (eGFR/103)^0.839 the formula gives about -36%, -65%, -75%, so the authors round.) Simulated (n = 1000 per group) mean AUC 3.81, 2.91, 3.2 mg.h/L at mean eGFR 61.6, 30.8, 20.5.

## Caveats
n = 20, all male, tablets crushed and given by feeding tube (authors invoke altered absorption and paralytic ileus to explain the lag, which outpatient studies did not need); only 5 patients with renal impairment, 3 with eGFR < 60; day-3 8-h sampling too short for impaired patients, so the PopPK renal exponent is weakly supported (bootstrap CI 0.18-1.55); V/F bootstrap mean 82 L, below the 105 L estimate, with a wide CI. Apparent CL and V are per oral dose (F unknown). Authors' comparison values from outpatients: CL/F 9.9 L/h, V/F 80.7 L (Marsot 2014); 11.6 L/h, 72.8 L (Simon 2018). Intravenous or intrathecal baclofen is not covered.
