# Marchand 2010: rufinamide population PK, valproate interaction and paediatric dosing by simulation

## Study
- Population PK pooled from 7 phase 2/3 adjunctive-therapy studies (partial or generalised seizures) plus 1 Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (LGS) study (study 022): 1072 patients, 5937 rufinamide concentrations, sparse sampling; 119 children (<12 y) and 99 adolescents (p3, printed 101).
- LGS study population used for simulation: 65 patients, 26 F/39 M; median age 14.0 y (4.1-35.8), weight 35.4 kg (15.5-138.5), BSA 1.2 m2 (0.7-2.6); valproate co-administered in 34 (52.3%), median valproate concentration 106.5 ug/mL (22.9-149); carbamazepine 10, barbiturate 14 (Table 1, PDF p4, confirmed on image).
- Oral rufinamide, final market image (FMI) formulation, b.i.d. Co-medications recorded: benzodiazepines, phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone, vigabatrin, topiramate, lamotrigine, valproate.
- NONMEM V, FO method (FOCE crashed). Simulations in Pharsight Trial Simulator 2.1.2 using the 65 LGS patients' covariates, 22 per weight band x 50 replicates = 1100 virtual patients.
- Assay and sampling times not given in this paper (published elsewhere, ref 4 Perucca 2008).

## Model and parameters
One-compartment, first-order elimination; exponential IIV on CL and V (p8-9, printed 106-107). Apparent (/F) values. Two clearance models, both in Table 3 (PDF p10, printed 108, confirmed on image); 95% CI in brackets.

Equation 7 (additive DDI, p10):
CL = 4.04 + 2.69(BSA - 1.77) - 0.387 VPA - 2.42 CVPA/(263 + CVPA) + 1.45 BAR + 0.000993 DCBZ + 0.702 VIG - 0.225 SEX

Equation 8 (multiplicative valproate and barbiturate, additive others; preferred, p13 printed 111):
CL = (3.99 + 2.75(BSA - 1.77)) x (1 - 0.186 VPA - 1.16 CVPA/(336 + CVPA) + 0.455 BAR) + 0.000986 DCBZ + 0.675 VIG - 0.242 SEX

VPA (yes/no) is used only when the valproate concentration is unknown (20/270 patients); CVPA in ug/mL; DCBZ carbamazepine total daily dose in mg; SEX 1 = female (Table 3 footnote).

Bioavailability: F = 1 - Emax x DOSE/(D50 + DOSE), DOSE = daily dose per kg - 1.96 mg/kg (eq 4, p9). F = 1 at 1.96 mg/kg/day; F = 0.56 at 43.8 mg/kg/day (text p9).

Volume: V = 73.8 x (BSA/1.77)^0.779 + 49.4 x adolescent (eq 5 as described, p9; Table 3).

Model-predicted CL/F (Table 4, PDF p14, printed 112, confirmed on image), multiplicative model, without / with valproate: male children (BSA 0.97, 27 kg, 43.8 mg/kg, F 0.53) 3.35 / 2.46 L/h; female children 2.89 / 2.00; male adults (1.85 m2, 72 kg, 12.7 mg/kg, F 0.83) 5.04 / 4.02; female adults 4.75 / 3.73. Valproate at the age-class median (100 for children, 71 for adults; footnote prints ng/ml).

Stated clearance decreases with valproate (p14, male, BSA 0.8 m2, vs no valproate): multiplicative 26% at 100 ug/mL and 35% at 150 ug/mL; additive 46% and 60%.

Background (Introduction, citing ref 4): absorption nearly complete after 600 mg fed; F falls with dose; protein binding ~34%; V about 50-80 L; t1/2 about 6-10 h; renal excretion of unchanged drug <2%.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- No PD model fitted here. Background only (p2, citing ref 4): a plasma concentration of about 15 ug/mL would reduce seizure frequency by 25%; adverse events rose with exposure.
- Exposure end point of the simulations: Cav over 24 h = (daily dose/24)/(CL/F) x fss (eq 1-3).
- Results: <30 kg on 1000 mg/day, median Cavss close to 20 ug/mL vs about 15 ug/mL in the other weight bands, 95th percentile >60 ug/mL (p11). Multiplicative model, <30 kg on 1000 mg/day: median 15 ug/mL without and 21 ug/mL with valproate 100 ug/mL (p15).
- No ke0, MEAC or potency ratio (not an opioid paper).

## Covariates
BSA (Haycock) on CL (linear) and V (power); sex on CL; adolescent class on V; daily dose per kg on F (Emax); valproate (concentration Emax, or yes/no), barbiturates, carbamazepine daily dose and vigabatrin on CL. Recommended maximum daily dose (Table 2): <30 kg 1000, 30.1-50 kg 1800, 50.1-70 kg 2400, >=70.1 kg 3200 mg/day; with valproate and <30 kg, 600 mg/day (multiplicative model; the additive model would have given 400 mg/day).

## Caveats
- FO estimation on sparse data; pooled heterogeneous studies and formulations.
- Unit misprints in Table 3/Table 4 (ng/ml for valproate concentration, ug/kg for D50); resolved by derivation below.
- The Emax for F exceeds 1 in magnitude, so F would go negative at very high mg/kg doses; it holds only over the observed range.
- The valproate x barbiturate interaction could not be estimated (95% CI included zero).
- Mechanism (carboxylesterase inhibition by valproate) is the authors' hypothesis, not tested.
- Sponsor-affiliated authors (Eisai; EMF Consulting).
