# Shafer 1993: advances in propofol pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics

## Study
Narrative review (adapted from 1990-92 ASA Refresher Courses) with simulations, clinical dosing guidelines for propofol (induction, maintenance, TIVA with opioid or ketamine, MAC sedation, ICU sedation, cardiac, paediatric, antiemetic use) and two appendices of dosing guidance. No new patient data, no sampling, no assay. 8 PDF pages.

Table 1 is read on the page image. Note the rows are absolute (L, L/min), not per kg. The text states metabolic clearance of propofol exceeds hepatic blood flow (extrahepatic elimination) and, in the text-layer, a total distribution clearance of 3-4 (unit garbled in the text layer, L/min or L/min/kg; the Table sums give 3.8 L/min for propofol and 3.2 for thiopental, so L/min).

Simulations (no numbers beyond these): Figure 2 (PDF p2): after a 6 h infusion about 15 min for a 50% fall in propofol effect-site concentration; thiopental far longer (curve reaches >100 min). Figure 3 (text, p2-3) compares propofol with alfentanil and sufentanil for 50% decrease; the text says for infusions of less than 8 h sufentanil falls faster than alfentanil despite alfentanil's shorter half-lives. No numbers given. Figure 5 (PDF p5): propofol vs midazolam time for 50% effect-site decrease; propofol roughly 25-30 min at 600 min infusion, midazolam about 100 min (read from curve, approximate).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Blood-brain (plasma/effect-site) equilibration: propofol "1 to 2 minutes", midazolam "2 to 4 minutes" (text, PDF p4, printed p17S, cites Buhrer 1990). Not stated as ke0, t1/2 ke0 or tPeak; the end point is EEG (the cited sources are EEG studies). Figure 4 shows time to peak effect-site concentration after a bolus (0-4 min axis).
- Figure 6 (PDF p6, printed 19S): propofol therapeutic ranges, ug/mL, approximate bars read from the graph: sedation about 0.5-1; TIVA propofol/opioid about 3-5; minor surgery propofol/N2O about 3.5-5; major surgery propofol/N2O about 4-7; TIVA propofol only about 6-9. Plasma/blood assay not stated in the figure. Text says opioid or N2O lowers the propofol need.
- No MEAC, no opioid ke0, no equianalgesic ratio. Opioid audit: none (alfentanil and sufentanil appear only as recovery-curve comparators in Figure 3, with no parameters in this paper).

## Covariates
Age/opioid: elderly and opioid-premedicated patients need about 1 mg/kg induction rather than 1-2.5 mg/kg (text, PDF p3). Infants/children: induction 2.5-3 mg/kg (PDF p5). No equations.

## Caveats
Review, opinion-based dosing, Table 1 unreferenced, population unknown. Scanned, low-quality PDF with a corrupted text layer.
