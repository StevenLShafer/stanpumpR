# Karlsson 1995: three residual error models for population PK/PD

## Study
Simulation study (NONMEM V, first-order method) plus one real data set. Simulation: one-compartment model with first-order absorption, typical CL 10 L/h, V 100 L, ka 1 per h; log-normal IIV with variance 0.3 on each (text of Methods); 16 subjects, 16 sampling times from 0.3 to 24 h; 100 data sets per each of nine conditions (CONT1-3, DUPL, CORR, TIME, SAMPL, BIEXP, ERRAT). Residual error is proportional (exponential), assay-like variance 0.1 throughout. Real data: antihypertensive given orally twice daily, 64 patients, samples at 1-12 h, a second profile in 35 patients; base model from Mandema et al. 1992. Drug is not named.

## Model and parameters
Three residual-error extensions: (1) replication error separate from pure assay error (variance of a common-per-time component plus a per-replicate one); (2) AR(1) serial correlation, corr = exp(-|t1-t2|/tcorr); (3) two-step (or spline) time-dependent error variance. Methods, not PK parameters. Simulation true values are listed above; fitted real-data values are in Table VI, not read.

## Covariates
Real-data model uses height, age, race and hydrochlorothiazide co-therapy on CL/V (from Mandema 1992); not the subject of the paper.

## Caveats
Findings from the text: ignoring replication error does not hurt parameter estimates; ignoring serial correlation inflates IIV and deflates residual variance but leaves fixed effects and SEs roughly unchanged; ignoring time-dependent error inflates ka and its variance, which two-step error fixes. Authors warn that the simulated misspecification was mild, so results should not be read quantitatively. In the real data set, interoccasion variability in CL and V removed most of the autocorrelation (residual correlation at 1 h fell from 0.6 to 0.18 per the text).
