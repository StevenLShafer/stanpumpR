# Atkinson 1989: N-acetylprocainamide (NAPA) kinetics by stable isotope in patients

## Study
- **Design:** simultaneous IV and oral profiling (p183). Each patient got 250 mg ¹³C-labelled NAPA IV, injected
  over 5 min, while swallowing a 500 mg NAPA hydrochloride (acecainide) tablet. The tablets assayed at 467.9 ± 11.1
  mg of NAPA base, the value used for bioavailability.
- **Population:** 5 men with ventricular arrhythmias (Table I, p183), off antiarrhythmics for at least 1 day,
  fasting.

| Patient | Age (y) | Weight (kg) | Hct (%) | CLcr (mL/min) | Cardiac output (L/min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 64 | 97.4 | 47.1 | 79 | 4.9 |
| 2 | 57 | 92.1 | 42.2 | 123 | 4.5 |
| 3 | 65 | 64.8 | 43.4 | 111 | 6.9 |
| 4 | 61 | 113.0 | 41.5 | 79 | 4.3 |
| 5 | 62 | 88.7 | 42.9 | 87 | 3.5 |

  Mean weight 91.2 kg (derived). Concurrent drugs: diltiazem, verapamil, nifedipine, captopril, atenolol, digoxin.
- **Sampling:** plasma at timed intervals and all urine for 12 h (p184).
- **Assay:** GC-MS with chemical ionisation, isotope-specific for NAPA and ¹³C-NAPA, precision within 5% over
  0.25-10 µg/mL (p185).
- **Model:** three-compartment mammillary disposition fitted in SAAM 23 (Fig. 1, p184). Two absorption
  compartments were added: a lag, an apparent dissolution rate ks, ka, and k0 for non-absorptive loss, with %F =
  ka/(ka + k0) × 100. Renal clearance comes from urinary excretion.

Cross-checks (derived):
- Each row's CLR + CLNR equals the printed total, and the volume and clearance means agree with the five
  individual values.
- CLR/CLcr for the five patients is 1.76, 1.80, 1.58, 1.57 and 1.54, mean 1.65, as printed.
- **VT/kg:** the printed mean, 1.45 (also in the abstract and the Discussion), does not equal the mean of its own
  five values, 1.49. The individual VT/weight values (137.8/97.4 = 1.41 and so on) are right, so the printed mean
  is the error.
- **Terminal half-lives** from each patient's Table II parameters (cubic eigenvalues): patients 1-4 give 11.0,
  8.1, 7.5 and 11.0 h, as printed. Patient 5 gives 10.5 h against the printed 10.2. Rounding the inputs within
  their printed precision gives 10.4-10.5 h, so the gap is not rounding.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Ventricular ectopic beats were counted in 5-min intervals (p183). The paper reports no concentration-effect
  analysis.
- NAPA reduced mean arterial pressure by less than 5 mm Hg (p185).
- No therapeutic range is given.

## Covariates
- CLR = 1.67 × CLcr, close to 1.68 in functionally anephric patients (ref 13). The mean ratio equals the
  age-corrected Reidenberg prediction, 1.65 ± 0.04.
- Vc agrees with the expected intravascular space, 13.0 ± 2.5 L, computed as PV × [1 + 2.59 Hct/(1 − Hct)] (eq.
  3, p185; p = 0.26).
- The extent of absorption correlated with the fast intercompartmental clearance (r = 0.89, p = 0.045), but not
  with measured cardiac output (p188).

## Caveats
- Five men, single dose, 12 h of sampling for a drug with a half-life near 10 h. Concomitant cardiovascular drugs.
- The normal-subject comparator (ref 1) was a capsule of recrystallised NAPA. The authors attribute its slow
  absorption phase to crystal size (p188).
- Formation of NAPA from procainamide is not studied.
