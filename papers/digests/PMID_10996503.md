# Gidal 2000: valproate concentration and lamotrigine clearance in developmentally disabled patients

## Study
Retrospective population PK of routine therapeutic-drug-monitoring data (pp. 24-25). All patients with mental retardation or developmental disability and epilepsy who received lamotrigine (LTG) 1995-1999 at one institution (Central Wisconsin Center). Only trough, steady-state levels (stable dose for at least 1 month) were used. n = 62 (Table 1, PDF p4): age 33.6 ± 11.3 y (range 10-61), weight 47.0 ± 9.9 kg, 29 female / 33 male. Medication groups (Table 1): LTG monotherapy 18, LTG+VPA 15, LTG+one inducer 24, LTG+multiple inducers 4, LTG+VPA+inducer 0, LTG+VPA+multiple inducers 1 (the abstract's group sizes differ; see open_questions). LTG dose 369 ± 236 mg/day (50-1200), 8.1 ± 5.9 mg/kg/day (1-36). Measured concentrations (µg/mL, mean ± SD, Table 1): LTG 6.8 ± 3.3 (n = 62), VPA 83.5 ± 20.7 (n = 16), CBZ 8.1 ± 3.4 (n = 16), PHT 12.1 ± 6.1 (n = 10), PB 25.5 ± 9.4 (n = 6). Assay: LTG by HPLC/GC (Lensmeyer 1996); other AEDs by fluorescence polarization. Serum, total concentrations. NONMEM IV, steady-state model Css = (D/τ)/(Cl/F); no bioavailability estimated (oral only). Covariates tested: age, sex, weight, tube feeding, VPA, inducer, multiple inducers, and the VPA, CBZ, PHT and PB concentrations. Funded by Glaxo Wellcome.

## Model and parameters
Observed Cl/F (mean ± SD, p. 25 and abstract): monotherapy 0.69 ± 0.2, with inducer 1.60 ± 0.65, with VPA 0.2 ± 0.05 mL/kg/min.

Final model (PDF pp. 4-5, printed pp. 26-27), confirmed on the rendered page:
- VPA = 0: Cl/F = θ1·WT^0.6 + θ3·IND + θ4·POLY
- VPA > 0: Cl/F = θ2·WT^0.6 + θ3·IND + θ4·POLY

IND and POLY are 0/1 indicators; POLY adds to IND (derived: 70 kg poly-inducer 0.176·70^0.6 + 2.19 + 3.20 = 7.64 L/h = 1.82 mL/min/kg, reproducing Table 4). The paper does not print the units of θ; the Table 4 values and the text's 2.27 L/h at 70 kg are reproduced only with Cl/F in L/h and WT in kg (derived: 0.176 × 70^0.6 = 2.25 L/h = 0.536 mL/min/kg).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD end point; no seizure or toxicity outcome analysed. Not an opioid paper. Interaction "shape": in the 16 patients with VPA levels (Fig. 1, PDF p5; VPA about 55-120 µg/mL), LTG Cl/F was below the monotherapy mean ± SD band at every measured VPA level, and Cl/F did not correlate with VPA concentration (r = 0.118), nor with CBZ (0.117), PHT (0.051) or PB (0.204) concentrations (p. 27). The authors infer the VPA inhibition is maximal within the usual therapeutic range; no data below about 50 µg/mL. They note VPA molar concentration (84 µg/mL = 579 µmol/L) is over 20-fold the LTG concentration (6.8 µg/mL = 27 µmol/L) (p. 29). Table 4's 6 µg/mL target is labelled illustrative; the authors state no definitive LTG therapeutic range exists.

## Covariates
Weight (WT^0.6 on the baseline term, so weight-normalised clearance is higher in light patients), presence of VPA (replaces θ1 with θ2), inducer (additive θ3), multiple inducers (additive θ4). Not retained: age, sex, NG/tube feeding, and the VPA, CBZ, PHT and PB concentrations. Table 2's univariate screen showed continuous weight and continuous age with 0 log-likelihood difference, while dichotomised weight (<50 kg) and age (<30 y) were significant, yet the final model carries continuous WT^0.6 (Table 3 backward step: removing weight raised the objective function by 7).

## Caveats
- Institutionalised, developmentally disabled patients, light for adults (mean 47 kg), including children (ages from 10 y); retrospective TDM troughs; one sample design.
- Only 16 patients on VPA, all at 55-120 µg/mL: the "no concentration effect" claim cannot speak to low VPA concentrations.
- Abstract and Table 1 group sizes disagree (see open_questions); Table 2's continuous-weight null versus the final WT^0.6 term is unexplained.
- θ units not printed; inferred from Table 4.
- Inducers pooled (CBZ, PHT, PB as one indicator).
