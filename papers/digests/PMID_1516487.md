# Galloway 1992: biosynthetic human proinsulin, a review of its development

## Study
Narrative review by Eli Lilly authors (and Indiana University) of all published and unpublished
preclinical and clinical work on recombinant human proinsulin (HPI), from chemistry and receptor
binding to the multicentre trials that were suspended in February 1988. It pools many studies; the
pharmacokinetic numbers come mainly from Lilly Clinical Pharmacology Study 3 (Howey et al., ref 59:
normal volunteers, 0.2 U/kg HPI or neutral regular human insulin (NRI), i.v. and s.c. into the abdomen,
followed for 24 h) and from Zilker et al. (refs 56, 61). n, age and weight of the Howey volunteers are
not given. Assays: mostly equilibrium competitive RIAs; a noncompetitive two-site radiometric assay in
the Zilker work (refs 56, 57). Potency convention: HPI 4 U/mg (0.0376 U/nmol), insulin 28 U/mg
(1 U = 6.25 nmol) (PDF p6, printed 671).

  mean transit time 1.29 h i.v., 3.75 h s.c.; mean absorption time / absorption half-life 2.5 h.
- Renal failure (Zilker et al. 61; 8 dialysis patients vs 8 healthy volunteers, i.v. bolus; see

  (ref 58); HPI 3.0-3.5 at 2550-40,000 pM in normals (ref 42); pork proinsulin 3.7 and 2.7 at 462 and

- Waldhausl et al. (97): HPI metabolic clearance 99-104 mL/m2/min (PDF p14, printed 679; text layer).
- s.c. conversion: 8-10% of an s.c. dose to intermediate forms, none to insulin; <1% after i.v.
  (Table 1 footnote, text p3-4).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- Not an opioid; no ke0, MEAC or analgesic end point.
- Potency: assigned 4 U/mg (0.0376 U/nmol) from the nadir blood glucose of fasted rabbits (Fig. 4);
  estimates from different studies 4 to 5.7 U/mg; multiple-dose clinical data support about 5.7 U/mg
  (0.0536 U/nmol), i.e. potency underestimated by about 40% (Table 8 footnote: 44.0/30.8 = 1.43)
  (PDF p6-9, printed 671-674; Tables 5, 8).
- In vitro: HPI receptor potency 0.56-1.4% of insulin (Table 1); 1-20% depending on method.
- Hepatospecificity (Revers et al., ref 42, glucose clamp, Tables 9-10): at equal concentrations HPI's
  effect on peripheral glucose disposal was 8% of insulin's and on suppression of hepatic glucose
  production 12% of insulin's. Recovery of HGP after stopping a 4-h infusion: 188 ± 11 min (HPI) vs
  55 ± 10 min (insulin) (Fig. 9, ref 84).
- Clamp (Table 7, Study 3, 0.2 U/kg): total glucose infused over 24 h 108 (i.v. HPI), 98.5 (i.v. NRI),
  135 (s.c. HPI), 122 (s.c. NRI) g; time of maximum infusion rate (median) 0.5, 0.33, 10, 4 h.
- Intrasubject CV of response lower for HPI than NPH (Table 13; 11.8% vs 19.3% in NIDDM, ref 69).
- Safety: 6 myocardial infarctions (2 fatal) in HPI patients (8.8%; n about 68, derived:
  6/0.088, the n is garbled in the text layer) vs 0 on NPH in study NPS1-HI (Table 17, PDF p20,
  printed 685; P = 0.018); trials suspended February 1988.

## Covariates
Renal failure: clearance under 40% of normal and half-life about four times longer (Zilker, ref 61);
the authors suggest dose reduction in renal impairment. Injection site altered HPI absorption and
effect (arm slower than abdomen or thigh) but not NPH's (Table 12, ref 72). No weight, age or sex model.

## Caveats
- A sponsor's review pooling unpublished data; the source studies' n and demographics are mostly not given.
- RIA cross-reactivity: immunometric assays read split and des forms as HPI (p4); the Howey urinary
  assay measured HPI plus split (32-33) and des (31,32) forms.
- Internal inconsistency: the text (PDF p5) compares HPI's 1.65 with "2.7-3.7" mL/min/kg called pork
  insulin, but those are Table 3's pork proinsulin values.
- Text says 0.2 U/kg is "~5.3 pmol/kg" (PDF p6); at 0.0376 U/nmol it is 5.32 nmol/kg
  (derived: 0.2 / 0.0376), so "pmol/kg" is a misprint. The same page gives "~5.3 nmol" elsewhere.
- HPI was never marketed.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Proinsulin: no model, no citation. **Not applicable.**
- Independent check of a modelled drug: stanpumpR models s.c. neutral regular human insulin
  (`R/drugs_insulinRegular.R:26`, Tham 2017 via `R/insulinTham.R:167`: V/F 178 L at 80 kg, CL/F 127 L/h
  at a 10 U dose x (Dose/10)^-0.282). The model does not cite this paper. Howey's comparator values
  (Table 4) imply a lower apparent clearance: for an 80 kg adult, CL = 7.72 x 80 = 618 mL/min = 37 L/h,
  and CL/F = 37 / 0.58 = 64 L/h (derived), against Tham's 127 x (16/10)^-0.282 = 111 L/h at the same
  16 U dose (derived). So Howey's implied CL/F is about 0.6x the library's. The methods differ (Howey:
  non-compartmental i.v./s.c. means over 24 h in an unstated number of volunteers, weight not given;
  Tham: pooled population model), and Howey's i.v. V (61.4 L, CV 96%) is a true volume, not a V/F.
  This is context, not a discrepancy in a cited use; the model's header does not discuss it.
