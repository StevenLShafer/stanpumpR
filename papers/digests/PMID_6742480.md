# Gourlay 1984: methadone minimum effective blood concentration after surgery

## Study
- **Patients:** 16 ASA I-II adults at Flinders Medical Centre, Adelaide, with no narcotic in the preceding 24 h
  (Table 1, p20). Age 42.9 ± 12.4 y, weight 73.5 ± 16.8 kg, 13 M and 3 F.
- **Operations:** upper abdominal (cholecystectomy, gastrectomy, highly selective vagotomy, colostomy) or anterior
  spinal fusion. Five of the six vagotomy patients had previously taken cimetidine.
- **Anaesthesia:** thiopental, pancuronium, N2O/O2, with enflurane if needed.
- **Dosing:**
  - Methadone 20 mg IV over 1 min after intubation.
  - In the recovery ward, IV top-ups of 2.5, 5 or 10 mg at least 45 min apart until pain was controlled.
  - Further IV doses on the ward when pain returned.
  - A dose required all three of: spontaneous report of significant pain, unstimulated RR > 10/min, and no
    significant depression of consciousness.
- **Salt or base:** the dose is stated as "methadone" without saying which.
- **Sampling:** whole blood, 1 mL, at 0, 2, 5, 10, 15 and 30 min, then hourly to 8 h, then 4-hourly to 55-56 h
  after the first dose. An extra sample was taken whenever pain returned.
- **Assay:** the method of their ref 14. Sensitivity limit 10 ng/mL of blood; reproducibility 3-5% at 30 ng/mL and
  2-3% at 500 ng/mL.
- **Pain:** 10-cm VAS, hourly between 08:00 and 18:00 on postoperative days 1-2.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
**Opioid audit:**
- **MEC/MEAC:**
  - Value: 57.9 ± 15.2 ng/mL (range 34.5-80.3), whole blood, total methadone. Racemic is implied; enantiomers were
    not separated.
  - Population: 16 postoperative surgical patients.
  - Definition (Methods p21 and footnote): the blood concentration sampled when the patient said pain had increased
    enough to need more analgesia, immediately before a supplementary dose.
  - The authors call it the **minimum effective concentration (MEC)**. The footnote says they had earlier called it
    MEAC (refs 13-14) but changed the term because their patients had slight, rising pain when sampled.
  - So it is a pain-return (MEC-type) value, not a post-titration MEAC.
- **Earlier values:** the paper quotes its earlier single-estimate MECs of 30 ± 11 and 33 ± 12 ng/mL (p20), and
  31.6 ± 11.1 and 32 ± 11 ng/mL (p24).
  - The authors attribute the higher 1984 MEC to more upper-abdominal surgery (87% against 28%).
- **Correlation:** MEC correlated inversely with duration of analgesia (r = -0.84, P < 0.001; Fig. 3).
- **Pain during analgesia:** mean VAS 1.5 ± 1.3 cm (Table 2, p22). The Discussion (p24) gives 1.3 ± 1.2.
- **Side effects:** a 5 mg top-up lowered RR from 16-18 to 12-14 and increased sedation, abating by 30-45 min.
- **Not reported:** no ke0, t½ke0, tPeak or hysteresis analysis, and no equianalgesic or potency ratio.
- **Example patient** (patient 7, Fig. 1): individual MECs 70, 69, 92 and 75 ng/mL. The text and Table 3 both give
  77 ± 10.6 over 4 estimates. The four values average 76.5 (derived), so 77 is a rounding.

## Covariates
None modelled. A higher MEC went with shorter analgesia. Surgical site is offered as the reason for the difference
from the earlier study.

## Caveats
- Small n (16). The concentrations are whole blood, not plasma; no blood/plasma ratio is given.
- Enantiomers were not measured. The salt/base basis of the dose is not stated.
- The MEC is the pain-return concentration, not a steady-state MEAC.
- Minor internal differences:
  - Mean VAS: 1.5 ± 1.3 (Table 2) against 1.3 ± 1.2 (p24).
  - Further ward injections: "range one to four" (p25), while recovery-ward top-ups are 1-3 (Table 2).

## Verification against stanpumpR
**Value: matches.** 60 ng/mL is this paper's 57.9 ng/mL, rounded.
- The 1982 Gourlay study gave about 32 ng/mL. So if Inturrisi's 0.06 µg/mL (see `PMID_3829576.md`) came from
  Gourlay, this 1984 paper is the one it fits. That last point is my inference: Inturrisi's reference list was not
  checked here.

**Label: discrepancy.**
- The paper measured an **MEC**: whole blood sampled when pain returned, immediately before a top-up. The authors
  explicitly withdrew the term MEAC for it (footnote p21).
- The library calls it a MEAC. `inst/help/models/meac.md:5-10` draws exactly this MEC/MEAC distinction and
  relabelled fentanyl's Gourlay 0.63 ng/mL as an MEC (`docs/opioid-review-2026-10-27.md` item 1).
- Methadone's 60 ng/mL is the same kind of quantity but is still called Gourlay's MEAC in `R/drugs_methadone.R:235`,
  `inst/help/drugs/methadone.md:37` and `inst/help/models/meac.md:23`.
- The matrix (whole blood) is not stated anywhere in the repo. The library plots plasma racemic methadone base.

**Citation: cannot verify / incomplete.** The repo should name this paper (Anesthesiology 1984;61:19-26).

**Band 48-120 ng/mL:** a library construct (×0.8 to ×2.0), not from the paper. The paper's patient means span
34.5-80.3.
