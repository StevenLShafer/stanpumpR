# Lowenstein 1988: barbiturate anaesthesia for refractory status epilepticus, 14 patients

## Study
- Design: retrospective review (Jan 1980 - Oct 1985) of 8 patients plus a prospective protocol (from Oct 1985) in 6 patients, University of California San Francisco hospitals (Methods, PDF p. 1, printed 395).
- Population (Table 1, PDF p. 2, printed 396): 14 patients, ages 14-79 y; retrospective 49/49/55/45/28/28/34/55 y, prospective 53, 36, 69, 79, 63 and 14 y (patient 14 is 14 years old). Weights not reported. Refractory status: failed at least IV phenytoin and phenobarbital and present > 2 h.
- Drugs (Table 1): retrospective: thiopental (pts 1, 2, 5), methohexital (pts 3, 4, 8), pentobarbital (pts 6, 7). Prospective: pentobarbital in all 6.
- Prospective protocol (Table 2, PDF p. 4, printed 398): pentobarbital load 15 mg/kg over 1 h, maintenance 1-2 mg/kg/h, extra 5 mg/kg loads (to a maximum of 30 mg/kg in the first 12 h) as needed for seizure control or burst-suppression; stop at 12 h and observe; reinstate for 24 h if seizures recur. Table 2 footnote: the initial maintenance now recommended is 0.5 mg/kg/h, increased by clinical and EEG response.
- Doses actually given, prospective (Table 1): loads 14-19 mg/kg (15 in four); infusion 0.3 (pt 10, renal failure, empirically 25% of protocol) to 2.5 mg/kg/h; first-course durations 11-17 h; patient 14 had further courses of 64 h and 77 h at 1.0-4.0 and 2.0-4.0 mg/kg/h plus boluses totalling 43 mg/kg. Figure 1 labels patient 10's infusion 0.25 mg/kg/hr (Table 1 gives 0.3).
- Assay: serum pentobarbital; method not stated. Sampling opportunistic ("when possible").
- End point: EEG burst-suppression (bursts of mixed-frequency activity on a relatively inactive background) and clinical/EEG seizure control.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End point: EEG burst-suppression; all 6 prospective patients had seizures aborted within 2 h of starting pentobarbital, concurrent with burst-suppression (PDF p. 3, printed 397). Loss of all motor response and brainstem reflexes except the pupillary light reflex.
- Concentration associated with effect: 4.0-21.0 mcg/mL serum during burst-suppression coma (Discussion). The two lowest-level patients (renal failure pt 10; pt 14 at 4.0-8.0) still held burst-suppression. No concentration-effect model, EC50 or ke0.
- Haemodynamics: mean arterial pressure fell in 9 of 14 (median 20 mm Hg, range 15-35); 7 needed low-dose dopamine (p. 2-3).
- Recovery (Table 3): brainstem reflexes 6-24 h, motor activity 1-72 h, commands/communication 2-18 days after stopping.
- Not an opioid paper: no MEAC or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None modelled. Renal failure (pt 10) handled by an empirical 75% cut in maintenance; patient 14 needed more drug (authors presume increased hepatic metabolism).

## Caveats
- Small n (6 prospective with levels; serial levels in 5); mixed retrospective/prospective; no weights; assay not described.
- Levels sampled opportunistically; peak timing unspecified; infusions mostly 11-17 h, shorter than or similar to the 11-23 h half-life, so the 4.0-21.0 range is not a steady-state range (derived: about one half-life of infusion).
- The authors' half-life was shorter than in healthy volunteers (their refs 36, 37: Brodie 1953, Ehrnebo 1974), not against any model.
- Population mostly adult (36-79 y) plus one 14-year-old; Helfer 2026's paediatric model is fitted in children.
