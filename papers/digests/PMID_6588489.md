# Bergan 1984: metronidazole tablets, suppositories and IV, crossover bioavailability

## Study
- Randomised Latin-square crossover, 11 healthy volunteers (3 male, 8 female), mean age 24.6 y (21-32), weight
  59.6 +/- 6.7 kg (48-72); height not given. Two used oral contraceptives; one took one paracetamol-codeine tablet
  (PDF p3-4, printed p46-47).
- Four single doses at >= 1-week intervals, fasting >= 8 h, food at 3 h: two 400 mg tablets (0.8 g) from May & Baker
  (MB) and from Leo Rhodia (LR), each with 100 mL water; 1000 mg suppository (May & Baker); 800 mg IV in 160 mL over
  30 min (printed p46-47). The abstract's "400 mg tablets" is the tablet strength; the dose was 0.8 g (Table 1 header).
- Serum sampled at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, 48 h; urine to 36 h. HPLC (muBondapak C18, tinidazole
  internal standard) for metronidazole and hydroxymetronidazole (OH-M) (printed p47).
- AUTOAN 2/NONLIN, individual fits: one-compartment first-order (with lag) for tablets and suppositories and for OH-M;
  two-compartment for IV metronidazole (automatic selection chose one compartment in 5 of 11) (printed p47-48).
  F = AUC ratio dose-corrected to IV. Mean +/- SD; Wilcoxon tests.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK-PD modelling. Discussion (PDF p16, printed p59): at least 6.3 mg/L inhibits about 90% of common infecting
anaerobes (citing refs 5, 11; a later line says 6.2 mg/L); a large proportion of B. fragilis sensitive to 2-3 mg/L.
Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
None studied.

## Caveats
- Young, mostly female, light (59.6 kg) volunteers; n = 11; height not reported.
- The abstract and the Results disagree on bioavailability (80.4/84.1 vs 92.6/98.5%) and slightly on IV CL, V and t1/2.
- One subject had markedly slow ("flip-flop") tablet absorption and small tablet AUCs (printed p54).
- Two-compartment fit for IV with only one compartment chosen automatically in 5 of 11; k12, k21 SDs large.
- Table 5 is missing from the archived copy; OH-M table mislabelled "Table 1".

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **F = 0.841** (R/drugs_metronidazole.R:163, help :7, users-guide :419): the number is the abstract's second tablet
  figure, transcribed correctly, but the paper's own Results (92.6 and 98.5%), Discussion (93 and 99%) and Table 3
  mean AUCs (0.909 and 0.970, derived) all put tablet F higher. The header's "0.804 and 0.841" (:25) quotes only the
  abstract. **discrepancy** (source internally inconsistent; the repo picked the abstract value without noting the
  conflict). Using ~0.95 instead of 0.841 would raise every oral curve by about 13%. See open_questions.
- **"Paired-route study of conventional tablets"** (header :24-25, help :7): matches (same 11 subjects received IV and
  both commercial tablets).
- **ka claim**: header :26-28 and help :7 say 1.336 /h is "the only published first-order estimate found". This paper
  reports first-order ka for two commercial 400 mg tablets, 3.971 and 3.905 /h, with lag 0.11-0.31 h (Table 3), so the
  statement is wrong. **discrepancy** (descriptive; the value in use is not from this paper).
- **Comparison with the library's IV model (da Silva Neto 2021, not citing this paper)**: Bergan's IV CL 4.13 L/h in
  59.6 kg volunteers is 1.28x the model's 3.22 L/h at 70 kg, and 1.45x the model's 2.85 L/h scaled to 59.6 kg
  (derived: 3.22 x (59.6/70)^0.75). Bergan's Vd,ss 39.9 L (Vd,area 39.1 L) is close to the model's 37.6 L at the
  reference man (derived: 0.556 x 67.56) and larger than the model would give for these lighter subjects. Hence
  Bergan t1/2 6.57 h vs the model's 8.1-8.4 h. Different populations (healthy young vs colorectal-surgery adults
  aged 18-81; sampling from 0.25 h vs from 1 h); the model header does not predict the direction. Bergan's
  two-compartment fit (Vc 27.6 L, alpha 3.2 /h) is consistent with the header's warning that an early distribution
  phase cannot be excluded.
- Units: mg/L = mcg/mL, h -> min handled in code (/60). No unit errors.
