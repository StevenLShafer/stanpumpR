# Bergan 1984: metronidazole PK and bioavailability after tablets, suppositories and IV

## Study
Randomised cross-over in 11 healthy volunteers (3 male), mean age 24.6 y (21-32),
mean weight 59.6 ± 6.7 kg (48-72), all with normal clinical/lab findings (PDF p2/printed
45-46). Four single doses given ≥1 week apart in a Latin-square order, after ≥8 h fast:
400 mg conventional tablets from two producers — May & Baker (MB) and Leo Rhodia (LR) —
dosed as 2×400 mg = 0.8 g; 1000 mg (1.0 g) rectal suppository (May & Baker); and 0.8 g
(160 mL) IV infused over 30 min. Blood sampled at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 36,
48 h; urine over timed intervals to 36 h. Parent metronidazole (M) and the hydroxy
metabolite (OH-M) assayed by HPLC (internal standard tinidazole). Curve fitting by
AUTOAN 2/NONLIN: one-compartment first-order model for all oral/rectal M curves and all
OH-M; two-compartment open model for IV M (automatic model selection gave one compartment
in 5 of 11). Half-life from ln2/kE (one-comp) or ln2/β (two-comp); fraction absorbed f
from the ratio of dose-corrected AUC after oral/rectal vs IV.

## Model and parameters
All values are means ± SD from **Table 3** (PK by dosage form, PDF p10/printed 53) and
**Table 4** (two-compartment IV, same page). Concentrations are mg/L (= mcg/mL), total
serum. 0.8 g doses except the 1.0 g suppository.

IV (0.8 g), Table 3: AUC 198.31 ± 25.19 mg·h/L; elimination rate kE 0.158 ± 0.040 /h;
serum t½ 6.57 ± 0.49 h; Vd,area 39.08 ± 5.01 L; body clearance 4.13 ± 0.50 L/h. (Abstract
rounds these to t½ 6.5 h, Vd 38.1 L, CL 4.09 L/h; body text to t½ 6.6 h, Vd 39.1 L, CL
4.1 L/h, PDF p2 and p7.)

IV two-compartment (Table 4): A 7.11 ± 3.01, B 20.82 ± 2.06 mg/L; α 3.234 ± 2.021 /h;
β 0.107 ± 0.006 /h (t½β = ln2/β = 6.5 h, derived); k12 0.898 ± 0.805 /h; k21 2.166 ±
1.387 /h; Vc 27.64 ± 4.85 L; Vd,ss 39.93 ± 10.83 L. (Vc is ~47% of body weight, PDF p15.)

MB-tablet (0.8 g): lag 0.109 ± 0.137 h; cmax 18.42 ± 2.56 mg/L; tmax 1.25 ± 0.52 h; AUC
180.28 ± 26.79; absorption rate 3.971 ± 2.844 /h; kE 0.210 ± 0.027 /h; t½ 6.10 ± 1.56 h.

LR-tablet (0.8 g): lag 0.310 ± 0.098 h; cmax 17.96 ± 3.94; tmax 1.65 ± 0.63 h; AUC
192.38 ± 29.69; absorption rate 3.905 ± 4.204 /h; kE 0.110 ± 0.028 /h; t½ 6.62 ± 1.53 h.

Suppository (1.0 g): lag 0.842 ± 0.284 h; cmax 10.13 ± 2.82; tmax 4.83 ± 1.16 h; AUC
151.56 ± 42.21; absorption rate 0.577 ± 0.365 /h; kE 0.114 ± 0.037 /h; t½ 6.77 ± 2.46 h.

**Bioavailability — two inconsistent sets in the paper:**
- Abstract (PDF p2/printed 45): tablets 80.4% and 84.1% (= 0.804 and 0.841), suppository
  53.8%. No SD given. MB listed first throughout, so 80.4% = MB, 84.1% = LR.
- Discussion (PDF p11/printed 54), "estimated from the AUC per dose unit relative to IV":
  MB 92.6 ± 14.6%, LR 98.5 ± 14.3%, suppository 61.9 ± 16.0%.
- Table 3 mean AUCs give (derived): MB 180.28/198.31 = 0.909; LR 192.38/198.31 = 0.970;
  suppository (151.56/1.0)/(198.31/0.8) = 0.611.

The abstract set is systematically lower than the body set and is left unexplained; the
paper does not reconcile them. stanpumpR uses the abstract's 0.841 (see open_questions).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No effect-site (ke0) model and no EC50/Emax: metronidazole is an antibacterial and the
paper has no PK/PD link. Relevant susceptibility/antibacterial facts: peak OH-M appears
~8 h after IV (abstract); OH-M "remains antibacterially active," with a serum half-life
2-3× longer than parent (PDF p15/printed 58). Only 9-12% of an oral dose is excreted
unchanged in urine (PDF p15). The discussion (text layer, printed 58-59; not render-
confirmed) cites ≥6.3 mg/L to inhibit ~90% of common pathogens and 2-3 mg/L for
*Bacteroides fragilis*. No MEAC/analgesic concentration and no equianalgesic ratio apply
(not an opioid).

## Covariates
None modelled. Doses are not weight-normalised (fixed 0.8/1.0 g in 59.6 kg adults). Vd,area
and body clearance in Table 3 are flagged "corrected for bioavailability as determined in
each person" (footnote, PDF p10).

## Caveats
Small n (11), young healthy volunteers (mean 24.6 y, 59.6 kg) — not the surgical-
prophylaxis population the library's IV model was built in. Single doses only. The
internal bioavailability inconsistency (abstract 80.4/84.1% vs body 92.6/98.5%) is the
main caveat (see open_questions). Scanned document; all numbers used here were confirmed
on rendered page images. Large SDs on several oral absorption-rate estimates (e.g. LR
absorption 3.905 ± 4.204 /h) reflect one subject with markedly retarded tablet absorption
(flip-flop fitting, PDF p11).

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **F = 0.841 (R/drugs_metronidazole.R:163; app-help:7; header:25).** The paper's abstract
  prints tablet bioavailabilities of 80.4% and 84.1% (PDF p2/printed 45, render-confirmed),
  so 0.804 and 0.841 are a faithful transcription of the abstract and 0.841 is the LR
  tablet. **Matches the abstract.** But the paper's own detailed analysis (discussion, PDF
  p11/printed 54, render-confirmed) gives MB 92.6 ± 14.6% and LR 98.5 ± 14.3%, and Table 3
  mean AUCs imply ~0.909/0.970. The repo therefore uses Bergan's *lowest* bioavailability
  figure, roughly 0.09-0.14 below Bergan's considered estimate. Recorded as an
  open_question [decision]; this is a source-level inconsistency, not a repo transcription
  error. → **partly-verified.**
- **ka 1.336 /h (R/drugs_metronidazole.R:162).** The header attributes this to an
  experimental 150 mg tablet study (PMC9024553), NOT Bergan; Bergan's tablet absorption
  rates are 3.971 (MB) and 3.905 (LR) /h (Table 3). No conflict — Bergan is cited only for
  F. **Matches** (Bergan not the source of ka).
- **IV disposition (da Silva Neto 2021 model).** The library does not use Bergan's IV PK,
  but as an independent check: Bergan's IV clearance 4.13 L/h (absolute, 59.6 kg, young
  healthy) and serum t½ 6.5 h are both faster/shorter than the library's da Silva Neto
  model, whose header predicts t½ 8.4 h at 70 kg in older surgical patients; da Silva Neto's
  CL 3.22 L/h/70kg scales to ~2.83 L/h at 59.6 kg (derived), below Bergan's 4.13 L/h. The
  direction (healthy young volunteers clear faster than surgical patients) is expected and
  the library's header already notes the early-phase uncertainty of its source. No repo
  claim depends on Bergan's disposition. **Cannot verify** against a repo value (uncited),
  noted as a consistency check.
