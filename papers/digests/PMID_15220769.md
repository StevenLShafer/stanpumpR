# Vanluchene 2004: Spectral entropy vs BIS and AAI during propofol

## Study
- Single-centre (Ghent), 10 ASA I patients, 18-45 y, ambulatory gynaecologic/urologic surgery; weight 62.0 ± 3.8 kg, age 37.7 ± 16.2 y, height 165 ± 6.5 cm, 8 men / 2 women (PDF p. 4, printed 37). Excluded: weight <70% or >130% of ideal, neurologic disorder, recent psychoactive drugs incl. alcohol.
- Propofol 1% at a constant 50 mg/min until burst suppression >=80% on BIS or MAP <50 mmHg. No premedication or other drugs; spontaneous ventilation by facemask with O2.
- Ce propofol was calculated by RUGLOOP II using the Schnider three-compartment plus effect-site model, with ke0 set for a time to peak effect of 1.6 min after a bolus (PDF p. 2, printed 35). No blood propofol was assayed.
- Measures recorded every 10 s average: State entropy (SE, 0.8-32 Hz), Response entropy (RE, 0.8-47 Hz), A-Line ARX index v1.5 (AAI, from MLAEP), BIS (version 4.0 XP, 15 s smoothing).
- Analyses: baseline CV over the first 5 s, burst-suppression relation, prediction probability (PK), individualized Spearman correlation, NONMEM V sigmoid Emax population fit (log-normal IIV, additive residual error).

Model: Effect = E0 + (Emax − E0) · Ce^γ / (Ce50^γ + Ce^γ), as printed on PDF p. 4 (printed 37). Table 3 Emax values are negative (about −62 to −88) and E0 + Emax gives plausible floors (SE 8.7, RE 14.7, AAI 8.8, BIS 8.4; derived), so Emax is the change from baseline, whereas the printed formula reads as if Emax were the asymptote. The paper does not reconcile the two (see caveats).

Other tables: Table 1 (baseline CV, PDF p. 5): SE 89.2 ± 1.4, CV 1.610; RE 97.5 ± 1.9, CV 1.955; AAI 73.0 ± 23.1, CV 31.697; BIS 95.6 ± 4.6, CV 4.801 (CV in %, derived check 1.4/89.2 = 1.6%). Table 2 (PDF p. 6): prediction probability median (min-max) SE 0.86 (0.60-0.96), RE 0.89 (0.67-0.96), AAI 0.87 (0.81-0.99), BIS 0.91 (0.72-0.94); individualized Spearman SE −0.841, RE −0.860, AAI −0.869, BIS −0.891 (SE differs from BIS, P<0.05).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: processed EEG indices (SE, RE, BIS) and MLAEP-derived AAI, against calculated (not measured) propofol Ce. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, potency ratio or analgesic end point.
- ke0 / t½ke0: none estimated. The Schnider ke0 was used, tuned to tPeak 1.6 min; no hysteresis analysis, since Ce was assumed correct.
- Ce50 for loss of the index, 4.15-4.92 (units as above) with gamma 2.7-5.3; entropy curves steeper than BIS/AAI. Burst suppression: SE/RE fall monotonically with BSR (quadratic fit R² 0.72 SE, 0.71 RE; Spearman −0.62, −0.63, PDF p. 4); BIS tracks BSR only above 40%.
- RE − SE (EMG contribution) falls to about 0 with rising Ce (Fig. 7).

## Covariates
None examined; 10 subjects.

## Caveats
- Ce is a model prediction (Schnider) with a rapid ramp of 50 mg/min, so Ce is not at steady state against the effect and no PK was measured; Ce50 reflects this particular infusion.
- Ce50 here is the concentration for half-maximal change of the monitor index, not a clinical end point (no responsiveness testing).
- n = 10, 8 men; the authors note a single sigmoid may not suit a wider range.
- Printed Emax sign convention vs the formula (see above) is unclear.
