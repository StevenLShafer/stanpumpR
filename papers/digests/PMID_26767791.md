# Brookfield 2016: one-compartment magnesium in pregnant women, with and without preeclampsia

## Study
Prospective cohort, Lucile Packard Children's Hospital Stanford, Oct 2012 - May 2014 (NCT01709630). Women 18-45 y
prescribed MgSO4.7H2O for preeclampsia, preterm labour or extreme prematurity; dialysis excluded (PDF p1). 151
enrolled, 40 excluded, 111 analysed: 92 preeclamptic (82 preeclampsia, 9 with neuroprotection, 1 with tocolysis) and
19 non-preeclamptic (5 tocolysis, 7 neuroprotection, 7 both) (Figure 1, PDF p3, rendered). 687 maternal Mg levels,
66 umbilical-cord levels (PDF p2). Dosing: 4 g over 20 min then 2 g/h as clinically indicated. Sampling as in Du
2019 (baseline, 0.5, 1, 2, 4 h, every 6 h, then 1-12 h after stopping). Assay Siemens Dimension RxL Max. NONMEM 7.2,
FOCE-I; bootstrap (1000) for CIs.

Table 1 (PDF p3, rendered; printed as mean +/- SD, though the spreads look like SEs): age 29.3 +/- 1.3 y, weight
87.6 +/- 3.8 kg, gestational age 33.7 +/- 0.9 wk, baseline Mg 1.96 +/- 0.04 mg/dL, baseline creatinine 0.80 +/-
0.06 mg/dL, preeclamptic 92 (83%). Not split by group in the table; the text says preeclamptic women were heavier,
delivered later and had higher baseline creatinine (PDF p2).

## Model and parameters
One compartment (2-compartment evaluated, not retained). Weight linear on V, referenced to 70 kg (Appendix A, PDF p8;
the appendix formula is written for CL and V, but Table 2 gives CL in L/h with no weight term and the Results name
weight on V only). Preeclampsia as a dichotomous effect on CL. Additive residual error. Gestational age,
antepartum/postpartum status and creatinine were not significant (PDF p2). Whether the dependent variable was total
Mg or the change from baseline is not stated (Figure 2 plots observed total concentrations, about 1.5-11 mg/dL).

Internal inconsistency: the Results text gives the "residual (unexplained) variability" as 18% (PDF p2), but in
Table 2 18 is the %RSE of the additive residual 0.73. Appendix C (PDF p8-9) gives weight-based loading-dose and
infusion-rate equations for each group (AUC-matched to a 70-kg woman); not used by the repo.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. A minimum serum Mg of 4.8 mg/dL (cited) is used for simulation; preeclamptic women over 100 kg took
about twice as long to reach it (Figure 4, PDF p5). No ke0.

## Covariates
Weight on V (linear, 70 kg); preeclampsia on CL (5.88 vs 3.98 L/h). Creatinine not significant, which the authors
attribute most likely to creatinine being drawn only as standard of care (PDF p6).

## Caveats
Only 19 non-preeclamptic women; IIV on non-preeclamptic CL 3.7% looks implausibly small. Baseline handling not
described. Weight at admission only. Table 1 spreads are probably SEs though labelled SD.
