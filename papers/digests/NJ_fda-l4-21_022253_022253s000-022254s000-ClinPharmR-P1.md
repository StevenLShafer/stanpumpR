# FDA 2008: Clinical pharmacology review of lacosamide (NDA 22-253/22-254), part 1

## Study
Not a study: the Office of Clinical Pharmacology (DCP2) review of the original lacosamide NDAs, covering more than 30 in-vivo PK studies and in-vitro reports. The DCP2 reviewers were reviewing the diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) NDA together with the epilepsy NDA 22-253. Population PK and exposure-response were reviewed separately by Pharmacometrics (Dr. Hao Zhu, dated June 5, 2008). IV/oral bioequivalence (SP645, SP657, SP658), IV dose proportionality (SP834), CYP2C19 (SP643) and the carbamazepine/valproate studies were reviewed by DCP1 (Dr. Tandon, June 10, 2008). None of those reviews is in this file (summary table, PDF pp 8-9). Large parts are redacted under b(4), including the DPN exposure-response text (section 2.2.3 and Figure 2.2.3.1) and the PM summary paragraph (p 13).

## Model and parameters
Non-compartmental values only. No population model and no compartmental parameters appear in this part.

- Tmax 0.5-4 h oral; half-life about 13 h; steady state after 3 days on twice-daily dosing; absolute bioavailability about 100%; Vd about 40-60 L after IV and oral dosing (total body water); protein binding under 15% (Summary p 5; 2.2.9.1-2.2.9.2 p 28).
- Accumulation factor about 2.3 on twice-daily dosing, consistent with a one-compartment model and t½ 13 h (2.2.8.3 p 25).
- Renal clearance of unchanged drug about 2 L/h, below GFR, so there is net tubular reabsorption (p 6, p 31).
- SP619 mass balance (100 mg [14C], n=5 oral and n=5 IV, healthy males): about 95% of radioactivity in urine, under 0.5% in faeces. Table 2.2.9.4.2 gives urine 96.8 ± 2.6% IV and 94.2 ± 3.1% oral. Urine (median % of sample radioactivity, Table 2.2.9.3.1): unchanged LCM 33.9 oral / 39.7 IV, SPM 12809 (O-desmethyl, inactive) 31.8 / 30.0, polar fraction 18.1 / 19.6. At steady state 30-40% of the dose is excreted unchanged and 20-30% as SPM 12809 (p 31).
- SP619 total radioactivity in plasma (Table 1, review p 142): AUC0-inf 62.22 (oral) vs 71.78 (IV) µg eq·h/mL, Cmax 3.362 vs 3.384, t½ 15.04 vs 16.26 h, tmax 0.50 vs 1.00 h. Oral exposure was 86.7% of IV in plasma.

Single doses in healthy males (SP835, Table 2.2.8.1.1, medians): at 100/200/400/600 mg, Cmax was 2.26/4.55/8.63/11.50 µg/mL, AUC0-inf 45.43/94.31/195.48/297.70 µg·h/mL, t½ 13.31/12.93/12.36/14.99 h and tmax 2-3 h. Steady state (Table 2.2.8.1.3): on 200 mg bid, Ctrough was 5.70 ± 0.83 (SP603) and 5.58 ± 1.07 (SP661) µg/mL, and Cmax,ss 9.78 and 11.85 µg/mL. Troughs on 200 mg bid were 5.8 ± 2.4 µg/mL in epilepsy (SP586) and 7.7 ± 3.0 and 5.7 ± 2.6 µg/mL in neuropathic pain (Table 2.2.8.5.1). Plasma concentrations in trials of 200-600 mg/day ran from about 5 to 20 µg/mL, or 20-80 µM (review p 114). SPM 12809 exposure is about 10% of the parent (Table 2.2.8.4.3: 7-15%), with t½ 15-23 h.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No exposure-response model or coefficients appear in this part. The DPN exposure-response analysis (responder analysis, Figure 2.2.3.1) is redacted, and the review refers to the PM review. Dizziness, tremor, balance disorder and coordination abnormality rose with dose; dizziness was 5.2% on placebo vs 7.3/13.6/25.3% on 200/400/600 mg/day (Table 2.2.4.1, DPN pool). For the thorough QT study (SP640, 400 and 800 mg/day), ΔΔQTcI at Tmax on day 6 was -9.4 ms and -7.4 ms. Not an opioid: no MEAC, ke0 or tPeak.

## Covariates
- Renal impairment (SP641, 100 mg single dose, n=8 per group, Table 1 and Table 2.3.2.4.1). The AUC ratio vs CLcr > 80 mL/min was 1.27 for mild (50-80), 1.22 for moderate (30-50) and 1.59 for severe (< 30). Cmax was little changed. CL/F was 2.13, 1.68, 1.74 and 1.34 L/h, and t½ 13.2, 18.2, 15.4 and 18.3 h, across healthy, mild, moderate and severe. CLR in this table is printed as 0.59/0.35/0.28/0.14 "L/h", which conflicts with the text's ~2 L/h; the unit or period is probably mislabelled. Hemodialysis for 4 h starting 2.5 h after the dose lowered AUC to 0.54 of a dialysis-free day.
- Hepatic impairment, Child-Pugh B: AUC ratio 1.61, Cmax ratio 1.50.
- Age and sex (SP620): AUC ratio 1.33 for elderly vs young males and 1.13 for elderly females vs elderly males. The sex difference largely disappears after weight normalisation. The population PK analysis found no age or sex effect in partial-seizure patients.
- Race and CYP2C19 genotype: no clinically relevant effect on lacosamide.
- Carbamazepine, phenytoin or phenobarbital: about 20% lower exposure in population PK. In the healthy-subject carbamazepine study, lacosamide AUC ratio was 1.011 (Table 3).

## Caveats
This is part 1 of a multi-part review with heavy b(4) redaction. The population PK and exposure-response reviews (Pharmacometrics) and the IV bioequivalence reviews (DCP1) are cited but not included. The review is mostly about DPN. Most PK studies were in small groups of healthy male Caucasians. The proposed doses (up to 600 mg/day) exceed the approved maximum of 400 mg/day.
