# Gueorguieva 2008: IL-1Ra (anakinra) plasma and CSF PK in SAH patients

## Study
Open phase I PK study in 8 patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage (6 F, 2 M; White; mean age 51 y, range 39-69; mean weight 70 kg, printed range "64-150" kg) with an external ventricular drain; 7 analysed (patient F excluded). Dose: 100 mg IV bolus, then 2 mg/kg/h IV for 24 h (Kineret); one renally impaired patient (creatinine 177+ umol/L, CrCl 47 mL/min) received 1 mg/kg/h and was analysed separately. Plasma (5 mL) and CSF (3 mL) sampled during and after the infusion; IL-1Ra by ELISA (total, not free). NONMEM, ADVAN 9, population (mixed-effects) fit; plasma fitted first, parameters fixed, then CSF. Model: two-compartment plasma, elimination from central; one-compartment CSF fed from plasma, no return to plasma (Fig. 1, Appendix p. 324). V_CSF is not identifiable: only rate constants kin,CSF = P/V_CSF and kout,CSF = CL_CSF/V_CSF are estimated.

Derived by authors (p. 323): with V_CSF assumed 150 mL, P = 0.29 mL/h and CL_CSF = 0.3 mL/min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model, no ke0, no EC50. Reported concentrations: CSF at 24 h (approximate steady state) mean 358 ng/mL (range 115-886), CSF:plasma ratio 0.01-0.04 (about 25-100-fold gradient). Authors compare with rat neuroprotective CSF levels of 91-232 ng/mL (refs 25, 26). No MEAC; no equianalgesic ratio (not an opioid).

## Covariates
Renal function: the one impaired patient had CL 1.41 L/h vs 6.96 (about 0.2x); Vss similar (8.25 vs 10.1 L). Authors cite Yang 2003 healthy CL 8.2 vs 1.2 L/h in renal impairment, Vss 9.8 vs 8.7 L. Not included as a covariate (n=1). No weight or age covariate tested.

## Caveats
Seven patients; SAH patients with possibly leaky BBB; single dose regimen; CSF drug assumed not to return to plasma; V_CSF unidentifiable; ELISA measures endogenous IL-1Ra too (baseline not modelled here; Ogungbenro 2016 adds it); between-subject variances have large RSEs.

## Used by stanpumpR
**Background only.** Its 6 patients are part of the 31 of Ogungbenro 2016 (PMID 26476629), whose
updated fit `R/drugs_anakinra.R` uses for intravenous doses since #984. This paper is not the
source of any parameter; its value to the repository is that its demographics are **the only ones
printed for that cohort**, which is what the model's `BODY SIZE ANCHOR` paragraph rests on.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- Cited locations: none for a parameter; `R/drugs_anakinra.R`'s BODY SIZE ANCHOR depends on this paper's demographics (see above).
- The comparison below was made against the model BEFORE #984, when it had only Urien 2013's subcutaneous route. It stands as an independent check of that route, not of the intravenous one, which now comes from Ogungbenro 2016 and so cannot be checked against its own source.
- Independent comparison with R/drugs_anakinra.R:85-89 and :104-105 (CL = 0.847*W^0.47 L/h, V = 2.581*W^0.76 L, ka 0.38/h; header lines 65-75 give 6.24 L/h and 65.2 L at 70 kg): clearance here 6.96 L/h (IV, absolute) is 1.12x the model's apparent SC 6.24 L/h at 70 kg, i.e. the model runs 10% lower. This agrees in direction with the header's note that Yang 2003 healthy adults (170 mL/min) are higher than the model. The volume differs far more: Vss 10.1 L here vs V/F 65.2 L in the model (about 6x); the model's one-compartment SC volume is apparent, so the elimination half-life (model 7.2 h at 70 kg) is longer than the 5.3-6 h terminal half-life measured here (consistent with the header's label comparison). Verdict: **partly-verified** (clearance agrees within 10%; volume cannot be reconciled, different route and structure). See open_questions.
