# Schepers 2003: oral sustained-release methamphetamine in plasma and oral fluid

## Study
NIDA intramural closed-ward study. 8 adults (4 M, 4 F), age 35.3 ± 4.2 y (26.3-39.8), height 171.5 ± 9.8 cm,
weight 72.0 ± 17.6 kg (54.7-103.2) (p. 124, PDF p. 4). All 8 received four oral 10-mg S-(+)-methamphetamine HCl
doses within 7 days; 3 weeks later 5 of them received four 20-mg doses. Formulation: Desoxyn Gradumet 10-mg
**sustained-release** tablets (one or two) inside a lactose-filled gelatin capsule; the label's dissolution data are
quoted as 20-40% released at 30 min, 35-55% at 1 h, 60-80% at 4 h (p. 122). Doses are stated as the hydrochloride
salt. Plasma (sodium heparin, NaF) drawn up to 24 h in the first session of each dose level only; oral fluid to 72 h,
by three collection methods (citric acid candy, citric-acid Salivette, neutral Salivette). Assay: SPE + GC-MS,
LOD = LOQ 2.5 µg/L for METH and AMP. PK by WinNonlin noncompartmental analysis; individual plasma data also fitted
to a one-compartment model with lag, first-order input and output (p. 124). Pharmacodynamics: BP, HR, RR, SpO2,
temperatures, pupil diameter, to 11.5 h, first low and high sessions and a placebo session.

## Model and parameters

Means ± SD (range). 10 mg: n = 7 for plasma (no plasma from volunteer Z; the header says n = 8). 20 mg: n = 5.

Oral fluid METH (Table 1, citric acid candy): cmax 106.1 ± 100.8 µg/L (10 mg), 192.2 ± 120.8 (20 mg); t1/2 7.1 ± 2.3
and 8.1 ± 1.9 h; AUC0-24h 1118.0 ± 983.4 and 1874.9 ± 1237.6 h·µg/L. The 20-mg range column for oral fluid is
misaligned in print (the "5.5-10.5" range sits on the AUC row and "673.8-3891.0" on the Vd row; they belong one row up).

One-compartment fit: the Results (p. 125) print K01 = 1.07 ± 0.60 "h" and K10 = 10.76 ± 4.20 "h" (n = 13, CV < 42%),
but the Discussion (p. 128) gives K01 = 0.93 ± 0.61 h^-1 and K10 = 0.085 ± 0.067 h^-1 (n = 13). The two statements
disagree (Results confirmed on the rendered page; Discussion from the text layer only); the Results values look like half-lives mislabelled (derived: ln2/0.085 = 8.2 h), but the paper does not
say. Neither is used by stanpumpR.

Other findings: plasma AUC 2.1 ± 0.7-fold higher after 20 than 10 mg (P = 0.009); plasma AMP AUC 21% ± 25% of METH
(molar); oral fluid/plasma (S-P) ratio median 2.0 (range 0.0-23.0, n = 67), R² = 0.222; overall plasma METH t1/2
10.0 ± 5.2 h and overall tmax 6.3 ± 3.0 h (p. 128). Table 3 (p. 130) compiles other studies' oral, inhaled and IV
METH tmax/cmax/t1/2 (e.g. Cook capsule 0.125 mg/kg: tmax 3.3 ± 0.9 h, cmax 20.1 ± 7.0 µg/L, t1/2 9.1 ± 2.2 h).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Not an opioid. No ke0, tPeak, EC50 or effect-concentration model. AUC of time-effect curves (12 h): only the 20-mg
dose raised diastolic BP (10.1%, P = 0.003) and heart rate (21.7%, P = 0.036) versus placebo (Fig. 3, p. 129);
systolic BP, temperatures, SpO2 and pupil diameter did not differ from placebo. Skin temperature fell for 2 h.

## Covariates
None modelled. Plasma AUC was dose dependent; t1/2 was not.

## Caveats
Small n (7 and 5 for plasma), plasma sampled only to 24 h for a drug with a ~10 h half-life (t1/2 and CL/F from
24-h data are uncertain); sustained-release formulation, so tmax, cmax and K01 describe the Gradumet product, not an
immediate-release dose. Doses are METH·HCl; whether CL/F uses salt or base mass is not stated (base fraction ≈ 0.80,
derived from molar masses). Internal inconsistency in K01/K10 between Results and Discussion. Concurrent multiple
dosing (four doses in 7 days) means session-one plasma is single-dose, later sessions only 24-h points.
