# Fay 2021: bupropion and metabolites in pregnancy versus postpartum

## Study
Prospective paired study, University of Washington. 8 pregnant women taking bupropion chronically for therapeutic reasons (age 28-42 y per Table 1 text layer; regimens 150-450 mg, mostly XL, one SR, one IR BID; several with diabetes, two smokers). One steady-state plasma sample plus dosing-interval urine per trimester (T2, T3) and at 6-12 weeks postpartum (PP). T2 samples from 6, T3 from 6, postpartum from all 8; labour-and-delivery maternal plus umbilical cord venous plasma from 3 (p. 783). Sampling 1.5-7 h after a morning dose or 11-15 h after an evening dose; a single draw was taken to represent average Css (extended-release formulation, long half-lives). Plasma and urine assayed by LC-MS/MS (racemic and chiral; urine after acid deconjugation for total metabolite). Subjects genotyped for CYP2B6 and CYP2C19 variants. Statistics: geometric mean (range), paired t test on log values; subject 7 (regimen changed postpartum) excluded from the statistics. Power: 7 subjects for 90% power to detect a 50% fall in BUP concentration (p. 783).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model. Non-compartmental quantities (Table 2, PDF page 6, printed p. 785; geometric mean (range), n = 5 pairs in each comparison):
- Css is dose-normalised, umol/L per gram of dose per interval (printed "uM/g"); CLR and CLf in L/h (unit as printed, not per kg).
- Formation clearance CLf = total metabolite recovered in urine after deconjugation / Css of its parent; CLR = Ae unchanged / Css.
- Racemic BUP Css (uM/g): T2 0.33 vs PP 0.53 (P 0.4787); T3 0.44 vs PP 0.41 (P 0.8056).
- OH-BUP Css: T2 15.1 vs 16.3 (0.7839); T3 16.5 vs 13.3 (0.6896). OH-BUP CLf (L/h): T2 50.7 vs 13.4 (0.1619); T3 24.9 vs 15.9 (0.228).
- Threo Css: T2 3.4 vs 4.1; T3 4.9 vs 4.7. Erythro Css: T2 0.49 vs 0.67; T3 0.63 vs 0.80. All P > 0.2.
- Threo CLf: T2 89.4 vs 34.5 (0.2477); T3 56.6 vs 68.5 (0.5949). Erythro-OH CLf: T2 10.8 vs 5.1 (P = 0.025); T3 9.4 vs 7.6 (0.556) -- the only P < 0.05 among pregnancy-versus-postpartum rows.
- Metabolite/parent Css ratios (T2 vs PP; T3 vs PP): Threo/BUP 10.3 vs 7.8, 11.2 vs 11.5; Erythro/BUP 1.5 vs 1.3, 1.4 vs 1.9; RR-OH-BUP/BUP 43.8 vs 32.5, 33.7 vs 35.6; SS-OH-BUP/BUP 2.2 vs 1.5, 1.9 vs 1.6; none significant.
- Creatinine clearance (text, p. 783): 153.6 +/- 49.3 mL/min T2, 145.0 +/- 54.1 T3, 117.7 +/- 48.4 PP; T2 vs PP P = 0.0436, T3 vs PP P = 0.5693.
- Cord venous / maternal plasma ratio (Figure 2, PDF p. 6; n = 3, mean +/- SE): BUP 0.5 +/- 0.1, S,S-OH-BUP 0.6 +/- 0.2, R,R-OH-BUP 0.4 +/- 0.1, Threo 0.7 +/- 0.1, Erythro 0.6 +/- 0.1.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None measured. No concentration-effect data, no MEAC, ke0, hysteresis or potency ratio (not an opioid). The paper states, from other work, that efficacy and side effects of bupropion correlate with parent and active-metabolite concentrations (p. 780).

## Covariates
Pregnancy (trimester versus postpartum) only. CYP2B6 and CYP2C19 genotypes are tabulated (Table 1) but not analysed as covariates. Authors note T3 samples were all taken at 34-35 weeks.

## Caveats
n = 8 enrolled, 5 pairs per comparison, wide ranges; the authors say the power covers a 50% change and cannot exclude a less-than-2-fold change in CLf, nor CYP2B6 induction. Single-draw Css assumption. Mixed formulations (XL, SR, IR BID) and regimens, comorbidities (diabetes, renal insufficiency in one subject). R,R/S,S-OH-BUP ratio (14-20) lower than earlier volunteer reports (40-65). Cord data from 3 subjects. The abstract's "no significant" findings sit beside one P = 0.025 (Erythro-OH CLf, T2) uncorrected for multiplicity (about 40 comparisons).
