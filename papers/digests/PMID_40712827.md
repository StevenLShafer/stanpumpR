# Li 2025: zonisamide PBPK in pregnancy, fetus, postpartum and neonate

## Study
Simulation study, no new subjects. PBPK in PK-Sim/MoBi 11 (11.3): an 18-compartment whole-body nonpregnant model,
extended in MoBi with 9 pregnancy compartments, 3 postpartum (breast) compartments, and scaled to neonates in the first
week. Elimination by CYP3A4 (Michaelis-Menten via Kcat) and NAT1, plus renal first-order (fGFR); binding to red-cell
carbonic anhydrase I and II modelled as binding partners. Fitted to digitised literature concentration-time data
(training and validation sets, Figs S4 and 1); pregnancy validated against steady-state troughs (Kondo 1996, Oles 2008,
Reimers 2018, Kawada 2002; Fig. 2A-H). Virtual populations of 100: nonpregnant women 18-40 y (baseline), first (1-12 wk),
second (13-26 wk) and third (27-38 wk) trimester, postpartum week 1, neonates. Baseline dose 300 mg once daily. NCA by
the R package ncappc.

Pregnancy changes (Table 2, PDF p3): fu from albumin by Eqs 3-4 (albumin P = 14.7 exp(-0.0454 x) + 31.7, x gestational
week; KA constant); CYP3A4 Kcat per Zheng 2021; maternal NAT1 unchanged; placental NAT1 expression 0, 4.76, 25, 108%.
Postpartum week 1 (Table 3, PDF p4): CYP3A4 and NAT1 as in the third trimester; fu,milk 0.95; Kmilk/plasma 0.93; plasma protein scaling 0.96;
Kcat CYP3A4 0.74, NAT1 0.03 L/min. Neonate: CYP3A4 at 13% of adult, CA at one sixth.

Fit: nonpregnant 80.46% within 1.25-fold, 98.56% within 2-fold; pregnancy 38.1% and 95.24%; fetal/cord 75% and 100%;
postpartum plasma and milk 85% and 100% (PDF p4-6). Simulated cord/maternal ratio 0.98 (p9). Postpartum (3rd-trimester
575 mg carried on) exposure 1.25 x baseline; 475 mg (1.58 x) restores it; 3rd-trimester 450 mg group 0.95 x. Neonatal
relative infant dose 30-33% in week 1 (Table S7, not seen); neonatal levels fall below 10 ug/mL on day 4 (9.57) or day 2
(9.63) without breastfeeding.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PD model. The dosing target is the TDM minimum effective concentration of 10 ug/mL (trough; ref 44, Verdier 2010),
and baseline exposure at 300 mg QD. Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio.

## Covariates
Gestational week (albumin-driven fu, CYP3A4 Kcat by trimester, placental NAT1); postpartum and neonatal physiology as
above. Body size by PK-Sim population defaults (not stated numerically).

## Caveats
Pregnancy validation rests on troughs only, several panels from one or two women; the authors note the pregnancy model
may underestimate. CA I/II assumed unchanged in pregnancy; maternal NAT1 assumed unchanged. Postpartum restricted to
week 1. The Discussion (PDF p8) gives the MED multipliers as 0.92, 1.14 and 1.5; the results, abstract and Fig. 2N doses
(275/350/450 mg against 300) give 0.92, 1.17, 1.5, so 1.14 is a misprint. Fig. 2N's own baseline box is 225 mg QD.
Supplementary tables (S1-S7) were not available.

## Verification against stanpumpR
- **Citation: matches** (authors, journal, volume, article number 103912, DOI).
- **1.92: matches**, with a wording note. The 1.92 is the dose multiplier (575 vs 300 mg) that brings simulated
  third-trimester AUCss back near baseline (PDF p6, Fig. 2M), which the registry describes exactly. The R header's
  "1.92-fold change" is that dose multiplier; the simulated exposure ratio itself is 0.48, i.e. a 2.08-fold apparent
  clearance change (derived: 1/0.48). The two differ because 575 mg slightly overshoots in Fig. 2M; neither is used.
- **Not used: matches.** `ZONISAMIDE_PREGNANCY_CL_FACTOR` is 1.6 (R/drugs_zonisamide.R:81) from Reimers and MONEAD;
  the PBPK value enters nowhere in the code. For comparison, Li's third-trimester prediction (exposure 0.48 x, about
  2.1 x clearance) runs above the library's 1.6 and above Reimers' nadir (1/0.551 = 1.81); the comparator is Li's own
  virtual nonpregnant women, not the library's Silva model.
- **Nonpregnant clearance (uncited comparison).** The PBPK input plasma clearance is 0.35 mL/min/kg from the label
  (Table 1; about 1.47 L/h at 70 kg, derived). The library's Silva 2025 CL/F is 0.761 L/h (R/drugs_zonisamide.R:15,
  :102), about 0.18 mL/min/kg at 70 kg (derived), half the label figure. The header already notes Kochak's healthy-adult
  CL/F of 0.60-0.71 L/h, closer to Silva, so this paper does not settle it; the label's 0.35 is only an input here.
- **Band.** The 10 ug/mL minimum effective trough used here matches the lower edge of the library's band (10-40,

- **Protein binding.** fu 0.60 (40% bound) agrees with the registry note and the header's "about 40% bound" (:57).
