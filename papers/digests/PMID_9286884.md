# Kazama 1997: fentanyl reduction of propofol Cp50 for various stimuli

## Study
133 non-premedicated adults, 20-55 y, ASA I-II, BMI <=30, lower-abdominal surgery (Hamamatsu). Randomized to fentanyl target 0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 or 4.0 ng/mL (5 groups) and to a low (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9 ug/mL) or high (12-26 ug/mL) propofol series. Fentanyl infusion started 20 min before propofol; propofol held 10 min at each step, then stimuli in order: verbal command (with shoulder rub), 50 Hz / 80 mA 10 s tetanus, laryngoscopy, laryngoscopy plus intubation; skin incision once per patient. Response = gross purposeful movement; patients not paralyzed. Probit analysis gave Cp50 and Cp95 (propofol plasma concentration with 50% / 95% probability of no response).
Delivery: home-built computer-assisted infusion with Gepts 1987 propofol PK, corrected by a linear regression from a 12-patient pilot because measured exceeded predicted (r 0.84); fentanyl PK from McClain and Hug. Assays: propofol plasma HPLC-fluorescence (LOD 19 ng/mL, CV 8.2%); fentanyl plasma GC-MS (LOD 0.2 ng/mL), sampled at 20 and 90 min. Mean / absolute prediction error: propofol -4.1% / 20.2%; fentanyl 33.4% / 41.3% (PDF p7).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit (fentanyl): no MEAC or analgesic concentration; no ke0, t1/2 ke0, tPeak or hysteresis measured (the authors assumed 20 min of constant fentanyl target ensured plasma-effect-site equilibration, PDF p13). No equianalgesic ratio. Fentanyl exposure is plasma concentration (target, assay-checked at 20 and 90 min), not effect site.
Fentanyl reduction of the propofol Cp50 (percent, from the text, PDF p7-8): at 1 ng/mL 11 / 34 / 31 / 32 / 32% for verbal command / tetanus / laryngoscopy / intubation / skin incision; at 3 ng/mL 17 / 51 / 50 / 51 / 55%. Above 3 ng/mL the further reduction was smaller (ceiling). Cp50 order: verbal command < tetanus = laryngoscopy = skin incision < intubation; intubation to skin-incision Cp50 ratio 1.7-1.9.
Cited comparators (Discussion p13-14): Smith 1994 reported Cp50 for LOC 3.3 and skin incision 15.2 ug/mL (whole blood), with 63% / 89% reductions at 1 / 3 ng/mL fentanyl; this paper's 32% / 55% are smaller (authors suggest the incision site).
Hemodynamics: the systolic BP rise after stimulation was attenuated mainly by fentanyl concentration (multiple regression, Table 4, not in the PDF); propofol alone did not blunt it.

## Covariates
None modeled beyond fentanyl concentration.

## Caveats
Propofol concentration is a model-predicted, regression-corrected target checked against plasma assay (plasma, not whole blood). Prediction errors were large (propofol 20% absolute, fentanyl 41% absolute). Sequential stimuli in the same patient; 12 hypotensive patients excluded from part of the analysis; single center, ages 20-55. Tables 1-4 are not in this PDF.
