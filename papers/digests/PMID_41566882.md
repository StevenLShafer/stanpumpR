# Retout 2026: baloxavir popPK in children and adults, exposure matching

## Study
Pooled popPK of baloxavir acid (active metabolite of the prodrug baloxavir marboxil) from 6 studies (T0821, CAPSTONE-1, CAPSTONE-2, miniSTONE-2, T0822, T0833): 1,795 patients, 6,399 plasma concentrations (PDF p3, printed 1049). Ages 1 to adult (n = 104 aged <6 y, 141 aged 6-<12 y, 1,550 aged ≥12 y; p9). BLOCKSTONE (PEP, 370 PK participants, 754 concentrations) and T0835 were excluded from model building and handled by Bayesian post hoc estimates. Single oral dose: 2 mg/kg (<20 kg), 40 mg (≥20-<80 kg), 80 mg (≥80 kg); Japanese paediatric studies used lower doses (10 mg for 10-<20 kg, 20 mg for 20-<40 kg). Assay: LC-MS/MS plasma baloxavir, LLOQ 0.1 ng/mL (0.5 ng/mL in miniSTONE-2); 6% of data were below LLOQ and omitted. NONMEM 7.4.0, SAEM; full covariate model after GAM screening. Relative bioavailability fixed at 1.00 except the 10 mg tablet in T0822 (0.88).

## Model and parameters
Two-compartment, first-order absorption with lag time, first-order elimination. Table 1 (PDF p4, printed 1050; confirmed on the rendered page). Values are oral apparent (per F); parameters are absolute, not per kg, for a 70 kg reference.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD (effect-site) model, no ke0, no EC50. Not an opioid. Exploratory exposure-response only: time to alleviation of influenza signs and symptoms (TTASS, CARIFS-based) in miniSTONE-2 by low vs high AUC0-inf (median split; low AUC 747-4,573, high 4,604-12,553 ng·h/mL; n=37 and 36), curves overlapped (Figure 3, p8). PEP failures were comparable across quartiles of AUC0-inf and C240 in BLOCKSTONE (Table S7, not in the PDF). The target exposure was the adult 5th-95th percentile of simulated exposure; C240 (240 h) was used to match PEP exposure. Highest concentration in a thorough QT study (T0816) 478 ng/mL, no QTc effect (data on file; p7).

## Covariates
Bodyweight (allometric, exponents above, reference 70 kg) on CL/F, Q/F, Vc/F, Vp/F; race (Asian) lowers CL/F, Vc/F and Q/F; sex (female) lowers ka; age raises ka. Patient type (high risk vs otherwise healthy) had no effect. Same dose gave about 2x higher exposure in Asian than non-Asian patients of equal weight (p5). The comparator for "Asian lower" is non-Asian patients in the same pooled model. Half-life is shorter in lighter patients because CL scales less than volume (p3, p6). Clearance maturation was not modelled (p9).

## Caveats
Race effect mechanism unknown (AADAC genotype does not explain it, per the authors). Tlag very poorly estimated (shrinkage 92%). No patients under 1 year (separate model in development). Vp/F IIV fixed. Exposure matching rather than a direct efficacy trial for Asian children and for PEP. Supplement not available here. Adult comparison: previous adult model (Koshimichi 2020) CL/F 10.8 L/h vs 11.0 here, BW exponent on CL 0.362 vs 0.467; Japanese paediatric model exponent 0.632 (p9-10).
