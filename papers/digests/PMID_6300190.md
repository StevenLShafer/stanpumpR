# Adrogué 1983: bicarbonate space in chronic acid-base disturbances (dog)

## Study
- 53 acute infusion studies in 36 unanesthetized female mongrel dogs weighing 10.2-18.2 kg (PDF p2, printed 868). The dogs were kept on a synthetic diet.
- Five groups, each brought to a chronic steady state before the infusion:
  - metabolic acidosis: HCl or lysine-HCl, 2-8 mmol/kg/day for at least 7 days; 9 dogs, 15 studies
  - respiratory alkalosis: 9% O2 chamber for at least 7 days; 7 dogs, 8 studies
  - respiratory acidosis: 8 or 11% CO2 chamber; 7 dogs, 9 studies
  - metabolic alkalosis: ethacrynic acid and a chloride-free diet; 13 dogs, 17 studies
  - normal acid-base status: 4 dogs, 4 studies
- Dose: NaHCO3 5 mmol/kg IV, as a 1 N solution over 10 min (PDF p3, printed 869).
- Sampling: arterial blood at 30, 60 and 90 min from the infusion midpoint. Urine was collected in 30-min periods.
- ECF volume was measured by radiosulfate space, and its change by chloride-space arithmetic.
- Bicarbonate was corrected for the rise in PaCO2: 0.18 meq/L per mmHg (0.26 in respiratory alkalosis). The correction was never more than 1.3 meq/L.
- Apparent bicarbonate space = retained bicarbonate (dose − urinary excretion) / Δ[HCO3−] extracellular, as % body weight.
- Retained bicarbonate was split into the following parts:
  - in the ECF
  - titrated by plasma protein (0.11 mmol/g/pH)
  - in red cells (0.5 × plasma)
  - titrated by hemoglobin (60 mmol/L RBC/pH)
  - the remainder, labelled "intracellular" (soft tissue and bone)

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- The end points are physiological: change in plasma pH, the apparent bicarbonate space, and how retained base was distributed.
- The space never reached a steady state. It kept growing over the 90 min, so the authors argue it cannot be given one value even at a fixed bicarbonate level (Discussion, PDF p9).
- Not an opioid; no MEAC, ke0 or potency ratio applies.

## Covariates
- Initial extracellular [HCO3−] was the dominant determinant (Fig. 2). Taking all groups together, the space did not depend on initial pH: it fell with pH in the metabolic groups and rose with pH in the respiratory groups.
- ECF volume differed by no more than 4% of body weight between hypo- and hyperbicarbonatemic dogs. The difference in apparent space was about 20% of body weight.

## Caveats
- Dogs only, with intact kidneys, so some of the dose was excreted. In the metabolic acidosis group, ongoing net acid excretion could make the space even larger than calculated.
- The red-cell, protein and "intracellular" fractions rest on assumed buffer values and an assumed plasma volume of 4.5% of body weight.
- Only 90 min of observation.
- Tables I, II and IV were not read: they are rotated and garbled in the text layer.
