# Fattinger 2000: nasal mucosal versus gastrointestinal absorption of nasal cocaine

## Study
- 17 healthy volunteers (12 men, 5 women), aged 21-45 years, weight 48-82 kg (mean weight not given); experienced
  cocaine users, not dependent (PDF p2 / printed 306).
- Three sessions 4-7 days apart, balanced Latin square: cocaine HCl **IV 0.4 or 0.5 mg/kg** over 1 min; **oral 3 mg/kg**
  in a gelatin capsule; **nasal 3 mg/kg** as a 10% solution sprayed over 30 s with a McIntosh atomizer (a spray of
  solution, not insufflated powder) (p2/306). Doses are stated as cocaine hydrochloride per kg.
- Venous samples: IV 0-300 min (13 times), oral 15-300 min (12 times), nasal 5-300 min (11 times) (p2/306).
- Assay: GC with nitrogen-phosphorus detection. The paper calls the matrix "serum" in the abstract and the
  deconvolution methods, "plasma" in the assay sentence and figure legends.
- Analysis: nonparametric, subject-specific population deconvolution with longitudinal (template + distortion)
  natural cubic splines (Fattinger & Verotta 1995); disposition function from the IV data, input rate functions for
  the oral and nasal sessions. A two-site model (Eq. 4) partitions nasal input into a mucosal input of unknown shape
  and a swallowed fraction (1 - N) absorbed with the oral input shape; fitted to pooled nasal data in NONMEM with an
  additive plus proportional error; 95% CIs for N and E_NA from likelihood-ratio profiles (p3/307).
- Eq. 4 assumes 100% bioavailability of drug absorbed through the mucosa and that all drug not absorbed nasally is
  swallowed. Adding a parameter for mucosal bioavailability did not improve the fit (p5/309).

## Model and parameters
No compartmental model, rate constants, volumes or clearances are reported: disposition and input are spline
functions. Summary results:

- Oral input: near zero at first, peak ~45 min in most subjects (later in some), small second peak ~75 min in some,
  negligible after ~150 min. **Median oral bioavailability 33% (95% CI 27-41%)**, from each subject's area under the
  input rate function per dose unit (p3/307; abstract p1/305).
- Nasal input (total): well above zero at once, peak ~10 min, second peak ~45 min in most subjects (often as high as
  or higher than the first), negligible after ~190 min. **Median nasal bioavailability 43% (95% CI 30-47%)**; the
  largest individual bioavailability was more than 5x the smallest (p4/308).
- Two-site model (Eq. 4): fraction of the nasal dose absorbed through the mucosa **N = 19% (95% CI 11-26%)**; the AUC
  method (Eq. 6) gave **12% (2-22%)**. Fraction of total systemic exposure from mucosal absorption **E_NA = 31%
  (23-37%)**; the discussion says oral absorption supplies about 70% of the total reaching the circulation (p5/309).
- Mean mucosal input (Fig. 7, dashed) is highest at time zero, falls, and has a small second peak ~90 min; mean GI
  input (dotted) peaks ~40 min (read from Fig. 7, p5/309).
- Fit: normalized RMS error oral 0.21, nasal 0.28; normalized mean error oral -0.0006, nasal -0.016 (p3/307).
- Mean predicted concentration after 3 mg/kg nasal (Fig. 2 solid line) peaks at roughly 370 ng/mL near 75-90 min;
  after 3 mg/kg oral (Fig. 1) roughly 390 ng/mL near 60 min (read by eye from the figures, approximate).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No effects were measured: no ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, hysteresis, EC50 or threshold concentration. Not an opioid paper;
no MEAC or potency ratio. The discussion argues (without data in this paper) that nasal use is preferred for its
faster input and higher early concentrations rather than greater bioavailability.

## Covariates
None examined. No size covariate; doses were per kg.

## Caveats
- The 19%, 31% and 33% have different denominators (nasal dose; total systemic input after a nasal dose; oral dose).
  Eq. 5 as printed, E_NA = N/(N + F_OR), does not reproduce 31% from N = 0.19 and the median F_OR = 0.33 (derived:
  0.19/0.52 = 0.37); the pooled fit's F_OR presumably is not the individual median. The numbers cannot be chained.
- Healthy experienced users; doses only 3 mg/kg (nasal, oral) and 0.4-0.5 mg/kg (IV); linearity across doses is assumed,
  not tested.
- Nasal dosing was a sprayed 10% solution, not snorted powder.
- Serum versus plasma is stated inconsistently.
- Nonparametric: no portable parameters for a compartmental engine; the authors say first- or zero-order input
  models would be inappropriate for these input shapes (p5/309).
