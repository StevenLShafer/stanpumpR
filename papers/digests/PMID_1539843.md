# Hughes 1992: Context-sensitive half-time

## Study
Computer simulation, no patients. Published multiexponential unit disposition functions for six drugs (Table 1) were converted to compartment models (Table 2). BET-type infusions holding the central concentration at 100 ng/mL for 1 min to 500 min, and to steady state, were stopped and the time for a 50% fall was defined as the context-sensitive half-time (CSHT). Central concentrations computed every 5 s with the authors' own software. Fentanyl and alfentanil models chosen per Shafer and Varvel 1991 (ref 7); sufentanil per ref 8; midazolam and propofol from models with good CCIP performance.

Results (Figs. 1-2, read from graphs, approximate): fentanyl CSHT rises steeply after ~2 h of infusion to roughly 260-280 min at 8 h (~300 min at steady state); sufentanil stays below ~40 min up to 8 h despite the longest elimination half-life; alfentanil plateaus near 60 min; propofol and midazolam lowest/intermediate; thiopental ~150 min at 8 h. Text gives the 1 min to 8 h range as 1-280 min; the abstract says 1-306 min.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Plasma (central compartment) only; no ke0, MEAC or potency. The paper does not include remifentanil (not yet available).

## Covariates
None.

## Caveats
Simulation of literature models; conclusions depend on the chosen parameter sets. Results read from figures. Internal inconsistency in the CSHT maximum (280 vs 306 min).
