# Ansari 2022: calcium chloride for uterine atony, pilot RCT with population PK

## Study
- **Design** (PDF p. 1-3; printed page = PDF page, article number 110796): double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled
  pilot trial with a nested population PK analysis; Lucile Packard Children's Hospital, Stanford, August 2018 to
  September 2019. Registry NCT03867383; Stanford IRB protocol 43076.
- **Population**: 40 parturients at cesarean delivery, 18-45 yr, with at least two atony risk factors. Excluded:
  creatinine > 1.0 mg/dL, digoxin, calcium channel blocker, cardiac disease, placenta accreta spectrum (PDF p. 2).
  Table 1 (PDF p. 5, page image): calcium group age 34.5 (6.6) yr, height 160.9 (8.3) cm, weight 81.4 (16.2) kg,
  BMI 31 (6); placebo 33.5 (3.7) yr, 161.6 (6.8) cm, 78.2 (13.4) kg, BMI 30 (6) (mean (SD)). Magnesium infusion was
  a risk factor present in 5 (25%) calcium and 2 (10%) placebo patients (not an exclusion in this trial).
- **Drug**: 1 g calcium chloride diluted to 60 mL saline (n = 20) or 60 mL saline (n = 20), infused over 10 min by
  syringe pump from umbilical cord clamping (about 100 mg/min), with oxytocin 2 U bolus + 7.5 U/h. The salt's hydration
  is not stated.
- **Sampling/assay** (PDF p. 3): in consenting patients, 3 venous samples from the foot/saphenous vein: baseline before
  delivery, one 1-30 min after the end of infusion, one 30 min to 3 h after. Ionized calcium and pH by i-STAT 1.
  24 patients sampled (13 calcium, 11 placebo; PDF p. 5).
- **Model** (PDF p. 3): NONMEM (FOCE) with PLT Tools; one compartment, input to the central compartment; exponential
  IIV on V and CL, additive IIV on the baseline calcium (baseline estimated, not subtracted); additive plus proportional
  residual error; pH tested by likelihood ratio (3.84). CIs by -2LL profiling and bootstrap.

- Median prediction error 0%, median absolute prediction error 3%; pH did not improve the model (PDF p. 5).
- No IIV magnitudes, residual-error values or SEs reported.
- Derived: t1/2 = 0.693 x 76 / 0.93 = 57 min; predicted rise at the end of a 10-min infusion of 6.80 mmol
  (1 g CaCl2.2H2O) = 0.084 mmol/L (derived, one-compartment closed form), about a quarter of the observed rise
  (see open questions). Fig. 2's fitted line rises to about 1.47 mmol/L at about 13 min and falls to about 1.25 at
  130 min (read from the figure), a decline consistent with t1/2 of about an hour.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
No PK/PD model; no ke0, tPeak or EC50. Not an opioid paper: no MEAC or potency ratio. Clinical end point: atony
(second-line uterotonic, EBL > 1000 mL or surgical intervention) 4/20 (20%) calcium vs 10/20 (50%) placebo, RR 0.38,
P = 0.07, 95% CI 0.14-1.02 in Results / 0.15-1.07 in the abstract, NNT 3.3 (Table 3, PDF p. 6; PDF p. 4).
EBL 750 [600-800] vs 850 [650-1000] mL (P = 0.15); obstetrician tone score 89 vs 80 (P = 0.13); crystalloid
1200 vs 1750 mL (P = 0.03). Side effects 30% in both groups; no adverse events (Table 2, PDF p. 5).

## Covariates
pH tested, not retained. No size or other covariates.

## Caveats
- Pilot, n = 20 per arm; only 13 calcium recipients sampled, 3 samples each (2 after dosing).
- Abstract and Results disagree on the V CI upper bound (94 vs 91 L) and the RR CI (0.15-1.07 vs 0.14-1.02).
- The modelled scale (V 76 L) does not match the observed rise for a 6.8 mmol dose; dose units of the fit unstated.
- Single fixed dose and rate; all parturients, bleeding, many with oxytocin and magnesium.
- Acknowledges Steven Shafer's help with the PK analysis (PDF p. 7).
