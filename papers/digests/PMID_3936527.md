# Ausems 1985: accuracy of alfentanil kinetics in the TIAC computer-assisted infusion device

## Study
Leiden. TIAC (Titration of Intravenous Agents by Computer; Janssen Scientific Instruments, Hewlett-Packard 86), a
two-compartment, BET-scheme (Schwilden 1981) device recomputing every 15 s, programmed with the Schüttler and Stoeckel
1982 alfentanil kinetics (Table I). n = 35, ASA 1, three groups (Table II, mean ± SD):
- Lower abdominal: 10 women, 25-49 yr; age 37 ± 9 yr, 66 ± 16 kg. Manual pump (WTI SP1002) boluses/infusion;
  TIAC used retrospectively to predict concentrations.
- Upper abdominal: 10 (4 women), 23-54 yr; 44 ± 12 yr, 72 ± 12 kg. Same as above.
- Interactive: 15 women (11 lower abdominal, 4 major vaginal), 20-50 yr; 36 ± 8 yr, 63 ± 11 kg. TIAC prospectively
  controlled the infusion; induction target 400 ng/mL, then steps of 50 or 100 ng/mL up or down by clinical signs.
Manual groups: induction alfentanil 150 µg/kg, then infusion + boluses titrated to response. All: 66% N2O in O2,
diazepam 10 mg PO, atropine 0.5 mg IM. Arterial samples (schedule p. 1219). Assay: radioimmunoassay or gas
chromatography (Michiels 1983). Performance: prediction error (measured − predicted) in ng/mL and as % of measured;
bias (mean PE), precision (SD of PE), mean absolute PE, per patient then averaged by group.

Individual bias was significant in 29 of 35 patients (p. 1222); the most extreme interactive patient ran
about 150 ng/mL below prediction (Fig. 1). No significant differences between groups.

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
Opioid audit (alfentanil):
- Average plasma alfentanil required to block responses to surgical stimulation with 66% N2O (Table II): lower
  abdominal 305 ± 61 ng/mL; upper abdominal 427 ± 129 ng/mL (significantly higher); interactive 276 ± 87 ng/mL.
  End point: clinical signs of inadequate anaesthesia (per Ausems, Hug and de Lange 1983); arterial plasma, total
  concentration (RIA or GC). These are intraoperative requirements, not a postoperative MEAC.
- No ke0, t½ke0, tPeak, hysteresis or potency ratio reported (rapid blood-brain equilibration only mentioned,
  citing Scott and Stanski 1984).

## Covariates
None in the model; the authors list age, weight, sex, smoking, comedication and disease as possible sources of the
variability.

## Caveats
Two of three groups are retrospective predictions of manually given doses; small groups; the PK set came from 7
healthy women; assay method varied (RIA or GC). The discussion quotes Schüttler 1983 as measured 0.45 vs predicted
0.40 µg/mL alfentanil, the reverse of that paper (target 0.45, measured 0.41).
