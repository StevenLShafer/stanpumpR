# Ouellet 2001: gabapentin popPK in infants and children

## Study
Pooled data, Pfizer: 2 single-dose PK studies in 48 healthy children (10 mg/kg; capsules 4-12 y, liquid 1 month-4 y;
422 serial samples to 24 h) and 5 safety/efficacy studies in 205 children with epilepsy (10-65 mg/kg/day in three
doses at 08:00, 14:00, 20:00; 318 sparse steady-state samples, mean 1.55 per patient) (Table 1). Total N = 253;
age 6.5 ± 3.6 (0.08-13) y, weight 27.8 ± 15.1 (3.6-82.9) kg, height 119 ± 28 cm, BSA 0.94 ± 0.36 m², CLcr
59.5 ± 27.1 mL/min (108.3 ± 25.0 mL/min/1.73 m²) (Table 2). HPLC-UV (range 0.02-5 µg/mL PK studies, 0.5-20 patients).
NONMEM V, first-order method; ADVAN2 TRANS2. CLcr by Schwartz (k 0.45 < 1 y, 0.55 ≥ 1 y) multiplied by BSA/1.73,
i.e. absolute mL/min.

## Model and parameters
One compartment, first-order absorption with lag; combined residual error, separate for healthy and patients.
Final model (Table 4; 95% CI):
CL/F (L/h) = θCLcr × CLcr (mL/min) × (1 + θRAC × RAC); Vd/F (L) = (θ2 + θWGT × WGT) × (1 + θPOP × POP).

(IIV column assignment from the scrambled table follows the abstract: about 30% for CL/F and Vd/F, larger for ka,
tlag.) Residual error (Table 5): healthy 9.78% + 0.25 µg/mL; patients 25.3% + 1.32 µg/mL.
Post hoc CL/F: < 5 y 6.0 mL/min/kg (2.3-13.5); ≥ 5 y 4.0 (1.73-7.49). Vd/F 1.82 L/kg healthy, 3.04 L/kg patients.
Slope 0.116 L/h per mL/min = 1.93 mL/min per mL/min (p. 239); adult slope (Blum 1994) 1.61.
Predicted F assuming no net tubular effect 52%; measured urinary F in children > 4 y 39.2% (Haig 2001).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
None. Dosing conclusion: 40 mg/kg/day (< 5 y) and 30 mg/kg/day (≥ 5 y) for similar exposure.

## Covariates
CLcr (proportional, no intercept) and race on CL/F; weight (linear) and population on Vd/F. Age, sex, BSA,
formulation, concomitant AEDs not retained.

## Caveats
Children only (1 month-13 y), normal renal function (CLcr range set by body size); first-order (FO) estimation;
sparse patient data and assumed dose times; volume differs unexplained between healthy and patients. Race effect may
reflect bias in creatinine-based CLcr. Industry authors.
