The reason is what the EEG measures, not the numbers. It is not solely a measure of drug effect: on emergence it is nearly unchanged for several minutes after the drug is stopped, then the patient opens their eyes and it changes instantly, showing brain arousal with the return of consciousness -- so the index is a combination of drug effect and brain state, and a sigmoid Emax model would credit the drug with a change the brain made. Nor do the drugs move it in one direction: ketamine and midazolam ACTIVATE the EEG, opioids do little except in huge doses where they DEPRESS it (the basis of the Stanford opioid experiments that determined their ke0), and the hypnotics ACTIVATE then DEPRESS it. The full reasoning is in the header of R/pdEffect.R and on inst/app-help/models/percent-effect.md."

# Vereecke 2005: AAI 1.6 composite index vs BIS and AAI 1.5, propofol Ce

## Study
- Single-centre (Ghent), 13 ASA I patients (10 women, 3 men), 18-65 y, ambulatory gynaecologic/urologic surgery; weight 64.0 ± 3.8 kg, age 38 ± 6.0 y, height 168 ± 7.8 cm (PDF p. 4, printed 503). Exclusions as in the sister study (weight <70% or >130% of ideal, neurologic disease, psychoactive drugs).
- Propofol 1% at a constant 300 mL/h (= 3 g/h = 50 mg/min, derived) by RUGLOOP until burst suppression >=80% or MAP <50 mmHg. No other drugs, no opioids; spontaneous ventilation, 100% O2 6 L/min.
- Ce propofol calculated by RUGLOOP with the Schnider three-compartment plus effect-site model, ke0 set to tPeak 1.6 min after a bolus (PDF p. 2, printed 501). No propofol assay.
- Compared BIS (XP 4.0, 15 s smoothing), AAI1.5 (MLAEP only), AAI1.6 (composite of MLAEP, EEG 30-47/10-20 Hz power ratio and burst suppression; k0-k2 depend on SNR, artifact index, stimulus intensity), AAI1.6 truncated to 0-60 (AAI1.6-60). Data 10 s averages; 5 s baseline taken at Ce 0.
- Analyses: baseline CV, burst-suppression relation (Spearman), prediction probability PK (jackknife SE, Bonferroni t-test), individualized Spearman, NONMEM V sigmoid Emax (log-normal IIV, additive error).

## Pharmacodynamics and end points
- End points: processed EEG/MLAEP indices against calculated propofol Ce. Not an opioid paper; no MEAC, potency ratio, or analgesic end point.
- No ke0 / t½ke0 estimated; the Schnider ke0 giving tPeak 1.6 min was assumed. No hysteresis analysis.
- Ce50 for half change of index 5.71-6.78 (BIS 6.25), gamma 1.98-5.09 (Table 3). AAI1.5 plateaus near 7 and cannot reach 0; ED95 for loss of consciousness around 19 on AAI1.5 (Discussion, PDF p. 7).
- Sister study (PMID_15220769) gives BIS Ce50 4.92 and gamma 2.69; the two BIS Ce50s (6.25, 4.92) differ by 27% on the same protocol, and the 210% CV here shows how poorly Ce50 is determined for BIS in 13 patients.

## Covariates
None examined.

## Caveats
- Ce is predicted, from a fixed-rate infusion (rapid rise), so Ce50 is confounded with effect-site lag; no sedation or responsiveness end point tested.
- Authors caution the AAI1.6-60 fit is distorted by truncating data above 60 (PDF p. 8).
- AAI1.6-60: E0 57.40 + Emax −60.00 = −2.6, below the 0-60 scale floor (derived), an artefact of the fit.
- Two authors were Danmeter employees and AAI inventors (footnote, PDF p. 1).
- n = 13, mostly women.
