# AGENTS.md — AI Agent Orientation

**stanpumpR** is an R package + Shiny app for PK/PD simulation. It calculates and plots predicted plasma ($C_p$) and effect-site ($C_e$) concentrations for IV and extravascular anesthetics using **analytical closed-form 3-compartment solutions** for every linear model (an exact matrix exponential for the transit chain), with two exceptions that step: the Michaelis-Menten engine (an adaptive Dormand-Prince 5(4) pair, for nonlinear disposition or input: saturable elimination, target-mediated binding (linagliptin, vildagliptin) and exenatide's saturable subcutaneous input) and the inhaled agents (`advanceClosedFormGas()`, a matrix exponential in short steps with the uptake that couples the gases held at its value at the start of each step, so a small error remains that shrinks with the step). `advanceGasManBaseline()`, GasMan's own stepping scheme restated in R, is a validation reference the tests call, not an engine the app runs. Stack/version details: `DESCRIPTION`. Entry point: `app.R` → `stanpumpR::run_app()`.

## Architecture: Reactive Request-to-Render Pipeline (`R/app_server.R`)

*Full details available in `docs/architecture.md`*

1. **Inputs**: Patient covariates, dose grid (`R/dose-grid.R`, `inst/www/dose-grid.js`), clinical events, plot settings.
2. **Validate**: `doseTableClean()`, `eventTableClean()`, `testCovariates()`.
3. **Resolve PK**: `recalculatePK()` calls `getDrugPK()`, which converts covariates → micro rate constants → eigenvalues (`cube()`) → $k_{e0}$ (solved from `tPeak` by `ke0FromTPeakBolus()`, or `ke0FromTPeak()` for an oral `tPeak`) → exponential coefficients.
4. **Simulate**: `processdoseTable()` calls `simCpCe()`, which first expands any `qd`/`bid`/`tid`/`qid`/`q4h` dose into its repeats to the end of the plot (`scheduled.R`; returned as `$scheduled` for export) and any `Plasma target` / `Effect site target` rows into a TCI infusion schedule (`tci.R`, Shafer & Gregg 1992; returned as `$tci` for the rate panel and export), then dispatches to:
   - `advanceClosedForm0.R` (IV, standard PK)
   - `advanceClosedForm1.R` (time-varying PK with events)
   - `advanceClosedFormPO_IM_IN.R` (extravascular 1st-order absorption: PO, SL (sublingual), IM, IN, SC (subcutaneous: the incretins, insulins, enoxaparin and fondaparinux, carried on the RA tissue depot; `R/subcutaneous.R`) and RA, regional anesthesia, a local anesthetic injected into tissue — `ka_RA`/`bioavailability_RA`/`tlag_RA`, units `raUnits`; tests `test-route-ra.R`)
   - `advanceMichaelisMenten.R` (numerical, with saturable, Michaelis-Menten elimination — the one non-superposable engine, for phenytoin, voriconazole, GHB, exenatide, cetagliptin, linagliptin and vildagliptin, and for autoinduction (ifosfamide, cyclophosphamideMcCune, suzetrigine), and for macitentan's parallel absorption paths; integrated between the time line's points by `mmDormandPrince()`, an embedded Dormand-Prince 5(4) pair with error control at `MM_ADAPTIVE_RTOL` (1e-6) and `MM_ADAPTIVE_ATOL` (1e-9 of the largest dose or hourly rate given SO FAR: the scale is a running maximum, because a later dose of 1e9 mg/kg in a 500 kg patient set the whole run's tolerance at 460 mg and the first, ordinary dose read as none, Carbon's third UI audit, 2026-11-15), which since 2026-11-15 is every drug's default (Shafer's decision of that day, after the calibration below; a block's `adaptiveStep = FALSE` selects the fixed RK4 step, which the tests use as a second method, and which is held within RK4's region of stability for the fastest process while drug is being absorbed, `mmStateRates()`: at a bare 2 minutes voriconazole's ka of 100 /h made a single oral dose fall to zero at two hours and a second reach 1e90 mg/L, in the library since the drug was added). Calibrated on sixteen regimens of the seven drugs against a 0.1-minute step: largest error 1.9e-6 of the level, against the fixed step's 3.2e-3 (oral voriconazole); 52 weeks of GHB 9.4 s to 0.7 s, linagliptin 16.6 s to 3.4 s. An absorption state left below the absolute tolerance is emptied, and a system with every state below it and no input is zero for the rest of the interval (a stiff corner of the Bayesian search, a neonate at 20x clearance, stepped noise for 240,000 derivatives); a step that cannot be estimated is refused, and one that fails at `MM_ADAPTIVE_MIN_STEP` stops the simulation with a message (it once looped for ever, Carbon's second UI audit); a drug declares it with a `michaelisMenten` block (`vmax`, `km`, per-product `saltFactor`, optional `kConversion`/`ka_IM` for a prodrug route like fosphenytoin's `mg PE`; optional `clLinear` for a parallel linear clearance (with `vmax` 0 when there is no saturable elimination), `saturableSC` for exenatide's zero-order-then-saturable SC input, `rapidBinding` or `targetBinding` for target-mediated disposition (linagliptin, vildagliptin), and `kaTransit` for a transit compartment before the oral depot; `saturableDistribution` (`vmax`, `km`, `k21`) for a compartment held as an amount, filled saturably from the unbound central concentration and emptied at first order, beside the PK set's linear peripheral compartment (paclitaxel, Joerger 2006), or with `volume` in place of `k21` a carrier saturated by the central concentration both ways, net `vmax (Cu - Cs)/(km + Cu)` (nab-paclitaxel, Chen 2014; read it as `sd[["volume"]]`, since `$v` partially matches `vmax`); `autoinduction` (`cl`, `kEnz`, `emax`/`ec50`, `imax`/`ic50`) for a clearance multiplied by a relative enzyme amount that starts at 1 and that the drug induces, by raising its formation or inhibiting its degradation, carried through the whole dose table (ifosfamide, Kerbusch 2000; cyclophosphamideMcCune, McCune 2009; 2026-11-10, Shafer's request; tests `test-autoinduction.R`), with an optional `hill` making the induction sigmoid (suzetrigine's Hill coefficient of 10, FDA review of NDA 219209, 2026-11-16, Shafer's request); `metabolite` (`name`, `fraction`, `v1`, `cl`, `q`, `v2`, `induced`, `mwRatio`) for an active metabolite advanced IN the engine, formed from that fraction of everything the parent eliminates and cleared at `cl` (times the enzyme when `induced`), returned as `CpMetabolite`/`C2Metabolite` the way the transit-chain engine returns enalaprilat (suzetrigine's M6-SUZ; `getDrugPK()` names it in the PK set and refuses it beside an `X$metabolite`); `zeroOrderDepot` (minutes) for an oral dose released into its depot at a constant rate after the lag (suzetrigine's tablet); `kaDoseDependence` (`emax`, `d50`, `hill`) for an absorption rate that falls with each oral dose's size in mg, each distinct size its own depot (`simCpCe()` passes `DoseMg`; tests `test-drugs-suzetrigine.R`, against an independent integration of the published model); `oralPaths` (a list of `share`, `ka`, `lag`, `zeroOrder`, `into`) for each plain oral dose split among parallel absorption paths, each its own depot after its own lag or zero-order release, one of which may empty into the in-engine metabolite, the shares constant or a logit of log(1 + dose in mg) (`mmPathShare()`; macitentan and aprocitentan, Bartolucci 2021, supplied by Shafer 2026-11-16; tests `test-drugs-macitentan.R`, against an independent exact solution); `rapidBinding$plotted = "unbound"` to plot the unbound central concentration where the bound pool is a tissue store (vincristinePediatric, Nijstad 2022: kon 1300/h is beyond RK4 at the 2-min step, so its binding is carried at quasi-equilibrium, measured within 1.1% of the full ODE at 5 min and 0.8% from 30 min); a PK set with `cl2 > 0`, or `k23`/`k32` in the block (GHB; both or neither, mapped to the same state as Q = k23 V, Vp = V k23/k32), adds a peripheral compartment, and elimination and distribution act on the unbound concentrations). Integrates the whole dose table at once, so `simCpCe()` dispatches here before it splits oral formulations; tests `test-michaelis-menten.R`)
   - `advanceTransitChain.R` (**exact**, by matrix exponential — for a transit-chain absorption whose repeated eigenvalue no closed-form coefficient can carry: enalapril, whose eight identical Erlang transit compartments share one eigenvalue eight times over, so `separateEigenvalues()`'s 1e-8 nudge would make the partial-fraction coefficients scale as 1/nudge⁷. The system is linear, so this is not a numerical solver: it is exact to rounding, like the closed forms, and unlike `advanceMichaelisMenten()` it approximates nothing. A drug declares a `transitChain` block (`n` chain compartments, the first n−1 emptying at `ktr` and the LAST at the PK set's `ka_PO`; `kMetabolic`, the share of the PK set's `k10` that forms the metabolite rather than leaving the body; and an optional `metabolite` giving the receiving drug's `name`, its own chain `n`/`ktr` — every compartment of which empties at `ktr`, the asymmetry being the source's — plus `v`, `kel` and optional `k12`/`k21`). The engine advances both moieties together and returns the metabolite's columns itself, because the closed-form metabolite route convolves exponential amplitudes a defective system does not have, so `simCpCe()` dispatches here before `runEngines()`; `getDrugPK()` refuses the block alongside an `X$metabolite`, several oral formulations, saturable sublingual absorption, parallel systems, Michaelis-Menten elimination, an osmotic/molar basis, pulsed or zero-order oral release, a dose-phase split, dose-dependent parameters or an INR. One matrix exponential per distinct step length, memoised on it and augmented with a unit-rate state so the same one serves the homogeneous propagator and the rate response; the non-uniform time line repeats a few dozen step lengths across thousands of points, so a drug costs a few dozen exponentials. Since 2026-10-30 it also carries what PR #548's R/transit.R (deleted 2026-10-31) approximated with two parallel depots 1e-5 apart (levodopa, its intestinal gel, opicapone: one transit compartment and the depot at one rate, i.e. `n = 2`, `ktr = ka_PO`): an **effect site** when the PK set has a ke0 (one more linear state, exact), an **oral rate** (`mg/hr PO`, an enteral infusion) into the head of the chain, lagged and scaled by F like an oral dose, and a dose-dependent oral bioavailability (`oralSaturation`, applied by `simCpCe()` before dispatch). **Time until threshold** (2026-10-30, Shafer's decision): a defective system leaves no exponential amplitudes for `recoveryStates()`, so `transitChainRecovery()` root-finds `c' exp(A s) x` directly — `r(s)' = c' exp(A s)` once on a uniform grid shared by every point, screened by a RIGOROUS chord bound (A is a generator, so `exp(A h) = exp(-delta h) exp(N h)` with `exp(N h)` entrywise convex: `chordBound()`), then bisected to `RECOVERY_TOL`, brief excursions to `RECOVERY_TOL_PEAK` as in `R/recoveryCalc.R`; NA through a lag; the metabolite's own stays NA. Tests `test-transit-chain.R` (the Erlang density, the analytical bolus and constant-rate solutions, the closed-form oral engine where the chain degenerates, and an independent RK4 of all 13 states; the time until threshold against the closed-form engine where the chain degenerates, a dense independent search, and a 1e-9 excursion), `test-drugs-enalapril.R`)
5. **Plot & Render**: `simulationPlot.R` generates `ggplot2` output.

**% MEAC overlay** (`R/meacOverlay.R`): Graph Options **% MEAC on opioid panels** (`input$showMEAC`, scenario option `showMEAC`) draws each opioid's effect-site % MEAC as a thin black line on its own panel, labelled at the right-hand edge, on ONE scale shared by every opioid panel (its top is the highest % MEAC any opioid reaches, `meacOverlayBreaks()`, mapped onto each panel's data range), so a parent and a metabolite of very different potency can be compared at a glance. It shares the right-hand edge with *Time until threshold*: ticking either unticks the other, and `simulationPlot()` drops the overlay when `plotRecovery` is on or under normalization. Scenario `hydrocodone-hydromorphone-meac`; tests `test-meac-overlay.R`.

**Physiology sliders** (`R/physiology.R`; Shafer, 2026-11-13, from his chapter 2, Figures 10-12): the sidebar's **Physiology** panel (`physiologyPanel()`) has four log-scale sliders, `input$physiology_liver`, `_renal`, `_cardiac`, `_gi`, each holding log10 of a multiple of normal function (0.1 to 10, `PHYSIOLOGY_MIN`/`MAX`; app.js shows the multiple). `getDrugPK(physiology =)` (threaded through `recalculatePK()`, the companion models, `simulateDrugsWithCovariates()`, and the antiseizure and dose-size rebuilds through `PK$physiology$factors`) changes the PARAMETERS once, before the engines, which are untouched. Hepatic clearance follows the well-stirred model, `CL_H'/CL_H = q l / (q (1-E) + l E)` (`hepaticClearanceFactor()`; q = cardiac output, liver blood flow being a fixed share of it; l = liver function), needing only the drug's extraction ratio; renal function scales only the renal share, in proportion for filtration and by the same well-stirred form (renal blood flow a share of cardiac output) for a drug with a `renal_extraction_ratio` (propofol's renal metabolism, 27% of its clearance, renal E 0.58, Takizawa 2005; Shafer, 2026-11-13); the rest (esterases, Hofmann) is unchanged; cardiac output also scales Q2/Q3 and V1 in the same direction (`PHYSIOLOGY_V1_CARDIAC_EXPONENT`, an assumption); a first pass in an absolute oral F follows the new extraction ratio, capped at 1; GI scales ka only; ke0 is the normal one (solved from the unchanged model, or the model's own) times cardiac^`PHYSIOLOGY_KE0_CARDIAC_EXPONENT`, carried as `ke0Physiology`, so tPeak moves; the exponent is 0.35, the exponent of brain blood flow on cardiac output within autoregulation (Levine 1994, Ogoh 2005, Vlastra 2020; Shafer's decision, 2026-11-14, after a simulation showed a flow-limited drug's ke0 falls in proportion to brain flow when the arterial curve is held) -- do not change it without his decision; a row with `ke0_permeability_limited` TRUE keeps its ke0 (morphine, barrier-limited, so brain flow drops out; Shafer, 2026-11-14). The inhaled agents respond to the cardiac slider only (Shafer, 2026-11-15, from Eger and Shafer's chapter on inhaled anesthetic kinetics): `simulateGases(physiology =)` builds the engine's body with `gasBodyPhysiology()`, which scales `Q_cardiac` and redistributes it, the brain and heart (`PHYSIOLOGY_GAS_PROTECTED_SHARE`, 16% of resting output: brain 12% + heart 4% for the reference man, Williams and Leggett 1989 Table 17, read in full) keeping share x cardiac^`PHYSIOLOGY_KE0_CARDIAC_EXPONENT` of normal output and the rest shared in proportion (the shock "heart-brain circuit"); off normal the brain and heart become their own compartment, state 3 (the effect site, "Brain"), and the rest of the vessel-rich group a sixth state (`gasTissueShares()`, `gasStateCount()`), each with the group's flow per litre so that at normal output nothing changes and the GasMan validation holds; `gasWashout()` takes the simulation's own `sim$body`, the coupled recovery handles four or five states per gas, and `viewerGasRun()` draws the whole group (volume-weighted tension). Generated help block `physiology-gas-table`. A fifth control, **Body temperature** (`input$physiology_temperature`, 28-37 C, `PHYSIOLOGY_TEMPERATURE_*`; Shafer, 2026-11-16), acts on the inhaled agents and, through the physiology vector's `temperature` element (°C, in `PHYSIOLOGY_DEFAULT`, so it reaches every rebuild), on the intravenous drugs with a physiology row: `physiologyTemperatureFactor()`, exp(`PHYSIOLOGY_TEMPERATURE_CLEARANCE_SLOPE` (T − 37)), 6.37%/C from Michelsen 2001's remifentanil fit over 20-35 C, multiplies the liver's capacity inside the well-stirred model, the kidney's and every other route's, never blood flow (Shafer's decision of 2026-11-16, after the literature recorded in `R/physiology.R`; papers by Gideon Koren are not used). For the inhaled agents: `simulateGases(temperature =)` keeps it as `body$temperature`, and every reader of the gas properties (the engine, `gasWashout()`, the coupled recovery, `macRecoveryTime()`, `gasDrugEntries()`, `viewerGasRun()`) takes `gasPropertiesAt(body$temperature)`, which multiplies each volatile agent's blood:gas AND every tissue:gas coefficient by `exp(-slope (T - 37))` (`GAS_SOLUBILITY_TEMPERATURE_SLOPE`, Zhou 2002 in human blood; Zhou 2001: tissue:blood unchanged) and its MAC by `gasMacTemperatureFactor()` (Liu 2001's regression MAC = 0.0784 T - 1.203, read in full: 4.6%/C, not the 5.1% its text says). Nitrous oxide's MAC follows Antognini 1994's regression in rats, MAC = 0.03827 T + 0.3836 atm (`gasMacTemperatureFactor(T, "nitrousOxide")`, `GAS_MAC_N2O_ZERO_TEMPERATURE`; 2.1%/C; applied to people on Shafer's decision of 2026-11-16, no human data existing), its solubility by Allott 1973 (2.33%/C in blood and aqueous tissues, 1.13%/C in fat, `GAS_N2O_SOLUBILITY_TEMPERATURE_SLOPE`); cardiac output and ventilation are left to their own controls, typed thresholds do not move. Generated help block `physiology-temperature-table`. Only drugs with a row in `inst/extdata/physiologyParameters.csv` whose `verification` is `read_full_text`/`read_abstract` respond; `pending_verification` rows record what is missing; the panel lists the drugs the sliders do not change (`physiologyNote()`). A drug whose elimination lives outside the PK set (Michaelis-Menten, parallel systems, INR/ACT, dose phases) may not have a row (`physiologyUnsupported()`). The Bayesian update fits at NORMAL physiology and applies the sliders to the individual afterwards (`bayesianAtPhysiology()`, `drugPK(normalPhysiology = TRUE)` in `bayesUpdate()`; Shafer, 2026-11-16), so a slider is a what-if on the fitted patient and does not refit. Temperature and cardiac output are deliberately not coupled (Shafer, 2026-11-16: fever and cold have different mechanisms; the clinician sets each). Help page `inst/help/models/physiology.md` (generated block `physiology-table`); tests `test-physiology.R`.

**Peripheral compartments** (`R/compartments.R`): Graph Options **Peripheral compartments** (`input$showCompartments`, scenario option `showCompartments`) adds, directly under each drug's panel (V1) and before its TCI panel, one panel per further compartment: `V2`, `V3` (drug's concentration units), an inhaled agent's `MRG`/`FG` (gas states 4 and 5, from `gasDrugEntries()`), and `Depot`, the bioavailable amount still to be absorbed (all depots summed; enters after the lag; unit from `depotUnits()`). **Antiseizure drug interactions** (`R/antiseizureInteractions.R`): Graph Options **Include antiseizure drug interactions** (`input$antiseizureInteractions`, scenario option `antiseizureInteractions`, enabled while the dose table holds an interacting pair or carbamazepine) applies the pharmacokinetic interactions among the epilepsy drugs — enzyme induction (carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital) of lamotrigine, valproate, topiramate, tiagabine, lacosamide, eslicarbazepine, cenobamate, zonisamide (Silva 2025 `1.63^INDDL`) and ethosuximide (Giaccone 1996, pooled `CL x1.66`), ethosuximide's own induction of lamotrigine (Hagemann 2024, adult `CL x1.16`; larger in children), inhibition and albumin displacement (valproate, including a variable `CL x0.85` on ethosuximide from Pisani 1984), zonisamide on phenytoin's Km, and carbamazepine's autoinduction. The interactions and their verified PMIDs are `inst/extdata/antiseizureInteractions.csv` (built by `data-raw/antiseizureInteractions.R`, checked by `test-antiseizure-interactions.R`); 35 applied rows, the four required victims (phenytoin, valproate, lamotrigine, carbamazepine) among them, plus zonisamide and ethosuximide as victims and ethosuximide as a perpetrator (lacosamide is induced by all three of carbamazepine, phenobarbital and phenytoin — the phenytoin row from Contin 2013, `CL x1.44`); 14 cross-check rows (the consensus reviews Patsalos & Perucca 2003, Riva 1996, Spina 1996, Tanaka 1999 and the secondary lamotrigine/zonisamide/valproate-binding/ethosuximide studies) and 10 recorded (not applied). Several inducer factors (topiramate, tiagabine, lacosamide, ethosuximide) are *pooled* any-inducer covariates applied per inducer row, which over-predicts a multi-inducer regimen under additive-excess — a structural limitation flagged in `docs/antiseizure-interactions-review.md` for the engine-level review. Each interaction is a saturable function of the perpetrator's own plotted concentration, `fold(C) = 1 + (Fmax-1) C/(EC50+C)`, calibrated so `fold(Cref)` is the published categorical factor at the perpetrator's typical band value `Cref` (EC50 defaults to the band's lower edge; `ec50_basis` records it); inductions run through an enzyme-turnover model `dE/dt = kdeg(fold-E)` with a de-induction half-life of 86.1 h (Punyawudho 2009), inhibition and displacement are immediate. Several inducers of one victim add in their excess, `1 + sum(Ei-1)`; inhibitions multiply. `processdoseTable(..., antiseizureInteractions = TRUE)` calls `applyAntiseizureInteractions()` after every drug is simulated alone: it computes each victim's multipliers from the perpetrators' concentrations, cuts them into near-constant segments (`asmSegments()`, `ASM_INTERACTION_TOLERANCE`), rebuilds the victim's PK with one set per clearance segment (`asmSegmentPK()`, which needs the covariates `getDrugPK()` now keeps for every `asmInteractionVictims()` drug) and re-simulates through the event engine, or for phenytoin feeds Vmax/Km multipliers to `advanceMichaelisMenten()` via `PK$interactionMM`; victims are also perpetrators, so it iterates to a fixed point (`ASM_INTERACTION_CONVERGENCE`, `ASM_INTERACTION_MAX_ITERATIONS`). `simCpCe()` reads a victim's segment events from `PK$interactionEvents`. Each changed drug is drawn beside its own uninteracted curve through the companion mechanism (`addAntiseizureReferenceEntries()` builds a `companionOf` entry `"<drug> (no interactions)"`; `alternativeModelParent()` maps it back). Help page `inst/help/models/antiseizure-interactions.md` (generated blocks `antiseizure-interaction-table`, `antiseizure-interaction-sources`); scenarios `valproate-lamotrigine-inhibition`, `carbamazepine-lamotrigine-deinduction`, `carbamazepine-autoinduction`, `phenytoin-valproate-displacement`, `carbamazepine-phenytoin-induction`. Not modelled: unbound phenytoin (totals are plotted), metabolites (the epoxide), drugs outside the group, and any interaction only a product label quantifies (rows with `status` `recorded`).

**Bayesian updating** (`R/bayesian.R`, `R/bayesian-server.R`; Shafer, 2026-11-13; help page `inst/help/models/bayesian.md`): a sidebar panel, **Bayesian Updating**, holds a table grid of measured concentrations (Drug, from the committed dose table; Time, typed in the dose table's format and kept as text with the format it was typed in, converted when the format changes; Conc., in the units of the drug's plot, so the INR for warfarin and the ACT for heparinCPB) and two sliders, **PK variability** (CV %, default 25) and **Residual variability** (CV %, default 5; named "residual", not "assay", by Shafer, because it also carries timing and model error). A complete row takes effect as it is entered (the grid sends every commit; debounced, no Apply). `bayesUpdate()` in `app_server()` sits between `drugPK()` and `processdoseTable()`: `bayesianUpdateDrugs()` finds the maximum a posteriori **clearance and volume factors** of each measured drug and rebuilds it through `getDrugPK(individual = list(clearance, volume))`, which applies `individualizeModel()` to the model's own parameters before anything is derived. The scaling is BAYES.C's (the original STANPUMP, Maitre and Shafer: CL and V1 estimated, k12/k21/k13/k31 fixed), for every engine: the volume factor multiplies every volume AND every intercompartmental clearance (so the distribution rate constants are the model's); the clearance factor multiplies cl1, a Michaelis-Menten drug's Vmax and clLinear (Km unchanged); a metabolite's kFormation moves by clearance/volume (with k10); parallel systems (warfarin's R) take both factors; a target capacity held as an amount moves with the volume. Absorption, bioavailability, ke0/tPeak and the INR/ACT links are the model's. `PK$individual` is kept so `doseSizePK()` and `asmSegmentPK()` rebuild the individual's drug (both through `pkRebuildArgs()`, which also carries the library rows the PK was built with). A transit chain's `kMetabolic`, a share of k10, moves with k10 as `kFormation` does (enalapril's update failed without it). A TCI target row is delivered at the rates the library's model computes (an updated drug, and each candidate of the search, carries that model as `tciModel`, which `simCpCe()` hands `tciSchedule()`): the update changes the PK model, never the dose table (Shafer, 2026-11-14); before, the controller ran on each candidate's model, every prediction was the target, and a measurement could not move the estimate. Both variabilities are **log-normal** (Shafer: a parameter or a concentration cannot go negative; 0.1X is as far as 10X): eta = log(factor), sd `bayesianSD(cv) = sqrt(log(1 + cv^2))`, objective `sum(eta^2)/omega^2 + sum((log meas - log pred)^2)/sigma^2` (Vozeh 1985, as BAYES.C's funk(); only the ratio omega/sigma moves the estimate), searched from 0 within `BAYES_FACTOR_LIMIT` (20x) either way by `bayesianSearch()`: Gauss-Newton with Levenberg-Marquardt damping (the objective is a sum of squares), NL2SOL's secant term for large residuals, an active set at the bounds and Nielsen's damping rule, which needs 6 to 53 simulations where `optim()`'s L-BFGS-B needed 12 to 102 on the same panel, at the same optimum (2026-11-14; a phenytoin fit to four levels over 28 days went from 48 s to 8 s). The Michaelis-Menten engine's derivative computes only the terms a drug has (bit-identical output, 1.5x faster on phenytoin), and the engine's adaptive step (`mmDormandPrince()`, first for phenytoin and since 2026-11-15 for all seven drugs; Shafer's bound is 1%) took phenytoin fits from 168 s to 2 s for a level at ten weeks, 48 s to 3 s for four levels over 28 days. An update stops after `BAYES_MAX_SECONDS` (30 s, all drugs together; no simulation is started after it), because a worker runs one session at a time: a drug not finished is not updated, said, and returned in `failed`, which the app caches with the fits so that it is not tried again until its inputs change (Carbon's second UI audit, 2026-11-15: six interacting antiseizure drugs over 52 weeks held a worker past eight minutes). That bounds the fit, not a single simulation: the same six drugs took 87 s to simulate once, the plot included, and take 52 s since `getDrugPK()` builds its PK sets as a list rather than with `assign()`/`get0()` in its unhashed frame (quadratic in the number of sets, which the interactions make by the hundred) and the engine finds each point's jumps once, and 33 s since the event engine finds each segment's points with `findInterval()` (`pkEventIndex()`) where a row-name lookup ran `pmatch()` over every row for every point, and the Michaelis-Menten engine takes an interaction's switch as a plain point of its time line, not a knot with fill after it (Carbon's fourth UI audit, C3); what is left is phenytoin integrated through every interaction segment and the victims' event engine, a project of its own. A level the model predicts as zero (inside an absorption lag, which no clearance or volume can move) is set aside and said, where it made every candidate unreadable and the drug's other levels went unused; and the first dose is the first above 0. What remains is the R derivative's cost at every point of the time line; a compiled derivative (C, through deSolve or Rcpp) or the pair's interpolant instead of landing on every point are the next steps, the first being a decision (the package has no compiled code). The prediction is what the plot draws: `processdoseTable()` on the drug (plus its metabolite parents, or every drug with the antiseizure interactions on) under `withOutputTimes()`, `$wide$Plasma`; drugs are updated in dose-table order, except that a parent goes before a metabolite it forms, and with the antiseizure interactions on the round is repeated, each drug fitted against the others' latest estimates, until no estimate moves (`BAYES_JOINT_PASSES` 6, `BAYES_JOINT_TOLERANCE` 1e-3; Carbon's fourth UI audit, 2026-11-15, H7 and H8: updated once each, morphine fitted before codeine was drawn at the population's curve, and of two interacting measured drugs the first was drawn 21% from its own fit). Each fit is searched from the model's drug (`initial`), never from a previous round's estimate, and compares the levels with the model's drugs together, which is what the population line draws; a drug's own doses and its parents' decide whether it has been dosed, not every interacting drug's. A miss beyond tenfold is reported as a likely misreading (`paperReviewTenfold()`), and a document with no model gets one line. **The checks' false alarms** (the effort calibration of 2026-11-10, `docs/paper-review-calibration.md`, which ran 126 reviews of 18 library papers and found most error findings on these verified papers came from the checks): a group with no disposition parameter of its own (an NCA, a PD model, a model-building step) is not reported as failing to close a model, nor a group without its own effect site as missing another group's ke0 (`paperReviewGroupFindings()`); one group spelled two ways is one (`paperReviewCanonicalGroups()`); a derived quantity printed under `conditions` the `at` patient cannot carry, as a mean or median of individuals, or for one of several patients a model with no separating covariate gives the same value is a concern, not an error; a decrement time after a stated infusion (`infusion_minutes`, or a length written beside it) is compared at that infusion (`paperReviewInfusionMinutes()`); and a covariate term that fails only outside the population studied is a concern (`paperReviewStudied()`). The effort is `high` with 128,000 output tokens (decision 8); a review still running after `PAPER_REVIEW_LATE_SEC` (10 min) offers *Stop and run a quick review*, the same manuscript at `PAPER_REVIEW_QUICK_EFFORT` (`medium`), the full review stopped through `paperReviewExtractAsync(onJob =)`, which ends its background process by the ID it wrote (`stop_mirai()` alone, with no dispatcher, leaves it running and billed), its place in the shared key's 200 reserved first, the manuscript held in `store$inFlight` until the answer returns, and its report bannered (`review$quick`; Shafer, 2026-11-13). Tests `test-paper-review-*.R` are network-free; the live test needs a key and `STANPUMPR_LIVE_API=1`.

**Illicit drugs** (`R/illicit-drugs.R`): drugs in the `Illicit drugs` category (`ILLICIT_DRUG_CATEGORY`) are opt-in, evidence-labelled research models of drugs of abuse (diamorphine, carfentanil, cocaine, methamphetamine, LSD, psilocybin, MDMA and GHB; `R/drugs_diamorphine.R` and the files beside it). Every one is **plasma only** (`tPeak` 0, `MEAC` 0, no band), and each offers dose units **only** for a route whose input model the human evidence identifies: `inst/extdata/illicitRouteRegistry.csv` carries one row per drug and route with its `route_status` (calibrated / anchored / observed / unsupported), `parameter_status`, analyte, matrix, dose basis and source, and `test-illicit-registry.R` refuses a route that has units without an `offered` row, or an offered route whose input model is not identified. There is no pulmonary route, so a smoked route is recorded and not offered; it must never be aliased to `IN`. An input SHAPE the engine lacks is a different case from a missing parameter: MDMA's published zero-order depot filling is DISCRETISED as two lagged half doses at the quarter points of its window, measured against a numerical evaluation of the exact solution in `test-drugs-MDMA.R` (0.01% at the peak, 0.53% over the curve), so it stays a `published_fit`. An engine the library lacked is a third case, and the answer is to build it, not to approximate: GHB (Chen 2020) is two compartments with saturable elimination, so `advanceMichaelisMenten.R` gained its optional peripheral compartment for it. Where a source's dose basis differs from the plotted analyte, the **unit names the basis** (`g PO oxybate`): GHB's dose is the salt and its curve the acid, 21% apart, carried by a `saltFactor` and an exact rescaling of `vmax` and `km`. A new illicit model is validated against sources it was *not* fitted to where any exist — GHB against Liechti 2016 (+6%, −1%) and Palatini 1993 — because that is what catches a basis error. Help page `inst/help/models/illicit-drugs.md`, which carries the generated block `illicit-pk-table` (`helpIllicitPKTableMarkdown()`): one row per illicit drug at the reference adult giving the analyte plotted, the structure, V1, the elimination (a clearance, or Vmax and Km where it saturates), the absorption and the half-life, built from the models so it cannot drift. A persistent checkbox, **Show illicit drugs** (`input$showIllicitDrugs`), off by default, gates them: when off they are not offered in the dose-table autocomplete or the Add a dose dialog (`drugChoices()` → `visibleDrugNames()`), and the startup menu never lists them (`startupDrugChoices()`); when on they appear, with their names drawn in red in the dose table (the dose grid, from the `Illicit` flag `doseGridDrugsMessage()` sends it, and said in words to a screen reader). Restoring a bookmark whose dose table holds an illicit drug turns the opt-in on so the drug is not hidden from its own table. Tests: `test-illicit-drugs.R`.

**Scheduled doses** (`R/scheduled.R`): a bolus, PO, SL, IM, IN or SC unit followed by `qd`, `bid`, `tid`, `qid` or `q4h`, an oral or sublingual antiseizure unit followed by `qam` or `qpm` (separate morning/evening slots), or an SC unit followed by `qwk` (once weekly) (`"mg PO bid"`, `"mg PO qpm"`, `"mg SC qwk"`; the full list is `scheduledUnits`, intervals in minutes are `ALL_SCHEDULE_INTERVALS` in `R/constants.R`: the daily `SCHEDULE_INTERVALS`, the `SLOT_SCHEDULE_INTERVALS` (`qam`/`qpm`) and `WEEKLY_SCHEDULE_INTERVAL`) gives the dose at the time entered and repeats it every interval until the end of the plot. Rules within a drug (oral rules revised 2026-10-28): a scheduled dose of 0 stops its own sequence; a later non-zero whole-day scheduled row (`qd`/`bid`/`tid`/`qid`/`q4h`) replaces the running whole-day sequence from its own time; two scheduled rows in one lane at the same time keep the last entered. On the oral and sublingual routes, taken as one: a later whole-day scheduled dose is a regimen change that ends EVERY running oral schedule (the `qam`/`qpm` slots included); an ordinary oral/sublingual dose of 0 stops every oral schedule; and an ordinary oral/sublingual dose ABOVE 0 is an extra one-off — a catch-up or breakthrough dose — that leaves the schedule running (not an oral rate unit or a row with no readable time). `qam` and `qpm` are two independent daily slots (oral/SL only) that do NOT replace each other, so a split regimen (cenobamate 200 qpm + 50 qam, the morning dose usually smaller because the antiseizure drugs are sedating) is entered as two rows; a later `qam` replaces only `qam`, a later `qpm` only `qpm`. The other routes keep the old rule: only a scheduled 0, or a later scheduled dose for that route, ends it (`scheduledSequences()`). **Skip next dose**, a button in both plot dose dialogs while the clicked drug has a scheduled dose on any route at or after the time clicked (never an infusion or a TCI target, which are delivered continuously and have no next dose), writes the skip into the dose table by those rules — a scheduled 0 in the schedule's own unit at the skipped time (`0 mg PO qam`, `0 mg PO bid`, `0 mg IM qid`), which stops only that schedule's lane, and the scheduled row again one interval later, unless the schedule ends first (`skipNextScheduledDose()`). As with TCI, the repeats never enter the editable dose table: `expandScheduledDoses()` runs first in `simCpCe()`, turning each scheduled row into its base unit plus the repeats, so the engines and `doseRoute()` only ever see ordinary units; the repeats are returned as `$scheduled` (Drug, Time, Dose, Units), carried through the simulation cache, and merged with the TCI rows into the exported dose table by `exportDoseTable()`. Which drugs offer the frequencies is set by their `Units` in `drugDefaults_global.csv` (opioid analgesics, antibiotics, steroids; not the anaesthetics, TCI-only drugs, or mannitol, which is infusion-only); `qam`/`qpm` are offered only by the oral antiseizure maintenance drugs (cenobamate, carbamazepine, valproate, phenytoin, ethosuximide, topiramate, lacosamide, lamotrigine, zonisamide, levetiracetam), on their primary oral form. Tests: `test-scheduled.R`.

**Stop** (`R/stop-administration.R`): both plot dose dialogs offer **Stop <drug> at <time>** while the clicked drug has something running at the time clicked — an infusion (any rate unit, any route), a TCI target (the latest of target and manual rate on the intravenous route; at one time the target wins), a schedule with a repeat still to come, or an inhaled agent's setting (`ongoingAdministration()`). It writes only the 0 rows the existing rules read as a stop, at that time (`stopAdministration()`): a 0 rate, a 0 target, a scheduled 0 in the schedule's own unit (which stops every route), a 0 setting. Tests: `test-stop-administration.R`, `test-stop-administration-server.R`.

**The TCI API** (`R/tci-api.R`, `R/tci-api-server.R`; normative reference `docs/tci-api.md`, help page `inst/help/tci-api.md`): a stateless HTTP API under `/tci/v1` that hands a TCI pump controller its next rate for one drug in one patient, re-deriving the model state from the controller's delivery history on every request (the STANPUMP principle). `tciNextRate(request)` (exported, pure R, no Shiny, no plumber) takes the body as `jsonlite::fromJSON(txt, simplifyVector = FALSE)` gives it, rebuilds the seven-amplitude state from the history (rate changes, delivered segments, hand boluses, infusate changes), projects it through the declared `timing.latency`, calls the app's `tciRate()`, applies `pump.maxRate` / `minRate` / `rateResolution` (always rounding down) and returns the response list (`rate`, `segment`, `fallback`, `timing`, `state`, `prediction`, `model`, `warnings`); validation raises the classed condition `tci_api_error` (`status`, `code`, `message`, `pointer`). Field names, unit tokens (the library's spellings: `mL/hr`, `mcg`; one closed list, no aliases), error codes, HTTP statuses and the `TCI_API_*` bounds in `R/constants.R` are fixed by `docs/tci-api.md`: within `/tci/v1` only add, never change a meaning. `tciApiRouter()` / `run_tci_api()` wrap it in plumber (`inst/api/entrypoint.R` for `rsconnect::deployAPI()`, `inst/api/openapi.json` hand-written); shinyapps.io does not host it. Tests: `test-tci-api.R` (the closed loop reproduces `tciSchedule()` rate for rate at zero latency; every error and warning code) and `test-tci-api-http.R` (in-process `pr$call()` on `rookRequest()` from `helpers.R`; skipped without plumber). [FauxTCI](https://github.com/StevenLShafer/FauxTCI) (served at https://stevenlshafer.github.io/FauxTCI/) is a browser pump built to this contract: a JavaScript port of the controller validated against fixtures that its `test/fixtures/generate.R` produces from this package, so a change to `tciNextRate()`'s numbers or to `docs/tci-api.md` is a change FauxTCI must follow.

**Time units** (`R/utils-time.R`; `docs/architecture.md#time-units`): minutes / hours / days / weeks are entry and display only — the engine, events, scenarios, `input$maximum` values, `simulationKey()` and exported Time columns stay in minutes. The app opens in minutes from 0 to `TIME_MAXIMUM_DEFAULT` (180), the intravenous default; the first repeating oral dose (`hasRepeatingOralDose()` in `R/scheduled.R`: a non-zero scheduled PO/SL dose or a `mg/day PO` rate) prescribed while the settings are still exactly that switches them once per session to days, 0 to `REPEATING_ORAL_PLOT_MINUTES` (30 days), through `timeApi$showTimeSettings()` (`repeatingOralSwitch` in `app_server()`; Shafer, 2026-11-13); a scenario, a restored bookmark, the long-term prompt or settings the user changed first settle it so it never fires. The app opens in elapsed time (`TIME_MODE_DEFAULT`, `"relative"`, which `timeFormat()` also takes for an unknown mode; actual/clock time is a choice for minutes and hours, and a bookmark that saved neither a format nor a Time Display is still read as clock, which it was made in). The dose table holds its Time strings as typed, in the format (`c(unit, mode)`) recorded by `doseTableFormat()`; `doseTableClean()` reads them with that record, never the selectors. ## Key Rules

- **Adding a drug** requires all six (full procedure: `docs/adding-a-drug.md`). Items 5 and 6 apply to a drug a user can dose (a `drugDefaults_global.csv` row with a `Category`); a metabolite-only entry needs only item 6, and the carrier gases and ventilation, with no `Category`, need neither:
  1. `R/drugs_<name>.R` (covariate model function)
  2. `inst/extdata/drugDefaults_global.csv` (row with colors, units, MEAC, and the `Category` that lists it in the startup menu)
  3. `tests/testthat/test-drugs-<name>.R` (unit test — pin values with `expect_equal_rounded()` from `tests/testthat/helpers.R`)
  4. `inst/help/drugs/<name>.md` (the narrative for the drug's generated help page; `test-help-drugs.R` fails without it)
  5. its row in `data-raw/pregnancyRegistry.R`, rebuilt into `inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv` (`test-pregnancy.R`; see *Pregnancy PK* below)
  6. its row in `inst/extdata/therapeuticRanges.csv` (`test-therapeutic-ranges.R`; see *Therapeutic ranges* below)
- **The program chooses the model, never the physician** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-11-09). stanpumpR never asks a clinician to pick between published models of one drug: most physicians have neither the data nor the expertise to make that choice, so offering it is an uninformed random selection. Where the library holds several models of the same drug, route and product, the program selects the one whose SOURCE POPULATION best matches the patient entered (absent anything better, by the age and weight ranges the source studied; a population the patient falls outside is not chosen while one that contains the patient exists), and the help page says which was chosen and why. Several models may still be DRAWN together where comparison is the point (the Minto/Schnider companions, the antiseizure "no interactions" companion), but the default curve is the program's choice. **The selector** (`R/modelSelection.R`, 2026-11-10): `MODEL_GROUPS` lists each drug's candidate models with their source populations (`ageMin`/`ageMax`, `weightMin`/`weightMax` where printed, `n`, `year`); `selectModel()` takes the candidates whose ranges include the patient and chooses the one fitted to MORE PATIENTS, then the newer (Shafer, 2026-11-10), or, if none includes the patient, the nearest by years then kilograms outside the ranges. `modelGroup()` builds the group's drug function, whose signature is the union of its candidates' (so `getDrugPK()`, the help pages, the Patient Profile field readers and `pregnancyDrugs()` need no change), calls the chosen candidate, prefixes its reference with the choice and records `$modelChosen`; a group's metabolite (`MODEL_GROUP_METABOLITES`: doxorubicin forms doxorubicinol) is itself a group on the same table, so parent and metabolite always come from one source. Folded: etoposide (`etoposideKroghMadsen`, `etoposidePediatric`, `etoposideDuong`), carboplatin (`carboplatinDuffull`, `carboplatinPediatric`), melphalan (`melphalanNath`, `melphalanPediatric`), methotrexate (`methotrexateIbarra`, `methotrexatePediatric`), vincristine (`vincristineIgarashi`, `vincristinePediatric`), oxaliplatin (`oxaliplatinBastian`, `oxaliplatinNikanjam`; displayed in ng/mL), fluorouracil (`fluorouracilBriki`, `fluorouracilTan`), cyclophosphamide (`cyclophosphamideJoerger`, `cyclophosphamideMcCune`), paclitaxel (`paclitaxelJoerger`, `paclitaxelCrombag`: Crombag 2019's 684 adults to 84 years, Joerger from 85; 2026-11-13), temozolomide (`temozolomideOstermann`, adults 30-79 years; `temozolomidePanetta`, Panetta 2003's 39 patients 0.7-21.9 years, BSA on CL/F and V/F, held proportional to BSA outside the studied 0.45-2.1 m2; the nearer wins between the ranges, Panetta to 25.9 years; 2026-11-16) and doxorubicin with doxorubicinol (`doxorubicinJoerger`/`doxorubicinolJoerger`, `doxorubicinPediatric`/`doxorubicinolPediatric`, `doxorubicinPerezBlanco`/`doxorubicinolPerezBlanco`; displayed in ng/mL). The candidates keep their functions, tests, help pages and CSV rows with no `Category` (not offered); a model function is unit-agnostic (its CSV row's `Concentration.Units` sets the amount unit), which is what lets one group display one unit. `cytarabine` predates the selector and chooses inside its own function. Not yet folded: the non-chemotherapy pairs (levodopa/levodopaSimon, milrinone/milrinonePediatric, digoxinIV/digoxinIVPediatric, apixaban/apixabanVTE, dabigatran/dabigatranHip, dapagliflozin/dapagliflozinKobuchi, liraglutide/liraglutideWatson and the like), some of which differ by indication or product rather than population and need Shafer's decision before they are grouped. A new alternative model is added as a candidate for that selector, with its source population's ranges recorded, not as a new name for the clinician to choose.

- **FDA labels** (`R/fda-labels.R`, `papers/labels/README.md`; Shafer, 2026-11-13): the US prescribing information for every drug a user can dose is kept in `papers/labels/` (public documents, unlike the archive's papers; `papers/` is not in the built package) and indexed by `inst/extdata/fdaLabels.csv`, one row per drug and label (`status` `label`, `no_us_label` or `not_obtained`, with a `note` saying why; `source` `archive` for a copy of the stanpumpRPapers PDF its digest card reads, never replaced, `dailymed` for the current label downloaded from DailyMed by its `setid`, or `drugsatfda` for one saved by hand from Drugs@FDA). Each drug's help page lists its labels as links to the files on GitHub (`helpDrugFdaLabelHTML()`). `data-raw/fdaLabels.R` checks the files and refreshes the DailyMed rows; Drugs@FDA (accessdata.fda.gov) blocks scripted downloads, so a label found only there is added by hand. A new drug with a `Category` needs its row (`test-fda-labels.R`). R CMD check tests the built package, which leaves `papers/` out, so the files are checked by their own workflow, `.github/workflows/fda-labels.yaml` (`checkFdaLabels()`: every listed PDF present with its md5, no unlisted PDF, every named card in `papers/digests/`).
- **Therapeutic ranges are required wherever one exists** (`docs/adding-a-drug.md`, *The therapeutic range*): every drug model's band (`Lower`/`Upper`/`Typical`) is its therapeutic reference range when one is established for the analyte, matrix and use the row plots, and its help narrative names the source (with a DOI), the analyte and the sampling time, or says why there is no band. Look in the class consensus guideline first: Table 5 of the AGNP-TDM update 2026 (Hart 2026, *Pharmacopsychiatry* 59:166-295, which supersedes Hiemke 2018 and covers psychiatric and antiseizure drugs, with alert levels and sampling times), then ILAE/Patsalos 2008 and 2018 for an antiseizure drug it omits, then the FDA label and EMA SmPC, then exposure-response analyses. A range for a parent plus a metabolite is drawn on one row only when that row reliably carries most of the sum (bupropion on hydroxybupropion). Every drug with a `Category` (`THERAPEUTIC_RANGE_CATEGORIES` is every category since 2026-10-27) and every metabolite of one has a row in `inst/extdata/therapeuticRanges.csv` (`R/therapeuticRanges.R`: analyte, matrix, sampling, limits, source, how far it was read, the range it replaced, the label's statement, `band_status`), printed on its help page and held to the band columns by `test-therapeutic-ranges.R`; a drawn range must have been read in a published source that states it — the guideline itself, or a guideline or harmonisation read in full that quotes it with its citation (Reimers 2018 quoting ILAE 2008 for ethosuximide), with `verification` saying which — never taken from memory; one not read so is `pending_verification` and is not drawn. Where Table 5's range is for another use or analyte than the band (ketamine for depression, methadone and morphine for opioid use disorder or pain, oxycodone for pain, buprenorphine for opioid use disorder, lorazepam for oral anxiolysis, midazolam for oral premedication, diazepam plus nordiazepam), the row is `band_differs`, records the guideline's range, keeps the library's band, and the help page prints both. A band that is not a therapeutic range at all (an anaesthetic's effect-site target, an opioid's MEAC multiple, an antibiotic's MBC band, a display range of what ordinary doses reach) is `library_band`, its notes saying what it is. The review of every category is `docs/therapeutic-ranges.md`.
- **Pregnancy PK is mandatory for every model** (`R/pregnancy.R`, `inst/extdata/pregnancyRegistry.csv` built by `data-raw/pregnancyRegistry.R`, `docs/pregnancy.md`, help page `inst/help/models/pregnancy.md`): every drug a user can dose (every `drugDefaults_global.csv` row with a `Category`) has one registry row giving what the literature says of its pharmacokinetics in late pregnancy, with sources, evidence read and the papers still to obtain; `test-pregnancy.R` fails without it. A new model therefore comes with a pregnancy literature search (PubMed and PubMed Central: a population model with a pregnancy covariate, a paired pregnant-against-postpartum study, a controlled comparison or a TDM series). Where a magnitude was measured, the model adds `pregnant = FALSE` as its last argument and applies the factor under `isTRUE(pregnant)`, with a named constant, a `PREGNANCY` header section giving source and arithmetic, and `pregnancyReference(applied, source)` appended to its `reference`; the registry row is `applied` and its `cl_factor`/`v_factor`/`f_factor` are what `test-pregnancy.R` checks against the model at the reference woman. A renal term takes the 50% rise in filtration through `pregnancyGfrFactor(pregnant, creatinine)`, which applies it only when the serum creatinine is blank (a measured pregnant creatinine already carries it). A change known only in direction, or measured too weakly, is recorded (`unquantified`), never applied; a model fitted in pregnant women is `source_pregnant`. Each drug's help page renders its row (`helpDrugPregnancyHTML()`). The flag is the third trimester/term; it reaches only models that name it, and the flag off must change nothing.
- **Body size scaling is mandatory** (`docs/weight-adjustment.md`): a drug function is `<name>(weight, height, age, sex, adjustToFFM = TRUE)` and either carries its own size covariate (Eleveld-style) or scales its 70 kg reference parameters with `pkSizeFactors()` — volumes by `$volume`, clearances by `$clearance` — passing the `legacy*` factors that reproduce the published scaling when the switch is off. Tests pin both switch positions. **The reference patient is a 70 kg, 170 cm, 35-year-old MAN, deliberately** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-30, `docs/weight-adjustment.md`, *Why the reference is a man*): it is an anchor, not a claim about the typical patient, and a woman is therefore scaled down by construction — `pkSizeFactors()` returns 0.824 on volumes and 0.865 on clearances at the same weight, height and age, but those are the GENERIC factors, and a model carrying its own weight covariate evaluates it at `pkWeight` instead, so alprazolam's own female ratio is 0.824 on BOTH its clearance and its volume. Do not apply 0.865 universally. Greenblatt 1993's alprazolam cohort (`papers/digests/PMID_8357297.md`) measures a slope matching the reference MAN in a cohort two-thirds women, which is a real tension and is recorded as one; the fix it suggests, a SAME-SEX reference, was considered and REJECTED, because it would give two patients with the same fat-free mass different pharmacokinetics (a 99.2 kg, 170 cm woman has the reference man's 54.5 kg exactly) and would substitute a claim of parity for the premise that lean tissue clears drug. Do not "fix" the male reference without reopening that decision.
- **Renal covariates**: a model with a creatinine-clearance or eGFR term adds `creatinine = NULL` to its signature (getDrugPK passes the Patient Profile's serum creatinine only to models that name it) and computes renal function with `R/renalFunction.R`, passing `adultEquivalentCreatinine(creatinine, age, sex)` to an equation fitted in adults (Cockcroft-Gault, CKD-EPI), which puts a child's creatinine on the adult scale, or `patientCreatinine(creatinine, age, sex)` only where the source applied its equation to children's own creatinine (pregabalin). A blank field is NULL, which means an assumed normal creatinine for the patient's age and sex (`assumedCreatinine()`: `SCR_ASSUMED_MALE` / `SCR_ASSUMED_FEMALE`, 0.9 / 0.8 mg/dL, from 18, the median for age below; the help pages and the test pins quote the adult values, so changing them is a change to both); the drug header and `reference` string must say so.
- **Serum albumin** (2026-11-06, Shafer): a model with an albumin term adds `albumin = NULL` (g/dL) to its signature; `getDrugPK()` passes the Patient Profile's **Serum albumin** (`MIN_ALBUMIN`-`MAX_ALBUMIN`, 0.5-6 g/dL, bounds chosen, not sourced) only to models that name it, and to a metabolite's model; NULL means the model's own reference value, which its header and `reference` must state. A power of albumin floors at `MIN_ALBUMIN`. Read by methotrexate (Ibarra: CL x (ALB/4)^0.594), methotrexatePediatric (Taylor: Q2 x1.17, Q3 x1.34 at or below 2.5 g/dL) and docetaxel (Bruno: + 0.225 x ALB in g/L, the mean 40.1 g/L when blank); the hover tip and the Cautions field-readers table are built from the signatures.
- **Serum AST** (2026-11-10, Shafer): a model with an AST term adds `ast = NULL` (U/L) to its signature; `getDrugPK()` passes the Patient Profile's **Serum AST** (`MIN_AST`-`MAX_AST`, 1-5000 U/L, bounds chosen, not sourced) only to models that name it, as it does `albumin`; NULL means the model's own reference, which its header and `reference` state. Read only by epirubicin (Ralph 2003: CL 72.9 x (1 - 0.135 ln AST) L/h, the AST held within the studied 7-815 U/L because the equation reaches zero clearance at 1648, and a normal 20 U/L when blank, `EPIRUBICIN_REFERENCE_AST`).
- **Dose basis: the approved product, unless the paper says otherwise** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-11-10). Unless a paper states the basis of its doses, the drug's units reflect the FDA-approved product, and its parameters are read in that product's units: if the approved product is the base, the PK are in units of the base; if it is a salt (the usual case), the PK are in units of the salt, and doses are entered as labelled with no conversion. Where the paper does state a basis that differs from the product (bendamustine, fludarabine, Joerger's doxorubicin), the conversion is made and the header says so. A header records which case applies.
- **Age terms**: a covariate term that is a negative power of age ((age / 65)^-0.429) is infinite at age 0, a legal entry, so it sees `max(age, ADULT_AGE_TERM_FLOOR)` (18, `R/constants.R`): apixaban, apixabanVTE, rivaroxaban, fondaparinux, paroxetine, citalopram, lisinopril, olmesartan, and `egfrMDRD()` in `R/renalFunction.R` (enoxaparin, fondaparinux and the antidiabetics that read it). **The rule: every age term floors at 18 unless the PK study explicitly permits a lower floor** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-29). A lower floor needs the source itself to have fitted the term in younger patients, and it is set at the youngest age the source studied, never below. Example: valproate's (age / 15)^-0.0154 (Teixeira-da-Silva 2022, which fitted patients aged 0.1 to 93 years) floors at 0.1 years (`VALPROATE_MIN_AGE`). Tests: `test-age-floor.R`.
- **No infinity, no division by zero** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-29): we cannot anticipate how everyone will use stanpumpR, so every model must stay finite for any legal patient (age 0 to 90, the full weight and height ranges, both sexes, both switch positions). `checkDispositionFinite()` in `R/getDrugPK.R` stops any model that hands the engines a volume that is not finite and positive or a clearance that is not finite and not negative, naming the drug and parameter; `test-pk-finite-guard.R` sweeps every drug over extreme patients. Guard a covariate term at its source (an age or weight floor, a bounded ratio) rather than relying on that stop: the stop turns a silent NaN into a clear error, but the patient still gets no curve.
- **Parents and their metabolites must agree** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-31): a parent that forms an active metabolite which is also a model in the library must be consistent with THAT model, because the metabolite curve is always drawn with the library's own metabolite model (`getDrugPK()` resolves it by name) and only the ratio of formation to metabolite clearance is identifiable from concentration data. So a parent never carries a retyped copy of a metabolite parameter: it reads it from the model (`libraryReferenceClearance()` in `R/metaboliteCoefficients.R`, or a call to the model as oxycodone and hydrocodone do), shares one definition with it (`enalaprilatSet()`, `prednisolonePairSystem()`), or was fitted jointly with it in one source. `METABOLITE_FORMATION_BASIS` records which, one row per parent, and `test-metabolite-consistency.R` holds every declared parent to a row and checks that a doubled metabolite clearance doubles a `reads` parent's formation. It runs both ways: **when you revise a metabolite's model, find its parents** (`METABOLITE_FORMATION_BASIS$parent[METABOLITE_FORMATION_BASIS$metabolite == "<drug>"]`) and, for a `joint` parent, refit or rescale its formation in the same pull request; **when you add or revise a parent**, check its metabolite's model. Codeine retyped morphine's 75.3 L/h after the morphine model had moved to 74.0, and sat 1.8% high until 2026-10-31.
- **Two oral absorption paths or a sum of systems**: a second lagged oral depot is `ka_PO2`/`fraction_PO2`/`tlag_PO2` (diclofenac, meloxicam; with a metabolite, aspirin, whose salicylate the metabolite engine forms from both depots); a drug plotted as the sum of independent linear systems returns `parallelSystems` + `doseFraction`, which `simCpCe()` runs separately and adds (ketorolac S + R); a system can be limited to some `routes`, which is how meloxicam's oral fit and its separate IV fit (ANJESO) share one drug. Both in `docs/adding-a-drug.md`.
- **Intravenous calcium** (`R/drugs_calcium.R`; Ansari 2025, parturients at cesarean delivery, offered to every patient as the only adult PK of intravenous calcium): one drug, `calcium`, for both salts, plotted as the *change* in ionized calcium, mmol/L, above an unmodelled baseline, so a patient given both gets one summed curve. Every unit names the salt (`g chloride`, `mg/min gluconate`; `calciumUnits` from `CALCIUM_SALTS` in `R/constants.R`, no bare `g`), read by `doseProduct()` in `R/routes.R`. The CSV `Concentration.Units` `mmol` means amounts in millimoles: the model returns `molar = list(molecularWeight = ...)`, grams of the product as dosed per mole of what is plotted, as one number or a vector named by product (`validateMolar()` in `getDrugPK.R`); `simCpCe()` converts each dose by the weight its unit names (`molarWeightOfDoses()`, which refuses a unit naming no product), and calcium gluconate's is calcium chloride's 147.01 × the fitted dose ratio 3.11. Plasma only, no band. Scenario `calcium-salt-substitution`. Tests: `test-drugs-calcium.R`.
- **Non-linear or non-mammillary sources** (saturable binding, reversible metabolite pairs, a racemate of two enantiomers) must be reduced to the linear engine with the reduction documented in the drug header: see `R/drugs_cefazolin.R`, `R/drugs_hydrocortisone.R`, `R/drugs_prednisolone.R`, `R/drugs_citalopram.R`. The exception is dose-dependent oral or sublingual bioavailability, which a drug declares as an `oralSaturation` or `sublingualSaturation` block and `simCpCe()` applies to each such dose before the engine (`oralSaturationFraction()` in `R/routes.R`; forms `saturable` (gabapentin, buprenorphine SL; an optional `hill`, suzetrigine), `rising` (sertraline) and `power` (paroxetine)). A pulsed extended-release product (Adderall XR) declares an `oralPulses` block: each `mg PO XR` dose becomes fixed fractions at fixed delays, each absorbed as the default oral form (`expandOralPulses()` in `R/oral-pulses.R`, run after the scheduled expansion; example `R/drugs_mixedAmphetamineSalts.R`; tests `test-oral-pulses.R`). The same block keyed to the reserved `ORAL_PULSE_DEFAULT_FORM` (`"default"`, and not alongside a formulation) instead pulses every plain `mg PO` row and leaves its unit alone — a different use of one mechanism: a formulation's pulses model a real product's release, where the default form's **discretise a published input shape the engine lacks** (hydrochlorothiazide's Weibull, `R/drugs_hydrochlorothiazide.R`), so a drug using it owes its header the shape it stands for and the measured cost of standing in for it.
- **Inverse Gaussian absorption** (`R/ig_absorption.R`, help page `inst/help/models/inverse-gaussian.md`, tests `test-ig-absorption.R`): a closed form for the convolution of an Inverse Gaussian input density with the disposition exponentials — the smooth, differentiable input that a lagged first-order or zero-order depot approximates with a discontinuity, and the first-passage-time density of Brownian motion with drift. `igAbsorptionConc()` and `igDensity()`, base R only: the complex branch needs the Faddeeva function and `faddeevaW()` (Weideman 1994) is vendored in, so there is **no dependency** — the 2026-08 draft called a CRAN package that does not exist, and `RcppFaddeeva`, the name #367 corrected it to, was archived from CRAN in 2022. That branch is the COMMON case, not an edge: `b = lambda/(2 mu^2) - k_i` is negative for a typical fast distribution exponent, in 57 of the 84 parameter sets swept. Exact to 1.6e-14 at concentrations above 1e-10 of peak, measured against two references the obvious one cannot supply — plain `stats::integrate()` has an ABSOLUTE accuracy floor and is itself wrong by 6e-8 at 1e-40 magnitudes and 1.7e-2 at 1e-318, so the test file keeps a tilted-IG-CDF form (real branch, no cancellation anywhere) and rescaled quadrature (both branches), with a guard asserting plain quadrature is the weaker of the two so that nobody "fixes" the closed form to agree with it. **NO DRUG DECLARES IT**, deliberately, and it is **not dead code**: the help page's generated figure (`ig-absorption-figure` in `R/help-generated.R`) calls it on every render, so deleting it reddens `test-help-content.R` as well as its own tests. #641 deleted the file on 2026-10-31 — correctly, it being 221 lines of comments with no caller, and CI stayed green — and the working version survived only in a stash; that is why the verification lives in a test file and not in an `if (FALSE)` block. Two decisions are open in its header, for Shafer: how a drug should declare it (a branch in the absorption dispatch, `R/advanceClosedFormPO_IM_IN.R`, NOT a dose-row rewrite like `oralZeroOrder` or `oralPulses`, it being a different input kernel rather than a re-expression of an existing one), and whether any drug converts to it. **No existing drug may be converted silently**: every model was fitted with its own input shape, so a conversion is a fitting question with its own error budget, measured at the kink times and held to the standard that drug already meets. Hydrochlorothiazide HAS been measured (2026-11-01) and REFUSED: an IG matches its published input's CV and skew to 2% and still lands 6.6x the documented standard, the whole error in the upstroke before 1.5 h, because Van Wart's depot is a time-varying rate constant and not an input density at all. Do not re-derive that.
- **Antibiotics: the MIC line and the MBC band** (`R/antibioticThresholds.R`, `R/antibioticMbc.R`; help page `inst/help/models/antibiotics.md`, generated block `antibiotic-mbc-table`): both markers are **free** drug. The threshold (`endCe`) is free drug at the MIC of the drug's main organism (`antibioticMicTable()`). The shaded band (`Lower`, `Typical`, `Upper`) is free drug at the expected MBC of the common pathogens (`antibioticMbcTable()`, one row per drug and pathogen, role `principal`, `core` or `excluded`), by one rule in `antibioticMbcBand()`: lower edge the principal pathogen's MBC50, typical its MBC90, upper edge the highest MBC90 among the pathogens the drug kills at attainable levels. Clindamycin's principal pathogen is not the MIC line's organism: it is bacteriostatic against *S. aureus*, which is `excluded`. A free concentration reaches the plotted curve through `antibioticPlottedConcentration()` (unbound; divided by fu; or the one-site saturable fit for ceftriaxone and clindamycin, whose `BindingKd`/`BindingCapacity` are in the MIC table). The model functions read their band from `antibioticMbcBand()`; `test-antibiotic-mbc.R` holds the table, the CSV, the models and the help in step and pins the bands. Most MBCs are an MIC times a published MBC/MIC ratio, said so in `Basis`; the help page lists the documents (EUCAST rationale documents, CLSI M100/M26-A, FDA STIC, the harmonised SmPCs behind the EMA Rocephin and vancomycin referrals, whose public summaries are read, unreadable 1970s-80s MBC tables) a revision should read first.
- **Pharmacogenetic covariates**: `cyp2d6`, `cyp2c19`, `cyp2c9` and `cyp2b6` reach only models that name them in their signature (`CYP2D6_VALUES`, `CYP2C19_VALUES`, `CYP2C9_VALUES`, `CYP2B6_VALUES` in `R/constants.R`; Patient Profile fields **CYP 2D6**, **CYP 2C19**, **CYP 2C9**, **CYP 2B6**). `cyp2c9` is read by phenytoin, on its `Vmax`, by meloxicam, and by the three vitamin-K antagonists, warfarin, fluindione and acenocoumarol. `cyp2b6` is read only by methadone, where it scales each enantiomer's non-renal clearance (Kharasch 2015; the rapid and ultrarapid phenotypes take the normal metaboliser's values, and `R/drugs_methadone.R` says why). A model that declares `cyp2c19` or `cyp2b6` gets a generated clearance-by-phenotype table on its help page (`R/help-drugs.R`). Scenarios: `codeine-cyp2d6`, `methadone-cyp2b6`, `phenytoin-saturable-metabolism`. Warfarin's genotypes are fields too: **VKORC1** (`VKORC1_VALUES`, also read by fluindione, acenocoumarol and the Hamberg companion) and **CYP4F2** (`CYP4F2_VALUES`, read only by the Xue model), whose defaults are **AA** and **CC**, each the most sensitive genotype, by Shafer's rule that an unentered genotype should predict the LARGER INR rise so a dose chosen from the curve errs toward underdosing (an underdose is corrected by raising the dose; an overdose risks bleeding and vitamin-K reversal) — so the VKORC1 default is deliberately NOT the most likely genotype outside Asian populations, and `VKORC1_DEFAULT` carries the ancestry distribution of the three genotypes (Limdi 2010) so a user can enter the likely one, as `inst/help/patient-profile.md` does for the user. `CYP2C9` keeps the most likely genotype, `*1/*1`, because that one field is shared with phenytoin and meloxicam. The three vitamin-K antagonists read it too, and for them `*1/*1` is not the most sensitive genotype that the VKORC1 rule would choose. Shafer decided (2026-10-31) to keep `*1/*1` as the most common diplotype (Moridani 2006, *Clin Biochem* 39:606-612: 77% of controls, 67% of an anticoagulation clinic), with `inst/help/patient-profile.md` (*The CYP2C9 default*) saying that a patient taking warfarin should be genotyped, that choosing the most common rather than the most sensitive genotype is his subjective judgement, and inviting feedback from clinicians who titrate warfarin and phenytoin. The four CYP fields and the Serum creatinine field each carry a hover tip naming the drugs that read them, built from the model signatures by `patientFieldTooltip()` in `R/patient-fields.R` (a note per drug in `PATIENT_FIELD_READER_NOTES`, e.g. meloxicam's renal term is intravenous only), so a new model that names a covariate joins its tip by itself; tests `test-patient-fields.R`.

- **Antihypertensives** (`R/antihypertensives.R`, `docs/antihypertensives.md`, help page `inst/help/models/antihypertensives.md`): evidence-gated, and **no drug in the library plots a blood pressure**. The commonly used antihypertensives share no defensible concentration-to-BP model, most have no complete published PK either, and where a response was fitted the endpoint is usually not a resting pressure (exercise SBP, exercise HR, isoproterenol-induced tachycardia, urinary sodium, ACE inhibition, renin activity, a MAP/HR resistance proxy, or the *probability* of crossing a MAP threshold). Twelve records are implemented as drugs, all concentration only -- `clevidipine`, `fenoldopamPediatric`, `carvedilol`, `azilsartan`, `chlorthalidone`, `hydrochlorothiazide`, `lisinopril`, `olmesartan`, `valsartan`, `benazepril`, `enalapril` (with the `enalaprilat` it forms) and `fosinopril`, the ones with something to say about them being: `clevidipine` (whole blood; Table 1's fitted CL and V1, read in full 2026-10-27, with the unprinted k12/k21 chosen to reproduce its derived Vss, half-lives and AUC share to their rounding; until then V1, V2 and Q were *derived* from CL, Vss and both half-lives, the abstract printing no V1 -- an abstract that omits a parameter is not a paper that omits it, `docs/antihypertensives.md` section 17) `fenoldopamPediatric` (named for its population, as `methylphenidatePediatric` is, so it cannot become an adult default; there is deliberately no adult `fenoldopam`) and `carvedilol` (apparent oral, **zero-order release**: `R/oral-zero-order.R` is the engine mode it needed, since Hwang 2023 reports first-order and dual absorption both failed to describe its densely sampled absorption). Overlapping releases are **summed**, because the infusion rate on the time line is a step function and independent row pairs would replace rather than add. `getDrugPK()` refuses the mode alongside an active metabolite, several oral formulations, saturable absorption, parallel systems, Michaelis-Menten elimination, an osmotic/molar basis, pulsed oral release or an active first-order oral depot (a positive `ka_PO`/`ka_PO2`, which the expansion would leave unused), and `expandOralZeroOrder()` itself refuses a standing oral rate row beside a release, which would be replaced rather than added to. Both reproduce all ten exposures the paper simulated, which the tests pin. `hydrochlorothiazide` (Van Wart 2013; apparent oral, plasma, renal covariate only, `R/drugs_hydrochlorothiazide.R`), whose published absorption is a **Weibull** (shape 2.84) and therefore an input shape with no constant `ka` at all: it is DISCRETISED into eight pulses of the default oral form (the `ORAL_PULSE_DEFAULT_FORM` key described above, which this drug is the first to need), absorbed at exactly 1.0 /h because that is the Weibull's own asymptotic rate constant rather than a fitted number, with the fractions and delays fitted MINIMAX -- the objective is the worst point, which is what the standard limits -- over the paper's four renal functions so that one pulse set serves every patient; `test-drugs-hydrochlorothiazide.R` checks the result against an independent numerical integration of the exact Weibull system at each of those four, not against the fractions, and its bounds ARE the documented standard (0.53% over the curve, 0.01% at the peak) rather than a loose fence. **Measure such an error at the pulse times**: it peaks exactly where a lagged depot starts and the approximation kinks, and `simulationTimeGrid()` puts a point at every dose time, so a uniform grid that steps over the kinks flatters the approximation -- on this drug a 0.02 h grid said 0.45% where the truth was 0.64%, and the six-pulse set it validated was outside the standard. Clevidipine was the registry's first `external_validation_status` other than `none` (enalapril is the second; see below): Ericsson's second 1999 paper (Eur J Clin Pharmacol, read in full) reports as measurements the V1, k10, bolus intercepts and fast-phase AUC share that the implemented record then derived, and the derivation applied to that study's own four numbers returns its measured V1 to 1.1% (0.09 of its standard error); because that paper is a complete fitted model the library deliberately does not plot, `execution_status` gained `complete_not_implemented` for records read and verified but used only to check an implemented one (`docs/antihypertensives.md` section 11). Four published endpoint relations are evaluable through `antihypertensiveEndpoint()` against a concentration the caller supplies; it returns the *change* the drug term predicts, never an absolute pressure, and every Emax is stored as a positive magnitude with an explicit direction so `C = EC50` gives half the **fall**. `enalapril` and its active metabolite `enalaprilat` (Faisal 2019, *Front Pediatr* 7:281; `docs/antihypertensives.md` section 16) are the registry's only ORAL record with **absolute** rather than apparent disposition: both moieties are cleared renally with no further metabolism, so fitting urine with serum closes the mass balance and bioavailability became a parameter of its own (0.606, against the literature's 60%). The parent is one compartment behind eight Erlang transit compartments, eliminating renally (`KREN`) and by conversion (`KM`); the metabolite is two compartments behind two more transits — which is why the library has `advanceTransitChain.R`. `enalaprilat` is metabolite-only (no `Units`, no `Category`, as `salicylate` is) and reads its parameters from the one place that defines them, `enalaprilatSet()`, so its help page and the plotted curve cannot drift; intravenous enalaprilat is deliberately NOT offered although the product exists and these parameters are absolute, because they were fitted to metabolite formed from an oral dose. The only covariate is weight¹ on the parent's central volume, normalised to the study mean 69.76 kg, so **there is no renal term although both moieties are cleared renally and by nothing else** — the sharpest form of that gap in the group. It is also the registry's second `external_validation_status` other than `none`, and the first validated against MEASUREMENTS rather than a second derivation: Arafat 2005 (*J Clin Pharm Ther* 30:319-28), two brands of 10 mg enalapril maleate measured with specific immunoassays, reports metabolite peaks of 54.8 and 57.2 ng/mL where this model gives 55.9 — and **that is what settles the dose basis**, which Faisal's text leaves open (whether F1 = 0.606 absorbs the maleate-to-base fraction 0.764), because a factor of 1.308 either way falls outside the measured range. **A basis ambiguity a source leaves open is often decidable from a study that never entered the fit**, which is the same reason the illicit-drug rule above demands an unfitted check. The same study disagrees by 1.9x on the metabolite AREA and its authors report their own 12-24 h concentrations as lower than the literature's, so that is recorded not reconciled (the Sun-1994-against-benazepril rule); and its PARENT is no check at all, the authors saying their enalapril peaks are "about 10 times those published in the literature" — **a validation source's own caveats about its assay are part of the comparison, and a study can check one analyte and not another**. Section 16 also records three corrections the full text forced on section 14.3, two of which are one mistake: **a quantity is not "ambiguous" because the parameter table alone does not settle it** — read the prose that defines the parameters before reaching for section 14.3's rule, and resolve a source's identifier against the index rather than transcribing it (section 14.3 cited a journal and DOI that do not exist, while every number it recorded was right). `fosinopril` (Hui 1991, full text; `docs/antihypertensives.md` section 18) is the registry's THIRD external check and the first that DISAGREES, and it is in the library at all because a NOT PURSUED verdict turned out to be wrong twice over: "no longer in clinical use" (it is still used as a generic, Shafer 2026-10-29) and an identifiability failure whose arithmetic was right about Gehr 1993 and was then carried to Hui 1991, which nobody had opened -- the row's own text admitting the check "was applied before they were read". **A section 4 verdict is about one source's printed numbers, not about a drug**, which is the same failure mode as sections 16 and 17: a judgment about a document, formed without the document. Hui's Table IV prints SIX numbers per group (V_init, CL, Vss, three half-lives), exactly what a three-compartment model has, and they close it LINEARLY because the Methods define the area as A/alpha+B/beta+C/gamma and V_init as Dose/(A+B+C), so the three intercepts solve three linear equations; all 729 corners of the printed SEM box are admissible, where esmolol's collapse. ONE drug, oral only, plotted as fosinoprilat: the diacid exists only as a research dose, so unlike enalapril/enalaprilat there is no second drug, and the ester hydrolysis is taken as complete in the gut before absorption, with the dose basis READ from the Methods (7.5 mg fosinoprilat "equimolar to 10 mg fosinopril sodium", so 0.75) rather than computed from molecular weights. Its absorption comes from Singhvi 1988 (PMID 2967089) and its bioavailability is that paper's CAPSULE value of 0.29, read -- see the bioavailability rule above, which this drug's first draft is the cautionary case for. Its renal covariate is a **two-plateau ramp, not a proportion**, because clearance falls 42% from normal to impaired (p < 0.001) and is then FLAT across 11-72 mL/min/1.73 m2 as the hepatobiliary route takes 63-85% of a shrinking total -- and the US LABEL contradicts it in words, saying clearance "does not differ appreciably from normal" over a creatinine clearance of 10 to 80, which reconciles only if read as flatness ACROSS that range (its own healthy clearance being 26-39 mL/min, half of which is the plateau's 15.5). Only the clearance carries the covariate, which is what makes the impaired terminal half-life a PREDICTION: 14.7 h against the measured 14.0, 14.1 and 16.9, numbers the model was never told. **A header that states a consequence the code does not produce is a test waiting to be written** -- the first draft re-derived the whole vector at the impaired clearance, which holds the half-lives fixed by construction, and the test written for the header's claim caught it. Before adding a record, check identifiability the way `docs/antihypertensives.md` section 4 does: for two compartments, CL + Vss + *both* half-lives closes the system, CL + Vss + the terminal half-life alone does not, and a formally closed system can still be too ill-conditioned to use (esmolol).

- **Comparing sources: only clearance is real** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-27). Clearance is the one pharmacokinetic parameter that measures a physiological process. Every other number — the volumes, intercompartmental clearances, rate constants, the number of compartments — is a mathematical construct that usefully relates the dose given to the concentration measured; none maps to an anatomical space or an organ, so do not read V1 as plasma volume or V3 as fat, and do not "correct" one source's volumes against another's. Those constructs also carry the artifacts of how a study was done, which change with its date while the drug does not: **central volumes fall with publication date**, because early studies sampled late (so the back-extrapolation to time 0 is lower, V1 higher) and drew venous blood, where later ones sample early and arterially; and **total volumes rise with publication date**, because more sensitive assays follow the drug longer, which lengthens the terminal half-life and the volume computed from it. So when two sources disagree, compare clearance first; treat a difference in V1, the terminal half-life or the terminal volume as weak evidence about the drug unless the sampling site, the first sample time and the assay's limit are comparable; and note that an artifact has a direction (an insensitive assay shortens a half-life, it cannot lengthen one), which can rule it out. Example: tramadol's ULTRAM label check, `R/drugs_tramadol.R`.
- **A difference of two measured numbers is a fraught measurement** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-29). The variance of a difference is the sum of the variances, so **the difference between two means is easily dwarfed by their scatter** -- and a paper will often print the difference as though it were a measurement in its own right. Two cases, and the printed uncertainty tells you which you have. INDEPENDENT samples: the SEM of the difference is `sqrt(se1^2 + se2^2)`, which can exceed the difference itself; benazepril's whole two-compartment derivation rests on `AUC(0-inf) - AUC(0-24h)`, whose two areas come from n = 45 and n = 60, so they are NOT paired and its header is right to call the resulting 25% a FLOOR rather than a bound. PAIRED samples (the same subjects in both arms): positive correlation shrinks it below that sum, and **comparing the printed SEM of the difference with the independent sum is how you detect the pairing** -- Hui's mean absorption times print SEMs of 1.3, 3.0 and 4.4 h where independence would give 3.05, 3.59 and 7.32, implying correlations of 0.83, 0.70 and 0.93, so he computed them per subject and his standard errors are honest. Either way, TEST THE DIFFERENCE BEFORE BELIEVING A TREND IN IT: the trend across impairment that Hui's text claims for those same honest numbers reaches only t = 1.2, 0.2 and 1.1, so his own data do not support his own sentence (`docs/antihypertensives.md` section 18.6). And a model's value for such a quantity is usually not a measurement at all -- fosinopril's `1/ka` carries no standard error, because `ka` was set to a time-to-peak midpoint -- so it cannot be put in a significance test against one.
- **Bioavailability is read, never fitted to another study's exposure** (2026-10-29, from fosinopril's correction). F is clearance-INDEPENDENT by construction: a bioavailability study is a within-subject crossover, `F = (AUC_po/D_po)/(AUC_iv/D_iv)`, so the subject's clearance cancels out of it. So when a drug's DISPOSITION comes from one study and its ABSORPTION from another -- which in this library is most oral drugs with an intravenous source -- **read F from the absorption study and let the model compute the exposure**. Fitting F so that the model reproduces the absorption study's own concentrations while it runs on the disposition study's clearance double-counts the difference between the two populations, and every patient's curve is then wrong by that ratio: fosinopril's first draft did exactly this and came out a fifth low, because Singhvi's volunteers cleared the drug 1.47-fold faster than Hui's patients. The exposure difference between the two sources is a FINDING to state, not an error to absorb into a parameter. A second trap sits beside it for any radiolabel study: **a secondary source can relabel an analyte**, and a review that quotes "plasma fosinoprilat" areas was quoting TOTAL RADIOACTIVITY, of which only 75% was the diacid, and its peak range ran from the diacid's lowest peak to total radioactivity's highest, which is the error to expect when the paper reports both (`R/drugs_fosinopril.R`, `docs/antihypertensives.md` section 18.6).
- **A label is frozen at approval; later findings outrank it** (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-10-29). A label is published on the best available science *at the time of approval*, and after that the science advances and the label does not. The reason is institutional rather than scientific: asking the FDA to change a label exposes a company to fresh regulatory scrutiny of matters it believed settled, so there is a standing disincentive to revise. **So a finding published AFTER the label's date takes precedence over the label.** Read that as a statement about the label's STANDING, not as a date comparison: the later finding still has to address the same question -- the same population, analyte and fact, on evidence that applies to the patient being simulated. A later paper that does not speak to the label's claim does not displace it, and a different species or a different population is not a later finding about this one. What the rule denies is a label's authority as CURRENT science; it does not waive the relevance test every source has to pass. Two corollaries worth knowing. First, **a revision date is not a review date**: the archived fosinopril label is "Revised: 10/2021" and its renal-impairment paragraph is still a 1991-era summary, so the date on the front tells you nothing about which paragraphs were revisited. Second, the rule has a companion for the case where nothing came after: where a label's PROSE and the study it rests on disagree, the study's NUMBERS win and the prose is what the user has to be warned about -- a label sentence is a regulatory summary written to guide dosing, so "does not differ appreciably" is a clinical judgment where a paper's 42% at p < 0.001 is a measurement (`R/drugs_fosinopril.R`, `docs/antihypertensives.md` section 18.4). A label remains the best source for what it alone records -- the dose basis, the salt, the marketed products, a molecular weight -- and the usual place to start when no population model exists (methylene blue, ephedrine).

- **The stop hook's "unpushed commits" warning is usually wrong in a cloud session** (2026-11-01). The cloud container is a shallow clone whose fetch refspec covers only `master`, so `origin/<branch>` is never updated after a push, and a branch GitHub deleted on merge keeps its stale ref. The stop hook (`~/.claude/stop-hook-git-check.sh`, which the cloud launcher writes into the container at every session start together with `launcher-settings.json`; it is Anthropic's harness, not this repository and not the user's own settings) counts `origin/<branch>..HEAD` and so reports unpushed commits that are already on GitHub, about twenty times in one evening, and "no remote branch" for a branch that has been merged. Do not push again on its word and do not change the git configuration: verify once against the remote itself: the SHA field of `git ls-remote --heads origin refs/heads/<branch>` (`| cut -f1`; the output is the SHA, a tab and the ref name) equal to `git rev-parse HEAD`, or, where the remote has since moved on, containing it (`git fetch origin <branch> && git merge-base --is-ancestor HEAD FETCH_HEAD`); after a merge, the pull request itself (`gh api repos/<owner>/<repo>/pulls/<n> --jq .merged`), which holds for every merge method where an ancestry test does not (a squash or rebase merge rewrites the commits, and a shallow clone may not hold the history the test needs); and then treat the warning as noise for that head. The same stale ref also makes `origin/<branch>` an unsafe base for a new commit: before committing on a branch another push may have moved, fetch the SHA the PR reports and move the branch onto it (`git fetch origin <sha> && git checkout -B <branch> <sha>`, only while the branch carries no local commits of your own, which `checkout -B` would discard: rebase any onto the fetched SHA instead; a fetch alone only records the commit in `FETCH_HEAD` and leaves the branch where it was), or the push is refused as a non-fast-forward (#654, 2026-11-01). The fix belongs in the hook, comparing HEAD with `git ls-remote`'s answer before warning, but the launcher's own permission rules deny editing the file and a fresh copy arrives with every session, so neither a session nor the user can apply it: the warning has to be read as this note says, and the fix is feedback for Anthropic (drafted 2026-11-01).
- **Deploy**: GitHub Actions — PRs auto-deploy to a test environment; a push to `master` deploys to production (shinyapps.io) only after `R-CMD-check` has passed on it (`workflow_run` in `shiny-deploy-production.yaml`), and the deploy installs that exact commit (`@sha`), fails if the installed `RemoteSha` differs, and writes the SHA into the deployed `config.yml` as `commit`. The version and that commit are the first line of the debug log and the tooltip of the Source link (`stanpumpRBuild()`; the config first, the DESCRIPTION's `RemoteSha` as a fallback, since shinyapps.io does not carry it), so a live session can be matched to a commit. A manual run (`workflow_dispatch`) deploys the commit it is run on without the check. **Preview security** (2026-11-02, from an external audit, verified): the PR preview (`shiny-pr-2-deploy.yaml`, `pull_request_target`) runs the PR's own code in the job that holds the shinyapps.io token (renv restore and Rscript read the PR's `.Rprofile`; installing the package runs its R code), and a shinyapps.io token is account-wide, so the preview must never hold production's: it deploys only branches of this repository (never a fork; review a fork and push its branch here), only PRs that change more than `papers/`, carries no mail credential (Email Slide in a preview says "Email is not configured"), takes `SHINY_TOKEN`/`SHINY_SECRET`/`SHINY_ACCOUNT` from the `preview` environment (a separate shinyapps.io account), and posts its comment from a job that runs no PR code. Production's token and the mail credential belong only in the `production` environment. Every third-party Action is pinned to a full commit SHA. **Accepted risk** (Shafer, 2026-11-02): master and `production` have no required reviewer, so one compromised maintainer or agent identity can reach production; kept for the speed of automatic merging, to be revisited if traffic grows.
- **Keeping the models current**: a weekly Claude Code Routine in the cloud (`docs/literature-watch.md`) searches PubMed for new PK studies of every drug in the CSV, screens them and emails the maintainer the promising ones with an assessment; it never writes code. Incorporating a paper is an ordinary drug addition or update started by the maintainer. A change to what the routine does is recorded on that page.
- **Windows binary**: every push to `master` rebuilds a Windows binary zip (`.github/workflows/windows-binary.yaml`), installs it into an empty library, runs `.github/scripts/windows-smoke-test.R` under a timeout, and replaces the rolling release `windows-binary` (one publish at a time, skipped when master has moved past the build; asset `stanpumpR.zip`, one file for x64 and ARM since the package has no compiled code). It is the documented way to install on Windows (README, `inst/help/scripting.md`, `inst/apidocs/api.md`): a source install there can fail with a bare "lazy loading failed" because Windows 11 Smart App Control blocked a DLL it could not verify during the build.
- **Adding an R package**: add to `DESCRIPTION` first, then `renv::install("pkg")` + `renv::snapshot()`, commit `DESCRIPTION` + `renv.lock` together. For a package that isn't on CRAN, also add it under `Remotes:` in `DESCRIPTION` (as `user/repo`; none is needed today, `undomanager` now coming from CRAN) and install with `renv::install("user/repo")`, so the deploy can resolve it. A package used only by an optional entry point (plumber for the TCI API and the Simulation API) goes in `Suggests` and is guarded with `requireNamespace()`. It is still snapshotted: `renv/settings.json` sets `snapshot.dev: true`, so `renv::snapshot()` records `Suggests` too, which is what puts devtools (the Quick Commands), rsconnect (the deploy workflows) and testthat in `renv.lock`. That does no harm, because the deploy workflows delete `renv.lock` before `rsconnect::deployApp()`, so the lock never reaches the deployed app; the `requireNamespace()` guard is what keeps the app working without the package.


## Source papers (the private archive on Google Drive)

The PDFs behind the models and the `papers/digests/` cards are the private archive `papers/README.md` describes: the Google Drive folder **`stanpumpRPapers`**, ID `1Hi6HmnULQsIlETZw4lo5rhxkfog33vRR` (about 1,120 PDFs plus supplements, labels and notes). A cloud session reads it through the claude.ai **Google Drive connector** (`mcp__Google_Drive__*`, loaded with ToolSearch). Verified 2026-10-30:

- **Check access first**: `get_file_metadata` on the folder ID. "Requested entity was not found" means the connector cannot see the folder: check the ID first (the folder is in My Drive, not a shared drive), and if the ID is right, the likely cause is the one met on 2026-10-30, a connector granted per-file access only (it then sees just the files Claude itself created), which the user fixes by reconnecting Google Drive with full access at claude.ai/customize/connectors. Connectors are read when a session starts.
- **Find a paper by name, never by listing the folder.** A card's `file:` field is the PDF's exact Drive title, so `search_files` with `title = 'PMID_30185446.pdf'` finds it, and `title contains 'PMID_30185446'` finds it with its supplements (`_mmc1`, `_sup1`, `_ESM1`, `_SDC1`, `_S5.tif`, `_erratum`). Listing by `parentId` breaks down after about 300 results (later pages repeat earlier ones and stop adding new files), so it cannot enumerate the archive. A first page can come back empty with a `nextPageToken`: keep paging until there is no token before concluding a file is absent.
- **Read the PDF itself, not Drive's text rendering.** `read_file_content` scrambles two-column and older layouts — words, table rows and columns interleaved — so a parameter must never be read from it. Instead `download_file_content` the file: the base64 result is too large for the context and is saved to a tool-results file, which `jq -r '.content' <saved file> | base64 -d > <scratchpad>/papers/<title>` turns back into the PDF (check its size against the Drive `fileSize`). Then `pdftotext -layout` (installed) keeps the columns and tables aligned, and the Read tool shows the pages themselves for a table or figure the text leaves ambiguous.
- **Pre-extracted copies**: the archive's pipeline keeps a per-document text extraction in `_build/text` (folder ID `15t6cris4d2O94AFNbUGntzsLLYfCt2YN`) and a JSON file in `_build/context` (`1Ukcl3qgZAVaLA6z0j7baIeZYHh5R9Fde`), each named by the stem of the card's `file:` with `.txt` or `.json` (`PMID_35157004.txt`, `NJ_label-naropin_020533_2022-06-16.txt`). They are convenient for searching a paper, but a number that goes into a model is confirmed on the PDF.
- **Read-only, and the papers stay out of the repository.** Never create, edit, move or trash anything in the archive (`_build/` and its digest pipeline own it). A downloaded PDF or its extracted text goes in the session's scratchpad and is never committed: the sources are copyrighted, and the digests are publishable only because they hold facts and paraphrase, not copies (`papers/README.md`).


## The Help Tab (`R/help-*.R`, `inst/help/`)

The in-app help is a `bslib::nav_panel("Help")` with a sidebar (search + contents) and one `uiOutput`. Pages are either Markdown in `inst/help/<id>.md` (rendered by `shiny::markdown()`, i.e. commonmark — no pandoc, no MathJax) or generated from the code: one page per drug (`help-drugs.R` runs the drug model at six reference patients: volumes and clearances, half-lives and ke0, the extravascular routes' `ka`/bioavailability/lag, and for a Michaelis-Menten drug a **Saturable elimination** section giving Vmax, Km, the peripheral volume implied by `k23`/`k32` and the salt factors — without which the first two tables describe a saturable drug as a linear one, since its CL1 is only the limit below Km and its peripheral compartment is not V2), one per teaching scenario (`help-scenarios.R`), plus the drug index, scenario index and bibliography. `help-content.R` holds the registry (`helpStaticPages()`), the renderer and the search; `help-server.R` is called once from `app_server()`. Links between pages are `[text](help:page-id)`; `[text](scenario:id)` makes a button that calls `applyHelpScenario()`, which writes `doseTable()`/`eventTable()` and the inputs, then `bslib::nav_select()`s the Simulator. All clicks go through three delegated handlers in `inst/www/app.js` (`help_goto`, `help_scenario_load`, `investigator_show`), so the help adds no per-page inputs. One page is a calculator that runs entirely in the browser: **Converting between parameter sets** (`models/pk-converter`, 2026-11-01), Shafer's spreadsheet Convert.xls (five ways of defining a 1-, 2- or 3-compartment mammillary model, each giving all the rest), a table of the whole model in one fixed layout whichever panel defined it (volumes, clearances, exponents, half-lives, coefficients, fractional coefficients, micro rate constants: Shafer's order, 2026-11-01; the fractional coefficients are output only, since they cannot define a model), plus ke0 from the time to peak effect, an SVG graph of the unit disposition function and the effect site, and the plasma and effect-site decrement times (any percentage, 50 by default) against the duration of a TCI infusion that held that site constant, the infusion being R/tci.R's Shafer-Gregg controller ported step for step (`tciTrajectory()`; `test-help-pk-converter.R` holds the port to R's `tciRate()` to 1e-8).
