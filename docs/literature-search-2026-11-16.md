# PubMed sweep for population pharmacokinetic sources, 2026-11-16

A one-time backlog search, done on Steven L. Shafer's request of 2026-11-16: "Search PubMed for
papers that describe the population pharmacokinetics of approved drugs in humans. Identify those
papers that would bring new drugs, or better pharmacokinetics, into stanpumpR." The weekly
literature watch (`docs/literature-watch.md`) only looks at papers entered in the past eight days on
drugs already in the library. This sweep looks back over the whole literature and also covers drugs
the library does not have yet.

**What this is and is not.** Every citation comes from PubMed: each PMID was fetched with the PubMed
connector, and each was checked for a free PubMed Central copy. Each paper was screened from its
abstract only. A number quoted here is what the abstract says, and "not in abstract" means the value
has to be read in the full text. No paper has been checked against its full text, and no paper here
may be implemented from these notes. Under `docs/adding-a-drug.md`, every parameter must be read in
the paper itself, and an abstract that omits a parameter is not a paper that omits it (section 17 of
`docs/antihypertensives.md`). Each drug got about three searches, so a better model may still be
found under other search terms.

**Method.** Six parallel searches, one per area:
1. new perioperative drugs;
2. upgrades to the anaesthesia, opioid, local anaesthetic, neuromuscular and analgesic models;
3. anti-infectives;
4. cardiovascular, ICU and haemostasis drugs;
5. neurology and psychiatry;
6. obstetrics, transplant, oncology, antidotes and the "Other" category.

Each new candidate was checked against `inst/extdata/drugDefaults_global.csv` and the seven
registries (anticoagulant, antidiabetic, antihypertensive, antiseizure, chemotherapy, Parkinson
disease, vasoactive). Each upgrade was compared with the `reference` string of the current
`R/drugs_<name>.R`. A paper was kept if it was a human population or compartmental PK study that
reported parameters with covariates, or a PK/PD link (ke0 or time to peak effect). Reviews, animal
work, assay papers, NCA-only studies, PBPK-only studies and case reports were dropped. I re-fetched
a sample of 25 of the reported PMIDs myself and compared each with what its search reported; all 25
matched.

**Priority.** A papers are important for perioperative or ICU care, or are a common drug, and have a
complete model that looks implementable. B papers are useful but niche, need a covariate the app
lacks, or keep their details in the full text. C papers are marginal.

**Totals.** 279 papers kept across six tables (Appendices 1-6), 54 of them A. Each table lists
the 1-3 best papers per drug, followed by the drugs searched without a usable result, and notes.

## 2. Questions for you

These came up in more than one search and need your decision before the papers they affect can be
used.

1. **Total or free drug for the new beta-lactams.** Most of the new antibiotic models report total
   concentration. The library plots unbound cefazolin and uses saturable binding for ceftriaxone and
   clindamycin (`antibioticPlottedConcentration()`), so each new beta-lactam needs the same choice
   made before it is added.
2. **Inverse-Gaussian absorption.** Gaudreault 2012 (ropivacaine after femoral nerve block, PMID
   23054251, *J Pharmacokinet Pharmacodyn*) fits block absorption as an inverse-Gaussian input plus a
   slow first-order input. It is the first drug source found that would exercise `R/ig_absorption.R`,
   so it bears on the decision still open in that file's header.
3. **An intravenous lag.** Landiolol (Honda 2008) has a 0.82-min lag after IV dosing. Either the lag
   is dropped and its cost measured, or the IV engine gets a lag field.
4. **Labetalol in pregnancy.** Fischer 2014 (PMID 24297680) gives an oral clearance factor for
   pregnancy (1.4x at 12 weeks, 1.6x at 40 weeks). The library's labetalol is intravenous only, and
   its pregnancy registry row records the IV disposition as unchanged (Rubin 1983). The two do not
   disagree in finding, but taking Fischer would mean adding an oral route.
5. **Covariates the app does not have**, which recur across the B rows: birth weight, a bypass flag,
   alpha-1 acid glycoprotein, time since surgery, heart failure and body temperature. Each would be
   held at a reference value unless a field is added.
6. **Esketamine's hepato-portal compartment.** It needs a documented reduction to a linear
   mammillary model with first-pass loss, under the rule for non-mammillary sources.

### Notes

- A-priority rows (5): dantrolene (Podranski 2005), clonidine (Potts 2007), palonosetron (Lee 2019),
  succinylcholine (Roy 2002), nalmefene (Kyhl 2016). Each has a complete IV disposition model in an
  adult or a size-scaled population; two (Roy, Potts) carry the effect site or extravascular routes too.
- Classic anaesthesia drugs (methohexital, thiopental, pancuronium, atracurium, pyridostigmine,
  promethazine, diphenhydramine, prochlorperazine) have only 1980s-1990s sources whose parameters are
  in the full text, not the abstract; Hudson 1983 (methohexital) has no abstract or DOI in PubMed.
- Structures stanpumpR cannot take as published: esketamine's hepato-portal compartment and split
  nasal/swallowed input; atracurium's peripheral elimination; mivacurium's isomers and interstitial
  link compartment; naltrexone XR's 3-phase release; liposomal bupivacaine's biphasic release.
- Covariates held at reference if implemented: bilirubin and total protein (tapentadol), SIRS and
  hepatic dysfunction (pantoprazole PICU), race (esketamine), post-cardiac-surgery volumes (clonidine).
- US-market status statements not taken from PubMed (levobupivacaine, edrophonium, dolasetron) are
  flagged "to verify"; the fospropofol and nalmefene statements come from the PubMed abstracts cited.
- One butorphanol search hit the PubMed rate limit and was not repeated; no butorphanol population
  model was found by the other searches.

- **remifentanil**: Eleveld 2017 (current) plus Kim 2017 for obesity cover the range. Nothing better.
- **remimazolam**: Eleveld 2025 (current, 6-93 y, 21-171 kg, 20 studies) is the best available. Masui 2022 (PMID 35708787) and Colin 2025 (above, C) add nothing it lacks.
- **sufentanil**: no new IV adult or paediatric model better than Gepts 1995. Candidates found were narrow: CPB (Khaowroongrueng 2024, PMID 39039947, n=20), ECMO, ventilated neonates (Pokorná 2021, individual fits), epidural in children (Borsuk 2017), and epidural in labour (Nie 2025, already in the archive). - **midazolam IN/buccal/IM**: no population model of an FDA-approved nasal or IM product surfaced (Brill 2014 covers oral).
- **vecuronium**: Vega-Villa 2014 (current) is the newest population model; nothing better.
- **cisatracurium in ICU / children**: Boyd 1996 (PMID 8785138, ICU, 1-cmt NONMEM, V 21.9 L, CL 549 mL/min, n=6). Too small; no paediatric popPK found.

- **bupivacaine (liposomal, Exparel)**: no population compartmental model surfaced in PubMed for the search used.

### Notes

- **Highest-yield upgrades:** oxytocin (the current source is unpublished data; Shafer 2025 BJA is a published LC/MS model with an IN arm). Fentanyl (the current parameter set is printed in no paper; Bae 2020 prints a full 3-cmt set and a MEAC). Dexmedetomidine (Morse 2020's universal model would replace the Dyck/Zuppa age switch). Hydromorphone and morphine (Meissner 2024 PK + Olofsen 2026 ke0 on the same 51 volunteers).
- **Effect-site gaps found:** ketamine (assumed tPeak 3 min; Olofsen 2022 measured t½ke0 8.3 min); hydromorphone (19.6 min tPeak against t½ke0 0.75 h); morphine (0.71 h); dexmedetomidine IN/IV Ramsay t½ke0 3.3 min (Li 2018, against the MOAA/S-based 14.9-min tPeak in use). Rocuronium already carries a ke0. Cortínez 2007 (PMID 17681967) is in the archive.
- **Covariates the app lacks:** birthweight (Wu 2021 fentanyl), CPB flag (TXA, sufentanil, lidocaine cardiac models), AAG (Aarons 2011 ropivacaine), postoperative time (Foong 2025 lidocaine). Hold these at reference or treat the population as a separate candidate in a `MODEL_GROUPS` selector, as AGENTS.md prescribes for population-specific alternatives (the program chooses the model, never the physician).
- **New routes the library lacks:** dexmedetomidine IN, ketamine IM, TXA IM/PO, midazolam PO, oxycodone IM/buccal/epidural, sufentanil SL, aprepitant PO, oxytocin IN.
- **ID converter:** it refused PMIDs 41656122 and 41474167 (2026). Their PMCIDs above come from the PubMed record.
- Gaudreault 2012's inverse-Gaussian block absorption is the first drug source found that would exercise `R/ig_absorption.R`. That file's header leaves open both how a drug declares it and whether any drug converts, so this is for Shafer to decide.

### Searched, nothing usable

- nafcillin: no human population PK paper on PubMed. Flucloxacillin/cloxacillin models exist (e.g. cloxacillin in arthroplasty, Beijer 2026) but neither drug is marketed in the US.
- acyclovir (adult IV): no adult population model found in two searches; the only adult popPK is Kably 2025 (oral valaciclovir after liver transplant, listed as C).
- valacyclovir / valganciclovir: covered by the acyclovir and existing ganciclovir models (prodrug x conversion factor, as the library already does for valganciclovir).
- doxycycline (IV): only oral formulation/bioequivalence modelling (Hopkins 2017, C) and a paediatric oral model (Thompson 2019, 31548185, not fetched in detail).
- ceftaroline (adult ICU): Chauzy 2022 (36059138, n=18 ventilated, augmented clearance) is too small and nonlinear in CrCL; Riccobene 2016 preferred.
- remdesivir: only small or literature-derived models.
- U checks with no clear upgrade: piperacillin (Kong 2025 already spans neonates to 94 years), voriconazole (Friberg 2012 already integrates children and adults), tobramycin (only CF/inhaled paediatric models found), metronidazole (infant paper Commander 2022 reuses an older model), clindamycin and cefalexin (not searched beyond the joint search; nothing surfaced).

### Notes

- Searches were capped at about 2-3 per drug and mostly used `"population pharmacokinetic*"` or NONMEM in Title/Abstract, so a better model could still exist under other terms.
- Several A/B rows report total concentrations while the library's cefazolin plots UNBOUND drug and ceftriaxone/clindamycin use saturable binding (`antibioticPlottedConcentration()`): each new beta-lactam needs the same decision (total vs free) before implementation.
- Kim 2021 (cefoxitin) is the only anti-infective model found that was built for TCI (arterial sampling, 3-cpt), which suits stanpumpR's closed-form engine and TCI controller directly.
- Values marked "not in abstract" need the full text; PMCIDs indicate free full text.
- Every new N drug also needs its pregnancy registry and therapeutic-range rows; pregnancy sources found in passing: penicillin G (Obura 2025), cefazolin (Elkayal 2021), azithromycin (Sutton 2015, Boelig 2026), amoxicillin (Dionne 2026, 42311647: oral CL/F 34 vs 22 L/h pregnant vs postpartum), oseltamivir (Pillai 2015), rifampin (Denti 2015, 26643345).
- No anti-infective registry exists in `inst/extdata`; if one is created these rows are its seed.

### Searched, nothing usable

- angiotensin II (Giapreza): no pharmacokinetic model in PubMed; only reviews and case reports. Endogenous peptide, half-life under a minute.
- dronedarone: PBPK/DDI papers only (Leow 2023, Hügl 2023, Wen 2022).
- ranolazine: no population PK in the searches.
- adenosine: vasoactive registry already `no_suitable_model` (Blardi, dose-dependent kinetics); Uematsu 2000 (PMID 10930971) is NCA/tolerability only — does not change the verdict.
- sodium nitroprusside, sodium bicarbonate: registry already `no_suitable_model`; not re-searched.
- andexanet alfa: no PK model in abstracts (Siegal 2017, PMID 29296829, is a PD/reversal study).
- idarucizumab: Glund 2019 (PMID 31050868, PMC6852568) is NCA in REVERSE-AD patients (exposure rises with renal impairment); no compartmental parameters in abstract.
- enalaprilat IV: only 1986-87 papers (Reams 1986, PMID 3023555; Ajayi 1987) without a model; enalaprilat in the library is metabolite-only by design (oral-formed parameters).
- losartan / irbesartan: Karatza 2019/2020 use gastric-emptying and delay-differential absorption models (not representable); the antihypertensive registry already records losartan Lo 1995 as negative.
- sacubitril/valsartan: Jin 2026 (PMID 42053769) is peritoneal-dialysis HF only; registry already rejected Yang 2022.
- captopril, ethacrynic acid: nothing in the searches.
- vasopressin, adult dobutamine, esmolol, nicardipine: nothing beyond what the registries hold (Modi 1993 nicardipine is the current source; Tabbutt 2008, PMID 18692637, paediatric esmolol, has no PK parameters in the abstract).
- levosimendan: not US-approved; noted only, not searched.
- potassium chloride, statins (atorvastatin/rosuvastatin): not searched (out of scope / low priority per task); clevidipine, hydrocortisone already present.

### Notes

- Highest-value new IV drugs for the OR/ICU: landiolol (Honda 2008, complete in abstract), aminocaproic acid (Stricker 2015, complete in abstract, free full text), tenecteplase (Tang 2022, free full text), furosemide IV (Van Wart 2013, already queued in the antihypertensive registry; preterm model Randell 2026 free).
- Landiolol's published models all carry a ~0.6-0.8 min "lag time" on an IV input; stanpumpR's IV engine has no IV lag, so the implementer must decide whether to drop it (it is mixing/sampling delay) or approximate it.
- Antifibrinolytic and EACA CPB models (Eaton 2015, Butterworth 1999) change parameters during bypass; the library's `courseSteps` / event mechanism might carry pre/during/post-CPB sets if a CPB time is entered as an event, but the app has no CPB covariate.
- Protamine is only an interaction model with heparin; it is not a standalone curve.
- Terlipressin's active moiety is lysine-vasopressin; Nilsson 1990's abstract does not model it quantitatively.
- Oral antihypertensives found here (amlodipine, candesartan, telmisartan, ramipril, nifedipine, torsemide) are apparent-oral chronic models; they fit the antihypertensive registry's "concentration only" pattern but are low perioperative priority.
- Several PMIDs carry 2026 dates and journal volumes as returned by PubMed; they were taken as fetched.

- Not searched, because there was no clear gap or the value was low: cyclosporine (an adult IV model would be the gap), everolimus (a transplant model), lithium (adults), amiodarone (children), the stimulants and fosaprepitant.
- Hydrocortisone: the paediatric models (Michelet 2020, PMID 32621587, doi 10.1530/EJE-20-0231; and its adult basis Melin 2018, PMID 28762136, doi 10.1007/s40262-017-0575-8) carry the same saturable CBG binding that the library already linearises from Bindellini 2024. They are not an upgrade without that reduction.
- The Charles 2008 caffeine abstract on PubMed has lost its exponents (it reads "CL (L/h) = 0.167 (WT/70) (PNA/12)"). Read them in the full text; do not guess them.
- The dose basis needs care: caffeine citrate against caffeine base (the library already halves citrate doses), MMF against MPA, aminophylline against theophylline, and betamethasone phosphate/acetate against betamethasone.
