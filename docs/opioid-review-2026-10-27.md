# Adversarial review of opioid potency, 2026-10-27: disposition

An adversarial review by ChatGPT ("Opioid models overstate unified analgesic potency") covered the opioid models at commit `f6b14ff`. It did not run R. Every claim was treated as a lead: PMIDs and numbers were checked against PubMed abstracts, and code claims against the code at `f8ef8eb`. The opioid drug files were unchanged between the two commits. Full texts that could not be reached are named below. This file records what was done with each finding.

## Found while checking, and withdrawn

Allegaert 2015 lists how its adult estimates moved "relative to those estimated without the paediatric data": CL to M1 −13%, other CL +23%, VP1 +13%, M1 intercompartmental CL +85%, VM2 +45%. Working back from its Table 3, that seemed to put Holford 2014's VP1 near 45 L, against the library's 90. The first version of this file and of the PR raised that as an open question. Holford 2014 itself, read once the author supplied it, settles it the other way. Its Table 2 is the library's set exactly: VP1 90 and VP2 79 L/70 kg; CLPO 18.4, QP 105, CLPM 10.5, CLMO 84.2 and QM 274 L/h/70 kg; VM1 78.9 (fixed) and VM2 131 L/70 kg. Allegaert's percentages therefore compare against some other fit, not Holford's published table: VP1 going from 90 to 51 L is a fall, not a 13% rise. The library's tramadol vector is a correct transcription of Holford 2014. Working back from another paper's percentages is not a substitute for reading the source.

## Follow-up: tramadol moved to Allegaert 2015

After this review the author decided, on 2026-10-27, to replace Holford 2014 with Allegaert 2015 and its maturation. The two adult fits agree closely after the first quarter hour of an intravenous dose: the same 4.3 h terminal half-life, M1 exposure within 6%, and Vss 185 against 169 L. Allegaert's smaller central volume (51 against 90 L) raises the first few minutes' concentrations, and its maturation halves a term neonate's clearances. The dose basis (hydrochloride, × 0.8784) is unchanged. The absorption constant (1.232 /h, peak still at 0.99 h) and desmetramadol's ke0 (0.0708 /min, peak still at 150 min) were re-solved. First pass now matures with the formation clearance. The investigators page had named Holford 2014's first author "Sophie Holford"; both papers give **Sam Holford**, which is now corrected.

The author then supplied the ULTRAM label (NDA 020281, revised March 2008), an independent check that neither source fitted. On the label's Table 1 (healthy adults, 100 mg tramadol hydrochloride), the model is about 17% low for the single-dose tramadol peak (257 against 308 ng/mL), 25% low at steady state on 100 mg qid (444 against 592), and about 30% low for M1 (38 against 55 single-dose, 76 against 110 at steady state). Its half-life is 4.3 h against the label's 5.6 to 6.3 h. The cause is clearance: the label's intravenous clearance in men, 6.4 mL/min/kg, is 26.9 L/h at 70 kg, against Allegaert's 32.1, while the volumes agree (2.6 L/kg, 182 L, against a Vss of 185 L). Holford's fit had the same half-life and the same M1 shortfall. By the author's decision, oral F became the label's 0.75 (it had been 0.70, the European capsules' figure), and the remaining gap is recorded rather than tuned away. That is in the drug header, the help page and a test that pins the ratios. Desmetramadol's ke0 was re-solved for the new F (0.0930 /min).

### Follow-up: the oxycodone dose basis

The author supplied the full texts of Pöyhiä 1991, Kirvelä 1996 and Liukas 2011 and decided, on 2026-10-27, to rescale. Pöyhiä's CL and Vss, scaled patient by patient by 0.07 × weight over the tabulated dose, become 14.5 mL/min/kg and 3.46 L/kg. Kirvelä's control medians, × 1.4, become 23.4 and 3.35. With Liukas's 11.9 and 3.7 unchanged, the pooled reference is CL 16.5 mL/min/kg (was 13.2, +25%) and Vss 3.51 L/kg (was 2.93, +20%). Kirvelä's ratios (Vc/Vss, Q/CL, uraemic/control) are each subject's own and do not move.

Intravenous concentrations fall by about a fifth. Oral exposure barely moves, because F is the pooled clearance over Lalovic's CL/F: 0.84, up from 0.67, and above the 60-80% Lalovic quotes. The absorption constant re-solves to 0.0099 /min; 0.01 is kept (mean peak 30.3 ng/mL after 15 mg). tPeak for Lalovic's 11-minute ke0 is now 12.09 min (was 12.35), the terminal half-life 3.3 h (was 3.4), and oxymorphone's first-pass fraction is unchanged, F/CL being Lalovic's. MEAC stays 12: the consistency-with-morphine range becomes 9 to 12 ng/mL (was 11 to 15).

By the clearance-first rule the three studies now disagree more than before: Liukas 11.9, Pöyhiä 14.5, Kirvelä 23.4 mL/min/kg. The model runs 15 to 35% above Liukas's four age groups, and its age effect relative to the youngest group is within 15%; `test-drugs-oxycodone.R` pins both, and recomputes the pool from Pöyhiä's table.

### Second follow-up (2026-10-29): all three studies are on the base

The rescaling above was wrong in direction for two studies and missing for the third. The author ruled that Oxanest, the product Pöyhiä, Kirvelä and Kokki 2012 gave, is the hydrochloride trihydrate (Kokki: 1 mg corresponds to 0.78 mg of oxycodone), and, after the evidence below, that all three intravenous studies computed clearance on the base.

- **Pöyhiä.** 0.07 mg/kg of the trihydrate holds 0.0544 mg/kg of base, the papers' "0.05". Table 1's dose column is the base in the trihydrate drawn up in round volumes (4.0, 5.25, 5.5 and 6.0 mg × 0.777 give its 3.1, 4.1, 4.3 and 4.7 mg), printed beside CL. That CL was computed on it is inferred; the paper says only "standard methods".
- **Kirvelä.** Same group, product and wording, no dose column: by analogy with Pöyhiä.
- **Liukas.** The paper says only "5 mg" (OxyNorm, the anhydrous hydrochloride), but Table II rules it out: a mean CL/kg cannot be less than dose ÷ mean(AUC × weight), so the 60–70 year group (8.6 mL/min/kg, AUC 6.4 ± 1.2 µg·min/mL, 83 ± 9 kg) was divided by at most 4.66 mg, whatever the correlation of AUC with weight. To second order, the expected mean CL/kg on the 4.48 mg of base is 12.2, 8.8, 8.6 and 7.9 in the four groups, against the reported 11.9, 8.6, 8.6 and 7.9; on 5 mg it is 13.6, 9.9, 9.6 and 8.8.
- **Lalovic.** The same test puts its CL/F on the 15 mg as labelled (expected 1.44 L/min, reported 1.44; 1.29 on the base), so F stays tied to it.

The dose is entered as the anhydrous hydrochloride, so each study's CL and Vss is multiplied by 351.83/315.37 = 1.1156: Pöyhiä 12.2 mL/min/kg and 2.90 L/kg, Kirvelä 18.6 and 2.67, Liukas 13.3 and 4.13. The pool is the published one × 1.1156: CL 14.7 mL/min/kg (was 16.5) and Vss 3.27 L/kg (was 3.51). The 1.33 and 1.4 of 2026-10-27 had put Pöyhiä and Kirvelä on the trihydrate's milligrams read as hydrochloride.

Intravenous concentrations rise about 12% (AUC +12.1%, the effect-site peak after 10 mg 82.5 → 90.9 ng/mL). F becomes 0.75, inside the 60–80% Lalovic quotes, so oral exposure does not move (AUC after 15 mg 10.48 → 10.49 µg·min/mL; ka 0.01 kept, re-solving to 0.00999 with a mean peak of 30.0 ng/mL). tPeak for the 11-minute ke0 is 12.36 min, the terminal half-life 3.4 h. Pöyhiä and Liukas now agree within 9%, and the model is within 8% of each of Liukas's age groups. The consistency-with-morphine MEAC range becomes 10 to 13 ng/mL; MEAC stays 12. Kokki 2012's labour clearance, taken on the trihydrate dose by the author's ruling, is 0.80 L/min on this basis, 0.87 of the model's 0.92 for her size; it stays recorded, not applied.
