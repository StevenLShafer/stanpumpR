# Adversarial review of the vasoactive and antihypertensive models, 2026-10-27: disposition

A second ChatGPT review ("Audit vasoactive models and clinical claims") covered the 23 vasoactive models and the antihypertensive category at commit `f6b14ff`, before the 2026-10-24 review's fixes (#571) were merged. It did not run R. Under the rule in `AGENTS.md`, each claim was treated as a lead and checked against the code and, where reachable, the primary source. This file records what was done with each finding. Where a finding repeats one from 2026-10-24, that review's disposition (`docs/vasoactive-review-2026-10-24.md`) is cited.

| # | Finding | Checked against | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Nitroglycerin offers a bolus unit beyond its infusion-only fit, and its single compartment falls more slowly after a stop than the drug does | The code; 2026-10-24 finding 7 | **Fixed** in PR #572 (merged 2026-10-27), which withdrew the bolus unit. The slow offset was already documented in the header and help page. |
| V2 | Enalaprilat, a metabolite with no dose units, is offered in the dose-table autocomplete and the Add a dose dialog | `visibleDrugNames()`, `app_server.R` | **Confirmed and fixed.** It applied to all nine metabolite-only rows (desmetramadol, salicylate, desethylamiodarone, hydroxybupropion, norfluoxetine, desvenlafaxine, hydroxyrisperidone, dehydroaripiprazole, enalaprilat). `visibleDrugNames()` now drops a drug with no dose units (`isDosableDrug()`). A click on such a drug's panel, a path the review did not name, now opens the dose dialogs on the drug that forms it (`formedMetaboliteParent()`). Tests in `test-illicit-drugs.R`. |
| B1 | Pediatric fenoldopam's band (0.8 to 1.2 mcg/kg/min ÷ CL = 15.7 to 23.5 ng/mL) tops out at 150% of the label's paediatric maximum | Hammer 2008 full text (PMC2576052); Corlopam label (in a search excerpt) | **Confirmed and changed, on Dr. Shafer's decision.** The paper is internally inconsistent: its abstract says 0.8 to 1.2 (open-label titration), its Conclusion 0.2 to 0.8 (blinded phase: 0.2 the lowest effective rate, 0.8 the maximum effect), and the label titrates to a maximum of 0.8. The band is now 0.2 to 0.8 mcg/kg/min, 3.9 to 15.7 ng/mL, typical 15.7. The header, help page and test say why. |
| B2 | The digoxin band (0.5 to 2 ng/mL) needs its indication and sampling time | The three digoxin help pages | **Documented.** digoxinIV already said the range applies six hours or more after a dose. The oral and paediatric pages now say the same, that it is the historical laboratory range, that heart failure aims for its lower half, and (paediatric) that it is an adult range carried over. No number changed. Guideline targets the review quoted (2022 HF, 2023 AF, 2025 ISHLT) were not read here and are not cited. |
| B3 | Pediatric milrinone's typical 167 ng/mL is an adult cardiac-index C50, and 100 to 300 a simulation target | The milrinonePediatric help page | **Documented.** The page now says both. |
| M1 | Pediatric milrinone imputes postmenstrual age as a term birth | Header and help page | **Already documented**; the help page now says which way the error goes for a premature infant (clearance overstated, concentration too low). No gestational-age field was added. |
| M2 | Norepinephrine's propofol term and lag, dopamine's baseline and lag | The headers | **No change.** Already documented as model limits; the review agrees they are not errors. |
| M3 | Clevidipine accepts a bolus from an infusion-only study | — | **No change.** The review classes it as an untested extrapolation, not a confirmed failure. |
| R1 | Clevidipine: the full text of Ericsson 1999 (*Br J Clin Pharmacol*, PMID 10336577) prints V1 0.36 L/kg and Emax 45.4% / EC50 12.1 nmol/L, which the code calls unpublished or unverifiable | The publisher's PDF, supplied by Dr. Shafer after the first pass | **Confirmed; model changed on Dr. Shafer's decision.** On the first pass PubMed Central was unreachable, and the claim was recorded as a possible conflation with the companion EJCP study (whose V1 is also 0.36). The PDF shows the review was right: Table 1 prints the fitted CL 0.121 and V1 0.36, and the Results give Emax 45.4% and EC50 12.1 nmol/L. The model now uses the fitted V1, with the unprinted k12 and k21 chosen to reproduce Table 1's derived quantities: V2 0.194, Q 0.0167 L/min/kg (previously the derived V1 0.369, V2 0.191, Q 0.0164). The Emax and EC50 are now `primary_full_text` and are still not evaluated. `docs/antihypertensives.md` section 17. |
| R2 | Valsartan's DOI 10.1111/bcp.13069 is a metformin paper | PubMed (PMID 27450071 vs 27504853) | **Confirmed and fixed** to 10.1111/bcp.13082 in the model, the data-raw script and both regenerated CSVs. |
| R3 | Olmesartan: 629 participants / 5119 concentrations are wrong; "Japanese 3.0% faster" should be slower | PubMed abstract (PMID 16372830); the full text, supplied by Dr. Shafer | **Confirmed and fixed.** 7911 concentrations from 472 people: 89 healthy volunteers and 383 hypertensive patients, 355 Western (338 Caucasian, 17 Hispanic) and 117 Japanese. Table II counts 592 individuals (209 healthy, 475 Western) because the 30 Western men of a five-way crossover were "treated as separate individuals" (its footnote b), which reconciles it with the text. The code's 355 / 338 / 17 were right; only 629 and 5119 were wrong. Table IV's race coefficient is -0.030, and the Results say CL/F was "3.0% lower in Japanese than Westerners". In passing, "healthy volunteers clear olmesartan 21.3% faster" was also wrong (the Results: "21.3% lower in hypertensive patients than healthy volunteers") and is corrected. |
| R4 | digoxinIV's reference string puts Cheng's PMID after Hornestam's clause | The code; PubMed | **Confirmed and fixed.** Cheng's link follows Cheng; Hornestam's DOI follows the 87-min claim, which now names its endpoint (ventricular rate in acute atrial fibrillation, not inotropy). |
