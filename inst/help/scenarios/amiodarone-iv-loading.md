## What to look for

This is the product label's regimen for the first 24 hours of intravenous amiodarone: 150 mg over the first 10 minutes (15 mg/min), 360 mg over the next 6 hours (1 mg/min), then 540 mg over the remaining 18 hours (0.5 mg/min), 1050 mg in all. The plot is in **hours**, so the dose table shows the 10-minute and 6-hour-10-minute rows as 0.1666666667 and 6.166666667 hours.

The rapid load takes plasma amiodarone to 5.6 mg/L at 10 minutes. Distribution into the peripheral volume, with a half-life of 13 minutes, brings it below 2.5 mg/L by about half an hour and to 1.4 mg/L at one hour. On 1 mg/min it reaches its lowest, 1.11 mg/L, at about 2 hours, and then climbs slowly to 1.29 mg/L at 6 hours 10 minutes, because the 700 L peripheral compartment is still filling.

When the rate is halved the concentration **dips**: to 0.87 mg/L at about 7 hours 30 minutes, below 1.0 mg/L from 6.5 to 15.6 hours, before it rises slowly again to 1.12 mg/L at 24 hours. At 6 hours 10 minutes the drug leaving the central compartment, by elimination and by distribution into the still-filling tissues, almost matches the 1 mg/min going in. Halving the input leaves the outflow unchanged for the moment, so the concentration falls, with the 13-minute distribution half-life, until the two balance again at the lower rate. By 24 hours 1045 mg has been given (the 0.5 mg/min has run for 17 hours 50 minutes, 535 of the label's 540 mg): 611 mg is in the peripheral compartment, 410 mg has been eliminated and only 24 mg is in the 21 L central compartment.

There is no shaded band. The familiar 1.0 to 2.5 mg/L window is for trough concentrations in chronic therapy, and on this regimen the typical patient spends the first day at its lower edge. Watt and colleagues found, on a similar regimen, at least three of eight patients below 1.0 mg/L between 3 and 16 hours.

## Try next

- **A supplemental dose.** The label allows a further 150 mg over 10 minutes for a breakthrough arrhythmia. Switch *Time units* to **minutes** (the dose times become 0, 10 and 370) and add two rows at 720, **15 mg/min** and **0.5 mg/min**: rows at the same time add up, and an infusion row replaces the running rate, so together they give the extra 15 mg/min on top of the maintenance rate. Add **0.5 mg/min** at 730 to return to maintenance. The concentration peaks at 6.5 mg/L at 730 and is back to 1.5 mg/L an hour after the dose began; at 24 hours it is 1.22 mg/L instead of 1.12. Given instead as a single **150 mg** bolus at 720, it peaks at 8.1 mg/L.
- **A heavier patient.** Set the weight to 120 kg. The label's doses are fixed, so the concentrations are lower: a peak of 4.3 mg/L, a dip to 0.69 mg/L and 0.89 mg/L at 24 hours. Untick *Adjust weight to fat-free mass* for the published per-kilogram model, which scales every volume and clearance with total weight: 3.3, 0.51 and 0.66 mg/L.
- **Beyond the first day.** Switch *Time units* to **days** (Max time becomes 2 days) and choose a Max time of **3 days**. The 0.5 mg/min row runs on, and the concentration rises to 1.39 mg/L at 48 hours and 1.57 at 72 hours. That is as far as this model should be taken: its 42-hour terminal half-life comes from short observation, and it would reach a steady state of 30 mg/h (0.5 mg/min) ÷ 15.4 L/h = 1.95 mg/L within days that the real drug approaches only over weeks.
