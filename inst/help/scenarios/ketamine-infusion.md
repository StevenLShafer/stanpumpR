## What to look for

The bolus of 0.5 mg/kg produces a plasma peak far above the analgesic band (0.1 to 0.16 mcg/mL), because ketamine's small central volume (0.063 L/kg) makes the plasma peak sharp. The effect site (tPeak 3 minutes) does not follow it all the way up, but it still peaks at about 0.4 mcg/mL, more than twice the top of the band, and comes down into the band only at about 40 minutes. The infusion at 0.25 mg/kg/hr then holds the effect site in the band, near 0.14 mcg/mL, until it stops.

When the infusion stops at 120 minutes, the concentration falls out of the band within a quarter of an hour. Ketamine's high clearance (about 1.9 L/min at 70 kg) makes its offset fast.

## Try next

- Double the infusion to 0.5 mg/kg/hr and see the effect site climb through the band towards concentrations at which psychotomimetic effects appear.
- Remove the bolus. How long does the infusion alone take to reach the band?
- Change the bolus to 2 mg/kg, an induction dose, delete the two infusion rows and set *Max time* to 2 hours: the effect-site peak is about 1.6 mcg/mL, an anesthetic concentration, and recovery to the analgesic band takes about an hour and a half.

## Background

[Ketamine](help:drugs/ketamine) is Domino's model in volunteers, with weight scaling. Norketamine, an active metabolite that accumulates during infusions, is not modelled, so the analgesia from a long infusion is understated.
