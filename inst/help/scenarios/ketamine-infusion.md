## What to look for

The bolus of 0.25 mg/kg produces a plasma peak far above the analgesic band (0.1 to 0.16 mcg/mL), because ketamine's small central volume (0.063 L/kg) makes the plasma peak sharp. The effect site (tPeak 3 minutes, an assumption) does not follow it all the way up, but it still peaks at about 0.5 mcg/mL, three times the top of the band, and comes down into the band at about 25 minutes. The infusion at 0.12 mg/kg/hr then holds the effect site in the band, near 0.12 to 0.14 mcg/mL, until it stops.

When the infusion stops at 120 minutes, the concentration falls out of the band within about ten minutes. Ketamine's clearance is high (about 1 L/min at 70 kg, Domino's 0.848 L/kg/h) and the infusion was holding the effect site only just inside the band, so its offset is fast.

## Try next

- Double the infusion to 0.24 mg/kg/hr and see the effect site climb out of the top of the band after about 20 minutes, to 0.22 mcg/mL by two hours, towards concentrations at which psychotomimetic effects appear.
- Remove the bolus. How long does the infusion alone take to reach the band? (About two hours and forty minutes: the bolus is what gets there quickly.)
- Change the bolus to 2 mg/kg, an induction dose, delete the two infusion rows and set *Max time* to 4 hours: the effect-site peak is about 4 mcg/mL, falling to 0.6 at 30 minutes and 0.36 at an hour, and the effect site is back in the analgesic band only after nearly four hours. The height of that peak rests on the assumed 3-minute time to peak effect.

## Background

[Ketamine](help:drugs/ketamine) is Domino's model in volunteers (clearance 0.848 L/kg/h), with weight scaling. Norketamine, an active metabolite that accumulates during infusions, is not modelled, so the analgesia from a long infusion is understated.
