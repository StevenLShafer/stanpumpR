## What to look for

The bolus of 0.25 mg/kg produces a plasma peak far above the analgesic band (0.1 to 0.16 mcg/mL), because ketamine's small central volume (0.058 L/kg) makes the plasma peak sharp. The effect site (tPeak 3 minutes, an assumption) does not follow it all the way up, but it still peaks at about 0.3 mcg/mL, twice the top of the band, and comes down into the band at about 35 minutes. The infusion at 0.12 mg/kg/hr then holds the effect site in the band, near 0.11 to 0.13 mcg/mL, until it stops.

When the infusion stops at 120 minutes, the concentration falls out of the band within about ten minutes. Ketamine's clearance is high (about 0.9 L/min at 70 kg) and the infusion was holding the effect site only just inside the band, so its offset is fast.

## Try next

- Double the infusion to 0.24 mg/kg/hr and see the effect site never come down into the band after the bolus: it settles at about 0.2 mcg/mL, towards concentrations at which psychotomimetic effects appear.
- Remove the bolus. How long does the infusion alone take to reach the band? (It does not get there in the two hours; continued, it takes about three and a half hours. The bolus is what gets there quickly.)
- Change the bolus to 2 mg/kg, an induction dose, delete the two infusion rows and set *Max time* to 4 hours: the effect-site peak is about 2.3 mcg/mL, falling to 1 at 30 minutes and 0.5 at an hour, and the effect site is back in the analgesic band only after nearly three hours. The height of that peak rests on the assumed 3-minute time to peak effect.

## Background

[Ketamine](help:drugs/ketamine) is fitted to Domino's concentrations in volunteers (clearance 0.79 L/kg/h), with weight scaling. Norketamine, an active metabolite that accumulates during infusions, is not modelled, so the analgesia from a long infusion is understated.
