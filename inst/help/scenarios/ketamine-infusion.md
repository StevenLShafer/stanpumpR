## What to look for

The bolus of 0.5 mg/kg produces a plasma peak far above the analgesic band (0.1 to 0.16 mcg/mL) and an effect-site peak within it, because ketamine's small central volume (0.063 L/kg) makes the plasma peak sharp and the effect site (tPeak 3 minutes) does not follow it up. The infusion at 0.25 mg/kg/hr then holds the effect site in the lower part of the band for two hours.

When the infusion stops at 120 minutes, the concentration falls out of the band within half an hour. Ketamine's high clearance (about 1.9 L/min at 70 kg) makes its offset fast.

## Try next

- Double the infusion to 0.5 mg/kg/hr and see the effect site climb through the band towards concentrations at which psychotomimetic effects appear.
- Remove the bolus. How long does the infusion alone take to reach the band?
- Change the bolus to 2 mg/kg, an induction dose, and set *Max time* to 1 hour: the effect-site peak is around 1 mcg/mL, an anesthetic concentration, and recovery to the analgesic band takes about half an hour.

## Background

[Ketamine](help:drugs/ketamine) is Domino's model in volunteers, with weight scaling. Norketamine, an active metabolite that accumulates during infusions, is not modelled, so the analgesia from a long infusion is understated.
