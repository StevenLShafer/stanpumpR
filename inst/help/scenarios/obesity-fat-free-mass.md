## What to look for

A 120 kg, 170 cm man given fentanyl 2 mcg/kg and sufentanil 0.2 mcg/kg, both per kilogram, so the actual doses are 240 mcg and 24 mcg. With **Adjust weight to fat-free mass** on, as it is by default, the two opioid models are scaled to his fat-free mass (about 71 kg) rather than to his 120 kg total weight. His fat-free mass is only 31 per cent above the reference man's, while his total weight is 71 per cent above, so the models give smaller volumes and lower clearances than total-weight scaling would, and the per-kilogram doses he received produce higher concentrations than the same per-kilogram dose gives the reference man.

## Try next

- Untick **Adjust weight to fat-free mass** in the Patient Profile. Both curves drop: total-weight scaling inflates the volumes and clearances by the full weight ratio, diluting the same dose. The gap between the two settings is how much per-kilogram dosing on total weight departs from fat-free-mass dosing in this patient.
- Change the patient to a 70 kg man and the two settings coincide: the reference man's factors are exactly 1 either way.
- Try a 70 kg woman against the 70 kg man with the switch on. She has about 18 per cent less fat-free mass, so her concentrations run higher for the same per-kilogram dose, although per-kilogram dosing treats them identically.

## Background

[Scaling to fat-free mass](help:models/fat-free-mass) explains why clearance tracks lean tissue, how the scaling is anchored to the reference man, and which models it affects. Propofol and remifentanil would not respond to the switch, because they already carry fat-free mass inside their models.
