## What to look for

This is the highest loading regimen in the amiodarone product monograph that Pollak, Bouillon and Shafer simulated: 1600 mg/day for 3 weeks, 800 mg/day for the next month, then 400 mg/day. The plot runs for a year in **days**.

Serum amiodarone overshoots. It climbs through the 1.0 to 2.5 mg/L window during the first days and keeps rising for as long as 1600 mg/day continues, peaking at about 3.0 mg/L on day 21. It is above 2.5 mg/L for about two weeks, falls slowly on 800 mg/day (2.54 mg/L on day 51) and settles towards 400/229 = 1.75 mg/L on maintenance. These are the typical patient's values; the paper's Figure 4 shows that some patients go much higher.

The overshoot is the point. A loading dose that is right for the first week is far too much in the third, because the peripheral compartment is filling the whole time and less and less of each dose leaves the plasma for it. Pollak's stepped regimen (see the related scenario) was designed to step down with it.

## Try next

- Try the monograph's lowest regimen instead: 800 mg/day at 0, 600 mg/day at 7 and 400 mg/day at 37. It never overshoots, but it takes six to nine months to reach the concentrations the high regimen reaches in a week: 1.13 mg/L at a week, 1.47 at three months, 1.74 at a year.
