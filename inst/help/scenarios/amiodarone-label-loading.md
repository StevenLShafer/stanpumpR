## What to look for

This is the highest regimen in the amiodarone product monograph that Pollak, Bouillon and Shafer simulated: 1600 mg/day for 3 weeks, 800 mg/day for the next month, then 600 mg/day. The plot runs for a year in **days**.

Serum amiodarone overshoots. It climbs through the 1.0 to 2.5 mg/L window and keeps rising for as long as 1600 mg/day continues: above 2.5 mg/L from about day 12, it peaks at about 3.0 mg/L on day 21. When the dose drops to 800 mg/day it falls quickly, to about 2.2 mg/L by day 25, and then climbs again as the peripheral compartment goes on filling, back above 2.5 mg/L by about day 48 (2.54 mg/L on day 51). On 600 mg/day it dips once more and then rises for good: it crosses 2.5 mg/L again at about day 127 and stays above the window for the rest of the year, approaching 600/229 = 2.62 mg/L (2.61 mg/L at a year). Desethylamiodarone rises with it, to 2.3 mg/L by the end of the year.

Two lessons are here. A loading dose that is right for the first week is far too much in the third, because less and less of each dose leaves the plasma for the filling tissues. And the maintenance dose decides where the level ends up: at steady state the concentration is the dose rate divided by clearance, 600 / 229 L/day, which is above the window for the typical patient. The paper's Figure 4 shows that about half of the population stays above 2.5 mg/L on this regimen; these are the typical patient's values.

## Try next

- Change the last row to 400 mg/day. The overshoots stay the same, but the level now settles towards 400/229 = 1.75 mg/L, inside the window.
- Try the monograph's lowest regimen instead: 800 mg/day at 0, 600 mg/day at 7 and 400 mg/day at 37. It never overshoots, but it takes six to nine months to reach the concentrations the high regimen reaches in a week: 1.13 mg/L at a week, 1.47 at three months, 1.74 at a year.
