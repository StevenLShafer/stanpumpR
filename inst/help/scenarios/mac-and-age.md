## What to look for

An 80-year-old given 2 per cent sevoflurane in 6 L/min of oxygen. The sevoflurane panel shows the alveolar tension climbing towards 2 per cent as it would in anyone. The **MAC equivalents** panel shows it reaching about 1.2 MAC: at 80, MAC is about 1.6 per cent, not 2.1.

Open **Patient Profile** and change the age to 20. The sevoflurane panel does not change; the gas engine's uptake does not depend on age. The MAC-equivalents panel does: the same 2 per cent is now about 0.85 MAC, because MAC at 20 is about 2.4 per cent.

The relation is Mapleson's: MAC falls about 6 per cent per decade, MAC(age) = MAC₄₀ × 10^(−0.00269 (age − 40)). A vaporizer setting that is right for a 20-year-old is half a MAC too much for an 80-year-old.

## Try next

- Try age 1 (set the unit to years, or 12 months). MAC in this relation keeps rising below age 40; the formula is a meta-analysis across ages and does not capture the dip in neonates.
- Turn on **Time until threshold** and read the sevoflurane line at 30 minutes at age 80 and at age 20. The threshold, 0.1 MAC, is age-adjusted too, so it is lower in the older patient and takes longer to reach.
- Open **Settings → Drug Thresholds**: the volatile agents' thresholds are shown at the patient's age, and follow MAC if the age is changed.

## Background

[Inhaled anesthetics](help:inhaled-agents) separates MAC, a property of the agent, from MAC equivalents, the patient's alveolar concentration as a multiple of it. [Sevoflurane](help:drugs/sevoflurane) tabulates MAC by age.
