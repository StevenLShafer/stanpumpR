## What to look for

A single axillary block: 600 mg of mepivacaine (40 mL of 1.5% solution with epinephrine 5 mcg/mL), given as **mg RA**. This is the dose Simon and colleagues studied, and the curve is the model fitted to their mean arterial concentrations.

Mepivacaine is absorbed from the injection site through **two depots in parallel**. Just under half the dose (47 per cent) is absorbed with a half-time of about 4 minutes, the rest (53 per cent) with a half-time of about 2.3 hours. The two together give a shape that one depot cannot:

- The plasma concentration is already 2.3 mcg/mL at 5 minutes and 3.2 mcg/mL at 10 minutes, as the fast depot empties.
- It peaks at about 3.8 mcg/mL at 30 minutes (Simon measured 3.89 at 25 minutes) and is still 3.6 mcg/mL at an hour: a plateau, because the slow depot keeps supplying drug while the body eliminates it.
- After that it falls slowly, to about 2.1 mcg/mL at 4 hours and 0.8 mcg/mL at 8 hours. The slow depot, not elimination, sets that tail.

The single depot that fits the same data best (a half-time of 25 minutes) peaks at 5.2 mcg/mL at about 70 minutes, a third too high and too late. That is why mepivacaine carries two depots; the drug file can switch back to one.

The curve is total mepivacaine (both enantiomers, bound and unbound) in plasma. It is not the block, and it does not predict toxicity on its own. The fit is to one study's means after axillary block with epinephrine, and three hours of sampling cannot establish the slow depot's tail.

## Try next

- Add **lidocaine 600 mg RA**: the same dose of lidocaine with epinephrine from the same study. Lidocaine has one depot, and it rises more slowly, to a lower and later peak (about 2.8 mcg/mL at 40 minutes), and is mostly gone by 4 hours.
- Give a supplementary 300 mg at 90 minutes, as a block that has not worked might be topped up. Most of the first dose is still in the slow depot or in the body, so the peak rises to about 5.1 mcg/mL about 20 minutes after the second dose.
- Change the units to **mg**: the same 600 mg given intravenously by accident. The concentration starts at about 8 mcg/mL, more than twice the block's peak, and is still higher than the block's at an hour.
