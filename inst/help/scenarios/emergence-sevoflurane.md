## What to look for

Two hours of 2 per cent sevoflurane at a low fresh gas flow of 2 L/min, then the vaporizer off and the flow up to 8 L/min. Through the anesthetic the alveolar tension climbs to about 1.7 per cent and the brain follows closely. The **MAC equivalents** panel shows about 0.8 MAC for this 40-year-old.

The **time until threshold** line on the sevoflurane panel gives, at each moment, how long after turning the vaporizer off the brain tension would take to fall to 0.1 MAC (0.21 per cent at this age), with the fresh gas flow turned up so that there is no rebreathing. It climbs slowly through the two hours as muscle and fat fill. At 120 minutes, when the vaporizer is turned off, the brain tension then falls to the threshold at the time the line predicted. Hover on the line at 30, 60 and 120 minutes.

The fall is fast at first and slow at the end: the brain washes out quickly, but muscle and fat, which have been loading for two hours, return agent to the blood and hold the last fraction of a MAC up. This is the inhaled agents' context sensitivity.

## Try next

- Load [emergence from desflurane](scenario:emergence-desflurane) and compare the time-until-threshold lines at 120 minutes. Desflurane's lower solubility (blood:gas 0.42 against 0.65) makes its emergence faster.
- Set *Max time* to 6 hours and move the vaporizer-off row to 300 minutes. After five hours the fat compartment matters and the tail is longer.
- Change the oxygen flow at 120 minutes from 8 L/min to 2 L/min, leaving the vaporizer off. Now the circuit is rebreathed and the washout is much slower than the line predicted, because the line assumes no rebreathing.
