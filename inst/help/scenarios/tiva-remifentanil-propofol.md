## What to look for

A total intravenous anesthetic: propofol 1.5 mg/kg and remifentanil 1 mcg/kg at induction, infusions of 120 mcg/kg/min and 0.15 mcg/kg/min for an hour, and the events panel marking intubation at 2 minutes and emergence at 70.

The **p response** panel shows the probability of response to laryngoscopy from the Bouillon interaction surface. Three lines: with both drugs, with propofol alone, and (always 1) with opioid alone. At intubation the combined probability is low, while the propofol-alone line says that this much propofol by itself would leave most patients responding. The gap between the two lines is what the remifentanil is doing.

After the infusions stop at 60 minutes, the remifentanil is gone within minutes and the combined line rises towards the propofol-alone line; the propofol then falls and the patient wakes. The remifentanil's analgesia leaves before the propofol's hypnosis does, which is why a longer-acting opioid is usually given before emergence from a remifentanil-based anesthetic.

## Try next

- Set the remifentanil infusion rate to 0 at time 0 (keep the bolus). The combined line now tracks the propofol-alone line after the bolus wears off.
- Halve the propofol infusion to 60 mcg/kg/min and double the remifentanil to 0.3 mcg/kg/min. The probability of response at the stimulus is similar; the propofol concentration is much lower. This is the clinical trade the interaction surface describes.
- Replace remifentanil with fentanyl 2 mcg/kg at 0 and 1 mcg/kg at 30 minutes. Fentanyl is converted to remifentanil equivalents through its MEAC.
