## What to look for

Sevoflurane 2.5 per cent in 6 L/min of oxygen, with a minute ventilation of 6 L/min, for an hour, in a 70 kg, 40-year-old man (MAC 2.1 per cent). Press **Visualizer** under the plot. Each box is one place the agent can be: the breathing circuit (what is inspired), the lungs (end-tidal), the vessel-rich group (the brain and the other well-perfused organs, the effect site, in blue), muscle and fat. A box's fill height is the agent's partial pressure, on one scale for every box. Its width is its capacity, its volume times how soluble the agent is in it, so the filled area is the agent it holds. Agent always runs from a higher level to a lower one: the boxes' levels are the gradients. Press **Play**.

**The lungs fill in a minute; the brain in ten.** The fresh gas flow equals the minute ventilation, so almost nothing is rebreathed and the circuit stays at the vaporizer setting (the gold line). End-tidal reaches 1.12 per cent in 30 seconds and 1.42 per cent in a minute. The vessel-rich group, 6.6 L of capacity behind three-quarters of the cardiac output, follows: it is within a tenth of the end-tidal level from 8.4 minutes, and at 1 MAC from 16 minutes, four minutes after end-tidal.

**Then the brain stops taking agent; muscle and fat do not.** Watch the arrows on the blood line under the boxes. At one minute 27 mL/min of vapour goes into the vessel-rich group; by 20 minutes, 0.4 mL/min. Muscle takes about 11 mL/min, and fat 4, from the first minutes to the end of the hour. At an hour muscle is at a third of the end-tidal level and fat at 2 per cent of it.

**Fat's level is low, its store is not.** Sevoflurane's fat holds 493 L of gas against the vessel-rich group's 6.6 L, so its box is the widest even compressed to ten times the narrowest (the note above the boxes says so). Muscle holds more agent than the vessel-rich group from 13 minutes, and fat from 36 minutes, at a level a seventy-fifth of the brain's. At the end of the hour the patient holds 1,052 mL of vapour: 147 in the vessel-rich group, 604 in muscle and 245 in fat. That store is what [emergence](scenario:visualizer-sevoflurane-emergence) has to empty.

**Most of the agent is thrown away.** At 6 L/min, 88 per cent of the agent delivered goes out through the pop-off valve: 7.9 of 9.0 L of vapour. The amounts card keeps the account.

## Try next

- Press **3D view** (or **v**): the same compartments as glasses, the lungs in the middle, the tissues behind them and the circuit to the left, each glass's floor area its capacity and its liquid as tall as the partial pressure. Drag to turn the scene and watch fat's wide, shallow glass fill.
- Load [Watching an overpressure induction, then low flow](scenario:visualizer-sevoflurane-overpressure): the same patient, induced faster and maintained with far less agent thrown away.
- Change the sevoflurane to desflurane at 6 per cent. Desflurane is a third as soluble in fat: its fat box holds 189 L, and every tissue fills faster.
