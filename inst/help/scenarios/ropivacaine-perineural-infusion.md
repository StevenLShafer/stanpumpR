## What to look for

A 65-year-old has a peripheral nerve block with a catheter: 150 mg of ropivacaine as the initial injection (30 mL of 0.5%, **mg RA**), then 0.2% at 8 mL/hr through the catheter, which is 16 mg/hr (**mg/hr RA**), for two days, stopped at day 2 by a row of 0 mg/hr RA. The plot is in **days**. The infusion runs into the same tissue depot as the injection and is absorbed from it at the same rate.

- **The block peaks late**: ropivacaine is absorbed slowly from tissue in this model (a half-time of about 2 hours), so the injection and the first hours of infusion together peak at about 1.3 mcg/mL at about 3 hours.
- **The infusion sets the plateau**: the concentration settles at 0.89 mcg/mL, the rate divided by the clearance (16 mg/hr ÷ 18 L/hr), and stays there from about day 1. Absorption decides only how long it takes to get there.
- **Stopping is slow**: after the pump stops at day 2 the concentration takes about 4 hours to halve and about 11 hours to fall below 0.1 mcg/mL, because drug in the tissue keeps arriving long after the infusion ends.

## Where to be careful

The model is from older patients (61 and over) and has no age term, so it overpredicts a younger patient's exposure. Its clearance and binding are constant, so the plateau is flat. In patients, total (bound plus unbound) ropivacaine rises over two or three days of perineural infusion, because alpha-1-acid glycoprotein, which binds it, rises after surgery, while the unbound concentration, which causes toxicity, stays much steadier. A measured total concentration on day 2 or 3 will usually be higher than this curve and is not by itself evidence of toxicity. The absorption rate was matched to the peak after a single axillary block, and absorption over days through a catheter, or from other sites, may differ. See [Ropivacaine](help:drugs/ropivacaine).

## Try next

- Delete the 150 mg RA row: the infusion alone is still below half its plateau at 4 hours, and reaches 92 per cent of it at 12 hours. That is why the block is given first.
- Halve the infusion to 8 mg/hr (0.2% at 4 mL/hr): the plateau halves, to about 0.44 mcg/mL.
- Change the drug in all three rows to [bupivacaine](help:drugs/bupivacaine). It is absorbed faster and cleared faster in its model, so the same doses peak higher and earlier (about 1.6 mcg/mL at an hour) but settle lower (about 0.51 mcg/mL). In practice bupivacaine is infused at lower rates than ropivacaine; see [the bupivacaine infusion scenario](help:scenarios/bupivacaine-perineural-infusion).
