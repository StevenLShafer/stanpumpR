## What to look for

Acetaminophen 1000 mg by mouth every 6 hours, entered as a single row of 1000 mg PO qid: the app repeats the dose at 6, 12 and 18 hours. Four grams a day is the labelled maximum for adults.

The plasma half-life, 2.3 hours, is much shorter than the 6-hour interval, so little is left when the next dose arrives. The dashed plasma line peaks at 11 to 13 mcg/mL after each dose and falls to about 2 mcg/mL before the next, a six-fold swing. The drug accumulates only a little: the second peak is 12.6 mcg/mL against the first's 11.0, and from the third dose on the curve repeats itself. That is steady state, reached within a day. The average plasma concentration at steady state is 6 mcg/mL: the dose rate times the bioavailability of 0.86, divided by the clearance of 23.9 L/h.

The solid effect-site line smooths the swings. Once steady, it runs from 3.3 to 8.2 mcg/mL, inside the shaded band for almost the whole day after the first dose. It is above 5 mcg/mL, acetaminophen's threshold in the drug library, from about half an hour to four and a half hours after each dose, and below it for the last two hours before the next. Over the day it is above 5 mcg/mL 60 per cent of the time.

The plot says nothing about the liver. The model does not represent the metabolite that injures it, and the 4 g ceiling comes from that toxicity, not from these concentrations.

## Try next

- Change the row to 1000 mg PO tid, 3 g a day, a lower ceiling often used for older patients or those at risk of liver injury. The effect site now falls to about 1.7 mcg/mL before each dose and is above 5 mcg/mL for only 38 per cent of the day.
- In **Graph Options**, tick **Time until threshold**. At each effect-site peak the line reads how long the effect site would stay above 5 mcg/mL if no more doses were given: after the last dose, 2 hours 49 minutes.
