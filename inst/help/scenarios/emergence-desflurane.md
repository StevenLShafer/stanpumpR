## What to look for

The same anesthetic as [emergence from sevoflurane](scenario:emergence-sevoflurane) with desflurane at 6 per cent, about one MAC at age 40, for two hours at a low flow, then the vaporizer off and the flow up.

Desflurane's blood:gas partition coefficient is 0.42 and its tissue solubilities are low, so the alveolar tension approaches the inspired tension faster during the anesthetic and the brain tension falls faster after it. The **time until threshold** line, to 0.1 MAC (0.6 per cent at this age), is lower than sevoflurane's at every moment and climbs less through the two hours.

The MAC-equivalents panel shows about 1 MAC during the case; sevoflurane at 2 per cent gave about 0.8. To compare the two agents fairly, compare the time-until-threshold lines at the same MAC equivalents.

## Try next

- Change the desflurane setting to 4.8 per cent, which gives about the same MAC equivalents as the sevoflurane scenario, and compare the emergence times.
- Change the patient's age to 80. MAC falls to about 4.7 per cent, 6 per cent is now 1.3 MAC, and the threshold (0.1 MAC) falls with it.
- Add nitrous oxide 4 L/min and reduce the oxygen to 2 L/min during the case. The MAC equivalents are summed; at emergence, each agent's line is the time for that agent alone.

## Background

[Desflurane](help:drugs/desflurane) has the parameters. The model has no vaporizer: the setting is taken as delivered, and the sympathetic response to a rapid rise in desflurane is not represented.
