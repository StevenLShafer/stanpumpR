## What to look for

One 500 mg tablet, taken fasting by a 40-year-old, 70 kg man. The dashed plasma line starts to rise after a lag of 5 minutes and peaks at 5.5 mcg/mL 34 minutes after the dose: an immediate-release tablet is absorbed quickly. The plasma half-life is 2.3 hours.

The solid line is the effect site, which follows the plasma with an equilibration half-time of 53 minutes (Anderson and colleagues, 2001). It peaks at 3.1 mcg/mL nearly two hours after the dose, at 1 hour 56 minutes. At that moment the plasma has fallen to the same 3.1 mcg/mL, and the two lines cross: the effect site stops rising exactly when the plasma falls to meet it.

The shaded band, 3 to 15 mcg/mL, is the effect-site range that adult doses reach. A single 500 mg tablet touches its lower edge for about an hour and never reaches 5 mcg/mL, acetaminophen's threshold in the drug library. Anderson's pain model, fitted in children after tonsillectomy, puts the concentration for half the maximum effect at 10 mcg/mL and the maximum at 5.2 points on a 10-point pain scale. On that model, 3.1 mcg/mL gives about 1.2 points of relief at its peak.

## Try next

- Change the dose to 1000 mg, the usual adult dose. Every concentration doubles: the effect site peaks at 6.2 mcg/mL and stays above 5 mcg/mL for about two and a half hours. The relief on Anderson's model rises to about 2 points, not 2.4, because the effect flattens as the concentration nears 10 mcg/mL.
- Give the 1000 mg intravenously: change the units from mg PO to mg. The plasma starts at 23 mcg/mL, but the effect site peaks at only 7.3 mcg/mL, at 90 minutes. The 53-minute equilibration smooths away most of the difference between the routes, and the tablet loses only the 14 per cent that is not absorbed.
