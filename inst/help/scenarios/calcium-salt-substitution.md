A typical parturient of the source study (34 years, 80 kg, 162 cm) receives **1 g of calcium chloride** as 100 mg/min for 10 minutes. The panel is the **change in venous ionized calcium** above her own baseline, in mmol/L, not a concentration of the salt; her baseline (about 1.15 mmol/L in the study) is not part of the model, so the curve starts at zero. *Adjust weight to fat-free mass* is unticked, so the published parameters apply unscaled and the plot reproduces the paper's figure 5.

The ionized calcium rises to 0.24 mmol/L at 5 minutes and peaks at **0.39 mmol/L** at the end of the infusion. It then falls fast, with a 3.5-minute distribution half-life, to 0.27 at 15 minutes and 0.19 at 30: the peak has **halved about 30 minutes** after the infusion began, as the study reported. By 60 minutes it is 0.16 mmol/L, and the decline has slowed to the 109-minute half-life of the model, which is as far as the data go. There is no shaded band and no effect-site curve: the ionized calcium is the end point.

Now the substitution that prompted the study. In the dose table, change the units of both rows from `mg/min chloride` to **`mg/min gluconate`** and apply: the same 1 g over the same 10 minutes. The peak falls to **0.13 mmol/L**, a third of what it was. Calcium chloride and calcium gluconate are one drug here, with one curve, because both raise the same ionized calcium, and the units carry the salt because the gram does not: 1 g of calcium chloride is 6.8 mmol of calcium and 1 g of calcium gluconate is 2.2 mmol. The authors cite deaths from giving a gram of the gluconate in place of a gram of the chloride to hyperkalemic patients.

## What to look for

- **The equivalent dose.** With the units still `mg/min gluconate`, set the first row's rate to **300 mg/min**, 3 g over 10 minutes: the curve is back at 0.375 mmol/L at 10 minutes, 96% of the calcium chloride curve. The model's fitted ratio is 3.11 g of calcium gluconate per gram of calcium chloride (95% CI 2.77 to 3.48), close to the 3.05 the molecular weights of the hydrated salts give; 311 mg/min reproduces the chloride curve exactly. That bioequivalence is what the study was designed to measure, and the reason the two salts can be used interchangeably at a 3:1 ratio.
- **The package inserts' maximal rates.** The calcium gluconate insert allows 200 mg/min, the calcium chloride insert 100 mg/min. At 200 mg/min of gluconate for 10 minutes (2 g) the rise is 0.25 mmol/L, two-thirds of what 100 mg/min of chloride gives: the inserts' limits are not equivalent either.
- **Both salts in one patient.** Set the units back to `mg/min chloride`, set Max time to 120 minutes, and add two rows: `100 mg/min gluconate` at 60 minutes and `0 mg/min gluconate` at 70. The gram of gluconate adds its 0.13 mmol/L on top of the 0.16 that remains of the chloride, so the second hump reaches 0.27. One patient has one ionized calcium, and the curve is the sum.
- **A bolus.** Replace the two rows with **1 g chloride** at time 0. The model's peak jumps to 0.62 mmol/L (6.8 mmol into a 10.9 L central volume) and is down to 0.26 by 10 minutes. The study never gave a bolus, so this peak is an extrapolation of a model fitted to 10-minute infusions; the curves coincide with the infused dose from about 30 minutes. The same gram as `g gluconate` peaks at 0.20.
- **Fat-free mass on.** Tick *Adjust weight to fat-free mass*. This 80 kg woman's fat-free mass is below the 70 kg reference man's, so her central volume falls from 10.9 to 9.3 L and the calcium chloride peak rises to 0.45 mmol/L. The source found no effect of weight between 55 and 100 kg; the scaling is the library's assumption so that children and very large patients get a proportionate volume (see [Fat-free mass](help:models/fat-free-mass)).
- **A child.** Set 5 years, 20 kg, 110 cm, male, with the switch on, and give **20 mg/kg chloride** (0.4 g) as a bolus: 0.85 mmol/L. With the switch off the child would have an adult's 10.9 L and the same dose would raise the ionized calcium by only 0.25 mmol/L. No children were in the study; this is what the scaling assumption does, not a measurement.
- **Beyond the first hour.** Set Max time to 240 minutes. Calcium chloride is at 0.11 mmol/L at 2 hours and 0.05 at 4 hours. Sampling stopped at 60 to 90 minutes, and the authors expect a slower terminal phase than this, so the model returns to zero faster than ionized calcium really does.
