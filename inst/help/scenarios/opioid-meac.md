## What to look for

Morphine 10 mg, hydromorphone 1.5 mg and fentanyl 100 mcg are roughly equianalgesic doses, given together at time zero. On the three concentration panels they are on different scales and cannot be compared. On the **% MEAC** panel they can: each is plotted as a percentage of its own minimum effective analgesic concentration.

Fentanyl's effect site peaks within four minutes at several times MEAC and is back below MEAC within an hour. Hydromorphone peaks at about 20 minutes. Morphine's effect site is still rising at an hour, peaks at about an hour and a half, and is near MEAC at four hours. Same analgesic dose; onset and offset differing by an order of magnitude, set by each drug's ke0 and disposition.

The **total opioid** line adds the three: it shows that for the first hour the patient's analgesia is mostly fentanyl, and after that mostly morphine.

## Try next

- Remove the fentanyl and morphine and give hydromorphone 0.5 mg every hour for four hours: the pattern of a PCA with a lockout.
- Give the morphine as 2.5 mg every 10 minutes for 40 minutes instead of 10 mg at once. Because the effect site lags by over an hour, the plot shows why titrating morphine to effect every five minutes overshoots: the effect of the earlier doses has barely begun when the next is given.
- Change the patient's weight to 100 kg. Morphine and hydromorphone scale with weight in these models; fentanyl scales allometrically. The curves move by different amounts.
