## What to look for

Lamotrigine 200 mg twice daily and carbamazepine 400 mg twice daily together from the start, with carbamazepine stopped on day 28. Tick **Include antiseizure drug interactions**.

While carbamazepine is on board, lamotrigine sits near 4.6 mg/L with the interaction, against about 7.2 mg/L on the lighter *no interactions* line: carbamazepine induces the UGT enzyme that clears lamotrigine, so lamotrigine runs about a third lower. When carbamazepine is stopped on day 28 its own level falls away within a few days, but lamotrigine does not jump back at once. It climbs over the next ten to fourteen days, because the extra enzyme carbamazepine built has to be degraded, and that has a half-life of about 3.6 days. By day 42 lamotrigine has rejoined its uninduced line near 7 mg/L.

That lag is the clinically dangerous part: a patient whose lamotrigine was stable on carbamazepine can drift into toxic levels a week or two after carbamazepine is withdrawn, long after the carbamazepine itself has gone.

## Try next

- Watch the carbamazepine panel: its own line falls to near zero within about four days of stopping (half-life ~15 hours in the induced state), far faster than the lamotrigine recovery. The slow step is enzyme turnover, not drug washout.
- Replace the stop with a switch to valproate on day 28. Lamotrigine then overshoots: de-induction raises it while valproate's inhibition raises it further.

## Background

[Antiseizure drug interactions](help:models/antiseizure-interactions) describes the enzyme-turnover model. The de-induction half-life, 86 hours, is from Punyawudho 2009 (intravenous stable-labelled carbamazepine); the carbamazepine factor on lamotrigine is from Rivas 2008.
