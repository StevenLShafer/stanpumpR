## What to look for

A target-controlled infusion of propofol: an effect-site target of 3 mcg/mL at the start, stepped to 2 at thirty minutes and to 0 at sixty. You entered three concentrations, not a single dose; the pump worked out the rest.

- The **effect site** (solid) rises smoothly to 3 and holds there, with no overshoot. That is what targeting the effect site buys: the pump gives the largest loading dose that just reaches the target.
- The **plasma** line (dashed) overshoots well above 3, then falls as the effect site catches up; the two meet at the target at the time of peak effect, and the plasma then holds at 3 to keep the effect site there.
- Below the concentration panel is the **TCI rate panel**, in the drug's colour. It shows the pump rate: a loading dose written as a number, then the declining infusion that holds the target, a step down at thirty minutes, and off at sixty.
- With *Time until threshold* on, read how the time to wake lengthens the longer the target is held.

## Try next

- Change *Effect site target* to *Plasma target* for the first row. The plasma now rises straight to 3 with no overshoot, and the effect site lags up to it: faster to a plasma number, slower to effect.
- Add a manual bolus of propofol during the infusion and watch the pump stop giving drug until the concentration is back at the target.
- Set the first target higher, to 5, and see the larger loading dose.
