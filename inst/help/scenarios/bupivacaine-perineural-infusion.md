## What to look for

A peripheral nerve block with a catheter: 100 mg of bupivacaine as the initial injection (**mg RA**), then 10 mg/hr through the catheter (8 mL/hr of 0.125%, entered as **mg/hr RA**) for two days, stopped at day 2 by a row of 0 mg/hr RA. The plot is in **days**. The infusion runs into the same tissue depot as the injection and is absorbed from it at the same rate.

- **The block sets the peak**: about 1.0 mcg/mL at an hour, as the injected 100 mg is absorbed.
- **The infusion sets the plateau**: by about 12 hours the concentration has settled at 0.32 mcg/mL and stays there. At steady state the plasma concentration is the rate divided by the clearance (10 mg/hr ÷ 31 L/hr), whatever the absorption rate; the absorption rate and the 2.4-hour elimination half-life only decide how long it takes to get there.
- **Stopping**: the concentration halves in about 2 hours and is near zero by 8 hours. Drug already in the tissue keeps arriving for a while after the pump stops.

## Where to be careful

The plateau is flat because the model's clearance and binding are constant. In patients, total (bound plus unbound) bupivacaine rises steadily over two or three days of perineural infusion, because alpha-1-acid glycoprotein, the protein that binds it, rises after surgery. The unbound concentration, which causes toxicity, stays much steadier. So a measured total concentration on day 2 or 3 will usually be higher than this curve, and is not by itself evidence of toxicity. The absorption rate comes from one study of a single femoral and sciatic block; absorption from other sites, and through a catheter over days, may differ. See [Bupivacaine](help:drugs/bupivacaine).

## Try next

- Delete the 100 mg RA row: the infusion alone reaches three-quarters of the same plateau by 4 hours and 98 per cent by 12, with no analgesia from a block in the meantime, which is why the block is given first.
- Double the infusion to 20 mg/hr: the plateau doubles, to about 0.64 mcg/mL, and so does the time spent near it.
- Add a 50 mg RA bolus through the catheter at day 1, as a top-up for breakthrough pain.
- Change the drug in all three rows to [ropivacaine](help:drugs/ropivacaine), which also offers **mg/hr RA**. Its slower absorption puts the block's peak at about 3 hours, its lower clearance (in a model from patients over 60) puts the same infusion's plateau higher, at about 0.56 mcg/mL, and it takes about 9 hours to fall below 0.1 mcg/mL after the pump stops.
