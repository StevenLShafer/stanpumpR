## What to look for

A peripheral nerve block with a catheter: 300 mg of lidocaine as the initial injection (20 mL of 1.5%, **mg RA**), then 1% at 10 mL/hr through the catheter, which is 100 mg/hr (**mg/hr RA**), for two days, stopped at day 2 by a row of 0 mg/hr RA. The plot is in **days**. The infusion runs into the same tissue depot as the injection and is absorbed from it at the same rate. The shaded band, 0.5 to 1.5 mcg/mL, is the range aimed at by intravenous lidocaine infusions for systemic analgesia.

- **The block sets the peak**: about 1.7 mcg/mL at a little over an hour, from the injection and the first hour of infusion together.
- **The infusion sets the plateau**: by about 8 hours the concentration has settled at 1.19 mcg/mL, the rate divided by the clearance (100 mg/hr ÷ 84 L/hr), inside the systemic analgesic band. Lidocaine's clearance in its model is about three times bupivacaine's and five times ropivacaine's, so it takes a far higher rate, 2.4 g a day, to hold a similar plasma concentration.
- **Stopping**: the concentration halves in about 1.5 hours and falls below 0.1 mcg/mL by about 4 hours. Lidocaine on its own leaves the plasma much faster than that; what sets the fall here is the drug still in the tissue, absorbed with a half-time of about an hour.

## Where to be careful

Lidocaine is seldom infused through perineural catheters: its block is short and wanes with repeated dosing, and the large amounts needed carry a systemic load. The scenario is here for the pharmacokinetics. The absorption rate was fitted to a single axillary block with epinephrine; catheter infusions are usually plain solutions, absorbed from other sites, over days, and may be absorbed faster. The clearance in the model is constant and typical. In practice lidocaine's clearance depends on hepatic blood flow, falls with heart failure and liver disease, and its metabolites (monoethylglycinexylidide, glycinexylidide), which are not modelled, accumulate over days of infusion and add to its toxicity. Total concentrations also rise over days after surgery as alpha-1-acid glycoprotein rises. See [Lidocaine](help:drugs/lidocaine).

## Try next

- Change the two **mg/hr RA** rows to **mg/hr**, an intravenous infusion at the same rate. The plateau is exactly the same, 1.19 mcg/mL, because at steady state the plasma concentration is the rate over the clearance whatever the route. But after the pump stops, the intravenous plateau halves in about 15 minutes and is below 0.1 mcg/mL in about 75, against an hour and a half and four hours through the tissue: here absorption, not elimination, is the slow step.
- Delete the 300 mg RA row: the infusion alone is at 0.4 mcg/mL at an hour, below the band, and reaches 1.07 mcg/mL by 4 hours.
- Halve the infusion to 50 mg/hr: the plateau halves, to about 0.60 mcg/mL, the bottom of the band.
- Double it to 200 mg/hr: the plateau doubles to 2.4 mcg/mL, above the band and about halfway to where central nervous system toxicity begins.
