## What to look for

A woman at term, 30 years, 80 kg, 165 cm, with **Pregnant** ticked, receiving remifentanil 0.1 mcg/kg/min for an hour, a background rate at the top of the range used for labour analgesia. Remifentanil reaches its plateau within ten minutes, and the height of the plateau is set by clearance alone: the steady-state concentration is the infusion rate divided by the clearance. The review the library follows (Ansari, Carvalho, Shafer and Flood 2016) reports that remifentanil clearance more than doubles in pregnancy, so the model's clearance is doubled and the plateau is half what the same infusion would give her if she were not pregnant.

## Try next

- Untick **Pregnant** and apply: the plateau doubles. The time to reach it barely changes, because the volumes are unchanged and the approach to a plateau is governed by the same rate constants either way; what changes is the level.
- Tick **Minto / Schnider** under Graph Options: the Minto model, the one most TCI pumps implement, is drawn beside the Eleveld model, with its clearance doubled in the same way.
- Replace the infusion with the boluses of a patient-controlled regimen, 40 mcg at 0, 3, 6, 9 and 12 minutes. Each bolus peaks in the effect site in about a minute and a half and has largely gone by the next, which is why remifentanil, alone among the systemic opioids, can follow contractions.
- Give morphine 5 mg intravenously instead, with Pregnant ticked and then unticked. Its clearance rises by 70 per cent in pregnancy, but with an effect site that takes 90 minutes to peak and a half-life of hours, the difference takes an hour to become visible.

## Background

[Pregnancy](help:models/pregnancy) lists every drug of the review that is in the library and what, if anything, the field changes. [Remifentanil](help:drugs/remifentanil) describes the Eleveld and Kim models. The fetus is not modelled: remifentanil crosses the placenta but is cleared by the fetus's own esterases, which is why it is favoured over other systemic opioids in labour.
