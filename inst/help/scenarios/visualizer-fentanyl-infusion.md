## What to look for

Fentanyl 100 mcg at time zero and an infusion of 2 mcg/kg/hr (140 mcg an hour) for four hours, then stopped, in a 70 kg, 40-year-old man. *Peripheral compartments* is on, so V2 and V3 are drawn under the fentanyl panel. To watch it, double-click the fentanyl panel, press **Visualizer**, then **Play**.

**V3 takes most of the drug.** In this man V1 is 12.1 L, V2 35.7 L and V3 224 L. When the pump stops at four hours, 660 mcg has been given. V1 holds 24 mcg, V2 70 mcg and V3 314 mcg, nearly half of everything given, and 252 mcg has been eliminated. V3's level, 1.40 ng/mL, is still below the plasma's 2.0, so V3 is still filling.

**V3 keeps filling after the pump stops.** V2 turns round at once: its arrow goes red within a few seconds of the stop. V3 goes on taking drug for another 14 minutes, until its level meets the falling plasma's at 1.42 ng/mL, and only then does its arrow turn red.

**Then the periphery feeds the plasma.** Over the four hours after the stop, 149 mcg is eliminated, and 137 mcg of it first came back into V1 from V2 and V3, 94 mcg of it from V3. That returning drug is why the plasma falls so slowly. It takes 86 minutes to fall by half (the effect site, 93 minutes): fentanyl's context-sensitive half-time after a four-hour infusion. At eight hours V3 still holds 223 mcg, and its level is above the plasma's.

**Fentanyl's effect site barely lags the plasma at this pace.** The blue bar tracks V1 closely through the infusion, and both are 2.0 ng/mL at four hours.

The boxes are not drawn to scale: V3 is 18.5 times V1, and the visualizer caps the ratio at 10, as the note above the boxes says. The heights, the concentrations, are exact.

## Making a video

1. Choose the 2D or 3D view (**3D view** or the **v** key) and, in 3D, drag the scene to the viewpoint you want.
2. Leave the speed at 1 s = 5 min, where this eight-hour run opens: the video is 96 seconds long, and the stop falls at 48 seconds.
3. Press **Video**, then **Save MP4**.

For teaching, play it to the stop, pause, and ask which way the arrow between V1 and V3 points now, and which way it will point in a quarter of an hour.

## Try next

- Stop the infusion at one hour instead of four (move the 0 mcg/kg/hr row to 60). Much less drug has reached V3, so much less comes back, and the plasma falls by half in 25 minutes rather than 86.
- Replace fentanyl with remifentanil at 0.25 mcg/kg/min. Its clearance is so high that nothing returns from the periphery for long, and the plasma falls by half in about 2 minutes after four hours, as after any length of infusion.
