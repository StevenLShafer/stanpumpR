## What to look for

Ibuprofen 400 mg and acetaminophen 1 g by mouth, taken together, fasting, by a 40-year-old, 70 kg man. Each drug has its own panel, with the plasma dashed and the effect site solid.

Acetaminophen is absorbed faster. Its plasma peaks at 11.0 mcg/mL 34 minutes after the dose; ibuprofen's peaks at 23.6 mcg/mL at 62 minutes, because its absorption half-time is 27 minutes against acetaminophen's 11.5. Both effect sites follow the plasma with equilibration half-times of about an hour (53 minutes for acetaminophen, 62 for ibuprofen). Acetaminophen's effect site peaks at 6.2 mcg/mL at 1 hour 56 minutes, ibuprofen's at 16.1 mcg/mL at 2 hours 44 minutes.

The two drugs are cleared at much the same rate: the plasma half-life is 2.3 hours for acetaminophen and 2.0 hours for ibuprofen. Yet ibuprofen's effect site is above its threshold, 6.3 mcg/mL, from 48 minutes to 7 hours 17 minutes after the dose. Acetaminophen's is above its threshold, 5 mcg/mL, only from 1 hour 2 minutes to 3 hours 35 minutes. The difference is the dose against the threshold, not the half-life. This dose of ibuprofen takes its effect site to two and a half times its threshold, while 1 g of acetaminophen reaches only a quarter above its own, and falls back through it soon after the peak.

The thin black **time until threshold** line, read on each panel's right axis, says this from the start. It is blank for the few minutes before each tablet starts to be absorbed. It then shows how long until the effect site falls back through the threshold: a little over 7 hours for ibuprofen and about 3½ hours for acetaminophen, falling a minute per minute.

The panels do not add the two drugs. Hannam and Anderson, modelling adult dental-pain data, found their effects additive but with a ceiling. With more than about 5 mg/kg of ibuprofen, acetaminophen added little in the first hours after the dose; the combination's benefit showed more as a longer effect, 4 to 8 hours after it.

## Try next

- Halve the ibuprofen to 200 mg. Its effect site peaks at 8.1 mcg/mL and is above 6.3 mcg/mL only from 1 hour 30 minutes to 4 hours 39 minutes. That is 3 hours 9 minutes, less than half the 6 hours 29 minutes of 400 mg: halving the dose brings the peak down to just above the threshold.
- Give the acetaminophen intravenously instead: change its units from mg PO to mg. Its effect site peaks higher and sooner, at 7.3 mcg/mL at 90 minutes, and is above 5 mcg/mL from 27 minutes to 3 hours 54 minutes. That is under 3½ hours, still about half as long as the ibuprofen tablet.
