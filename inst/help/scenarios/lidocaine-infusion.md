## What to look for

A 100 mg bolus (about 1.5 mg/kg) followed by 100 mg/hr (about 1.5 mg/kg/hr): the bolus takes the plasma briefly far above the band and the effect site just above it, to about 1.8 mcg/mL at 10 minutes. The effect site comes back inside the band, 0.5 to 1.5 mcg/mL, at about 45 minutes and settles slowly towards the infusion's plateau, 1.2 mcg/mL, where it stays until the infusion stops at four hours.

The band matters more for lidocaine than for the anesthetic drugs because toxicity is close above it. Central nervous system symptoms (perioral numbness, tinnitus, light-headedness) begin above about 5 mcg/mL in most patients and seizures follow at higher concentrations. Hover at 4 hours to see how much margin the regimen has.

Lidocaine is a two-compartment model: there is no slow compartment, so the concentration approaches a true plateau rather than drifting indefinitely, and the offset after the infusion stops is not context-sensitive.

## Try next

- Double the infusion to 200 mg/hr and set *Max time* to 12 hours. The plateau is at about 2.4 mcg/mL in this model; a patient with reduced hepatic blood flow (heart failure, cirrhosis, or the reduced cardiac output of anesthesia) would clear less and sit higher.
- Give a second bolus of 100 mg at 60 minutes, as might happen if a bolus were repeated by mistake.
- Turn on **Time until threshold** to see how long after stopping the drug stays above 0.5 mcg/mL.

## Background
