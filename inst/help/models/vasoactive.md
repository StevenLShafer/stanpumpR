## Vasoactive and resuscitation drugs

The intravenous vasopressors, vasodilators, rate-control and antiarrhythmic drugs, and resuscitation drugs of intensive care are listed under **Vasoactive and resuscitation** in the startup menu: [atropine](help:drugs/atropine), [digoxin](help:drugs/digoxin) (oral) and intravenous [digoxin](help:drugs/digoxinIV) and [digoxin in children](help:drugs/digoxinIVPediatric), [diltiazem](help:drugs/diltiazem), [dopamine](help:drugs/dopamine), [epinephrine](help:drugs/epinephrine), [esmolol](help:drugs/esmolol), [labetalol](help:drugs/labetalol), [magnesium sulfate](help:drugs/magnesiumSulfate), [metoprolol](help:drugs/metoprolol), [milrinone](help:drugs/milrinone) and [milrinone in children](help:drugs/milrinonePediatric), [nicardipine](help:drugs/nicardipine), [nitroglycerin](help:drugs/nitroglycerin), [norepinephrine](help:drugs/norepinephrine), [phenylephrine](help:drugs/phenylephrine), [procainamide](help:drugs/procainamide), [propranolol](help:drugs/propranolol) and [verapamil](help:drugs/verapamil). [Clevidipine](help:drugs/clevidipine) was already in the library and stays under **Antihypertensives**. Every one is a **concentration model only**: no blood pressure, heart rate or rhythm is predicted. An effect site is drawn only where the time to peak effect is known, and solved from it: norepinephrine (74 seconds to peak arterial pressure after a peripheral bolus, Joachim 2024), diltiazem (3.1 minutes, from Salerno 1989's 4.3-minute peak fall in heart rate after a 2-minute infusion), nicardipine (72 seconds to the largest fall in arterial pressure after a bolus, Cheung 1999), esmolol (2 minutes to the lowest heart rate after a bolus, Krumpl 2017), metoprolol (3.3 minutes, the peak reduction in heart rate in Ritchie 1998's effect compartment), verapamil (4 minutes, the label's 3 to 5 minutes to peak therapeutic effect), atropine (90 seconds, the clinical time to peak heart rate), milrinone (5 minutes, the peak rise in cardiac index after a bolus in Butterworth 1995) and intravenous digoxin (87 minutes, from the effect compartment Hornestam 2003 fitted to the fall in ventricular rate). Digoxin and milrinone, with their published target ranges, have shaded bands.

### Why not all of them

A drug is offered only where a published model could be read and checked. That rule removes more of this group than of any other in the library, for three reasons.

**Many are endogenous.** Epinephrine, norepinephrine, dopamine and vasopressin are made by the body, so a study measures the drug given plus the patient's own, and a model needs an endogenous production term. The engine has no constant input, so these drugs are plotted, where they are plotted at all, as the concentration **above** the patient's own, as [intravenous calcium](help:drugs/calcium) and magnesium are. For epinephrine that baseline is under 1% of an infusion's concentration; for norepinephrine it is nearer 7%.

**Several have no kinetics in the usual sense.** Nitroprusside breaks down in blood within minutes and is not itself measured; the cyanide and thiocyanate it releases have kinetics of their own but no transferable model of how much is formed. Adenosine's half-life in whole blood is a second or two, and its clearance falls as the dose rises. Sodium bicarbonate has a "bicarbonate space" that changes with the patient's own bicarbonate, not a volume and a clearance.

**Most of the rest were fitted decades ago.** Where only the abstract could be read, an abstract can still fix a model: two half-lives, a clearance and a central volume determine a two-compartment model, and a fifth number, when there is one, checks it. That is how labetalol and diltiazem were built, and each drug's page gives the check. The other papers were read in full, and two of them turned out not to be usable as published: nicardipine's Cheung 1999, whose table does not form a consistent compartmental model (nicardipine is built from Modi 1993, and takes only its time to peak effect from Cheung), and methylene blue's (Walter-Sack 2009), which reports only non-compartmental summaries. But most abstracts print a clearance and a terminal half-life only, which is one or two numbers short, and a half-life is never permission to invent a volume.
