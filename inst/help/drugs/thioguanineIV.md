### The model

Kitchen and colleagues (*Clin Cancer Res* 1997;3:713-717) gave thioguanine by continuous intravenous infusion, 10 or 20 mg/m² per hour for 24 or 36 hours, to 18 patients aged 4 to 25 with refractory cancers. Two compartments: a central volume of 56 L/m², and elimination that saturates, with a maximum rate of 3.7 µM per hour and a Michaelis constant of 2.5 µM. Doubling the infusion rate raised the concentration about fourfold, which is what saturation does.

### Body surface area

The central volume, and with it the maximum elimination rate, scale with body-surface area (Mosteller's formula; the paper does not name one).

### What is plotted

Plasma thioguanine, in mcg/mL (1 µM is 0.167 mcg/mL). At the end of 24-hour infusions the model gives 1.0 and 3.2 µM, from which the paper's own formula returns its clearances, 987 and 608 mL/min/m². The trial aimed for a concentration above 1 µM, the level that killed leukaemic cells in culture; that is a dose-finding target, not a therapeutic range, and is not drawn.

### Where to be careful

- **An intravenous research route.** Thioguanine is approved only as an oral tablet; this model is of an investigational intravenous form, and says nothing about the tablet.
- **The active drug is inside cells.** Thioguanine works as its nucleotides in red and white cells, whose levels TPMT and NUDT15 genotype govern; they are not modelled.
- **Children and young adults** (4 to 25 years) given infusions of 24 to 36 hours.
- **Pregnancy:** no pharmacokinetic data were found; nothing is changed.
