### The model

Carlsson Petri and colleagues' population analysis of the phase 3 trials in type 2 diabetes (*Diabetes Ther* 2018): one compartment with first-order absorption, an apparent clearance of 0.0478 L/h and volume of 12.2 L for an 85 kg reference woman, and an absorption rate of 0.0286/h fixed from richly sampled studies. Covariates act on clearance only: body weight as (weight / 85 kg)<sup>0.774</sup> (the supplement's Table S3), the only important one, which makes exposure 40% higher at 55 kg and 27% lower at 127 kg, as the paper reports; clearance 4% higher in men; and 1.2% lower from 65 to 74 years, 3.9% lower from 75. Race, ethnicity, the injection site (the curve is the abdomen's) and the paper's renal-impairment groups, whose eGFR equation it does not name, are not applied; each changes clearance by 8% or less. At 1 mg weekly the reference subject's average steady-state concentration is 124.5 ng/mL, 30.3 nmol/L, against the paper's 29.8 nmol/L. The half-life is about a week, and three to five weekly doses are needed to approach steady state.

### Product and route

Ozempic injection only (`mg SC`, `mg SC qwk`), at the 0.5 and 1 mg the source studied. Wegovy (2.4 mg, in obesity) is outside the fitted doses and population and is held in the registry. The oral tablets are a different product with a bioavailability under 1%: [oral semaglutide](help:drugs/semaglutideOral), a separate model. Overgaard and colleagues' two-compartment fit of intravenous and subcutaneous data (2019) is held in the registry: its absorption is fitted separately for each study strength of the product, and it is the disposition model on which the oral tablets' analysis is built.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only, in ng/mL (1 nmol/L = 4.11 ng/mL). The exposure-response models for HbA1c and body weight at week 26 or 30 relate the steady-state average concentration to a change over months; they are not a glucose curve and are not drawn. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics).
