### The model

Carlsson Petri and colleagues' population analysis of the phase 3 trials in type 2 diabetes (*Diabetes Ther* 2018): one compartment with first-order absorption, an apparent clearance of 0.0478 L/h and volume of 12.2 L for an 85 kg reference woman, and an absorption rate of 0.0286/h fixed from richly sampled studies. Body weight is the only important covariate, on clearance alone. Its exponent is in the paper's supplement, which could not be read; 0.78 is derived from the exposures the paper reports (40% higher at 55 kg, 27% lower at 127 kg). At 1 mg weekly the reference subject's average steady-state concentration is 124.5 ng/mL, 30.3 nmol/L, against the paper's 29.8 nmol/L. The half-life is about a week, and three to five weekly doses are needed to approach steady state.

### Product and route

Ozempic injection only (`mg SC`, `mg SC qwk`), at the 0.5 and 1 mg the source studied. Wegovy (2.4 mg, in obesity) is outside the fitted doses and population and is held in the registry. The oral tablets are a different product with a bioavailability under 1% and are not this drug. Overgaard and colleagues' two-compartment fit (2019), whose 0.0348 L/h clearance is the value for diabetes and not its healthy reference, is held in the registry until its table is transcribed.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only, in ng/mL (1 nmol/L = 4.11 ng/mL). The exposure-response models for HbA1c and body weight at week 26 or 30 relate the steady-state average concentration to a change over months; they are not a glucose curve and are not drawn. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics).
