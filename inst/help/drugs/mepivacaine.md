### The model

Burm and colleagues (*Anesth Analg* 1997;84:85-89) gave racemic mepivacaine intravenously to healthy volunteers and measured the two enantiomers separately: R(&minus;) clearance 0.79 L/min and steady-state volume 103 L, S(+) 0.35 L/min and 57 L. The central volumes and distribution clearances were not reported, so each enantiomer is **approximated** as one compartment with its steady-state volume. That keeps each enantiomer's clearance and volume, but gives terminal half-lives of 90 and 113 minutes against the measured 113 and 123, and no early distribution phase.

A racemic dose puts half into each enantiomer. The sum of the two is a biexponential with positive coefficients, and that is exactly the response of a two-compartment model, which is what the engine runs: its central volume is 73 L and its small "peripheral compartment" is a mathematical device, not a tissue. The total concentration plotted is R plus S, exactly as the two parallel pools would give it; either enantiomer alone is not reported.

### Regional anesthesia (RA)

A dose in **mg RA** is a tissue injection, absorbed from a depot by first-order kinetics, bioavailability 1, no lag. Simon and colleagues (2002) measured arterial plasma concentrations after 600 mg of 1.5% mepivacaine with epinephrine 5 mcg/mL for axillary block, with a mean peak of 3.89 mcg/mL at 25 minutes. Their own absorption half-time, 3.8 minutes, joined to this disposition predicts a peak of 7.2 mcg/mL. Mepivacaine is therefore absorbed through **two depots in parallel**, fitted to their 17 published mean concentrations from 5 to 180 minutes with this disposition and bioavailability 1: 47 per cent of the dose with a half-time of 4.2 minutes, and 53 per cent with a half-time of 139 minutes. The model peaks at 3.8 mcg/mL at about 27 minutes. It is an internal fit to one study's means, it describes axillary block with epinephrine, and three hours of sampling cannot establish the slow depot's tail. The single depot that best reproduces the same curve (0.0272/min, a half-time of 25 minutes) peaks at 5.2 mcg/mL at about 70 minutes, a third too high and too late: one depot, with a disposition that has no distribution phase, cannot give both the fast rise and the flat plateau. The drug file can switch to it (`MEPIVACAINE_RA_TWO_DEPOT`).

A dose in **mg** is intravenous.

### Effect site and typical concentrations

None, as for [bupivacaine](help:drugs/bupivacaine): total plasma concentration only, and no band.
