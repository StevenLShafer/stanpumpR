### The model

From Helfer and colleagues' 2026 study of children given intravenous pentobarbital (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2026;66:e70204, doi:10.1002/jcph.70204): two compartments, allometric on body weight, with a clearance of 5.21 L/h, a central volume of 37.4 L, an intercompartmental clearance of 18.1 L/h and a peripheral volume of 63.9 L at 70 kg. No covariate beyond weight was kept.

### Route and extrapolation

Intravenous only (`mg`, `mg/kg`, `mg/hr`, `mg/kg/hr`), as pentobarbital is used for refractory status epilepticus as a titrated infusion. The model is paediatric; an adult receives the 70 kg allometric values, which sit at the high end of adult clearance, so an adult's levels during a long barbiturate coma may be underpredicted. A second paediatric model (Ketharanathan and colleagues, *Clin Pharmacokinet* 2023) needs C-reactive protein, which the app does not take, and is not used.

### Band

4 to 21 mg/L, for the **treatment of status epilepticus** only. These are the serum concentrations observed during burst-suppression coma, which Helfer and colleagues take as the steady-state target for seizure control. They cite Lowenstein, Aminoff and Simon (*Neurology* 1988;38:395-400; barbiturate anesthesia for status epilepticus in 14 patients), which has not been read here. The typical line at 12.5 mg/L is the midpoint, not a value from either paper. Helfer's recommended seizure regimen, 5 mg/kg then 1 mg/kg/hr, approaches about 13 mg/L at 70 kg. The band does not apply to sedation: Helfer uses 1 to 5 mg/L (peak) for preoperative sedation and 5 to 15 mg/L for deep sedation, and regards concentrations above 45 mg/L as potentially toxic. Titration is to the electroencephalogram, not to a level. Pentobarbital is not in the AGNP consensus guidelines' 2026 table. There is no effect site.
