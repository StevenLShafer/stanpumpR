### The model

Rambiritch and colleagues' analysis of 24 poorly controlled South African adults with type 2 diabetes at steady state on 2.5 to 20 mg a day (*Clin Pharmacol Adv Appl* 2016): two compartments with first-order absorption at 0.53/h, no lag, apparent clearance 2.16 L/h, central volume 11.7 L, intercompartmental clearance 3.84 L/h and peripheral volume 68.1 L. No covariate analysis was done, so the parameters are scaled by body size alone. The authors note that their peak concentrations were about twice those of other studies, and the two-compartment model's terminal half-life (about 37 hours) is much longer than the 4 to 8 hours of their noncompartmental analysis, so the curve's tail is uncertain.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only, in ng/mL, of the parent drug. The active hydroxy metabolites, which carry part of the glucose lowering and accumulate in renal failure, are not modelled. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics).
