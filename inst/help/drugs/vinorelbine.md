### The model

Vinorelbine comes from Variol and colleagues (*Eur J Clin Pharmacol* 2002;58:467-476): 175 patients given vinorelbine alone, intravenously (20 to 45 mg/m²) or as the oral soft capsule (60 to 100 mg/m²), with intravenous and oral data fitted together. Three compartments describe it, with a delayed first-order oral absorption:

- clearance 41.9 L/h, central volume 22.2 L, and peripheral compartments of 136 L and 916 L;
- half-lives of 7 minutes, 3 hours and 37 hours;
- by mouth, 36% of the dose is absorbed, with a half-time of 1.4 hours after a delay of 9 minutes (fasting).

### What is plotted

Vinorelbine in **whole blood**, in ng/mL, which is how the study measured it. Doses are entered in mg as labelled (vinorelbine base), intravenously as a dose or an infusion, or by mouth (`mg PO`); a dose prescribed per m² must be multiplied by the body surface area first. There is no effect site and no band.

### The parameters chosen

The paper's covariate equation gives a clearance of 53 L/h at its average body size, 27% above its own Table 3 (41.9 L/h), and its figure's late concentrations follow the table. The table's values are used, so no covariate is applied: body surface area, a creatinine clearance below 70 mL/min, raised transaminases, a platelet count above 400, raised alkaline phosphatase (on the oral fraction) and food (a longer delay) all appear in the paper's equations and not in the curve, apart from the library's own scaling for body size.

### Where to be careful

- **Liver impairment** lowers clearance and is not represented.
- **CYP3A4 inhibitors** raise exposure. Not a monitoring tool.

Every parameter was read in the paper's PDF.
