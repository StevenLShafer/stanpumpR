### The model

High-dose etoposide in children with neuroblastoma comes from Duong and colleagues (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 2019;85:136-146): 51 children aged 1.7 to 8.3 years given etoposide as a 96-hour infusion before autologous transplantation (with carboplatin and melphalan). Two compartments describe it, scaled by weight alone:

- at 15.5 kg, clearance 0.59 L/h, central volume 2.7 L, and a peripheral compartment of 3.8 L exchanging at 0.07 L/h.

The clearances scale with weight to the ¾ power and the volumes in proportion, about a reference weight of 15.5 kg (see [carboplatinPediatric](help:drugs/carboplatinPediatric)).

### What is plotted

Plasma etoposide in mcg/mL (mg/L). Doses are entered in mg or as an infusion rate. There is no effect site and no band.

### Where to be careful

- **The late tail is unreliable.** The peripheral compartment is essentially unidentified (its estimate's relative error is 234%) and gives a terminal half-life of 42 hours, where 3 to 8 hours is usual in children.
- For conventional one-hour infusions, [etoposidePediatric](help:drugs/etoposidePediatric) (Urien 2011) was fitted to richer early data. Children 1.7 to 8.3 years. Not a monitoring tool.

Every parameter was read in the paper's PDF.
