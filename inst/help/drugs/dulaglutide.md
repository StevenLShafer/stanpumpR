### The model

Geiser and colleagues' phase 3 population analysis of 1067 patients with type 2 diabetes on 0.75 or 1.5 mg once weekly (*Clin Pharmacokinet* 2016): two compartments with first-order absorption, on the true scale because an absolute bioavailability study fixed the bioavailability. Absorption 0.00769/h (a half-time of almost 4 days), clearance 0.0593 L/h, intercompartmental clearance 0.0201 L/h, volumes 2.25 and 3.75 L. Bioavailability is 0.47 at 92.5 kg, times exp(−0.00877 × (weight − 92.5)): body weight, the only covariate, acted alike on clearance and volume, so it was put on bioavailability. The apparent clearance at 92.5 kg is 0.126 L/h. These are the values the FDA's review tabulates.

At 1.5 mg weekly the model gives a steady-state exposure of 12.9 mg·h/L over the week and a peak of 92 ng/mL at 83 kg, against 14.0 and 114 observed (coefficients of variation 30 and 35%); a single dose peaks at about 58 hours (observed median 48).

### Product and doses

Trulicity, `mg SC` and `mg SC qwk`. Bioavailability 0.47 is applied to every dose. The phase 2 analysis made bioavailability fall with the dose, but the final model gives it for 1.5 mg only, and the observed steady states at 0.75 and 1.5 mg are close to dose-proportional. The 3 and 4.5 mg strengths are outside the fitted doses.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only, in ng/mL. The FDA's model of fasting glucose and HbA1c over months is not drawn. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics).
