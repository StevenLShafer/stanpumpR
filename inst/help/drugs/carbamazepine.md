### The model

From Graves and colleagues (*Pharmacotherapy* 1998;18:273-281), 829 adults on maintenance carbamazepine: one compartment, apparent oral, clearance (0.0134 × weight + 3.58) L/h, volume 1.97 L/kg, absorption 0.441/h. At 70 kg the clearance is 4.52 L/h and the half-life about 21 hours.

### The chronic state only

Carbamazepine induces its own metabolism, so clearance roughly doubles over the first few weeks. The engine is time-invariant and cannot cross that transition, so this is the **induced, steady-state** kinetics of a maintenance population — the clinically useful case. A single dose in a treatment-naive patient is cleared far more slowly (half-life 25 to 65 hours); that curve is not produced. Clearance is 0.749 times as high at age 70 and over.

### Formulations and what is not plotted

Suspension and immediate-release and chewable tablets share these parameters; the extended-release tablet (`mg PO ER`) is given slower absorption and 0.89 relative availability (the Tegretol-XR label). The active 10,11-epoxide (about 10 to 15 per cent of the parent) is not plotted, and co-medication (phenytoin raises clearance about 40 per cent) is not applied. There is no effect site. Carbamazepine is no longer available intravenously in the US (Carnexiv withdrawn 2026).

### Band

4 to 12 mg/L (ILAE; Patsalos and colleagues, 2008).
