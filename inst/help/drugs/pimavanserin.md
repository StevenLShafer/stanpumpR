### The model

Pimavanserin treats the hallucinations and delusions of Parkinson's disease psychosis. Its model is the sponsor's final population model of patients with Parkinson's disease psychosis, as printed in the FDA's clinical pharmacology review of Nuplazid (NDA 207318, Table 6): one compartment, apparent clearance 587 L/day for a patient 168 cm tall and 71 years old, rising 0.85% per cm of height and falling 0.76% per year of age, apparent volume 2021 L, with absorption at 7.43 per day after a lag of 0.07 days (1.7 hours). The half-life is about 57 hours and steady state takes about 12 days; on 34 mg a day the average concentration is about 68 ng/mL.

### Doses as labelled

The model was fitted to doses of pimavanserin **tartrate** (the trials' 40 mg); the label prescribes the base (34 mg capsules, 10 mg tablets). Enter doses as labelled; the model converts.

### Where to be careful

The absorption was fixed from earlier analyses of troughs, so after a single dose the model peaks at about 13 hours, later than the label's median of 6 hours (range 4 to 24); the steady state, which the model was built for, is unaffected. The active metabolite (N-desmethyl, half-life about 200 hours) is not modelled. CYP3A4 inhibitors triple exposure (the label's dose is then 10 mg) and inducers lower it 91%; neither is applied. QT prolongation rises with the concentration. No effect site and no band: the AGNP update 2026 gives 40 to 85 ng/mL, but for schizophrenia, from a study at 34 mg, and itself warns that the range may not apply to patients with Parkinson's disease (Hart 2026, [doi 10.1055/a-2860-7861](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2860-7861)).
