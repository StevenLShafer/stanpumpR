### The model

None of the published studies of oxycodone fitted a population model, so this one is assembled from five of them. The intravenous disposition pools three Finnish studies: Pöyhiä and colleagues (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 1991), the control patients of Kirvelä and colleagues (*J Clin Anesth* 1996), and the youngest group of Liukas and colleagues (*Drugs Aging* 2011). Weighted by the number of subjects, the pooled clearance is 14.7 mL/min/kg and the steady-state volume 3.27 L/kg. That is 1.03 L/min and 229 L at 70 kg.

The split into two compartments comes from Kirvelä's per-subject biexponential fits. The central volume is 13% of the steady-state volume (30 L at 70 kg), and the intercompartmental clearance is 2.3 times the clearance (2.40 L/min). The terminal half-life is 3.4 hours. The central volume is the least certain number: the early intravenous concentrations in these studies disagree widely.

### Dose basis

A dose is entered as oxycodone hydrochloride, as labelled, and the curve is oxycodone base, so the model's clearance and volumes are the hydrochloride dose over the base concentration. The three intravenous studies all computed theirs on the base instead, though none says so. Pöyhiä and Kirvelä gave Oxanest, the hydrochloride trihydrate, at 0.07 mg/kg, which both papers call "0.05 mg/kg free base" (it holds 0.054). Pöyhiä's table lists each patient's dose as the base in the trihydrate drawn up, beside the clearance; Kirvelä, the same group with the same product and wording, is taken to have done the same. Liukas gave 5 mg of the anhydrous hydrochloride, but its own table of clearances, areas under the curve and weights is consistent only with a dose of about 4.5 mg, the base in 5 mg of hydrochloride. So each study's clearance and volume is multiplied by 1.116, the hydrochloride's molar mass over the base's (351.83 over 315.37 g/mol). Steven L. Shafer decided this reading in October 2026. Before it, the library had scaled Pöyhiä and Kirvelä by 1.33 and 1.4 and left Liukas as printed, which put clearance 12% high and intravenous concentrations about a tenth low; before that, the values were pooled as printed, 10% low. Oral concentrations did not move with either change, because bioavailability is taken from the ratio of the pooled clearance to Lalovic's oral clearance (below).

On the hydrochloride basis the three studies' clearances are 12.2 (Pöyhiä), 13.3 (Liukas) and 18.6 (Kirvelä) mL/min/kg, and the model is within 8 per cent of each of Liukas's four age groups.

### Oral and intravenous

Oxycodone can be given as **mg** (intravenous bolus), **mg/hr** (intravenous infusion), by [target-controlled infusion](help:tci) (**Plasma target** or **Effect site target**, in ng/mL, on the intravenous model), **mg PO**, or as oral liquid, **mg PO liquid** or, for paediatric dosing, **mg/kg PO liquid**. The liquid is simulated exactly like a tablet: the ROXICODONE label and an FDA bioequivalence review both found oral solution and immediate-release tablets equivalent in peak and exposure, with the solution absorbed no faster. Doses are entered in mg, not mL; the 20 mg/mL concentrate is for opioid-tolerant patients only. Oral bioavailability is 0.75: the pooled intravenous clearance divided by the oral clearance that Lalovic and colleagues measured (*Clin Pharmacol Ther* 2006), which keeps the oral exposure where Lalovic measured it. That is inside the 60 to 80 per cent Lalovic quotes from the literature. Absorption has a rate constant of 0.01/min (a half-time of 69 minutes) and no lag. That reproduces the 30 ng/mL peak of Lalovic's mean curve after 15 mg and the concentrations over the following 12 hours. The model's peak comes at 35 minutes, earlier than Lalovic's mean of 65 minutes. See [the oral oxycodone scenario](scenario:oral-oxycodone) and [Oral, intramuscular, intranasal and regional anesthesia doses](help:models/absorption).

### Covariates

- **Age.** Liukas found clearance 28 to 34 per cent lower in patients over 60. With the renal part removed, clearance falls by 0.53 per cent a year above 30, so it is 0.74 of the young value at 80.
- **Renal function.** Kirvelä's patients in end-stage renal failure had a clearance per kilogram 0.75 of the controls'. Clearance falls linearly with CKD-EPI eGFR below 100 mL/min/1.73 m², to 0.73 at an eGFR of zero. A blank creatinine assumes a normal value for age and sex, so renal impairment counts only when a **creatinine** is entered.
- **Size.** Under the default [fat-free-mass scaling](help:models/fat-free-mass), volumes follow fat-free mass and clearances its 0.75 power. Unticking the box scales both linearly with total weight, as the per-kilogram studies reported them.

In children the model is less certain. Balyan and colleagues' 30 children aged 2 to 17 cleared oral oxycodone about a third faster than this model predicts for their size.

### Effect site

Lalovic measured pupil constriction after oral oxycodone and found that the parent drug alone explained it, with an effect-site equilibration half-time of 11 minutes. Its metabolites contributed nothing. The model's tPeak of 12.36 minutes after an intravenous bolus reproduces that ke0 in a 70 kg adult. So oxycodone's effect follows its plasma concentration closely, and an oral dose's effect peaks about an hour after the dose.

### MEAC and typical concentrations

MEAC is 12 ng/mL, set for consistency with morphine's 8 ng/mL. None of the five studies behind this model measured analgesia, and Lalovic's EC50 of 30 ng/mL is for miosis. At equal intravenous doses this model reaches about 1.25 times morphine's concentration, and intravenous oxycodone takes 1 to 1.3 times as many milligrams as morphine for the same analgesia, which puts a MEAC consistent with morphine's at 10 to 13 ng/mL. Measured directly in adults, the values are higher. Kokki and colleagues (*Basic Clin Pharmacol Toxicol* 2012;110:469-475) titrated intravenous oxycodone after laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Pain returned at a mean of 21 ng/mL (95% confidence interval 13 to 29), and relief came at 45 to 50 ng/mL. Morphine's 8 ng/mL sits at the low end of morphine's own literature in the same way, so the two are kept together. The audit of the MEAC and time to peak effect of every opioid in the library (October 2026) confirmed 12 on this basis. One direct measurement does match 12. Olkkola and colleagues (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 1994;38:71-76), the source Kokki's group cites for an analgesic concentration of 12 ng/mL, gave 0.1 mg/kg intravenously to 18 children aged 2 to 10 after eye surgery. Twelve needed rescue analgesia when an observer scored their pain at 2 or more, a mean of nearly three hours after the dose, and the plasma concentration fitted to each child's curve at that moment was 12 ng/mL (± 8; median 10, range 3 to 32). The children were mostly asleep and in mild pain, and the rescue came at the return of pain, so this is nearer the concentration at which pain returns than a MEAC, and it is fitted rather than measured. The shaded band is 10 to 20 ng/mL. The AGNP consensus guidelines' 2026 update (Hart and colleagues, *Pharmacopsychiatry* 2026;59:166-295, Table 5) gives 5 to 100 ng/mL, 12 hours after a dose, for pain, a spread that depends on tolerance; it is not drawn.

### Active metabolite

Oxycodone forms **oxymorphone** by CYP2D6, so a dose of oxycodone adds an [oxymorphone](help:drugs/oxymorphone) row. Oxymorphone was undetectable or barely detectable after intravenous oxycodone in the Finnish studies. The model sets its AUC at 1 per cent of oxycodone's after an intravenous dose. After an oral dose, more forms on the first pass through the liver. The model puts that AUC at 3.35 per cent, between Balyan's 2.7 per cent in genotyped normal metabolisers and Lalovic's 4 per cent. The **CYP 2D6** field scales both. Balyan found intermediate metabolisers at about 0.63 of normal, and the model uses 0.65. See [Active metabolites](help:models/metabolites).

### Pregnancy

**Pregnant** changes nothing for oxycodone. Kokki and colleagues (*Basic Clin Pharmacol Toxicol* 2012;111:182-188) fitted a population model to 15 women given intravenous oxycodone in the first stage of labour: clearance 0.92 L/min and a steady-state volume of 195 L, at a median 82 kg. The women were given Oxanest, the hydrochloride trihydrate, and the fit is taken to be on that dose, so on this library's hydrochloride basis it is 0.80 L/min and 169 L. This model gives a non-pregnant woman of that size 0.92 L/min and 197 L, so the labouring women's clearance is 0.87 of it and their volume 0.86. That is a comparison with a model rather than a control group, so nothing is applied. Kokki's shorter half-time in labour (2.6 hours against 3.8) is against another study's healthy volunteers, whose clearance was lower than this model's. Until October 2026 the library multiplied the clearance by that ratio of half-times (3.8/2.6 = 1.46, through Ansari and colleagues' review, *Anesth Analg* 2016;122:786-804), which would give these women 69% more clearance than Kokki measured; it was dropped. See [Pregnancy](help:models/pregnancy).
