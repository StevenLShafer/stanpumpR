### The model

From Silva and colleagues (*Eur J Pharm Sci* 2025;207:107023), 64 adults with refractory epilepsy: one compartment, apparent oral, clearance 0.761 L/h, volume 48.1 L, absorption 0.671/h.

### Caveats

The half-life this implies, about 44 hours, is shorter than the 63 to 69 hours healthy adults show (Kochak 1998): the refractory cohort was largely on enzyme-inducing drugs, which raise clearance, so a monotherapy patient's accumulation may be underpredicted. The source's inducer drug-load covariate (clearance × 1.63 per defined daily dose of carbamazepine, phenytoin or phenobarbital) is applied, as × 1.63 per inducer at its reference concentration, only when the **Include antiseizure drug interactions** box in Graph Options is ticked (see [Antiseizure drug interactions](help:models/antiseizure-interactions)). Zonisamide binds saturably to red cells, so its plasma kinetics are mildly non-linear at high doses; the model, like the source, is linear. Capsule and oral suspension are label-bridged and share these parameters.

### Band

10 to 40 mg/L, 24 hours after a dose: the AGNP consensus guidelines' 2026 update (Hart and colleagues, *Pharmacopsychiatry* 2026;59:166-295, Table 5, https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2860-7861; laboratory alert level 80 mg/L), the same range as the ILAE's (Patsalos and colleagues, 2008).
