### The model

Intravenous droperidol, from Lehmann and colleagues (*Br J Anaesth* 1988;61:297-301): 30 ASA I–II surgical adults under neuroleptanalgesia (14 to 65 years, 48 to 90 kg) given 5, 10 or 15 mg intravenously, sampled from 1 to 360 minutes. The kinetics were linear over the three doses and unrelated to age or weight. The paper gives a triexponential for each dose's mean plasma curve; those three curves, each divided by its dose, were fitted together with one triexponential and converted to a three-compartment model:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| V1 | 10.75 L |
| k10, k12, k13 | 0.0602, 0.230, 0.0616 per minute |
| k21, k31 | 0.0739, 0.0135 per minute |
| Clearance | 0.646 L/min |
| Volume at steady state | 93 L |
| Half-lives | 1.7, 24 and 135 minutes |

The model follows each of Lehmann's three mean curves within 20% from 6 minutes on; in the first few minutes the three published curves differ among themselves. Lehmann's mean of the individual clearances is higher, 798 mL/min: the mean of individual values is usually higher than the clearance of the mean curve.

### How it compares

- **Gupta and colleagues** (*Pharm Res* 1992;9:694-696) infused 3 mg over 24 hours in eight healthy men and found a plateau of about 3.2 ng/mL and a clearance of 657 mL/min. This model predicts 3.2 ng/mL for that infusion.
- **Fischler and colleagues** (*Anesthesiology* 1986;64:486-489), ten anaesthetised adults given 150 µg/kg: steady-state volume 1.40 L/kg (93 L at their 66 kg, the same as here) and a clearance of 14.1 mL/min/kg, the highest of the adult studies.
- **Cooper and colleagues** (*SAGE Open Med* 2018), seven healthy men: noncompartmental clearance 563 mL/min. Their compartmental model, which this drug used at first, had no sample in the first 25 minutes and a volume of about 20 L, and is no longer used.
- **Grunwald and colleagues** (*Anesth Analg* 1993;76:1238-1242), twelve children of 3.5 to 12 years given 0.05 mg/kg: 4.66 mL/kg/min and 0.58 L/kg, with a half-life of 101 minutes. Scaled to a 20 kg child, this adult model gives about 13 mL/kg/min and 1.4 L/kg, **about 2.5 times too high**, so in children it predicts concentrations about 2.5 times too low.

### A separate drug from intramuscular droperidol

[Intramuscular droperidol](help:drugs/droperidolIM) runs a different study's **apparent** parameters, and neither set may be moved to the other route.

### Covariates and effect

No covariate was found. The values are read as the 70 kg reference adult's and take the library's [fat-free-mass scaling](help:models/fat-free-mass). No concentration-effect relation for sedation, nausea or QTc has been established, so there is **no effect site** and no band. Every one of these studies notes that the two-hour elimination half-life does not explain droperidol's long clinical action.
