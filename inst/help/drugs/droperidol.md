### The model

Intravenous droperidol, from the intravenous branch of Cooper and colleagues' intranasal-intravenous crossover (*SAGE Open Med* 2018;6:2050312118813283) in **seven healthy men** (median 23 years, 73 kg). The intravenous dose was 0.02 mg/kg over 10 minutes. Two compartments, absolute values: clearance 15.3 L/h, V1 3.16 L, intercompartmental clearance 9.66 L/h, V2 16.9 L. The terminal half-life is 2.0 h.

### The first 25 minutes are not observed

The first intravenous sample was taken **15 minutes after the infusion ended**, so nothing was measured in the first 25 minutes after a dose began. The authors say their joint model underestimates intravenous clearance and probably **overpredicts early concentrations**: their own noncompartmental clearance was 33.8 L/h, more than twice the model's. Older data agree that the model's volumes are small: Fischler and colleagues (*Anesthesiology* 1986;64:486-489) found a clearance of 14.1 mL/min/kg (about 59 L/h at 70 kg) and a volume of 2 L/kg in anaesthetised patients. Read the early curve after a bolus as an upper bound, not a prediction.

### A separate drug from intramuscular droperidol

[Intramuscular droperidol](help:drugs/droperidolIM) runs a different study's **apparent** parameters, and neither set may be moved to the other route. The intranasal parameters from Cooper's study are not used.

### Covariates and effect

None. The values take the library's [fat-free-mass scaling](help:models/fat-free-mass). No concentration-effect relation for sedation, nausea or QTc has been established; the QTc literature reports changes by dose group, so there is **no effect site** and no band.
