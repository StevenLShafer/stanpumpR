### The model

Morphine's parameters are from Lötsch and colleagues (*Clin Pharmacol Ther* 2002;72:151-162), a population study of morphine and morphine-6-glucuronide after an intravenous bolus in eight young volunteers (four men and four women, 23 to 30 years). The library uses their Model A, a standard three-compartment model whose parameters are **absolute**, not per kilogram: volumes of 17.5, 86 and 196 L, an elimination clearance of 74 L/h (1.23 L/min), a rapid intercompartmental clearance of 134 L/h and a slow one of 19.5 L/h at a body surface area of 1.83 m². The slow clearance carries the paper's one covariate, the fourth power of body surface area: Q3 = 19.5 × (BSA/1.83)⁴ L/h, so at 2.2 m² it is 2.1 times its value at 1.83 m². The library applies it over the adult range only, 1.5 to 2.2 m² (below); until October 2026 it held the median rate constants and could not follow the covariate at all.

### Oral: tablet and liquid

Oral morphine is entered as **mg PO tablet** (immediate-release tablet), **mg PO liquid** (oral solution) or, for paediatric dosing, **mg/kg PO liquid**, each also available qd, bid, tid or qid. Both are calibrated to the studies of Atrux-Tallau and colleagues (*Clin Drug Investig* 2022;42:1101-1112), of morphine sulfate in fasted volunteers. Doses are labelled mg of morphine sulfate, as prescribed. The tablet comes from the pivotal study (39 subjects, 30 mg Sevredol tablets): peak 28.5 ng/mL at a median 45 minutes, AUC 117.4 ng·h/mL. The liquid comes from the smaller pilot crossover (17 subjects), where Oramorph solution peaked about 30% higher than the tablets (37.9 ng/mL) at the same median time, and every formulation's AUC agreed to within 10%.

The two forms share Lötsch's disposition and effect site and differ only in how fast they are absorbed. Both have a bioavailability of 0.29, set from the pivotal tablet AUC, because the pilot found the same exposure from each. Both start to be absorbed 17.6 minutes after the dose, after which the liquid is absorbed faster (half-time 37 minutes against the tablet's 53). The plasma peak is about 35 minutes after liquid and 45 minutes after a tablet, and the liquid's peak is a third higher. The liquid's faster absorption rests on the pilot study's 17 subjects. Because morphine's effect site is so slow, the difference in peak effect is much smaller than the difference in plasma peak.

Modified-release morphine is not modelled, nor is food, which slows absorption. Oral morphine forms much more morphine-6-glucuronide than intravenous morphine, because of first-pass glucuronidation, and that active metabolite is not in the model (below), so the effect of an oral dose is understated.

### Covariates

Body surface area on the slow intercompartmental clearance, as above, in both positions of the switch, as the library does for every model with a size covariate of its own, **bounded to the adult range of 1.5 to 2.2 m²**: a fourth power fitted to eight young adults is an extrapolation outside it, and in a small child it would make the slow clearance tiny (0.6 L/h at 0.8 m²) so that the deep compartment hardly filled. Below 1.5 m² the clearance therefore holds its 1.5 m² value, 8.8 L/h, and above 2.2 m² its 2.2 m² value, 40.7 L/h. The other five parameters are the published values with the switch off, and under the default [fat-free-mass scaling](help:models/fat-free-mass) the volumes scale with the patient's fat-free mass and the clearances with that ratio to the 0.75 power.

### Effect site

The time to peak effect is 93.8 minutes, the slowest in the library by far. It is the library's own value, carried from the program's opioid tables, not from Lötsch 2002, which measured no effect. On this disposition it is an equilibration half-time of about 2.3 hours, slower than the 0.6 hours Dahan and colleagues found for cold-pressor analgesia. The same group's pupil measurements (Lötsch and colleagues, *Anesthesiology* 2001;95:1329-1338) gave morphine a plasma-to-effect-site transfer half-life of 2.8 hours (range 1.8 to 4.4), and Skarke and colleagues (*Clin Pharmacol Ther* 2003;73:107-121) 2.6 hours for pain tolerance, which with these kinetics would put the peak later still. Morphine is relatively hydrophilic and crosses the blood-brain barrier slowly, so its effect-site concentration lags the plasma by well over an hour. A bolus of morphine therefore never achieves an effect-site concentration near its plasma peak, and the clinical corollary is that titrating morphine to effect every five minutes overshoots: the effect of the previous dose has barely begun.

### MEAC and typical concentrations

MEAC is 8 ng/mL (0.008 mcg/mL in the plotted units) and the shaded band 6.4 to 16 ng/mL. These are the library's values, not Lötsch's. For comparison, Dahlström and colleagues (*Clin Pharmacokinet* 1982;7:266-279) measured a mean minimum effective concentration of 16 ± 9 ng/mL in 10 patients using morphine patient-controlled analgesia after major abdominal surgery, which is the top of the band: the plasma concentration 2 minutes before each demand, by a specific gas chromatographic assay, 21 ± 11 ng/mL in men and 13 ± 6 in women, with a four-fold spread between patients; their mean plasma concentration during self-administration was 21 ± 12 ng/mL. Oxycodone's, hydrocodone's and oxymorphone's MEACs were chosen with reference to morphine's, but each is a separate value, so changing one does not move the others. The AGNP consensus guidelines' 2026 update (Hart and colleagues, *Pharmacopsychiatry* 2026;59:166-295, Table 5) gives 5 to 400 ng/mL for pain and 50 to 520 ng/mL for opioid use disorder, spreads that depend on tolerance; they are not drawn.

### Active metabolite

Morphine-6-glucuronide is an active metabolite that accumulates in renal failure. It is not in the current model. Morphine is itself now the modelled metabolite of [codeine](help:drugs/codeine): giving codeine adds a morphine row, which is where codeine's analgesia appears. A second-generation link to morphine-6-glucuronide would need a two-stage cascade, which the engine does not yet do. See [Active metabolites](help:models/metabolites).

### Where to be careful

Renal function, which governs the metabolite, is not a covariate. The model describes young healthy volunteers; the elderly and the renally impaired lie outside it. The naloxone scenario shows how morphine's slow kinetics outlast its antagonist: [Naloxone after morphine](scenario:naloxone-morphine).
