### The patch

Scopolamine is offered as the only scopolamine product the FDA approves in the US, the **Transderm Scōp** patch, labelled "1 mg/3 days": it delivers about 1 mg of scopolamine to the circulation over three days. It is entered as a delivery rate on the **transdermal (TD)** route: one patch is **0.333 mg/day TD**, entered when it is applied, and its removal is a rate of **0** (the plot's **Stop** writes it). Two patches at once would be 0.667 mg/day. For postoperative nausea and vomiting the label applies one patch behind the ear the evening before surgery and removes it 24 hours after surgery; for motion sickness, at least 4 hours before it is needed, for up to 3 days.

### How the patch is modelled

The patch delivers scopolamine into the skin at a constant rate while it is worn, and the skin releases it into the circulation first-order. The label says that after removal "there is some degree of continued systemic absorption of scopolamine bound in the skin layers" and that plasma concentrations then fall with a half-life of 9.5 hours, far longer than scopolamine's own 1.5 hours, so 9.5 hours is the half-life of the skin depot. The concentration reaches 80% of its plateau by 24 hours and keeps falling for a day or more after the patch comes off, which is when the label warns that withdrawal symptoms may appear.

### The disposition

Scopolamine's own pharmacokinetics come from Liem-Moolenaar and colleagues (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 2011;71:886-898): 90 healthy men given 0.5 mg intravenously over 15 minutes, measured by LC-MS/MS. Two compartments: clearance 2.53 L/min, central volume 66.3 L, intercompartmental clearance 4.78 L/min, steady-state volume 250 L. The paper does not name the salt of its dose; read as scopolamine base, this clearance gives the patch a plateau of 91 pg/mL, against the label's average of 87 pg/mL, which settles it.

### No band

No therapeutic range is established; 87 pg/mL is what the patch delivers on average, not a target. The curve is free scopolamine (the label's total, with conjugates, is about four times higher). Intravenous scopolamine is not offered: no intravenous product is marketed in the US.

### Where to be careful

The label warns of eclamptic seizures in severe preeclampsia, of hyperthermia, of confusion and agitation especially in the elderly, and of withdrawal symptoms a day or more after removal. The effect-site lag differs by effect (17 minutes for heart rate to over three hours for alertness), so the curve is plasma only.
