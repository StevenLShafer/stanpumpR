### The model

el-Desoky and colleagues (*Am J Ther* 2005;12:320-327) studied forty Egyptian children with heart failure, aged 4 months to 15 years, maintained on intravenous digoxin 10 mcg/kg/day, with two serum samples each. Two compartments described the data: clearance 0.38 L/h/kg at a serum creatinine of 0.6 mg/dL, falling 76% per mg/dL above it, central volume 1.38 L/kg, intercompartmental clearance 0.47 L/h/kg and peripheral volume 9.11 L/kg. The peripheral volume was fixed because three quarters of the children gave only trough samples. The paper's abstract prints the clearance equation incorrectly; this is the full text's Table 4.

### What is plotted

Serum digoxin in ng/mL, with the 0.5-2 ng/mL range shaded. Serum creatinine is the child's own, held to the studied 0.2-1.3 mg/dL; a blank field means an assumed normal value for age. No effect site is drawn: no time to peak effect was measured in children.

### Where to be careful

Two samples per child is thin, and the authors themselves ask whether it "makes sense". The adult intravenous model is [digoxinIV](help:drugs/digoxinIV). Every parameter is per kilogram, applied to the fat-free-mass weight when [the scaling switch](help:models/fat-free-mass) is on. See [the vasoactive and resuscitation drugs](help:models/vasoactive).
