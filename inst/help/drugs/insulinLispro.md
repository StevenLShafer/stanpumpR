### The model

Tham and colleagues pooled 16 pharmacokinetic and clamp studies, mostly in healthy volunteers (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2017), into one-compartment models with an absorption rate for each formulation and an apparent volume and clearance for each insulin. Their Table 2 typical values are used; its dose effects and weight effects, whose reference values and equations are in the paper's supplement, are not applied. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** of the injected insulin, the exogenous insulin alone: the endogenous baseline the paper estimated is not added. Insulin lispro U-100 (Humalog) has a volume of 43.0 L and clearance of 30.5 L/h. 72.9% of each dose is absorbed first-order at 0.989/h after a lag of 0.265 h; the other 27.1% enters the circulation at a constant rate over 1.06 hours. Where that zero-order share enters and which branch the lag delays are in the paper's supplement, which could not be read; the usual form is used, the share entering the circulation directly from the moment of injection and the lag on the depot.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC` (three times daily with meals), and by **insulin pump**, `U/hr SC`, which Humalog U-100's label permits. A pump rate runs from its row's time until the next `U/hr SC` row; its zero-order share is given as that of each 2-minute portion of the delivery. At a steady rate the concentration settles at the rate over the clearance: 1 U/hr gives 32.8 mcU/mL. Humalog U-200, which must not be used in a pump, is not offered.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn; a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics) and [subcutaneous doses](help:models/absorption).
