### The model

Sirolimus's parameters come from Dansirikul and colleagues (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 2006;62:420-434), a Bayesian population analysis of whole-blood concentrations in 25 adult kidney transplant recipients (mean 44 years, 73 kg) taking sirolimus once daily, with the two-compartment structure informed by the rich single-dose profiles of two earlier studies. Two compartments, apparent oral parameters: clearance 16.9 × 0.742^(age/44) L/h (12.5 L/h at 44 years), intercompartmental clearance 20.4 L/h, central volume 117 L, peripheral volume 583 L, absorption 2.3 per hour. The terminal half-life is 56 hours; the label gives 62 ± 16. Weight had no effect, so the values are scaled from the library's reference man; age is held at 18 below.

### Cyclosporine

Most of the source's patients took mycophenolate rather than cyclosporine. Cyclosporine slows sirolimus's clearance, and the label's exposures were measured with it: at 2 mg daily the label gives an AUC of 230 ng·h/mL and a trough of 7.6 ng/mL, against this model's 150 and 3.6. With cyclosporine the concentration is about a third higher than this model shows. Nor are the other CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein inhibitors and inducers, liver disease, sex (the label: clearance 12% lower in men), haematocrit or CYP3A5 genotype modelled.

### The band

The band is the label's trough range after cyclosporine is withdrawn, 12-20 ng/mL from the second year after transplantation (16-24 ng/mL in the first), by chromatographic assay; immunoassays read higher and are not interchangeable. With cyclosporine the label gives no number. It is a trough range, drawn against the whole curve.

### Dosing

Sirolimus is dosed as itself (Rapamune tablets 0.5, 1 and 2 mg; oral solution 1 mg/mL). The source did not say which form its patients took; the label says the tablet's bioavailability is 27% higher than the solution's and the two are not interchangeable. Because the half-life is long, steady state takes about two weeks without a loading dose.
