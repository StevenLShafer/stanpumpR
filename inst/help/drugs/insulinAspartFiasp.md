### The model

Fiasp is insulin aspart with two added excipients, niacinamide, which speeds the early absorption, and L-arginine. The model is Novo Nordisk's population model of insulin aspart, which the FDA's Clinical Pharmacology Review of Fiasp (NDA 208751, 2017) reports and agrees with: 135 adults with type 1 diabetes, each given both Fiasp and NovoLog, in two glucose-clamp studies at 0.2 U/kg and a meal study at 0.06 to 0.28 U/kg. Each injection enters a **depot**, which empties at 1.53/h into the first of **three transit compartments**; each transit empties at its transit rate into the next, and the last into one central compartment, CL/F 67.8 L/h and V/F 78.6 L. Fiasp and [NovoLog](help:drugs/insulinAspart) share everything but the transit rate, 55.8/h for Fiasp against 16.1/h for NovoLog, and a relative bioavailability (Fiasp's fixed at 1, NovoLog's 0.99): the faster transit is what moves Fiasp's curve earlier. Three compartments at one rate cannot be written in the closed forms the library uses elsewhere, so the drug is solved exactly by matrix exponential.

The clearance and the volume are proportional to body weight. **The review does not print the weight they refer to**; stanpumpR uses 75 kg, the mean weight of the 135 subjects and the weight the review's own comparisons are adjusted to. From 65 years of age the clearance is 0.905 times as large, the review's age-group term. The review's terms for sex, insulin dose, BMI and antibody binding are not applied, because their reference values are not printed. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** (1 mcU/mL = 6 pmol/L) of **total** insulin aspart, free plus antibody-bound, as the review's assay measured it, the injected insulin alone.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC` (three times daily with meals), and by **insulin pump**, `U/hr SC`, which the Fiasp label permits. A pump rate runs from its row's time until the next `U/hr SC` row and passes through the depot and the three transits, exactly. At a steady rate the concentration settles at the rate over the clearance: 1 U/hr gives 14.7 mcU/mL in a 75 kg adult. Intravenous Fiasp is approved but not offered: the model was fitted to subcutaneous doses alone.

### How it compares

After 0.2 U/kg in a 75 kg adult the model peaks at 91 mcU/mL (546 pmol/L) at 55 minutes, against 89 mcU/mL at 64 minutes for NovoLog; the review's own simulation of a typical subject peaks about 3.5% higher and 3 minutes later. The volume, 78.6 L, is about seven times insulin aspart's volume after an intravenous dose, and the model's half-life of elimination, 48 minutes, is far longer than the intravenous 7 minutes: absorption is the slowest step, and a model of subcutaneous data alone cannot tell which of its rate constants is absorption. The curve is unaffected, but the Peripheral compartments panel's Depot and the Visualizer's central volume follow the model's assignment, not the anatomy. The tail falls faster than the measured one: the label's apparent terminal half-life is about 1.1 hours.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn (the review's model has an effect compartment and a glucose-infusion-rate link, not used here); a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's.
