### The model

Tham and colleagues pooled 16 pharmacokinetic and clamp studies, mostly in healthy volunteers (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2017), into one-compartment models with an absorption rate for each formulation and an apparent volume and clearance for each insulin. Their Table 2 typical values are used; its dose effects and weight effects, whose reference values and equations are in the paper's supplement, are not applied. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** of the injected insulin, the exogenous insulin alone: the endogenous baseline the paper estimated is not added. Human regular insulin U-100 is absorbed first-order at 0.67/h with no lag, on a volume of 178 L and clearance of 127 L/h, so the absorption and elimination half-lives are both about an hour and a 10 U dose peaks at about 20 mcU/mL some 87 minutes after injection.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC`, with twice and three times daily schedules. Not by pump in the library, and not intravenous: the model is fitted to subcutaneous data on an apparent scale.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn; a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics) and [subcutaneous doses](help:models/absorption).
