### The model

From Ingwersen and colleagues (*Eur J Pharm Sci* 2000;11:247-254), 130 patients on tiagabine monotherapy, children to the elderly: one compartment, apparent oral, clearance 6.10 L/h and volume 52.0 L for a subject of 170 cm, absorption 1.25/h, half-life 5.9 hours (the paper's population half-life is 5.72 hours). The abstract prints the volume as 62.0 L, a misprint that would give a half-life of 7 hours; the paper's final model (its Table 4, Results and Discussion) gives 52 L, and the library used 62 L and said 7 hours until October 2026.

### Size and co-medication

Both clearance and volume scale with body **height** in the source, and the library now applies the paper's own terms: clearance × (height/170)^2.13 and volume × (height/170)^3.44. Height is the model's size covariate and weight does not enter, so the *Adjust weight to fat-free mass* switch does not act on tiagabine (until October 2026 the library substituted [fat-free-mass scaling](help:models/fat-free-mass) for the unretrieved height terms). This was a monotherapy population; enzyme-inducing co-medication (carbamazepine, phenytoin or phenobarbital) raises clearance about 67 per cent (Samara 1998), which is applied only when the **Include antiseizure drug interactions** box in Graph Options is ticked (see [Antiseizure drug interactions](help:models/antiseizure-interactions)). There is no effect site.

### Band

20 to 200 ng/mL, 6 hours after a dose: the AGNP consensus guidelines' 2026 update (Hart and colleagues, *Pharmacopsychiatry* 2026;59:166-295, Table 5, https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2860-7861; laboratory alert level 400 ng/mL), the same range as the ILAE's (Patsalos and colleagues, 2008). Tiagabine is 96 per cent protein bound; the range is total drug.
