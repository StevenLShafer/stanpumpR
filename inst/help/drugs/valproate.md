### The model

From Teixeira-da-Silva and colleagues (*Pharmaceutics* 2022;14:811), who fitted 1,751 routine levels from 836 patients aged 0.1 to 93 years: one compartment, total valproate, apparent clearance 0.646 L/h and volume 14 L at 70 kg, clearance scaled by weight to the power 0.75. The half-life is about 15 hours and 1,000 mg/day averages about 64 mg/L at steady state.

### Formulations and routes

One disposition, a product-specific input: the delayed-release (enteric divalproex) tablet is the default (`mg PO DR`, absorbed at 0.78/h); the syrup or capsule (`mg PO liquid`) is faster (2.64/h) and the extended-release tablet (`mg PO ER`) slower (0.38/h) and 0.89 as available, the basis for giving 8 to 20 per cent more drug on conversion. The injection (`mg`, `mg/hr`) is interchangeable with the oral products mg for mg; all doses are in valproic acid equivalents.

### What is not modelled

The published age term is omitted until its equation can be read (the paper's own worked examples do not reproduce the exponent the handoff gave). Co-medication is not applied, so the curve is valproate **without** phenytoin, phenobarbital or carbamazepine, each of which raises clearance by 40 to 65 per cent. Valproate's binding to albumin saturates in the therapeutic range, so the unbound concentration rises faster than the total; only total valproate is plotted.

### Band

50 to 100 mg/L total (ILAE; Patsalos and colleagues, 2008).
