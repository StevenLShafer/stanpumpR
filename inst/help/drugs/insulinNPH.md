### The model

Tham and colleagues pooled 16 pharmacokinetic and clamp studies, mostly in healthy volunteers (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2017), into one-compartment models with an absorption rate for each formulation and an apparent volume and clearance for each insulin. Their Table 2 typical values are used. Its dose and weight effects (powers of the dose and of body weight) are not applied: their reference dose and weight were in the supplement's equations, which are missing from its published PDF. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** of the injected insulin, the exogenous insulin alone: the endogenous baseline the paper estimated is not added. Human NPH (isophane) insulin is absorbed first-order at 0.129/h after a lag of 0.378 h, on the human-insulin volume and clearance (178 L, 127 L/h): a 10 U dose peaks at about 7 mcU/mL some three hours after injection.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC`, once or twice daily.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn; a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics) and [subcutaneous doses](help:models/absorption).
