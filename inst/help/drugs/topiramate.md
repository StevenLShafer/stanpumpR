### The model

From Bamgboye and colleagues (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2026), who gave 20 adults a stable-isotope-labelled intravenous microdose alongside their oral topiramate: three compartments, linear, allometric on weight (the pharmacokinetic weight under the default [fat-free-mass scaling](help:models/fat-free-mass), total body weight with the switch off), with a clearance of 1.31 L/h, a central volume of 9.84 L and steady-state volume near 58 L at 70 kg. The terminal half-life is about 30 hours.

### Route

Oral (`mg PO`, with schedules). Absolute oral bioavailability is complete (109 per cent against intravenous, Clark 2013; about 100 per cent, Ahmed 2015), so the apparent and true parameters are the same and bioavailability is 1 — the label's figure of 80 per cent is contradicted by both intravenous studies. No absorption rate was estimated; the 1-hour median time to peak sets a label-anchored 2.0/h, recorded as a calibration. The extended-release capsules have no published input model and are not offered, nor is the investigational intravenous product.

### What is not modelled

Enzyme-inducing co-medication (which raises clearance about 1.6-fold), and the empirical dependence of apparent clearance on daily dose that an earlier oral model carried, which a linear engine cannot hold.

### Pregnancy

With **Pregnant** ticked, the elimination clearance is multiplied by 1.6; the volumes, the distribution clearances and the absorption are unchanged. In the third trimester the dose/concentration ratio was 67.4 against 38.8 L/day outside pregnancy in the same women (Ohman and colleagues, *Epilepsy Res* 2009;87:124-129; 1.74-fold), and the concentration/dose ratio 34 per cent lower (Westin and colleagues, *Epilepsia* 2009;50:480-485; 1.52-fold); 1.6 is their geometric mean. Topiramate is little bound, so total and unbound change alike. See [Pregnancy](help:models/pregnancy).

### Band

5 to 20 mg/L (ILAE; Patsalos and colleagues, 2008).
