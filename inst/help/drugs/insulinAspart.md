### The model

The model is Novo Nordisk's population model of insulin aspart, which the FDA's Clinical Pharmacology Review of Fiasp (NDA 208751, 2017) reports and agrees with: 135 adults with type 1 diabetes, each given both NovoLog and Fiasp, in two glucose-clamp studies at 0.2 U/kg and a meal study at 0.06 to 0.28 U/kg. Each injection enters a **depot**, which empties at 1.53/h into the first of **three transit compartments**; each transit empties at its transit rate into the next, and the last into one central compartment, CL/F 67.8 L/h and V/F 78.6 L. NovoLog and Fiasp share everything but the transit rate, 16.1/h for NovoLog against 55.8/h for Fiasp, and a relative bioavailability, 0.99 for NovoLog. Three compartments at one rate cannot be written in the closed forms the library uses elsewhere, so the drug is solved exactly by matrix exponential, on the engine built for [enalapril](help:drugs/enalapril)'s transit chain.

The review's clearance and volume are proportional to body weight, but **the review does not print the weight they refer to**. stanpumpR takes them as describing a 75 kg adult: 75 kg is the mean weight of the 135 subjects, and the review's own comparisons are adjusted to it. The review's own simulation of a typical subject supports this reading (see below). stanpumpR's models all start from a 70 kg reference man, so the review's values are moved from 75 to 70 kg by the review's own proportional weight term, giving a clearance of 63.3 L/h and a volume of 73.4 L at 70 kg (Steven L. Shafer, 2026-11-18). From there they are scaled to the patient like every other model in the library ([body size scaling](help:models/fat-free-mass)). With the switch on, the volume scales by fat-free mass and the clearance by its 0.75 power. With it off, both are proportional to total weight, which reproduces the review exactly. From 65 years of age the clearance is 0.905 times as large, the review's age-group term. The review's terms for sex, insulin dose, BMI and antibody binding are not applied, because their reference values are not printed. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** (1 mcU/mL = 6 pmol/L) of **total** insulin aspart, free plus antibody-bound, as the review's assay measured it, the injected insulin alone.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC` (three times daily with meals), and by **insulin pump**, `U/hr SC`, which the NovoLog label permits. A pump rate runs from its row's time until the next `U/hr SC` row and passes through the depot and the three transits, exactly. At a steady rate the concentration settles at the rate times 0.99 over the clearance: 1 U/hr gives 14.6 mcU/mL in a 75 kg adult. Intravenous NovoLog is approved but not offered: the model was fitted to subcutaneous doses alone.

### How it compares

The volume, 78.6 L at 75 kg, is about seven times insulin aspart's volume after an intravenous dose, and the model's half-life of elimination, 48 minutes, is far longer than the intravenous 7 minutes: absorption is the slowest step, and a model of subcutaneous data alone cannot tell which of its rate constants is absorption. The curve is unaffected, but the Peripheral compartments panel's Depot and the Visualizer's central volume follow the model's assignment, not the anatomy. The tail falls faster than the measured one: the review measured a terminal half-life of 68 minutes in the same studies, and the label says 81 minutes in healthy men. The NovoLog label's 82 mU/L after 0.15 U/kg in type 1 diabetes (free insulin, an older study the model was not fitted to) compares with 67 mcU/mL of total insulin aspart here, at 64 minutes against the label's 40 to 50.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn (the review's model has an effect compartment and a glucose-infusion-rate link, not used here); a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics) and [subcutaneous doses](help:models/absorption).
