### The model

Caffeine's parameters in the preterm neonate come from Lee and colleagues (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 2002;54:31-37): 18 preterm infants with apnoea in Singapore (24 to 33 weeks' gestation, 676 to 1700 g at birth), given intravenous caffeine citrate, 20 mg/kg (10 mg/kg of caffeine base) over 30 minutes and then 5 mg/kg (2.5 mg/kg base) a day, sampled for up to three weeks. One compartment, with weight the only covariate (gestational age, sex and ethnic group were not):

- clearance = 0.004248 × weight + 0.00154 L/h
- volume = 0.6299 × weight + 0.259 L

At 1.1 kg that is a clearance of 6.2 mL/h, a volume of 0.95 L (0.87 L/kg) and a half-life of 106 hours; the model predicted a separate group of ten neonates without bias. The label's 0.8-0.9 L/kg and half-life of 3-4 days agree.

### Who it describes

Preterm neonates in the first weeks of treatment, from about 0.7 to 1.8 kg. Caffeine clearance rises steeply over the first months as the liver's CYP1A2 matures (by 9 months it approximates the adult's, the label says), and postnatal age was not available in this study, so the model reads neither age nor gestational age, and in an older infant, a child or an adult it is far too slow. It is named for its population so that it cannot be mistaken for an adult model.

### Dose

Caffeine is labelled and dosed as **caffeine citrate**, which is half caffeine base: "20 mg of caffeine citrate is equivalent to 10 mg of caffeine base" (Cafcit label). Enter the dose as caffeine citrate (**mg/kg citrate**, and **mg/kg citrate qd** for the daily maintenance dose); the curve is caffeine. Only the intravenous route is offered: the label says the absolute bioavailability "was not fully examined in preterm neonates".

### The band

No therapeutic range is established: "A therapeutic plasma concentration range of caffeine could not be determined from the placebo-controlled clinical trial" (label), in which levels ranged from 8 to 40 mg/L. The band is the **conventional window of 5 to 20 mg/L** that Lee and colleagues quote and their infants stayed within. "Serious toxicity has been reported in the literature when serum caffeine levels exceed 50 mg/L" (label).
