### The model

Pramipexole's model is Boehringer Ingelheim's population analysis of early Parkinson's disease (study 248.524, with healthy-volunteer and renal impairment studies), as reported and re-run by the FDA in its clinical pharmacology review of extended-release pramipexole (NDA 22-421). Two compartments: apparent clearance 29.2 L/h, central volume 35.6 L, intercompartmental clearance 115 L/h and peripheral volume 313 L at 75 kg. The immediate-release tablet is absorbed first order at 0.517 per hour after a lag of 0.221 hours. After a single tablet the model peaks at about 0.9 hours, earlier than the label's 2 hours, because its small central volume and fast distribution were fitted mostly to sparse steady-state samples; the daily exposure, which the model was built to predict, matches healthy-volunteer studies. The terminal half-life is about 10 hours with normal renal function. The model reproduces the FDA reviewers' own simulated exposures to within 0.3%.

### Doses as labelled

US tablets are labelled in mg of **pramipexole dihydrochloride monohydrate**, and the concentration is of the base (0.125 mg of the salt is 0.088 mg of base, the strength the European label states). Enter doses as on the US label; the model divides by 1.431.

### Renal function and size

About 90% of a dose is excreted unchanged in the urine, and clearance falls 0.74% for each mL/min that the Cockcroft-Gault creatinine clearance is below 121 mL/min, from the Patient Profile's **Serum creatinine** (an assumed normal creatinine for age and sex when blank). The review does not say which estimate of creatinine clearance it used; Cockcroft-Gault is assumed. Weight raises the peripheral volume 2.26% per kg from 75 kg. Sex, age and race had no further effect.

### What is not plotted

No effect site or band: the trials found no relation between concentration and response, the effect builds over days of titration, and no therapeutic range is published. Cimetidine (AUC +50%) and other cationic drugs secreted by the kidney are not applied. See [Parkinson disease drugs](help:models/parkinsons).
