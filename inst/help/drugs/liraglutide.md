### The model

Røge and colleagues' integrated analysis of 29 adults with type 2 diabetes (*CPT Pharmacometrics Syst Pharmacol* 2015): one compartment with first-order absorption, apparent volume 0.112 L/kg, clearance 1.83 × 10<sup>−4</sup> L/min/kg (0.77 L/h at 70 kg) and absorption 9.41 × 10<sup>−4</sup>/min. Absorption is slower than elimination (an absorption half-time of 12 hours against an elimination half-life of 7), so the curve falls with the absorption half-time, close to the label's 13 hours, and peaks about 13 hours after a dose. Clearance and volume are per kilogram, at the pharmacokinetic weight.

### Routes

Subcutaneous, `mg SC` and once daily `mg SC qd`. Watson and colleagues' pooled model (2010), with a sequential zero- and first-order absorption, is held in the registry until its parameters are transcribed.

### Renal impairment

None is modelled, and none is seen. Scheen's review of incretin-based therapies in chronic kidney disease (*Clin Pharmacokinet* 2015) gives an apparent clearance of 0.79 L/h after 0.75 mg with normal renal function (the model at 70 kg: 0.77 L/h), with no trend from mild impairment to dialysis.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only, in ng/mL (1 nmol/L = 3.75 ng/mL). The same paper's meal-test model of glucose, insulin and gastric emptying is an endpoint of its own and is not drawn. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics).
