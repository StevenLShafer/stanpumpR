Isoflurane is entered as a **vaporizer setting** in per cent of one atmosphere. With a blood:gas partition coefficient of 1.3 it is the most soluble volatile agent in the library: its alveolar concentration lags the inspired concentration more than sevoflurane's or desflurane's, and it washes out more slowly, more so after long anesthetics because of its high tissue solubility (fat:gas 70). Its MAC of 1.1 per cent at age 40 is the lowest of the three volatile agents; a given vaporizer setting therefore delivers more MAC equivalents of isoflurane than of sevoflurane.

The engine's structure and parameters are Gas Man®'s; see [The inhaled-gas engine](help:models/gas-engine). To compare agents, load [emergence from sevoflurane](scenario:emergence-sevoflurane) and replace the sevoflurane rows with isoflurane at about 1.1 per cent.

Not modelled: isoflurane's coronary vasodilation and other hemodynamic effects, and its metabolism (about 0.2 per cent).
