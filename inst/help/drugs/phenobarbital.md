### The model

From a 2025 study of 37 critically ill adults in refractory status epilepticus (*Epilepsia*, doi:10.1111/epi.18517), given intravenous and enteral phenobarbital: one compartment, bioavailability 0.96, absorption fixed at 1.9/h, clearance 0.38 L/h and volume 34.3 L allometric on ideal body weight (median 68.8 kg). The half-life is about 63 hours.

### Routes and size

Oral (`mg PO`, with daily schedules) and intravenous (`mg`, `mg/kg`, `mg/hr`). Ideal body weight is Devine's; in children and short adults it is meaningless, so below 18 years, or wherever the ideal weight is less than half the patient's weight, the model uses the [pharmacokinetic weight](help:models/fat-free-mass) instead, which is an **extrapolation** of an adult critically-ill model. With the fat-free-mass switch off, total body weight is used.

### What is not modelled

Co-medication (valproate inhibits, rifampicin induces), phenobarbital derived from primidone, renal replacement, and urinary alkalinisation. The critically ill clear phenobarbital faster than healthy adults (80 to 100 hour half-life classically), so outpatient accumulation may be underpredicted.

### Band

10 to 30 mg/L, 12 hours after a dose, with 20 as the typical value (its midpoint, not a target): the phenobarbital range of the "Primidone and phenobarbital" row of the AGNP consensus guidelines' 2026 update (Hart and colleagues, *Pharmacopsychiatry* 2026;59:166-295, Table 5, https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2860-7861; laboratory alert level 50 mg/L), which takes it from the product information. Phenobarbital has no row of its own in that table. It replaced the ILAE's 10 to 40 mg/L (Patsalos and colleagues, 2008) in October 2026.
