### The model

Tham and colleagues pooled 16 pharmacokinetic and clamp studies, mostly in healthy volunteers (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2017), into one-compartment models with an absorption rate for each formulation and an apparent volume and clearance for each insulin. Table 2's values are used with the covariates of the supplement's equations. The volume grows linearly with weight, 768 L × (1 + 0.00728 (weight − 71.4)). The bioavailability falls as the injection grows, (dose/35 U)^-0.300, applied to each injection by its own size, so 20 U delivers 18% more per unit than 35 U. Weight and dose are held within the studied 42.8 to 110 kg and 14 to 58 U. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** of the injected insulin, the exogenous insulin alone: the endogenous baseline the paper estimated is not added. Insulin glargine U-100 was fitted to Basaglar in 209 healthy volunteers, on an immunoreactive assay with a C-peptide correction: volume 768 L, clearance 74.5 L/h. 82.2% of each dose is absorbed first-order at 0.0830/h, so slowly that the curve is nearly flat over a day; the other 17.8% enters the circulation at a constant rate over 0.612 hours, straight into the circulation from the moment of injection, as the paper's Figure 1 draws it. The curve is immunoreactive glargine, not the active metabolite M1 that most of a dose becomes.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC`, once daily. The fit is to Basaglar; Lantus was an external check only, and Semglee, Rezvoglar and glargine U-300 (Toujeo), whose depot behaves differently, are not this drug. Glargine is not a pump insulin.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn; a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics) and [subcutaneous doses](help:models/absorption).
