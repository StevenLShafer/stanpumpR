### The model

Tham and colleagues pooled 16 pharmacokinetic and clamp studies, mostly in healthy volunteers (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2017), into one-compartment models with an absorption rate for each formulation and an apparent volume and clearance for each insulin. Their Table 2 typical values are used. Its dose and weight effects (powers of the dose and of body weight) are not applied: their reference dose and weight were in the supplement's equations, which are missing from its published PDF. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** of the injected insulin, the exogenous insulin alone: the endogenous baseline the paper estimated is not added. Insulin glargine U-100 was fitted to Basaglar in 209 healthy volunteers, on an immunoreactive assay with a C-peptide correction: volume 768 L, clearance 74.5 L/h. 82.2% of each dose is absorbed first-order at 0.0830/h, so slowly that the curve is nearly flat over a day; the other 17.8% enters the circulation at a constant rate over 0.612 hours, straight into the circulation from the moment of injection, as the paper's Figure 1 draws it. The curve is immunoreactive glargine, not the active metabolite M1 that most of a dose becomes.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC`, once daily. The fit is to Basaglar; Lantus was an external check only, and Semglee, Rezvoglar and glargine U-300 (Toujeo), whose depot behaves differently, are not this drug. Glargine is not a pump insulin.

### What is not plotted

Concentration only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn; a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics) and [subcutaneous doses](help:models/absorption).
