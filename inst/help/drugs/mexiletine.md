### The model

Mexiletine's parameters come from Vozeh and colleagues (*Eur J Clin Pharmacol* 1982;23:445-451), a population analysis of 452 serum concentrations in 58 patients with arrhythmias (mean 66 years; 27 with heart failure) taking oral mexiletine. One compartment, apparent oral parameters proportional to body weight: clearance 0.38 L/h/kg, volume 5.3 L/kg, absorption 3.1 per hour after a 0.3-hour lag. At 70 kg the half-life is 9.7 hours. Heart failure, liver disease and sex did not change the fit.

### Against the paper and an intravenous study

The paper's own predictions are reproduced: after a 500 mg loading dose, 1.23 mg/L at 1.5 hours and 0.91 at 6 hours (the paper: 1.25 and 0.95), and an average of 1.17 mg/L on 250 mg every 8 hours (about 1.2). Young healthy men given mexiletine intravenously (Campbell 1978) cleared it about 45% faster than these older patients with heart disease.

### Dosing

Mexiletine is dosed as the hydrochloride, as the US capsules (150, 200 and 250 mg mexiletine hydrochloride) are labelled; the paper gives its doses without naming the salt, and mexiletine has only been marketed as the hydrochloride.

### The band

The band is the paper's therapeutic range, 0.8-2 mg/L of total mexiletine in serum (Vozeh and colleagues 1982, doi 10.1007/BF00605996, quoting its references 2, 5 and 10; no sampling time is specified); central nervous system effects (tremor, dizziness, confusion) become more frequent above 2 mg/L.

### Where to be careful

CYP2D6 (poor metabolisers clear about half as much), CYP1A2 inhibitors (fluvoxamine) and smoking, urine pH (acid urine speeds renal clearance), liver disease, and the slower absorption after a myocardial infarction are not modelled.
