### The model

McCune and colleagues (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2009;49:88-102) studied 22 children (1.3 to 9.4 years, 8.7 to 52.8 kg) with neuroblastoma, given cyclophosphamide 400 mg/m² over 30 minutes daily for 5 days, each dose followed by topotecan. One compartment of 13.1 L/m², cleared by two routes: one that does not change (1.83 L/h/m²) and one carried by an enzyme the drug induces (1.79 L/h/m² before the first dose).

### Autoinduction

Cyclophosphamide raises the formation of the enzyme that activates it, up to six times its baseline, slowly (half-life about 105 hours). So over a 5-day course the clearance rises and later doses give lower concentrations. For a median child (14.2 kg, 95 cm) the model gives an area of 105 mg·h/L after the first dose and 70 after the fourth, where the paper's medians are 112 and 82: the typical child of the model induces a little more than the median child of the study. The enzyme is carried through the whole dose table.

### What is plotted

Plasma cyclophosphamide, in mcg/mL, scaled by body-surface area (Mosteller). The active metabolite 4-hydroxycyclophosphamide and carboxyethylphosphoramide mustard, which the paper also modelled, are not carried: their rates depend on a measured GFR, which the Patient Profile does not have.

### Where to be careful

- **Children only.** The program uses this model for children; the adult model is Joerger 2007, with no induction.
- **Twenty-two children**, all given topotecan with each dose.
