### The model

Hallik and colleagues (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 2020;86:318-328) studied 28 critically ill neonates in two Estonian intensive care units on their first days of life: gestational age 30.4 weeks (median; 22.7 to 41), birth weight 1618 g (465 to 4380), recruited at 6 hours of age and given dobutamine at 5 mcg/kg/min, rising by 5 every half hour to at most 20, for a median of three and a half days. One compartment with first-order elimination described 119 arterial plasma concentrations; saturable elimination did not improve the fit. Clearance is 41.2 L/h at the median birth weight of 1.618 kg, scaled by birth weight to the 0.75 power and multiplied by a maturation function of postmenstrual age (half-mature at 37.4 weeks, Hill coefficient 2.67); the volume is 5.29 L at 1.618 kg, in proportion to birth weight (3.27 L/kg, more than in older children or adults, as befits the large body water of the first days of life). For the study's median infant that is 155 mL/min/kg, and 10 mcg/kg/min reaches a steady state of about 65 mcg/L.

### What is plotted

Plasma dobutamine in ng/mL (mcg/L), and an effect site for **heart rate**: its equilibration rate constant, 6.59 /h (a mean effect time of 9 minutes), comes from the paper's heart-rate model, the only one of its six haemodynamic responses that lagged the plasma. Mean arterial pressure and cerebral oxygen extraction followed the plasma with no delay, and cardiac output and ejection fraction were fitted to it directly. The time to peak effect after a bolus depends on each infant's clearance; it is about 13 minutes for the median infant. No heart rate, pressure or cardiac output is drawn, and there is no shaded band: dobutamine has no therapeutic range, and the paper's concentrations of half-maximal effect (25 mcg/L for arterial pressure, 39 for heart rate, 117 for left ventricular output) describe different endpoints in this population, not a target. Dobutamine is offered as an infusion only, in mcg/kg/min or mcg/min.

### Covariates

**Birth weight** is the Weight field: in the first days of life it is the weight the model was fitted on; later, enter the birth weight rather than today's. **Postmenstrual age** is the **Gestational age at birth** field plus the age. Left blank, the birth is taken as at term (40 weeks), which overstates the clearance of a premature infant and so draws the concentration too low: on the first day of life, an infant born at 30 weeks clears dobutamine at two thirds of the rate of one born at term (maturation 0.36 against 0.55). Both are applied to the fat-free-mass weight when [the scaling switch](help:models/fat-free-mass) is on and to total body weight when it is off; off reproduces the published model.

### Where to be careful

This is a model of **neonates in the postnatal transitional period**, and of nothing else: there is no adult or older child's dobutamine model in the library. The study covered 22.7 to 41 weeks of gestation, 0.47 to 4.4 kg and the first days of life; the volume falls over the first week as body water is lost, which the model does not carry. Residual variability was large (58%), which the authors attribute mostly to the accuracy of neonatal infusions at low rates through long dead spaces, and between-infant variability in clearance was 29% after the covariates. Concomitant dopamine, antenatal steroids, haemoglobin, albumin and ductal size were tested and did not improve the fit. See [the vasoactive and resuscitation drugs](help:models/vasoactive).
