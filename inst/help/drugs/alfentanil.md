### The model

Alfentanil's parameters are those of Scott and Stanski (*J Pharmacol Exp Ther* 1987;240:159-166), who gave fentanyl and alfentanil by infusion to surgical patients aged 20 to 89 and modelled the plasma concentrations and the EEG. The volumes are 2.19, 6.70 and 14.5 L and the clearances 0.199, 1.43 and 0.247 L/min.

### Covariates

None. The parameters are for a typical adult, and the same dose in milligrams gives the same curve at any weight or age. Scott and Stanski found that age did not change alfentanil's kinetics but did increase the brain's sensitivity to it; the pharmacodynamic finding is not represented. Dose per kilogram if you want size taken into account.

### Effect site

The time to peak effect is 1.4 minutes, from the Shafer and Varvel analysis: alfentanil equilibrates with the brain almost as fast as remifentanil, which is why it was the opioid of choice for brief, intense stimuli before remifentanil existed.

### MEAC and typical concentrations

MEAC is 39 ng/mL; the shaded band is 31 to 78 ng/mL. Concentrations of 200 to 400 ng/mL are typical during surgery with an inhaled agent, and loss of response to intubation with propofol or an inhaled agent needs roughly that.

### Where to be careful

Alfentanil's small volumes and low clearance give it a terminal half-life of about 1.5 hours and an offset that, unlike remifentanil's, lengthens with infusion duration, though much less than fentanyl's. Its elimination depends on CYP3A4, so inhibitors (erythromycin, some antifungals) can prolong it substantially; the model does not know this.
