Desflurane is entered as a **vaporizer setting** in per cent of one atmosphere. Its blood:gas partition coefficient of 0.57 is the lowest of the volatile agents and its tissue solubilities are low (fat:gas 13), so its alveolar concentration tracks the inspired concentration closely and emergence is fast even after long anesthetics. Its MAC of 6.6 per cent at age 40 (Mapleson's 1996 meta-analysis; GasMan and the US label give 6.0) is the highest of the volatile agents, so vaporizer settings of 5 to 8 per cent are typical.

The engine's structure and parameters are GasMan®'s, except the blood:gas coefficient, which is Esper's (2015), and MAC, which is Mapleson's (1996); see [The inhaled-gas engine](help:models/gas-engine). Scenarios: [emergence from desflurane](scenario:emergence-desflurane), for comparison with [emergence from sevoflurane](scenario:emergence-sevoflurane).

Not modelled: the sympathetic activation with rapid increases in desflurane concentration, airway irritation, and the heated pressurised vaporizer it requires; the model treats the vaporizer setting as delivered.
