### The model

Tham and colleagues pooled 16 pharmacokinetic and clamp studies, mostly in healthy volunteers (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2017), into one-compartment models with an absorption rate for each formulation and an apparent volume and clearance for each insulin. Table 2's values are used with the covariates of the supplement's equations. The volume grows with body weight, 178 L × (weight/80)^1.62. The clearance falls as the injection grows, 127 L/h × (dose/10 U)^-0.282, and regular U-100's absorption slows likewise, 0.67/h × (dose/10 U)^-0.275. Each injection is simulated with the parameters for its own size and the injections are added, as the model was fitted to single doses. Weight and dose are held within the studied 53.1 to 124 kg and 3 to 100 U. Tham tested weight on clearance and did not retain it. With *Adjust weight to fat-free mass* ticked, stanpumpR scales the clearance to the 0.75 power of fat-free mass as well; the paper gives no split of men and women from which to find its subjects' typical patient, so the scaling is anchored at the 70 kg, 170 cm reference man, who receives the published clearance. Unticked, the clearance is as published. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** of the injected insulin, the exogenous insulin alone: the endogenous baseline the paper estimated is not added. Human regular insulin U-100 is absorbed first-order at 0.67/h with no lag, on a volume of 178 L and clearance of 127 L/h, at the 10 U reference dose and 80 kg, so the absorption and elimination half-lives are both about an hour and a 10 U dose peaks at about 20 mcU/mL some 87 minutes after injection. In pregnancy, enter the pre-pregnancy weight: a term-pregnant weight enlarges this volume by weight alone, and with the switch ticked raises the clearance through fat-free mass, with no pregnancy data behind either.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC`, with twice and three times daily schedules. Not by pump in the library, and not intravenous: the model is fitted to subcutaneous data on an apparent scale.

### Effect site

Every insulin shares one effect site, with an equilibration rate constant (ke0) of 0.876 per hour, so that the effect lags the plasma insulin by the time insulin takes to act, and each product's own absorption sets when its effect peaks. The constant reproduces the 23-minute peak of the glucose infusion rate after an intravenous bolus of regular insulin (Howey 1994; Galloway 1992's study, 20 minutes) and puts the effect peaks of subcutaneous lispro, regular insulin and NPH within an hour of the clamp studies' (2.2, 2.5 and 5.0 hours against about 1.7 to 1.9, 2.9 to 3.2 and 5.4 to 5.9: lispro 0.3 to 0.6 hours late, regular insulin 0.3 to 0.7 and NPH 0.4 to 0.9 hours early, differences that follow each product's absorption model), and it agrees with the FDA's analysis of 72 clamps after subcutaneous insulin aspart, 0.996 per hour (95% CI 0.88 to 1.11). How it was derived is on the page [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics).

### What is not plotted

The concentration and its effect site only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn; a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's. See [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics) and [subcutaneous doses](help:models/absorption).
