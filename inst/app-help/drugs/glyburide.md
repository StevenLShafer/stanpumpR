### The model

Rambiritch and colleagues' analysis of 24 poorly controlled South African adults with type 2 diabetes at steady state on 2.5 to 20 mg a day (*Clin Pharmacol Adv Appl* 2016): two compartments with first-order absorption at 0.53/h, no lag, apparent clearance 2.16 L/h, central volume 11.7 L, intercompartmental clearance 3.84 L/h and peripheral volume 68.1 L. No covariate analysis was done, so the parameters are scaled by body size alone. The authors note that their peak concentrations were about twice those of other studies, and the two-compartment model's terminal half-life (about 37 hours) is much longer than the 4 to 8 hours of their noncompartmental analysis, so the curve's tail is uncertain.

### Effect site

The effect site is glyburide's own, from Rydberg and colleagues' study of eight healthy volunteers given 3.5 mg intravenously and by mouth, with the effect measured as the fall in blood glucose against placebo (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 1997): an equilibration rate constant (ke0) of 1.59 per hour. On that study's own intravenous disposition the effect peaks 38 minutes after a bolus. The constant is used as published rather than turned into a time to peak effect on this model's disposition, because Rambiritch's model, fitted to oral data, falls far more slowly than Rydberg's measured intravenous curve, and a 38-minute peak solved on it would need a ke0 of 3.7 per hour. Rambiritch's own pharmacodynamic analysis found no direct link between concentration and effect.

### What is not plotted

The parent drug's concentration, in ng/mL, and its effect site. The active hydroxy metabolites, which carry part of the glucose lowering (more potent at low concentrations and slower to reach their effect sites) and accumulate in renal failure, are not modelled, so the glucose lowering outlasts the parent's effect site.
