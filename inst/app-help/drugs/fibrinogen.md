### The model

Bellon and colleagues' population analysis (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 2020) of 31 patients with congenital afibrinogenaemia, 18 men and 13 women aged 1.5 to 48.7 years and weighing 10.8 to 93.5 kg, each given a single 60 mg/kg of LFB's fibrinogen concentrate (Clottafact), with Clauss fibrinogen followed for up to 14 days. One compartment:

- clearance 0.00288 × weight^0.556 L/h;
- volume 0.0960 × weight^0.808 L.

At 70 kg the clearance is 0.44 mL/h/kg, the half-life 67 hours, and 1 mg/kg raises the fibrinogen 2.35 mg/dL; at 20 kg, 0.76 mL/h/kg, 49 hours and 1.85. RiaSTAP's label (14 patients aged 8 to 61): clearance 0.59 ± 0.13 mL/h/kg, half-life 79 hours, 1.7 mg/dL per mg/kg, which is the label's dosing rule. Children clear fibrinogen faster per kilogram, as both sources found.

### One drug for the concentrates

Clottafact is not sold in the US; RiaSTAP, Fibryga and Fesilty are. Infused fibrinogen is the patient's own protein, cleared the same way whichever plasma it came from, so this model stands for the class, and RiaSTAP's label for the US products.

### What is plotted

Plasma fibrinogen in **mg/dL**: the patient's own plus the fibrinogen infused. The patient's own is the Patient Profile's **Fibrinogen baseline**, shown while fibrinogen is in the dose table. Left blank, it is the source population's, afibrinogenaemia, 0 mg/dL. For a bleeding surgical or obstetric patient the measured fibrinogen should be entered: a normal adult's is 200 to 450 mg/dL, and a pregnant woman's at term 400 to 600.

The shaded band runs from 100 mg/dL, RiaSTAP's target level maintained until haemostasis in congenital deficiency, to 450 mg/dL, the top of normal; its typical line is 200 mg/dL, the bottom of normal. Guidelines for massive haemorrhage target higher levels (150 to 200 mg/dL), which are not drawn.

### Covariates and doses

- **Weight**, on clearance and volume, as fitted; with the fat-free-mass switch on, the pharmacokinetic weight.
- **Pregnancy**: no pharmacokinetic model in pregnancy has been published, and nothing is applied. In haemorrhage at caesarean delivery with hyperfibrinolysis, infused fibrinogen rose and then fell early despite larger doses (Hureau 2026), a loss this model does not show.

`mg/kg`, `g` or `mg`, as a bolus.

### Not modelled

The patient's own fibrinogen is held constant: its consumption in bleeding, its dilution by fluids and its acute-phase rise after surgery are not drawn, and neither is the loss of infused fibrinogen to fibrinolysis.
