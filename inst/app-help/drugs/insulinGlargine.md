### The model

Tham and colleagues pooled 16 pharmacokinetic and clamp studies, mostly in healthy volunteers (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2017), into one-compartment models with an absorption rate for each formulation and an apparent volume and clearance for each insulin. Table 2's values are used with the covariates of the supplement's equations. The volume grows linearly with weight, 768 L × (1 + 0.00728 (weight − 71.4)). The bioavailability falls as the injection grows, (dose/35 U)^-0.300, applied to each injection by its own size, so 20 U delivers 18% more per unit than 35 U. Weight and dose are held within the studied 42.8 to 110 kg and 14 to 58 U. Tham tested weight on clearance and did not retain it. With *Adjust weight to fat-free mass* ticked, stanpumpR scales the clearance to the 0.75 power of fat-free mass as well; the paper gives no split of men and women from which to find its subjects' typical patient, so the scaling is anchored at the 70 kg, 170 cm reference man, who receives the published clearance. Unticked, the clearance is as published. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** of the injected insulin, the exogenous insulin alone: the endogenous baseline the paper estimated is not added. Insulin glargine U-100 was fitted to Basaglar in 209 healthy volunteers, on an immunoreactive assay with a C-peptide correction: volume 768 L, clearance 74.5 L/h. 82.2% of each dose is absorbed first-order at 0.0830/h, so slowly that the curve is nearly flat over a day; the other 17.8% enters the circulation at a constant rate over 0.612 hours, straight into the circulation from the moment of injection, as the paper's Figure 1 draws it. The curve is immunoreactive glargine, not the active metabolite M1 that most of a dose becomes. In pregnancy, enter the pre-pregnancy weight: a term-pregnant weight enlarges this volume by weight alone, and with the switch ticked raises the clearance through fat-free mass, with no pregnancy data behind either.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC`, once daily. The fit is to Basaglar; Lantus was an external check only, and Semglee, Rezvoglar and glargine U-300 (Toujeo), whose depot behaves differently, are not this drug. Glargine is not a pump insulin.

### Effect site

Every insulin shares one effect site, with an equilibration rate constant (ke0) of 0.876 per hour, so that the effect lags the plasma insulin by the time insulin takes to act, and each product's own absorption sets when its effect peaks. The constant reproduces the 23-minute peak of the glucose infusion rate after an intravenous bolus of regular insulin (Howey 1994; Galloway 1992's study, 20 minutes) and puts the effect peaks of subcutaneous lispro, regular insulin and NPH within an hour of the clamp studies' (2.2, 2.5 and 5.0 hours against about 1.7 to 1.9, 2.9 to 3.2 and 5.4 to 5.9: lispro 0.3 to 0.6 hours late, regular insulin 0.3 to 0.7 and NPH 0.4 to 0.9 hours early, differences that follow each product's absorption model), and it agrees with the FDA's analysis of 72 clamps after subcutaneous insulin aspart, 0.996 per hour (95% CI 0.88 to 1.11). How it was derived is on the page [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics). For glargine the effect site follows immunoreactive glargine, which stands in for the active metabolite M1 the plasma curve does not separate, so its timing is an approximation of when M1 acts, not a measurement of it.

### What is not plotted

The concentration and its effect site only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn; a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's.
