### The model

Rocuronium's parameters are from Kleijn and colleagues (*Br J Clin Pharmacol* 2011;72:415-433), who fitted rocuronium, sugammadex and their complex together in 446 subjects aged 1 to 91 years from eight trials. The library's [sugammadex](help:drugs/sugammadex) comes from the same fit, so the two drugs now describe the same patients. This row is Kleijn's rocuronium **alone**: a **two-compartment** model of total rocuronium in plasma. At the source's reference (70 kg, 43 years, creatinine clearance 119 mL/min) clearance is 0.269 L/min, central volume 4.73 L, intercompartmental clearance 0.279 L/min and peripheral volume 6.76 L.

Until November 2026 the library used Plaud and colleagues (*Clin Pharmacol Ther* 1995;58:185-191), eight adults with fixed rate constants per kilogram. Plaud's clearance, 9.78 mL/kg/min, was more than twice the 4 mL/kg/min of other studies and the label; Kleijn's, 4.05 mL/kg/min at the 70 kg, 35-year-old reference man, agrees with them. After an intubating dose the plasma concentration is therefore several times higher from about 15 minutes on than it was with Plaud's model, and recovery is later.

### Covariates

Every parameter carries a weight term: the volumes are proportional to weight and the clearances to its 0.75 power. The weight the equations see is the pharmacokinetic weight under the default [fat-free-mass scaling](help:models/fat-free-mass) and total body weight with the switch off, as for sugammadex. Clearance falls with **age**, by 0.68% of its value at 43 years for each year, and the peripheral volume grows with it; as printed, both run linearly across the whole range studied, so a child's clearance is higher than an adult's of the same weight (by 28% at 1 year). Below 1 year, the youngest age studied, the age terms are held at 1 year. **Creatinine clearance** enters only the central volume, which shrinks a little as renal function improves; it is Cockcroft-Gault at the **Serum creatinine** from the Patient Profile, or at an **assumed normal creatinine** for age and sex when that is left blank (see [Renal function](help:models/covariates)), held within the 4.3 to 239 mL/min the source studied. Kleijn found no effect of sex, so a woman differs from a man only through her fat-free mass and her creatinine clearance. The source's term for Asian patients (a 21% lower intercompartmental clearance) is left at its reference value, as stanpumpR collects no such covariate.

### Effect site

The time to peak effect is 4.5 minutes, the population median time to maximum block at the adductor pollicis after 0.15 mg/kg in Cortínez and colleagues (*Br J Anaesth* 2007;99:679-685; range 2.0 to 7.7 minutes). Solved against Kleijn's kinetics it gives a ke0 of about 0.40/min, twice Cortínez's own 0.19 to 0.20/min: Kleijn's plasma curve falls more slowly in the first minutes than the curve Cortínez's ke0 was estimated with, so a faster equilibration is needed to peak at the same time. Kleijn's own ke0 (0.134/min without sevoflurane, from the train-of-four ratio) would put the peak at about 8.6 minutes on the same curve, later than any of Cortínez's patients, and is not used. Onset at the vocal cords is faster than at the adductor pollicis, and is not what the curve shows.

### Typical concentrations and recovery

The shaded band is 1 to 2.2 mcg/mL and the default recovery threshold 1 mcg/mL. Here "effect" is a concentration, not a twitch height: the model does not include the sigmoid relationship between concentration and block, so the plot cannot show train-of-four counts. The threshold of 1 mcg/mL is roughly the concentration at which recovery of neuromuscular function begins, and *Time until threshold* is then the time to the start of recovery, not to full reversal: after 0.6 mg/kg in a 70 kg, 40-year-old man, about 39 minutes. See [the rocuronium scenario](scenario:rocuronium-recovery).

### Where to be careful

Rocuronium's duration is prolonged by hepatic dysfunction, in some studies by renal failure (Kleijn found creatinine clearance on the central volume only, not on clearance), by the inhaled agents (a pharmacodynamic potentiation: Kleijn's sevoflurane slowed ke0 by 57% and lowered the EC50 by 40%) and by hypothermia, and shortened by sugammadex, none of which is modelled. [Sugammadex](help:drugs/sugammadex) can be given in the dose table, but only its own concentration is simulated: Kleijn's binding of the two, and the faster loss of rocuronium from the effect site that free sugammadex causes, need states the library's engines do not have, so sugammadex does not change the rocuronium row. The model has two compartments, and its terminal half-life (about 38 minutes in an adult) is shorter than the label's 1.4 to 2.4 hours: after long infusions or large doses, the late, low concentrations fall faster here than they will in a patient.
