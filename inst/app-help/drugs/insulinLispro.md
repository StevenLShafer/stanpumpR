### The model

Tham and colleagues pooled 16 pharmacokinetic and clamp studies, mostly in healthy volunteers (*J Clin Pharmacol* 2017), into one-compartment models with an absorption rate for each formulation and an apparent volume and clearance for each insulin. Table 2's values are used with the covariate of the supplement's equations: the volume grows steeply with body weight, 43.0 L × (weight/80)^2.48, held within the 52.6 to 86 kg the lispro studies covered (the paper's own check in obese subjects was external). Lispro has no dose term. Concentrations are in **mcU/mL** of the injected insulin, the exogenous insulin alone: the endogenous baseline the paper estimated is not added. Insulin lispro U-100 (Humalog) has a volume of 43.0 L and clearance of 30.5 L/h. 72.9% of each dose is absorbed first-order at 0.989/h after a lag of 0.265 h; the other 27.1% enters the circulation at a constant rate over 1.06 hours. The zero-order share enters the circulation directly from the moment of injection, and the lag delays the depot only: the paper's Figure 1 draws the zero-order input from the injection site straight into the central volume, and its supplement defines the lag as that of the first-order absorption. In pregnancy, enter the pre-pregnancy weight: a term-pregnant weight enlarges this volume by weight alone, with no pregnancy data behind it.

### Routes

Subcutaneous injection, `U SC` or `U/kg SC` (three times daily with meals), and by **insulin pump**, `U/hr SC`, which Humalog U-100's label permits. A pump rate runs from its row's time until the next `U/hr SC` row; its zero-order share is given as that of each 2-minute portion of the delivery. At a steady rate the concentration settles at the rate over the clearance: 1 U/hr gives 32.8 mcU/mL. Humalog U-200, which must not be used in a pump, is not offered.

### Effect site

Every insulin shares one effect site, with an equilibration rate constant (ke0) of 0.876 per hour, so that the effect lags the plasma insulin by the time insulin takes to act, and each product's own absorption sets when its effect peaks. The constant reproduces the 23-minute peak of the glucose infusion rate after an intravenous bolus of regular insulin (Howey 1994; Galloway 1992's study, 20 minutes) and puts the effect peaks of subcutaneous lispro, regular insulin and NPH within about half an hour of the clamp studies' (2.2, 2.5 and 5.0 hours against about 1.8, 3.0 and 5.4 to 5.9), and it agrees with the FDA's analysis of 72 clamps after subcutaneous insulin aspart, 0.996 per hour (95% CI 0.88 to 1.11). How it was derived is on the page [Antidiabetic models and their limits](help:models/antidiabetics).

### What is not plotted

The concentration and its effect site only. Glucose, a clamp's glucose infusion rate and HbA1c are separate endpoints and are not drawn; a unit of one insulin is not converted into another's.
