## What is here

The **Antidiabetic** drugs in the startup menu are named, source-specific population pharmacokinetic models of glucose-lowering drugs. Each reproduces one published analysis of one product, by one route, in the population it was fitted in. **Every one is plotted as a concentration only.** None predicts blood glucose, and none is a dosing algorithm.

## A product is not a molecule

The key of each model is the **product, formulation and route**, not the ingredient. Exenatide is the immediate-release injection (Byetta); the extended-release microspheres (Bydureon) and BCise suspension release it over weeks and are not modelled. The oral semaglutide tablets (Rybelsus) are a separate drug from the injection, and the newer tablets the 2026 label says are not interchangeable mg for mg with Rybelsus are not modelled. Insulin U-100 and U-500 are absorbed at different rates. The glargine model was fitted to Basaglar on an immunoreactive assay and is not a model of Lantus, Semglee, Rezvoglar or glargine U-300 (Toujeo), whose depot behaves differently. Humalog U-200 was a validation set only and is not offered, and neither is its pump use, which its label forbids. Insulin degludec is one drug for Tresiba U-100 and U-200, whose exposures are equivalent at the same dose in units; its degludec-insulin aspart and degludec-liraglutide combinations are not modelled. Biosimilars, other insulins (the aspart biosimilars and premixes, glulisine, detemir, icodec), the fixed-ratio pens (Soliqua, Xultophy) and every product not listed above are kept in a registry with their evidence status, not offered as a curve borrowed from a related product.

## Concentration, not glucose

No glucose curve is drawn, and no drug has a band or a *Time until threshold*. Two groups have an effect site, drawn as the effect-site concentration, to show when the drug's action peaks:

- **Every insulin shares one effect site**, ke0 0.876/h (an equilibration half-time of 47 minutes), because the delay belongs to insulin's action, not to the product. Each product reaches it through its own absorption, which is what separates the effect peaks of regular insulin, lispro and NPH. The rate constant is solved from the time to the peak glucose infusion rate of a euglycaemic clamp after 10 U of regular insulin intravenously, 23 minutes (Howey 1994; 20 minutes in Galloway 1992), on insulin's own two-exponential intravenous disposition: a 5.1-minute distribution phase (Galloway 1992) and a 46.3-minute terminal phase, with a mean residence time of 32 minutes (Howey 1994). Without the fast phase the same peak would need a rate constant four to six times larger. It was checked against effect peaks after subcutaneous doses, which the derivation did not use: regular insulin 2.5 h against the measured 3.0 h, lispro 2.2 h against 1.7 to 1.9 h, NPH 5.0 h against 5.4 to 5.9 h. The FDA's review of Fiasp, fitting an effect compartment to 72 clamps of subcutaneous insulin aspart, found 0.996/h (95% CI 0.88 to 1.11).
- **Glyburide** has the effect site Rydberg and colleagues fitted to the fall in glucose after intravenous and oral glibenclamide in healthy volunteers (1997), ke0 1.59/h. Its metabolites M1 and M2, which Rydberg found active with effect sites of their own, are not modelled.

The other drugs have no effect site: their glucose lowering is not a function of the plasma concentration with a delay that a ke0 could describe. The published pharmacodynamic models belong to endpoints of their own, each with its own population and time scale, and none is drawn:

- **HbA1c and fasting glucose** over weeks to months: semaglutide (exposure-response at week 26 or 30), tirzepatide and dulaglutide (FDA disease models), empagliflozin, dapagliflozin and canagliflozin.
- **The meal glucose-insulin system** in type 2 diabetes for liraglutide (Røge 2015), and a published type 1 diabetes glucose model for lispro (Hovorka 2004).
- **The single-dose glucose response** to metformin in healthy adults (Chae 2012).
- **Urinary glucose excretion** for the SGLT2 inhibitors, **DPP-4 inhibition** for the gliptins, and **the glucose infusion rate of a euglycaemic clamp** for the insulins. A clamp's glucose infusion rate is what it takes to hold glucose constant, not the patient's glucose.

Adding the effects of two drugs, or of exogenous and endogenous insulin, is not supported by any of them.

## What each curve leaves out

- **Covariates not applied.** Tham's endogenous-insulin baseline, the insulin aspart model's sex, dose, BMI and antibody terms (whose reference values the FDA review does not print), empagliflozin's smoking, race, protein and liver-enzyme terms, and ertugliflozin's race and food effects. Each drug's page lists what is applied and what is not.
- **Insulin is the exogenous insulin alone.** Endogenous secretion, the baseline Tham estimated, and the C-peptide correction are not added, and an insulin unit is never converted into another insulin's.

## The registry

`inst/extdata/antidiabeticRegistry.csv` lists every product the specification named, implemented or not, with its pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic evidence status (PK-FIT, PK-AUDIT, PK-CONSTRAINT, PK-TEMPLATE and the PD equivalents), its route permissions where the product's label decides them, and its source. `inst/extdata/antidiabeticParameters.csv` gives every number of the implemented models with its units, whether it was estimated, fixed or derived, the population and the source. Both are rebuilt by `data-raw/antidiabeticRegistry.R`. A product stays registry-only until a complete source model has been read and checked; borrowing a related product's parameters is not allowed.
