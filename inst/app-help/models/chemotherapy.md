Thirty-three chemotherapy drugs are in the library, under **Chemotherapy** in the startup menu, each with its own help page naming its source (the [drug index](help:drugs/index) lists them). High-dose methotrexate, for example, has an adult model ([methotrexate](help:drugs/methotrexate), Ibarra 2023) and one for children and young adults ([methotrexatePediatric](help:drugs/methotrexatePediatric), Taylor 2023, the MTXPK.org model), and the program chooses between them by the patient's age. Most are linear mammillary models, solved in closed form. Several need more: saturable elimination ([fluorouracil](help:drugs/fluorouracil), [paclitaxel](help:drugs/paclitaxel), [paclitaxelProteinBound](help:drugs/paclitaxelProteinBound), [thioguanineIV](help:drugs/thioguanineIV)), a clearance the drug induces ([ifosfamide](help:drugs/ifosfamide), and cyclophosphamide in children), binding to tissue (vincristine in children), a clearance that steps down during the course ([busulfan](help:drugs/busulfan)), and a gallbladder store emptied into the gut ([oxaliplatin](help:drugs/oxaliplatin)). Where the library holds several models of one drug the program chooses among them by the patient's age and weight. The Patient Profile's **Serum albumin** reaches methotrexate, docetaxel and protein-bound paclitaxel, and **Serum AST** reaches epirubicin. All are plasma only, and every parameter was read in the paper's PDF. The rest of this page is the evidence plan: every drug and product considered, the published population models that could describe it, what each would need from the engine, and what stands in the way. It is built from the registry in `inst/extdata/chemotherapyRegistry.csv` and cannot drift from it.

## What a chemotherapy curve can and cannot say

A curve here is a **population prediction of plasma concentration** for the typical patient with the covariates entered. It is not therapeutic drug monitoring, not a Bayesian estimate from a measured level, and not a dose recommendation. Several distinctions matter more for these drugs than for anaesthetics:

- **What is measured.** Carboplatin may be reported as intact drug, as ultrafilterable platinum or as total platinum; cisplatin as parent, as free platinum or as total platinum; irinotecan as the lactone or the carboxylate, with its metabolite SN-38; the liposomal products as encapsulated, released or total drug. A model describes only the species its source measured, and the help page of each implemented drug names it.
- **Exposure targets are not bands.** The established targets for these drugs are areas under the curve over a stated interval (carboplatin's Calvert AUC, 5-FU's AUC over a 46-hour infusion, busulfan's cumulative AUC or average steady-state concentration) or a time above a concentration (paclitaxel). None of them is a concentration band, so none is drawn as one. Methotrexate's glucarpidase and leucovorin thresholds are rescue criteria, not a therapeutic range, and a simulation never replaces a measured level.
- **No effect site.** No chemotherapy model here has a measured equilibration between plasma and effect, and toxicity (myelosuppression, cardiotoxicity, neuropathy) is cumulative and delayed. Every model is plasma only.
- **Genotype guidance is not a clearance.** The CPIC and DPWG guidelines for DPYD (fluoropyrimidines), UGT1A1 (irinotecan) and TPMT/NUDT15 (thiopurines) recommend dose changes; they do not estimate a change in clearance, so none is applied as one.
- **Doses as the label writes them.** Each drug offers the dose units of its FDA label: mg/m2 and mg/m2/hr for most, mg/m2/day for the continuous infusions of fluorouracil and cytarabine, g/m2 for high-dose methotrexate and ifosfamide, mg/m2 PO bid and qd for capecitabine and temozolomide, units/kg and units/m2 for bleomycin, mg/kg/day for cladribine's seven-day infusion and mg/kg PO qd for thioguanine. A per-m2 dose is multiplied by the patient's Mosteller body surface area from the Patient Profile's weight and height ([Units](help:units)). Carboplatin's Calvert dose is calculated in mg, not mg/m2, and is entered in mg.
- **Routes the label allows and the models do not.** A model describes the route its source studied, so some labelled routes are not offered: methotrexate intramuscular, subcutaneous and intrathecal; bleomycin intramuscular, subcutaneous and intrapleural; cytarabine subcutaneous and intrathecal; temozolomide intravenous; cyclophosphamide oral. Each drug's page says so. If you need a route stanpumpR does not offer, please contact Steven Shafer (steven.shafer@stanford.edu). For a one-time simulation in the meantime, a published pharmacokinetic study of that route can often be uploaded to the **Single Paper Review** tab, which reads its model and simulates it.
- **Dose normalisation is not a covariate.** Dosing in mg/m² does not mean a model's clearance scales with body surface area. Each model keeps the size relation its source fitted.

## The models considered

The **first wave** is carboplatin, high-dose methotrexate, 5-fluorouracil and busulfan, the drugs with the best-established exposure targets. The **second wave** is the rest of the drugs asked for (cisplatin, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, solvent-based paclitaxel, vincristine, irinotecan, etoposide), and the **expansion** list follows. The *Engine* column is judged from the abstract against the engines as they stand: a linear model of up to three compartments, a parent with one metabolite, saturable (Michaelis-Menten) elimination with one peripheral compartment, or a mechanism the engine does not carry (a chain of metabolites, enzyme autoinduction, saturable tissue binding, enterohepatic recirculation, release from a liposome, intracellular formation).
