stanpumpR

2D view (the first): each box is a compartment. Its fill height is the concentration, on one scale for every box and the whole run, so drug can be seen flowing down each gradient. Its width is the volume, in proportion while the largest compartment is at most 10 times the smallest; beyond that the widths are compressed (a log scale of volume) so every compartment stays visible, and the view says so. The thin blue bar beside V1 is the effect site; each arrow's thickness is the rate. 3D view (Charles Minto's): the same quantities in three dimensions, the vessels' floor areas sized as the boxes' widths are.

Each cylinder is a compartment. Its floor area is the volume and the liquid's height is the concentration, so the liquid itself represents the amount of drug.

Each arrow is a flow of drug: the arrow's thickness (its cross-section) is the rate. Red flows into V1; green flows out of it. The arrow from above is the pump; the arrow from the front left is drug absorbed from a depot (an oral, SC, IM or other extravascular dose).

Every flow is read from stanpumpR's own amounts as they change, so it holds for any model, saturable ones included.

The blue ribbon is the effect site, flat because its volume is negligible: its height is Ce.

Gold marks the target.
